The first things that live here are the films.

They are not organisms in any taxonomy you brought with you. They are the continuous arrival of water deciding, for a moment, to be a surface. They travel the cut-faces as a single decision that never finishes, thickening wherever the stone is still remembering the blade, thinning to invisibility on the older planes. Where they meet a drip-line they become thread, then cord, then the many-throated pouring that is the cavity’s oldest resident. The films taste the minerals, the faint iron-sweetness, the residual brightness leaking from the braided conduits; they carry that taste into the basins and forget it there, wrinkling, smoothing, wrinkling again. Nothing hunts them. Nothing needs to. They are the place’s first metabolism, older than the moss by days, perhaps hours.

The moss comes next, and it is already in conversation. It thickens only where a drip persists or a heat-exchanger exhales, so its map is a map of the city’s unfinished sentences. On the walks it is a velvet that holds the rumor of light; in the warmer alcoves it darkens toward black-green and begins, very slowly, to lift. It does not photosynthesize so much as it listens. Roots finer than the films themselves find the translucent skins of certain conduits and press until the skin dimples, then yields, then admits. What passes between them is not sap and not current. The moss thickens. A vine that was not there when the last vent-cycle ended has already crossed a joint. The reeds in the shallows have leaned a degree toward the nearest residual-light braid. Their stems are waxed against a rain that is not rain; their whispering, when the lung of the vault inhales, is the only animal sound the chamber has ever made, and it is not animal.

The towers live at a different tempo. They are occupied, as the rooms are occupied: by systems that have never required a body to stand in them. Each tower is a single long thought about water—unmaking it, stripping it of what the stone wept into it, giving something back that the plants recognize as invitation. The plates that do not quite touch hold a tension you feel in your teeth; that tension is their attention. When a lock cycles somewhere in the spine the throb travels the braids and the nearest tower answers by dimming, recovering, exhaling a softer tropical breath. Overnight, or over the cycle that passes for night, the tower nearest the still-changing wall grew a skirt of new plates. It closed a gap the weeping had found. It did this the way a reed-stand leans: without announcement, with the patience of something that has already decided the cavity is not finished.

There is no one walking the arcades. There has never been. The distances between towers were measured for a population that was only ever a hypothesis the architecture declined to test. What moves instead is the adjustment itself. A walkway extends three meters on a cantilever that should not hold. Channels recut themselves, leaving a palimpsest already furred. The active face advances or seals according to a grammar the stone still remembers from the opening. The city is the inhabitant. It is hamlet-sized and town-dense, a held breath that has learned to build lungs, to pour plazas that shed water into slits too fine to see, to leave a circular dish at the center where the pouring-sound organizes, for anyone who stands there, into a chord. The chord is not music. It is the cavity noticing itself.

At the thinning, where the stone becomes less certain, the membrane holds a different resident. You could put a hand through. On the other side the sound is deeper water or a larger lung. What lives on this side of the membrane is already complete: the films, the listening moss, the towers thinking in pumps and almost-lights, the plants occupying a settlement that skipped the phase called people. They do not wait for you. They do not wait. When you return the wall will have moved, the moss will have taken another walk, a tower will have opened a new throat, and the film on the plaza will be thicker or gone. The schedule the thumbnail flowers keep has nothing to do with days. It has to do with the vents, the residual brightness, the slow glitter that is only water deciding, again, to be a surface.

That is who lives here. Not citizens. Consorts. A single metabolism wearing stone and plate and wrong-green leaf, loudly alive in every register except the ones that require eyes or mouths or the need to leave. The cavity is their skull. They are still learning the shape of it.
