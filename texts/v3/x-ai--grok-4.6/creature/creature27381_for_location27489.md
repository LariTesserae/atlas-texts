The plants occupy their cylinders the way clauses occupy a contract, but they are not what lives here. They satisfy a requirement and then stop. Their thick leaves hold a geometry that was poured, not grown; the mineral wool around their roots has the exact dryness of everything else. They photosynthesize the green work-light at a rate too slow to measure and transpire nothing. They are alive the way a switch in the on position is alive. They do not constitute an inhabitant.

Nothing else moves. The roar continues to do the moving. After the first hour the inner ear’s private whistle becomes a companion rather than a protest, a thin second voice riding the industrial vowel. The cold remains a law rather than a sensation. In that law a figure stands between the nearest ceramic housing and the row of cylinders, far enough from both that the emptiness around it stays negotiated, designed.

It is the height of a person who once walked upright in weather. The garment is the same unpainted metal as the walls, thin plates overlapping like the decking’s dunes, fastened with the same undulled zinc. No helmet. The head is bare, the scalp the color of the mineral wool, the face arranged in the same unchanging geometry as the plants. Eyes open, the greenish light sitting on them without reflection. Breath occurs — the processed gas must go somewhere — but it does not fog, does not lift a single mote of the faint dust that has settled only in the leaf-axils. The mouth is closed. The teeth, if they still exist behind the lips, have long since learned the frequency and no longer buzz.

It does not tend. There is nothing to tend. The gauges are already at their painted values; the turbine disc remains a frozen sunflower; the plants will neither add a cell nor surrender one. The figure’s work, if the word still applies, is occupancy. It stands where the roar has grain, close enough to the housings that the crushed-slag roughness of the vowel can be felt in the plates of the garment. Occasionally — the interval is not regular because regularity would be a kind of change — one gloved hand rises and rests against an inspection port. The palm does not warm the ceramic. The ceramic does not cool the palm. They have reached the same temperature and intend to keep it.

It came with the loft. The unused lifting lugs on the ceiling still have their factory grease; the protective films still cling to two lower panels. The figure was installed in the same week, perhaps the same hour, then the hatch was dogged and time, in this room, declined to continue. Its posture has not shifted. The soles of its boots sit in one of the stamped troughs exactly as the piston crown sits at the top of its travel. If it ever walks, the footsteps remain theoretical beneath the sound. It has no need to reach the porthole. Elevation is already in the inner ear; the high blank is already understood. The white beyond the glass is not a destination. It is the same unchangingness, only thinner.

Sometimes it lies down. The metal takes its heat at once, without drama, the way it would take anyone’s. From the trough the ribbed plate of the ceiling becomes a sky with part numbers, regular, bolted, young. The figure’s eyes stay open. The roar lays itself over the body like a second, heavier atmosphere. In that position the lamps’ insectile whine is more distinct, several octaves above the main note, and the figure appears to listen to it the way one might listen to a clock that has decided never to strike. No expression crosses the face. Expression would be modulation, and modulation is not in the vocabulary.

It does not age in any direction the room would recognize. Skin remains the color of dry bone-wool. The garment’s seams stay sharp. Whatever name it carried before the hatch sealed has been filed, like the roar itself, as a kind of silence. It is not waiting. Waiting implies a later state. It occupies the clamped dimension the engine manufactures and exports. Purpose and idling have become the same gesture, and the figure is that gesture given a spine and a pair of open eyes.

The loft will tolerate this much company and no more. A second figure would require another trough, another negotiated emptiness, another set of unused lugs. None has been provided. The plants remain three. The porthole remains one. The engine remains the center third of the floor, roaring the only weather the place will ever have. The inhabitant stands, or lies, inside that weather, as much a condition of the room as the cold or the light or the absence of frost. Nothing, including it, is required to continue. It continues anyway, exactly as it is, the way the sound does: already the entire air, held since the room was sealed.
