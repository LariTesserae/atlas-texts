The dish holds almost nothing that would answer to a name. What grows does so without ambition, pressed into the north-facing seams and the inner elbows of the standing chambers: moss that has learned the exact thickness of a sleeping body, liverworts that overlap like the scales of something that once swam and then chose stone, a willow that has forgotten height and now travels the rock in the manner of a vein. In the pockets the green is indecent, swollen, a stuffed insistence that drinks the drip and the rime and then, when the wind scours the basin dry, gives the water back as a darker, slower breath. Step away and the life thins to maps of lichen, a few grasses combed into submission, the occasional saxifrage flaring red as if the cold itself had bloomed. These are not inhabitants so much as the place’s private weather, a lushness that knows it is permitted only where the wind cannot quite reach.

The tarn keeps its own counsel. Under the skim of ice something dark occasionally shifts—an adjustment rather than a movement—and is still. It may be a larva that has decided centuries are a reasonable lifespan. It may be a leaf that has learned to wait. It may be neither. The black water films and clears on a clock older than any pulse. When the rare thaw arrives and every gutter speaks at once, the tarn receives the whole descending voice of the town and then, as if the sentence were finished, falls silent again. What lives in it lives by remaining almost not there.

Animals treat the dish as a rumor they do not intend to confirm. A hare the color of dirty snow will sometimes sit on the worn lip, consider the thin air and the long drop beyond, and turn away. Small birds use the chambers as momentary lee, leave their only recent amendment on the floors, and are gone. Their absence is part of the inventory. The ecology here is process without census: freeze and release, wet and unwet, the slow farming of lichen, the louder farming of wind. The town is an instrument; the climate is still practicing the same short piece.

And yet the volume is not empty. Between the standing chambers there is enough air to lose a shout, and often the shout is already present, belonging to no throat that could be named. The wind inhabits rather than passes through. It arrives already organized, finds the radial streets, and is split, braided, slammed, returned as a second and third weather. It has syllables. It has the long groan of air forced through apertures that were once doors or never were. On ordinary days it is a continuous low roar that never quite resolves into speech. On the days a front crosses the plateau the sound becomes physical, pressing the ears from inside, and the chambers boom like cisterns that remember they were built to hold something other than air. Even the fog, when it sits in the dish like a bowl of milk, carries a faint sizzle of droplets striking moss. The wind is the last civic infrastructure, and it is not finished.

Walk the streets long enough and the civic plan continues past the last wall, into the living shoulder of the plateau where no block was ever set. The same intervals appear as joints in the bedrock, as parallel runnels, as the suggestion of rooms that were never excavated—or never built. It is possible the town is simply what the mountain came to, the hardest ribs left standing after everything softer went to grit. It is possible someone arranged the ribs and then waited until arrangement and erosion became the same labor. There are places where the fitted slabs of the plaza are not fitted but continuous, a single rock persuaded into the idea of a floor. There are places where a chamber’s ceiling is a natural bedding plane and its walls are stacked, and the two meet without apology. No tool-mark argues either way. The place offers a finished shape that still works.

What lives here has taken the same long view. It does not announce itself with footprint or breath that could be measured in the thin air. It has been handled by the same freeze that keyed the blocks, worn to the same rounded competence. In the three or four chambers that still close overhead it keeps a little stillness against the roar, facing the prevailing wind as if that were the only visitor still expected. It has no metal, no cut that remembers a blade. Its thicknesses are the last competent ones. Sometimes, when the fog condenses and begins to drip, the drips find the old gutters and the thing that lives here listens the way the stone listens—without surprise. It has signed every joint with moss. It has let the wind through every room. It does not invent anything now except the weather, and the weather has been practicing on this same small, high, emptied, carefully organized hollow for longer than naming lasts.

At night the geometry remains readable in negative: the dish still itself, the chambers still black mouths, the wind still employed. The pale pavement holds the last of the light and gives it back without warmth. In the moss pockets the green goes almost black, waiting. The tarn is a colder copy of the sky. What lives here does not sleep so much as continue the old completion, the sentence the mountain finished and then, out of some mineral courtesy, left standing. It is not a spectacle. It is a consistency that will not agree to be only one thing—civic or mineral, built or left, inhabitant or the place itself remembering how to hold a little stillness, a little water, a little green, against the long, smooth drop beyond the rim. The slopes go down for a very long time, but they go down without drama. The work is already done. The thing that lives here is simply what remains when a height has been allowed to finish speaking, and the speaking included, almost as an afterthought, the image of a town, or the habit of one, or the patience to wait until the distinction no longer mattered.
