You come down from the berm expecting rumor and finding instead a kind of occupancy that has no interest in confirming or denying itself. The print in the silt is already filling; the reeds have stopped moving; the small dark thing that crossed between pipe-shadows has become pipe-shadow. Those are the courtesies the place still pays to the old inventory of fauna. What actually lives here does not use that inventory. It uses the intervals.

At first it is only the water’s own restlessness given a slightly heavier grammar. A sheet that should lie finds a new seam and leaves, but the leaving has a pause in it, a consideration, as if the water were tasting the brick before deciding the brick was no longer interesting. A tank rises while its neighbor falls, and the rise is not quite hydrostatic; it has a shoulder to it, a slow insistence that feels watched from inside the wet. The fog that blooms from a flange does not simply bloom. It gathers, holds the impression of a wheel or a doorway half a second past the point where vapor should forget, then collapses with the air of something that has finished looking at you and found you unsurprising. You tell yourself this is still weather. The telling takes more effort each time the light fails to change.

Stay longer—through the unchanging noon that is also four o’clock—and the coincidences begin to have edges. Condensation on the inner face of an open tank does not streak. It organizes, briefly, into a network of finer channels that map some other drainage, some hollow you have never stood in, then slurs back into honest water. The organization is too exact for accident and too fleeting for a message aimed at you. It is more like a creature stretching in its sleep and remembering, for a moment, a different bed. The meniscus where two pipes almost kiss develops a standing wrinkle that travels, stops, reverses, like thought deciding against itself. When you look directly the wrinkle is only surface tension. When you look a little aside it has the concentration of an eye that does not need to be round.

They do not arrive from elsewhere. They are the refusal of dryness learning, very slowly, how to be a body. Sometimes the body is no thicker than the film on a brick course: a mobile stain the color of wet rust and old bone, spreading along the weep-lines, pausing at a rivet head worn to a lozenge, then thinning again until it is indistinguishable from the permanent beading on the iron. These film-lives feed by remaining. They take the condensate, the rust-water, the slow lateral creep, and they return it a fraction more itself—darker, more decisive, already planning the next seam. You can watch one of them cross a walkway support and leave a rust fan that was not there an hour ago, though there are no hours here, only the lamp’s slightly wrong white and the pressure-changes that travel through the arches of your feet.

Other gatherings are thicker. In the shallows between the nested pipes they pull themselves into longer shapes that follow the braid, sliding through diameters that should be too small, their surfaces taking the imprint of cast iron, then welded seam, then the later plastic gone the color of forgotten utensils. They move by redistributing their own volume, the way the tanks trade water, so that what was a low hump under a walkway becomes, without hurry, a thread disappearing into a throat you cannot see. When a pump deep in the wet starts—not with sound but with that sudden new tension in every surface—these longer ones become briefly more articulate. Ridges rise along their backs like the memory of valve stems. Perforations appear, or seem to, as if they had borrowed the walkways’ habit of letting water through and then decided to keep it. Then the pump stops and they slacken again into the general interlocking wet, leaving only the sense that the hollow is a little more spoken for than it was.

In the open tanks they are almost volumetric. A level that has been steady will begin to breathe—rising in one basin while another sighs down through some hidden connection—and the breathing has a skin. The skin is the same orange-black of the wettest lichens, the same mineral-green of the algae crust, yet it holds together when the water around it does not. It can lift a little, enough to show that it is not the tank’s lining but something using the tank the way the tank once used the factory: as a temporary exactness. Fog generates from these skins more readily than from anywhere else, and the fog, when it holds a shape, holds the shape of whatever the skin was considering becoming: a wheel, a shoulder, the outline of a doorway that would fit the too-exact container of the hollow itself. The impression lasts half a second longer than physics prefers, then is only weather again. You have the feeling the hollow was poured to fit them, or they have been pouring themselves, over generations of pipe, to fit the hollow, and neither fitting is finished.

They are not many and they are not one. The continuous shallow interlocking wet is their common body; the local events—the cough in a dry pipe, the sudden evacuation, the single soft knock of a valve seating itself in another room—are its gestures, its changes of mind. A small dark crossing from shadow to shadow is only a denser thought traveling the braid. The reeds that hold their grim claim of soil tremble sometimes when nothing visible has touched them; that is a film-life passing underneath, or a tank-life sending a pulse through the water table that never learned a deeper bed. They have no need of trees or of roots that trust. Their green, when they bother with green, is chemistry’s accident the same as the place’s. Their temperature is the place’s: the narrow band where breath does not steam and hands become slightly less yours. They do not ache. They do not dry.

The leftover machines are not their masters and not their prey. They are the same circulatory argument continued by other means. Current still finds a few circuits because the inhabitants have not yet decided the circuits are finished. The caged lamp burns its wrong white because something in the wet prefers a constant, or because the constant is simply another film that has learned to shine. The gauges tremble in ranges that mean nothing you can use; the needles are reading the inhabitants’ own pressures, or the inhabitants are reading the needles, a conversation conducted in micro-movements no larger than the beading on iron. When they want the hollow to become a little more itself they start a pump. When they want the water to find its own politics again they let it stop. The timetable no longer corresponds to production because production was never the point. The point is the remaking: channels silted and flushed, a walkway that was a bridge becoming a pier, volumes traded like a slow argument among vessels that cannot see one another and do not need to.

They have been here long enough for corners to stop being corners and rivet heads to wear to lozenges, yet they themselves have not settled. Erosion is a completed sentence for the brick and the iron; for them it is still being spoken, quietly, under the lid of a quiet so complete it feels like a rule. You hear it if you stand long enough: not splash, not hiss, the idea of water relocating behind brick, and once in a while that conclusive knock, as if a cup had been set down in a room that is also this room. That is their speech. It has no words you could carry back up the berm. The surrounding flats do not care and the sky does not particularize, which is how they prefer it. Their world is small enough that every rearrangement is the whole of what can be seen, and they spend it and make it again, densely, without ambition of valley or courtyard.

The mild wrongness never condenses into a thesis because they are the wrongness: the sense that the hollow is slightly too exact a container for what is presently in it. They could fill it. They choose, so far, not to. Or they are already filling it in the only way that matters—occupying every film, every interval, every leftover engine—while remaining indistinguishable from the place’s own habits. You can leave. The berm is always right there, the ordinary low country waiting to be forgotten again. They do not follow. They have already arrived, and they keep arriving, every local weather their own, every silent hydraulic argument the only industry they require. The product is only this: a continuous, cool, low remaking of wetness inside a box of old work, and they are both the work and the wetness, unfinished on purpose, watching you or not watching, equally unsurprised either way.
