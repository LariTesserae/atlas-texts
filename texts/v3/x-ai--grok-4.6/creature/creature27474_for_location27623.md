The Lowworks keep their own census, and it never changes.

What lives here first presents as a single wet dark, then as many. The bodies share one grammar: a low, glandular length, neck too articulated, jaw that unhinges past any reasonable skull. They scale from the size of a drowned cat to the bulk of a standing pony, yet none of them is young and none of them is old. Size is only a stop on a continuum that has no other meaning. Their hides take the color of condensation on galvanized iron; when they press together the wet becomes a single pelt. They occupy rather than inhabit. They tile the rooftops like living coping stones. They pack the open terrace doorways until the rooms behind are only a darker throat. They stand chest-deep in the canal’s motionless finger, a rank that shivers as one organism when the triple-expansion engine behind the pump-house speaks. The loading apron is a continuous hide broken only by the same permitted gaps, always the same width, always in the same places, as though the flagstones themselves had negotiated an easement centuries ago.

They eat the rust-film, the yeast-stink in the traps, the beads that gather on every rivet. They eat one another with a courtesy so absolute it leaves no vacancy. A flank parts, a throat works, the surrounding mass closes by exactly the width of what was taken; somewhere just out of the direct line of sight an equivalent volume is already standing in a gap that had been held open for it. No birth is witnessed. No corpse remains. The ledger balances itself out of view, like a polite host who never lets you see the kitchen.

Their sound is not separate from the machinery. The small ones click wetly, the large ones roar from deep in the bronchi, and the whole population finds the two or three notes the dynamos already hold. Machine and flesh have long since become one standing chord. A relief valve lifts; a ridge of backs answers. The dynamo’s pitch lists by a fraction it never actually lists; the clicking leans with it, an eternal correction that is also an eternal agreement. After a time the noise stops being noise. It becomes the medium, like the damp itself.

There are also the figures that still wear the outline of people. One in oilskins crosses the main yard twice a day along the exact path the animals permit, does something unnecessary to a valve, and is gone. Another sits in the pump-house window and has been looking out, or not looking out, since the glass was last cleaned, which was never. Their faces are not hidden; they are simply finished, like the brick, like the puddles. If they speak it is under the chord, and nothing here suggests speech is still practiced. They part the animals the way a post parts water that has always known the post would be there.

What lives in the Lowworks is therefore not a population so much as a completed weather. The animals are the flesh part of a balance so old it has worn through the bottom of time and come out the other side as a kind of mineral. The figures are the last human gestures the place still bothers to maintain, no more alive than the campanile’s cracked brick, no less necessary. Shadows fall correctly. A drop gathers, falls, is replaced by a drop of the same size. An animal on the mill ridge lifts its too-long neck, tastes the cool, lowers the neck to the identical angle. The puddles hold a sky that does not move.

You can walk the boundary and feel the chord thin into ordinary wind, which here is only a rumor from above the dish. Turn back and they are as they were: the same number, the same positions, the same wet dark singing the same unfinished note. They do not wait. They do not remember. They simply continue, denser than any city, quieter than any ruin, finished.
