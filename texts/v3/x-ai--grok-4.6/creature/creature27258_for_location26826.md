The lives here do not arrive or persist. They precipitate, the way the rain does: hard, brief, already leaving. What occupies the basin is occupancy itself, a density so complete that every surface is both tenant and tenement, every interval of air already leased to something that will not finish the hour.

Begin where the light first fails, in the stacked nights of leaf. The paddles are not merely eaten; they are inhabited while they photosynthesize, their rubbery flesh tunneled by pale, thread-thin borers that drink the same sap the leaf is still making. These borers do not stay borers. By the time a leaf yellows they have already extruded a second, flatter body that clings to the underside like a second cuticle, waiting for the next rain-sweat so they can dissolve and reassemble lower down. Their waste is luminous for the length of a breath, then becomes food for the umbrellas beneath, which are not fungi exactly but decisions the stems keep making: veined, collapsing, ringing with a faint internal percussion when the air turns thirsty. Touch one and it is already something else, a rope of linked thumb-bodies pouring itself toward the next damp crease.

Lower, the ground-walkers do not have territories so much as arguments that last the length of a stride. They are low, many-legged, their carapaces still showing the same unfinished grain as the rock, edges that catch and tear. Each one is a negotiation already failing: mandibles clicking against the neighbor’s, a brief lock of limbs, then a parting that leaves both slightly altered, a plate shed, a scent trail reversed. They move in overlapping sheets so dense that the soil itself seems to crawl. When a slope shears—pale stone flashing like a new wound—they do not flee. They become the stitching, their bodies the first opportunistic roots, some of them already sprouting the pale cups that will open and be stripped before the scent finishes traveling. The ones that survive the hour are not the same ones that entered it. A hollow empty at one breath is, at the next, a knot of fur and clicking that was bark a moment before, the wood having briefly decided to be animal.

Above them the heavy-winged thing keeps changing its mind. It is never fully seen, only felt as a rearrangement of the nearer dark, a downdraft that smells of crushed fruit and iron because that is what it has just been: fruit, then the iron of something’s opened side. Its wings are not for traveling so much as for erasing direction. Each beat unmakes the path it just chose. The midges ride those beats like weather, sheets that have weight, thickening the air until breathing is a kind of wading. They do not swarm; they are the air’s decision to become bodies, linking and unlinking over stems, tasting everything, becoming the next taste.

And then there are the ones who leave the pointed stick, the stone whose edge is slightly too useful. They are not a people. They are the impatience given a temporary spine. You meet them as a displacement first, a moving absence in the brown-violet dusk, the way other shapes lean away. Their bodies have the same young angularity as the rock walls: shoulders still deciding their slope, fingers that end in unused edges. They do not wear the living things; the living things wear them, films and ropes and bells of tissue using their heat the way the ferns use the seeps. They fashion what they need in the moment they need it—a stem bitten to a point, a flake struck from a fresh fracture—and they drop it the instant the need passes, because the basin has not yet invented the kind of time that would make keeping worthwhile. Their intelligence is the same hourly lease as the roots. They drink from a seep and by evening the seep drinks from them; they learn the new mouth of the vine and are already elsewhere, their own mouths beginning to forget the shape of what they just tasted.

At the lowest point, where the water should pool and does not, the mosaic of mud-cracks is raced across by thread-roots that are also the last of the walkers, the first of the borers, the unfinished thought of something larger that shifted far off and sent its rearrangement here as a brief hierarchy of sound. Condensation beads on every hair—theirs, the plants’, the rock’s—and is drunk before it falls. The air tastes of copper and leaf-blood because that is what they are made of, briefly. They do not last. Nothing here does. A body that was one chorus of rasping pods is, by the next breath, the friction of actual insects, then the patterned wet that only looked like eyes. Categories fail at the speed of growth. What lives here is the failure itself, the surplus of living so complete there is no remainder, no quiet in which a single image could settle and stay.

Tomorrow the rock will have cracked a little further and they will be the new edges, already tasting the next draft.
