The high country does not keep its tenants distinct. What lives here first appears as motion through mist: the pale browsers in their thousands, long-shanked and limestone-colored, coats permanently beaded, moving in loose constellations that dissolve whenever the ground lifts or a new escarpment weeps into being. They are not herds so much as weather made flesh, grazing the black-green moss cushions and the wiry sedges that cling to meltwater hollows. Their silence is almost complete; hooves meet wet stone with a sound no louder than the drip that never stops. They pass through the settlements as freely as through the unfinished basins, using the pale linear traces when it suits them, vanishing into low cloud as if the plateau itself were inhaling.

Smaller lives crowd the same wet. Fist-sized amphibians cling to every weeping face, their bodies the same dull sheen as the condensate, singing at the edge of hearing—more vibration than voice, timed to the faint tick of cooling rock. Gray birds with shortened wings ride the thermals that still rise from faults not yet cold, never quite landing, nesting only in the lee of towers that have already begun to lift with the newest ridge. Insect swarms hatch in the tea-colored pools that have not learned drainage, darken an afternoon, and are gone when the cold reasserts itself. Everything here is provisional, hatched or born into a map that is still being drawn.

Among them move the others, the ones who built—or grew—the sparse nodes. They are few, matching the vegetation’s density rather than opposing it. You see them first as pale figures on the traces, walking, or as the brief gleam of a silent capsule hovering a hand’s breadth above stone. Their clothing sheds water the way the buildings do, slightly iridescent, taking the color of mist and wet rock so that at a distance they are only another punctuation in the emptiness. Faces stay half-hidden in the constant film of moisture; breath hangs and does not dissipate quickly. They do not hurry. When a slope shears overnight into a new pale escarpment they wait, or the structures wait with them—foundations adjusting, walls briefly translucent while the rock finishes deciding. No one remarks on the mechanism. It is simply how the place breathes.

They live inside the adaptive geometries: towers and low complexes that follow contours still forming, surfaces that look poured rather than assembled. At night the light they give is no brighter than late dusk, sourceless, the same temperature as the air. By day the buildings recede into the weather. People come and go along the same routes the browsers use. Conversations, when they occur, are short, almost murmured, as if speech itself were another form of condensate that should not be wasted. Most of what needs saying is already said by the land: the subsonic shift of another unfinished rise, the drip from a thousand faces, the muffled wingbeats that never quite land.

They share the cold, the wet, the sense that nothing has settled. Some walk the newest ridges at what would be noon, watching ordinary light catch on ordinary stone, then turn and continue because the next node is half a day’s walk and the drainage is still being invented. Others remain inside, tending whatever quiet work the structures require—or simply inhabiting the empty room between sentences. The animals come and go through the same spaces; birds nest against the taller walls; the pale browsers sometimes stand in the lee as if the city were only another fold of highland. Nothing is excluded. The density is too thin for exclusion.

What lives here is therefore not easily separated into people and beasts and grown stone. The same unfinishedness holds them all. The browsers find the newest moss. The amphibians sing to the latest fracture. The gray birds ride heat that will not last. The walkers and the capsule-riders adjust when the ground adjusts, speak little, leave no track. Even the towers themselves seem only another form of life that has learned to lift when the plateau lifts, to weep when the rock weeps, to remain just this side of frozen, just this side of finished. The continent is still being born; everything that lives upon it is writing itself at the same quiet speed, with the same great deal of empty room between one presence and the next.
