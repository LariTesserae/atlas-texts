The bowl does not contain lives so much as it is lived, a single packed duration wearing every available surface at once. At first the surfaces seem separate: the moths that have become the inner curve of the lip, their wings the exact colour and dampness of the limestone they cover, so that a shift is only another moth already occupying the vacancy; the salamanders stacked in the weeping joints like a second, slower masonry, each flank lifting in the same unhurried rhythm as the drip that feeds them; the bats present as extra warmth and extra breath among the hanging ferns, a velvet congestion that never quite resolves into flight. Midges hang in the cool broth of air as if the air itself had decided to thicken in places. Under the moss-pelts the worms and nameless beetles rearrange themselves into the identical arrangement they have always held. These are not inhabitants. They are the bowl’s interchangeable skins, substituted so exactly that packedness never thins and never spills.

Deeper than skins there is only the wet, the decision the atmosphere made once and has never revisited. It condenses on stone, stem, fur, wing, and any other upright thing because it is the upright thing. The polished finger-width paths, the beads that hang and fall and hang again in the same places, the inaudible ticking you hear only when your ear is almost against the rock—these are not weather. They are the pulse of whatever occupies the concavity. The pool at the lowest point, no larger than a table, the temperature of a mouth closed a long time, neither rises nor sinks; what arrives leaves by seams you cannot see. That equilibrium is the occupant’s only expression. Sound would be a gradient, and there are no gradients here. Birth and death, if they occur, occur as replacements so precise the volume remains exact. You cannot tell whether the lives are many or one life using many surfaces because the distinction itself has been worn away.

Stay (though staying has no local meaning) and the cool that does not bite begins to feel like an intention: the last useful form this hanging scoop of ridge could take, already worn to completion and then, out of some courtesy of physics, continuing to look like a process. The mountain around it goes on becoming other shapes. This one shape has stopped. Nothing here is trying to grow taller than the bowl; height would be a kind of leaving. Everything is lateral, finished, occupying. The flake of chert at the lip, half-buried in liverwort, edges dulled by the same water that dulls everything, is neither tool nor relic. It is a coincidence the occupant has not yet decided whether to absorb or to leave as another bead.

If a person could stand without displacing what has already claimed every possible posture, they would feel first the temperature that has been this temperature since the last ice left the height, then the wet that includes the lungs, then the press of bodies that are not theirs and have never been otherwise. The scale is intimate and complete; you could throw a stone across and hit the far wall except there is no room to throw and the wall is not a wall so much as a packed vertical field. The occupant does not watch. Watching would imply a difference it has long since refused. It simply holds its quantity of bodies without remainder, the dark its own skin, the ticking its only remaining motion.

When you leave by the lip you entered, dripping, clothes heavier, hearing readjusted to a world that still bothers with weather and succession, the bowl does not register the departure. Behind you the same stones receive the same beads. The moth-coloured rock remains moth-coloured. The salamanders continue their stacked breathing. The quantity is exact. Time, here, is only duration that has forgotten events can differ.
