The first thing that occupies the chamber is the pale growth itself. It does not arrive or take root so much as it simply continues, a single patient occupation that has had centuries to forget the difference between individual and mass. Cushions of moss have thickened until they are less like plants than like a second, slower floor, each bead of melt held for days on the nap before it is drawn downward into a darkness that is both soil and body. From those cushions the fronds rise not as separate lives but as successive thoughts of the same density: overlapping, layered, never reaching because there is nowhere left to reach. They climb the older stems because the volume must be filled; they flatten against the ceiling because the last hand-span is still volume. Their color is the color of things that have long since stopped needing to be seen. They take the drop when it comes, hold it, convert it with a thrift so exact it feels like courtesy.

For a long time that seems enough. The chamber is lush the way a closed fist is lush: complete, unwilling to waste a cubic centimeter, decomposing its own fallen tissue so quietly the blackening never becomes event. Microbial films do the unseen work, unhurried, the only motion that never quite stops. Nothing pulses. Nothing startles. The silence is not empty; it is metabolized.

Then the remnants begin to read differently. The panel in the north wall is not merely warmer; it is the place where the chamber’s interior weather is still negotiated. Water that touches it is metered, sheared into films too even for chance, drawn inward as if the stone itself were thirsty in a more organized way. The alloy ring half-buried near the narrow end is no longer an object the roots have claimed. The roots have become its vascular tissue; the ring is a valve that has forgotten it was ever separate, a slow sphincter of metal and living thread that still, on some calendar too long for breath, adjusts flow or charge or the phase of ice. The regular depressions in the ceiling are not scars. They are the flattened upper surface of the same organism, leaves that have grown into the negative space the way bone grows into a poorly set fracture and then calls the new shape finished.

What lives here is therefore not a tenant. It is the pocket’s own continuance given a body large enough to fill every available contour. Stone is its cooler skeleton, the old poured floor its calcified memory of an earlier rigidity, the vegetation its only remaining soft tissue. The artifacts are organs that outlasted the distinction between made and grown. Energy arrives as residual planetary heat a few meters overhead, as the latent work of phase-change, as whatever quiet conversion still occurs behind the panel’s unmarked face. The budget that should not close does close, because the inhabitant has had time to become the equation rather than solve it.

It has no center that could be cut out. Awareness, if the word still applies, is distributed the way damp is distributed: everywhere in thin films, nowhere as a single bright point. It registers the rare creak of ice as a change in its own skin. It registers a visitor’s blood-heat as a brief, localized insult that the cold immediately begins to correct. It does not hunt, does not flee, does not wait for light or spring or permission. Its sole laws are the ones already written into the room: fill the volume, waste nothing, treat cold as climate rather than wound, refuse the pulse. A new frond is not birth; it is a redistribution that takes as long as the ceiling needs to bead and release a few hundred times. A dying stem is not death; it is substrate being returned to the common felt.

Stay long enough and the darkness stops being something the inhabitant endures. The darkness is the medium it secretes, the same way it secretes the faint mineral-sweetness and the padded quiet. Above, seasons turn without reaching. Here the only weather is the slow internal weather of a life that finished arriving ages ago and has since only deepened its occupancy. It does not require an explanation. It occupies. It layers. It takes the drop. It holds.
