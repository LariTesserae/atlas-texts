The lives here do not arrive as arrivals. They accrete, the way moisture finds every plane and then decides, slowly, to become a thread.

At first there is only the sense that the emptiness is occupied by occupancy itself. A pathway that was not there a moment ago carries the faint, lingering warmth of something that recognized a need and then withdrew. In the water-pockets the reflections sometimes include a second set of towers, and in those brighter, remembered cities a figure stands at a balustrade that does not exist in this arrangement, watching the same dim sky with an attention that feels older than the rock. The figure is never quite the same twice. Sometimes it has the suggestion of shoulders, of a spine that continues past where a spine should end. Sometimes it is only a density, a place where the bronze light thickens as if deciding to be seen.

When they do appear in the present tense they do so the way the towers repair: without announcement, according to rules that treat visibility as optional. They prefer the mid-level terraces and the interiors whose volumes refuse to match their exteriors. There the air is heavier, almost drinkable, and they stand in it the way the stone stands in its own sweat. Their surfaces—call them skins only for convenience—hold the same slow beads of condensation. The beads do not roll off. They are absorbed, or perhaps they are how these beings metabolize the unused potential of the young rock, the entropy the city has chosen, for now, not to obey. A faint internal pulse travels just beneath the surface, matching the softer glow along the tower walls, as if they and the architecture share a circulatory system that has not yet been told it is two things.

They are tall in the way a colonnade is tall: not a measurement but a continuation. Limbs unfold from joints that do not stay in one count. A step can carry them across a terrace and also, without contradiction, along a corridor that exists only inside the step. Their faces, when the dimness consents to resolve them, have the quality of wet slate that has learned to look back. Eyes or their equivalents drink the gloaming rather than reflect it. They do not blink; the membrane simply thickens and thins, processing whatever faint luminescence the plants and the city itself offer. Mouths, if they have them, are for the subsonic pressure that lives in bone and sole. Speech, when it occurs, is felt as a change in the thickness of time: a walk that should have taken an hour delivers you later, or earlier, and you understand you have been answered.

They move seldom in the open because the open is not their weather. The basin’s structural emptiness is a courtesy they extend to the still-forming land, or perhaps a necessity; too many of them at once and the rock might finish deciding too quickly, or in the wrong direction. When they must cross the damp earth between grove and tower they do so as rumors of motion. The mosses of drowned velvet close over their traces before the next long afternoon. A single enormous leaf falls, and in the wet collapse you almost glimpse the hem of something that was using the leaf as camouflage, or as a door.

In the isolated jungles other lives keep their own counsel. High in the flying-buttress trunks the jointed shapes shift once, twice, then become canopy again. They are larger than any catalogued animal and assembled according to a different arithmetic of limbs. Plates overlap like the leaves they mimic, but the plates sweat the same deliberate threads of moisture and pulse with the same unused light. Their calls, when they come, are low and wet and seem to originate from several throats at once, or from one throat that occupies more space than the body that houses it. They do not hunt in any way the eye can follow. Growth is their engine too; a tendril that was not there at dusk may be a sensing organ by the next gloaming, tasting the condensation for information the towers have not yet needed. The groves remain discrete because these lives prefer islands. The passage through openness and silence between them is a kind of fasting, or a kind of listening.

Smaller things exist in registers the dim light does not easily show: condensations that briefly cohere into walking droplets, or fungi that photosynthesize the memory of brightness stored in the water-pockets. They are not separate from the larger lives so much as their punctuation, the quiet ticks of an ecology that treats scarcity as a structural principle rather than a lack. Everything here lives by concealment or by the opposite of concealment: by being so thoroughly part of the becoming that there is no edge left to hide behind.

The city itself is one of them, or they are the city’s way of walking around. Foundations that keep going, materials that shift when unobserved, pathways that forget themselves—these are not tools but metabolisms. The inhabitants repair the towers the way the towers repair the inhabitants, treating entropy as a local habit they have, for the moment, chosen to indulge. In certain groves where time pools thicker they sit or stand or simply occupy, and the young rock beneath them grows a little less sharp, as if being used, gently, as nourishment or as conversation.

They have no names that carry in air. Need is recognized, and a way appears. A tremor passes through the basin as something deep finishes deciding its shape, and they pause, all of them at once, in every register, feeling the land become a fraction more itself. Then the condensation resumes its slow threads, the leaves resume their luminous work, and the lives resume the long, unhurried labor of remaining possible in a place that is still rehearsing the idea of having inhabitants at all.

You can walk a very long time and meet no one. You are never outside them.
