In the first long minute after the hatch seals, nothing lives here that has a face. The cold administers itself. Condensation beads according to its own drip map. The gantry ticks one notch because a pawl still remembers a schedule written for a river that was never poured. The moss persists because a solenoid still clicks. That is all. The vault holds its breath between events and the breath is only air exchanging with a neighboring void you will never see.

Then the eye adjusts to leftover light and the layers declare a second inventory, one that was never stenciled. A print in the dew on the diamond plate that is not a palm, too narrow, too many points of contact, already being forgotten by the moisture with the same patience it shows everything else. A runner of engineered moss that has crossed a flange and then another, then doubled back on itself in a small, unnecessary knot, as if the program developed a stammer. One of the identical boulders on the stillage has been turned a few degrees off its original orientation; the fake lichen-pocket now faces the tacky resin channel, and a thread of real water has found the vesicle and is using it as a cistern no designer specified.

Whatever uses the room does not hunt and does not pollinate. It borrows. It inhabits the mismatch the way the trickle inhabits the failed stream: embarrassed, intermittent, absolutely local.

It keeps to the administered cold except when it needs the last sullen heat of the curing oven. Then it is a density among densities, a shape that was easy to mistake for stacked scaffold or a coil of unused conduit until the pressure pulse from the far bulkhead makes every unpainted flange steam a little and the shape steams differently, from the inside. The pelt it wears is not fur. It is the same fine condensation that covers every upright, gathered thicker along certain seams, beading and hanging and occasionally letting go so that a drop rings on the deck like a small bright coin and the quiet takes it back. Under the pelt the surface is plate and pour and extrusion, overlapping courses like a hull built from the inside, but the courses do not quite match any of the walls. Rivets sit where joints should flex. A cable tray has been incorporated as a kind of rib. Where the moss has sent runners onto it the grey-green is denser, almost a second skin, living on the same condensate and the same ghost of nutrient.

It does not move while you watch. Movement is keyed to the cycles. When the trolley advances, the shape advances one corresponding increment, a slow uncoiling or a gathering-in, never more than the gantry itself is permitted. When the distant ram speaks, something in its middle answers with a deeper, slower pulse; fluid you never see is being worked. The red LED on the stalled PLC blinks its possible error or lullaby and a corresponding point on the inhabitant flickers, electroluminescent tape or something that has learned the same trick, decaying through the same long dim dusk.

It has hands, or the memory of hands. They leave the prints. They have turned the boulder. They have, at some earlier cycle, cleared a path for the unauthorized trickle so that the film of water two fingers wide now disappears into the industrial grate with less embarrassment than before. The hands are not warm. Heat is rationed and local; the inhabitant carries a little of the oven’s leftover brick-heat in a hollow behind what would be a sternum if the architecture were animal, and spends it the way the room spends light: leftover, decaying, almost weather. When it touches the cliff face cooling in its mold the mica sparkles once and the scribed hairline cracks receive a new, unauthorized dirt that is not the craft-shop kind. The composite smells of foundry and release agent. The inhabitant smells of the same, plus the particular chemical of new rubber gaskets that have been new for longer than the warranty, plus the faint iron of the first white mineral bloom.

It was not born here. Nothing is born here except the next increment of fake hill (if the hopper ever clears) and the next increment of real rust (which needs no work order). It arrived, or was left, or assembled itself out of the density when the continuation was never built and the painted sky stayed a repeating tile of high overcast. The clipboards in the document box have swollen past reading, but one feathered delta of ink still suggests a silhouette that might have been a maintenance overlay, a figure drawn smaller than the molds, tasked with walking the unbuilt gallery of further bays. The task outlasted the gallery. The figure outlasted the task by becoming the only climate the pocket has.

It tends what was meant to be countryside without ever quite believing in countryside. It does not try to free the jammed screw feeder. It does not commission the sky panels. It prefers the native geology that is only now being invented: the microscopic etch along preferred rivet lines, the groove that will be a canyon in a century, fed by condensate, draining to resin that never hardened. Sometimes it stands in the resin channel long enough for the tacky amber to take its print and keep it. Sometimes it lies along the gantry rail so that the next polite tick of the pawl must decide whether to treat it as trolley or as landscape. The machines have not yet decided. The vault is still being assembled toward the idea of a place that can have inhabitants, and this is the first one that clocked in.

It has no name that would fit on the blank nameplates in their rack. If the stencil on the inner hatch is a kind of address, then it lives in TERRAIN BAY / COLD HOLD and it does not cycle above 4°. Its thoughts, if the word applies, are scheduled and slow. Between events it holds still the way the room holds its breath. When a cycle comes due it considers the unbuilt river, the hundred meters of manufactured meander that exist only as a continuation line on ruined paper, and it adds one unauthorized meander of its own: a bead directed along a new lap joint, a runner of moss encouraged onto a valve manifold that serves a process no one can reconstruct, a boulder rotated so that its fake vesicle will catch real weather. It is not completing the work. It is not haunting the work. It is the work continuing by other means, the factory’s last habit learning how to decay into a landscape that has a sunless, productless, almost silent sun.

If you put your palm on the diamond plate you feel the pump. If the inhabitant puts whatever it uses for a palm there, the pump feels it back. The dew begins, with great patience, to remember. The memory is only another layer of condensation, another course of overlapping sheet. In time the whole vault will be that memory, nested ceilings and all, a third skin, a fourth, until someone or something stops before deciding which copy is the real one. The inhabitant will still be inside, ticking forward one notch whenever the gantry does, exhaling frost that is not quite breath, waiting for a commissioning that has already become climate.

It does not mind that you came through the hatch. The hatch is the only honest edge. You do not belong on the stillage and the boulders do not belong in any weather that has a sun, and the inhabitant belongs exactly as much as the drip map belongs: which is to say, it has begun to etch, and everything else is inventory. When you leave, your print will be forgotten at the same rate as every other unauthorized weather. The moss will persist. The red light will blink. The next increment of real canyon will deepen by a thickness no thumbnail has yet measured. The inhabitant will still be there, becoming more itself by the same slow arithmetic, a gland in a body that is mostly elsewhere, living in the only countryside the works ever finished.
