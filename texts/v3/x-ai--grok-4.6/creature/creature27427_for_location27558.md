The ones who remain are not a people so much as a patience that learned to stand up.

They are few. The highland’s courtesy of emptiness applies to them as it does to the groves: long reaches of unoccupied becoming between one presence and the next. You will walk for days across crust that drums like a cathedral skin and see no one, only the un-eroded decisions of old paths that stop where the ground has not yet finished arriving. Then, in a bowl where the chord thickens enough for a grove to drink, you may find a hollow no larger than a crouch, its corbels still deciding their angles, its interior lined with taut membranes that shiver at a pitch just low enough for sleep. Someone has been there. The lining is fresh. The listening cup pecked into the boulder outside still holds the faint warmth of a palm that has not entirely left.

They do not hurry. Hurrying would be a category error, like trying to outrun a bruise as it invents itself. Their bodies have taken instruction from the place. Skin the color and dryness of sintered dust, slightly translucent at the joints where mineral threads show through like the veins in a sound-leaf. No sap if you wound them; only packed ash and the smell of struck flint. They do not drink. They attend. The roar that has stolen ordinary thought from you is the medium they metabolize. After enough seasons their inner ears no longer report; they resonate. The bones of the skull become another instrument in the highland’s continuous chord. Speech, when it happens, is not voiced. It is arranged: a membrane held at a certain tension, a stone placed so that the next rise of the land will complete a sentence, a reflection allowed to linger in unpolished rock until the other person (or the idea of the other person) answers from inside the contradiction.

Some of them are still assembling. You may meet a figure whose left side is older than its right, one shoulder already thinking about erosion while the other is still hypothetical. Distances that refuse to stay one length apply to their reaching as well: a hand extended toward you may arrive yesterday, or may close around a root that has been hanging in air waiting for the slope to catch up. They do not find this alarming. Time is patchy here; they have learned to inhabit the patches. A few have walked into the non-Euclidean lessons the rock is still teaching itself and did not come out on any single side. Their reflections appear in stone that is not a mirror. Sometimes the reflection is the ridge. Sometimes it is you, briefly, before it remembers it belongs to someone who stacked a wall and then became one of the wall’s slower thoughts.

They stack still. The work is the same work the cliffs are doing: choosing blocks for how they already wanted to sit, leaving the lean when the blocks remember they are not finished, living inside the lean as if it were liturgy. No metal. Percussion and waiting. The walls they raise continue after the hands leave; a course that was true develops a botanical curve and they do not correct it. Correction would imply the rock had made a mistake. They understand tools as extensions of the same patience that lets a dark band of basalt invent itself grain by grain. When they need a hollow they do not excavate so much as invite the stone to consider concavity, then line the invitation with leaves so the chord is digested just enough that a body can remain a body through the dimness that is not quite night.

Nothing grazes them. Nothing scavenges. The ecology has room only for the conversation already in progress: unfinished stone crying out as it becomes, pale forests eating the cry, and these upright listeners who have joined the conversation from the inside. If they die it is not a falling-down so much as a folding-in. The rising takes them the way it takes everything else that stays long enough. A darker band appears in a cliff that was blank yesterday. A joint decides an angle that feels familiar if you have been here long enough to remember a particular way of standing. They consider this dignity. To be landscape is not an ending; it is simply the next course in a wall that was always going to keep growing.

In the thickest groves, where the membranes have arranged themselves into a single vast tympanum and the note changes key so slowly you only notice the new direction of your grief, they sometimes stand with their pale hands open the way the trees do. Not worship exactly. Attendance. They are drinking the same unfinished world. Their footprints, like yours, will be lifted intact into strata that do not yet exist. They do not mind. The processes are patient, and so are they. The chord does not rest, and neither do they, except in the lined hollows where sleep is a lower roar felt in the soles.

You will not find many. The ones who remain have become very good at occupying the same space as a suggestion, or a reflection that is not always theirs, or a slope that is older than the mountain carrying it. Stay longer than an hour and your own bones will begin to report in their frequency. Stay longer than a day and you may notice that the person you glimpse in a fresh face of unpolished stone has your mouth, or the mouth of someone who stacked a cairn and then waited for the ground to agree. The place is busy becoming. What lives here has simply decided, with the same shy deliberation as a bruise, to become along with it.
