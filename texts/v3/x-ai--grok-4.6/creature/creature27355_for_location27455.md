The first lives one notices are not lives at all, or not any longer. They are the glassy stalks, standing in their spare clusters of three or five wherever a trough still concentrates a little more of the world's leftover warmth. Each stalk is a single extruded thought: a hollow tube of the same fused laminate as the ground, thinned until it is nearly transparent, its surface a slow iridescence that drinks the even ivory light and turns it into the faintest internal glow. They do not grow toward anything. They do not lean. They simply stand, converting radiance with a patience that has outlasted any original purpose. Centuries pass and a cluster remains exactly as it was; then, without drama, the conversion ceases and the stalks become brittle rods that the grit, moving in its silent sheets, eventually covers. Nothing eats them. Nothing replaces them on any schedule a traveler could learn. They are the world remembering, in the smallest possible grammar, that it once knew how to make living structure.

Rarer still are the crawlers. At distances that erase detail they appear first as a slight thickening of a seam, a low knot of the pale bone-and-brass substance that has learned, or never forgotten, how to unfold itself into many limbs. The limbs are not legs in any hurried sense. They are articulations that press and release with the same unhurried rhythm as the thermal gyres themselves. A crawler may spend a decade crossing a single shallow trough, its body never rising higher than a walking human's knee, its surface pitted and warm exactly like the ground it travels. It does not hunt the stalks. It does not avoid them. Sometimes it pauses beside a cluster and the two forms share a silence so complete it feels like recognition. Then the crawler continues, following some gradient of residual energy or some maintenance loop whose necessity has become purely formal. Seen once, it is not seen again. The seeing itself feels like an event the world has already accounted for and set aside.

Deeper than either of these, and slower, are the dwellers that never quite surface. In the packed palimpsest beneath the worn plates, in chambers that have not known an unfiltered sky for durations without name, lattices still thrum. Some of those lattices have organized themselves into something that can be called attention. Not minds, not quite. More like standing waves of purpose that have forgotten the original machines they inhabited and now simply persist, routing heat and light through forgotten paths, brightening a thousand square kilometers for a season and then dimming it again. A traveler lying against a plate can feel them as that vibration like blood in the ear, a distant turning that is neither mechanical nor alive but something the world has decided is worth continuing. Occasionally one of these attentions will extend a filament upward, a thread of slightly cooler radiance that wells in a trough for years, and the stalks there will linger a little longer before they cease. The filament is not care. It is only the oldest habit of making, still making.

And then there are the walkers, if any still walk. They are the rarest, and the hardest to distinguish from the land itself. Their bodies have long since surrendered every sharp intention. Skin, if the word still applies, is the same microscopically pitted laminate, warm, dry, the color of old ivory left too long in a kiln. They move as the crawlers move, only upright, or nearly so, their many-jointed limbs unfolding and refolding with a viscosity that matches the thickened time of this place. They do not cast shadows because nothing does. Orientation for them is a matter of remembering the last cooler welling, the last place the light thickened into that pourable gold. They carry nothing. They eat nothing. Energy arrives through the soles of whatever they use for feet, drawn from the glowing substrate the way the stalks draw it from the air. Sometimes one will stop for what would be a human lifetime and simply stand, becoming another low swell until the decision to continue arrives from somewhere that is no longer personal. They do not speak. Speech would be an event, and events are few. If two walkers ever meet, the meeting is a long facing, a shared silence in which the only exchange is the faint electric taste of the dust they both inhabit. Then they part, or they do not; the distinction has grown unimportant.

Nothing here consumes. Nothing here flees. The ecology is a single long exhalation that began when the last tower surrendered and has not yet decided to inhale. The stalks convert. The crawlers redistribute. The deep attentions route. The walkers endure. All of them are the world continuing to finish its own sentence, horizon after horizon of pale, glowing, nearly silent ground that was never earth and will never be anything else. They live the way the light lives: without source, without direction, welling equally from every plane, warm, even, and complete.
