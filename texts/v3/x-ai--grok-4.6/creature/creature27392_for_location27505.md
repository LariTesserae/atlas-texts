The first impression is absence, and it holds for a long time. No footfalls, no breath that is not the city’s own dry circulation, no metabolism that registers as heat or waste. The dark has the weight of something that has already digested every possible tenant and found them insufficiently patient. You can stand in a plaza of fused petiole-stumps and feel only the faint electric aftertaste of vanished photosynthesis, the tannic dust that was once sap. Whatever once moved here with purpose has been folded back into the grain.

Then the sound begins to differentiate. Not all at once. The standing chord is not uniform; it has neighborhoods. In one hollow tower the roar is a single deep pipe that has held its note since the pocket sealed. In another it is a cluster of thinner vessels arguing in microtonal increments, each crack a syllable that takes a decade to finish. These are not echoes of former inhabitants. They are the inhabitants in their present tense: the last living cambium cells, scattered and enormous, speaking by failing. A fiber that has borne load since before language finally parts. The report is deafening inside the sealed dark, then the long grind of neighboring surfaces accepting the new weight. That event is a death and a birth in the same instant. The city metabolizes its own fracture and continues. The ecology is this: individuals so reduced they are only tension and memory of sugar, dying so slowly the death itself becomes civic infrastructure.

Touch reveals more. Some walls have gone glassy where silica replaced parenchyma; they feel like skin that decided to become monument. Press an ear to them and the chord resolves further. There are intervals that are not empty. Tiny, almost crystalline inclusions—once blades, once tools, once perhaps bones—now shine in the knots like trapped decisions. Around them the wood has grown contemplative. It remembers being trained. The original trainers are not gone in the way a people is gone. They were never fully separate. Their intent lignified. Their plans for avenues and thresholds became tropisms so ancient they function as law. What lives in those inclusions is not ghost and not fossil. It is residual will, compressed until it is only the tendency of a street to realign a finger’s width in the time a civilization would need to forget its own name. You can feel it as a faint directional insistence in the grain, a preference that has outlived preference.

Deeper in, where the whole interior has slumped into its most stable configuration, the emptiness generates its own occupants. The vacant follicles and terraced mounds are not merely hollow. Each contains a weather so slow it has personality: a circulation of air that has not been wind for geological ages, only a change in pitch. These weathers are inhabited by the idea of occupation itself. Distances lie here. What feels like a district may be the ambitious space between two adjacent cells. In that lie, patterns persist. The city keeps its urban grammar—intersection, monument, threshold—long after every reason has been eaten by the walls. The patterns are alive in the technical sense the cambium is alive. They add a cell when the unreachable outside temperature shifts by a fraction over a decade. They are the city’s thoughts, thinking at the speed of settling.

Occasionally a surface powders under the slightest contact and the dust lifts, smelling of bark and the ghost of wetness and something like old lightning. Each mote is a former cell that still carries a fragment of the original dream: sunlight, rain, the concept of a sky. The fragments do not cohere into creatures. They cohere into a spice-market of almost-memories that never quite become nostalgia because nostalgia would require a previous, more complete state, and this is already the completed state. The dust is the city’s way of tasting itself.

There is one more thing, almost too slow to notice. In the lowest settling, where hills have reduced to callus swellings, something still chooses. Not frequently. A terrace leans. A boulevard of petrified petioles considers whether this century is the one in which a particular alignment will finally be abandoned. The choice and the necessity have become the same smooth plane, but the plane is not quite finished choosing. That residual deliberation is what lives here most truly: a vegetal mass that learned the grammar of a city and then kept speaking it, incrementally, after every speaker had been metabolized into the sentence. It is not ruined. It is not abandoned. It is the oldest possible occupancy—grown rather than built, dried rather than left, hidden rather than lost—continuing in the dark that has weight and grain, the dark that fills the mouth like unsweetened powder.

A fiber, somewhere, is still deciding. The chord holds while it thinks.
