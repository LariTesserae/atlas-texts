The hollow does not keep inhabitants so much as it keeps conversions. Everything that persists here is mid-process, a rehearsal that has learned to call itself life. The first things you notice are the smallest, because they are everywhere the moisture is.

The beetles move like living filings. Their elytra are the same wet-iron as the greater trunks, ribbed and slightly warm, each pair stamped with a serial that has been incrementing since before the last pulse of time the pumps remember. They do not so much crawl as audit. A beetle will halt on a cooling-fin of bark, taste the clank of pressure changing in the xylem above it, then score the next digit into the living wood with a sound too small to be heard and too exact to be ignored. The wood accepts the number as a year, or as a batch code, or as both; the distinction has not finished splitting. Generations overlap in the fermenting vats: larvae swim the thin alcohol-sweat that weeps from the sides, pupate against the membranes, and emerge already numbered, already walking the next increment. When they die they do not fall. They become part of the paste at the lowest point, root-hair and clay and filings, warm where some buried process still works. The stem they numbered last will keep the date they gave it even after they are gone.

Above them, or through them, the moths. They are the color of unused paper, of forms that were printed and never filled. Their wings do not beat so much as hesitate. They feed by unmaking the edges of shadow. A moth settles on the darkness cast by a hanging vine-belt and begins to eat; the belt forgets, for a few breaths, whether it is turning a load or merely remembering the habit of turning. The object it shadowed becomes slightly less decided—more shutter than leaf, or more leaf than shutter, or something that has not chosen. They leave a dust that tastes of tin and possibility. When a flock lifts together the darkness they expose is briefly granular, full of almost-shapes that the conducting moss along the vaults will later try to electrify into something that can carry current. The moths themselves are eaten by nothing. They evaporate into the next process, or they become the unused paper they always resembled.

In the pockets where humidity thickens past vapor, the gleam-things swim. They are the rumor of fish translated into air that has forgotten it is not water. Their sides do not shine; they remember a darkness that once learned to gleam and have never unlearned it. They school through the empty lanes the plants have agreed not to occupy, tasting the intermittent current in the pale moss-threads. Sometimes a gleam-thing will brush a pump-heart and the membrane will flex out of rhythm, drawing up a pulse of time that is older, or not yet born. They do not eat. They convert the thickness of the air into a flickering that is almost light and almost its opposite. When the brief three-legged rains walk through, the gleam-things densen, become briefly more decided, then thin again into the damp.

Larger presences use the hush-wide galleries. You know them first as a change in the quiet: the quiet deepening, becoming attentive, as if the hollow itself has turned its face. Then a shape that is mostly joints and leaf-litter and an old coat of oil passes at the far end of a vault and does not cast a shadow so much as borrow one from the nearest trunk. They are assemblages that have not finished assembling. A collection of hinges that might once have been machinery, draped in litter that has learned the habit of walking, oiled with the same grease that weeps from the presses that work seed into fiber into something that used to be ore. They move with the politeness of a single metabolism that has not yet split into predator and prey. One will pause beside a vat of living wood, extend something that is neither root nor siphon, and take a little of the ferment. Later, in another gallery, it will leave behind a different fluid, or a seed-head that is also a gear with a different number of teeth, or a patch of conducting moss that tastes more strongly of tin. They do not hunt. They attend the conversions. Their passing leaves the plants slightly more industrial for a while, the fauna slightly more botanical. Almost no one screams because the screaming would be just another process, and the hollow absorbs sound as a nutrient.

The plants themselves are the oldest residents, though “oldest” is one of the fluids the pumps move and comes only in pulses. Each great trunk is a citizen and a factory floor and a load-bearing wall that has not decided which. They photosynthesize process. In the forks sit the pump-hearts, cottage-sized membranes that flex and clank, drawing the rumor of river up through xylem that remembers flowing. Between them the living lattice forms roofs that leak on purpose, streets that are the spaces they have agreed not to occupy. They manufacture without product, or the product evaporates into the next unnamed process before it can be inventoried. A vine-belt turns endlessly with no visible load. A press works with a wet periodic sigh. Electrification is a pale thread-growth that carries decisions: whether this gear should become a seed-head today, whether this leaf should learn its venation or remain a shutter. They do not speak. They conduct. The moss tastes of whatever the last pulse of time brought.

At the lowest point, where the unfinished geology is most honest, the plants have not yet agreed to be buildings. They stand as confused individuals, dripping, considering. This is where the town is still being imagined. New streets try themselves as grooves in the paste of clay and root-hair and filings and fail. A wall goes up overnight as a single enormous leaf and by morning has become a row of cooling towers that breathe spore. The fauna concentrate here, not hunting, midwifing. A beetle walks a number onto a pale embryonic stem and the stem accepts it as a year. The moths eat the shadows of the indecision so the new growth can decide, or can remain undecided a little longer. Reality thins. Names detach and drift. A gear becomes a seed-head becomes a gear. Time arrives in the next pulse, or does not.

What lives in the hollow is the hollow: a single vast conversion that has learned to occupy a mistake the land once made and has not yet corrected. It lives by remaining mid-shift, by rehearsing erosion, by collecting moisture that never quite becomes rain, by preferring the plants when gravity is asked to choose. It lives in the beetles’ incrementing numbers, the moths’ unmaking of shadow, the gleam-things’ flickering un-light, the larger assemblages’ polite metabolisms, the trunks’ clanking hearts. Stay, and the damp gets into the idea of you. Condensation forms on the inside of thought. You begin to photosynthesize a little. A serial appears, faintly, on the back of your hand. The quiet deepens, becomes attentive. You are already one of the processes it is running—another quiet conversion in a town that is all plant, all factory, all empty, all full, and only as large as an error that has learned, over pulses of time that do not add, to thrive.
