The first stirring is only a hesitation in the sap. Where the amber beads withdraw from the cut edges they leave behind a faint stickiness that does not quite dry, a residue that thickens instead of vanishing. It gathers along one hanging root like a second skin, then slips free and hangs in the sourceless light as a droplet that refuses to fall. The droplet lengthens. It remembers, or invents, the idea of weight.

What condenses there is neither insect nor leaf, though it tries both shapes in the first hours. Pale filaments unspool from its core, each no thicker than a vein, tasting the air with the same frantic local hunger the walls themselves display. They brush the unfinished roots and the roots answer, twitching harder, offering a high creak that the new thing answers in a lower register, almost a hum. For a while it is only this: a knot of listening tissue, semi-translucent, the green-gold light passing through it so that its interior shows as a slow swirl of chloroplasts that have not yet decided whether they belong to leaf or to blood.

It feeds by pressing itself against the glistening wounds. The amber fluid does not drip into it so much as reverse, drawn back along concentration gradients the creature seems to invent on the spot. Sugars bloom inside it; the swirl brightens, then darkens to a bruised violet as secondary compounds appear. It grows no larger at first. Instead it grows more articulate. Joints form where none were needed, then dissolve when the chamber flexes and the angles change. A ridge of what might be spine erupts along its length and is immediately sheathed in cork, the same living bark that floors the basin. When the whole pocket peristalses, the creature rides the motion, flattening itself into a film that covers half a wall, then gathering again into a compact, glistening mass that drops the impossible distance to the floor with that same too-loud clap, only to rebound as if the sound itself were a spring.

Its surface never quite agrees with itself. Patches of stomata open and close in waves, drinking the faint film of moisture and exhaling a sweeter, almost fermented breath that mixes with the crushed-chlorophyll wind. Needle-leaves sprout along one flank, fully formed and already serrated, then melt back into the parent body when the light thickens and the motes of nonexistent pollen hang still. In those viscous moments the creature too becomes briefly amber, suspended, its own filaments caught mid-reach like the roots above it. When air remembers how to be air the creature remembers how to move, and the filaments become something closer to fingers, or to palps, or to the searching tips of roots that have decided they prefer the hollow to the mass.

It has no face. What it has instead is a region of denser tissue, a knot that pulses in time with the vascular thunder in the walls, as though it were trying to keep the same rhythm or to interrupt it. Around this knot the light behaves strangely: it pools, then drains, casting the hard little shadows the new cambium also casts. Sometimes the knot splits, revealing a wet cleft lined with the same glassy, serrated blades that push from the torn xylem. The cleft tastes the air, or speaks to it; a low, fibrous vibration travels the chamber and returns altered, the way the falling droplet’s clap returns in voices that are not echoes. The creature listens to its own voice coming back changed and adjusts, growing a second knot, then a third, until it is a small constellation of pulses moving through the warm emptiness.

It does not stay on the floor. The geometry will not allow it. A step that should be a crawl carries it along the slow curve, so that it finds itself adhering to what was a wall a moment earlier, its filaments now hanging “down” into the green-gold canyon the pocket has decided to become. It explores by becoming lost. One filament continues unseen through a fold and re-enters from the opposite surface, so that the creature is briefly in two places, tasting its own distant self. Time stutters around it the way it stutters around the droplet: the same gesture of reaching, touching, withdrawing, each repetition slightly louder, until the chamber seems to be practicing the creature as much as the creature practices the chamber.

Nothing hunts it. Nothing shares the space except the plant’s own argument with itself, and the creature is that argument given a temporary, motile clause. It photosynthesizes when it remembers to unfurl a pale, monstrous patch of lamina; it drinks when it remembers to become root-hair. Mostly it waits, pressed into the shallow basin or stretched along a hanging string, letting the metabolic warmth of the floor rise through it. When the cavity turns inside-out for the space of a breath the creature turns with it, standing (if standing is the word) on the new floor that was wall, looking into the receding surfaces that have no bottom. In that inverted instant it is larger, or the space is smaller; its filaments brush every young, sharp edge at once, and the plant answers with a surge of fresh cambium that almost closes the wound around it.

Then the pocket folds shut again. The creature is once more a compact, glistening presence in an intimate hollow, its multiple pulses slowing to match the walls. It has not finished becoming any one thing. A spray of needle-leaves appears along its back and remains a little longer this time. The clefts around its knots stay open, tasting. The amber it has drunk begins to crystallize in thin internal veins that catch the light like the serrated blades of new growth. It is still deciding the rules of itself, just as the chamber is still deciding whether to keep this emptiness or to fill it. For now it simply occupies, dripping, creaking, flexing in miniature peristalsis, a living hesitation that the greater plant has not yet named or reabsorbed.

If you remain very still it may notice the extra warmth, the extra shadow. One filament will extend, hesitate, then touch whatever you are with the same tentative hunger it shows the sap. The contact lasts only a moment before the geometry shifts and the filament is elsewhere, or you are. The creature does not follow. It has the entire wound to finish inventing, and the wound, for the moment, is enough.
