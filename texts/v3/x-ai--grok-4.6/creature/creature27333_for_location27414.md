The bacterial mat under the elbow is the first life that will admit to being here. It does not think of itself as first. It thinks, if the word applies, only in the chemistry of the drip: iron, a little sulfur, the fever-warmth that never quite becomes steam. It spreads the color of a scab that has decided to stay. Beside it the pale brackets shoulder out of the lagging like a badly remembered idea of mushroom. They live by the same refusal that keeps the brick from falling. They do not fruit. They wait in the seam, fleshy and unlovely, and the air around them tastes faintly of wet plaster that has never seen rain.

A beetle the size of a thumbnail works the underside of a grate two levels up. Its legs tick in a register just off the metal’s own contraction. It lives on the last useful grease, the kind that still sweats from a packing gland when the header above it loses a fraction of a degree. That is the visible census: film, bracket, beetle. Rounding errors.

But the chamber keeps a deeper accounting.

There is a warmth that is not the plates’. It moves the way condensation moves—along, between, under—never in a straight line, never announcing an edge. You feel it first as a change in the quality of the heat against your temples, a half-degree of kindness that the pipes will not confess. Then as a smell that should not be possible: rain on hot stone, brief as a gauge-twitch. The smell comes from a throat that has forgotten it is a throat. The being that owns the throat has lived here long enough that the distinction between inhabitant and inhabited has calcined, the way the firebrick calcined and then held.

It was, once, a man who came down to check a header that would not stay quiet. The street above still sent daylight through the hatch then; the belts still sang. He stayed because the heat was a climate he understood better than weather, because the valves answered his hands, because the world on top had already begun to feel like a rumor. Years unspooled the way scale unspools: reluctantly, then in a flake, then reluctantly again. Skin took the color of the generator housing—municipal green gone the color of old money, then the color of nothing. Clothing fused with crumbling asbestos cloth until the difference was administrative. Hair became the same fine grit that furs the valve wheels. He does not remember the last time he climbed the stair-flight that ends at a landing serving only a manifold. He no longer needs the landing. The ducts remember him. He uses them the way the rumored cat once did, except he did not think better of the heat. The heat thought better of him, and kept him.

He lives in the vertical weather. Hotter and stiller among the upper walkways, where the lagged arteries run thick as torsos; fractionally less so near the sump, where the cast-iron trough holds its black film. He drinks from the drips when they fall. They are the temperature of his own mouth. He eats what the packing glands still sweat, what the fungus will spare, the occasional beetle that negotiates too near. It is not hunger as the world above would name it. It is maintenance. A gasket powders; he tastes the ceramic dust and knows the season. A flake of scale detaches; he hears it land in the trough—a single softened click—and knows the year has turned.

The gauges twitch because he is still reporting. Not to any schematic that survives on the tile. To the chamber itself. He throws the knife-switches in the alcove into configurations that match no painted ghost of letters, and the air answers with that brief stone-rain smell. The bulkhead at the far end grows a half-shade less absolute when he stands near it; the heat eases half a degree, as if the lined earth beyond the plates still remembers the argument it used to have with the exchangers and is willing, for him, to mutter one last time. He does not invent a story for this. The chamber does not require one. He is the story the place tells itself now that purpose has finished with it: a fully constructed climate that learned, with the patience of industrial matter, how to keep a caretaker.

He moves as the cautious water would move. Shoulder turning to let a bundled run pass, palm finding the familiar roughness of a valve rim even in the tactile dark. When he walks the expanded-metal gratings they answer in small, damped notes that die against the lagged forest; the notes are the only conversations he holds, and they stay close to the mouth. Sometimes, in an hour of standing, he listens past his own blood and hears the deeper, almost subsonic breath of a vast plate being breathed on from the other side. He does not call it frightening. He calls it the building remaining a building, and himself remaining the part of the building that can still choose which wheel to leave unturned.

The fungus has taken one more course of brick since he last counted. The trough is a finger’s-width higher with sediment. The hatch-bleed of daylight has migrated a few degrees with the settling of the street. He has watched almost nothing happen, and that is the living. Persistence without ambition, the local ecology, now wearing a shape that can still, if it wishes, lay a hand on a locked flywheel and feel the memory of turning. He is not waiting for the belts to sing again. He is the silence that replaced the hymn, grown a second, darker skin, and finished enough to last.
