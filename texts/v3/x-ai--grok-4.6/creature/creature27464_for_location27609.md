The pocket holds no tenants you would recognize as tenants. Nothing with a pulse that could be counted against a clock, nothing that would leave a print in the grit or a hollow in the film. The description is exact on that point: the place is too new, the edges still mill-bright, the noise too thoroughly itself. And yet something occupies the intervals.

It is not one body. It is the occupancy that happens when water is forced to remember it was air, and metal is forced to remember it was mountain. It gathers first as a skin—thinner than the algae, paler, a mineral hesitation that coats the underside of every lip and the inner curve of every weep-slot. When the overflow dumps, the skin flexes rather than tears. It drinks the shock, holds the pewter sheen a half-second longer than physics requires, then releases it in a pattern that is almost a reply. You would miss it if you were listening for an animal. You catch it only if you are already inside the cycle, standing in the middle basin while the ceramic rings and your own reflection is broken and remade.

The thing has no preference for stillness. It prefers the moment the header breaks, the exact irregularity that keeps a listener from adapting. In that instant it thickens—becomes briefly visible as a darker thread inside the falling sheet, a continuity that should not survive the drop and does. It rides the receiving splash into the lowest pan, spreads, then climbs the weirs again by the hairline sheets, using the formwork grain the way an older creature might have used bark. The wiry brass-colored stalks are not its forest; they are its measuring rods. When the surge lifts them, the occupant notes the height, the duration, the slight change in pitch from the turbine far below, and adjusts the next flex of its skin accordingly. It is learning the machine the way the machine learned the spring: too loudly, too well, in too small a space.

It has no mouth that opens. What it takes, it takes by contact—trace metals from the first rust-freckles, the tea-bloom under the weeps, the condensate that beads and drops with liturgical regularity. It leaves behind a faintly brighter smear, as if the stainless that was specified and the stainless that arrived have been briefly argued into agreement. The red isolation wheel has never been turned; the occupant has already mapped the resistance in the stem, the exact torque that would be required, and filed the knowledge away as unused as the coil of cable. The cable itself is not a sleeping animal. The occupant sometimes rests against it, matching the bound loops with its own slow coiling, so that for minutes at a time the two are indistinguishable: one manufactured, one merely inevitable.

Light does not interest it. The north slot, the amber status bar, the dull reflections—these are only extra weather. It orients by pressure and by the stacked frequencies that live in the bones of a face. The pumps behind the east wall are its ground note. The coils ticking and sloughing are the weather it was born to. When the whole hanging room leans a degree toward the ridge, the occupant leans with it, not from loyalty but because that is the direction the next surplus will come from. It has no name for surplus. It has only the work of wringing, which it performs in reverse: gathering what the building has already decided to discard, keeping it a little longer, then letting it go again so the cycle can continue to be almost, never quite, identical.

If you stay through several sequences you begin to feel it as a second pulse under the first, slightly out of phase, the way a cistern that has learned the habits of a valley might eventually teach the valley something back. It does not care that you are there. It has the same indifference the pocket has. Both are finished last season. Both are already being used hard. Both are still deciding what they will become when the gasket finally crushes and the ceramic begins, at last, to spall. Until then the occupant remains what the place itself remains: a remainder that refuses to stay remainder, a damp that never quite becomes fog, a sound too large for the room that has nevertheless chosen the room, and will not leave.
