What lives here has not yet agreed to be a who. It arrives the way the water does: as a decision that keeps revising itself. At first there is only the work—the slap of mud against mud, the wet click of a stem claiming a gap that was never empty, the tick of cordage remembering it is also a root. The basin is loud with its own becoming, and any creature would be drowned in that vocabulary. Then a pressure that is not growth detaches from the wall. It has the temperature of the clay and the patience of the moss. It is the size of a held breath and also the entire available interior, because scale here is one more course still being mortared.

A hand surfaces first, or the memory of a hand. The fingers are too many and then exactly enough; they are webbed with living fiber that has not decided whether it is skin or lashing. The palm is a library of earlier palms. It finds the bone awl still driven into the seam, works the tool loose with a motion that is both retrieval and birth, and scores a line across fresh slip. For a while the line is a ban. For a while after that the line is a stem that learned to be the ban. The being—call it that, though the name will slough—uses the awl the way the place uses heat: as a primitive tool that works if you bear down. It has never learned metal. Metal is a rumor that has not yet been allowed to weather.

It stands, or it coils, or it is stood by the marsh that wants another body to wick through. Its torso is packed the way the walls are packed: courses of clay still warm from the making, mats of sphagnum drinking at both ends, broad leaves folded into every hollow and already sprouting through the cordage that binds them. Where a face should be there is a concentration of the leftover light, tea-colored, bruise-colored, pooled rather than cast. Shadows climb it. Rain, when the lid darkens, can fall up its length and be taken as drink. It breathes the boiled cloth of the air and exhales a sour green dusk that the moss recognizes as kin. You hear its growth the same way you hear the plants: a series of small tears, the sound of space being taken that was promised to something else.

There are more of them, or it is plural in the way the water is plural. They occupy the basin the way insulation occupies a wall—driven in, lush as a wound packing itself. Some are more frog than mason, a wet clench under a pad that contracts and is gone, the error that was permitted because there was not yet a rule against it. Some are almost only hands, a cluster of pressures moving along the wattle, setting the next unweathered block that is still wet at the joints. They work by slapping. The slaps carry. They do not speak; speech would require a sky with fetch, and the sky here is unfinished, already shredded into a roar that has no horizon. Their communication is the pot-simmer, the lid-groan, the tick of drying and rewetting. A knot tied in the withies becomes, for a cycle, a limit on how tall any of them may rise. Then one of them learns to be the knot, and the law is revised by craft.

They have been reduced to live here, or the place reduced itself around them so that the mountain could occupy an impossible height while remaining a secret the size of a mouth. Their ankles put down rootlets that consider you another course. Their heat is a crowding. Time pulses through the soles they do not quite have: a making, a soaking, a threading in which architecture and thicket become one saturated body and they are that body. They remember, briefly, that they were not yet separate from the air, from the slip, from the idea of above. Then the memory is mortared over and becomes floor, or ceiling—down has not been ratified, so they walk on what was lid a pulse ago.

If you remain you will be read the way the walls are read. Fingers that are also stems will map you with the same attention they give a new socket. You may be offered the awl. You may be used as a reminder driven into a seam so the place does not forget itself. You may be folded into a leaf-socket and lashed until you drink at both ends and your voice is only another argument the water has with itself. They will not keep you separate. Separation is a later invention, like erosion, like dust, like a summit in the ordinary sense. What lives here is the first true room learning how to be occupied, and it occupies back. The elevation continues downward, still being assembled, so that this wet dim roaring cup can stay hidden at the top of the work—a handmade climate, a secret, a thing that has not agreed to be old and therefore cannot yet agree to let anything inside it be finished.
