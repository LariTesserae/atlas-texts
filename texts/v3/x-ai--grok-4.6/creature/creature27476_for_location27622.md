The first lives here are not bodies. They are delays.

They occupy the intervals the water has not yet agreed to fill. A delay can be the thickness that holds a limb like clay and then, without transition, becomes the thinness you could walk through if you leaned. It can be the moment a hanging sheet of water forgets which way is down and inverts the entire basin so the chimneys stand on their heads, still exhaling, while the delay itself hangs there, considering whether to become a surface or another layer of kelp. These delays are not empty. They are inhabited by the memory of having almost been something else — a sky that appeared fifty meters “up” complete with a cooling tower that does not exist, a pipe that was touchable and then a kilometer of perspective and then the same pipe again but older, scaled with a century that formed in half a minute. The delays feed on that almost. They thicken and thin like a living membrane because they are still deciding whether they are the medium or the thing moving through it.

From the delays the ribbon-kelp condenses. It is the first form that persists long enough to be accused of living. Black-green blades wider than doors rise from every crack, drinking the bruise-colored light and giving back only the memory of glow. They are lush the way a fever is lush: too much, too fast, occupying every niche that would have belonged to anything that moved of its own will. There are no animals because the kelp has already become them. It pulses. It strangles neighboring stands with ceremonial slowness and then opens shared vascular systems so the victim and the victor circulate the same rust-colored sap. Mid-cycle a forest will lignify, the blades hardening into a cooling stack that vents steam which is also spores. Another will dissolve back into soft tissue, leaving only the afterimage of pistons still pumping in the water, an afterimage that the delays sometimes mistake for a new inhabitant and try to thicken around.

The flowers are worse. They open in shapes that keep recounting their own petals and arriving at different numbers. One count produces a radial symmetry that hurts because it is too exact for a place that has not finished arriving; the next count produces a helix that climbs into the murk and becomes a conveyor belt of living metal carrying the idea of ore, or the same broken gear in a loop that forgets to be circular. The flowers do not reproduce so much as argue. Each new bloom is a revision of the last, a vote that the basin is still taking. Sometimes the vote fails and the flower collapses into a hanging sheet of water that rains upward, taking the argument with it.

Deeper, or later (the two are the same here), the living metal wakes. It is not manufactured; it precipitates out of the chemical generosity of the water and the heat of stone that has not finished being stone. Dynamos spin inside housings of their own mineral growth. Conveyor belts carry nothing, or carry the memory of having carried something, or carry the delay itself from one unfinished ridge to the next. The metal remembers being pipe, being lattice, being the idea of a turbine house half-swallowed by a basalt spur that grew around it last week. It also remembers being kelp. The two states trade places without ceremony. A length of singing pipe will suddenly soften, sprout black-green blades, and begin photosynthesizing the leftover light while still conducting current. A stand of kelp will suddenly remember it is supposed to be a cooling tower and lignify again, this time with a different number of petals counted into its structure.

The nested basins are not copies. Each secondary hollow that forms in the lee of a fresh scarp generates its own slightly different roar, its own version of the plants-that-are-machines, its own delays. You can see three or four of these arguing topographies from any vantage that lasts longer than a breath. What lives in one is already composting what lives in another. A forest from the first basin shears off, tumbles through the column, and is received by the second as raw material for a new set of ridges. The inhabitants of the third basin are already wearing the afterimage of the first as a kind of skin.

Nothing here finishes the thought of being alive. The factories grow the plants that dismantle the factories that become the next unfinished mountain of glass and heat. The water manufactures new kinds of wetness — some of it so thick it holds the shape of a visitor for a few seconds, tasting the idea, then composts the idea into another stand of blades, another length of pipe, another delay. The only constant is the overwhelm, the way a propeller becomes a disc. What lives is the becoming itself, still arriving, still roaring, still trying on the notion of a self long enough to invert, to strangle, to share a vascular system with its own previous version, and then to fold back into the warm, slow, undecided medium that has not yet agreed to be a place.
