The plants are only the first and loudest answer. They occupy the wet corners and the hanging loops with a kind of vegetal insistence, but they are not alone. Beneath the moss cushions, in the powdery grit that the heat has sealed into a faint crust, thread-like nematodes move at the pace of mineral weathering. Their bodies are the exact gray-white of basalt dust; they do not wriggle so much as lengthen and shorten, drawing faint, temporary grooves that the next rare drop erases. They feed on the stewed rot the mosses release, converting it into more of themselves with an efficiency that has no surplus, no waste heat of their own.

In the shallow basins the scum is not only mineral. Thin rafts of thermophilic bacteria form and dissolve, their colonies appearing as iridescent skins that catch the phosphorescence of the moss and return it as a duller, oil-slick green. When a drop finally falls, the rafts rupture and re-form around the impact, never quite the same pattern twice. These are the true inhabitants of the water, the ones for whom the cistern-heat is not endurance but home.

Deeper still, inside the cracked plinth and the pitted cylinder, something else persists. A film of iron-oxidizing archaea lines the inner surfaces, feeding on the slow bleed of rust. They are the reason the metal still feels faintly warm to a palm; they are the ghost of the pump that no one told to rest. Their work is the vibration you feel in the floor, translated into chemistry rather than motion.

And then there is the one that walks.

It does not walk often. For seasons that have no names it remains folded into the thicket of arborescent ferns, its outline indistinguishable from the buttressed trunks and the curtains of aerial roots. Skin the color and texture of new-torn basalt, filmed always with the same trembling beads the walls carry. When it does move it does so with the deliberation of the vines themselves, testing each foothold against the talus as though the stone might still remember being sharp enough to cut. Its limbs are longer than they first appear, jointed in ways that suggest both the old catwalk grating and the looping cables that once ran here. It drinks from the basins with a patience that matches the falling drops, and it touches the last living gauge with a fingertip that leaves no print, only a brief extra warmth.

It has no name it still uses. The air it exhales is the same viscous medium it inhales; breathing is simply another form of remaining. Sometimes, when the hairline crack admits its gray suggestion of day, the being stands beneath it and lets the thread of warmer water run across its shoulders, as if remembering a different weather. Then it returns to the ferns. Roots have begun to follow the lines of its usual paths, and the condensation on the newest fractures sometimes sketches the same routes it takes, as though the chamber itself is learning the shape of its only remaining witness.

It is not a caretaker and not a prisoner. It simply continues, the way the heat continues, the way the last filament sometimes remembers how to glow. In the long intervals when nothing else moves, you might mistake it for another machine the earth forgot to turn off. Only the faint, irregular rhythm of its breath—slower than the drops, older than the rust—marks it as something that chose, once, to stay.
