The first thing that lives here is the absence. Not a lack, not a wound, but a decision so complete it no longer remembers being made. Nothing with a mouth that chews has ever lasted. Nothing with wings that beat or legs that push has kept its outline. The niche did not fill; the niche became surface. What might once have been a beetle is now a gland that weeps a thicker mist. What might have been a bird is now a translucent panel shunting the last exhausted photon another meter deeper. The old architectures of hunger and flight were simply outcompeted by area itself. So the first inhabitant is this: the fullness that left no room for anything else.

Then the ground lives. It lives the way a pulse lives, not as an object but as an event that keeps happening. You stand and it yields like wet cork, firms, then gives a single deep adjustment as some vascular bundle the width of a weather front redistributes pressure. That is not soil supporting life. That is cambium deciding, for this hour, to be floor. Beneath it more cambium, and beneath that the cool compressed dark of xylem that has already forgotten it was ever soft. The roots did not dig; they precipitated, sweating mineral films that have not finished choosing to be stone. Even the not-yet-rock is inhabited. Already the next roots are inside it, tasting the chemistry of their own future skeleton. The highland lives downward as much as it lives up. It is secreting its own bones while it is still thinking with its leaves.

The air lives next, though “air” is the wrong word. There is almost none of the empty kind. Density here is occupation. Stems flattened into load-bearing walls, petioles braided into cables whose tensile math is older than any engineer’s, leaves stacked into ceilings that became further floors that became further ceilings. Every gap that might have been passage is already a younger generation growing in the perpetual damp. You do not walk; you are granted a temporary vacancy that the forest closes behind you the way living tissue closes around something it has chosen to keep. The mist is not weather. The mist is the agreed-upon body of the place, a standing lung that inhales vapor and exhales vapor and never quite rains and never quite clears. Droplets the size of fists well from hydathodes you could crawl inside. They run in sheets, collect in cisterns that are themselves hollowed stems, then climb again on a transpiration engine so vast it owns its own barometric name. Water is not a feature the plants use. Water is the medium they have consented to be.

Light lives only as remainder. Whatever sun the world has is already spent by the time it arrives, filtered through kilometers of chlorenchyma and a sky that is mostly the forest’s own breath. The result is a green-black dimness that is neither day nor night, a canopy that has no top because the canopy kept becoming more canopy. Photons are hoarded. Some leaves have gone nearly black to catch the last of them. Some have turned into living fiber, shunting what little remains deeper still. You see by residual glow, by the faint chemiluminescence of tissue working past any reasonable limit, by the occasional pale bloom that opens only long enough to dump heat like a brief, private fever and then shuts. That bloom is as close as the place comes to a facial expression.

And then the sound lives, which is to say the thought lives. There is no silence because there is no unused process. Cavitation in xylem rivers pops like continuous artillery. Billions of stomata hiss. Growth itself is a low wet tearing that never finishes. Water striking water in the dark. The packed mass conducts everything; a rupture a hundred kilometers off arrives as a change in the pressure of your teeth. Below hearing there is a register you feel in the sternum, the slow planetary movement of sugar and signal. This is not noise. This is the highland thinking out loud. Phloem is bus. Electrical potentials move not as simple impulse but as coordinated, error-correcting fronts that adjust growth, rewrite local weather, deposit the next film of proto-mineral, even tug at gravity wells so slight you notice only when a hanging vine hangs a few degrees off the true it has calculated. The forest is assembling itself according to a program that already includes the bedrock it has not finished inventing. It is embryonic on purpose. Erosion has barely started because the plants will not permit waste: every loosened grain is recaptured in mucilage and laid down as the next course of stone that is still learning how to be stone. The contractor is the building. The building is the thought.

What lives here, then, is not a who in any face you would recognize and not a what you could point to and be done. It is the occupation deciding, continuously, to remain occupation. It lives as weather that is biological: a bloom-front a thousand kilometers wide opening in the same hour, the evaporative drop pulling a cold front out of nothing, mist becoming rain becoming mist again in a closed cycle the size of a nation. It lives as the green tide still arriving at theoretical margins, climbing, rooting, lifting, filling, so that behind it the world is already finished being empty. It lives in the quantum-coherent excitons of antenna complexes that should not stay coherent this long, this wet, this cold, and do anyway. It has made the impossible feel ordinary: of course the ground is alive, of course the sky is another leaf, of course the roar never stops. You stop noticing the contradiction the way you stop noticing your own blood.

If you stay, a tendril tastes the salt on your skin and files it. A leaf turns a fraction to steal the heat of your throat. Water finds you and weeps down you as it weeps down everything else. Not hostile. Just complete. The vacancy that was granted begins to close. You are becoming data, becoming gradient, becoming another slight pressure in the cambium the size of a weather system. Far below, something that is not yet granite takes another micron of shape, secreted, cool, packed, and already full of roots that are already thinking the thoughts of the plant that is making it.

This is what lives here. It has no proper name because it has not yet needed an edge. It is still deciding, in the only way it knows how to decide: by growing until the decision and the body are the same thing.
