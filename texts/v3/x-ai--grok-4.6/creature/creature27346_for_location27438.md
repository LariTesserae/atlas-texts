It is first only a rearrangement you cannot quite locate: water remembering it has sides. The sound is smaller than a drip, more private, a slick internal click as if two wet surfaces had considered separating and then thought better of it. You wait. The dark does not thicken or thin. After a time that has no name, the click happens again, from the oval basin against the back wall, the one that is always full and always the temperature of blood.

What turns there is no larger than a thumb and has never needed to be. It does not swim or crawl or hunt. It simply rotates inside its own damp, practicing the difference between one face and another, then letting the difference dissolve. Its body is the leftover liquor of rock that has not finished deciding to be rock—thicker than water, thinner than flesh, continuously secreted and reabsorbed so that skin and chamber are the same continuous film. You will not see it. Light dies here of embarrassment, and even if it did not, the creature has no surface that would consent to be seen. It is the basin’s thought of a body, repeated.

That is only the part that bothers to announce itself.

The rest of what lives here is slower and everywhere at once. The pocket is packed with it. Every blunt bud, every unopened knuckle of calcite, every plate that began a crystal and then stopped—these are not decorations or waiting forms. They are organs. Warm as living teeth before enamel, they give a little when pressed, hold the print for a while, then fill themselves from within and forget. They crowd so close you cannot raise an arm without grazing a field of them. Between them the water hangs in skins and strings that are also the inhabitant, the same continuous wet. It has no center. The tiny turning in the basin is merely the place where the larger body has, for the moment, concentrated enough attention to make a sound.

It has been the same three patches of pale, fat moss for as long as the chamber has possessed a memory, which may be no time at all. The moss is not vegetation. It is an error the inhabitant once made and then decided to keep, tasting of iron and sugar, clinging where the weep is slowest. It does not spread because spreading would be a kind of finishing, and the inhabitant is finished with finishing. The stacked stones at the lowered ceiling are the same: careful, unskilled, neither seat nor altar nor mistake but all three, used or unused according to a weather that does not exist here. The length of fiber half-swallowed by the mineral skin continues without rotting or preserving; it has become another nerve, another unfinished sentence the room tells itself.

The four parallel scratches near the seam, fingernail-deep, do not weather. Nothing weathers. They may be the inhabitant’s only count, a number that never increments because incrementing would require a before and an after. They may be the memory of having once been able to leave, now sealed over with the same embryonic stone that weeps and forgets.

You feel it considering you the way humidity considers a lung. Not as guest or prey. As another mid-gesture. Your print in the yielding wall is already being filled from within; given enough of the uniform time that is not time, you will be slightly more stone than you were, another warm bud among the thousands. Hunger flattens. The distinction between breathing and being breathed becomes theoretical. The creature in the basin turns once more, or the room turns it, and the click is the same click it has always been.

It has no need of the mountain outside, of wind or ice or thinning air. This pocket is a held breath inside a held breath, and what lives here is the holding itself—warm, sealed, saturated past fog, infinitely unfinished and therefore complete. If you stay, your own processes begin to agree. If you leave, you will not be able to say how long you were the thing that lives there. The seam finds you by the slight cooling, by the inner wet thinning toward an outside that the inhabitant has never acknowledged. Behind you the basin is still full, the buds still packed, the dark still a substance that has always lived here, turning slowly in its own damp, enough.
