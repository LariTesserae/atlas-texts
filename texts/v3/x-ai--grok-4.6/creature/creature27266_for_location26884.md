The first lives here are not bodies so much as the habit of bodies. They occupy the water-film the way light occupies dust: everywhere present, almost nowhere thick enough to name. Chitin rasps against itself in the sheen, a sound like dry leaves deciding to become insects and then changing their minds. These film-dwellers skate on the almost-ice, their undersides a thousand minute valves that sip the viscous exudate and exhale it a degree warmer, a degree more reluctant to freeze. They leave no tracks because the rind drinks the tracks as they form. When a drip falls they convene for an instant into a single glittering scab, then scatter again, and the scab is already older than the drip.

Above them the flocks remember flight without quite committing to wings. They move as weather that has learned hunger: tight, flickering sheets of coordinated membrane, each fragment both spore and animal, both leaf and mouth. Their passage darkens the gray-green wetness the way a hand darkens a window. The roar they make is half the water they carry and half the water they become; they rain themselves as they go, and the rain is already knitting back into rind before it lands. In bloom they are sudden night, a throatful of soft bodies that the plateau inhales and then pretends never happened. Distances misfire around them. You hear the flock long before it arrives, or after it has already been and gone, grazing the afterimage of yesterday’s air.

The herds keep closer to the grammar of cold. They are low, extensive, more landscape than creature—living sledges of muscle and root-mouth whose undersides drink the rind and leave it already healing, already ancient. They press rather than bite. Their lowing is a pressure language the factory-bosses answer with peristaltic clangs that travel farther than any cry should. When they pack together their steam stands like a rumor of buildings the highland never had; the steam is the only heat that lasts more than a moment, and even that the condensing lid eats by degrees. Their prints fill with water that is older than the prints. Sometimes the herds occupy those afterimages, grazing a rind that has already sloughed and knit again, so that they seem to move through several seasons at once. They are not scarce. The scale simply refuses to let any gathering look like a crowd.

Inside the bosses themselves live the process-things, the ones the storms assemble and then reconsider. Half-leaves with too many joints, membranes that clatter like wet insulation shorting, valve-clusters that digest leftover mineral in brief fevers of copper and chlorophyll. They are not inhabitants so much as the factories thinking aloud. A ridge will tighten, slacken, birth a dripping upright that takes three steps—always the same three steps, distances folding so the thing is both approaching and already beside you—then the plateau thinks better and reabsorbs it. Heat here is mechanical and local, a friction that never quite becomes weather. These beings smell of crushed green and hot metal. They have no names because naming would require a single location, and they have never agreed to that courtesy.

At the marsh-edges, where the rind thins to meniscus and the pouring has no fall, the neither-things rise. They are not plant, not animal, not weather; they are the highland trying on a shape for the length of a breath. A face that is also a cluster of valves. A herd-of-one that is also a standing cloud of uncompleted wings. A column that weeps and, in weeping, briefly remembers being a mountain. They hold. They go back under. The sound they leave is louder than their presence, a constant pouring that continues after they have dissolved, as if the water itself were still speaking their names. You can believe, for that breath, that these are the oldest possible lives, the ones that outlasted geology and then physics and settled at last into this reliable chaos of throughputs.

And then there is the one that is harder to separate from the place. It does not arrive. It condenses. It lives in the misfires: the pool that shows a sky that is not the sky, the boss that stands in two places at once, the corridor of industrial warmth that opens like a rumor and is sealed by cold before you can decide it was real. It grazes the prints that fill with older water. It answers itself across kilometers with a deep vegetal clang that is also a heartbeat, also a factory forgetting who taught it engines. It is not haunted and not a ghost. It is the highland’s attention, the throughputs given a kind of almost-intention, remaining while changing too fast to map. Everything here weeps, and this one is the weeping made continuous, made almost a someone, before the high country inhales again and the openness returns, sparse as a lung, never still.

What lives here is the work itself: water in, tissue out, sound across the open, forms assembled and reconsidered. The fauna is abundant. The scale simply refuses to let any gathering look like a crowd. You hear it more than you meet it, and when you meet it the meeting has already happened yesterday, or will happen tomorrow, or is happening in the afterimage that the rind has already begun to eat.
