The first lives are not quite lives. On the remembered-south brick a colorless film holds, neither plant nor decision, only a patience that has learned the mineral taste of the circulating flood. It does not spread; it occupies the same few square meters it has always occupied, thickening by the thickness of a thought every decade. In the intake grates the sessile mouths are more definite: tiny, radial, working the silt the way a sleeper works a blanket. They open and close with the pump-tide, never hurrying, never quite filtering enough to matter. From the rusted jib the single thong of kelp hangs as it has hung since before memory, bladdered, aligned, a length that has forgotten how to grow and so remains. These are the garden’s only citizens. They endure because the water has not yet found a reason to erase them.

A pale crab, thumbnail-small, sometimes walks the vertical face of a boiler. Its legs find the pitting the way fingers find old braille. It does not forage so much as continue a path it began when the metal was warmer. You never see two. The basin’s scarcity is a courtesy they keep.

Deeper, inside the unopened honeycomb of cooling tubes, the longer presence keeps its territory. It is not eel, not worm, not quite the memory of either. Length without hurry, pale as the calcite that beards every porthole, it moves only when a hanging chain lifts and settles. The motion is not hunt and not flight; it is the adjustment of a body that has become another pipe, another flexing sleeve gone stiff. It feeds on whatever the returning water still carries—flour-fine rock, the occasional flake of emulsified oil, the microscopic rain of its own shed skin. You do not meet it because meeting would require a difference the place no longer keeps. The creature and the labyrinth have the same temperature, the same viscosity. They have arranged that.

The suited figures are rarer still, and slower. At first they are only another hung coat, another density the galleries have learned to ignore. The fabric is brick-colored, patched and re-patched with the same precipitate that coats the iron, so that after a time the suit is less worn than grown. A gloved hand rests on a valve-wheel and waits through an entire stroke of the distant pumps, palm listening for the courtesy of metal that still seats true. When the figure moves it chooses a seam the thickness of itself, trailing a faint wake that the cold water drinks before the wake can become a story. There are never many—three, perhaps four, circulating through the residual corridors the way the water itself circulates, lifted a little, cooled, returned. They do not speak. Speech would be vibration, and vibration has been almost abolished. Instead they touch: a palm to another’s shoulder-plate, a brief pressure that travels through the inner ear like the tiny percussion of a check-valve deciding, once again, to be a check-valve.

They tend nothing that needs tending. The engines have already worn themselves to honesty; seats are true, oil the color of weak tea. The figures’ labor is only to be present at the moment a permission arrives, to rest a hand where a hand has always rested, to become, for a few minutes, another temporary limb the town grows and then reabsorbs. Whether they still remember dry floors, or names, or the wind the chimneys were sited for, is a distinction the water does not keep. Their heat is a small problem the basin solves the way it solves every other density: by accepting it, by circulating it, by letting it become another quiet process among the ones that write and erase the same low dune across the old cobbles every forty minutes.

Nothing here finishes. The film thickens by microns. The crab completes another vertical shore. The longer presence adjusts a chain. A suited figure waits, and the pumps decide, and the dark continues its work of never quite leaving. The basin is already almost full. What lives in it lives as the fullness itself—another held breath, complete, unwilling, almost gelled.
