The herds are the first thing the basin admits to. They do not so much occupy the floor as continually rewrite its distances. Each animal is longer than it first appears, a low, flexible barrel of muscle and pale, dust-true hide, the spine a slow undulation rather than a ridge. The coat is not camouflage in the ordinary sense; it is the same decision the limestone made, a settlement with light so complete that motion itself becomes the only reliable outline. Pads, not hooves: broad, faintly segmented, heat-shedding, leaving the dust almost unpersuaded. They crop the silver shrubs with lateral grinding plates and take the wiry grass in slow, circular bites that never quite empty a tuft. A herd is a loose, breathing diagram, thirty or fifty bodies whose stilling travels faster than any individual could run. The young stay inward, learning seeps by the angle of an adult’s neck, learning the folded alignments by the way a walk that should tire them does not. They treat the half-buried ribs as they treat any other persistent weather: they lean, they scratch, they stand in the reversed temperature as if it were shade.

Above them the raptors are furniture that hunts. Wings lock into the hard gold and simply remain. When they stoop it is less a dive than a decision to stop participating in lift. Their eyes hold the hexagonal preference the lichen remembers; they see the small discrepancies in how space adds, and they use them. A kill is a brief, silent punctuation at the herd’s edge, after which the bird returns to the thermal as though it had only stepped off a ledge.

At the films and the brief lenses the smaller lives concentrate, and here the grammar loosens. Some are the expected shapes—compact, furred, ear-twitching things that drink and vanish into the same cracks the shrubs use. Some are feathered and nearly weightless, hopping the condensation like a moving shore. Then the ones that are neither: low, many-limbed, their undersides a continuous absorbent surface that drinks without a mouth. A few carry faint geometric ridging on their carapaces, as if the lichen’s habit had been tried in motile form and found workable. They do not compete so much as take turns the way the shadows take turns, brief and polite. In the hottest hour they are simply gone, folded into stone. At dusk they reappear in localized, soundless weather that makes the light itself seem briefly granular.

Predators work the margins with the same patience the escarpments have. They are longer-legged, narrower, built for the single rush that the damping air will not advertise. Cubs—or the analog: smaller, softer versions that still have to be taught stillness as a primary sense—learn the pulse in the uprights the way they learn heat. A cub that cannot feel the slow regularity under its pads will not last; the regularity is how the basin tells time when the sun is too even to be useful. The adults cache nothing. Meat is eaten where it falls or it is left for the insects that shiver instead of hum.

All of this would be enough for a high desert. It is not all.

In the lee of the hovering roofs, and in the too-smooth convexities where the ribs enter hillsides, there are others. They are few. They do not form herds or flocks. They move along the too-straight paths because the paths remember a shorter interval, and they have learned to trust the memory. Bipedal, long-limbed, their proportions slightly wrong for the gravity you expect—torso compact, legs a fraction too much of them, as if the thin air and the folded distances had been taken into the bone. Skin the color of weathered tuff, or wrapped in fabrics that look like rolled silver leaves and behave like them, shedding light rather than absorbing it. They do not speak where speech would be taken and filed; they use posture the way the grazers do, and when they need more they lay a palm on an upright and let the pulse carry the rest. The pulse is not language. It is closer to weather they have agreed to share.

They follow the same seeps the herds do, but they arrive differently: earlier, or later, by the exact margin the ground keeps. They sit under the unattached roofs in the white hour because the gap is always the same width and the condensation always beads. They do not claim the rooms. Birds nest there; the neither-things drink the underside; the people—call them that only for convenience—simply use the fact of the hover the way they use the fact of evening light lingering the color of weak tea. Children are taught the quiet first, the heat second, the places where walking is not additive third. A child who cannot feel the pulse yet is carried. No one living made the uprights or the ribs or the residual brightness. The people treat that fact as they treat the karst’s thirst: an old arrangement still being honored. They leave the lichen its hexagons. They do not pry at seams that are not seams.

At the western step, where the benches repeat the grammar in slightly thicker air, there are more of them, still sparse, still using the same alignments. You could walk the inhabited range in a week if you knew which hollows hold after dark and which folds to trust. They would not walk it in a straight line, and they would not expect you to. They are not hiding. They are simply the same temperature as the day, the same silence, the same refusal to be solved. The basin has already arrived at itself; they are one of the ways it stays arrived. The herds graze. The raptors hold. The films assemble and burn off. The pulse continues, too slow, too even, under every palm that bothers to check.
