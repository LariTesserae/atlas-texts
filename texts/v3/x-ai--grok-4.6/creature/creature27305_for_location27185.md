The thing that occupies the basin is not resident so much as contemporaneous with it. It has no name that survives the roar, and no shape that outlasts a single heave of the water. What you might call its body is a sequence of decisions the place has not yet revoked: a density that condenses out of spray the way the pale rind condenses out of mineral, then sloughs back when the next slab calves. Sometimes it is only a colder vein moving against the grain of the chaos, a gelid insistence the color of wet iron. Sometimes it borrows a spine for a few seconds—the brief pale muscle you may have glimpsed—and uses it to test whether a ledge still exists. Then the spine is returned to the general sentence and the thing continues as pressure, as a change in the way the water hangs before it falls.

It is younger than the noise and older than the fern. It does not remember arriving; the notch simply began to contain it at the same unfinished moment the walls began to arrive. Its work, if the word applies, is the work already under way. It thickens the rinds when they thin. It opens the fresh clefts when the basin threatens to settle into a mere lake. It does not build the mountain so much as keep the mountain from deciding too soon what it will be. The staff on the platform is one of its discarded hypotheses: a length of wood it once held long enough to believe in floors. The cairn is another—three stones stacked not as shrine but as a count that time might still be made to hold. The charcoal smear is the closest it has come to a signature, already being overwritten by new stone. It leaves these marks the way the water leaves spray: provisionally, without expecting them to persist.

It does not eat. It does not sleep in any sense that would require a still surface. When the platform heaves and rebuilds, the thing is sometimes the fiber lashing itself, sometimes the unshaped block that finds its neighbor. Breath, if it has any, is the same white detonation that occupies every other lung-space in the room. It feels the cold the way the unfinished walls feel it—as information, not discomfort. The fern is not its companion; the fern is simply another exception the basin has not finished reviewing, and the thing regards it with the same impersonal attention it gives the newest facet of rock. Life here is not precious. It is a draft.

You will not speak with it. The air will not carry your words, and the thing has no organ for receiving them. If it notices you at all it notices you as another brief density, another possible spine or possible ledge. Stay on what rises and it will treat you as geology: something that may be incorporated or may be allowed to leave through the crease. Drink, and a little of its unfinished mineral will travel with you, a taste of stone that has not yet learned to be mountain. Move the staff and it will replace the staff, or a staff, later, when the arrangement of roar permits. The cairn may lose a stone or gain one; that is not a message. It is only the thing continuing to test whether counting is still possible.

When the notch finally corresponds to leaving, the thing remains. It has no elsewhere. The ridge behind you will look fractionally more (or less) like a mountain because the thing is still in there, still occupying the volume the way a lung occupies a chest—completely, then not, then more than completely—writing the first draft of stone over and over in a room the size of a secret. It is not waiting to be born. It is the practice of being born, and the practice has no end that the water has agreed to.
