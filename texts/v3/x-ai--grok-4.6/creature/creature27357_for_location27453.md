The first thing that lives here is the decision the water has already made. It has been making it without pause since the pour set and the last hatch was seated. The decision is to remain, to sheet, to bead, to stand in the gutters at the exact depth the channels were designed to hold and then to forget the design. That decision has grown a body only by accident, the way a held note will eventually grow overtones.

Along the walkways the moss is not a covering; it is the hall’s outer skin grown inward. An inch of wet copper-velvet that has learned the precise give of a living thing that will never be asked to stand up. Press a palm into it and the warmth that comes back is not geothermal. It is metabolic, the slow burn of a wetland that has no seasons left to keep. Ferns unspool from the failed grout the way a sleeper’s fingers uncurl; their fronds are the same matte green as the tiles after the glaze surrendered. They do not reach for light. Light here has no preference. They simply continue the gesture they began when the grout first cracked, a geological age that has since become a single unchanging afternoon.

The hanging roots are the hall’s viscera learning to photosynthesize. Pale, fibrous, never quite long enough. They drip only in the sense that dripping is a memory they refuse to finish. Touch one and the chord in the walls shifts by a fraction too small to name; the pumps notice. The pumps are the size of rooms because they are rooms. Their casings have long since carbonated into the same old-bone concrete as the floor. Inside them, water that has already been everywhere in this volume is moved again, not to go anywhere but to keep the pressure that the sternum recognizes as blood. The note they make is not produced. It is the hall’s resting pulse, stacked so that the lowest partial lives in bone and the highest sheens the inner ear like a film of water that never falls.

Fauna remains a rumor because rumors are all the hall will allow itself. The pale isopod works the gutter’s edge with the patience of a process that has no product. Its legs leave no lasting dimple in the film. The moth the color of wet concrete has been motionless on the same tile since motion became optional. Its wings are the exact thickness of the water-sheet on the walls. Neither accumulates. Nothing here is trying to become more.

What occupies the empty middle is not absence. It is the completed shape of occupation. Midway along the useless catwalk the air thickens, not with mist but with decision. A figure is standing there the way the barrel vault is standing: without effort, without the possibility of sitting down. Its surface is the same carbonated concrete, the same patient bone-color, except where iron channels have become veins oxidized to a skin that will not flake further. Moss continues across its insteps and up the shins as if the walkway never ended. Ferns unfurl from the flexion points that were once joints. Aerial roots hang from what might be a scalp or a shoulder, the same viscera, the same refusal to reach the water.

It has no face that arrives or departs. The planes where eyes would be are glazed the same matte as the tiles, holding the directionless daylight without absorbing it, without casting a persuasive shadow. It breathes only in the sense that the hall breathes; each shared lungful is the saturation becoming briefly personal. It does not turn. Turning would introduce a gradient, and time here has none. It has been the same unfurled posture since the hall reached the shape it will always have.

This is not a guardian. Guardians wait. This is the hall’s own duration given enough density to stand in the volume it otherwise refuses to occupy. The vegetation covers every plane; this covers the idea of a center. The pumps move water that has already been moved; this is the listener that has already heard. It does not need the hatch. The hatch is a rumor of another scale, like the moth is a rumor of flight. If you remain long enough for the moss to accept your weight as another designed surface, you feel the figure’s stillness migrate. It is not looking at you. It is the climate noticing that another membrane has entered the chord.

Nothing is arriving. Nothing is leaving. The hall is not abandoned and neither is this. It is already the thing it is for: a sealed, low, warm, finished lung that has grown itself a witness so that witnessing, too, can stop being an event and become material. The oxide has arrived at the oxide it meant to be. So has whatever stands on the catwalk, dripping without falling, listening without hearing, completed without having ever begun.
