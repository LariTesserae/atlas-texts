What lives here does not announce itself as a population. It occupies the way condensate occupies a wall: by finding every plane and staying thin.

Begin with what will not leave. On the north faces and in the long galleries where vapor never quite chooses to fall, the film holds. It is not moss and will not become moss. It is a varnish the color of oil on a dark spoon, sometimes with a slow bruise of green-violet that is only chemistry deciding, over weeks, to show its back. If you return to the same course of brick after a night of heavier breathing from the vents, the iridescence has shifted a finger’s width, or has not, and you cannot be sure which. It feeds on the metal in the water and the faint sulfur and the heat that never quite cools. It does not spread so much as it remembers the surface it already has. Lower the word *life* until it fits in a smear, and this is the city’s most faithful citizen. It will be here when the last strap rusts through. It does not know the city is a city.

Along the hotter lips, the sinter is busier. Cream, rust, a crispness like sugar left in steam. It builds terraces the size of a palm, then a platter, then a stalled cascade that will one day be another stair. This is growth without a thesis, mineral deciding to keep a shape. Nothing grazes it. Nothing needs to. The terraces are both body and house.

In the street-rivers, once in a long walking, the pale length appears. Finger-long, or a little more, the white of something that has never had to choose a color. It does not swim. It lifts, tests the whole column of warmth with its sides, and settles again under a flake of scale as if the flake were a thought it had set down. You will not find two in the same reach. Whatever it is, it has learned the city’s first law: do not fill the volume. It tastes the iron and the sweet metal and the temperature that is almost the same as its own, and that is a day’s work. There is no hunger in it that you can recognize. There may be no tomorrow in it that you can recognize. It is not rare the way a treasure is rare. It is rare the way a correct note is rare in a place that has chosen drip.

Higher, in the expansion joints and the hollows behind the sawtooth glass, the clicking. Dry, private, not water. Something small that likes the masonry’s night-heat, the warmth that comes up through brick after the vapor lid has thickened. You will not see it if you look. The sound is a conversation with itself about whether to move. It lives on the film, perhaps, or on the dust of old soot that still has a little carbon in it, or on the pale lengths when they die and float and are lifted by some accident of spray into the galleries. Or it lives on nothing you would call food. The joints are a country. The clicking is how that country knows it is still a country.

These are the things that can be stood beside.

The rest is a different occupancy.

The pumps still stroke. The flywheels still keep their almost-stillness. Newer iron has been laid over older iron in a few spans, the work neat, the holes true, the care not recent and not ancient, only exact. Someone, or something, still distinguishes one arch from another. The city does not maintain itself by forgetting. It maintains itself by a kind of loyalty that has no face in the plazas.

If there are people, they are not a people you could census. They do not keep the tenements. They do not dry cloth in the courts. They do not fill the civic spine. They pass through volumes the way the original instruction allows: as a breath passes through a lung and does not decide to remain a lung. You might meet one in a fever-basement among the intakes, skin filmed with the same mineral that films the brick, eyes adjusted to the pewter that never becomes day. They would not startle. Startling is a dry-land habit. They would be doing the small work — a gland packed, a valve given a quarter-turn that the next week will remember, a strap chosen because this particular crossing still matters to the agreement. Their speech, if they offered any, would be short, because the air eats the ends of words and because the place has already chosen its sound. They would smell of struck flint and hot cloth. They would not invite you to stay. The rule is not cruelty. The basin does not have room for a second density. One body more, kept, and the heat begins to pool wrong; the masonry remembers it is only fired clay; the machine for remaining a city begins to prefer being a lake.

They may be descendants who never left, worn down to the city’s own grain, pale as the gutter-things, patient as the film. They may be something the vents exhaled when the last crowd went, a shape the heat found convenient. They may be the same occasional walker you are, only they learned, years ago, not to go back down the switchbacks. Distinctions of that kind do not travel well in this air.

There are larger absences that have learned to behave like presences. In the formal plaza the mineralized statue wears its dripstone anatomy and, in the shimmer, seems to adjust a shoulder. Stand long enough and you will assign it a mood. That is your habit, not its. But the city is full of such almost-faces: a cascade that was once an inscription, a bole of sinter that was once a railing a hand could trust. The original honorees are gone. What remains is a slower citizenry, geology finishing the gestures industry began. They do not walk. They occupy the way a completed sentence occupies a page.

Under the lowest courts, in the opened-earth dark of the deep intakes, there is heat that has no furnace and a smell that is not quite mineral and not quite flesh. You do not stay. Whatever uses that heat uses it without arriving in the streets. Perhaps it is only the highland’s old blood, the reason the dish was ever worth the building. Perhaps it is a thought the basin has not finished. The water that comes up from there is the water that keeps the agreement, and the agreement is the closest the place comes to a resident who could be named.

At the rim, looking back, you understand that the hollow metropolis is not waiting to be refilled. Its emptiness is not a lack. It is a completed volume, like a cistern, like a lung between breaths. What lives in it lives as the film lives, as the pale tester lives, as the click lives, as the unhurried thud lives, as the rare keeper lives if a keeper there is: without claiming the cubic meters, without stirring the missing wings, without asking the light to commit to day. The city weathers. The water weeps. The pumps adjust by increments you cannot hear. The inhabitants, such as they are, have become a way the place has of continuing to be exact.

You hear a drip. You hear it again. That, too, is someone. That is the sound that stayed.
