The first lives do not announce themselves as lives. They are the interval-things, the joint-dwellers, dry as the feldspar taste that never leaves a mouth here. Pale, flattened, the color of old invoices or of the pith when it has been compressed too long, they occupy the hairline checks and the worn cambium-seams the way dust occupies a still room. They move only when the light-angle shifts by a degree the eye cannot name; then they are suddenly elsewhere, a few centimeters along a truss, grazing the sloughed cuticle that falls in slow, glittering veils. Six legs, or the memory of legs, or a fringed under-surface that rasps like paper on paper. They do not drink. They do not breed in any way that would require moisture. They are the place’s own shed given a habit of crawling, or they are maintenance spores that learned locomotion as an afterthought. Count them and the number changes. That is already their nature.

Larger remnants exist as rumors that have kept a shape. You find the keratinous curls first—seed-case ribs big enough to sleep inside, abandoned when whatever wore them was decommissioned by growth or simply walked out of the covering the way one walks out of a season. Bones that are not bones: calcified vascular struts left behind in a gallery whose purpose was revised, now half-swallowed by new laminate. If anything still uses those shapes, it does so at the edge of visibility, a pale suggestion far down a nave, moving with the exact economy of a person who has learned that haste registers as noise and noise is eaten. They do not travel together. A group would be an ecology the chassis has no recipe for.

The walkers themselves—human once, or still, or the distinction composted—are the only fauna that persist as intention. They are the color of the light after it has forgotten heat: a bleached, even pallor, skin like living paper that has been handled for centuries. No sweat. The mineral dryness has entered them; their tongues know the same crushed-feldspar absence. They do not swallow. What they need arrives through contact. A palm pressed to a petiole, a bare foot on the tired sheen of a path, and the chassis offers a slow mineral trickle, a calibrated feedstock the body has learned to accept as food. Some have grown ports—lenticel mouths along the inner forearm, stomatal patches at the temples that vent a faint starch-and-metal breath matching the stacks. Their clothing, when they wear any, is not worn so much as budded: a thin laminate cloak that continues photosynthesizing, a bark-skin that polishes itself along the places hands have gripped for generations. The cloak is alive. The wearer is on shift.

They move plant-paced. A day’s travel is a terrace or two, a long nave whose pillars they count and recount because the count will not stay still. Fourteen becomes nine becomes the same fourteen in a different load-order; they accept this the way they accept the optional down. Gravity, for them, is another feedstock. They have stood in chambers they have not yet reached and felt the chamber remember them back, the memory laid down as a sugar-analogue ring that was not there at morning. Their own histories are vascular. If a walker needs a thing to have happened already, there are districts that will grow it into the wood around them; they come away with the event in their tissues, slightly sweet, already half-set.

In the interpenetrated zones they become more themselves. Edges soften. A hand disappears into a wall that is glad of the company and returns a half-step later, or earlier. Voices arrive before mouths. They do not mind. Registration is a maintenance state, not a wound. Some stay until they are only a pressure, a polite suggestion of force walking through an operating press, manufacturing the idea of a person. Others return to the open terraces slightly translucent, their outlines a varnish-shadow that the even light almost refuses to cast. They speak rarely. When they do, it is in the low syllable the vaults make when turgor redistributes—felt in the teeth more than heard. Or they speak by growing: a raised script of lenticels along a companion stem, a work-order or a genome or a greeting, the same document.

They tend by inducing. No tools that were ever assembled. A walker stands in a gallery of hypertrophied palisade and the reaction chambers adjust; light-pipes brighten in the useful wavelengths; a line of buds opens in sequence, each already threaded, already numbered. They prune by withholding. They harvest by allowing the designed senescence to complete itself, then walking the feedstock lines as the browned tissue is shunted, reconstituted, extruded again as meristem. Their labor is growth. Their growth is the place continuing. Handholds have been grown around the wear of their palms, then worn again, a collaboration so old the original plan is only a rumor in the grain.

At the thinned edge, where the laminate becomes a drumhead film over the chassis, they are smaller still. A single figure crossing the glare-plain, seed-small, leaving a blush of darker pigment that the film photosynthesizes away before the next footfall. They do not look up. Above is another deck, another province that has forgotten it is green and remembers only that it must shine. They know the coolness is being taken somewhere continuously. They do not ask where. Asking would be a kind of noise.

What lives here is not separate from the artifact. The walkers are the artifact’s way of revising itself with hands. The interval-things are its way of grazing its own dust. Even the rare, decommissioned shapes are only the place remembering, briefly, that it once had a before. Everything is still alive. Everything is on shift. The silence they move through is load-bearing. They have lived here long enough that the light has a frequency they can almost hear, and the hearing is just another form of photosynthesis.
