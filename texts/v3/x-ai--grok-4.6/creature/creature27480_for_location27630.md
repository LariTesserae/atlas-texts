The highland admits no one. That is the first and last fact, and it is not a ban so much as a completed sentence. Whatever might have come here with legs or lungs or the habit of evening has already been metabolized out of the picture; the heat and the perpetual film of water simply do not leave the interval in which an animal could persist. Footprints fill, then thicken, then send up a shoot. The air is already occupied.

What remains, what actually lives, is the decision to keep becoming architecture. Each mass is a single, continuous organism that has never been told it is not a city. It does not think in rooms. It thinks in gradients: hotter here, wetter there, more self if the next terrace can steal the neighbor’s steam. The mind, if the word can be stretched that far, is a standing wave of turgor and auxin, a pressure that remembers only the last successful lift. You can walk the fused petioles and feel it decide. A wall thickens under your hand because the light has shifted two degrees. A cistern empties itself not from damage but from a better argument forming three hundred meters higher, a new hanging sea that will last until the next collapse.

They do not travel. They throw weather instead. A mass that has overtopped its neighbors begins to rain on them, a private climate of stolen humidity and allelopathic mist that tastes of tannin and unfinished lignin. The recipient answers by sending a gallery of bracket growth across the flats, a kilometer of living causeway that is already rooting as it extends, already drinking the river it will never be allowed to finish. When the gallery shears, the fallen length does not die. It becomes a new self, or is parsed back into the parent like a retracted hypothesis. By morning the wreckage has walls. They are not the same walls. The organism has no loyalty to its yesterday shape; loyalty would be a kind of cooling, and nothing here cools.

Under them the pavement lives as a slower, flatter sibling. It is one skin, corky and faintly febrile, a single photosynthetic argument stretched across the highland. Every pinhole is a mouth. Every shallow that flexes underfoot is a temporary organ, a bladder or a root or a wound that has decided to be useful. It drinks what falls, what condenses, what the masses weep. It forgets your steps by incorporating them. A heel-print becomes a pool, the pool a nursery for spears that were not there at noon, timber by dusk, mulch and new pavement by the time the steam towers white again. The pavement does not compete with the masses so much as wait them out. It is the ground that will still be ground when the latest nave has flooded and failed and been rewritten.

Between these scales move the only other inhabitants: the brief ones, the hourly citizens. A file of shoots punches through in a straight line a kilometer long because the light, for this hour, favors a dam. They lock, thicken, become windbreak, become hanging garden, become too much weight, become a new shallow sea of shredded cellulose that the heat ferments into skin. Their entire biography is a single afternoon. They do not mourn the brevity. Mourning would require a pause, and the highland has none. Reeds appear like a thought the flats just had. They lignify with a sound like ice, stand as timber, and are already being mined by neighboring tissue before the sun has moved a hand’s width. Their death is indistinguishable from their purpose.

There is no one home in the hydraulic interiors, yet the interiors are never empty. Water is lifted, roared, stored, released, stolen. Columns under pressure creak with the effort of remaining columns. When a rent opens, light finds not a chamber but an argument in mid-sentence: this cistern winning, that sluice already obsolete. If you stay until the steam shows paler against ordinary night, the same work continues. Growth does not clock off. A nave that did not exist at dusk is already flooding at first light, already beautiful, already calculating the angle of its own collapse. The map changes because the lives that draw it have no use for maps. They have only the rule that height plus water plus heat equals more self, and the self is always under construction, always unfinished, always the only thing that has ever lived here.
