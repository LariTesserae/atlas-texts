The hollow holds no creature that would answer to the ordinary names of living things. Nothing walks its clay, nothing feeds, nothing turns its head toward the even lid of sky. Yet something remains, and has remained so long that the distinction between remaining and being the place itself has worn as smooth as the fitted stones.

It occupies no single spot. At first it seems only a thickening of the gray light where the broken rectangle of wall once tried to make a corner, a density that the eye accepts as shadow until it remembers there are no shadows here. Then it is the faint give of the soil under a foot that has not quite landed, the coolness that rises as if the clay itself has been waiting to be noticed. It is the interval between the falling beads of condensation: not the tap, but the silence that already knows the tap will come and has room for it.

When it gathers enough to be looked at, it has the shape of something that once sat and then forgot to rise. Knees, perhaps, drawn close; a spine that follows the same tired lean as the upright slab; a head that has lowered until it is only another weathered plane among the stones. Its surface is the pale leather of the basin floor, pressed and re-pressed, faintly damp. Lichen the color of dried bone has mapped it in the same slow continents that cover the north faces of the rocks; the lichen does not distinguish. Narrow stems rise through what might once have been fingers or the hem of a garment, and the stems are no taller, no greener, than those that rise anywhere else.

It does not breathe. The mild, unmoving warmth of the air is the only respiration it requires, shared equally with the clay and the still pans of water. It does not watch, because watching would imply a difference between itself and the low circle of sky, and that difference finished weathering away before the last right angle softened. What it contains is the completed memory of the hands that set the stones: not images, not names, only the faint knowledge of edges that were once sharper, of a purpose that once interrupted the basin and then became indistinguishable from it. The memory has the same texture as the moisture in the soil: always present, never standing, never gone.

Sometimes, at intervals longer than seasons that do not arrive, the clay settles a fraction further and the figure settles with it, a single almost inaudible sigh that is already accounted for. The pans do not ripple. The stems do not sway. The light remains the same thinned gray. The hollow continues being exactly itself, and what lives there continues being the hollow, patient, finished, without objection or notice, as though the idea of another country where things still change were only a rumor the stones once overheard and then forgot to keep.
