The hollow does not keep inhabitants so much as it keeps arguments that have learned to walk, to fly, to pack themselves into temporary densities and then forget the packing. Nothing here has finished deciding what it is; the rock itself is still choosing its joints, and the things that live in it inherit that indecision. They are warm because the place is warm, because warmth is the only reliable weather, and because whatever first chemistry woke in the seeps has not yet invented the luxury of going cold.

Begin with the smallest and most patient: the films. They are not colonies so much as decisions that have not yet been revoked. Colorless felts drink the newest moisture and thicken until a terrace edge looks sueded, then twitch once, a single coordinated ripple, and go still again. Sometimes the twitch is hunger. Sometimes it is only the stone beneath them settling a fraction and the film choosing, for that instant, to be muscle. Veils of the same stuff—thinner, almost translucent—stretch between pillars that were not there at first light. They catch mineral dust and the fine dark silt of dead fliers and turn both into more veil. You can watch a veil write itself across a fresh face in the time it takes a condensation thread to decide it is rain. Then a new ridge lifts, the veil tears with a sound like wet paper, and the pieces crawl or are carried and begin again. They taste of iron and the memory of rain. They are the hollow’s first grammar.

The mud-eaters come next, or perhaps they have always been arriving. They pull themselves out of cooling slurry like fists that have decided to have wrists. Each is a compact, glistening knot of segments, the color of the mud they are made from and are unmaking. They eat by applying themselves: the underside rasps, the body ticks—an audible, metronomic conversion of sediment into more ticking body. They do not travel far. A ledge that did not exist an hour ago will, by mid-afternoon, be stippled with them, each one mining its own birthplace. When two meet they do not contest; they briefly fuse along one flank, share the ticking, then separate larger. Their waste is a finer, warmer mud that the films prefer. In places the eaters have processed so thoroughly that the terrace itself feels softer, almost respiratory, and later arrivals—whatever they are—sink to the ankle and must be pulled free.

Above them the volume is never empty. The fliers occupy it the way smoke occupies a burned room: completely, restlessly, with sudden compressions that look like anger. Their wings are a compromise the light has not finished judging—neither feather nor skin nor chitin, but a dull, slightly greasy membrane that flashes the color of old silver when the central column catches them. They turn in knots so tight the flock becomes a single dark muscle flexing against itself. The call is one note, struck and struck again a half-beat late by ten thousand throats, so the air between the terraces rings like a rail after a hammer. They nest in cavities that are still widening. Whole generations hatch into a space already becoming corridor or chimney or nothing. Their dead sift down as a dark flour that later stone will lift and call soil, though nothing green will ever claim it. When a new pillar punches through a flock they part without complaint and are already calling from the far side, as if the pillar had always been there and they had merely forgotten.

Lower, in the damp runs and the newest sags, the braided ones move. They are longer than any single intention, rivers of warm muscle that climb one another, vanish into holes born that morning, emerge from holes that will close by dusk. Individually they are difficult to see; the eye keeps sliding off into the current they make together. A body will surface—slick, segmented or perhaps only folded, the color of wet basalt—then be overwritten by three more. When two such rivers meet they do not fight. They reorganize: a sudden local weather of limbs and wet percussion, a brief storm of reallocation, and then they are going elsewhere, or they are one animal that has remembered a different shape. They pack a fresh depression so densely that later traffic walks across their backs and does not notice the difference. The ones underneath do not die. They wait, metabolizing the weight, until the sag lifts again and they spill outward like a tide that had only been delayed. Everything here is a little too warm. When you stand near them the air itself feels occupied.

And then there are the hands.

They are never the center. They leave rings of stacked plates already half-drowned by seeps, the plates pried from walls with something that scored parallel scratches. They leave lean-tos of cooling slag whose interiors smell of grease that is not the ordinary fauna’s. They leave grooves in salt too regular for claw, too shallow for language—just the record of someone testing whether the new rock would keep an edge. Once, a split cobble with a single purposeful flake gone, already being climbed by film. Whoever makes these things does not stay. Or they stay the way everything else stays: as another moving density, using the hour the hollow discards a thing in the hour it discards it. You catch glimpses that refuse to resolve—upright postures that might be posture, a flash of something that is not wing and not braid, a brief darkening in a lean-to that could be bodies or only the memory of bodies. They are primitive the way a first thought is primitive. They do not build a village because the floor will not keep one. They do not cut a path because the path is already becoming a ridge or a river or a flock. They are simply one more way the hollow has of arguing with itself, briefly, with tools that will be film by evening.

All of it is loud the way a hatchery is loud, or a market that has been folded underground, or a weather system that has not yet been told it is finished. The rock cracks its new joints. The fliers tear silk. The eaters tick. The braids slap and slither. Water is never scenery; it is a mood that beads, runs, is inhaled, returns as steam the color of remembered daylight. A terrace sheds its entire condensation at once and for ten breaths there is rain that has never seen sky. A temporary river appears, runs white, is swallowed. The films write and erase faster than rot. The fliers hatch into vanishing rooms. The braids become landscape and then remember they are not.

You can stand on a vantage that dawn did not contain and look across steam-lines, pale evaporite scars, dark moving seams, the far wall climbing toward the bright lip of the world that has not fallen, and feel that the hollow is still inventing its own occupants. They are the density. They are the loudness. They are most of what the place is doing: eating the unfinished mineral of their home, becoming it, un-becoming it, becoming it again, wet-handed, swarming, not yet a fauna so much as the overcrowded argument that will, if the rock ever cools enough to keep a shape, someday be called one. The old surface watches. Down here nothing looks up. There is too much still to decide.
