The first of them are not the ones who leave marks. They are the volume itself given appetite. You cannot number them because number implies an outside from which to count, and there is none. A fist-sized beetle with a carapace the color of a kiln-fired shard will stop on a leaf-knife, taste the silica dust with palps that click like two dry sticks, and then the leaf will simply be gone, folded into the beetle’s interior chemistry without wetness or waste. Its wings, when it lifts, rasp a note that is already part of the basin’s single voice. Beside it a moth the span of two hands hangs motionless; its mouth is a rasp, its body a folded husk that never knew nectar. It does not fly so much as unlatch and drift, adding its own dry frequency to the roar. The pale runners appear and vanish in the same breath: four limbs, then six, then a sudden curl and they are wheels of sparse fur and too many teeth, rolling across a newly shouldered ridge of unbarked matter, tasting it, packing it, becoming part of the substrate they consume. They graze everything. Distinction is a surface habit they never acquired. A larva works a crack in fresh rock the way a tongue works a tooth; a flyer stitches the air above a slump; something long and jointed, bark-tongued, samples the mineral rumor and decides, in a motion too slow and too complete to be called hunger, that the rumor is now itself.

They have made other arrangements than blood. Their interiors are oils, terpenes, a pale sap that gums on contact with air and hardens into a second skeleton. Heat does not kill them; it is the medium they were poured into. Eyes, when they have them, are not for dark. Some surfaces are nothing but eye, faceted and dry, reading the parchment light as a map of chemical gradients. Others have none. For those the roar is sight, taste, kinship. Frequencies that curdle the air are, to them, the shape of a nearby stand deciding to become a cliff, or the precise location of a seed-case about to detonate, or the low furnace-sound of fiber stacking itself into the next hill. They do not hunt. They occupy. A cubic arm’s-length without a body, a wing, a jointed limb, a throat is a theoretical space the basin has not yet bothered to fill.

Then there are the ones who stand, sometimes, and leave the marks.

They are not visitors either, though the stones and the platforms and the half-eaten faces might let you think so. They emerge from the same closed chemistry, the same restless mineral arriving from below. Their skin is the color of the light: old paper, dry, faintly luminous in the veins as if they too had swallowed lamps and forgotten the habit of flicker. It sheds in plates, like the older trunks, and they grow back through the shed as if trying to become stone and failing, and trying again. Joints are too many or too few; a shoulder will unfold an extra plane of chitin-lace when the ground heaves, then fold it away when the heave passes. They do not sweat. They do not drink. What moves inside them is the same thick sap, the same private solvents. When they cut themselves—on a leaf-knife, on the black glass they strike from certain nodules—the wound gums and becomes a scab you could strike a spark from. They use that spark. Not for warmth; the kiln-heat is already everywhere. For the rare, brief language of flame against a seed-case, or to harden a lashing, or to mark a living pillar with a mouth that might be singing before the new growth eats the mouth.

They cannot stay. That is not a tragedy they recognize. A shelter lashed against a fluted, hollow trunk (it rings when they strike it, a note they answer with their own dry throats) will be inside the trunk by the time the membrane overhead has flexed twice. They take this as grammar, not insult. They move as the basin moves: a dry avalanche of their own making, platforms abandoned mid-weave, scrapers of volcanic-memory glass left where a butte decided to walk. Their tools are what can be broken, split, knotted, endured. Fiber so tough it sings in the lurching wind. Stems that are load-bearing ideas. The black glass that might still remember fire from a time the cavern was not yet a cavern. They work quickly, because the land will not wait, and the work itself is another layer of the noise—the clack of nut-hard flowers they have gathered for percussion, the rasp of scraper on glass, the low groan of a new lashing drawn tight.

They hear the second sound, the quieter furnace under the roar. Fiber stacking. Crystal stitching a vein. A thousand thousand jaws at work on the idea of a landscape. Some of them press a palm to a pillar and feel, through their own parchment skin, the weight of the country above, and they answer it with a mark that will be gone before they return—if returning remains a concept the topography continues to support. Their mouths are files, or membranes, or both. When they speak it is not words separable from the basin’s voice. It is a structured addition: a frequency that maps a fresh ridge, a warning of a stand about to slump, a counting of the dry grit sifting down from the parent-membrane. They have too many ears, or surfaces that are all ear. The deafening is, for them, a conversation they were born inside.

In the rarer still pockets—the lee of a breathing butte, the hollow of a trunk wider than any of them—they will sometimes sit without purpose. Not rest; there is no rest here, only a temporary agreement with gravity. They listen to things becoming. They watch a pale spear ignite from a seed that never swelled, leaves like hands opening in a hurry, already silvered, rusted, browned. A child of theirs (the distinction is loose; they pack their fallen into the substrate the same way the plants do, the same way the runners do) will put out a new joint, or a surface that is all eye, and no one will remark it. Adaptation is not an event. It is the weather.

They know the rumors. That the membrane is the first plant and they are only its interior weather. That the forest grew a sky because the true sky was gone or unendurable. They do not decide. Decision is a surface habit. They cut another flake from the black glass. They lash another platform that will not last. They walk a day and remain inside the same grammar of place, horizon made of bodies, light that discloses everything and rests nothing. The basin is not finished. Neither are they. That is the whole of the law they keep, and the law keeps them, warm and dry and deafening, jaw and fiber and spark, becoming.
