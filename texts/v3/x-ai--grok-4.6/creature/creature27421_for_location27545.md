The films come first, though they do not know they are first and would not last long enough to learn the word. They are not quite alive in any way that has rehearsed itself. On stone so new it still tastes of the decision that made it, they appear as a discoloration the light has not yet agreed to ignore. A mineral blush. A pigment that happened because the photons, having traveled hundreds of unscattered meters, arrived with enough remaining insistence to pry an electron loose. The water drinks the leftover heat at once; the films remain only as long as the current forgets to notice them. They photosynthesize the way frost photographs a window: because the surface was there and the temperature paused. No roots. No decision to stay. When the shear arrives they lift away in pale shreds and become part of the rumor of particles, which is almost no rumor at all.

Farther out, in volumes so large they have weather of their own, something else briefly assembles. A hanging meadow, fibrous, photosynthetic, the color of an idea that has not chosen its kingdom. It occupies a cube of ocean the size of a vanished country and waves in densities that have never seen air. For a day, or a week that is still only local, it is the only crowding the water will admit. Then an internal wave that has traveled a thousand kilometers as a single moving cliff remembers its purpose, or a storm cell grinds through, or the seafloor simply revises its elevation, and the meadow unwrites itself. The column returns to emptiness. You will not meet another. The plants, if that is what they were becoming, remain unconvinced.

Nothing else has been given permission. No bodies that move with intent. No schools that would give the blue a second rhythm. No hunters, because there is not yet enough density to justify the invention of hunger. The silence that should follow from this absence is theoretical; the water will not allow it. What occupies the frequencies instead is the sound of instruction: matter being told its slope, its heat budget, the precise angle at which a current may turn and still remain water. That vibration lives in the new rock more continuously than any film. Press a palm to a face that arrived this afternoon and you feel it—the hum that is older than thunder and younger than mountains, the only inhabitant that does not get planed away or subsided by evening.

If there are minds here they are not housed. They are the permissions themselves: the way the ocean stays impossibly high without spilling, the way day is still permitted to look like day, the way erosion is accomplished by hesitation rather than centuries. They have no objects, no cables, no lights hung by a hand. They live as laws that have not yet hardened into habit. Sometimes an entire province of seafloor finishes itself in an afternoon, lifts until the surface crawls with white water the size of a nation, then thinks better of the height and returns to the deep. The storm that was its birth travels on, looking for the next arrival. That gesture—creation and erasure sharing one motion—is the closest thing this place has to a resident that persists.

The rest is cool water that remembers being sky, a pale sun that has not been asked to do anything remarkable, and the long intervals of blue-white nothing in which a world is allowed to exist a little at a time. What lives here has not yet accumulated enough to cast a shadow that lasts. It clings, it occurs, it is sheared away, it is instructed. It has not been given the luxury of remaining.
