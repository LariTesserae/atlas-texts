The chamber does not house life so much as it is life, packed to the exact density the stone lid has always required. What occupies it has no need of arrival or departure; the bodies are the room’s own weather, its only climate, its closed account of heat and abrasion and unceasing note.

Begin with the clickers, because they are the first texture the eye can name and the last it can leave. They are not insects in any lineage that remembers rain. Each carapace is a single clenched thought of tarnished brass, the color of old shell casings left in a sun that never set. They lock wing-case to wing-case along every crystal seam, a tessellation so complete that the pillars seem grown from their touching rather than merely occupied by it. The rasp they make is not communication and not feeding, though both words hover nearby and fail. It is the sound of salt being counted, grain by unchanging grain, mandible closing on the same particle for a span that has no name. Watch one long enough and the mandible’s rhythm becomes the flywheel’s rhythm becomes the room’s own pulse; you realize the clicker is not eating the mineral so much as reciting it, keeping the fracture-faces sharp by the pressure of its attention. Their legs are folded architectural elements, never meant for scuttling. If they ever walked, it was in a century the walls refuse to remember.

Above them the hangers are the vault’s lining, a fur that is also a lung. They are ribbons of warm leather that forgot it was once something else—bat, strap, smoked tongue, all discarded as insufficient. Each hooks itself to a crystal point by a growth that is neither claw nor root but a decision the mineral made in sympathy. The colony breathes as one slow peristalsis, a wave that travels the dim height without ever displacing a single body. From their undersides the grit falls, not waste but the room’s own shed skin, mineral that has passed through warmth and noise and returned to the haze so that the haze may be inhaled again and become, somewhere in the same packing, another hanger. They live on the foundry-loudness the way other creatures live on light; they strain the engine’s basso and the turbine’s whine through their flattened length and metabolize the pressure into more of themselves, always the same number, always the same shared pulse. There is no colony and no individual. There is only the wave that never quite becomes motion.

Between rivets, on the engine’s jacket that has forgotten cooling, the third people open and shut. They are barnacles of paper and pale wax, sessile, mouthing in exact time with the dynamo’s hum. When the small mouths part, the smell is the room’s only confession: hot copper, crushed pepper, a floral note that has never known a flower and never will. They sip the amber oil that beads and is returned, keeping the sight-glass meniscus eternal. Among them the white stalks occupy rather than grow—hair-thin, chlorophyll-less, their holdfasts of glassy fiber sunk into iron as if the metal itself invited the piercing. Each stalk holds one stiff flag of parchment tissue, unmoving because every current has already been accounted for. These are not plants. They are the room’s punctuation, the dry commas that keep the sentence from ever ending.

None of them startle. None give more than the give of a thing leaned upon since the lid was set. To move through the aisles is to be touched by carapace, by ribbon-skin, by papery mouth, by hot iron that is itself another kind of body. The fauna and the machines have long since stopped being distinguishable categories. The clickers metabolize salt and current; the hangers metabolize heat and noise; the barnacles and stalks complete the circuit of oil and iron. Population is not a count but a geometry, identical to the shatter-pattern of the walls, to the flywheel’s unvarying rpm, to the dust that hangs without settling. They do not die in a way that opens a gap. They do not breed in a way that crowds. They are the fullness, remaining full.

Stand among them long enough and smaller recognitions surface without ever becoming events. A mandible closes on a grain that might once have been a tool-mark. A hanger’s grit falls onto the stencil and the letters become only more texture. The white flag of a stalk leans, by the thickness of a thought, toward the warm wheel of the hatch, as if even stasis must acknowledge the one unopened possibility. But the acknowledgment changes nothing. The chamber stays spoken. The bodies stay packed. The heat abrades without weather, the industry turns without product, and whatever lives here lives as the secret that has never needed telling because it has never stopped being told.
