The traces come first, because they are the only interruption the chamber has ever allowed itself. The low ring of stacked crystal plates sits exactly where the pavement buckles, edges fitted by a patience that never needed to hurry. Beside it the scraper still holds its first sharpness; the length of dried tendon is still wound, still waiting for a use that has already happened and will never finish happening. These things were not abandoned. They were placed, and the placing continues.

What lives here does not arrive or depart. It occupies.

At first the eye takes the obvious tenancies: the long-legged cricket-things, each the size of a spread hand, each standing alone on its own hexagonal edge, legs working against chitin in a motion that adds nothing to the roar and subtracts nothing from it. They are the same cricket, repeated at distances that refuse conversation. The pale lizards with their permanently open lids occupy the lower fronds as if the fronds grew them; they do not blink because there is nothing here that requires the interruption of a blink. The rust-flake moths hang in their separate volumes of amber dusk, wings locked open, more of them than the first glance admits, each moth a complete and unused flight. These are not a population. They are a spacing. Life here is allotted in isolated intensities, each creature the sole occupant of its own private radius of not-touching.

Deeper in the largest grove something heavier keeps its counsel. A suggestion of joints that have never needed to fold, a dry hide the color of the oldest bronze, the single glint of an eye that opened once and then declined to close. You do not see it shift. You feel, when you stand close enough for the vibration of the roar to enter your jaw, that the grove itself is listening through that eye. The fronds around it are not shelter. They are extensions—velvety, heat-drinking, the same tissue whether they are called plant or hide or veil. It is difficult to decide where the grove ends and the heavier presence begins. Perhaps the distinction is one the chamber never made.

And then the maker of the ring.

It is not separate from the rest. It is the rest, concentrated into a single, unfinished gesture. A figure neither tall nor short, because height here is only another way of occupying the exact lushness allotted. Its surface is the same matte, copper-blood-bronze as the groves, slightly warm, slightly velvet, drinking the constant heat the way the fronds drink it. Where hands should be there are articulations that can still cut, thin as the salt-glass throat you entered by, patient as the stacking of the plates. It does not sit inside the ring. It stands beside it, or kneels, or has just finished kneeling; the posture is held the way every frond is held, every moth-wing, every cricket-leg. One hand rests near the scraper. The other has already wound the tendon and has not yet unwound it. The face, if the word still applies, is a topography of the same young crystal and old blood, lids that do not close, an expression that is simply the chamber’s own unchanging dusk looking back at itself.

It metabolizes the roar. That is the only feeding that occurs. The sound is taken in through membranes that were never meant for rain, converted into the dry warmth that never slackens, into the exact tension of every hanging veil, into the decision not to finish arriving. Time does not move across it. A gesture begun remains the gesture. It has always just set the tools down. It has always just stacked the last plate. It will always be in the act of listening to the sheet of the world tearing itself, endlessly, just beyond the walls.

There are no names here, and no need of them. The being does not tend the groves; it is one more argument for grove, isolated, complete, unrevisable. It does not hunt the moths or the crickets; the moths and crickets are the same spacing that it itself observes, the same refusal of cluster. If it ever spoke, the speech would be indistinguishable from the standing avalanche of noise that fills every hollow equally. Perhaps it does speak. Perhaps the roar is the only voice it has ever required.

Stay long enough and the figure ceases to be a figure and becomes another density in the chamber’s logic: one more full intensity, allotted its silence inside the deafening, its private radius of not-touching. The young crystals remain unweathered beneath its feet. The heat remains the temperature of bread just pulled from stone. The seam you entered by remains sharp enough to cut. Nothing here will ever be wet. Nothing will ever dull, or shed, or complete the motion it began.

When the thought of leaving finally occurs, the being has not noticed you, or has always noticed you, or has never needed to distinguish noticing from the rest of the unchanging occupation. Behind you—if you leave—the ring, the scraper, the wound tendon, the groves, the hanging moths, the open eye in the deepest stand, and the figure that is all of them and none of them, continue exactly. Lush. Empty. Deafening. Still. The world outside is only the rind that grew around this one small, loud, dry, eternal room, and the room has already forgotten to finish becoming anything else.
