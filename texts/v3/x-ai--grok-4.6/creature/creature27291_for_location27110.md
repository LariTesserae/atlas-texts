The first things that live here are not yet decided on their names. They arrive as concentrations of the room’s own glare, a brightening that thickens at the wetter seams until legs remember themselves and the habit of walking takes hold. What follows is a pale millipede only because the eye, desperate for a category, offers the word; it is longer than it should be and thinner than courtesy allows, a segmented suggestion whose thousand legs are a single shimmer rather than a sound. Columns of them braid the walls, drinking condensation that has not yet chosen to become drops. When two processions meet they occupy the same few inches without collision, as though each is walking a slightly different draft of the chamber. Their shed skins do not litter so much as they mineralize, becoming the next thin layer of floor; the generation that follows walks on the ghosts of the last and does not notice the difference.

Above them the air holds moths that have forgotten flight. They are flat as pressed flowers, the color of paper left too long in a window, and they move only when a current of coolness—more a change in the quality of light than a wind—hands them from one stillness to another. They do not land. Landing would require a decision the pocket has not yet scheduled. Midges hang in the brightest band like a motionless blizzard that occasionally relocates itself a finger’s width to the left; the space they vacate takes a moment to realize it is empty. On the dish-floor, around the isolated palms of water, isopods gather in numbers that ought to click and do not. They feed on the mineral film with the same unhurried courtesy with which they feed on one another. Nothing here is hungry in the ordinary way. Appetite is simply another process that has not agreed on a schedule.

Larger lives occupy the edges of the pocket’s logic. The watcher clings to a forming cornice, white and damp, the size of a closed fist and possessed of too many shoulder-blades, as if the idea of a torso had been sketched several times and all the sketches left in place. Its eyes have not opened so much as they have been omitted; the places where eyes should be are simply more of the same unfinished skin. It breathes—you see the flanks move—yet the chamber refuses the sound. Once in a long while it extends a tongue longer than the room should permit and drinks from a puddle without crossing the distance. The water dimples from beneath. The moth it takes from the air does not arrive in its mouth; the moth is simply no longer among the handed currents. New watchers assemble at the wetter seams when the old one has been still too long, first as a brightening, then as the suggestion of extra bones.

There are traces of a maker who may still be present in the way a decision can remain after the hand that made it has withdrawn. The stacked stones—three, or four, the count eddies—lean in a direction that is not quite down. Beside them the parallel scratches glow brighter than the surrounding rock, wounds through which the chamber’s light leaks. No tool remains, only the idea of lines, as if something had been trying to invent writing and had gotten as far as the notion that meaning might be cut. A ring of dampness too regular to be accidental marks the ghost of a vessel never brought. A hairline groove runs from the cairn toward the entrance-seam and then forgets itself. These are not ruins. They are the chamber practicing the habit of intention.

Sometimes all the small lives turn at once, not toward the cairn, not toward the crack, but toward a thickening in the air, a place where the glare condenses into a shape that almost has edges. Nothing comes through. The thickening thins. The millipedes resume their braids. Yet the memory of that facing lingers in the stone the way a fever lingers in skin. Distances stretch when they are not measured. The puddles reflect a larger, older, already-finished cave that this room has not earned, complete with real shadows and the sound of distant water. Blink and the reflection is only water again.

What lives here, then, is the unfinished itself: a silk-work of motion that does not displace the brightness, a geology still choosing its grain, an appetite that has not learned splash or rustle or haste. The lichen considers whatever rests too long. The white vein on the ceiling advances by a single crystal and then lies still, shining as if it contained its own weather. You could stay. The damp would find you. A fifth stone might appear on the cairn, or one might be subtracted. The pocket is still being born and has room in its schedule for anything that does not take up much space—anything willing to become, for a while, another quiet concentration of its sourceless light.
