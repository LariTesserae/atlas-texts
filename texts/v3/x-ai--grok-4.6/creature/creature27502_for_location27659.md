The first lives you meet are the ones that refuse to be named as inhabitants, only as occupancy. They occupy the water-sheets the way the sheets occupy the clay: provisionally, completely, without claiming ownership. Dark commas flex and vanish. Pale discs rotate once and forget the rotation. Things with too many joints skate the meniscus and never break it; their legs are the same temperature as the water and the water does not record them. Eyes sit on the tops of heads like afterthoughts that decided to stay. They watch without turning. When you step, the sheet closes over your print before the print can decide it was a wound. These lives do not flee. They simply continue being the surface.

In the thickets the occupancy thickens into bodies that have accepted the lattice as their own joints. Lizards the exact red-brown of wet brick lie along tool-marks as if the marks were veins they have chosen to inhabit. They do not startle because startling would be a second motion, and second motions have been discouraged. Moths the width of open hands hang in the air a finger’s breadth from leaves and never quite choose a landing; their wings are the same held breath as the flowers that refuse to drop. Birds occupy the bark-cord knots the way extra knots would occupy them: they are part of the tying. Their heads tilt in the same slow increments the mats use when they remember they are woven. Larger shapes move farther off, on the ramps and between the ziggurats. Cattle-outlines, dog-outlines, long-walker outlines. Their motion is genuine and also already completed; watch one descend and it is still descending after you have looked away and looked back. Sometimes it is standing in the shallows with its head lowered to a reflection that has never learned to ripple. The sound they make, when they make any, is filed immediately: a hoof that is only a fingertip rehearsing thunder, a cry that tears cloth that is already underwater. They are abundant. They are almost silent. They obey the agreement and also constitute it.

Deeper in, where the platforms step and the weepholes run like slow script, the occupancy becomes someone.

They are the height of people who have been walking since the first course was laid. Their skin is the same packed clay, still showing the flats of their own palms and the long ovals of their own heels, still faintly ridged where a comb of split reed was dragged across them in the last hour that counted as an hour. The marks do not fade with depth; if you could peel a flake they would continue inward, layer on identical layer, fabrication all the way through. Heat comes off them the way heat comes off the basin itself: steadily, without pulse, the warmth of work that began before arrival and has no intention of finishing because finishing has already occurred. Water beads on them and does not evaporate; the beads only join other beads and then rejoin the weeping faces of the ziggurats. Their eyes are the color of the standing sheets, so clear the palm-prints show through the irises like fossils that have not yet agreed to die. They do not blink often. When they do, the valley rearranges by a single course and then remembers itself.

They move the way the ramps move: young, frank, knife-edged. Nothing about them has been rounded. Rain has not been allowed to argue. Their garments, when they wear any, are reed mats cantilevered from the shoulders, living thatch trained over lattices of their own ribs, bark cord that is also tendon. Even their shadows are thin and made. They tend the islands of green with the same poverty of means that built everything: a cupped hand, a stone used as a beater, a comb, a length of cord. They do not plant so much as remind the plants of the boundary. Vines follow the tool-marks on their arms the way vines follow the tool-marks on the platforms. Flowers open against their chests and stay open. Nothing drops. The lushness is a held breath they also hold.

They do not speak. Sound comes unwillingly here, and they have agreed, without speaking, not to add to it. What noise they make is the noise the place already files away: a single drip with no source (it is them), a fiber stretching (it is them), something like a cough made of mud (it is them). When two pass on a ramp they do not greet. Their motion loops the way the larger shapes loop. You can watch one climb a ziggurat and, if you blink, find it still climbing, or already at the top looking out at a horizon that is only more tiled palm-prints, or standing in a doorway that is only a darker rectangle of the same material. Step through after them and the valley is the same valley rearranged, the same heat, the same held rain. Their roots, if they have roots, go into the brick and become brick, fiber becoming course becoming the next step they will take.

They eat what does not progress to rot: the fat leaves, the moss that drinks the weep, the papyrus that forgot its rows and remembered riot. They drink the ruled sheets. Their own wet footprints fill and do not fade; they walk on yesterday’s prints and tomorrow’s at once. The light, which occupies rather than travels, saturates them the same white-gold it saturates the clay. They have no sun to point to. They are the surfaces the brightness comes from.

Stand long enough and you understand they are not separate from the vow. They laid the basin and then became the laying. The construction does not stop at the plausible because they did not stop. A ramp ends in air and continues because they continued it, gravities unconsulted. They are finished, and still wet. Finished, and still showing the last fingerprints, which are also the first. Change would be vandalism they no longer know how to commit, so they do not. They watch the animals watching them. They watch you. Your prints join theirs and fill. The mat ticks once, like a knuckle they have decided not to crack, and then remembers the rule.

They live here the way the place lives: low, bright, handmade, dripping, green in islands, abundant and almost silent, and so completely arranged that the only thing left to do is remain. They remain. The clay remains new. The water remains undecided about the sea. You remain, if you stay, another layer identically marked, heat coming up through you, the world fabrication all the way down and all the way through.
