The lives here do not arrive or depart. They occupy the same way the trunks occupy: as a density that has already decided the intervals. What metabolizes does so by remaining, by holding a half-split or an incomplete spiral as if the holding itself were the only remaining chemistry. You will not find blood or chlorophyll or the small wet arguments of cells. You will find planes that have learned the habit of a leaf, a glitter that has learned the habit of a pulse, a warmth that has learned the habit of an afternoon that will not slide.

The first lives are the congregations themselves. Each stacked plate is a completed sentence in a grammar of mica and bronze-green; each overlapping palm of the high canopy is a decision that light shall be delayed and never quite arrive as the sun you walked in under. They do not drink. They do not turn. Their interior is a packing of dry glitter that refuses the name of sap and answers a break with restoration, so that the stem you thought you took is already back in its slot and you are holding only the memory of salt. They live by saturation. Volume is their only appetite, and it has been satisfied. Walk among them and your shoulders learn their nearness the way a hand learns the inside of a glove that was always that size. They have no youth and no age because the lock was applied at the first margin and left there. A bud remains half-open for a geology; its sudden pink is not a promise but a permanent interior, like the color inside a geode that no one will ever split.

Below them, in the ground-tangle, the stamped-metal ferns and the cups that never held dew live by the same refusal. Press them and the ozone comes off on your skin as a white-gold dust that will not wash, a bloom that is both spore and ash and neither. They fill every reachable interval so that walking is a negotiation with presence. Their life is the life of a proof that has reached QED and still radiates a little heat. Nothing chews them. Nothing frays them. They are mid-gesture, forever, and the gesture is occupancy.

Then there are the remainders, the sideways metabolisms that use the leftover hum the way a moth uses a streetlamp that has already gone out. They are rare enough to be rumor until they are not. A glint takes the exact habit of a wing and has no body; it shears the air without wind and is gone before the eye can decide whether it was bronze or the memory of bronze. You hear the idea of a rustle in leaves that do not move, a sound slightly previous to itself, as if the forest had already finished the motion and left you the echo as a courtesy. Sometimes the sinter shows a loop of impressions that closes on itself: the shape of weight, the suggestion of a pad or a claw, the physics withheld. The loop means nothing that will feed you. It is a track that has decided to be its own destination.

These remainders do not hunt or flee. They appear in the polite distances that become untrue. The clearing that stays two hundred paces off no matter how many steps you take will sometimes hold a taller density, a congregation that has the silhouette of something that once walked on two legs or four and then chose to become another vertical slot. When one eye has canopy and the other has empty white sky, the disagreement itself briefly lives: a fold, a watcher made of the mismatch, gone when the eyes agree again. You may find, at the edge of vision, a vine that has completed its clasp after all, but only for the duration of a blink, and then the finger-width of air is restored and the intention is once more the entire form.

Nothing here consents to a souvenir, because a souvenir would be a change and change is the one verb the basin will not conjugate. A stem snapped in the hand becomes, when the head turns, either unsnapped or a piece of warm salt that was never green or simply the denser forest it always was. The lives that persist do so by being already finished. They are the period left in place after the sentence was written across the mineral field. They do not weather. They do not rot. They do not remember a time before the lock because the lock is the only time they have.

Stand still long enough and the leftover tone under hearing resolves, almost, into something like breath that does not require lungs: a held warmth moving through the dry dark, a metallic sweetness that was never blood and yet knows the shape of it. That is as close as they come to speaking. The whole basin is their body, kilometers of grove and lesser grove, low swells that decided to be leaf instead of stone and then decided nothing more. You are permitted to walk as a courtesy. They do not mind you. They do not notice you. They have already filled every interval that could have held a noticing. What lives here is the pause itself, internally warm, unwilling to accept a new line, and it does not end at any edge you can reach.
