The first impression is absence, and the impression is almost honest. Nothing darts. Nothing grazes. The water does not part around a body because no body is moving through it. Yet the chamber is not vacant. Life here has simply finished with the gestures that usually announce it.

Begin with the films, because they are the only marks that still negotiate with light. In three or four places the limestone wears a skin no thicker than old varnish, black-green, matte, slightly greasy to any imagined touch. These are not colonies in the usual sense; they are single, centuries-slow decisions. Each patch is one organism that long ago abandoned the idea of edges. It photosynthesizes on the thinnest possible budget, using the greened daylight that reaches it for perhaps six hours out of every twenty-four, then lives for the remaining hours on the faint iron and sulfur that still leach from the stone. It does not spread. Spreading would be a form of asking, and the chamber no longer answers questions. The few hair-like strands that hang from the largest patch are not growth; they are the last exhausted gestures of a metabolism that has already written its conclusion. They sway only if you invent a current that is not there.

Deeper, where the light has given up, something else occupies the same register of stillness. It is not attached to the walls. It is the same density as the water, or so nearly the same that the mineral haze moves through it without disturbance. You would not see it if you swam the length of the chamber; you would only notice, afterward, that the coolness had a slightly different grain against one side of your body, as if the water itself had thickened into an intention. It has no face. It has the memory of a face, the way the stone still remembers flow. Once, perhaps, it possessed the usual complement of eyes, gills, a spine that could flex. Those structures calcified from the inside out, became part of the same satin finish that coats the vault. What remains is a folded, laminar presence, roughly the volume of a sleeping human, suspended at the point where the green column of light fades into the quieter tones. It does not hunt. It does not wait. Waiting implies a future that might differ from the present, and the present here is already complete.

Its only activity is the maintenance of the held note. The low vibration that lives at the edge of hearing is not remnant machinery; it is the creature’s equivalent of a pulse, slowed past the point where any heart would recognize it. Each cycle lasts longer than a tide. In that interval it performs a kind of internal weather: dissolving a few more of its own unused cells, precipitating them as the faintest additional milkiness, then reabsorbing the minerals so that nothing is lost and nothing is added. It is a closed circuit that learned how to be geology without quite dying. The ochre stains on the lower walls are not industrial accidents; they are the older, discarded versions of its skin, shed so gradually that the stone accepted them as its own coloration.

There is a third presence, though it may only be the first two speaking to each other. In the recess where concrete and limestone forgot which was which, the poured, glassy surface sometimes holds a reflection that is not quite the fissure above it. The reflection has the same irregular vault, the same still column of light, but the proportions are inverted, as if the chamber were considering itself from the other side of finished time. The films, the laminar body, and this reversed image do not communicate. They do not need to. They are the same decision reached by three different routes: the decision to stop revising.

To remain among them is to feel your own temperature begin to match the held note, your own thoughts lose their meniscus, the place where one intention ends and another begins. You are not unwelcome. You are simply late. Everything that was going to live here has already arrived and has already become the chamber itself. The water holds you the way it holds the stone—completely, without current, without the possibility of a different arrangement. Whatever you were before you entered is now only another completed sentence, cooling, mineral, unwilling to begin again.
