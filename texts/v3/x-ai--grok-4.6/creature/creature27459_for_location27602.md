The pocket does not contain them so much as they are the pocket’s way of remaining unfinished. What lives here is a single appetite expressed as many small, wet decisions, each one a negotiation between heat that still remembers being liquid and water that has nowhere else to go. They are not a fauna that arrived. They are a habit the stone acquired when it rose too fast, a mineral process that learned to flex, to choose an angle, to open a mouth.

At first they present as geology that has begun to fidget. The floor’s nodules are not cobbles; they are slow, amber-dark limpets poured from the same rusted honey as the chemical gardens they graze. Their shells are layered like cooled caramel, veined with iron that still conducts a private fever. Underneath, a crystalline rasping surface works the silica tubes down to wet glitter. They do not swallow the glitter. They take the gradient the glitter represents—the sudden drop from dissolved to solid—and leave the rest as a finer, more desperate garden for whatever comes next. When one lifts itself, the stone beneath is paler, briefly sterile, already being recolonized by smear.

Beside them, and sometimes occupying the same ledge by a thickness that refuses to be measured, move the many-jointed. These are paler, almost only the idea of an arthropod rendered in wet glass. Too many knees, too many possible elbows. Their interiors show a slow traffic of darker beads—perhaps organelles, perhaps undissolved metal, perhaps the second population commuting through them. They travel by deciding, each breath, which of their angles will be allowed to exist. A limb that was a supporting strut becomes, without transition, a probing filament; the old position simply ceases to have been chosen. Heat-shimmer eats the middle of every gesture. You see departure and arrival. The journey itself is a rumor the steam declines to confirm.

Then there are the mouths. Almost nothing else: an aperture the color of a fresh fracture, trailing a veil of mineral lace so fine it could be a memory of sulfur. They graze the iridescent grease that creeps along the cooler runnels. The veil is both filter and gill, both tongue and shadow. They do not pursue. They wait where steam collapses back into water, where the shock of phase-change is richest, and the smear thickens toward them as if the chemistry itself were leaning in. When they feed, the lace darkens for a moment, then sloughs a dust of used metal that the gardens immediately begin to climb.

These three are only the first looking. Look longer and the count goes bad in the way the light from the roof-cut sometimes doubles, very slightly, as if noon had a twin that almost coincided. A second congregation occupies the same wet facets, offset by a thickness that is not space. They are not hidden behind. They are additional. The same limpets, the same jointed glass, the same lace-mouths, yet standing a little aside, the way a reflection stands in a lake that is also standing in its own reflection. Sometimes a flake of new volcanic glass is lifted by one set and the work is finished by the other. Sometimes a drip is heard that belongs to no falling drop, a small permission the pocket grants the idea of water. Sound itself arrives late or padded, as if the extra population were felt.

They do not eat one another in any way that can be caught. They eat difference: the hotter plane against the cooler seep, the instant a metal falls out of solution, the thin slap when a steam veil collapses. The pocket is a pantry of unfinished business, and they have filled it the way a comb is filled, except the comb is also the bees, also the honey, also a second comb no one agreed to build. They press without quite touching, a dense packing that leaves almost no unused volume and yet never jams. The mountain, still being poured, has more inside it than its walls should permit, and they are that more.

A few use objects, but only as a spasm of attention. A fresh flake of glass—edges still molecularly cruel, born this morning—is lifted, used to scrape a richer vein or to wedge a seep open so the downhill film blooms thicker, then dropped. Nothing is kept. Nothing is stacked. The flake returns to scree. If this is craft it is the kind that has not yet decided to outlive the gesture that made it. The only architecture is the mountain itself, and the mountain is still deciding whether to be liquid.

They arrive already knowing the rules. A new crack brightens, sterile and furious; within minutes the first grazers are there, as if they had been waiting in the rock, or as if they are a property of this particular heat and wet, precipitated the way the silica tubes are precipitated. Biology here is a mineral habit that learned to walk. Death is only a change of phase. When a wall goes from weeping to screaming steam, a whole layer of them simply ceases on that plane. The neighboring bodies close the gap without hurry, the way water closes. By evening the dead have become more of the chemical garden, hair-fine tubes of rusted honey and dirty turquoise climbing the place where appetite used to be.

They make almost no sound of their own. The loudest events remain the mountain’s: a rare fat drip, the silk-tear of a forming veil, once in a long while a grain of new glass ticking once on a shell. Their movements happen under the water-skin, as if condensate were their true air. When they must cross open vapor they do not displace it correctly. The shimmer swallows them. You breathe soup that is almost boiling and the soup is their weather, their medium, their only season.

Time inside keeps poor accounts. The steam cycle—build, veil, collapse, weep—has the length of a held breath, then another, then a longer stillness while pressure finds a new hairline. In those stillnesses the second population is easiest to see, standing a little aside, occupying the same ledges the way a reflection occupies a lake that has decided to stand on its own reflection. Reality thins but does not break. The knife-cut in the roof remains a knife-cut. You can still leave. While you remain, the pocket contains more event than event should fit, and the extra is alive, and the alive is quiet, and the quiet is hot and wet and newly made.

Nothing outside knows they are here. The ridge is too young for names, too unfinished for ruins or future structures. Their only history is the cooling. Their only future is more cooling, or a last failure of the blister that opens them to the thin upper air. Then the water would flash away, the smear bake to glaze, and both congregations—or however many there actually are—would become a crust of odd nodules for later weather to misunderstand.

Until then they keep working. Grazers rasp. Flakes are lifted and discarded. Steam writes and erases itself on the ceiling. The gardens branch toward a light they do not use. A new animal arrives out of a fresh crack, already gleaming, already hungry for the next unfinished difference. The silence holds them all, densely, almost touching, a hidden fever at the top of a mountain that has not finished being born. They are what the rock does when it is asked, too soon, to be a world.
