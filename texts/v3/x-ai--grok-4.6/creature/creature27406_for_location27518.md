What lives in the cirque are not residents. They are the continuation. Each form is a syllable of the held note, a moving part that the valley grew when the ridgeline tore and left the machinery visible. Nothing arrived. Everything was already the process, given just enough outline to keep the stroke going.

The clickers occupy the moss the way valves occupy a pump. Thumb-sized, schist-colored, their skins a mosaic of tiny mineral plates that might be scales and might simply be the same flakes the walls still shed. They do not hop or swim. They swell and contract in exact time with the hydraulic pulse that travels the slabs, each contraction producing a dry click that is neither voice nor warning but another piston-stroke made audible. Dozens can share a square foot of sphagnum without touching. When a palm-sized flake of basalt detaches, falls, and is already moss, sometimes a new clicker is simply there, as if the impact had been a birth. They keep no territory. They keep the tempo.

On the bare, reflective planes walk the jointed ones. A body like a collapsed truss, too many articulations, legs that telescope and fold in sequences no arthropod ever needed. They place each tip with ceremonial care and leave no print; the new stone does not record them, or recognizes them as its own and refuses the impression. When they halt, steam lifts from every elbow and knee—the same ozone-and-fern scent that hangs in the spray. They pause at veins, listen with their whole frame, sometimes tap a facet so that another flake is released at the proper instant. Then they move on. Their circuit is the size of the cirque and takes exactly as long as it takes. They are maintenance and liturgy at once.

The cyclers hang in the weeping air. Bird-shaped only by courtesy: fuselages tapered at both ends, paired vanes that rotate in opposing cycles rather than beat. They station-keep in the mist, turning slowly, drinking condensation through a slit that is not a mouth. Somewhere inside them water and dissolved stone precipitate into bright metallic beads—hematite, copper, something that rolls gleaming into the nearest seam and is drawn back into the veins to become the next pulse. Their sound is a high harmonic threaded through the turbine-note, always the same and never repeating. They are the smelter and the refinery and the delivery system.

The midges are almost not bodies. They become visible only at the moment of contact, a faint blue-white spark and a crackle no louder than a thought. They are the system’s static given swarming form, the ozone made briefly locust. They do not land, do not feed, do not die in any way that leaves a corpse. They simply continue to collide and flash, the electricity of the place wearing a cloud of wings.

Other motions are less distinct. The pale saxifrage in its exact rows sometimes shivers in sequence, a wave traveling the ledge as though a single long muscle ran beneath the stone. The moss heaves with a slow peristalsis that matches the walls. In the tarn the wrong reflection occasionally contains a figure that is not you—standing on the offset shore, patient, perhaps one of the walkers enlarged or a clicker given different scale. If you watch long enough the two versions drift further apart until the other is performing an action that has not yet happened here, or happened already and been forgotten.

None of them register you. You are weather that failed to occur. Their ecology is a closed loop running at equilibrium; hunger was never designed in. They manufacture only the next identical moment: more seepage, more pulse, more click, more steam, more bead rolling back into the dark. The cirque itself is the larger body—an organ freshly faulted into daylight, skin still sharp, circulation still loud. The lives are its cells, its thoughts, its necessary clearances. It has no outside because it was never assembled. It is simply the process, cool and wet and dim, doing the only thing it has ever done.

You can stay. The clickers will click, the walkers will complete another silent lap, the cyclers will precipitate another bead. When you leave by the notch the sound does not fade behind you. It merely stops being yours. The factory continues in the dark, staffed by itself.
