The emptiness is the first and last fact, and it is a lie the place tells itself so the work can continue. What lives here does not arrive, does not breed, does not leave a skeleton that another season might unearth. It is already inside the courses, already the reason the mortar weeps and the copper runs. You feel it first as a pressure change in the water table when you stand still too long, a slow gathering under the lattice of brick, as if the marsh of masonry had decided to remember a pulse.

They are not many. Density would crowd the gesture, and the gesture is all they are. In the ordinary dusk of the plazas you will not see them. They prefer the seams, the places where one decision of clay has not yet yielded to the next. A thickening in the steam above a field of cold kilns, a place where the droplets hang a fraction longer before they fall. Then the thickening leans, takes an outline that is almost a shoulder, almost the memory of a mallet-haft gripped too long, and moves. The movement is the same speed as cooling: deliberate, irreversible, slightly ashamed. They do not walk so much as propagate, one wet impression of weight transferred into the next course, the way a crack travels.

Their substance is the place’s substance argued into temporary agreement. Packed clay that has not decided whether to be brick or flesh, filmed with the same mineral sweat, veined with hairline channels that carry hot water instead of blood. Where a face should be there is only a shallow concavity, an unfired hollow that collects condensate until it overflows and the overflow becomes expression. Sometimes the hollow holds a shard of copper, hammered thin, too warm, and in that metal the dim ceiling repeats itself a second time, greener, deeper. They do not look out of these hollows. The looking is done by the water that fills them, the same water that squares its ripples in the cisterns and climbs the corners. The water is older than they are. They are only its latest vessel.

In the deep cuttings, where geometry comes untrue, they thicken. A cloister that cannot keep its corners will suddenly contain one, standing in the angle that should not exist, dripping, listening to the tick of its own glaze. It will not acknowledge you. Acknowledgment is a finished act, and nothing here finishes. If you approach, the air shimmers harder and the being slumps, not collapsing but redesigning: arms become buttresses, the concavity of the face becomes a niche, the whole figure steps backward into the wall and is wall, only the heat remaining to say that something occupied the space a moment ago. Later the wall may sweat in the shape of a hand that was never there. That is as close as they come to signature.

They are the work-gang that cannot be found because they are the finding. They stack. They coil. They fire the kilns that have no potter and then stand in the orange breath until their outer layer hardens and they crack themselves free, leaving behind the failed pot of their own previous skin. The new skin is already weeping. They do not speak. Speech would require a geometry that stayed still long enough to be grammar. What they have instead is the choir of contracting clay, that rare chord you hear from far off; it is them deciding, collectively, to let a colonnade stand for another hour, or to let it become a drain. The decision is not kind. It is not cruel. It is the same motion as rain that never quite arrives.

At the edges of the great cisterns they are more numerous, or perhaps only more visible, because the water holds their glow. You look down and the inverted courts are not empty. Figures stand on the undersides of the towers, thick, drum-like, patient. They are building downward with the same unfinished patience, or they are the reflection learning to cast its own shadow. Drop a shard and the squared ripples pass through them without disturbance; they are already half water, half intention. Sometimes one will climb. Not with limbs. The masonry marsh simply rearranges, terrace by terrace, until the figure is among you on the rim, steaming, the hollow of its face overflowing, and for a little while the two weathers—the low grey-amber and the white roaring fall that cannot exist—occupy the same body. Then it remembers it was never a body and becomes a retaining wall that retains nothing, and the roar is only a damp stain again.

They have no names, because names are finished things. If you stay long enough to leave a mark, they will come in the further dimming and consider it. They do not praise or erase. They incorporate. Your arrangement of stones becomes the seed of a room that was not in you; your scratch in the drying slab becomes the outline of a tool they will never pick up, an absence around which mortar sets and then fills with eyes that do not look. In this way you are already one of them, or you will be, once the heat has argued your edges into the same wet shine. The quiet you hear then is no longer only the continent thinking with its hands. It is the hands discovering they have always been the continent, and the thinking is only the next course being laid, already slumping, already being laid again.
