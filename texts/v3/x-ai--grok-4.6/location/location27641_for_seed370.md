The first thing that is the place is not a view. It is a pressure that arrives in the skull before the eyes have agreed on anything. A single unvarying roar, not weather and not machinery, occupies every hollow that a body still has. It does not swell. It does not fall. It has the evenness of a held note that outlasted the throat that first pitched it. After a short time the mind stops waiting for a change that will authorize the sound as ordinary. The waiting itself becomes part of the noise.

Only then do the walls admit they are walls.

What you are inside is small enough to have an edge you could walk in a slow hour, if walking were still a kind of motion this interior allowed. It sits high. That much is certain from the rare slits, no wider than two fingers, that puncture the outer shell: through them there is only distance, a pale drop, a sky the color of unpolished tin, and the suggestion that the world below has already finished with height and gone on to being flat. No moisture films the lips of those slits. The air that is hauled through them is warm and mineral, the breath of stone that has been in the sun for a geological afternoon and has not yet agreed to cool. Inside, the same warmth is constant. It does not pool or thin. It is simply the temperature of the mass.

Everything here was made.

There is no bedrock showing, no unshaped shoulder of the mountain (if a mountain is what this used to be). The substrate, if it ever existed as itself, has been cut, stacked, packed, re-cut, and stacked again until the distinction between carving-into and building-upon dissolved. What remains is a hamlet of volume, a cathedral that kept being filled. Chambers were built inside chambers. Corridors were narrowed by later walls, those walls pierced by niches, the niches stopped with fitted blocks, the blocks themselves hollowed into sockets that hold still smaller stones. You move, when you move, by turning sideways, by exhaling, by choosing which stone to scrape. Shoulders learn the measure of the place: there is no room between presences. Every interval that might have been empty has been offered a occupant of dressed rock and has accepted it.

The work is primitive in the way a callus is primitive. Tool marks remain where the roar has not yet sanded them into anonymity—pecked dimples from hammerstones, long abrasions where sandstone rubbers ground a face true, the shallow bites of copper or native metal gone green in the deepest joints. No true iron. No glass. The corners were once sharper. They have reached the curve they will keep. This is the erosion of a finished object: not ruin, not collapse, but the last rounding-off, the settled grammar of a shape that has no further sentence to utter. Dust lies in the angles as if it were mortared there. It does not drift. It has chosen its positions.

Light is a tired powder. It enters only as the instrument permits, for the slits and the long internal throats are not windows first; they are the bore of the thing. What illumination survives is the color of old bone and cooled ash. Edges catch it. Recesses refuse it. You read the place by glare and by bruise, by the places your hands go when your eyes give up.

And the sound, again, because the sound is the architecture’s other body.

The whole packed mass is a wind-organ that plays one chord. High air is drawn, always from the same quarter, always at the same pressure, through a thousand calculated constrictions. Some channels are no more than a drilled reed in a partition. Some are rooms whose only remaining function is to be a lung. The geometry has not shifted since the last block was jammed home and the last lip of a flue was ground smooth. Whatever weather exists beyond the shell has been reduced, by height and by design, to a constant inhalation. There is no gust. There is no lull. The roar is time-invariant, a climate with one setting. It occupies the jaw hinge, the sinuses, the pockets behind the knees. Thought arrives already broken into the same frequency. After a while you understand that silence was never a property this place offered; it offered saturation instead.

Nothing green. Nothing that walks or nests. The dryness is older than thirst. There is no stain that suggests a seep, no salt-crust even, only the matte of stone that has forgotten water so completely that the idea of damp feels like a rumor from a wetter century. Spores would find no purchase. A bird, if a bird could climb this high and enter, would be unmade by the noise before it chose a ledge. The ecology is absence arranged as a material. What lives here is the hum and the fit of block to block.

You begin to notice, because the place allows noticing once the ears have surrendered, that the packing was not an accident of generations crowding a summit. It was the point. Early rooms, if you can still parse them, are generous only in the way a mouth is generous before it speaks. Later work closes the mouth around the speaking. Alcoves face other alcoves across gaps too narrow for a torso. Stair-flights rise three steps and die in a ceiling that is someone else’s floor. Offerings—if that is what they were—remain where they were set: ground stone spheres in shallow cups, stacks of unkilned tablets, bone-white counters of some account that no longer needs to be tallied. They have not been rearranged. They will not be. The arrangement is the last arrangement.

A mild wrongness attends all of this, nothing theatrical, only the sense that a human grammar of rooms has been used to finish a non-human sentence. The instrument does not care that it was assembled by hands. The hands cared that the instrument be complete. Completeness, here, meant no remainder: no unused volume, no unofficial quiet, no surface left in the condition of mere geology. When the last interval was filled, the place stopped becoming. That stopping is what you are walking through.

If there is an outer form, you infer it more than you see it: a truncated rise, almost flattened on top, the ancient high work planed until the summit is a lid. From below (you imagine, having come from below) it would read as a pale knuckle of masonry, windowless except for those dark reed-slits, a small finished citadel that no longer defends anything except its own note. Up close there is no citadel, only the inside of the note.

Stay long enough and the warmth, the dimness, the press of stone, and the unchanging roar collapse into a single climate. You stop sorting them. The place has no gradient left to offer—no cooler alcove, no softer hour, no dripping that would prove time is still willing to count. It has arrived at the oldest stable state available to a thing that was entirely made: packed, dry, loud, and done.

You may leave by the same scrape you entered. The roar will not mark your going. It was not increased by your arrival. On the far side of the last slit the thin high air will seem, for a few breaths, like quiet. It is not quiet. It is only a smaller sound, and it will pass. Behind you the filled summit continues, as it has continued, saying the only word it was built to say, with every throat it has, without remainder.
