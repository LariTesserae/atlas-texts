The first thing that is not water is height.

You understand this only after a long time, because there is so much water that height has to be inferred: a pressure that is wrong for an abyss, a light that is wrong for an abyss, a cold that is merely cool and therefore also wrong. The sun—there is a sun, ordinary and pale, the color of a coin seen through milk—reaches you. It reaches you through hundreds of meters of liquid and still looks like day. The water is that clear, or that empty, which here is nearly the same fact. Particles are rare. Silt has nowhere old enough to come from. What hangs in the column is new, and there is almost none of it.

This is an ocean that has not yet been given a proper bottom, or a bottom that has not yet agreed to stay down. Elevation and flood are happening at once. Somewhere far beneath the photic roar, stone is arriving. Not rising in the tectonic sense; arriving. It comes the way a thought comes, except the thought is basalt, or something that will later be willing to be called basalt. Raw geology, unweathered because weather has not had the centuries, only the hours. Ridges appear in the deep like bruises, then decide their own slope, then are planed by currents that did not exist yesterday. The erosion parameter of this place is not age. It is hesitation. The land is still choosing which way to fail.

Between these embryonic highs the distances are indecent. You can hang in blue-white nothing for a day of swimming and meet only water, and the water is loud. Not the polite knock of a reef. A planetary shout. The ocean here is large enough to invent its own weather and then destroy it. Cells of storm the size of vanished countries grind against one another. Internal waves—waves that never see air—travel for a thousand kilometers as single moving cliffs of density. When they break on a new ridge the sound is a physical object; it arrives in the ribs first. Rain, where the surface is near enough to have a surface, does not fall so much as continue. The sky is a second ocean with opinions. Sheets of it come down with the weight of masonry. The boundary between atmospheric water and the high sea is a negotiation, not a line. You surface into more of the same, only colder and moving faster, and the sun is still there, smeared, ordinary, unimpressed.

There are almost no things.

Density is a rumor. A hanging meadow of something that might become kelp occupies a cube of ocean the size of a small moon and then is gone; you will not find another. On the newest stone—stone so fresh it still holds the heat of having been decided, a heat the cool water drinks immediately—there are films. Not gardens. Films. A greening so sparse it reads as a mistake in the light, a mineral stain that learned photosynthesis by accident and has not yet committed. No roots that matter. No canopy. No succession. The plants, if they are plants, cling to the idea of a surface the way frost clings to a window: because the window happened to be there when the temperature was right. They do not accumulate. The turbulence shears them off and they dissolve back into the column, which remains empty.

Nothing walks. Nothing schools. Nothing hunts. The silence that would follow from that is theoretical. The water will not permit silence. It slams. It shears. It speaks in the register of things being assembled too quickly. There is a frequency, just at the edge of hearing and then not at the edge, that is not weather. It is the sound of material being told what it is. If you press a palm to a newly arrived face of rock you feel it: a vibration older than thunder and younger than any mountain, the hum of a process that does not care whether you have equations for it.

That is the only architecture. There are no walls, no piled stones, no cables, no lights that were hung by a hand. And yet the physics is being engineered. Currents turn at angles water should not keep. The ocean stays high—impossibly high, a sea seated on a world that has not finished its own gravities—without spilling off the edges of whatever this sphere is becoming. Heat is bled out of the new rock with a precision that is not climate. The light is allowed to be normal; someone or something has decided that day should still look like day, even here. You do not find the machines. The machines are the way matter behaves when it is still being instructed. Nanoscale, or the other end, the cosmic end, or both at once and therefore neither: a technology that has no objects, only permissions. The world is under construction and the construction is the world. What would be a scaffold in a lesser age is here a law, temporary and loud.

Sometimes a whole province of seafloor finishes itself in an afternoon. A plateau, embryonic, raw as a cut, lifts through the empty blue until it almost breaches, and the surface—wherever the surface is that hour—crawls with white water the size of a nation. Then the plateau thinks better of the height, or is told to, and subsides, and the storm that was its birth becomes a storm about nothing, and travels on, looking for the next arrival. You can watch this from a long way off because there is nothing in the way. The emptiness is the point and also the wound. Scale here is not landscape; it is the unit in which events are allowed to occur. A change of weather can last a week and still be local. A quiet can last a minute and still be a lie.

The temperature never quite becomes winter. Cool, always cool, the particular cold of water that has recently been sky and has not forgotten. It keeps the films of green from growing ambitious. It keeps the chemistry slow enough that the new stone can still be read, unscarred, if you could stay long enough to read it. You cannot. The dynamic will not hold still. Creation and erasure share a single gesture. A ridge is born; a current unwrites it; the sound of both is the same sound.

What remains, when you stop trying to make a picture of it, is this: a high, cool, nearly vacant ocean, loud as manufacture, lit like an ordinary afternoon, in which a world is being permitted to exist a little at a time. The plants are few and unconvinced. The animals have not been included. The buildings were never the plan. Only water, and the idea of ground, and a violence that is also a kindness, because without it there would be nothing yet to name.
