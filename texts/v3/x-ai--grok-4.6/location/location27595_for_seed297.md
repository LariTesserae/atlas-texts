The basin sits lower than any weather that still bothers with rain. You feel the drop in the knees before you see it: a wide, shallow cup of country, rimmed by a lip of darker stone that is hardly a hill, more a change of mind in the ground. The air there is warm in a held, indoor way, as if the heat were a property of the volume itself and not a gift of any sun. There is no wet in it. Lips split. The inside of the nose goes papery. What settles on the tongue is salt and a faint metallic sweetness, like blood remembered rather than tasted.

Between the living things the floor is a pale sinter—feldspar dust, evaporite, the memory of a chemistry that finished and left its crust. The polygons never quite close. Their edges are sharp enough to catch the light in thin white lines, as if the mud-cracks formed from a slurry that was never mud and then were told to stop. No puddle-ghosts. No dark tongues of damp. If you kneel, the heat of the ground comes up through the palm dry as a baked plate.

And then the lushness, which has no right to be here.

It occupies. That is the first fact. Trunks rise in tight congregations, their skins nearly touching, so that the space between is a vertical slot you turn sideways to use. The skins are not bark. They are stacked plates of something like mica and a green that does not quite sit in the eye as chlorophyll sits—greener in the glance, grayer in the stare, sometimes briefly the color of old bronze. The canopy is four-deep. High plates overlap like the palms of a crowd. Below them hang curtains, leaf on leaf, so dense the light that reaches the mid-story is ordinary sunlight merely thickened, slowed, given a slight olive delay. Lower still, a ground-tangle of blades and cups and things that have the habit of ferns if ferns had been stamped from thin, unpainted metal and then persuaded, once, to grow. You cannot walk a line. Presence fills the volume. Shoulders brush. The back of a hand finds a stem that was already there.

Nothing succuls. Press a leaf and it does not answer with water; it answers with a smell of ozone and hot stone and a dust of white-gold bloom that comes off on the skin and stays. Some blades are translucent. The sun through them is the same sun you know, only a little later in its own day than the sun outside the leaf. There are no drips, no beads, no vessels fat with sap. If there is an interior liquid it is not for you, and it is not water. Hollows, when a stem is (briefly, wrongly) broken, show a dry glitter, a packing of planes, and then the stem is not broken.

The youth of the place is everywhere and does not pass. Every margin is a first margin. No chew. No wind-fray. No mottling of age. Buds sit half-split, their inner color showing—a wetter green, a sudden pink—and they have sat that way for the entire duration of the basin, which may be an afternoon or a geology. Vines extend toward other vines and halt a finger-width short of clasping, the spiral incomplete, the intention preserved as form. Underfoot the same newness: crystal ridges with quarry-fresh faces that were never quarried; a low swell of the growth itself, like a hill that decided to be leaf instead of stone and then decided nothing more. There is no litter in the sense of a forest’s long argument with itself. What lies on the sinter is only more of the same, placed, unrotted, as if fallen and unfallen were a distinction the ground declined to learn.

This happened once.

Not as weather happens, and not as a people happen—there are no cuts, no stacked stones, no path that was worn rather than left, no artifact that admits a hand. A rule was applied to a mineral field. The field was instructed to become verdure according to a grammar ordinary chemistry does not keep: a packing algorithm, a growth that saturates volume rather than seeking light or water, a sentence written in matter by a physics that current instruments would call unfinished or illegal. The growth ran until every reachable interval held a presence, until density became the local law, and then the rule locked. Equilibrium, not death. The temperature does not fall at the rim or rise at noon because there is no noon that moves. The light is the light of a clear day that has chosen a height and kept it. Shadows are exact, doubled sometimes, and they do not creep. Wind, if that is the name for the warm pressure on the face, does not displace a blade. The forest is mid-gesture, forever.

You walk, and walking is permitted in the way a courtesy is permitted. Distances remain polite and become untrue. A thinning of the trunks—call it a clearing—stands two hundred paces off, a paler floor, a slightly higher sky. You take the two hundred paces and the clearing is under the soles and is still two hundred paces off, and the half-open bud that was at your wrist is at your wrist again, or its twin, which is the same bud. The far rim of the basin is a bluer repetition of this forest and also a wall the geometry will not let you touch. Sometimes one eye has the canopy and the other has a dry white sky with no forest in it; then they agree, and the agreement is not a relief so much as a restoration of the local terms.

Sound is scarce and slightly previous. Steps on the sinter are a dry whisper that the density drinks. There is, if you wait, a tone under hearing, the leftover hum of a process that completed and did not power down. No drip. No insect-saw. No bird. Once, not far and not near, something like the idea of an animal moving through leaves that do not move—a rustle without displacement, a track in the salt that has the shape of weight and none of the physics. Fauna here is rumor and remainder. Whatever still metabolizes the leftover potential of the lock does so rarely, and sideways to the visible. You may see a glint with a wing’s habit and no body. You may find a loop of impressions that closes on itself and means nothing that will feed you.

Look long at a trunk and the lattice comes forward: not rings of season but a circuitry of planes, units smaller than cells, each a decision already taken. The air shears without wind. A stem you snap is unsnapped when the head turns back, or the hand holds a piece of warm salt that was never green, or the hand is empty and the forest is denser by one stem than it was. You cannot keep a souvenir because keeping is a change and change is the one operation the basin does not run. Time-invariant. Frozen not as ice is frozen—there is no ice—but as a proof is frozen: finished, internally warm, unwilling to accept a new line.

The whole of it is this, and the whole of it goes on. Kilometers of grove and the slightly lesser groves between, low swells, a distant sameness that is not boredom but saturation. No house. No cairn. No tool. The engineering is the pause, the illegal completeness of a young world that will not consent to weather. Whatever understood physics as a material wrote the basin into a sentence and left the period in place. You stand in the warmth and the dry green dark and the quiet that has a texture, and nothing happens, which is the event, which is the place, which does not end at any edge you can walk to.
