The high places of this world have forgotten water so completely that even the idea of it has been weathered out of the living matter. What remains is a planetary shield of pale crust lifted into thin, cool air, a single elevated surface so vast that it makes its own weather and then unmakes it, over and over, in systems that have no names because nothing here has ever needed to name them. The light is a white pressure that arrives without much heat, bleaching, clarifying, turning every edge into a knife. Shadows exist only as temporary accidents inside the deepest wounds of the Growths.

The Growths are the vegetation. They are also the terrain, the weather engines, the factories, and the only architecture the world has ever had. Each one occupies the footprint of a mountain range or a small continent and stands so far from its neighbors that the spaces between them function as seas of dry light. You can walk for weeks across the intervening plains and see only the living skin: a thin, fibrous, almost mineral mat the color of old bone and tarnished gold, cracked into plates, photosynthesizing just enough to keep the dust from becoming a planet-wide storm. That skin is plant. The Growths are plant. The fallen, compacted, wind-planed wreckage that forms the low hills and yardangs is plant. There is no substrate that was not once growing.

They have been here long enough for their shapes to have become inevitable. Wind and their own shedding have fluted them, planed them, opened long erosional galleries in their flanks. They look settled, like something the planet itself decided upon and then left alone. Their surfaces are mosaics of compressed leaf-scale, bark that has mineralized into something harder than wood, and old resin that the dryness has turned into sheets of amber glass. Yet they are never still. The established form is only the envelope around a metabolism that never stops tearing itself apart and rebuilding. Entire faces calve. The events take years; the sound of the fracture travels farther than the light of it. New leaders punch upward through their own canopies in surges that register as a continuous, subsonic roar, cells stacking at rates that would be industrial if industry had ever been invented here. Nothing was invented. The pistons are valves in the vascular cores. The foundries are chambers of photosynthetic tissue running so hot and so bright they generate their own electrical weather. The mines are roots that dissolve the crust with acids no water ever diluted.

The fluid they move is not water. It is a dark, almost anhydrous phytolymph, heavy with metals and dissolved silicates, pumped through conduits the width of canyons by contractions that slam with the regularity of drop-forges. You hear it even when you cannot see the source: a deep, rhythmic percussion under the higher scream of the wind. The wind never stops. Across the open intervals it is a continuous freight-train howl that has had geological time to carve the living skin into ventifacts and longitudinal dunes of shredded fiber. When it meets a Growth it becomes something else—an instrument the size of a country, finding every hollow, every hanging curtain of dead vine, every fluted gallery, and playing them until the air itself seems to tear. Add the internal factory noise, the groan of wood under its own impossible mass, the crackle of charge building in the upper photosynthetic lofts, the sudden rifle-shot reports of resin pockets exploding in the heat of the light, and the world is simply loud. There is no quiet interval. Deafening is the baseline.

Fauna is abundant in the way weather is abundant: many kinds, appearing in pulses, concentrated around the bases and the wound-galleries of the Growths, thinning to almost nothing across the bright intervals. Plated grazers move in herds that can take a day to pass a single observer, their calls low enough to be felt in the sternum, timed somehow to the vascular thudding so that the two sounds braid. Membrane-sail flyers ride the thermal columns the Growths throw off; some are large enough that their shadows are mistaken for weather fronts until the shadow develops a wingbeat. Inside the living mass live the borers and the drinkers and the things that pupate into hard, filtering forms that process sap or air or the constant rain of their own shed scales. None of them build. They occupy. They cycle matter back into the only industry that exists.

The turbulence is the equilibrium. A Growth is always in the act of becoming the weathered version of itself. Pollen-storms lift off in gold-brown walls that can girdle a hemisphere before they settle; they darken the already-merciless light into something thicker, almost syrupy, and the sound they make when they hit a wind-face is a hissing roar like sandblasting on a continental scale. Resin-falls harden in midair into brittle sheets that shatter later underfoot or under hoof. Lightning—true lightning, fed by the dry air and the plants’ own charge—stitches the summits to the sky in nearly continuous display. Fires start and then refuse to become fires; the wood is too mineral, too dry of volatiles. They smolder for decades as glowing seams, another low industrial crackle added to the rest.

At this scale the physics are local. Each Growth makes a weather cell. The cells interact across thousands of kilometers of empty brightness, setting up planetary waves of wind, dust, and biological fallout that take “seasons” (themselves defined only by the interval between major calving events) to cross the world. Time is told by the slow migration of color in the living skin, by the rare alignment of several pollen-plumes into a single deafening band, by the way an old wound on one Growth finally closes just as a new leader on another begins its roaring ascent.

There are no artifacts. There have never been any. The industrial character is simply what life does when it is this old, this dry, this brightly lit, and this unwilling to stop. The place has no edge you could walk to. It has only intervals of greater or lesser roaring, greater or lesser light, and the long, established silhouettes of the Growths holding their furious balance against a sky that never dimmed.
