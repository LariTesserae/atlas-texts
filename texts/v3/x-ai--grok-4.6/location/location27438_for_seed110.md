The way in is not a path. It is a seam you find with the back of a hand, a vertical wrinkle in the mountain’s hide that the wind does not know about and the sun has never entered. You turn sideways. Stone takes your shoulders. Warmth arrives before space does—not the heat of a fire or of open day, but the heat of something held, like a mouth closed on a breath. Then the seam widens by a handspan and you are inside a volume no larger than a kneeling room, and the dark is not absence so much as a substance that has always lived here.

This place has no sky. It has a ceiling you can touch if you stand, and a floor that is not quite floor: a skin of mineral paste over older rock, still plastic, still deciding. The mountain around it is high and old. This pocket is not. The stone here is embryonic. It weeps. Every surface sweats a film that is neither rain nor spring but the leftover liquor of rock that has not finished becoming rock. If you press a palm to the wall the wall gives a little, the way cooled wax gives, and your print remains a shallow basin that slowly, over a time you cannot measure, fills itself from within and forgets you.

The air is saturated past the point of fog. Water does not fall so much as occupy. It beads on your lashes, on the insides of your wrists, on the small hairs of your face. It condenses and uncondenses in the same instant. The temperature is constant and animal—warm enough that the wet never chills, never quite comforts. Breathing is drinking. After a few minutes the body stops distinguishing lung from skin.

There is almost no green. What grows does so by error: a few threads of something like moss, pale and fat, clinging where the weep is slowest, tasting of iron and sugar. They do not spread. They have been the same three patches for as long as the chamber has had a memory, which may be no time at all. Vegetation here is an afterthought the place has not agreed to.

Density is not of plants. It is of presences. The pocket is packed. Nascent mineral bodies crowd every plane—thousands of blunt buds, unopened stalactites the size of knuckles, plates of calcite that have begun a crystal and stopped, stacked so close they nearly touch. You cannot extend an arm without grazing a field of them. They are warm. They have the texture of teeth before enamel. Between them the water hangs in skins and strings. Sound, when it happens, is small and private: a single detachment of a drop that does not splash so much as join. Long quiet after. Then another, from a different throat of stone, as if the room were remembering a drip rather than performing one.

Someone was here. Not often, and not recently in any way the dark keeps. Against the back wall, where the ceiling lowers, three stones have been stacked into a seat or an altar or a mistake. The stacking is careful and unskilled. Beside it a depression has been worked into the soft floor with something like a scapula or a shard of harder rock brought in from outside—an oval basin, thumb-smoothed at the rim, always full, always the same temperature as the blood. A length of fiber, plant or hair, lies half-swallowed by the mineral skin; it has not rotted and it has not been preserved. It simply continues. Near the entrance seam, at shin height, four parallel scratches mark the stone. They are the depth of a fingernail. They do not weather. Nothing here weathers. Erosion has not been invented yet. The geology is still in its first draft, and the draft has been left on the table.

A creature lives in the basin or visits it. You will not see it. Once in a long listening you hear a minute rearrangement of water, a body no larger than a thumb turning over in its own damp. It does not leave. It may be the same turning, again and again. Fauna is a rumor the room tells itself.

The unusual thing is not the dark or the wet or the unfinished stone. It is the equilibrium. Everything in the pocket is mid-gesture and will not complete. Crystals do not lengthen. Drips do not carve. The stacked stones do not slump. The moss does not die or fruit. Time is present the way humidity is present—uniform, without gradient, without weather. If you stay, your own processes begin to agree. Hunger flattens. The need to leave becomes theoretical. The dark does not deepen or lift. It has no edges to get used to.

Rules, such as they are: light is a foreign element and will not take. A spark brought in dies of embarrassment. Sound does not echo; the packed buds drink it. What you set down is incorporated, slowly, into the mineral film, and remains exactly as you left it, slightly more stone than before. The mountain around this room continues its ordinary life of wind and ice and thinning air. This room does not know that mountain. It is a held breath inside a held breath, high up, sealed, warm, and finished with the idea of change.

You find the seam again by heat and by the slight cooling of the air as the inner wet thins. Outside, if there is an outside, you will not be able to say how long you were gone. The pocket does not keep that kind of account. It keeps only the weight of water on unborn rock, and the quiet in which that weight is always, already, enough.
