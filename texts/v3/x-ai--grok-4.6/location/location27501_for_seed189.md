The chamber opened last night, or what passes for night in a place that has never seen a sky. It is no larger than a storeroom, a single held breath of space folded into the living mass that is everything else. You could touch both walls if the walls would stay still. They do not. Distances here refuse to agree with themselves; a step that should cross the floor instead carries you along a slow curve that never quite returns, so that the same bead of sap hanging from the ceiling appears first above you, then beside you, then receding as if the pocket has decided it is a canyon after all. The emptiness is genuine: almost nothing occupies the volume. A few pale, unfinished roots hang like cut strings and stop well short of the floor. Between them the air is simply air, warm and faintly sweet, stretching farther than the chamber’s skin should allow.

The walls are plant. Not lined with plant, not grown over stone: they *are* the plant, a single continuous body of living tissue that has only just torn itself a hollow. The tear is young. Edges stand knife-sharp where xylem and phloem were sheared apart; the cut surfaces still glisten, leaking a thin amber fluid that never quite drips. It beads, stretches, then withdraws as if the wound is already reconsidering. New cambium pushes out in translucent blades, glassy and serrated, catching the light that has no business being here. The light is ordinary daylight, green-gold and even, though there is no sun and no opening to any surface. It simply *is*, saturating the pith the way noon saturates a meadow, so that every fresh rupture throws a small, hard shadow.

Moisture lives only as a film. Condensation forms on the cooler faces of the new growth, gathers, and slides in hesitant threads that never become streams. Sometimes a larger droplet detaches and falls the impossible distance to the floor with a sound far too loud, a single wet clap that the chamber repeats in voices that are not echoes. The floor itself is a shallow basin of compressed cork and living bark, still warm from the metabolic surge that birthed the space. It yields slightly, then firms, as though the plant is deciding whether this cavity will be kept. Occasional rain is only the memory of rain: a brief, localized weeping from a ruptured duct high in the wall, a handful of warm drops that darken the pith and are reabsorbed before they can pool.

Sound is constant and disproportionate. Cell walls snap as they elongate; the noise is like dry twigs underfoot, but magnified until it fills the pocket. Vascular fluids rush in sudden pulses, a wet thunder that comes from inside the walls and from nowhere. The hanging roots, when they twitch (they twitch), produce a high, fibrous creak that the stretched geometry turns into a chorus. There is no wind, yet something like wind moves: pressure differentials born of the plant’s own breathing, warm exhalations that smell of crushed chlorophyll and old sugar. At irregular intervals the entire chamber flexes, a slow peristalsis that changes the angles of every surface at once. Features that were sharp become sharper; a ridge of new wood erupts along what had been a smooth plane, then subsides as if embarrassed.

Nothing else lives here yet. Fauna is a rumor, a single dark fleck that may have been an insect the size of a thumbnail, already gone, or perhaps only a shadow cast by a leaf that does not exist. The ecology is the plant talking to itself. Different tissues argue: a patch of what might once have been leaf, now grown monstrous and pale, photosynthesizes the sourceless light and feeds sugars back into the walls; a knot of root-hair tries to drink the condensation and finds only itself. Growth is frantic and local. In the time it takes to look away and look back, a spray of needle-leaves can appear, fully formed, then dissolve into the parent mass again. The plant is still deciding the rules of this new interior.

Reality does not hold. The chamber is below the surface of a world that is itself only plant, an endless interlocking body with no soil, no bedrock, only older and older layers of living matter compressing downward. This pocket is a recent error, or a recent thought. Geometry folds. A root that ends in mid-air continues, unseen, and re-enters from another wall. Time stutters; the same droplet forms, falls, and is caught by a leaf that was not there a moment earlier, over and over, each repetition slightly louder. Occasionally the light itself thickens, becoming briefly viscous, so that motes of pollen (there is no pollen) hang as if in amber before the air remembers how to be air. The plant does not notice, or notices in a way that has nothing to do with minds. It simply continues the work of being everything, and this small, loud, empty wound is only the latest clause in a sentence that has no period.

There are no tools here, no marks that were not grown. If anything once passed through with a sharpened stick or a flake of stone, the tissue has already closed over the memory. The place remains untouched because it has not yet finished becoming a place. It pulses, drips, creaks, and quietly refuses to stay the size it is. Stand still long enough and the floor will lift you a finger’s width, or the ceiling will lower until the hanging roots brush your hair, or the whole cavity will turn inside-out for the space of a breath and you will be standing on what was the wall, looking “down” into a green-gold emptiness that has no bottom and no top, only the same young, sharp, living surfaces receding in every direction at once. Then it will fold shut again, a pocket once more, intimate and hollow, waiting to see what it will decide to be next.
