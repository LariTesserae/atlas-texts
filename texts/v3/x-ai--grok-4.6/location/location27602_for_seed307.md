The pocket is smaller than a room and higher than sense. You do not arrive at it so much as fail to leave the mountain’s newest skin. A week ago—ten days, a month, the rock will not commit—this ridge was still a decision in the mantle. It came up fast and unfinished, a blade of glass and sulfur-salt that has not yet learned weather. Edges are still bright. Fracture faces still remember the tear. There is no soil because there has not been time for soil. There is no path because nothing has needed one.

Inside the spine, where a gas blister failed to become a crater and instead became a lung, the heat has nowhere to go but stay. The stone radiates as if it were still deciding whether to be liquid. Touch is theoretical. Water, forced up through hairline vents that did not exist last season, finds the pocket and does not know how to leave. It weeps from every plane. It beads, runs, flashes to steam where the wall is hottest, and condenses again on the cooler teeth of the ceiling, so the air is a standing rain that never quite falls. You breathe soup. The soup is almost boiling. Light comes in through a single knife-cut in the roof, ordinary daylight made thick by vapor, and the wet rock takes that light and returns it as a dull mineral glare—no glow of magic, no underground dark, just noon strained through a fever.

Nothing green has ever tried this place. What grows is chemistry remembering the shape of hunger. Hair-fine gardens of silica and iron climb the weeping planes in branching tubes the color of rusted honey and dirty turquoise. They are not plants. They snap if the temperature pulses the wrong way. A smear of something more like a stain than an organism films the cooler runnels—an iridescent grease that creeps a finger’s width in an hour and dies where the rock goes white-hot. That is the whole of the flora: precipitate and smear. The rest is mineral and appetite.

The appetite is the problem. The pocket is crowded past honesty.

At first the crowding looks like geology. The floor is cobbled with nodules, the walls studded, the steam-channels lined with pale, overlapping plates. Then a nodule flexes. Then a plate lifts a rim and sets it down a breath to the left, and you understand that you have been standing in a congregation. They are not large. Most could sit in a palm if a palm could bear the heat. They are too many. They occupy the puddles, the vertical films, the undersides of ledges, the interiors of one another’s cooling shadows. They press without quite touching, a dense packing of bodies that leaves almost no unused volume and yet never quite jams, as if the pocket has more inside than its walls should permit.

They make almost no sound. That is the first wrongness you can name. Overwhelming life should tick, rasp, hiss, quarrel. Here the loudest events are the ones the mountain itself supplies: a rare, fat drip; the silk-tear of a steam veil forming; once in a long while a grain of new glass letting go from the ceiling and ticking once on a shell. The creatures move in the condensate, under it, as if the water-skin were their true air. When they do cross open vapor they do not displace it correctly. Heat-shimmer swallows the gesture. You see a shape leave one stone and arrive on another without the middle of the journey agreeing to be watched.

Look longer and the count goes bad.

There are more of them in the same volume than the first looking allowed. Not hidden behind—additional. A second population occupying the same wet facets, offset by a thickness that is not space. Some are slow armored things, like limpets poured from dirty amber, rasping the chemical gardens down to wet glitter. Some are jointed too many times, pale and translucent, their interiors a slow traffic of darker beads; they travel by choosing which of their angles will be the ones that exist this breath. Some are almost only mouths and a trailing veil of mineral lace, grazing the iron smear. They do not eat one another in any way you can catch. They eat gradient: the difference between a hotter plane and a cooler runnel, the moment a dissolved metal falls out of solution, the thin shock when steam collapses back to water. The pocket is a pantry of differences, and they have filled it the way bees fill a comb, except the comb is also the bees, and also the honey, and also a second comb you did not agree to.

A few use objects. This is the only hint of anything like craft, and it is barely that. A creature will lift a fresh flake of volcanic glass—edges still molecularly cruel, born this morning when the ridge shrugged—and scrape a richer vein of precipitate, or wedge the flake to hold open a seep so the mineral film blooms thicker on the downhill side. The flake is dropped when the work is done. Nothing is kept. Nothing is stacked into shelter. The mountain is the only architecture, and it is still being poured. Tools appear as a spasm of attention and vanish back into scree. If this is technology it is the kind that has not yet decided to outlive the hand.

The silence is not empty. It is padded. Sound that should ring off wet stone arrives late or not at all, as if the extra population were a felt. Sometimes you hear a motion after you see it. Sometimes you hear a drip that does not correspond to any falling drop, a small permission the pocket grants the idea of water. Breathing feels loud and then, a moment later, as if it happened in another room.

Rules assert themselves without announcing a legislator.

Heat is the medium, not the weather. Everything here is a negotiation with a temperature that would cook recognized flesh. The creatures’ surfaces are wet-stone, wet-glass, wet-sulfur; they shine because they must continually shed a skin of boiling film. If the roof-cut clouds over and the light thins, they do not care. If a new vent opens and a wall goes from weeping to screaming steam, a whole layer of them simply ceases on that plane and the neighboring bodies close the gap without hurry, the way water closes. Death is a change of phase. The dead become more of the chemical garden by evening.

The pocket is still rising. You feel it in the teeth: a slow, irregular lift, the ridge adding itself. Fresh cracks brighten. A new seep starts, sterile and furious, and within minutes the first grazers have arrived as if they had been waiting in the rock. Perhaps they had. Perhaps they are a property of this particular heat and wet, precipitated the way the silica tubes are, biology as a mineral habit that learned to walk. The mountain is young enough that the difference between lava and animal has not been fully litigated.

Time inside does not keep good accounts. The steam cycle has a pulse—build, veil, collapse, weep—roughly the length of a held breath, then another, then a longer stillness while pressure finds a new hairline. In those stillnesses the second population is easiest to see. They stand a little aside from the first, occupying the same ledges the way a reflection occupies a lake, except the lake is also standing on the reflection. If you try to decide which set is real, the light from the roof-cut doubles once, very slightly, as if noon had a twin that almost coincided. The stone does not break. Reality only thins, the way metal thins when it is asked to be foil. You can still leave. The knife-cut is still a knife-cut. But while you are here the pocket contains more event than event should fit, and the extra is alive, and the alive is quiet, and the quiet is hot and wet and newly made.

Nothing outside knows this room. Clouds tear on the ridge and do not look down. Wind at this elevation is a blade and does not enter. There are no ruins because there has never been a reason to build, and no future structures implied. The only history is the cooling. The only future is more cooling, or a last failure of the blister, a cough that opens the pocket to the sky and lets the thin upper air in. Then the water would flash away, the smear would bake to a glaze, the creatures—both sets, or however many sets there actually are—would become a crust of odd nodules for some later weather to misunderstand.

Until then the place keeps working. Grazers rasp. Flakes are lifted and discarded. Steam writes and erases itself on the ceiling. The chemical gardens branch toward a light they do not use. A new animal arrives out of a fresh crack already knowing the rules. The silence holds them all, densely, almost touching, a hidden fever at the top of a mountain that has not finished being born.
