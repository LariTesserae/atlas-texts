The light has no source and no direction. It wells equally from every plane of the made world, a warm, even radiance the color of old ivory left too long in a kiln, so complete that shadow is only a rumor of lesser brightness. You stand on a surface that was never earth. It is a single continuous artifact, planetary in extent, worn by durations that have no name left into a topography of almost nothing: low fused swells no higher than a walking body, shallow troughs that wander for days before shallowing further into the same pale, pitted laminate. Elevation has been answered. What remains is the settled geometry after every tower, every wall, every sharp intention has been surrendered to time.

The material itself is neither ceramic nor metal nor glass, though it remembers all three. It is a collapsed laminate of photonic lattices and structural foams that long ago fused into one substance, now the color of bone and tarnished brass, microscopically pitted, macroscopically stable. Touch it and it is warm, always warm, holding a heat that never quite becomes uncomfortable. Fine grit—the last powdered remnants of higher structures—lies in the troughs like dry snow that will never melt. There is no water. Not a film, not a breath of humidity. Whatever volatiles once circulated are sealed in conduits kilometers down or locked into the chemistry of the dust itself. The air tastes of that dust and of something faintly electric, like the inside of a vast machine that finished its work and then simply continued to exist.

Because the world is one construction, density is not optional. The surface you walk is only the uppermost compressed page of a palimpsest that goes down without measured end. City has been laid on city, machine on machine, each generation of making settling into the last until the distinction between architecture and geology vanished. Passages too narrow for any body still thrum with lattices that have been running since the world was young. Chambers the size of rooms or of continents remain packed with engines whose purpose has outlasted language. You feel the saturation through your feet: a solid of artifacts, near-touching, high-saturation, no empty volume that was not itself designed and then filled. The erosion that flattened the heights also compacted the depths into their final, oldest-stable state. Nothing more will fall. Everything that could be made was made, and then time was allowed to finish the sentence.

Weather here is thermal and luminous. Slow tides of heat rise from the glowing substrate and organize themselves, over years, into planetary gyres. In their still centers the light thickens until it seems almost pourable, a denser gold. These circulations move the grit in sheets so thin they make no sound. Sometimes a gyre will stall for a decade and a region will bloom cooler, a faint bluish welling that travelers—if any still travel—use as a landmark that will not last. There are no clouds. There is no rain. The only storms are the rare, silent intensifications of light itself, when some buried network routes energy through forgotten paths and a thousand square kilometers brighten by a fraction, then dim again. The cycles are active, multiple, ceaseless, yet they proceed at a pace that feels geological rather than mechanical. The world is not dying. It is simply continuing, every process having found the rhythm that can be sustained forever.

Vegetation exists as an afterthought of the original design, or perhaps as a later infection of it. In the lowest troughs, where some residual process still concentrates trace energies, thin glassy stalks stand in clusters of three or five. They are neither grown nor built in any familiar sense; their surfaces iridesce faintly as they convert the ambient radiance. They do not spread. They do not compete. A cluster may persist for centuries and then simply cease, leaving a scatter of brittle fragments that the grit slowly buries. No forests, no even groves. The stalks are punctuation in an otherwise uninterrupted sentence of made ground.

What moves is rarer still. Once in a great while, at distances that defeat the eye, a low multi-limbed form of the same pale laminate crawls a seam. It may be a maintenance remnant, or the remnant of a remnant, performing a repair whose necessity has become abstract. It does not hunt. It does not flee. It is seen, if at all, and then the seeing is not repeated. The ecology of this place is one of endurance and gradient, not consumption. Energy flows; the rare motile things follow or ignore it according to programs that have outlasted their authors. There is no predator, no prey, only the slow redistribution of what the world still generates.

Sound is almost absent. The air is too dry, the surfaces too ancient and too complete, to carry much. Lie against a plate and you may feel, more than hear, a vibration like blood in the ear—the distant turning of some deep cycle. The grit, when the thermal tides strengthen, slides without rustle. Silence is the ground state; any noise is an event and events are few. Even the light, for all its intensity, is mute.

The rules are the rules of a finished making. Gravity holds. Things fall, dust settles, the low swells remain low. But distance deceives because variation is so slight, and time itself feels thickened, as if the tech that still undergirds everything has given duration a viscosity. The light does not cast true shadow; orientation becomes a matter of remembering which way the last cooler welling lay. One can walk for what would be weeks on another world and find only more of the same worn language, until a difference appears: a place where the plates have buckled a hand’s-breadth higher, or a depression where the radiance is faintly cooler. These are the world’s mountains and rivers now. They are enough. The place has weather of its own, seasons of light and heat that last years, physics scaled to its own vast, quiet, brightly-lit persistence.

It is the end-state of construction, the oldest stable form a fully-made world can take. Everything is artifact, and the artifacts have become the only possible landscape. Warm, dry, shadowless, packed with the memory and the continuation of making, it simply goes on, horizon after horizon of pale, glowing, nearly silent ground.
