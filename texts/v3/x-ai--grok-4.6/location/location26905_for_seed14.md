The basin has no name that has survived it. It sits beneath everything that still has weather, a long oval depression worn so far past mountains that the idea of a mountain here is a kind of insult. What remains is a floor: dark, nearly level, glazed in places with a skin of clay so fine it behaves like oil. You feel the slope only as a rumor in the ankles, a tilt of maybe a degree over a kilometer, the last argument the rock was able to keep.

You are already below the surface when you arrive. There is a surface somewhere far above, a lid of colder, thinner water and perhaps ice, but it does not belong to this place. This place begins where light has already failed and the pressure has settled into a constant, impartial weight. The water is not clear. It has not been clear in a geological age. It is packed — not crowded with creatures, not forested, packed the way a lung is packed with air, the way a millstone’s dust is packed into the cracks of the mill. Rock flour, glacial silt, the powdered bones of ranges that finished dying before anything with a spine learned to swim. You move through it the way a hand moves through wet sand at the tide line, except there is no tide line and the sand does not end. Grain touches grain. There is no empty interval. Density here is not a crowd; it is a medium.

Cold lives in it the way rust lives in iron. Not the theatrical cold of a blizzard. A settled cold, 0.5 on any honest scale, the temperature of a thing that has forgotten it was ever warm. It does not bite so much as occupy. Fingers go stupid. Thought slows to the speed of the silt.

And yet nothing is still.

The contradiction is the place. The stone finished changing a long time ago. It reached its final form and then kept existing out of habit. But the water never signed that treaty. Currents arrive from directions that do not exist on any chart — density tongues, ice-laden plumes that calve from some unseen shelf and sink like slow landslides, overturnings that start in the dark and run for days. A turbidity storm can last a week and leave the floor looking, to any instrument that could see, exactly as it looked before. There is nothing left to rearrange. The chaos spends itself on a surface already ground to its last possible sentence. You feel it more than you hear it: a pressure that walks across your ribs, a sideways shove that has no wind in it, the sudden grit of a newer, coarser silt against the teeth if you are foolish enough to open your mouth. Sound itself is a minority event. Once in a long while a groan travels through the packed water — ice speaking to ice, or rock remembering it has joints — and then the quiet closes again, thick as felt.

If there is a horizon it is made of the same darkness you are standing in. Scale here is open in the way a closed room is open if the walls are far enough that you cannot prove them. You can walk, or drift, for a long time and the floor will keep offering the same nearly-featureless proposition: a pale ridge no higher than a sleeping animal, the ghost of a channel, a scatter of cobbles worn so spherical they might have been conceived that way. Multi-feature, if you are patient. A valley’s worth of almost-nothing, repeating.

Vegetation is a rumor with poor evidence. In the lee of a cobble, sometimes, a film. Bacterial, probably. The color of old bruise if there were light to give it color. It does not grow so much as persist. Farther on, a single pale stalk, leafless, anchored in the clay and leaning forever in the prevailing shove. That is the forest. You could count the living green of this basin on the hands of a person who has lost fingers.

Fauna is rarer still and does not stay to be known. Something pale and segmented writes a brief signature in the silt and is gone. Once, at the edge of what you are willing to call vision, a larger darkness that is not the water — a body with purpose, or the memory of purpose, passing through and taking its cold with it. No schools. No gardens of feeding. The ecology is an afterthought the basin has not had the energy to cancel.

There are almost no made things. Almost. In one shallow scrape, where a current has peeled the clay, three flakes of stone lie in a cluster that is slightly too intentional. Edges still catch, just. Someone — a hand, a very old hand, working without metal — left them, or lost them, and the basin kept them the way it keeps everything, by failing to destroy them quite completely. That is the entire city. That is the entire age of tools. You could miss them and be right to.

Light is a theory. What little arrives has been filtered through a column of flour and ice and has given up its names on the way down. You navigate by touch, by the grain of the silt changing against the skin, by the way a current announces a rise you cannot see. If there is bioluminescence it is as rare as the fauna and as brief: a single coin of green, spent, gone.

The rules are simple and have no authors. What is heavy sinks. What is finer stays up longer and then also sinks. The water will always find a way to move because the temperature above is not the temperature here and the ice is always calving somewhere and the basin is a bowl that cannot help receiving. The rock will not become mountains again. The silt will not become soil. This is not a dying place so much as a place that finished its dying and then discovered that finishing is not the same as stopping. The turbulence is not weather visiting. It is the last habit of a world that has already spent its shapes.

Stay long enough and the packed dark begins to have texture you can almost trust — a coarseness that means a recent slide, a silk that means the storm has gone quiet for an hour, a sudden emptiness no larger than a fist where two currents parted and have not yet filled the seam. You put your hand into that seam and for a moment there is room. Then the basin remembers itself, and the room is gone, and you are inside the ancient, working dark again, being rewritten without being changed.
