The dark here is not night. Night is a temporary arrangement. This is a standing condition, a climate of unlight that has settled over a low, newly-made country and decided to stay. Heat holds in it the way heat holds in a closed room after the stove has been banked. The air is wet enough to drink if you walk with your mouth open. It condenses on everything that is cooler than itself, which is almost nothing; the rocks weep anyway, as if from habit, and the metal weeps a slower, oilier water.

What has come up from below is young. You can feel the youth in the edges. Banks of slag and poured stone still hold the pour-marks, the cooling cracks, the unfinished angles. Nothing has been rounded yet. The ground is a sequence of abrupt decisions: a shelf, a drop of half a body-length, a fan of sharp rubble that has not learned to be a beach. Water stands in all the low places and also in places that should not be low. It is never deep in the way a river is deep. It is a continuous shallows, a skin of warm black that the land has not finished deciding whether to wear or shed. When you step, the bottom is sometimes mud, sometimes grated iron, sometimes a surface that gives like cartilage and then remembers itself.

There is almost no green. What grows does so in clauses, not sentences: a stand of pale, jointed stalks in a rust-channel; a mat of something that might be moss if moss were made of wet felt and did not care about light; a single tree, if it is a tree, whose bark is already peeling in long industrial sheets. The rest of the space is occupied. Density here is not canopy. It is occupancy. Bodies and housings and remnants stand close enough that the air between them is a third material. You move by turning sideways, by lifting a foot higher than seems reasonable, by learning the intervals.

The factories are still working, after a fashion. They are scattered the way islands are scattered in a wide lake: a brick stack, a long shed on stilts, a constellation of tanks whose rivets you can read with your palm. They do not blaze. They do not clang. What they do is slower than weather. A belt will advance one plate in the time it takes a person to forget why they came. A press will close over the course of a season and open over the course of another. The products, when they appear, are already old. Sometimes they are objects that have no name in any inventory: a coil that is also a corridor; a gear whose teeth are doors; a length of cable that is warm and slightly muscular. The electricity, if it is electricity, moves like groundwater. You feel it more as a change in the taste of the air than as a spark.

Animals are the other occupation. They are not secretive. In a place without light there is no cover and no exposure, only nearness. Things with too many joints pick their way along the waterline, tasting the metal with mouths that are also feet. Pale crabs the size of helmets graze the condensing films. Something large and patient occupies the deeper channels; you know it by the way the water refuses to quite settle, by a warmth that is not the ambient warmth. Colonies of soft, clicking lives hold to the undersides of walkways and to one another. Wings exist but they are used for feeling, not for going. The fauna do not startle. Startling is a luxury of places with suddenness. Here everything has already arrived, or is arriving at a rate too gradual to constitute an event. They eat what the factories leave, and what the water precipitates, and occasionally one another, without hurry and without much sound.

Sound itself is a minority presence. Drip. The small collapse of a mud-lip somewhere far off. A single piston acknowledging its duty after a long consideration. Once in a great while a cry that might be an animal or a pressure-release, already finished by the time it reaches you. The quiet is not empty. It is loaded, like a held breath that has been held so long it has become a kind of weather.

The rules of the place are patient and slightly wrong. Distances do not stay agreed-upon. A shed you have been walking toward for what feels like an hour is suddenly at your shoulder, sweating, and the water around its pilings is showing a sky that is not this sky — a paler dark, pricked with things that might be stars or might be rivets seen from very far inside. Reflections in the standing water sometimes precede the thing they reflect. Sometimes they refuse to. Names for directions last only as long as you keep repeating them under your breath; stop, and the basin rearranges its loyalties. The young land is still negotiating with itself. A ridge that was slag yesterday is, this long dusk, a spine of something that wants to be geology and has not yet been refused. The darkness has thickness. You can put a hand into it and feel resistance, like wading. Deeper in, there are rooms that were never built, and in those rooms the same slow engines, the same warm water, the same animals that do not need to see you.

Nothing here is finished. That is the youth of it, and also the breakdown. The marsh-edge keeps being an edge of more than marsh. The heat does not break. The factories do not quite stop. The animals continue their uncounted work of being many. If there is a cycle it is longer than attention. You could stand in one place until your own heat matched the air and the weeping of the rocks became indistinguishable from your own, and the country would still be in the middle of becoming whatever it is becoming, in the dark, without any particular need to be seen.
