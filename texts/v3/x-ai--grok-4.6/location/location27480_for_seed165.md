The high places do not recede. They simply continue, ridge behind ridge, each one the same age as the last, worn to the same tired angle, as if the weather finished its argument a long time ago and left the evidence standing. You are already above the last real cloud. What hangs in the air now is not weather. It is a dimness with weight, the color of wet slate and old bruise, a light that has no hour and does not intend to acquire one. Cold lives in the stone more than in the air. It is a settled cold, the kind that has nowhere left to go.

The sound is there before the view finishes arriving. It is loud in the way a waterfall is loud when you have already been standing beside it for a year: not a surprise, not an event, just the continuous fact of the place. It has no gust, no lull, no grain of change. A roar with the patience of geology. Some of it seems to come from the long dry trenches that score the basin floor, polished grooves that remember water the way a scar remembers a blade. Some of it seems to come from the air itself, which is thin enough to taste of metal and condensation. Most of it has no direction. It occupies. Your ribs learn the frequency and keep it.

This is a basin the size of a forgotten district, high enough that the horizon is a ring of worn teeth and the sky sits close, a lid of darker stone. Distances here are open and also slightly wrong. You can see a far saddle, a pale fan of old talus, a single dark seam of seepage shining on a cliff, and the interval between them does not add the way walking should add. The space is empty in the strict sense: almost nothing per volume. A boulder. Another boulder a long way off. A low black shrub like a clot of iron wire, then nothing again for a long time. The emptiness is not waiting to be filled. It is the finished condition.

Moisture is intermittent and oddly local, as if the idea of wetness had been broken into pockets and left where it fell. In the lee of certain stones there are beads of water that do not run. They swell and hold, bright in the dimness, each one a small unspilled decision. Rime feathers the northern faces of the ridges and never thickens, never thins. A thread of a river exists in pieces: a dark slick for twenty paces, then a clean dry channel of basalt, then another slick, as though the water were only permitted in certain clauses of the landscape. Where it does appear it is very cold and faintly sweet, more condensation than rain, more seep than flow. You can smell it only when you are already kneeling.

Vegetation is an afterthought the stone has almost edited out. Lichen maps the older faces in rust and ash-green, so thin it might be stain. Here and there a cushion of something like moss, blackened, brittle, clinging to a crack that holds a teaspoon of damp. No trees. No grass worth the name. The few shrubs do not lean with any wind, because there is no wind that changes. They have the look of things arranged once and then forgotten by the arranger.

Erosion has the quality of a completed sentence. The ridges are not sharp. They are not crumbling. They have the long, accepted profiles of things that were ground down until the grinding and the ground agreed. Scree slopes rest at their angle of repose as if they have been resting there since the idea of repose was invented. Nothing new is being made. Nothing old is being unmade. The basin is in its own state, weathered into permanence, a high desert of stone that has finished becoming itself.

Scattered through this are marks that are almost buildings and almost not. A doorway stands on a slight rise, two uprights and a lintel of the same local rock, framing a rectangle of sky that is a different dimness from the sky around it, as though the door still remembered a room. No walls. No floor. Just the idea of passage, worn, lichened, older than any habit of people. Farther on, a spiral is cut into a cliff—or grows in the cliff—the groove as weathered as the uncut stone, so you cannot tell incision from anatomy. Follow it with your eye and the center refuses to be where the curve says it should be. In another place, flat stones are stacked in a leaning cairn that does not quite obey down. The lean is slight and constant. It has always been that lean. There are no tools among them, no metal, no cut that looks younger than the mountain. Whatever left these things left them the way a tide leaves salt: as a residue of an event that is no longer occurring.

Fauna is rare enough to feel like a rumor the land is still deciding whether to keep. Once, at a distance that might be a mile or a trick, something stands on a rib of rock. It has the general suggestion of a goat or an antelope, but the legs articulate one extra time, a quiet surplus of joint, and it does not move. It was not moving when you first noticed it. It will not be moving later. Closer in, if you are unlucky or patient, you may find a pale shape in a seep-pool that is not quite a fish and not quite a lung, holding still in water that does not ripple. Birds exist as pinned shadows against the dim lid of sky, wings open, never traveling. The ecology here is a set of exceptions, widely spaced, each one as unchanging as the stone that hosts it.

The temperature does not trend. It is simply cold, a high thin cold that has soaked into everything and then stopped adjusting. Breath is a brief ghost that does not blow away so much as thin and remain, a little flag of white that forgets to leave. Fingers ache in a steady register. There is no warmer slope, no colder hollow that is colder for a reason you can walk into. The cold is evenly distributed, like the sound, like the dimness.

And the dimness never lifts. There is no sun-disk to find, only a general pallor that might be coming from the ice-rime, or from the seeps, or from the sky’s underside, which sometimes looks less like air than like the inside of a vast mineral. Shadows are long and indecisive. Some of them fall in directions that do not match the others. A few shadows stand slightly off their objects, as if they arrived first and the stones have not yet caught up. In the deepest cuts of the basin the dark is thicker, almost viscous, and the loudness in those cuts is the same loudness, neither muffled nor increased. Equilibrium includes the light. Equilibrium includes the roar.

The weirdness is not an event that visits. It is the climate of the place, the way the world here has already finished breaking and then held the break. Reality did not shatter so much as seize. You notice it first as a delay in your own depth perception: a far ridge is also immediately under your hand, the grain of its lichen suddenly readable, and then it is far again, without having moved. Seams appear where two slopes meet, hairline and luminous, and in the seams a second basin shows, equally still, equally loud, perhaps colder, its doorway standing in a slightly different place. Distances refuse arithmetic. You pass the same weathered pillar twice on a straight line. The pillar does not mind. Time here is a surface. Frost can be seen on it. A bead of water hangs and hanging is all it will ever do; the sound it would make if it fell is already included in the roar, prepaid, permanent.

There are places where the ground is both ground and a kind of slow glass, and through it you can see the same landscape stacked a fraction off, the same empty intervals, the same rare unmoving animal, the same door. Not a reflection. A persistence. The breakdown has been incorporated the way erosion was incorporated: thoroughly, a long time ago, until it became the settled form. Nothing here is in the process of becoming stranger. The strangeness is the bedrock.

If you shout, the shout is taken into the existing loudness and does not come back as echo so much as as confirmation. The place was already saying that. Your voice is a brief bright thread in a cloth that does not unravel. Footprints, if the dust allows them, look as new a year later and also look as if they had always been about to be there. The dust itself seems decided. Even the condensation has the air of a held note.

Walk long enough—though long enough is a phrase the basin treats as decorative—and the features repeat without repeating: another seep, another leaning stack, another spiral whose center will not sit, another stretch of dry river that is wet only in its permitted clauses. The scale is regional and also recursive. You are in a valley-sized openness that contains more openness than its ridges should enclose. The emptiness is consistent. The cold is consistent. The dim roar does not learn your name and does not forget you. It has no need of either.

This is not a landscape waiting for a season. It is a high, damp-cold, nearly vacant equilibrium in which the world came apart just far enough and then stopped, loudly, forever, and weathered itself smooth around the stop. The doorways frame what they frame. The water does not choose to fall. The extra-jointed thing on the far rib of rock continues not to move. You can leave. The place will not notice the difference, because noticing would be a change, and change is the one erosion this height no longer permits.
