The warmth never arrives from anywhere. It is simply the condition of the rock, held and radiated in every direction at once, a low even fever that has no source and no relief. You feel it first in the sinuses, a dry pressure, then in the joints, as if the bones themselves have been asked to remember a fire they never stood near. There is no moisture to carry the heat differently; the air is a fine abrasive powder that tastes of iron filings and extinguished lamps. It does not move. Stratified layers of it sit one atop another like invisible sediments, so still that a raised hand leaves a slowly collapsing tunnel that may persist for hours.

This is not a cave. Caves imply an elsewhere, a surface that was left behind. Here the below is the entire country. Galleries open into plains of fractured mineral that recede past any ordinary horizon; the stone sky overhead sometimes hangs so far above that it becomes a second, darker geology, pocked with its own hanging forests of crystals. Then the space pinches without warning into slot canyons barely wider than a body, their walls a chaos of unsmoothed blades and rhombs, every edge still bright with the violence of its making. The formation is young in every surface you touch—fresh conchoidal fractures, hexagonal columns that look poured an hour ago—yet nothing further happens. No settling, no creep, no new growth. The sharpness is permanent, locked the moment the last plane finished knitting itself into the next.

Density is the true climate. Pillars and false floors and intergrown sheets occupy the volume until walking becomes a negotiation of apertures. You pass through forests of translucent spikes that ring once, faintly, if struck, then fall silent as if embarrassed. Shelves of paler mineral stack so tightly that one chamber’s floor is another’s ceiling, and the heat leaking upward through the thin plates is the closest thing the place possesses to weather. Life occupies every leftover interval. It is abundant the way grit is abundant. Pale, mineral-colored things the size of fists graze the dust with rasping mouthparts, their paths so perfectly recurrent that the same individuals (or their exact successors) occupy the same coordinates year after uncounted year. Thread-fine swarms hang in the still air like a second, breathing dust, filtering whatever sparse gradients of chemistry or residual heat still exist. Larger, slower bodies—slug-analogues the color of rusted salt—secrete precise acids that dissolve only selected veins, leaving the surrounding architecture untouched. They do not collide. The packing is too exact, a living close-work that fills space the way crystals fill a geode.

Almost none of it makes sound. A rasp, occasionally, like a fingernail drawn across unglazed pot, but the dry air and the deadening stone swallow even that within a few body-lengths. The quiet is not absence; it is a medium, thick and warm, in which the rare primitive artifact stands out like a shout that never arrived. Here and there: a low ring of fitted salt-blocks no higher than the knee, empty, its joints too regular to be accidental. A shallow triple-score on a wall, cut with a flake of something harder than the local matrix. A small cairn whose planes have been rotated a few degrees off the natural cleavage, enough to declare intention. They are infrequent. A long traverse might yield two, or none. The craft is early—pressure-flaked edges, acid-etched grooves that suggest the larger fauna were once used as living tools—and then it stops, as everything here stops. No elaboration, no second generation of building. The marks remain exactly as left, uneroded, unburied.

Light is a failed rumor. What little persists comes from stubborn, useless sources: a dull internal orange along certain faults where the warmth is infinitesimally greater, a rare mineral that continues a phosphorescence begun when the chambers first locked, the occasional pale organ on a creature that has no use for eyes. Most of the volume is a mineral dark that does not merely lack photons but seems to drink them. Vision is nearly ornamental. The fauna navigate by heat-shadow, by chemical memory written on the still air, by the faint seismic gossip of one another’s rasps traveling through the stone. You learn to do the same, or you remain a tourist in a country that does not acknowledge tourists.

The strangeness is not theatrical. It accumulates the way the dust does. Distances disagree with themselves; a gallery that took three days to cross one way requires only hours on the return, or the reverse, and the discrepancy never resolves into error. Some of the grazers occupy the same volume without merging, layered like the mineral sheets, so that you look through one herd and see another occupying identical space, both equally solid. Heat is uniform yet you feel currents that are not air—slow internal tides of temperature that have no corresponding motion. Time itself feels viscous, as if the pulse that crystallized the entire underworld also thinned the medium in which change occurs. Populations do not fluctuate. Paths do not wander. A fallen crystal (and they almost never fall) is already part of the equilibrium before it finishes settling. The abundant life moves, metabolizes, even preys, yet every action closes a loop that returns the whole to the same configuration. It is not frozen. It is complete.

You can walk for a lifetime in any direction and the country will continue to offer the same terms: warmth without source, dryness without thirst, sharpness without youth, density without crowding, life without green, silence without peace, and a darkness that is not waiting for morning because morning is a surface idea, and there is no surface here. Only the next aperture, the next packed interval, the next faint, primitive mark that someone once thought worth making and then never thought of again.
