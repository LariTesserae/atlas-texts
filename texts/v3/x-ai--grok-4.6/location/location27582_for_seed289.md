The chamber is no larger than a closet that has forgotten how to be a closet. You would miss the entrance even if you were looking for it: a hairline seam in the surface soil, a place where the ground simply declines to be ground for the width of two fingers. Drop through and the world compresses around you at once. There is no transition, no antechamber. One moment air and ordinary daylight; the next you are already inside, already touching six surfaces at once.

The rock itself has not finished deciding what it wants to be. It is geology caught in the first three seconds of thinking. Walls bulge with half-born crystals that never quite completed their lattices; they look wet even when they are dry, their faces still rounded as if the melt had only just begun to cool. Stalactites hang in embryonic nubs, no longer than a thumbnail, forever on the verge of dripping and forever refusing. The floor is a single unfinished slab that still remembers liquidity—ripples frozen mid-wave, a surface that should have been planed by a million years of water and instead stopped after the first hour. Everything is raw. Nothing has been given time to weather. The stone smells of the instant after lightning, mineral and new.

Yet the place is bright as a kiln. Every unfinished crystal is a lamp. They do not flicker. They do not dim. They pour out a white so complete it has no temperature, a light that has never learned the concept of shadow because it is generated from every direction at once. In a space this small the light has nowhere to go except into itself; it saturates the air until the air feels solid, until you can almost cup it. There are no gradients. The same intensity that paints the back of your eyelids also paints the undersides of the half-formed stones. Night is a rumor that never reached this far down.

Condensation beads on every surface and stays there. The beads do not grow, do not fall. They are the precise size they have always been. In three shallow dimples on the floor sit three pockets of water no larger than a child’s palm; their surfaces are mirrors that have never once trembled. The air itself is damp in the way a breath on glass is damp—present, cool, unchanging. You can feel the moisture on your skin but it never accumulates, never chills beyond the single note of coolness the chamber has chosen.

Vegetation exists only as an afterthought. In the dampest crease of the eastern wall a single pale lichen the color of old bone has taken hold; it covers an area smaller than a coin and has not advanced a millimeter in whatever duration this place understands as time. Two more patches, even smaller, cling to the underside of a crystal shelf. That is all. The stone does not welcome roots. The light does not encourage green. The plants are present the way a signature is present on a document no one intends to alter.

What occupies the space instead is life of another order, and it occupies every available gap. The chamber is packed. There is no interval between bodies. Thousands of small creatures—segmented, many-legged, some with carapaces like smoked glass, others soft and transluscent—press against one another and against the glowing rock and against you the instant you arrive. They do not crawl so much as exist in a state of maximum adjacency. Their legs interlock. Their antennae thread through one another’s joints. They breathe in perfect unison, a single tidal motion that never varies in depth or rate. Some have no eyes. Some have too many, all of them open, all of them reflecting the same unwavering glare. They are cool to the touch, the same cool as the stone, the same cool as the trapped water. They do not startle. They do not flee. They have nowhere to go that is not already themselves.

The sound is enormous. It has no source because every creature is the source and the stone itself seems to conduct it. It is a chord made of a thousand tiny stridulations, a continuous, ringing roar that fills the chamber the way the light fills it—completely, without remainder, without attack or decay. The pitch never slides. The volume never dips. It is the acoustic equivalent of the frozen crystals: a noise that began once and then simply continued being that beginning. After a minute you stop hearing it as sound and start feeling it as pressure, as another kind of density. The packed bodies vibrate with it. Your own bones pick it up and refuse to let it go.

Here and there, almost lost in the press of chitin and glowing mineral, are marks that do not belong to geology or to the animals. A shallow groove on one wall that looks too regular, as if something once worried at the stone with a patient, non-living edge. A small stack of three pebbles in a corner that could not have fallen that way. A single dark thread, neither plant nor animal, looped once around a crystal and left. They are faint. They are few. They change nothing. They feel like the last residual heat of a fire that went out before the rock finished cooling.

Nothing here has ever been otherwise. The light does not pulse. The water does not evaporate. The creatures do not age or reproduce or die in any way that would alter the count of bodies. The sound does not modulate. The embryonic stone does not continue its interrupted formation, nor does it weather backward into dust. The chamber is a single held breath, a pocket of world that reached equilibrium at the exact moment it came into being and then declined, forever, to take another step. You can stay as long as you like. When you leave, the seam in the soil will close behind you as if it had never opened, and the roar will continue at the same impossibly constant volume, heard by no one, changing nothing, forever just beginning.
