The descent is not a tunnel so much as a failure of the ground to stay a roof. You come down a stair of unfinished benches—raw cuts in pale stone that still show the tooth-marks of the last machines—and the air thickens before the light does. Warmth arrives first, wet and close, the kind that does not come from weather but from the rock itself, from water that has been underground long enough to forget the sun. Then the sound, which is not one sound but a saturation: water finding every edge and arguing with it.

This is not a cave in the old sense. It is a basin that has only recently learned it is below the world. The ceiling, where it exists, is a bruise of overcast stone and leftover day, far off and perforated by shafts that were never meant to be sky. Most of the space is open in the horizontal—kilometers of it, a drowned valley with no valley walls you can quite believe, only a slow climb of benches and spoil and weeping faces. You can see a long way and still not see out. Distance here is milky. Vapor hangs in layers that do not mix. Everything sweats.

The water is not a feature. It is the occupancy. It stands in sheets across the floor, no deeper than a knee in most places and then suddenly a throat, black and still, where the old workings punched through into something older. It films every cut. It beads on the undersides of ledges and falls in strings that never quite become rain. The stone weeps in curtains that have already begun to invent their own geology: pale rims, infant terraces, a chalky lace that was not here last year and will be thicker next. Erosion has not had time to decide what it is. The rock is still arriving. Faces are blocky, unrounded, the joints too clean, the rubble too angular. In places the floor is not floor but a crust of precipitated mineral over standing water, and your weight makes a sound like walking on a drumhead that has been left in a bath.

There is almost no green that you would call a plant. What grows does so as a film or a mistake. Algae in the warmer seeps, olive-black, slippery as viscera. A few ferns in the wetter niches where a shaft still delivers a rumor of day—thin, colorless, more architecture than leaf. Something like willow has found a spoil heap near an old adit and died halfway through the attempt, leaving a scribble of pale sticks. The rest of the living world has taken the absence as an invitation.

Fauna is the packing. There is no interval. The air is a body of midges and smaller things that do not bother with names, a soft abrasion against the skin, a taste of protein. Frogs occupy every lip of water and every vertical inch of wet stone; their throats work in overlapping pulses until the basin is one instrument with too many players. The sound is not loud the way a factory is loud. It is loud the way a crowd is loud when the crowd is the room. Under it, lower, the water talks to itself in a continuous unfastening—seeps, drips, the occasional collapse of a mineral shelf into a pool. And under that, when the frogs rest for a breath they do not actually take, the remaining engines.

They are not many. A pump house of brick the color of old tea, half in the water, its roof a suggestion. A line of rusted pipe that still, somewhere in its length, holds pressure and exhales into a trough with a sound like a man clearing his throat forever. A conveyor whose belt has become a hanging garden of slime. Rails that go into the water and do not come out. A single gantry, skeletal, standing in a pool as if it waded in to think and forgot to leave. Electrification is a rumor: a cable drooping, a lamp in a cage that burns a tired orange and makes the vapor look solid. Someone is still, somewhere, feeding a generator, or the generator has not yet understood it is alone. The machines do not hurry. Nothing here hurries. The water rises by the width of a fingernail across a season. The mineral crust thickens by the thickness of a thought. The frogs replace themselves. The pumps, when they fail, fail the way a habit fails.

Light is a courtesy. What falls from the shafts is already used, gray-gold, and it dies in the first fathom of mist. The rest is the lamp, and the pale of the stone, and the dull coin-shine of water that has taken the color of the rock into itself. You see by accumulation. Edges arrive late. A person at a distance is a darker humidity. At night—if the word still applies—the basin does not go dark so much as it admits that it was always dark, and the frogs, if it is possible, become more themselves.

The rules are patient and local. Water owns the horizontal. Stone owns the vertical until water teaches it otherwise. Anything built is a guest and is being rewritten as deposit, as perch, as the hard part of a new shore. Sound is a climate. You do not speak against it; you wait for a thinning, or you put your mouth close to a helmet, or you do not. The warmth does not relent. Clothes become another wet surface. Metal in the hand is the temperature of blood. Time is available in quantities that embarrass intention. A bootprint in the soft mineral mud will be a fossil before anyone comes back to see who made it, and even then the fossil will be underwater, and the water will be inventing a skin over it, and the frogs will have already decided the print is a room.

Walk far enough and the benches repeat: cut, weep, pool, ruin, cut. A second pump house, this one only a chimney and a smear of oil. A crane hook hanging from nothing you can see, dripping. A stretch of floor that is almost dry, almost, until you realize the dryness is a skin and the skin is singing with insects. In one bay the water is hot enough to ghost. In another it is merely warm and full of things that move without making a decision to move—pale fish, the flick of something jointed, the slow traffic of snails the size of thumbs. The air tastes of lime and iron and the sweet rot of whatever the water has been allowed to keep.

There is a town-scale to it, a regional patience. You could lose a day crossing and only change the proportion of mist to stone. Far off, a darker slot might be another working, or the place where the basin remembers it is supposed to be a river and tries, without success, to leave. The sky, when you find a shaft and look up, is a remote disk, sometimes with a weather in it that does not apply. Rain, on the rare occasions it reaches this far, arrives already defeated, a finer wet in the wet, and the frogs receive it as confirmation.

What is forming here has not agreed on a name. It is not a marsh, though it has the generosity of one. It is not a mine, though the mine is still the grammar of the walls. It is not a cave, because a cave has finished arriving. This place is the argument between those things, held in warm water, packed until there is no between, loud enough that thought has to wade, dim enough that the eye learns to hunt for the next pale ledge, the next dull lamp, the next place a machine has decided to become landscape. The stone keeps coming. The water keeps writing on it. The living keep using every sentence. Nothing is in a rush to be finished.
