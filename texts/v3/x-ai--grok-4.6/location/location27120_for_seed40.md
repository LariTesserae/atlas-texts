The plateau was poured, then bolted, then poured again. There is no older rock under it. If you chip the white terrazzo of a plaza you find more terrazzo, then a run of riveted plate, then a cavity full of still air and a ladder that does not go down so much as aside. The builders — whoever they were, or are, or will finish being — replaced the mountain with itself, only made. Elevation is a fact of the work: the whole thing sits in thin cool light, a regional sprawl of decks and tanks and galleries that occupies what used to be a high basin and now is only more of the same, edge to edge, horizon to manufactured horizon.

It is bright the way a machine shop is bright when every lamp has been left on for a century. The sky is a hard enamel. Surfaces throw it back: glazed brick the color of bone, galvanized sheeting, porcelain insulators the size of coffins, the long pale runs of condensation troughs. There is almost no color that has not been applied. What little vegetation lives here lives in the joints — a sparse, patient lichen of copper-green, a few wiry shrubs that have learned to root in the grit that collects where two walkways never quite meet. They do not spread. They occupy the designed leaks.

Water is a scheduling problem. It arrives as rime on the night-shift railings, as a brief rain that the gutters accept without sound, as beads that form on the undersides of pipes and fall with the regularity of a clock that has forgotten haste. Pockets of it stand in cisterns the size of city blocks, their surfaces skimmed by wind and by things that are not wind. The air is always a little damp. Breath shows. Metal sweats. In the deep courtyards a cool fog gathers at mid-morning and does not burn off so much as get used.

You can see a long way. The scale is open in the old sense: a valley’s worth of construction, multi-featured, town after town of stacked halls receding until the farthest stacks are only a brighter scratch against the brightness. Catwalks stitch the distances. Conveyors, frozen or nearly so, hang in midair like the ideas of rivers. From any high parapet the place reads as one continuous artifact — dense, near-touching, layered until the layers argue. Hab-blocks press against retorts. A foundry’s cooling fins become the ceiling of a dormitory. Stairwells corkscrew through the mass and come out in rooms that were not on the way. Density here is not crowding of people; it is saturation of made things. Every cubic measure has been claimed, revised, claimed again.

And yet it is quiet. The industry is real — electrified, scaled, full of engines — but the engines turn on cycles measured in seasons. A press the size of a nave descends the width of a finger in a week. Turbines idle so slowly their blades are architecture. What sound there is arrives late and thin: a single drip finding a tank, the tick of contracting plate at dusk, the almost-inaudible fizz of current in a busbar the thickness of a road. The abundant fauna does not break the hush. They are everywhere, the animals of this place. Pale moths with mica wings roost in the lamp-housings. Herds of something like goats, jointed in too many places, crop the lichen from vertical seams and leave no scrapes. Birds of folded tin nest in the dead loudspeakers and do not sing. In the cisterns, schools of fish like punctures in the water move as one thought and make no ripple that lasts. They are plentiful the way rust is plentiful, occupying every overlooked volume, and they are as patient as the machines.

The features are young. Edges have not been taught to soften. A balustrade is still a knife. New pours show the grain of forms that were struck yesterday or a hundred years ago; time is hard to read when nothing weathers in the ordinary way. Whole districts feel recently emerged, as if the plateau is still finishing the thought of itself — a stair that ends in air and will, eventually, meet a landing that has not been cast. Erosion, such as it is, happens as slow unmaking: a bolt backs out over a decade; a weld line brightens, then darkens, then is gone, and the plates remember a different seam.

Then there is the other thing, the thing the density did when it ran out of room and kept going. Reality here is a worn gasket. Walk a straight service tunnel and the windows show the same courtyard three times, then a courtyard that has never existed, then the first courtyard again with the fog in a different year. A door opened at noon opens at dusk inside. Some stacks occupy the same footprint and do not touch. You will find a factory floor that is also, without metaphor, the sky — workers (if they are workers) moving among rafters that are clouds, tightening fittings on nothing that can be named, their shadows falling both down and inward. The bright light doubles and fails to agree with itself. Reflections in the terrazzo show a second plateau, inverted, populated by the same silent herds, and if you stare long enough the two begin to trade moisture, a slow drip from one world into the other that the gutters, after a while, learn to accept.

Change is a long weather. The presses descend. The lichen advances a handspan in a generation. A new hall appears in a gap that was always too small for it, as if the place had been holding its breath. Fog comes, stays, is scheduled away. The tin birds shift on their perches. Far out, at the rim where the construction simply stops — a clean, unweathered drop into white air — you can watch the edge grow, or think you can, a sharpness extending itself by the thickness of a thought, year after quiet year, into a sky that was never asked.
