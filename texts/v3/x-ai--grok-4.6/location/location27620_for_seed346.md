The hollow sits lower than any map bothers to record, a shallow concavity no wider than a cathedral close, rimmed by stone that has not yet decided what it is. The rock here is still arriving. It sweats a mineral damp that never quite becomes rain, a cold film that beads on every surface and then withdraws, leaving a faint white bloom like forgotten frost. Between these episodes the air is merely raw, tasting of unfinished quartz and something faintly metallic that has not been named.

What occupies the hollow is a settlement packed so tightly the buildings seem to lean into one another for warmth they cannot feel. Walls of a material that looks like poured night—matte, slightly refractive, neither glass nor metal—rise in layers that ignore ordinary stacking. A balcony of one structure threads through the façade of the next; staircases terminate inside rooms that were never planned for them. The density is not merely architectural. Walkways hover a hand’s breadth apart. Conduits of no visible purpose pulse once every few hours, a slow peristalsis that rearranges the street grid by centimeters. You can stand at an intersection and watch a corner gradually become an alcove, then a threshold, then a wall again, the change so patient it registers first as a change in the quality of silence.

The silence itself is nearly complete. Sound travels a short distance and then simply fails, as if the air has been locally instructed to forget how vibration works. Occasional drips from overhead condensers (or from the stone itself) arrive late and without echo. There are no birds. Once, perhaps twice a day, something pale and many-jointed picks its way along a high ledge—too thin for any familiar fauna, moving with the unhurried caution of a thing that has not yet been assigned a metabolism. It never comes down.

Vegetation is an afterthought the place has not fully committed to. In the narrowest cracks, where the embryonic rock has cooled enough to crack, a lichen the color of old parchment holds on. It does not photosynthesize so much as transcribe: its surface sometimes displays fragmentary characters in a script no one present claims to read, then sloughs them off as powder. A few wiry shrubs persist near the dampest vents, their leaves edged with a rime that never melts. They do not grow so much as persist, marking time in increments too small to notice until a season has passed and one realizes a branch has relocated itself half a meter to the left.

Light here is ordinary daylight, but the hollow receives it as if through a slow filter. Shadows fall correctly and then, over the course of an afternoon, begin to detach. A shadow will linger after its object has moved, or will arrive a minute early, thin and exploratory. The effect is never dramatic. It is simply another patient process, like the way the paving underfoot occasionally forgets to be solid and becomes a brief, ankle-deep smear of colder air before remembering itself.

The technology that built this place does not announce itself with glow or hum. It is the reason the geology remains unfinished. Whatever engine or consensus or error first chose this depression decided that matter here could remain provisional, and the city has taken that permission as infrastructure. Walls sometimes contain rooms that exist only while you are looking at them. A corridor may open onto a view of the same corridor from three minutes earlier, the earlier version slightly damper, the later one already dry. These are not spectacles. They are maintenance. The inhabitants—few, quiet, wrapped in garments that seem to drink the residual moisture from the air—move through the discrepancies with the unhurried competence of people who have stopped expecting consistency. They do not speak much. When they do, the words arrive with a faint delay, as if traveling from a slightly different version of the street.

At the very center, where the density peaks and the buildings interpenetrate until distinction becomes a courtesy, there is a small plaza of unset stone. It is still cooling. Heatless steam lifts from it in threads that never quite become mist. The plaza is the only open space, and even it is not empty: residual construction lattices, finer than hair, continue their work of deciding where a fountain might one day stand, or a statue, or nothing at all. The lattices click once every few hours, a sound so soft it is felt more than heard, like a thought changing its mind.

The hollow has edges. Beyond the last wall the unfinished stone simply stops rising, leaving a lip of raw geology that looks as though it might resume tomorrow, or in a century. No one crosses that lip. The city does not expand. It only deepens, folding more carefully into the space it already occupies, waiting for the rock to finish arriving, or for the rock to decide it never will.
