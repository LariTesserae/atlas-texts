The pocket is smaller than a confession and fuller than a sea.

You do not come to it from a shore. There is no shore. There is a seam in low ground—clay that never quite dried, a depression no map bothers with—and the seam is younger than the weeds that hide it. The earth here split recently. The break is still sharp. Limestone and packed silt show their inner faces, pale as bone cut the same morning, unpolished by any long weather. Water did not so much flood the opening as occupy it the instant the opening existed, as if the water had been standing in the dark on the other side of a door that had not been a door until it was.

Inside, there is no surface. The pocket is all liquid. Cool, not cold: the temperature of a well that has forgotten summer. Pressure is even and close, a hand on every part of you at once, unhurried. What would be air in another place is water here, and what would be water is only a denser thought of the same substance. You do not swim so much as hang. Distance is a rumor that fails after an arm’s length.

The vegetation occupies nearly all of that arm’s length.

It is lush in the way a clenched fist is lush—too much living matter for the volume that contains it. Pale fronds, not green so much as the memory of green after light has been taken away, packed stem to stem. They have the textures of things that grew expecting a different physics: some are gelatinous and faintly ribbed, like lungs turned inside out; some are fibrous and abrasive, like drowned hair that learned to stand. They do not float. They are anchored in every direction at once—floor, the fresh fracture-walls, one another, and, wrongly, the places between. There is almost no water that is only water. To move is to part bodies. The parted bodies close again with a patience that feels like refusal.

In the dark you know them by touch and by the slight differences in coolness. A few hold a dimness that is not light: a residual grey, as if they once stored a noon and have been spending it for a long time, coin by coin. It is not enough to see by. It is enough to know that seeing used to be a rule.

The rock of the pocket is low country stone—soft, sedimentary, recently wounded. You can feel the sharpness of the break under the root-mats. Edges that have not yet learned to be old. Silt still loosens in threads when anything shifts, and the threads do not fall; they hang and slowly become more plant. Erosion here is a recent event still finishing itself. The pocket is a young mouth.

Sound is mostly the idea of sound. A pressure-change when a frond yields. A single grain of silt ticking against a stem, then nothing for a long while. Once in a great span there is a noise like a knuckle on wet wood, coming from no particular direction. It may be the stone settling. It may be the pocket remembering it is a wound.

Fauna is almost an absence with a shape. There are traces: a mucus-track along a stem that was not there last season; a bite-mark in a gelatinous leaf that has already begun to pearl over; a shed something the size of a thumbnail, translucent, ridged, already being digested by the plants. Whatever lives here does not live here often. It arrives on a cycle longer than weather. When it does, it must move the way you would—by insisting. There is no corridor for a body. The ecology has left no aisle.

Scattered through the mass, not placed so much as caught, are the built things.

A brick, its face still bearing a thumbprint glaze, held in a nest of roots as if the roots were considering whether to become a wall. A length of corroded hinge, useless, beautiful in the way a bone is beautiful when it is no longer required to be a bone. Two tiles, glazed a color you cannot name in the dark, fitted to nothing, their grout long since become a kind of moss. A ring of metal, too large for a finger, too small for a wrist, grown through by a stem that has taken its circle as an instruction. These are not ruins of this place. This place is too new and too small to have been built in. They feel imported by a logic the water has not explained. Perhaps they fell when the ground opened. Perhaps the pocket, in forming, took a mouthful of some other room.

There is no tool-work that belongs here. Nothing has been knotted, cut, driven, or lit. The plants do not bear the marks of harvest. The stone has not been dressed. Whatever intelligence is present is the intelligence of occupancy, not of making.

The weirdness is not an event. It is the grain of the water.

Geometry does not close. If you follow a wall with your palm, counting the sharp young facets, you will return to a brick you already passed and find it on the opposite side of a stem that was not a stem a moment ago. Up and down trade duties without announcement. Sometimes the packed vegetation thins—not into open water, but into a density that is the same density seen from inside, as if you had entered a leaf and found the pocket again, slightly cooler, the hinge in a different year of rust.

Time is thick and poorly mixed. A frond you parted is still parting when you look back with your hands. A silt-thread released at the fracture-edge arrives later as a complete plant, already ancient in its habits, already pressing for room. The pocket is young in stone and old in consequence. It formed recently and has always been forming. The two facts sit in the same cool dark and do not argue.

There are rules, if you stay long enough to be subject to them.

Nothing here is single. Every presence occupies the space of several. The plants grow into one another’s outlines until outline is a courtesy. The water is a medium and a citizen. The built fragments are seeds of rooms that will not be allowed to happen. If you remain, you will be packed. Not eaten—there is almost no hunger that looks like hunger—but included. The density has a politics: vacancy is a kind of rudeness, and the pocket is too small, too hidden, too newly torn to afford rudeness.

Light does not come. What you take for afterimage is the plants spending their inherited noon. Darkness here is not the absence of a sun. It is a climate.

On the slowest cycle—longer than a tide, shorter than a geology—the fracture-edges shed a little more of themselves. The pocket widens by the thickness of a nail. The vegetation takes the new volume before the water has finished arriving. Somewhere in that patient enlargement a second hinge may appear, or the same hinge again, or a window-frame that opens onto more of this, packed, cool, and quietly incorrect.

The place does not want a name. Names need air. It is a hidden full dark: a recently opened low place, all water, all occupancy, almost silent, almost lifeless, already too much, already not obeying the shapes that made it. If you left—and leaving would require the seam to remember it is a seam—the ground above would look like any damp hollow in unremarkable country. A person could walk over it and feel only that the earth gave a little, as earth sometimes does, and never know they had crossed a sea that has no surface and a room that has no permission to exist.
