The first thing that arrives is not the dark. The dark is already there, a settled medium, older than any of the fittings. What arrives is heat and noise together, the two of them married so long they no longer distinguish themselves.

You are below. The rock overhead is not a suggestion; it is a lid you can feel in the sinuses, a pressure that has nothing to do with barometers. The chamber is the size of a nave that forgot its church, or a market square that never learned a sky. You can walk from one worked wall to the opposite in a few hundred long strides if you keep to the floor, and the ceiling—when something up there briefly admits a rust-red filament of residual light—reads as maybe eight storeys, maybe ten. The volume is almost empty. That is the fact the place will not let you forget. Architecture clings to the perimeter and hangs in stacked galleries from the vault, but the air in the middle is a single unused room the size of weather.

The city is here. It is only that almost no one is.

What remains is infrastructure in its late, balanced age. Walkways of fused basalt and something paler, a ceramic that still holds a faint subsurface shimmer, terrace out from the walls in rings. Habitation shells—doorless now, or with doors that have forgotten how to finish closing—are socketed into the rock like teeth in an old jaw. Between them run the service spines: conduits thick as a torso, some of them still warm to the palm, some beaded with condensation that never quite chooses to fall. The materials have had centuries to agree with one another. Where the old cut-stone meets the later grown-lattice, the join is not a seam so much as a decision that finished being made a long time ago. Edges are rounded not by design but by the patient work of moisture and the very slight, constant abrasion of air that has been circulating since before the last occupants left their names.

The water is not a river. It is a climate. Humidity sits in the lungs. On the cooler faces of the upper galleries, films collect, thicken, and release themselves in irregular bright ticks that the emptiness turns into events. A runnel follows the lowest course of the floor, sometimes a sheet no deeper than a boot-sole, sometimes nothing but a darker stain and the smell of wet mineral. After the long warm intervals—there is no sun to time them by, only the machines’ own weather—the condensation becomes a brief interior rain. It does not last. It leaves the air tasting of iron and old electricity and then the heat takes it back. Pockets hold: a cistern lipped with salt-bloom, a basin under a broken downspout where the surface never quite stills.

Light is an accident and a memory. Most of the chamber is a darkness you navigate by heat and by the way sound returns from stone. Here and there a panel still performs a duty no one assigned it: a coin of amber along a stair, a vein of slow green in a ceiling rib, the residual glow of a material that once stored day from a surface world and has been spending the last of it for longer than living recollection. The glow does not illuminate so much as confess the nearest edge. Your hands learn the rest.

The sound is the city’s remaining occupation. It is loud the way a hall is loud when it has been emptied of everything except what the hall itself is doing. Deep in the walls, and under the floor, and in a tower of stacked housings against the far curve, engines continue. They are not engines in any diagram you could finish. Heat moves without an obvious working fluid. Pressure equalizes along paths that do not match the visible pipes. A low, continuous thunder lives in the rock—felt more than heard—and over it ride higher, rhythmic complaints: a cycling intake, a valve that has sung the same two notes for a geological afternoon, the slap and drain of water being lifted, used, returned. Because the volume is hollow, every mechanical act arrives twice, three times, a staggered congregation of itself. When the condensation rain starts, the ticks become a scattered applause that the vault turns into a single long sentence. There are hours—if hours are what the machines keep—when the whole place seems to be speaking in a language of resonances, and you are only the latest thing to stand in it.

Vegetation is a guest that never quite moved in. Along the perennial damp of the runnel, a pale, fibrous growth—not moss, not quite fungus—holds to the joins in thin seams. Higher, where a leak has been faithful, a few straps of something leaflike hang and sweat. They are the color of things grown without a spectrum. No one tends them. They do not spread far. Their smell is sweet and faintly chemical, like fruit left too near a battery.

Fauna is rarer than that. Once in a long crossing you hear a dry scuttle in a duct, or see, at the limit of a dying panel, a low shape pause and then decide you are not food and not threat and not interesting. Something uses the warm housings. Something smaller uses the something. The ecology is a rumor with occasional proof. It is enough to keep the silence from being complete, and not enough to populate the galleries.

The unusual is quiet, almost polite. It lives in the way the oldest lattice still repairs hairline fractures while you watch, the new material arriving from nowhere you can name, matching the weathered color as if embarrassed to be new. It lives in the fact that a dropped tool does not always fall quite straight near certain pillars—only a suggestion, a courtesy of the field, gone when you look for a cause. It lives in the sound itself, which sometimes arrives a half-beat before the motion that should have made it, as if the chamber has started to remember events in the wrong order and then corrected itself out of habit. None of this insists. The place is not a riddle. It is a room that has been doing its work long enough to have acquired a few private physics, the way an old house acquires drafts that do not match the windows.

What moves, moves. The heat breathes. The water films, falls, is taken up, is put down again. The engines complete cycles whose purposes have outlived their operators. Air slides along the galleries in slow, warm rivers and returns. A shutter somewhere high opens and closes on a schedule written for a population that is no longer a variable. Dust—there is almost no dust—makes a brief bright weather in a chance beam and settles back into the same thin skin it has worn for ages. The erosion is finished and ongoing at once: everything that was going to collapse has collapsed; everything that remains has found the angle at which it can last. The city is not a ruin. A ruin has stopped. This has merely become the shape of its own continuance, urban and hollow, loud and nearly vacant, warm and dark, damp in the way a mouth is damp, bounded on every side by rock that was cut, then grown into, then left to remember the cutting.

If you stay, you learn the routes the sound prefers, the stairs that still take weight, the one basin where the water is almost sweet. You learn that the emptiness is not absence so much as capacity: the chamber was built to hold more life than it now contains, and it has not revised the architecture to match. It keeps the space. It keeps the noise. It keeps the heat. It keeps, along the lowest course, that thin argument of water, and the sparse pale things that have decided, for now, that this is enough of a world.
