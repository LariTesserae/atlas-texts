The pocket sits where a mountain has not yet decided to finish being a mountain. It is a shallow, almost circular concavity no wider than a long shout, cupped high on a shoulder of raw basalt that is still rising, still clicking and settling into itself with the patience of something being assembled rather than weathered. Nothing here has been rounded. Every surface is first-generation: hexagonal columns half-unpacked from their molds, glassy rinds that have not yet flaked, seams of mineral still bright as if they were poured an hour ago and merely look ancient because the light is ordinary noon. The heat is dry and directional, radiating upward from the stone itself more than down from the sky. It smells of ozone and hot copper.

There is no water and never has been. The chemistry here runs on other solvents—sulfur vapor that never condenses, a slow exhalation of carbon monoxide that the plants drink like dew. Those plants are the only lushness, and they keep to themselves. They grow in isolated thickets, each thicket a dense, almost architectural knot of blue-black fronds and translucent paddles that store heat the way other leaves store sugar. Between the thickets the ground is empty: pale, sintered dust, the occasional unopened geode the size of a fist, nothing moving. The vegetation does not compete or spread; it occupies its allotted volume and then stops, as if the space between things were itself a necessary organ. In the densest clumps the fronds interlock into something that looks, from certain angles, like cooling fins or radiator stacks. They photosynthesize in the infrared as much as the visible, and they shed a fine metallic pollen that never quite falls, hanging in faint, heat-shimmered curtains.

Nothing animal lives here. The only motion is geological and vegetal, both operating on timescales that make a heartbeat feel like a seizure. Once every few hours a column of rock will lengthen by a finger’s width, extruding itself with a sound like a distant turbine winding down. The plants respond by unfurling a new paddle or sealing a heat-scar, equally unhurried. The air is mostly quiet. What sound exists is low and infrastructural: the sub-audible thrum of convection cells rearranging themselves beneath the crust, the occasional crystalline ping when a new facet finishes cooling. It is the noise of a factory whose workers have all gone home and left the machines to finish the shift on their own.

The pocket is hidden in the strictest sense. You do not approach it; you notice that you have already been inside it, the surrounding ridgelines having folded shut behind you like a lid that was never designed to open. Space here is slightly non-commutative. Distances refuse to add the same way twice. A thicket that is twenty paces away can, if you look aside, occupy the same coordinates as the one you just left. The heat haze is not always haze; sometimes it is a brief, local failure of occlusion, and you see the underside of the sky or the interior of a rock. These glitches are infrequent and polite. They do not announce themselves. They simply occur, then recede, leaving the ordinary light a little more ordinary by contrast.

What is being manufactured is the place itself. The industrial character is not added; it is the native grammar of the emergence. Veins of ore are drawn upward along invisible templates, annealed by the ambient heat, then left to stand as unfinished girders. The plants, whatever they are, appear to be both product and tooling—heat exchangers that also happen to be alive, or alive in the only way this chemistry permits. There are no artifacts, no bolts, no poured foundations. There is only the slow, patient output of a process that does not distinguish between geology and production. Reality is thin enough here that the distinction was never required.

Over longer durations the pocket will either finish or fail. The mountain will either accept this concavity as one of its features or reabsorb it. Until then the thickets continue their quiet radiators’ work, the rock continues its incremental extrusion, and the heat continues to rise through a space that is still learning how to be space. Nothing hurries. Nothing needs to. The world is still under construction, and this is one of the rooms it has not yet decided whether to keep.
