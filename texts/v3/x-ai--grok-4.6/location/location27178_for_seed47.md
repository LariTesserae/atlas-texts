The first thing that registers is not a feature but an absence of features at the distances you are used to using. The ground goes on. It does not stop being ground. After an hour of walking the horizon has not acquired a crease or a smear of forest or a suggestion of weather sitting on something taller than you. You are already on the tall thing. Everything that was ever going to be high has already been high for so long that height became a plane.

This is a peneplain that forgot to come down. Whatever orogeny built it finished its work in a previous age of the world and then the world kept sanding. The result is not flat in the sense of a table. It is flat in the sense of a law. Residual swells remain — continental rumours of mountains, rises so broad you walk an entire day up a slope you cannot feel — and between them the surface has settled into its last vocabulary: pavements of interlocking mineral, grit that has been grit since before your species had a word for time, polygonal plates the size of cities, their edges softened until they look poured. The rock is old in a way that makes “ancient” feel like a courtesy. It has the patience of something that has already heard every argument weather can make.

There is almost no water. What moisture exists is a rumour in the mineral — hydration locked in lattices, a bloom of salt where a vein once wept and then stopped weeping, frost that occupies the shadowed sides of grains and never quite becomes weather. The air is too thin and too cool to carry a proper cloud. You get, instead, dry veils: dust so fine it behaves like a gas, lifting in sheets that have no sound. The temperature sits in the long cool — not the theatre of cold, not ice-scream and fracture, just a steady withholding. Breath is visible if you look for it. You usually do not. Looking costs attention, and attention out here has a long way to go between objects.

Light is ordinary and therefore slightly wrong. A sun, white-yellow, unspectacular, doing the job a sun does. Because the air is scarce the shadows are hard and the distances bleach. Midday is a kind of visual silence: everything equally present, equally far. Dawn and dusk last. They have more surface to work across. Colour in the rock runs to iron-rose, bone, a green so desaturated it might be a memory of copper. Occasional darker seams — residual organics? slag from something that was never geology? — draw lines that can be followed for a week.

You walk a long time between anything that could be called a thing.

The emptiness is structural. Density here is a rumour of presence. A standing stone every forty kilometres. A depression that might have been a catchment when catchments still happened. A stain. The vegetation, such as it is, occupies the grammar of afterthought: crusts, not plants. Lithic films. Patches of something like moss that are not moss, grey-blue, brittle, feeding on what the rock exhales and on the thin ration of light. They do not colonise so much as persist. In a hollow you might find a taller thing — a stem with no ambition, a single dark plume that has been growing for a century and will be a stem with no ambition for a century more. Seeds, if they exist, travel in the dry veils and mostly fail. Failure is the local ecology’s most successful strategy.

Fauna is rarer than that. You hear nothing that is an animal. Once, at the edge of vision, a movement that refuses to resolve: low, many-legged or not-legged, the same colour as the pavement, crossing from one polygon to another with the unhurried guilt of something that knows it is not supposed to be the main event. Tracks do not last. The surface does not hold them. Whatever lives here has learned not to accumulate.

Sound is a private matter. Your own blood. The tick of cooling in whatever you carry. Wind exists — it must; the dust moves — but it has so little to push against that it arrives as pressure changes rather than noise. A front can walk over you and the loudest part is the idea of it. Conversations die at ten metres. Shouting is a kind of rudeness toward the scale.

And yet the place will not stay still.

This is the contradiction the plateau has decided to live inside. The geology is finished. The weather of stone is over. What remains is a turbulence that does not belong to climate in any language you brought with you. Across hundreds of kilometres the dry veils do not merely drift; they organise. They braid. They collapse into transient architectures — walls, rotating sheets, hollow columns that walk — and then forget themselves. The ground, which has no right to be busy, sometimes is. A polygon will lift a centimetre and settle. A seam will briefly shine as if remembering metal. Whole districts shimmer with a heat that is not thermal, a restlessness in the visible, as if the last form of the world were being continuously re-derived.

The cause, when you find it, is never in one place.

Scattered across the high worn are the works. They do not cluster. They do not amount to a city or a ruin in the way you have been taught those words. A ring half-sunk, its inner face too fine, refusing dust. A tripod the height of a weather tower, legs driven through pavements that should have shattered and did not. A patch of ground that is slightly more ground than the ground around it, as if resolution had been locally increased. A line — not carved, not built — along which compasses sulk and the dust prefers not to cross. These things are not maintained and they are not abandoned. They are operating at a duty cycle you cannot audit. Whatever physics they use has not ended; it has simply become part of the plateau’s weather.

You can stand near one for a day and learn almost nothing except that your instruments disagree with each other in polite, consistent ways. Approach another and the air in your lungs tastes briefly of thunderstorms that cannot happen here. A third will have a small climate of its own: a disc of stillness in the general unrest, or the opposite, a knot of the silent storm anchored to a point, the dust rising and falling like a thought the world cannot finish. None of this is loud. The most violent rearrangement you will witness — a ridge of pavement unzipping along ten kilometres, turning over, lying down again in a new and equally final arrangement — happens in a hush so complete you feel it in your teeth before you understand you have seen it.

The old story, if there is one, is that the works are what finished the mountains. Not in a day. In an age. They were set to extract a gradient, or to settle a debt, or to bring a surface to the state in which no further work could be done to it, and they succeeded so thoroughly that success became a continuing condition. The planet, or this face of it, or this manufactured lid on whatever is underneath, reached its last shape and then the tools that made the last shape declined to clock out. They keep deriving the same answer. The turbulence is the derivation. The silence is because there is almost no medium left to complain in, and because the processes involved do not spend themselves in vibration you can use.

At the extreme elevations — the residual swells, the places where the law of flatness thins — the air gets thinner still and the works get stranger by small degrees. You find a lonely pylon whose shadow does not match its outline. You find a basin of salt that is not salt, each crystal a closed instruction. You find, once, a garden: a rectangle of the grey-blue crust in a lattice too regular, tended by nothing, thriving on a light that is the ordinary sun plus a remainder. The temperature there is the same cool. Nothing drips. Nothing ticks. The stems, if they are stems, angle themselves toward a point in the sky the sun does not occupy.

Night is clean. Stars come down close because there is so little in the way. Some of the works answer them — not brightly; a patient correspondence, a change in the way a surface holds darkness. Meteors that would be ordinary anywhere else sometimes fail to complete their fall. They go out, or they become additional dust in a veil that was already passing, or they are received by something in the ground that has been waiting for metal in a particular mood. You do not hear the reception.

There are rules, insofar as a place this finished and this unstill will admit them.

You can travel for a month and not be lost, because lost requires a difference to be lost in. Navigation is a matter of counting works and trusting that the ones that move will move in orbits you can eventually name. Water you bring. Water you do not find, except as a theft from the hydration of stones, which is a long, unkind chemistry and tastes of the end of rivers. Fire is easy and pointless; there is little to burn and the wind, when it arrives, arrives as a subtraction. Shelter is the lee of a polygon or the still disc of a particular class of artifact. The rare animals will not approach the works. The crust-plants will. This is as close as the ecology comes to an opinion.

People have been here. The built record is the same grammar as everything else: scattered, faint, not sure it wants to be a record. A cairn of the local plate, stacked by hands, already being persuaded back into pavement. A hut whose corners have begun to forget they were corners. A vehicle, open to the sky, its metals the wrong generation, still warm in a way that is not solar. Someone came, understood enough to leave, or understood enough to stay and become another rarity. Their paths do not persist. The plateau’s last form includes a clause about paths.

What emerges, if you stay long enough for the place to finish arriving, is not a desert and not a ruin and not a machine. It is a stability that has to work continuously to remain still. The oldest surface. The highest leftover. An emptiness with weather of its own, and the weather is the ongoing proof that the proof is done. You walk, and the dust organises and disorganises on your left like a continent thinking, and nothing speaks, and the sun does the ordinary work of a sun across a distance that has outlasted meaning, and once in a great while the ground under you completes a thought it started before there were witnesses, and settles, and is again the only shape it has ever, finally, been.
