The chamber is no larger than a nave and already too full. You come into it the way water comes into a cellar: by finding the lowest wrongness and following it down. There is a crust above, thin as a skull, and through it the world still believes in weather. Down here the weather has been rewritten as a single climate of wet cold.

Everything is living and everything weeps. The walls are not walls. They are a packed, intergrown mass of pale rhizome and blade and bladder, pressed so tightly that the distinction between organism and architecture fails on contact. Where you would expect stone there is only compressed vegetation, cold as a butcher’s slab, slick with a film that never quite decides to be water. The film runs in sheets. It beads. It threads. It hangs in slow curtains from the vault of the ceiling, which is itself a hanging garden inverted: roots like organ pipes, pale and fat, dripping in overlapping tempos. The drips are loud. In a space this small they become a kind of weather system, a rain that never started and will not stop.

The temperature is a fact with weight. It sits in the chest. Breath does not fog so much as thicken, becoming a second, slower rain that joins the first. Ice should own this place and does not. The water stays liquid the way a held note stays a note: by continuous, expensive insistence. Touch a stem and the cold climbs your bones as if it had been waiting for a conductor. The plants do not mind. They are built for this. Their tissues are packed with lenses of clear gel that refuse to freeze, and inside those lenses something finer than cells is working—filaments, lattices, a frost of structure too regular to be accident and too alive to be machine. Current physics can describe the chemistry and then, quietly, stop. Heat is being moved somewhere it has no right to go. The chamber stays just this side of ice because the vegetation is spending it elsewhere, into a sink you cannot point at.

Underfoot the floor gives and then holds, a peat of living matter so dense it has begun to remember being ground. You sink to the ankle in a cold sponge that drinks and then returns a little of what it drank, so that every step is a negotiation with a marsh that has learned architecture. Between the thicker trunks—if trunks is the word for these fluted, pale columns that are half leaf, half cable—the smaller growth fills every leftover volume. Fronds overlap fronds. Bladders press against bladders. There is almost no air that has not recently been inside a plant. The density is not clutter; it is saturation. You move through a crowd that does not step aside.

Light arrives as a rumor. It is not dark. It is the dimness of a room after the lamps have been taken a long way down a hall. What glow there is comes from three untrustworthy sources: a seepage of day through the thin crust above, greened and thinned until it is only a suggestion of direction; a dull internal luminescence in certain bladders, the color of old bruises or weak tea; and the water itself, which seems, in the corners of vision, to carry a faint chalk-light as if remembering the sky. Shadows are soft and numerous. They layer. A thing can be visible and still not quite locatable.

The fauna is the rest of the sound. They are abundant in the way mold is abundant: not as individuals first, but as a condition. Soft lengths move inside the wall-mass, visible as traveling swellings. Things like isopods, but longer, with too many glistening plates, work the wet seams and click as they work—a dense, overlapping percussion that never quite becomes rhythm. In the hanging water there are clouds of minute bodies, not quite insect, not quite seed, that pulse in slow unison and make a high continuous tone, a wet electric hiss at the edge of hearing that the drips keep interrupting and then joining. Larger shapes occupy the few almost-clear volumes: pale, flattened animals that cling to the undersides of the hanging roots and breathe so slowly you can watch a single rib-cycle last the time it takes you to forget you were counting. When they shift, the whole column of water along a root shivers and the shiver becomes sound, a low intra-organ groan. Nothing here is in a hurry and everything is loud. The loudness is patient. It has the quality of a factory that has been running on the same shift for a geological age, except there is no factory, only this.

There are no artifacts. No cut surfaces, no joins that admit a tool, no geometry that confesses a hand. The chamber has the cleanliness of a thing that was never assembled. And yet it is under construction. The far end of the nave is unfinished in a way that has nothing to do with ruin. There the living mass thins. You can see, dimly, the raw face of whatever this place is eating: a mineral that is not yet stone, a grit still deciding its own hardness, pale and wet and being unmade by root-tips so fine they look like frost. The plants are not colonizing a cave. They are secreting one. They dissolve and incorporate and leave behind a hollow the shape of their own collective appetite. The process is embryonic. You are standing in a room that is still being born, and the birth is occurring at the speed of lichen, of karst, of a thought that refuses to finish.

The rules of the place are few and they do not care whether you learn them.

Water is always moving and never leaving. It cycles through tissue, air, drip, film, tissue again. Some of it is ordinary. Some of it has been inside the lattices long enough to come back changed: slightly heavier, slightly more willing to climb, carrying a faint taste of metal and wintergreen. Drink it and for a while you will hear the chamber more clearly, as if the drips had acquired grammar.

Growth is the only weather that matters. Over hours nothing obvious happens. Over a season the nave will be a handsbreadth longer, the unfinished face a handsbreadth farther on. The animals adjust without discussion. Their songs drop a fraction of a tone as the volume increases, then recover. The whole ecology is tuned to a room that is slowly becoming more room.

Cold is not the absence of energy. It is a product. The vegetation manufactures it the way other forests manufacture shade, and it uses the cold as both climate and tool. The unfinished mineral yields more willingly when it is this temperature. The fauna’s slow metabolisms are not a limitation; they are an agreement with a physics that prefers patience.

Information moves through the water and the gel-lenses faster than sap and by means no leaf should possess. A bruise on a far column will, after a long interval, be answered by a change in drip-rate near you. The high tone of the hanging bodies will shift. Something like a decision will have occurred, distributed, ownerless. You can stand in the middle of it and feel only that the chamber has, very slightly, paid attention.

The strangeness is not decorative. It is load-bearing. This is a pure-plant world that has learned engineering without ever becoming built, that keeps a liquid winter underground by methods that look like theology if you stare too long, that is loudly, slowly, eating a hole in the underside of a colder country and furnishing the hole with itself. The crust above flexes, almost imperceptibly, when the wind moves on the surface you cannot see. A little more day leaks in, then less. The drips continue. The plates click. The unfinished face shines with the wet of work that will not be finished in any life you will live.

If you stay, you will begin to match the pace. Your pulse will lengthen to fit the drips. The cold will stop being an assault and become a medium, like water to a fish that has forgotten there were ever shores. The chamber will not notice you as a visitor. It has no category for visitors. It has only more surface to cover, more mineral to unmake, more of itself to become. In time—its time—the nave will be a slightly larger nave, still too full, still weeping, still dim, still loud with the unending industry of things that do not believe in hurry. The crust will one day be too thin. That is a later problem, and this place does not keep later problems. It keeps only the next root-tip, the next click, the next drop finding the next hanging, and the cold, which it makes, and spends, and makes again.
