The world is a single fabricated lowland, poured and plated and joined until there is no elsewhere underfoot. You could walk a year and never leave the same intention: a continental pan of dark ceramic, sintered metal, glass-slag tile, and the dull pale compounds that were mixed to hold water without staining. There is no bedrock. There is no soil that was not mixed. The horizon is a ruled line of the same material, slightly lifted by heat-haze, and beyond it more of the same, weather of its own forming over a floor that does not end.

It is always after rain that never quite finishes. The air is warm and thick with its own condensation. Surfaces weep. Every vertical plane—and there are not many truly vertical planes; this is a low world, a world of lips, weirs, shallow steps, recumbent housings—bleeds a constant film. Water stands in the engineered shallows as a second skin, ankle-deep over miles, calf-deep in the collecting trays, never more than that, never less. The trays interlock. They were designed to interlock. Channels run between them with a geometry that still looks recently cut: sharp arrises, unrounded corners, the pour-marks of the last setting still visible as faint ribs. Nothing has worn them. Nothing will. Erosion here is a rumor from another physics.

What the water does is cycle. It lifts as haze in the warmth, hangs, beads, returns. The return is silent. You hear it only if you put an ear to a plate and wait: a continuous, almost inaudible sheeting, like cloth drawn over cloth. The great machines that keep the cycle exact sit half-submerged, their housings flush with the pan, their intakes and returns no louder than the water itself. They are industrial in the old sense—scaled, electrified, built for throughput—but they are finished. They do not manufacture. They maintain. Heat is exchanged. Water is lifted and let fall. Charge moves in buried runs you feel as a faint warmth through the tile. The equilibrium was reached and then locked. There are no gradients left that matter. Morning is not different from afternoon except by the angle of the light, and the light itself is ordinary, a high even day-brightness that might be a sun or a ceiling so remote it has weather underneath it. Shadows are short. Edges stay sharp in them.

There are no plants. There has never been a leaf. What grows, if the word can be used, is simpler and not green: mineral bloom in the wet seams, a pale scurfing, occasional mats of something that is more chemistry than organism, dull as wet paper. The world does not photosynthesize. It was not asked to.

What it has instead, in numbers that defeat counting, is fauna.

They are everywhere the water is, and the water is everywhere. They pack the shallows until the surface is a moving grain. They occupy the undersides of every overhang, the interiors of every grate, the warm galleries that run under the pan like a second, denser country. They hang in the haze in layers you walk through without meaning to, a soft collision of bodies that do not startle and do not sing. The air is full of them and still nearly silent. Wings, if they have wings, are not for display. Mouthparts work without sound. The overwhelming fact of them is tactile and visual, not acoustic: a saturation of presence, near-touching, stacked, each volume of the world already spoken for. You cannot put a hand into the water without meeting something living. You cannot lean on a housing without feeling the faint shift of whatever uses its heat.

They are not one kind. The shallows hold the broad, pale crawlers, many-legged, their shells the same unreflecting grey as the tile, as if they were poured with it. Between them move the softer things, unsegmented, pulsing in place, filtering the film. In the haze: small dark bodies in such number that at middle distance the air looks stippled, a weather of animals. Under the plates, in the galleries you glimpse through maintenance slits that no longer need maintaining, there are larger shapes, slow, pressed together in the warm dark, a density that has no empty interval. They do not hunt in any way you can watch. They do not flee. The equilibrium includes them. Birth and unmaking, if they occur, occur at rates that leave the count unchanged. The world was built with this load in mind, or the load arrived and the world adjusted once and then stopped adjusting. It is difficult to know which, and the difficulty is part of the mild strangeness that sits on everything here—not a violation of sense, only a tilt. Distances that should tire you do not quite. The same cluster of housings, seen from a mile off, looks newly placed and also as if it has always been exactly there. The animals’ faces, when they have faces, are too still, or have one feature too many by a small and consistent margin, a sixth dark wet point where five would have done.

The construction is total and young in the way a finished thing is young when nothing has been allowed to happen to it since. Maker’s stamps remain on hatch-rims. Bolt-patterns are bright. The great recumbent engines have the look of having been settled into the pour while the pour was still cooling, and then left. No rust. The alloys were chosen against rust, or the water was chosen against it, or time was. You can stand at a junction of channels and see the whole grammar of the place at once: tray, weir, gallery-mouth, housing, the low baffle that breaks a sheet of water into three quieter sheets, the ladder that goes down three rungs into a dark that is full of bodies and comes back up into the same day. Repeat that grammar for a continent. Repeat it until it generates its own wind, a slow warm sliding of haze from nowhere to nowhere, always the same wind, smelling of wet metal and the faint sweet mineral of the scurfing and the crowding life.

At night—if the light is allowed to go, and it is, down to a normal dusk and a normal dark—the water holds a dull residual gleam and the animals become a continuous slight pressure, a world-skin of them, breathing or the equivalent of breathing in a unison so complete it registers as quiet. The machines do not change pitch. The haze does not lift or thicken. You could sleep against a housing and wake to the same temperature on your cheek, the same film on the plate, the same uncountable attention in the shallows, and believe no interval had passed. That would be correct enough. The place does not travel in time so much as occupy it. It was made, it was filled, it was brought to balance, and then the making stopped meaning anything. What remains is the fact of the pan, warm and wet and inhabited past the point of metaphor, a manufactured marsh the size of a world, running forever in the same hour.
