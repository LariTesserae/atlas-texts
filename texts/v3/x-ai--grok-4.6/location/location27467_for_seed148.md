The hollow sits lower than the land that forgot it. You do not so much arrive as notice that the ground has already given way, a shallow basin no wider than a small town, its rim a lip of unfinished earth—raw clay still wet where it has never decided to become stone, pebbles that have not yet chosen their faces. Erosion here is not wear. It is rehearsal. The banks slump and then remember themselves. New rills appear overnight like thoughts and fill with nothing.

Above, there is almost no sky. What light there is arrives already spent, a grey damp that never quite becomes day. The air is cool enough to make breath visible and then take the visibility back. Moisture does not fall so much as collect: beads on every surface, a constant faint sweating of the world. Sometimes a brief rain walks through on three legs and is gone. Pockets of standing water exist as if by accident—black mirrors the size of a palm, a rusted drum, the cupped palm of a leaf the size of a door. They do not connect. There is no river. There is only the rumor of one, a dampness that remembers flowing.

The plants are the city.

They occupy the hollow the way architecture occupies a plan that was never drawn. Trunks rise as load-bearing walls, not trees exactly—columnar, fused, their bark a wet iron color, ribbed like cooling fins. Between them, galleries of living lattice: stems braided into vaults, leaves the size of shutters overlapping until they form roofs that leak on purpose. Streets are the spaces the plants have agreed not to grow into, and those spaces are wrong. The vegetation is total and the volume is empty. You walk a lane of dense green architecture and the lane itself is nearly void—vast, hush-wide, as if the plants occupy only their own surfaces and have surrendered the air. Density and abundance refuse to cancel. The contradiction is the weather.

This is a settlement of engines that photosynthesize. Industrial not in steel but in habit. Pump-hearts the size of cottages sit in the forks of the greater trunks, membranes flexing, drawing the intermittent water up through xylem that clanks when the pressure changes. Vats of living wood hold fermenting dark; their sides sweat a thin alcohol that never quite becomes drink. Drive-belts of vine turn slowly, endlessly, with no visible load. Somewhere a press works—seed, fiber, something that used to be ore—and the sound it makes is almost nothing, a wet periodic sigh. Electrification here is a moss that conducts, a pale thread-growth along the undersides of the vaults, carrying a current that tastes of tin when the air is damp enough. Factories without workers. Production without product, or product that evaporates into the next process before it can be named. The town is always mid-shift.

Fauna is everywhere and almost nowhere you can keep.

They are abundant the way condensation is abundant: on every surface, in every pause. Beetles with factory serials etched in their elytra, generations of them, the numbers incrementing. Soft moths the color of unused paper that eat the edges of shadow and leave the objects they shadowed slightly less decided. Things like fish move in the air where the humidity thickens, their sides flickering with a light that is not light, a darkness that has learned to gleam. Larger presences use the empty lanes. You hear them as a change in the quiet—the quiet deepening, becoming attentive—and then a shape that is mostly joints and leaf-litter and an old coat of oil passes at the far end of a gallery and does not cast a shadow so much as borrow one. Predator and prey are a single metabolism that has not finished splitting. Something is always being converted. The conversions are polite. Almost no one screams.

The rules are local and they do not travel.

Gravity here prefers the plants. Drop a stone and it may fall, or it may hang a moment longer than it should, considering the nearest stem. Distances inside the hollow do not add. A walk that should take three minutes takes the time of a shift change, or no time, and you arrive at a courtyard that was behind you. The courtyard is a depression within the depression, lined with the youngest growth—embryonic bark still pale, leaves that have not learned their venation, machinery that is still deciding whether it is fruit. Reality thins toward the center. Names detach from things and drift. You may put your hand on a wall and feel it recede through several possible walls, all of them true, none of them simultaneously present. The industrial and the botanical trade places without announcement: a gear becomes a seed-head becomes a gear with a different number of teeth. Time is one of the fluids the pumps move. It comes in pulses. Between pulses the hollow is older, or not yet born.

Sound stays low because the place absorbs it as a nutrient. Footfalls become part of the substrate. The engines murmur at the edge of hearing, a factory heard through rain, through sleep. When the brief rains come they do not hiss; they negotiate. The loudest events are biological and even those arrive muffled—a sudden unfurling, a fruit opening like a valve, a flock of the paper-moths lifting as one and making the darkness briefly granular. Then the quiet resumes, thicker, fed.

At the lowest point, where the unfinished geology is most honest, the plants have not yet agreed to be buildings. They stand as individuals, confused, dripping. Between them the ground is a paste of clay and root-hair and filings, warm in patches where some buried process still works. This is where the town is still being imagined by whatever imagines it. New streets try themselves as grooves and fail. A wall goes up overnight as a single enormous leaf and by morning has become a row of cooling towers that breathe spore. The fauna concentrate here, not hunting, attending. They are midwife to the next version of the hollow. You can watch a beetle walk a number onto a stem and the stem accept the number as a year.

Stay long enough and the damp gets into the idea of you. Condensation on the inside of thought. The cool never quite becomes cold. The darkness never quite becomes blind; there is always enough un-light to see that you are seeing incorrectly. The hollow does not want you to leave so much as it wants you to become one of the processes it is already running—another quiet conversion in a town that is all plant, all factory, all empty, all full, and only as large as a mistake the land once made and has not yet corrected.
