The dark here is not an absence but a medium, older than any eye that might have tried to drink it. It has weight. It has grain. It fills the mouth like unsweetened powder and the ears like a held breath that never quite ends. Cool, always: the precise temperature of a root cellar that has forgotten both roots and cellars, a mineral coolness that has nothing to do with stone.

You are inside a single feature. The entire city fits, if the outside world still has such measures, inside something you could close a fist around, or lose between floorboards, or mistake for a knot in a beam. The pocket is sealed. Whatever once connected it to weather, to rain, to the idea of a sky, has long since lignified shut. No water moves. What fluids once ran here finished their work so many ages ago that even the memory of wetness has been compressed into a faint, tannic dust that lifts only when the structure itself groans.

And the structure is plant, all of it, without remainder. Not plants growing upon a substrate: the substrate is plant. Floor, wall, “street,” “plaza,” the load-bearing members and the ornamental flourishes and the vast unoccupied volumes between them are one continuous, ancient body that learned, at some primordial stage, the grammar of a city and then never unlearned it. Xylem bundled into colonnades whose spacing is too regular to be accident. Phloem collapsed into dry, papery tapestries that still remember the direction of sugar. Cork cambium thickened into ramparts so slowly that the difference between wall and time vanished. Everything has been worn to its final, simplest curve. There are no thorns left, no serrated edges, no unopened buds. Erosion here was never water. It was the patient subtraction of centuries, the settling of a mass that had already chosen its last shape and then kept choosing it, incrementally, until choice and necessity became the same smooth plane.

The city is mostly empty. Density is a rumor. You can walk (or drift, or simply exist at a point) for what feels like a long time along a boulevard of aligned, petrified petioles and meet nothing but the next identical interval of space. The buildings—if the word still applies—stand far apart, each a hollow tower or a terraced mound or a single enormous, empty follicle, their interiors so vacant they generate their own faint weather: a circulation of dry air too slow to feel as wind, only as a change in the pitch of the sound.

The sound is the other inhabitant. It is loud. A standing chord made of a thousand hollow vessels speaking at once, like an organ whose pipes are the city’s own bones. Sometimes it is a low roar with no source. Sometimes it resolves into sudden, deafening reports—the crack of a fiber that has held since before language and has now, without drama, finished holding. Then the long grind of surfaces adjusting against one another in the dark, wood remembering it was once a river of cells. There are no animals to add their own noises. There never were, or they were metabolized so completely they became another layer of the architecture. The ecology is pure vegetal mass in its terminal state: living only in the most technical sense, a cambium that may still add a cell when the temperature outside the pocket (whatever “outside” means) shifts by a fraction over a decade.

Change is almost geological. A terrace might lean a finger’s width in the time it would take a civilization to rise and forget itself. The “streets” realign according to some internal civic plan written in the language of tropisms so old they have become law. Primitive tools remain, grown over, incorporated: a flake of silica that was once a blade, now a shining inclusion in a knot; a ladder of lashed stems that leads nowhere except to a balcony overlooking a void so complete it has its own silence inside the louder silence. Whoever trained the first shoots into avenues is gone, or was never separate from the plant that dreamed them.

Touch is the only map. Surfaces are fibrous, or they have powdered into a spice-market dust that smells of bark and the ghost of sap and something faintly electric, like the memory of photosynthesis in a place that has not seen a photon since the pocket closed. Some walls have gone glassy where silica replaced the original cells; they feel like cool, dry skin that was never skin. The low elevation is not a valley but a settling: the whole interior has slumped into its flattest, most stable configuration, hills reduced to callus swellings, towers to the height they could maintain without further ambition.

It is strange in the way a word is strange when you repeat it until it becomes only sound. The emptiness feels occupied by the idea of occupation. The roar sometimes seems to parse into syntax. Distances lie: what feels like a district may be the space between two adjacent cells that grew ambitious. The city keeps its urban rules—intersection, plaza, threshold, monument—long after the reasons for those rules have been metabolized into the walls themselves. Nothing here is ruined. Ruin would imply a previous, more complete state. This is the completed state. The oldest possible city, grown rather than built, dried rather than abandoned, speaking rather than silent, hidden rather than lost.

You could leave, if leaving were still a direction that existed. The pocket does not forbid it. It simply does not contain the concept anymore. The dark continues. The chord holds. A fiber, somewhere, considers whether this century is the one in which it will finally let go.
