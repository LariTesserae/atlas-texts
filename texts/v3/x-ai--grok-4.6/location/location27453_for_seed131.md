The hatch is smaller than a coffin lid and set flush in a floor that has forgotten it was ever meant to be walked on. You drop through it into a single chamber that has no outside, only itself.

It is a hall the size of a drowned nave, but it is not large in any civic sense. It is pocketed: a sealed volume cast inside something older and vaster that no longer answers. Every surface is made. There is no soil, no bedrock, no original stone. The floor is a single pour of concrete so thoroughly carbonated it has the color and patience of old bone. The walls are ribbed with iron channels and ceramic tile the glaze of which has gone matte, not from wear but from having finished wearing. The ceiling is a low barrel of the same stuff, weeping. Water does not fall here so much as decide, continuously, to be liquid. It beads on every bolt head, sheets down the tiles in films too thin to catch light, stands in ruled shallows along the floor’s designed gutters. The air is warm and so near saturation that breathing feels like sharing a lung with the room.

What grows does not grow from earth. It grows from the making. Moss has become upholstery an inch thick on the walkways, a wet velvet the color of tarnished copper. Ferns unspool from every expansion joint, their roots threaded into the hairline failures of grout that failed a geological age ago and then, having failed, became permanent. Long, pale aerial roots hang from the barrel vault like the viscera of a machine that learned photosynthesis. They never quite reach the water. Nothing here is trying to finish. The vegetation is lush the way a maintained wetland is lush: total coverage of every available plane, and almost no occupation of the volume. The center of the hall is empty. You could shout across it if shouting were possible.

It is not possible. The sound is not an event. It is the climate. Beneath the floor, or inside the walls—there is no longer a useful distinction—pumps the size of rooms move water that has already been moved, that will be moved again, that has been in motion since the hall reached the shape it will always have. The note is a stacked industrial chord, low enough to live in the sternum, high enough to sheen the inner ear. It does not pulse. It does not cycle. It is the same pressure against the same membranes it has always been. After a minute you stop hearing it as noise and start hearing it as the room’s blood. After ten you understand that silence, if it ever occurred, would be a kind of death the hall is not built to survive. The equilibrium is acoustic as much as hydrologic. Water in, water out, the same cubic meters, the same temperature, the same green load on the same made surfaces. Time has no gradient here. The moss is always this deep. The ferns are always this unfurled. The film on the tiles is always this exact thickness. Nothing is arriving and nothing is leaving. The hall is not abandoned. It is completed.

Light comes from a run of panels in the vault that have forgotten they were once electric. They give a steady, ordinary daylight, the color of a covered sky, without direction and without hour. It is enough to see by and not enough to cast a persuasive shadow. In that light the emptiness reads clearly: the long unoccupied middle, the rare verticals of hanging root, the single narrow catwalk that crosses nothing because there is nothing to cross to. Fauna is a rumor the room tells itself. Once, perhaps, a pale isopod the size of a thumbnail, working the edge of a gutter. Once a moth the color of wet concrete, motionless on a tile for a span that might have been an hour or a season. They do not accumulate. The hall does not feed crowds.

You can walk the perimeter in a few dozen steps. Every step is the same temperature, the same give of moss over made ground, the same transmission of the pumps up through the soles. The tiles record no new marks. The iron has oxidized to a skin that will not flake further; it has arrived at the oxide it meant to be. This is erosion’s last sentence, spoken and then left on the page. The structure has been old for so long that old is no longer a process. It is the material. Low, warm, wet, finished, loud, and hidden inside a world that has other business. The hatch above is the only suggestion that another scale exists. It does not open from this side. It does not need to. The hall is not waiting. It is already the thing it is for.
