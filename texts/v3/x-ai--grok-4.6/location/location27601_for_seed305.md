The high country does not begin. It is already there when you notice you have been inside it, a pale continent of new stone lifted so recently the ridges still remember being seafloor, or mantle, or something that had no name for up. The peaks are not worn. They are breaks. Edges hold an edge. Whole ranges stand like a single unfinished fracture, repeated until the horizon gives up and becomes more of the same fracture, paler, then paler still, under a light that has nowhere polite to sit.

The light is the first law. It does not fall so much as occupy. High thin air, ice-sheen on every unweathered face, and a sun that feels less like a star than a white engine left running. There is glare off the stone, glare off the rime, glare off the living things that have decided brightness is a nutrient. Shadows exist as rumors in the lee of knife-cols and under the stacked bodies of whatever is currently pretending to be a forest. You squint until squinting becomes a climate. On rare hours a second brightness wakes in the rock itself — a slow internal shine, as if the young crust is still cooling through a spectrum human eyes only catch as ache.

It is cold the way a held breath is cold. Not theatrical storm-cold. Patient cold. The kind that has already won and is now only administering. Moisture does not commit. It condenses, beads, feathers into hoar, then thinks better of becoming a river. The whole highland is damp in the way a window is damp in winter: a film, a taste of wet iron, occasional thin rains that arrive already half ice and are drunk before they can find a channel. Pockets of actual water hide in the thermal seams — dark, slow, almost oily with dissolved mineral — where the living engines leak heat. You could walk a hundred miles of ridgeline and never hear a proper stream. You would still finish soaked through the seams of whatever you wore, wrung out of fog and breath and the sweat of too many neighboring metabolisms.

Because there is no empty. Density here is not a statistic; it is the medium. Life has occupied the new stone the way rust occupies a wreck, except rust is shy. What grows is lush in a register that does not care about leaves as you know them. Feather-ice forests stand in wind-still pockets, each frond a laminated cold-plant that drinks rime and exhales a faint sweet ozone. Between them, and on them, and through them, copper-colored mosses thick as muscle, glass kelps that hold themselves upright in air as if the atmosphere were a dim sea, mats of something like lichen if lichen were armored and slightly warm to the touch. Vegetation does not crown the highlands so much as calk them. Every crack that the young uplift provided has been caulked with green-black, silver-green, the dull gold of frozen sap. It is not a jungle. It is a packing.

And the packing has animals in it the way a foundry has heat.

They are not visitors. They are the occupancy. You do not see a specimen; you see a gradient of bodies, sessile and walking and half-mineral, so continuous that “individual” starts to feel like a lowland idea. Some are the size of weather. They squat in the new cirques with flanks like cooling towers, dermal vents ticking, a subsonic idle you feel in the knees. They are not built. Nothing here is built. There are no walls, no roads, no cut stone stacked by intention. The industrial is metabolic. These great idle things crack the young rock for metals the way cattle crop grass, and what comes out of them is heat, a smell of rain on a battery, and a constant working sound — not roar exactly, more the layered complaint of a thousand transformers in a frozen yard. Sparks walk their hides at dusk, which is only a rumor of dusk, a slight cooling of the white.

Smaller fauna fill every leftover interval. Herds that are not herds so much as moving gravel, clicking, electrified, their coats grown in hexagonal plates that shed frost in sheets. Filterers hang in the updrafts along the fresh fault scarps, membranous, loud as flags in a gale, sieving whatever the stone still outgasses. Things like insects if insects came in buckets and sang in chord. The air is never not full. Flight is a crowd. Walking, if you insist on the concept, is a negotiation with hides, stalks, the rubbery give of a moss that is also a lung. There is no between. The between got used.

Sound is weather. Wind finds every unrounded edge and plays it; the ranges are a continent of reeds. Under that, the biological plant never shuts down: a bass you stop hearing only when you notice your teeth are aching, the higher sizzle of corona off antlers and ridgelines, the wet clock of condensation dripping into those rare dark pockets, the migratory thunder that is not thunder but a million plated shoulders deciding, over the course of a season, to be two degrees west of where they were. Loud, and almost unchanging. The volume is a condition, like elevation.

Time agrees. This place does not do events. It does processes. The uplift is still, in some deep sense, happening — a few millimeters, a sigh in the fault, a new scarp gleaming like a cut. The great engine-beasts shift their weight across decades. Forests of glass kelp lengthen toward a brightness that does not move. A rain comes, is absorbed, is transpired as fog, becomes rime on the next range, becomes rain again, a closed damp circuit with no mouth and no delta. You could plant a flag (you will not; there is no soil that is not already someone) and return in a century to find the flag digested, the view identically savage, the light still trying to erase the idea of dim.

The rules are few and they do not care if you learn them.

Brightness is not optional. Eyes adapt or they don’t; the life here grew mirrors and milk-lenses and a habit of never looking up.

Heat is a private property. Steal it from a venting flank if you must, but the owner will know. The cold is the public.

Electricity is a language and a diet. Touch the wrong moss in the wrong dryness and you become, briefly, a path. The fauna converse in voltage more than scent. After a while you can feel sentences moving through the packed ground, slow as seasons: hunger, claim, the long dull news of a mineral seam.

Nothing is made that lasts as a thing. There are no artifacts because manufacture here is digestion and regrowth. A bone is ore. A hide is a solar panel and then a meal. The highland refuses the category of tool the way it refuses valleys that have had time to become kind.

Water is a rumor you believe with your skin. Follow dampness, not channels. The living will have drunk whatever wanted to be a river.

And the strangeness, once your eyes stop flinching, is not decoration. It is load-bearing. There are places — not marked, only slightly more packed — where the fauna have braided themselves into a single working, a valley-wide gut that eats the mountain in place and excretes, over centuries, a finer mountain, paler, sharper, already being recolonized. There are silent corridors that are not empty but phase-locked, a million small bodies humming one note until the note becomes a kind of local physics and compasses sulk. There are nights that are brighter than the days because the ice-algae bloom in a hard ultraviolet and the engine-beasts open their vents and the whole range stands there like a machine that has remembered it is also a chapel. You may hear, under everything, a slower sound still: the new crust settling, pleased with its own edges, not yet taught by weather what a mountain is supposed to forget.

Stay long enough and the scale gets into you. Weather systems are born on one wall of this highland and die of old age on another without ever once seeing a sea. Fronts of condensation roll like pale herds. Charge-storms walk the ridges, stitching beast to peak to beast, and when they pass the mosses are darker, fed, faintly ticking. You understand, without liking it, that this is not a landscape with life on it. It is a single occupied surface, newly risen, brightly industrious, almost waterless, almost changeless, loud as a factory floor that has learned to be a wilderness and sees no reason to choose.

No one built this. No one could. The stone is too recent to have owners, and the life too complete to leave a plot. You are, at best, a moving dampness with opinions, briefly brighter than the rime. The highland does not mind. It has ranges to be, and a white engine overhead, and all the time in the world to finish becoming as sharp as it already is.
