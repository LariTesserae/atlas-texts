The stair does not feel finished. The risers still hold the memory of the chisel in clean parallel scars, and the walls, where a hand trails them, come away faintly grit-wet, as if the cut were made last season and the stone has not yet decided whether to scab or keep bleeding. Heat arrives before the chamber does. It is not the dry slap of a kiln mouth. It is cloth-heat, laundry-heat, the heat of a body that has been running and then stood still in its own steam. By the sixth turning of the stair the air has thickness. By the ninth you can taste metal and chlorophyll together.

The nave opens without ceremony. That is the only word that fits the volume, though no one here uses it. A single vault, longer than it is high, high enough that the ceiling stays a rumor of pale fracture and dripping dark. The rock is young in the way a scab is young. Faces of it still shine. Edges have not been given time to forget they are edges. In two or three places the vault is ribbed with glassy planes, cooled too fast, catching what little light there is and throwing it back the color of weak tea. The floor is not a floor so much as a negotiation: raw shelf, then grating, then brick that has been laid so recently the mortar still sweats, then grating again. Everything is used. There is no interval that has not been claimed.

They built inward and upward because there was nowhere else. The settlement occupies the chamber the way a lung occupies a chest. Walkways stacked on walkways, four and five deep, some timber, most riveted plate gone soft at the edges with condensation. Alleys that require a shoulder-turn. Doorways that open onto other doorways. Stairs that duck under pipes and emerge in someone else’s drying-loft. The buildings are not separate from the infrastructure; the pipes *are* the streets’ lintels, the flues are load-bearing, a cistern’s iron belly is the ceiling of the room beneath it. Laundry never crisps. It hangs in warm flags and is taken down no drier, only cleaner of dust. Windows, where they exist, are small and shuttered against a brightness that is not present. What light there is pools rather than falls: the low orange of lagged furnace-throats, the greener smear of cultivated fungi in glass jars wired to banisters, a few oil lamps turned so far down they are more suggestion than flame. Faces, when you meet them, come together late.

The heat has a source and everyone knows it without pointing. Along the nave’s long axis a channel has been boxed in plate, a river that is not allowed to be a river. It runs somewhere between simmer and sulk. You hear it only if you stand on the grating directly above and wait: a fat, infrequent gulp, like a throat. From that boxed water the works take their living. Slow turbines, lagged until they look like sleeping animals, turn with a patience that is almost insolent. A painted bolt-head on the nearest wheel will travel the width of a thumb while you boil a kettle. Belts as wide as a torso creep. There is a stamp somewhere in the lower works that reports once, perhaps twice, in an afternoon, a single felt concussion more than a sound, and then the hush reseals. They are drawing mineral flush from the young rock, and a dark sweet tar that condenses in coils, and fiber from the plants. The fiber is the reason the air smells the way it does in the upper galleries: boiled leaf, wet rope, a greenness cooked until it turns brown.

Because the chamber is a wet pocket in a drier body of stone, everything that can hold water does. Condensation beads on every unlagged surface and chooses, eventually, to fall. The drops are the settlement’s metronome. They tick on plate, on leaf, on the crown of anyone foolish enough to stand still beneath an unpatched seam. Troughs have been slung under the worst of the driplines; the troughs themselves sprout. Vegetation here is not an ornament and not quite a crop. It is a third architecture. Where a human interval of air tried to remain, the plants took it. Broad pale leaves the size of serving boards crowd the walkways at shoulder height and have to be lifted aside like curtains. Ferns pack the cool sides of pipes in wet jostling ruffs. Vines have been trained and then have trained themselves, crossing alleys on taut wet cables, knitting loft to loft until two families share a ceiling of leaves and pretend not to. In tubs wedged into every otherwise useless corner grow the fiber-trees: short, heat-drunk, trunks the color of old ivory, leaves so thin the furnace-glow shows through them as a map of veins. They are lush in the way a fever is lush. Nothing here is the green of a surface spring. It is the green of things that have never been asked to survive a wind.

There is almost no empty air. To move through the Hollow is to be in continuous slight contact: leaf against wrist, pipe-lagging against hip, another person’s damp sleeve, the furred moss that has made a pelt of the stair-rail. Density is the civic condition. Even the heat occupies. It stands in the alleys and does not step aside.

Animals are a rumor with occasional evidence. Once in a great while a pale thing the length of a finger undulates through a cooling-jacket’s overflow and is gone. Cricket-song, if it is cricket-song, happens in the walls at intervals too long to learn. Children are told about the pipe-eel the way other children are told about wolves, except no one here has quite the energy for fear, and the eels, if they exist, have never taken more than a sock. The silence is otherwise nearly complete. Voices stay in rooms. A laugh travels badly; the leaves take it, the lagged iron takes it, the wet takes it. What remains, if you stop on a mid-gallery and wait, is the boxed water’s gulp, the tick of condensate, the almost-inaudible creep of a belt, and, beneath even that, the stone’s own faint ticking as it continues, with geological manners, to finish being born.

That is the strangeness that does not announce itself and then does. The cavity is not done. Millimeters in a year, a hairline in a decade, the vault is still finding its shape. Fresh shine appears along old cracks like a thought someone had in the night. The settlement’s oldest rule, older than the bylaws painted on the cistern, is not to startle the rock. Hence the lagging, the slow engines, the voices kept small, the stamp that is allowed its rare report only because the alternative is a pressure no one wishes to store. They build to keep pace with an opening that refuses to be finished. A new landing appears against a wall that was blank last year, not because population required it, but because the wall offered, at last, a ledge. Mortar is mixed with the same patience as the turbines. Rust and moss trade places so gradually that a pipe can go from iron to garden without anyone being able to name the week it happened.

Time here is not weather. There is no weather. There is the condensation cycle, heavier after the channel surges, lighter when the works have drawn it down. There is the fiber harvest, which is a matter of walking the vines with a bone knife and filling slings, and which happens when the leaves tell them, not when a calendar does. There is the almost imperceptible brightening of the fracture-planes when the stone above, far above, somewhere in a dryness no one present has seen, takes a season of sun and conducts a rumor of it downward, a dimming of an already dim thing, easily missed. People age. Leaves replace themselves. The painted bolt-head goes around again. The young rock sweats and thinks about opening another hand’s-breadth, and does not, and then does.

At the far end of the nave, where the vault pinches and the heat is worst, there is a wall of the glassy fracture, floor to gloom. They have not built against it. They have hung a single unlit lamp there, as if in courtesy. In that dark mirror the settlement appears twice: once as it is, packed and dripping and slow, and once as a quieter city made of copper smear and leaf-shadow, inhabiting a chamber that has not yet quite consented to exist. You can stand there long enough for your own face to arrive in the glass. It will look like it has been waiting in the stone, damp, and is only now, reluctantly, coming out.
