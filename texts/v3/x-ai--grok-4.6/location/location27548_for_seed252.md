The chamber sits just under the skin of the world, not deep, not a mine, not a cavern system with a name. It is a pocket that was not here a short time ago. Something tore, or something cooked through, and the rock opened the way fruit opens when it splits on the branch. The break is still new. Edges have not learned to be kind.

You would call it a room if rooms were made of heat and wet stone and nothing else. The floor is not far from the ceiling. A tall person would feel the air change near their hair. The space is wider than it is high, but not by much, and most of it is empty. Between the few things that stand in it there is a great deal of unused air.

The light is the first fact. It is not daylight and it is not firelight. It does not come from a hole to the surface. The rock itself is bright. Not every surface equally: some faces of the chamber hold a white-gold glare that makes the eyes water, as if the mineral had been left too close to a furnace and had decided to keep the furnace’s idea of itself. Other faces only shine by scattering, wet and secondary. There are almost no hard shadows. Steam takes the light and rubs it into every corner until the pocket feels overlit, like a closed palm filled with a bulb. The brightness does not flicker. It holds. If you look too long at the brightest wall you see a faint grain in the glow, a standing texture, as if the light were being combed by something finer than the eye. Then you blink and it is only glare again.

It is hot in the way a bathhouse is hot after the water has been run for hours and the stone has agreed to remember. Breath comes in already warmed. Skin films at once. The heat is not even. Near the floor it pools; near certain seams in the wall it lifts in sheets you can see as a waver. Touch the wrong rib of rock and you will blister. Touch another and it is only as warm as a sleeping animal.

Water is everywhere and nowhere deep. The walls weep. Not metaphor: they produce water the way a cold glass produces water in a kitchen, except the rock is not cold. The water arrives from inside the stone, pressed out, or it condenses out of air that cannot hold any more of itself. It runs in threads. It finds the new edges and follows them. It gathers in shallow saucers no deeper than a knuckle, already rimmed with a pale crust that was not there yesterday and will be thicker tomorrow. The pools are clear and they are not still. Heat crawls through them. Fine currents draw grit in circles and then forget the circles. When a drop hits from the ceiling the sound is small and exact, and then a long time passes before the next one.

Steam is the weather. It does not fill the chamber in a blank. It rises in columns from the wetter seams, thins, hangs, and is pulled toward a vent you cannot quite see, a softness in one upper corner where the air is leaving. The steam comes in pulses. A few breaths of thicker white, then a thinning, then again. You can time a kind of minute by it if you want. When a pulse is thick the light goes milky and the far wall recedes. When it thins, every sharp thing returns.

There are no plants. There has never been a leaf in this air. What grows here is mineral and it is only beginning. Along one wall a field of needles has started, silica or salt or something between, each point still so new it looks temporary, like a thought that has not decided to stay. They catch the light and throw it in small hard sparks. On the floor, where water has stood longest, a skin of sinter is spreading in overlapping coins, cream and iron-stain. You can watch it if you are patient. The edge advances by a nothing you would miss if you left and came back in an hour, and would not miss if you stayed. Elsewhere the rock is bare and faceted, freshly broken, planes that still remember the stress that made them. No moss. No stain of old life. A faint iridescent film on one pool might be simpler than life or the first argument for it: a sheen that gathers, tears, gathers again.

The chamber is sparse the way a sentence with few words is sparse. A single standing blade of rock occupies the middle, not a column so much as a shard that failed to fall. It is wet on one side and dry-hot on the other. Around it, space. Against the far wall, three steps of terrace no higher than a hand, already being built by the water, already being dissolved in another place. That is most of the furniture. The rest is distance that happens to be enclosed.

Sound stays near the floor of hearing. Drip. The soft tear of a steam pulse starting. Once in a long while a tick from the ceiling as something cools a fraction or heats a fraction and decides to move. Air exchanges with a low continuous hush, more felt in the ears than heard. If you speak, the room does not give the word back to you whole. The wet kills echo. Then the quiet takes the rest.

It is not still. The place is working. Water arrives and leaves. Minerals leave the water and become stone. Heat arrives from below in surges you feel as a change in the smell of the air before you feel them on the skin: a brief metallic sweetness, then the old wet-mineral breath again. The crack that made the chamber is still settling. A grain of rock lets go and ticks on the floor and becomes another small fact. This is early ground. Nothing here has been rounded by a long argument with weather. The features are sharp because they have not had time not to be.

Once, if you wait, there is an animal. Not a population. A visit. Something pale and thin comes up through a hole at the edge of a pool that you had taken for a stain. It is the length of a finger, or a little more, and it does not look like it was built for this heat, which means it was. It tastes the water with a front that might be a mouth. It does not linger. It goes back the way it came, and the hole is only a hole again. After that the chamber feels emptier, which is a strange thing for a place that was already mostly emptiness to do.

Nothing here was stacked or cut or fastened. There is no tool mark. No soot from a torch. The brightness is not a lamp. And yet the way the light sits in the steam, the way it fails to fall off as fast as glare should, the way certain wet faces hold a grain that looks almost ordered — these are the parts that would trouble a careful measurement. The energy coming out of the walls is more than the simple glow of hot rock should budget for. Heat is being spent as light with an efficiency the ordinary minerals of a young vent do not usually have. You do not need the number to feel it. The chamber is too bright for its own temperature, or too cool for its own brightness. Standing in it, you only know that you have to narrow your eyes, and that the water on your skin is already drying into a salt you did not bring with you.

Above, the world goes on with whatever weather it has. Here the weather is this: pulse of steam, thread of water, a slow bright precipitation, a heat that works the stone from the inside, and long minutes in which the only event is a drop deciding to fall. The pocket will not stay like this. The needles will lengthen or dissolve. The crack will slump. The light may hold or it may find another seam and leave. For now it is a room the earth made by accident and has not yet finished, wet and glaring and nearly silent, with a great deal of unused air and a floor that is only beginning to know itself as a floor.
