The door is not a door so much as a seam someone has forgotten how to close. It sits in the lee of a fabricated cornice, high enough that the real weather, whatever weather still belongs to the unmade world, arrives only as a rumor of wind. The cornice itself is poured, then plated, then scored with a mason’s idea of bedding planes. From twenty paces it reads as rock. From two, the score-marks repeat at too-even intervals and the “lichen” is a ceramic frit fired on in a kiln. Everything you will stand on, lean against, or breathe has been decided.

You duck through. The chamber is smaller than the noise it contains.

It is a single volume, perhaps twelve paces by nine, not quite high enough to forget the ceiling. The scale is pocket: a room that has been asked to hold a climate. There is no natural substrate. Floor, walls, the shallow false-cirque of the “basin,” the ribs that pretend to be strata—all of it is made. Cast iron under enamel. Cast stone over a grid of channels. Copper sweating through its own green. Glass blocks set like frozen windows into the upper walls, each one fogged from the inside, admitting a dim, colorless smear that might once have been daylight. The light never improves. It has the quality of a lamp left in another room.

Water is the first inhabitant you notice, and it is everywhere short of drowning you. It does not fall as weather. It weeps. Designed pores stipple every vertical surface, and from each pore a bead fattened by cold detaches on a schedule so regular it has become a kind of silence underneath the roar. The beads run together into sheets. The sheets find gutters that were poured to look like rills just beginning to incise a slope that has not yet decided to be a slope. The rills feed a marsh that is only ankle-deep and yet has no bottom you would trust: a grating, then a second grating, then a dark that ticks and thrums. The water is near freezing. It smells of metal and of something organic that has been cold for a very long time. Breath leaves you as a standing cloud. The cloud does not drift. It hangs, condenses on the next surface, and is recaptured by the pores. The atmosphere is a closed lung.

Sparse green—if it is green. A film on the wetter enamel, pale and slick, more mineral than plant. In three places, where a drip has been allowed a ledge, a fern has been persuaded to exist. The fronds are white at the tips, almost translucent, and they do not grow. They occupy. That is the whole of the vegetation. There is not enough of it to soften anything.

Then the sound arrives as a fact you have been postponing.

It is deafening in the specific way of many loud things that have agreed never to take turns. Compressors in the walls, industrial and unashamed: the long cyclic haul of pistons, the electrical snarl of coils that have not been allowed to cool since they were first wound. Water slammed through necks too narrow for it. A belt somewhere slapping a housing. And through and over and inside all of that, the fauna.

They are the saturation. They are why the density of the room feels higher than its furnishings. You do not see them all at once because the light refuses you, but you feel the air thickened by bodies. They occupy the gutters and the undersides of the ribs and the fogged faces of the glass. They hang in the condensing layer like a second, living weather. They are not one kind of animal. That would be simpler. They are many kinds pressed into a volume that should have held two or three, and they are all here, and they have always been here.

Something like a grebe, but wrong in the neck, paddles the same two meters of shallows without ever quite arriving. Its wake is identical every time. Pale crustaceans tile the grating so completely that the metal shows only in seams; they feed on the biofilm and on each other in a ratio that does not drift. In the air, a traffic of what you first take for moths and then for bats and then for neither: membranous, cold-blooded, their wings beaded with the same water as the walls, their mouths working on nothing you can see. They do not collide. There is no space in which a collision would be an event. They have fitted themselves into one another’s paths the way the rills were fitted into the floor, and the fitting is old, and finished, and will not be revised.

Under the false ledges, in the dimmest band, things with too many joints wait in heaps that breathe. You would call them amphibian if the cold allowed it. The cold does not care what you would call them. Their throats go. The sound they make is part of the deafening: a wet, unvarying chorus pitched just off the frequency of the nearest compressor, so that the two seem to be arguing from a script.

There are more. There are always more. A long body, furred and dripping, occupies the same curl of gutter you noticed when you came in; it will occupy it when you leave. Eyes in the glass-block fog that are not on the outside of the glass. A sweetness under the metal-smell that is breath, layered, many-throated, never pausing long enough to be counted.

They do not startle. That is the rule you learn in the first minute and then spend the rest of your time failing to believe. Nothing here startles because nothing here is arriving. The chamber is at equilibrium. The compressors do not cycle down. The pores do not close. The grebe-wrong thing does not find the far end of its water. The ferns do not unfurl another increment. If a feather is loosed it is the same feather, and it lands in the same film, and is taken by the same mouth. Time in the room is a held note. You can walk through it; you cannot make it progress. The landscape is embryonic by design and eternal by maintenance: geology that has been built to the exact hour before it would have become itself, and then locked. The rills are always just-cut. The enamel “scree” at the basin’s edge is always the first unsettled clatter of a slope that will never finish failing. Erosion, here, is a costume worn by a machine that will not allow change. The world is under construction and the construction was completed long ago and the ribbon was never cut.

You begin to understand the elevation. This is not a high place in the sense of a view. There is no view. The glass gives back only the room’s own dimness, slightly paler. High means the cold can be kept without argument. High means the condensation can be harvested as if from a cloud that has been folded and put in a box. High means hidden. The whole peak, you suspect now, is an assembly—plates and pours and a skin of convincing ruin—and this chamber is a gland in it, a single industrial organ tasked with remaining the first wet hour of a mountain forever.

The fauna are not visitors to that hour. They are its occupancy. Perhaps they were stocked. Perhaps they condensed here the way the water does, some second product of the same pores. The strangeness is not theatrical. It is the slight wrongness of necks and joints, the way eyes track you without altering any other motion, the way the deafening never peaks or dips, the way your own footprints in the marsh-film fill and vanish as if rehearsed. You are the only gradient. The room works to erase you as a change: water closes, air re-thickens, a wing occupies the space your shoulder just left.

Stay long enough and the cold becomes a pressure behind the eyes. Stay longer and the sound stops being sound and becomes a structure you are inside of, like the ribs, like the gratings. The animals continue. The compressors haul. The beads detach. Somewhere under the second grating a dynamo holds the voltage flat. No one is coming to service it. No one is required. The equilibrium includes the idea of repair the way it includes the idea of dawn: as a rumor from a weather that is not admitted.

When you leave—if the seam still remembers how to be a door—the quiet outside will feel like a hole. You will carry a film of that water on your cuffs, and it will dry slowly, and the salt it leaves will not be quite salt. Behind you the chamber will be as it was. The grebe will be in mid-paddle. The fern will occupy. The mouthed air will be full. The mountain, which is not a mountain, will go on manufacturing the first moment of itself, wet and dim and crowded past reason, and it will not notice that you were the only thing that happened.
