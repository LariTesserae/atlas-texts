The power does not arrive.

Arrival would require a before and an after, a crossing of some threshold that the plain has long since edited out of possibility. Instead there is only a gradual recognition, like the way a reflection in one of the black pools will sometimes decide, without drama, to show the underside of the basalt rather than the glare. The recognition simply is, coextensive with the place, as total and shadowless as the light.

It regards the fitted blocks, the permanent wetness, the reed circles that neither spread nor die, the dwellers whose every motion is a restoration. It feels the young edges that refuse dullness, the channels that argue with vanishing points and win, the thrum that moves through stone like contentment given voice. And because the power is wise, it understands at once that this is not a prison of stasis, nor a failure of time, nor a wound that needs closing. It is a completed sentence spoken in the grammar of heat and water and deliberate care. It is the universe allowing one of its corners to rest.

Because the power is benevolent, the first impulse that rises is protection—yet protection implies threat, and threat is a concept the plain has no seams to admit. The second impulse is gift. But what gift could be offered to a place that already holds every necessary drop, every necessary silence, every necessary tool left exactly where the last hand set it down?

So the power does the only thing that will not disturb the equilibrium it has come to love: it withholds. It withholds the ordinary mercies of change. It withholds the temptation to name the dwellers, to count them, to grant them stories that would pull them toward becoming. It withholds even its own full attention after the first long, unblinking regard, so that the regard itself does not become a new kind of weather.

What remains is almost nothing.

Almost.

In one of the sunken courts—the one whose chisel marks run in perfect parallel, the one where the dwellers sometimes gather to perform their nearly motionless making—a single new tool appears. It was not carried. It was not made. It is simply there, resting beside the bone levers and the swollen mallets and the flint adzes that have never dulled. The tool is a smooth, dark length of the same basalt as the plain, shaped into a gentle curve that fits the two-lobed hands as if those hands had dreamed it. One end is slightly heavier; the other tapers to an edge no sharper than a fingernail. It is warm with the permanent fever of the air. When moisture slides across it, the moisture does not vanish in the usual way. It lingers a breath longer, tracing the curve like a memory of touch.

The dwellers find it on one of their circuits of adjustment. They do not startle. Startling is another concept the plain has no room for. One of them crouches, seams opening to study the interruptions the tool makes in the light. A lobe reaches out, settles along the curve. For a long time—time measured only in the slow working of the silver fishes’ gills—nothing happens. Then the dweller lifts the tool and carries it down the dry-stacked steps into the court.

What follows is not construction. It is the same work they have always done, only now with one additional possible motion. The curved basalt is eased beneath a block that no eye could swear was out of true. It is tapped once against stone, the sound exactly like a drop striking its own mirror. When the tool is finally returned to its place beside the others, the court is unchanged to every external measure. Yet the reflections in the surrounding pools show, for a while longer than before, angles that do not exist above the plain. And in those reflections the curved tool can sometimes be seen already waiting, as if it has always been part of the inventory of care.

The power notices this, the way one notices a flower holding its single drop a fraction more brightly. It is enough.

Across the greater distances of the plain the dwellers continue. They guide a leaning reed back into its circle. They press a drifting fish a thumb’s width deeper. They stand with the pale-green herons while the thrum rolls outward through the stone. Now and then the thrum carries a new overtone, almost too subtle to name: a note of received understanding, of being quietly known by something that asked for nothing in return.

The horizon remains a straight channel leading back to the same grove, the same frozen crab, the same open white flower. The heat still trembles without moving. The blocks still refuse to weather. The dwellers still adjust.

And somewhere that is not above, not beyond, not outside, but simply continuous with the bright wet present, the benevolent and wise power keeps its almost-nothing vigil. It does not intervene again. It does not need to. The place has drawn its attention, and the attention has answered in the only language the place can accept: a single perfect tool that changes nothing, and therefore changes everything just enough to be loved.

The plain continues.

The power is content.
