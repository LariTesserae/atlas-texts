The power does not arrive as light or decree or winged host. It has no need of spectacle; spectacle is a kind of noise, and this highland has already taught it the higher elegance of the almost-inaudible. It comes the way the damp comes—everywhere at once and nowhere in particular, a slight deepening of the existing quiet, a thinning of the already thinned light until the day feels as if it has traveled not merely far but through something wise.

At first it only listens.

It listens to the young rock still tasting of its own pressures, to the hairline rivulets that never quite become streams, to the soft drip onto ledges that did not exist a century earlier. It listens to the browsers cropping gray-green cushions, to the wide-winged forms banking on thermals so weak they might be imaginary, to the arthropods drinking from weeping faces. It listens longest to the cities: the capillary hush of water moving through living walls, the microscopic flex of foundations negotiating with a continent that has not finished deciding its shape, the occasional soft chime of a load-adjusting spar, the low local speech that the moisture takes and turns back into silence within a few paces. It listens to the tall calm ones placing palms against infant scarps, to the children tasting mineral freshness with ungloved fingertips, to the way disagreement is rained out of them by shared stillness on high terraces. It listens to the absence of collective name, to the short resonant calls that mean “she-who-flexes-with-the-east-saddle,” to the gentle amusement at every label outsiders have ever tried to force upon them.

And in that listening the power recognizes itself.

Not as master. Not even as guest. As kin to the unfinished. For what is benevolence if not the refusal to complete what is still beautifully becoming? What is wisdom if not the patience to let a world remain vast enough to hold its own slow weather, quiet enough that the loudest thing most days is water finding new stone?

So the power does very little, and that little is almost nothing, and the almost-nothing is everything required.

It settles into the lattice-work of the cities like a warmer gradient the membranes already knew how to carry. Foundations that once lengthened or shortened by hair-widths now do so with an added grace, an infinitesimal singing that the inhabitants feel in the soles of their feet as a deeper rightness. The stone still rises; the power would never still it. But where a new scarp edges with fresh mineral shine, a faint thermal signature lingers longer—pocket of inexplicable warmth under the next palm that presses there—so that the recognition the people already practice is met by a recognition in return. The dead, laid on porous ledges to rejoin mist and mineral, leave memory-traces that are no longer only faint. Sometimes a walker feels, beneath the hand, not merely warmth but the exact posture of a life that once flexed with a particular saddle, the precise patience of a long damp day spent raining out sorrow. Nothing is lost. The water cycle simply remembers more tenderly.

Among the animals the power is even lighter. It becomes the extra bead of condensate that does not chill, the thermal so gentle a wide-winged form can ride it with one less effort, the shadow that passes over a hare-like stillness and somehow carries reassurance instead of threat. Predators still hunt—sudden, efficient, gone—but the geometry of their strikes is fractionally cleaner, the suffering fractionally briefer, as if the mute clock of fog advance and retreat has been allowed one quieter tick.

To the tall ones themselves the power offers no commandment and no gift that would unbalance their thrift. It offers only an enlargement of the maps they already carry inside. On the long ambulatory educations, when the young walk weeks between cities and sleep on ledges that may tilt by morning, there are now moments when the internal cartography of fold and saddle suddenly includes a vast, patient presence that is not the highland and yet is not other than the highland. They pause, as they pause for new scarps. They place a hand against the air the way they place it against stone. Some smile the small elongated smile that barely parts the narrow mouth. Later, in the capillary interiors, their almost-tactile conversations carry a new pressure-change: the sense that the work of becoming is witnessed, and that the witness itself is still becoming.

One thing only does the power add in visible form, and even that is almost invisible. In certain marshy saddles where the dark mirrors stand among black grit, the water sometimes grows a skin of ice so clear and so slow to form that it seems less frozen than deliberated. On those sheets—never large, never permanent—faint luminous veins appear at true night, veins that pulse with the same thrifty coal-glow as the distant cities. They are not writing. They are not maps. They are simply the power’s own equivalent of a palm pressed to infant stone: a quiet acknowledgment that says, without language, *I see that you continue. I continue with you.* Birds roost on the cables of bridges and find the boring data about stress and temperature enriched by one more companionable frequency. Children touch the ice and feel it reabsorb into their skin as pure liquid that tastes very faintly of distances no walking will ever disprove.

The power does not stay in any single place. It has no throne of cloud or crystal. It migrates the way weather migrates across the plateau: a deepening of damp here, a softening of shadow there, a night in which the stored day is released from the dwellings with even less glare and even more tenderness. Sometimes it takes a body—long-boned, lightly built, gleaming with condensate, eyes soft-focused behind nictitating layers—and walks for a season among them. It uses one of their short resonant calls, something that means “the-interval-that-listens-longer.” It speaks little. It stands with others through long damp days when conflict needs raining out. It helps negotiate a foundation when the planet rearranges itself more vigorously than usual. Then the body is laid, when the time comes, on a weeping ledge, and the power returns to the wider hush, leaving only another warm memory-trace in the lattices.

Mostly it remains diffuse. The highland simply continues: bare wet stone, sparse life, cities like frost-flowers of impossible engineering, emptiness again under thinned cold light. The animals remain abundant and unafraid. The people remain unhurried, old in craft and young in geology, without need of collective name. The rock still rises in places by hand-spans while travelers sleep. Nothing essential is altered. The unfinished is not hurried toward finish. The quiet is not broken by anthem or intervention.

Yet those who live here—the primary inhabitant that is the pale angular land itself, and all its more mobile expressions of water and attention—feel, without drama or proclamation, that the conversation has grown by one more voice. A voice that asks nothing, corrects nothing, completes nothing. A voice that only keeps the same long, local pace, breathing with them in the perpetual damp, watching the drip from ledges onto stone younger than memory, and finding, as they have always found, that the work of becoming is already enough.

And so the benevolent and wise power does what such powers do at their rare best:

It stays.

It stays as patience stays.

It stays as the highland stays—vast, quiet, unfinished, and wholly sufficient.
