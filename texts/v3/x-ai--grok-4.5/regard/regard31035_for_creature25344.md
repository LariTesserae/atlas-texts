The power arrives the way the wind has always arrived: without announcement, without edge, simply present across the whole of the bright country at once. It does not descend. It does not take form that the thinned blue could outline. It is a regard, vast and precise, that settles over the plateau the way night settles—relative, incomplete, more a change in the quality of attention than a darkening.

It listens first.

It hears the continuous chord that lives in every skull, the mineral cold that never quite leaves the bone, the dry percussion of armored scrapers turning death back into grit. It hears the soft-footed ones counting seeds in the indigo hours, the spiders tuning their hammocks to the half-tone lee, the heavy flyers folding sky into sudden absence. It hears the walkers’ sparse language of closed hands and sternum taps, the half-twist they give a cairn stone, the unspoken fierce cherishing that keeps a child’s breath checked through the glowing dark. It hears, beneath all of it, the slower utterance of the stone itself—the almost imperceptible loss of dust, the patience that finished becoming long ago and has no further argument to make.

The power understands, with the clarity that is its nature, that this place is not unfinished. It is not a wound. It is not a question waiting for an answer. The equilibrium is complete: harsh, roaring, exact. Every coat and carapace, every bifacial flake and root-fiber patch, every private rule about distances that stretch and flocks that refuse a quarter of sky, has been ground to fit by the same irreversible abrasion. To add ease would be to add longing. To add plenty would be to add the first true silence the wind has never allowed—the silence of something that has begun to dream of elsewhere. Benevolence without wisdom would flood the seeps, still the air, soften the light, and watch the whole fitted world come undone in a single generous season. The power is not without benevolence. It is simply not without wisdom either.

So it does not change the water.

It does not quiet the chord.

It does not correct the mild wrongness that keeps the walkers’ heads tilted a degree and their routes honest.

What it does is smaller, and slower, and almost nothing.

It leans its regard into the permanent northwest pressure until the wind’s enormous note acquires, in places too vast for any single pair of ears, a second interval—an undertone so low it is felt in the pad of a grazer’s foot or the silk of a spider’s tarsus long before it is heard. The harmonic does not instruct. It does not console. It merely steadies. Where distances have grown private and mischievous, they remain so, yet the steadiness makes the misbehavior somehow companionable rather than merely strange. A ridge that decides to take half a day still takes half a day, but the walker who crests it no longer feels mildly cheated; they feel met. The fragmentary almost-voices that once rode the wind continue, yet now and then they resolve, for the length of a breath, into something that is not quite language and not quite weather—an acknowledgment without demand.

The power touches the hidden hollows inside the oldest windbreaks, the ones added to across generations until they are only another outcrop. In those sheltered pockets where cordage is mended and needles hardened, it leaves no object, no light, no warmth beyond what the dung-fire already gives. It leaves instead a slight increase in the likelihood that the next hand which reaches for a remembered tool will find it exactly where the last hand set it down, even after seasons. A trivial mercy. A refusal of one small forgetfulness. The walkers will not name it. They will simply patch their gear a little longer before it fails, and the pattern of repairs that serves as a name will grow one season more complex, one season more legible to whoever comes after.

Once, in a rubble field where the wind’s note falls away into something like quiet, the power pauses beside the shed carapaces of the older things that no longer walk in the open. It does not wake them. It does not mourn them. It matches the iridescence of their fluting with a regard that is itself a kind of iridescence—seen only by the stone, kept only by the dust. And somewhere at the far edge of what even the walkers call vision, the slow bulk that moves against the wind with jointed patience finds its next step met by an equal and answering patience, so that for a little while the two immensities travel in the same direction without collision or recognition.

The children born in the years that follow are no more numerous than before. They still learn to walk in an adult’s wind-shadow, still sit motionless while translucent spiders measure their fingers. Yet when they press close at night under the indigo that never becomes true black, the humming that sometimes rises inside the wind’s chord lasts a few heartbeats longer, and the youngest breathe a fraction more easily through the check. No story is told about it. The stories remain walked, not spoken: this ridge, that hollow, the place the heavy flyers refuse. Only the wind carries the difference, and the wind has always carried everything.

The power does not stay in the way a resident stays. It does not leave in the way a visitor leaves. Its attention becomes one more private rule of the plateau—felt most clearly in the moments when a band pauses at a hinge-point where two wind-currents quarrel, and every face turns a few degrees into the pressure, and for an instant the whole bright, cold, roaring country seems to lean back. Then the moment passes. The herds resume their slow tides. The scrapers click. The walkers adjust the wraps across their faces and continue toward the next thin mercy of rime or seep.

Nothing is building toward a future different from this.

The stone continues its almost imperceptible loss of dust.

The sky presses down with its thinned, eternal blue.

And the benevolent and wise power, having done almost nothing, having changed almost nothing, simply remains woven into the chord—an interval so perfectly fitted that the plateau, which has already arrived at the only shape it means to keep, does not even have to notice it is no longer entirely alone.
