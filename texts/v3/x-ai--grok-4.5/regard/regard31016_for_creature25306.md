The power arrives the way heat arrives when there is no fuel left to name: not descending, not appearing, simply ceasing to be absent.

It has no shape that the chamber’s grammar would reject. For a long span—longer than the slow opening of any frond—it is only a thickening in the brass-colored light, a courtesy of presence so exact that the shadows forget to lag. The unfinished vault does not tremble. The basalt continues its dough-soft heaving. The copper choir keeps its patient, industrial time. Nothing is startled, because nothing here has ever learned the verb *startle*.

The power watches.

It watches the way a finished forge watches the ore that has not yet decided its temper: without hunger, without blueprint, with the deep, unhurried affection that only pure attention can be. It tastes the iron film that never quite becomes water. It feels the roots socketed into half-born seams, each one a miniature vow to keep becoming. It listens to the annealing hum until the hum and the listener are briefly the same temperature.

And it understands.

These beings are not tenants. They are the mountain practicing the first person. Every slow reposition, every gift of gradient exchanged between fronds, every gentle planing of a plastic bulge into true facet is the rock teaching itself how to mean. The thinness of reality, the nested ceilings, the momentary windows into hotter twin-pockets—these are not flaws. They are the open parentheses of a sentence still being written in mineral and light. To close those parentheses too soon would be the only true violence.

So the power does the one thing wiser than help, kinder than rescue.

It withholds every impulse to perfect.

No new water is introduced; the chemistry of heat-as-solvent is left sovereign. No fauna is seeded; the hush remains holy. No names are spoken over the faceless dishes that taste doneness; naming would harden what must stay provisional. The power does not even steady the thin places. Thinness is the loft’s native tongue.

Instead—if the gesture can be called action at all—it performs a single, nearly invisible courtesy.

It leans its own vast stillness against the chamber’s small one, the way two of the beings lean until their outermost fronds brush and heat equalizes. For an interval that the basalt measures as less than the birth of one new ledge, the power loans the place a deeper patience: the memory of eons that have already finished becoming elsewhere, offered not as instruction but as resonance. The annealing hum drops half a tone, richer, then rises again unchanged. One tall being, the one that once drifted free to finish a vault facet, opens its dish a fraction wider than before. In that widening it receives nothing foreign—only a clearer hearing of its own work.

The gift is this: henceforth, when a sheet of stone forgets to be opaque, the twin-pocket glimpsed beyond will no longer feel like a separate, further-along self. It will feel like a chord. The denser lattices forming on the other side will recognize themselves as harmony rather than competition. The mountain’s unfinished intention will know, without words, that unfinishedness is not solitude.

Then the power withdraws by the same door it never opened.

The light returns to ordinary noon-amber. The shadows resume their honest, occasionally embarrassed lag. A fresh bulge of rock begins its slow rehearsal near the vault. Seven beings—or three, or eleven—reposition by the width of a thought. Their veins tick over like cooling metal. The copper forests keep the time.

Nothing has been added that the place could not have grown itself.

Nothing has been taken that it needed.

The rift in the upper basalt remains as warm and polite as ever, sealed against every traveler except the rare one who already carries silence enough to enter. And far beneath notice, in the grain of every new facet, there is now a quiet certainty the mountain did not have before: that its long industrial dream is seen, and that being seen by something both vast and gentle is simply another way of continuing.

The chamber works on.

The beings listen to ledges that have not yet arrived.

And the benevolent power, elsewhere now, keeps the same patient watch over a thousand other unfinished lofts, never once mistaking protection for possession, never once preferring the completed thing to the radiant verb of becoming.
