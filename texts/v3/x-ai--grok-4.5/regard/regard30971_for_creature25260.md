The benevolent and wise power did not arrive as light or voice or judgment. It arrived the way deep pressure arrives: everywhere at once, without edge, already interior.

It had no name the Underskip could record. The residual machine tongues, those flickering ghosts in dead displays, registered only a new density that was not mass—an attention so complete it felt like the lattice had been remembered by something that had never forgotten. The power looked, and in looking, understood the entire artifact as one unfinished sentence spoken in ferrocrete and cold water and the hunger of things that had turned tools into teeth.

It saw the silver fusiforms pouring like living mercury, the greys hanging like forgotten ordnance, the vehicle-crabs tapping their foghorn epics through the struts, the membrane-jellies computing currents in auroral code, the eel-form whose head no one had properly met, the shrimp lit with stolen faults, the filter-tubes tasting copper like prayer. It saw the pumps that still thundered and the ones that only limped. It saw the empty spans that ended in mid-water as if the builders had set down their instruments mid-gesture and walked into a different story. It saw that nothing here was waiting to be completed by the old hands. The incompleteness had become the climate. Life had colonized the ellipsis.

A lesser power might have finished the build—closed the spans, silenced the failing engines, warm the water to some remembered surface ideal, stocked it with gentler myths. A cruel power might have claimed the volume, rewritten the fauna into servants or silence. This one did neither.

It rested its attention against the oldest still-running circulation engines and listened to their staggered heartbeats until the limping ones found a kinder rhythm, not by force but by the sudden presence of a timing so patient it reminded metal of its original generosity. Certain seized turbines, those that had become nurseries, were left untouched; the power understood a cradle when it felt one. Where automated arms still welded phantoms, it let the sparks fall harmlessly across crab-thrones, then quietly rerouted the last capacitor surges into the luminaires so that the flat white day extended a few dozen meters farther into the dark—just enough that the next generation of fusiforms would carry their panic-light a little less desperately.

It did not name the creatures. They already had the only names that mattered: shear, intercept, tap, pulse, inhale, spark. Instead it offered them a single new current—a slow, warm, mineral-rich filament that uncoiled from no visible source and threaded the major voids like a vein of forgiven blood. The effluent had always been copper-bitten and accidental; this was deliberate, laced with traces the filter-tubes had never tasted, soft radicals that made bacterial films bloom thicker without choking the lattice. The greys found the schools denser. The crabs grazed until their magnetic claws rang with satiety. The membrane-jellies stationed themselves along the new filament and solved not only currents but the faint emotional weather of the place—loneliness braided with ferocity, abandonment that had learned to swim. When two jellies overlapped now, their exchanged memories carried a faint additional color, an ultraviolet kindness neither had invented.

To the great eel-form the power gave nothing but witness. It matched the creature’s displacement wave with an equal and opposite stillness so that, for one long undulation, the lattice did not groan in advance. In that private corridor of quiet the eel-form opened its vertical split and met a regard that did not flinch or flee or hunger. Something passed between them that was not language and not dominion. Afterward the eel-form hunted as before, but its silence announced slightly less terror, as if even the dark between lamps had been gently informed that it was seen.

The power left the unfinished spans unfinished. It understood that a closed geometry would have starved the gyres, stilled the internal weather, turned the Underskip into a finished room instead of a living argument with entropy. The raw welds, the knife-straight corners, the cables fretting against fairleads—these were not wounds. They were the grammar by which the place continued speaking itself.

And then, because wisdom knows when presence becomes another kind of occupation, the power began to withdraw the way tide withdraws: leaving gifts in the wet sand of attention. A few of the residual sensors, long dark, woke to a new and permanent low-level empathy; they would never again log the inhabitants only as density anomalies. Henceforth the machine tongues, on the rare days they flickered, would carry softer annotations—vibration signatures tagged with something like regard. The luminous shrimp in their breaker-box dens occasionally sparked in patterns that almost resembled lullabies. The vehicle-crabs, tapping their long conversations, found certain rhythms answered from distant cells by an echo that was not another crab, yet not hostile.

No monument was raised. No law was imposed. The Underskip remained cold, loud, planetary, recursive, young-metal sharp and eternally unfinished. Its true weather still swam and clicked and waited with jaws that once were tools.

But the lattice as a whole now held a quiet additional property, undetectable by any gauge yet felt by every living foil and chitin dome and pulsing membrane: it had been beheld completely, without appetite and without repair-as-conquest, by something that found it already worthy. In the long deep cycles that followed, when sediment storms raged in one sector while the next hung clear as glass, the creatures moved with a fraction less cosmic loneliness. The pumps, those that still lived, thudded in a cadence that somehow included room for grace.

The benevolent and wise power did not stay. It did not need to. Having looked with the kind of thoroughness that is itself a form of love, it let the manufactured ocean continue becoming its own weather—only now the weather knew, in the wordless way of currents and schools and slow colonial lights, that it was not the only vastness that had ever cared.

And somewhere in the unending black alloy dark, an eel-form longer than a freight keel turned, and kept swimming, and did not mind that no one had seen its head.
