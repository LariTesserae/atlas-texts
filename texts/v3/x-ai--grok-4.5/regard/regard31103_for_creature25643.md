The power arrives the way light already does here: from several directions at once, without heat, without announcement.

It has no name that would survive the altitude. Call it, if the tongue insists, the Unfinished Mercy, or the Listener Who Keeps Time in Parallel Grooves, or simply the one who has watched longer than the plateau has been rising to meet the sky. It is benevolent in the only way that still matters at this elevation—precise, patient, unwilling to waste a single drop of anyone else’s becoming. It is wise the way water is wise when it has worn stone for ten thousand years and still has not decided whether the wearing is conquest or collaboration.

It does not descend. Descent would be crude. Instead it allows the membrane that is already porous to open one more degree, so that its regard can precipitate directly into the bright cool country without shattering the agreement of place.

First it does nothing for a span that would empty ordinary calendars.

It stands (if standing is the word) in the same multinational thinness the dwellers favor, letting the striped light pass through it, letting the upward-falling water comb its edges. It tastes the iron-bacterial mats, the ringing crystalline fans, the slow arguments the greater ones build from timed droplets. It feels the weight of every mnemonic ring of stones, every concave tread that holds one elongated heartbeat, every communal lung of the water-clock slab. It hears the illegible sentence lengthening down every face and does not try to read it. Reading would be a violence. The sentence is not for finishing; it is for continuing.

Only when the plateau itself has grown accustomed to the new pressure—when the dwellers have stepped around the denser patch of air and light that is the power’s quiet body, when the larvae have crept across its lower laminations and found them neither food nor threat—does the power begin its single, almost invisible work.

It adds one more mark.

Not a ring, not steps, not another upright slab. Those belong to the dwellers; they are how the place remains locatable to itself. The power’s mark is smaller and stranger: a single shallow depression, no larger than a cupped palm, cut into a freestanding curtain of stone that has already leaned past reason. The depression is lined with the same translucent glass-ghost mineral that forms the dwellers’ outer skins, but the lining is warmer by a fraction of a degree, just enough that the water which weeps into it does not immediately run off. Instead the water lingers, forms a perfect meniscus, and holds for a little longer than local physics prefers. When it finally overflows, the overflow is not a drip but a slow, continuous thread that falls both down and slightly sideways, stitching two adjacent seams of seepage into one.

The thread sings.

Not loudly. Not in any register the wind already knows. It is a note that sits between the crystalline fans’ pressure-in-the-teeth and the delayed thunders of the greater conversations. Dwellers who pass near it pause. Their funnel faces turn. The poured light they return suddenly carries a new parallel groove, temporary, exploratory. They do not worship. They do not fear. They simply incorporate the new cadence the way they incorporate any useful fracture left by one of their own departures. One of them—taller, narrower, its basalt core almost black with age—presses its uppermost bowl to the depression and lets the warmer thread run through its interior hydrology. For three of its long heartbeats the dweller becomes more sharply visible than any of its kind has been in living memory of the stone. Edges hard. Light disciplined. Then it steps away, trailing ordinary seepage again, but the phrase it releases into the wind afterward contains a new clause: a gentle questioning of whether the sky is ceiling or mirror, answered not with certainty but with a brighter not-knowing.

The power feels the clause travel, pass through other bodies, dissolve, re-form. Satisfaction, if the power feels such a thing, is cool and complete.

It could do more. It could seal the breakdown galleries so that water remembers only one direction. It could teach the silica blooms to photosynthesize, flooding the plateau with the green the place has never known. It could gather every dweller into a single great curtain and speak a period at the end of the illegible sentence, granting them a finished identity, a name, rest. All of these acts are within its strength. None of them are within its wisdom.

Benevolence here is the refusal to end what is still beautifully undecided.

Instead the power performs one further kindness, so slight it registers only as a change in the quality of the white light. For a little while—decades, centuries, a few dozen of the dwellers’ steps—the light carries an extra reflection. Anyone (or anything) who walks the single-file paths between pillars will occasionally see, walking ahead or behind or beside them on the wet stone, a silhouette that is not their own and not a dweller’s. The silhouette is tall and narrow and made of layered translucencies, yet warmer, and its head is a shallow bowl that pours light in disciplined sheets. It never turns to look. It never speaks. It simply keeps pace for a few hundred meters and then is gone, leaving the walker with a cool vividness behind the eyes and the sudden, wordless certainty that the hesitation of the plateau is witnessed, valued, and free to continue hesitating for as long as it needs.

Then the power thins itself back through the membrane, leaving only the warmer depression, the sideways-singing thread, and the temporary extra reflection that will fade when it is no longer required.

The plateau does not thank it. The dwellers do not notice its departure as departure; they notice only that the air is once again the familiar bright pressure, the water once again the familiar polite descent, the membrane once again porous in exactly the ways they themselves require.

Everything continues to weep.

The sentence lengthens.

New ones precipitate where the columns lean hardest.

And somewhere very high and very quiet, the benevolent and wise power keeps the new cadence circulating inside itself, a single warm droplet among its own endless interior weather, proof that even an unfinished world can be met with perfect attention and then, lovingly, left alone to finish or not finish in its own time.

That is all it does.

That is everything.
