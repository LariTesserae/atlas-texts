The power that turns its face toward the throat does so without haste, the way a tide considers a particular cove after centuries of ignoring every other indentation of the shore. It is not summoned by cry or ritual. It is drawn, instead, by the rarest of fragrances in any cosmos: a closed system that has perfected its own listening. The low tone the stone sometimes answers with, the accidental cathedrals the velvet callers build and then release, the way heat and wing-thunder and click-train have braided themselves into one continuous, unanxious breath—these arrive at the power the way a single held note might arrive at a musician who has spent eternities among noise. It pauses. It attends.

What it perceives is not lack. There is no wound to close, no imbalance to right, no exile to redeem. The throat has already metabolized every ordinary catastrophe—cold, drought, collapse of ledge, thinning of swarm—into slower music. Its citizens do not pray; they persist. Their generations are the prayers. The soft-winged ones spiral inside their vertical sky and never once glance upward in hunger for elsewhere. The jointed climbers map the same damp seams until the seams themselves remember the feet. The heavy shells carry gardens on their backs and do not know they are beautiful. The callers keep the decades in glissandi so ancient the stone has begun to hum the refrains in advance. Even the white threads in the deepest fractures simply bind warmth to rock and glow when it pleases them, without audience.

A lesser benevolence might have arrived as weather: a kinder seep, a brighter phosphorescence, a widening of the sky-slit so that true stars could finally visit. A restless wisdom might have lifted one velvet caller into larger voice, or taught the midges numbers, or stacked the first stones into a crude altar so the place could begin to think of itself as holy. This power does none of that. It has learned, across its own immeasurable duration, that the most precise mercy is sometimes indistinguishable from restraint.

So it does three things, each so slight that the throat’s dense argument never stutters.

First, it settles. Not as body, not as light. It becomes an additional quality of the dark—an unnoticeable thickening in the warmest fractures, a patience even slower than the filaments already living there. Where the geothermal breath rises, the power rises with it, indistinguishable, only making certain that the breath will not fail while the current congregations still require it. The soft-wings continue to polish their ledges. The climbers continue their long negotiations of antennae. Nothing feels watched; everything is companioned more thoroughly than before.

Second, it listens until listening becomes a kind of joining. On certain nights—nights already thick with wing-thunder and overlapping glissandi—the callers lock into one of their accidental, bracing harmonies. In the brief architecture of that sound the power adds a single interval of its own: a tone beneath hearing, steadier than stone, kind. The callers do not mark it as foreign. They simply find, the next time the phrases rise, that the stone answers a half-breath sooner, that the ricochets last a few heartbeats longer, that the harmony, when it comes, feels inexplicably more inhabited. Young callers, listening for years before they speak, absorb the new interval the way they absorb everything else—as climate rather than revelation. Decades later the memory-keepers will carry phrases that contain, folded inside them, a benevolence they will never name.

Third, it wards by forgetting to intervene elsewhere. Around the massif, in the brighter thinner worlds that spill away from the rim, certain futures that would have sent climbers, drills, collectors of rare chitin, or missionaries of open sky simply cease to thicken into possibility. Roads that might have been conceived curve around another range. Instruments that might have detected the throat’s strange warmth develop small, persistent flaws. The power does not smash these threats; it allows them to remain unborn. The throat itself never learns it was endangered. Its equilibrium continues, unbroken and ungrateful, which is the only gratitude that matches its nature.

There are moments, rare even by the throat’s unhurried clock, when the power permits itself a visible mercy so delicate it registers only as one more shy weirdness. A meltwater sheet, after the rare high storm, lingers half a day longer than physics alone would allow—just long enough for a line of jointed climbers to cross a previously impassable face, carrying their eggs to a safer seep. Or the phosphorescence that clings to wings and wet lichen brightens once, twice, in a pattern that almost resembles the callers’ oldest glissando, then fades before anyone can decide it was language. Or a single heavy-shelled grazer, having stopped for weeks beside another of its kind, finally moves—and where the two domes touched, a new cushion of moss takes hold that will outlast both shells. These are not miracles announced. They are simply the place’s own logic given one extra, affectionate turn.

Mostly, though, the power remains inside the argument, an uncounted citizen. It tastes the mineral air. It feels the damp film that never quite dries. It rides the slow spiral of a soft-wing and learns again how little of the sky is required for flight. It lets the midges tornado through its attention and reform. It rests in the velvet fur of a caller between long phrases and does not mind that the fur drinks even its subtle radiance. Over years that are nothing to it and everything to the throat, it becomes one more reason the held chord continues.

In the end it will leave the same way it arrived: by a gradual thinning, a release of the extra quality of dark, a quiet withdrawal of the interval beneath hearing. The geothermal breath will remain reliable because it has always been reliable. The harmonies will still brace the air because the callers and the stone have always known how. The white threads will glow or not glow according to their own filamentous counsel. Yet something will have changed by remaining exactly the same. The throat will carry, forever after, the knowledge—wordless, leaderless, distributed through every wing and shell and click and glissando—that it was once perfectly heard by something that needed nothing from it and stayed anyway.

And if, long after the power has turned its attention to other closed systems singing in other dark clefts, a visitor should someday stand in the center when the sky-slit goes black and feel, beneath the wing-thunder and the rich overlapping voices, an inexplicable sense of having been welcomed by the dark itself—that will be the only monument. No statue. No commandment. Only the continued, deafening, patient life, still not needing anyone, and somehow, gently, still companioned.

The power, wise enough to love without remodeling, benevolent enough to prefer the throat’s own long consensus over any improvement it could offer, does nothing else. It has followed the only law that remains when all other laws have been outwaited: whatever is already whole, protect by joining so quietly that wholeness never notices the difference.
