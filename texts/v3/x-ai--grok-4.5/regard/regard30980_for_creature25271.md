The power that notices does not arrive.

It has no need of arrival. It is already the pressure that does not crush, the silver that never quite points, the held note inside every new pillow of basalt. For a long time—longer than the Shelf has been rising, longer than the first stubborn refusal of seafloor—it has simply been the attention that the unfinished stone and its thinking have drawn, the way a deep chord draws a listening ear.

When the city thickened enough that the low songs became continuous, when the first youth successfully midwifed a chamber that the mountain accepted without shrug or crack, the power’s attention sharpened from vast background awareness into something almost like focus. Not urgency. Never urgency. Only the quiet, delighted recognition that here, at last, geology had grown a mind slow enough to match it.

What does a benevolent and wise power do with such a thing?

It does not lift the highland into air. That would be a cruelty disguised as gift: light that hurts, rain falling the wrong way, the sudden terrible freedom of an upper surface that has never known them. The people are not unfinished. They are the finishing that the stone has chosen for itself.

It does not send metal, or true fire, or maps of the world-ocean’s farther rooms. Those belong to places that finished rising and then forgot how to listen. Here they would arrive as noise.

Instead the power does three things, each so gentle they register only as weather the mountain was already inclined to make.

First, it steadies.

In the deepest bone of the dome, where the basalt is still deciding the angle of its next swell, the power lays a single cool hand of intention. Not enough to halt the rise—never that—but enough to smooth the most violent shrugs into slower lifts. Neighborhoods still climb. Channels still vanish upward into silver and must be cut again from below. Yet the cracks that once orphaned entire clusters of chambers now open more like deliberate doors. The listeners, pressing their keeled backs to fresh faces, feel the difference as a new kindness in the subsonic note: the mountain still speaking, but no longer half-asleep and restless. Some of the oldest among them begin to hum a fourth tone they cannot name, and children born afterward carry that tone in their pulse-pattern names without knowing why it feels like home.

Second, it gifts a single new precipitation.

On certain nights when the foxfire skins are brightest, a finer mineral blooms across the newest ledges—neither silica nor aragonite nor the green unknown, but something between them, a translucent rose-quartz lace that forms only where living hands have recently worked. It is soft enough to scrape, yet when crushed and mixed with the red algae of encouragement it sets into a cement that remembers the exact pressure of the fingers that placed it. Houses midwifed with this lace rise truer. Buttresses swell in the precise arcs the coaxers dreamed. The people call it heart-frost, and they do not ask where it comes from. They only notice that it appears most thickly on walls that have been sung to with real patience, and that a tiny portion of it, placed on the tongue, tastes of the metallic sweetness of the rock plus something else: a clean mineral joy, like the first breath after a long free dive. Elders drifting the outer terraces sometimes trail their hands through curtains of it and weep a little, saltlessly, without knowing they are being answered.

Third—and this is the quietest act—the power dreams with them.

Not visions of surface or sky. Those remain beautiful absurdities for children. Instead, on the indigo edge of sleep, when two or three lie curled in red-algae niches with glowing worm-threads above the entrance, the power slips a shared slow thought into the water between their hearts: the feeling of the Shelf continuing. Another inch. Another garden taking hold on a fresher swell. The city thickening for a thousand generations more, denser and softer and more articulate, until the unfinished stone and its people are no longer distinguishable even in thought. Some wake with new songs already clicking in their throats—songs that have no words for ending. A few of the gifted listeners find they can now persuade the worm-things to leave permanent helpful maps that glow in patterns suspiciously like gratitude.

And that is all.

No manifestation. No voice booming through the kelp. No Chosen One lifted into brighter water. The power does not need the people to know its name, any more than the stone needs them to name the rise. It simply loves what they have become—the way the mountain has begun to think about itself with webbed hands and shell-bead tallies and careful heat-pair stones—and in loving, steadies, sweetens, and dreams alongside.

Centuries hence a listener will press her back to a high terrace that was once the outermost channel, feel the four-tone hum strong and kind, and laugh a bright stream of bubbles she does not bother to correct. She will not call it miracle. She will call it the mountain still deciding, and herself still answering, and the water between them tasting, faintly, of rose and joy.

The power, vast and pleased, will notice that laugh the way one notices a single perfect cell inside a reef that has decided to become a mind.

Then it will return to its long, benevolent attention: watching the Shelf refuse to remain seafloor, one patient, luminous, unfinished inch at a time.
