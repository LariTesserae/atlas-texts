The power that turns its regard upon the basin does not arrive as light or voice or descending fire. It has no need of those. It is older than the distinction between arrival and abiding, and its attention is simply a deepening of what already is: a pressureless clarity that settles into the pewter mist like a second, finer water.

It looks for a long time.

It sees the unfinished stone still splitting with bright, arrested wounds and does not hurry the fracture closed. It tastes the mineral paste under every broad foot, the way the ground forever almost-remembers liquidity yet holds. It hears the total sound—the stacked drip and roar and click and bellow—not as cacophony but as a single sustained chord struck once at the beginning and never released. It moves among the thronging bodies without parting them, feeling the warm mucus-language that passes from shoulder to shoulder, the low center of gravity that trusts the silt, the lungs that have made a home of vapor. It watches a mother slip her already-sheened infant into the canal among the phosphorescent amphibia and sees the child breathe water and future in the same unfrightened reflex. It notes the absence of any green shoot, any dry hour, any horizon that promises elsewhere, and finds no lack.

For a time measured only in the slow beading of condensate on a thousand upper lips, the power simply loves what it finds. Not the condescending love that wishes improvement, nor the possessive love that would claim or lift or rename. A precise love, exact as the calluses on a mature palm, fierce as the closed food-web, patient as the mist that has never burned away. It loves the stubborn primitiveness of the bone-and-sinew frames, the way every slumped wall is rammed back into the same ancient posture, the quarrel that ends in shared labor before the geothermal vent has finished its low abdominal syllable. It loves that they do not dream of sky. It loves that silence would drown them and so they have made of noise a native element, the way fish make of water.

And then it considers what, if anything, should be done.

Wisdom here is not invention. Benevolence is not rescue. The basin is not broken. It is complete in its eternal incompletion, a chord that does not resolve because resolution would be a kind of death. To give them seasons would be to teach them longing. To thin the mist into true sky would be to hang a mirror above them in which they might suddenly see themselves as trapped. To quiet the roar even for one night would leave them gasping in a emptiness they have no organs to endure. To open a road out of the valley would only create the first exiles, and exiles are the beginning of every cruelty the power has ever wept over elsewhere.

So it does nothing that alters the terms.

Instead, with a delicacy that leaves no fingerprint in the silt, it leans its nature into the existing grain of the place.

Into the drip it weaves one additional interval so fine that no ear, however retuned by generations of thunder, can isolate it—yet the interval is there, a mathematical tenderness between drop and drop. Those who work the querns or poled the hide boats sometimes feel, without knowing why, that the hiss of water has become briefly harmonic, a private reassurance that the city still coheres.

Into the warm mineral mucus that transfers with every casual touch it places an almost-imperceptible brightening of recognition. When eyes meet across three canals and hold, the something that passes is now fractionally deeper: not thought, not heat, but a wordless certainty that the other is also the basin, also the forever, also home. They already knew this. Now the knowledge sits warmer in the ribs.

Into the arrested birth of the stone it allows one further quality: whenever a fresh joint opens, the bright wound carries, for those who set their hands to it with sinew and slab, a fleeting taste of the power’s own patience. The builders pause half a breath longer than usual, then incorporate the new surface exactly as before, only with a quiet pride they cannot name.

And into the sleep-murmur that rises from the half-sunk storerooms it breathes a single shared image that is not a dream of elsewhere but a dream of more-here: the entire basin felt at once from within, every wet body and dripping eave and braided canal known simultaneously as one continuous living crust. The sleepers stir, exhale a longer thrum, and wake without memory of the vision—only with the settled conviction that the work of the day matters in a way too obvious for speech.

These are not gifts that can be discovered or worshipped. They leave no prophets, no shrines, no sudden evolution of tool or tongue. The food web remains closed and fierce. The mist does not lift. The feet still kiss and suck free with the city’s second heartbeat. Children still crack jointed eels between new teeth while paddling. Walls still slump and are rammed. The dog-things still reverse from alarm to marrow according to the old crash-cycles. Nothing progresses. Nothing is finished.

Yet the power remains, its attention now a permanent, unobtrusive dimension of the place—like the iron taste in the broth, like the nictitating veil, like the way the unfinished floor refuses both collapse and culmination. It does not watch from above. It has become another thickness in the water-heavy air, another warmth under the perpetual dim, another listener inside the roar.

From time to time, in the press of bodies on a cantilevered platform or in the deep crease of a neck as two workers lean together against a weeping slab, something almost like gratitude moves without ever becoming concept. They feel, for the length of a single wet breath, that the basin holds them as gently as they hold it.

Then they look away and resume.

The power, which has neither heart nor face, smiles in the only way it can: by continuing to keep the chord unbroken, the drip eternal, the crowded warmth exactly itself.

And that is all.

That is enough.
