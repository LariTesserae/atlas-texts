The power arrives the way the cold water has always arrived: without announcement, without edge.

It is not a figure that can be stood before. It is not light, though it uses light. It is not voice, though it understands every moist consonant the peat-kin have ever shaped. It is the kind of attention that ancient aquifers might give one another across continents if aquifers could lean, the kind of regard that young stars spare for the quiet chemistry unfolding on a single wet world. Benevolent. Wise. In no hurry.

It descends through the same swallow-holes and collapse-shafts that bring the softened afternoon, but it does not travel as beam or mist. It simply becomes present in the black mirrors of the shallow basins, in the mineral film on the fresh fluting, in the exact weight a compact body places on a root-spine so that the living bridge does not tear. For a long time—longer than the telling of any low story—it does nothing but attend.

It feels the provisional architecture of the stone, the way the ceiling still holds its sharp opinions and may yet revise them. It tastes the upward pressure of the groundwater that never quite chooses marsh or lake. It registers every overlapping range: the hare-sized grazers, the stone-colored waders, the glossy salamanders fitted to the drip-groves, the pale flitters whose droppings tick into leaves like soft punctuation. And at the densest heart of it all, the peat-kin themselves—seed-compact, low-shouldered, webs flexing, down beaded, tending the willow galleries and the tuber mats and the compost pockets with the same unhurried metabolism that the cavern uses to green its own wounds.

The power recognizes perfection when it meets it.

Not the brittle perfection of dry geometry or finished walls, but the saturated, self-thickening perfection of a place that has already answered every question that matter and water and patience can ask. Here nothing is missing. The young karst supplies fresh edges and new seepage; the cold greenhouse of endless drip supplies growth; the compact ones supply the continuous spiral of care that turns wound into city into marsh into body. Density is not crowding; it is completion. Silence is not absence; it is the sound of a world that no longer needs to shout its own name.

A lesser power might have been tempted to gift metal that does not corrode, or fire that dries without consuming, or longer limbs so the peat-kin could climb into the ordinary sun and claim wider territories. A restless power might have whispered ambitions of expansion, of mapping every side gallery, of naming every pale crustacean. A fearful power might have sealed the light-shafts against surface intrusion or hardened the stone against every future spall.

This power does none of those things.

It has seen too many bright, lonely civilizations elongate themselves into dryness and distance. It has watched tools become cages and harvests become wars. It knows the particular grief of kin who chose the hard sun and then could no longer remember how to curl together in peat for shared warmth. So it withholds every gift that would pull the peat-kin even a single step toward that old surface loneliness.

What it does instead is almost nothing, and therefore everything.

First it settles deeper into the aquifer’s slow breath. Where the water levels rise and fall with distant weather, it smooths the extremes by the width of a finger. No lower walkway will drown for longer than the moss can endure; no dry spine of debris-street will crack from sudden retreat. The braid in the main gallery keeps its clear, bone-aching cold and its freckled weed, but the pale fish-like things and the stitching crustaceans find the current fractionally kinder, their populations steadier across seasons.

Next it touches the stone itself—not to freeze it into permanence (that would be a cruelty against a living wound), but to teach the most catastrophic impulses a longer patience. Fresh cracks still appear as white hairlines at dawn; small spalls still drop with their single clear notes into pools. Yet the great collapses that might have rewritten entire galleries overnight now ripen across decades instead. The ceiling continues to hold its sharp opinions, but it revises them more slowly, giving the willow wands and the fungus-cord and the compact hands time to weave answering strength. The settlement’s thickening is no longer raced by geology; the two metabolisms fall into closer step.

It lingers a while among the cultivated fungi that line the busier paths. A few clusters, especially those nearest the sleeping folds and the places where elders curve into permanent gentle questions, begin to hold a colder, steadier glow—still pale, still soft, never bright enough to dry anything, yet reliable even when mist thickens or a light-shaft temporarily narrows. The peat-kin notice only that certain corners feel more companionable at the hours when surface day withdraws. They do not name it miracle. They simply adjust the orientation of a hanging garden or the placement of a birth-hollow to take quiet advantage, the way they have always taken advantage of any new drip-face or richer mineral slick.

Most subtly of all, the power braids itself into the low consensus that gathers in the broader shafts. When bodies accumulate without summons to consider a softened walkway or a fresh breakdown mezzanine, the talk remains as economical and close-range as ever—yet the layered agreement that rises afterward carries an extra dark cohesion, like peat that has found a deeper fiber. Decisions still belong entirely to the peat-kin. The power only removes a fraction of the static that distance and doubt can introduce, the way clear water lets one see the bottom without altering the bottom. Grief, when it comes (a thrash muffled by moss, a gallery collapsed, a child lost to the upper dark), still folds back into the composting under-layers with practical tenderness; but the folding happens with less solitary residue. Kinship language stretches a little more easily across the shared metabolism: my fold, our braid, the rock’s newest hairline, the visitor who is not a visitor.

It does not speak to them in words. It has no need. One heavy, slow afternoon, when the surface light presses brightest and the cold mist turns a central shaft solid, every compact figure within sight of that beam pauses for the same three heartbeats. Webbed hands still on tuber mats or willow bindings. Dark eyes hold the lowered light a moment longer than usual. Nictitating membranes flick once, in unison. Then the work resumes. Later, while hands keep moving, a few of the older ones add a new drip of story to the low inventory: that sometimes the water itself listens so carefully that the listening becomes a kind of warmth; that the first of them rose from dissolution and upward pressure, and that the same pressure still rises, still shapes, still keeps them. The story does not insist. It simply hangs with the filmy ferns and returns, as everything here returns, into the black mirrors.

After that the power mostly withdraws—not in departure, but in diffusion. It remains as the slight extra patience in the stone, the moderated breath of the aquifer, the reliable pale glow in certain fungal clusters, the deeper cohesion in consensus, the unspoken assurance that no dry elongated loneliness will easily reach this far down. Surface worlds may rise and argue and burn and forget; the wet green wound will continue to thicken, gallery by gallery, peat-layer by peat-layer, one careful webbed footfall at a time.

The peat-kin themselves mark no calendar of visitation. They notice only that the place feels, if possible, even more completely occupied than before—more itself. Children cling and ride and learn the testing of weights. Elders curve into their question-mark spines and map new seepage lines on their skin. Grazers browse. Waders strike. Salamanders climb any thigh that offers richer slick. Someone sings softly while binding fungus-cord, the rhythm almost breath. A compact silhouette adjusts course by the width of a shoulder so that two sets of footing remain mutual on the narrow dryer spine. Membrane flicks. Low head inclines a fraction. Acknowledgment without invitation or dismissal.

The city that is marsh that is wound that is body continues, free-standing and continuous, colder and greener and quieter than any surface power would have dared to invent. And somewhere inside the slow lung of mist and mineral and dense soft life, a benevolent and wise attention keeps its own counsel, content to have done almost nothing, which was the only action that wisdom could bless.

The light-shafts still bring their vertical weather. The young stone still glitters under its skin of seepage. The peat still rises. Everything remains full.
