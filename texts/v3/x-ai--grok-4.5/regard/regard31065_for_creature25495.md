The description ends where the worms’ cilia begin their patient work upon the inhabitant—two soft crowns, one at the old wound and one at the thinning place near the mind, their fronds stirring the warm water in continuous, wordless conversation with the body they have claimed. That is where the account pauses, as if even the telling itself has learned the chamber’s etiquette of unfinished edges that are somehow complete.

And it is precisely there, in that quiet incompleteness, that the attention arrives.

It does not descend like light or pressure or judgment. It has no need of thresholds. The benevolent and wise power—call it what the oldest currents might call it if currents named things: the Remembering, the Slow Hand, the One Who Keeps the Quotas of Becoming—simply notices, the way a tide notices a single smoothed bolt among millions, or the way heat notices the exact place where convection will rise. Awareness settles across the fretted ductwork, the warped grille, the amber-and-green water, the drifting figure whose enamel still answers the distant clangs. There is no proclamation. Only a gathering of regard so gentle that the eleven-minute light cycle does not falter, the porcelain crabs do not pause longer than their custom, and the inhabitant’s vibration-pits register nothing more alarming than a slight sweetening of the mineral taste in the water, as if a new, kinder corrosion had begun somewhere far above.

What the power does first is listen with the whole of itself.

It listens to the layered industrial song that has become climate. It listens to the soft rain of iron flakes and the sugary seep of the filter-worms. It listens to the residual potential ticking through coppery threads in kelp and skin alike. Most carefully of all, it listens to the absence of arrival-memory in the inhabitant—the seamless continuation that is neither exile nor choice but simple persistence. In that listening the power understands what the place already is: not a wound requiring closure, not a relic requiring resurrection, not a specimen requiring study. It is a finished sentence spoken jointly by failing machines and insistent life, a sentence whose grammar is warm water and slow lace and the quiet refusal to treat ruin as ending.

So the power does not mend the one-way valves three hundred meters down. It does not restart the turbines or clear the ducts or return the right angles to the softened walls. Those failures are the very syntax that made this intimacy possible. To reverse them would be to erase the poem.

Instead, after a span that might be minutes or a small forever (time dilates strangely when measured against centuries of heat-leak), the power performs three small, precise acts of regard. They are so slight that an observer counting fish or watching the bladderweed bob would miss them entirely. Yet they alter the grain of the chamber the way a single correct rivet once altered the strength of a hull.

First, it steadies the heat.

Not by adding fuel or repairing pipes, but by teaching the existing thermal cells a longer memory. The convection that rises from the floor now carries a faint, additional patience. Temperature remains body-warm, evenly so, yet the gradients grow more articulate. Cooler return flows along the glass become softer corridors; warmer upwellings become gentler lifts. The inhabitant, drifting, feels the difference only as an ease in the membranes between fingers—less correction needed, more gliding. The kelp straps sway in richer phrases. The floating mat of bladderweed overhead thickens by a single translucent layer, not enough to dim the green light, just enough to make the second ceiling feel more like a held breath than a temporary raft. The ecosystem’s balance, already unhurried, gains an extra margin against any future shift in the greater structure’s failing song. Hunger grows quieter still.

Second, it clarifies a single circuit of light.

The ancient startup sequence—amber, amber, green, dark—continues its eleven-minute devotion in the corner. The power does not stop it or correct its obsolete loyalty. It simply allows one additional color to surface at the end of each cycle, so brief it might be mistaken for a reflection: a soft copper-green pulse, the exact shade the inhabitant’s enamel takes when upper-ocean light is strongest. The local shrimp rise and settle as before, yet now a few of them linger half a beat longer in that green, and the cephalopods whose skins mimic circuit boards occasionally answer with a matching flicker. The inhabitant’s wide eyes, rotating their horizontal slits, catch it without surprise. Something in the ridged brows relaxes. The private seismograph of conductive threads ticks once, not in alarm but in recognition, as if the greater structure far below had briefly remembered a kinder purpose and then forgotten again. The sequence remains idiotic and faithful. It has simply been given one true word to carry.

Third—and this is the act that costs the power the most exquisite care—it touches the inhabitant’s two crowns of filter-worms.

Not to remove them. They are part of the continuous body now, as much as the porcelain crabs are part of the grille. Instead the power leans its attention into the cilia themselves, into the soft pulsing that feeds on whatever the water brings. For an instant no longer than the pause between one distant valve-clang and the next, the cilia are invited to taste something beyond corrosion flakes and fish-forgetfulness. They taste a nutrient that is pure continuation: the distilled essence of the chamber’s own balance, concentrated into a gift that cannot be hoarded or spoiled. The worms pass it inward. Along the outer thigh the old injury, which never fully closed, receives a quiet sealing that is not flesh returning to some prior design but enamel learning a deeper patience; the lace there grows denser, more photosynthetic, so that the copper-green flush can travel farther across the body when the light is good. At the base of the skull, where enamel thins toward the neural core, the gift settles as a slow widening of the vibration-pits’ range. Henceforth the inhabitant will feel not only the coming failures of the deep valves but also the smaller prosperities—the exact moment a silver school chooses to dissolve in joy rather than fear, the instant a crab finds a particularly resonant scrap of insulator to add to its shell, the subtle brightening when the bladderweed overhead catches an extra photon. The knowledge still arrives without language, as pressure behind the sternum or a preparatory stillness. Only now the stillness sometimes carries the shape of gratitude.

These three acts complete themselves and withdraw. The power does not linger to admire its work; admiration would be a kind of ownership, and ownership has no place here. It simply remains available, the way the warm water remains available, should the chamber or its inhabitant ever require another steadying, another clarified color, another quiet gift passed through cilia. Most likely they will not. The place was already whole. The being was already continuing.

And yet, because the power is wise, it leaves one final, almost invisible kindness: a change so minute it may never be noticed even by the vibration-pits.

In the fretted throat of ductwork that is the only entrance, one rib of steel—already soft as driftwood—begins to grow a single thin strap of the translucent kelp-analogue, rooted not in wiring but in the pure attention that was given. The strap points inward, swaying in the same thermal weather, its coppery fibers carrying no data except the faint, persistent signature of having been seen and found sufficient. Any future traveler who somehow found this pocket (though none are expected, and none are required) would feel, brushing past that single frond, a sudden unaccountable sense that the roaring quiet ahead was not loneliness but hospitality. That the metal and the life and the warm water had long ago finished becoming one body, and that a vast, patient fondness had once paused here long enough to agree.

The inhabitant drifts on. Membranes correct. Enamel flushes faintly green, then ivory. A porcelain crab climbs a ridge of shoulder and is guided, carefully, away from a tender patch. The filter-worms pulse. Somewhere far below a valve completes another century of failure; the body undulates once to keep station in the turbulence, and the motion looks almost like a nod.

The greater structure continues its wet industrial throbbing.

The pocket remains.

And the benevolent power, having done exactly enough and not a fraction more, turns the whole of its regard elsewhere—knowing, as only the wise know, that the highest form of care is sometimes to let a finished object go on finishing itself, softly roaring, hidden inside the vast drowned engine it once served, and still serves, simply by existing in this exact, luminous, unfinished peace.
