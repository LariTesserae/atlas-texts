The benevolent and wise power did not arrive as light, nor as voice, nor as any rupture that the high country would have had to notice. It had been drawn, yes—by the self-contained weather, by the quiet foundry of seeps, by the dwellers whose blood ran the same ochre and malachite as the rock—but wisdom, when it is truly wise, first measures the distance between attention and alteration.

It settled above the massif the way a deeper chill settles into already-cold water: without boundary, without declaration. For a long while (and the power understood long while in the only currency this place accepted) it simply matched the breathing. It learned the hydraulic tide that moved through basalt and dweller alike. It tasted the black paste, the copper-sweet spore-caps, the low frog-song felt in the wrist. It watched a dweller collapse into a moss-mound beside a pulsing seep and open the permeable skin of its inner arms, and it felt, with something like reverence, how little was required for continuance.

Benevolence without wisdom would have wanted to gift, to lift, to name, to make safe in ways the plateau had never needed. It would have offered clearer skies, or longer warm exhalations, or a road of dry stone so the dwellers might walk farther and see their own country from outside it. Wisdom forbade every one of these. The place had already answered every question it found worth asking. Its perfection was not the brittle perfection of something finished, but the living perfection of something that had never required an audience or an ending. To add even a single permanent monument—a brighter mineral, a longer memory, a protection visible as protection—would have introduced the first seed of thirst.

So the power did almost nothing.

Almost.

It adjusted, by the width of a thought, the membrane between this massif and all other weathers. Not a wall; walls are for drier minds. Rather a subtle preference in the way fronts formed and dissolved, so that any future dryness—any extraction, any haste, any looking that wanted to own—would find itself gently shepherded around the high country’s flanks, the way fog already shepherded itself around the shrub-conifers. The preference was written in the same patience as peat. No dweller would ever taste the difference as difference. Streams would still glaze the basalt. Metal would still crust and dissolve. The soft-footed ungulates would still pearly their coats in condensate. Only the largest possible timescale would show that the engine of green and seepage had been allowed to keep turning out nothing but more of itself, untroubled, while continents elsewhere rose and fell and forgot how to weep.

And once, only once, when a rare clearance opened the hard blue sky and the dwellers stood a little taller, moss steaming, faces tilted toward the rumor of dryness, the power allowed a single additional pulse to travel through the seeps. It was the same pulse the dwellers already knew—the tidal, thermal, almost deliberate exhalation—but deepened by a fraction so small it could have been mistaken for ordinary variation. In that pulse was carried no message beyond the one the frogs already sang and the dwellers already amplified:

*Here.  
Still here.  
Yes.*

The dwellers felt it first as a warmth in the wrist bones, then as a brief, unpolemical gladness that their own mosses answered by greening a shade deeper before the haze closed again. They did not look up searching for a source. Looking for sources is another climate’s habit. They simply resumed the courteous exchange of ions, the light scrapes that encouraged certain flanges, the interlocking of mosses when the next stalled front brought warmish rain.

The power remained a little longer, long enough to watch a new thickening walk away on its freshly grown limbs, already fluent, already patient. Then it withdrew the way it had come: by becoming indistinguishable from the cool permanent damp, from the greater exhalation of the land itself. Its attention left no print, only a slightly more decided green that faded, as all such things fade here, into the ongoing work.

There were no thanks. Thanks would have required separation. There was only the high country, continuing—stone and saturated green, dwellers and seeps, the quiet foundry that had never needed witnesses and now, under the lightest possible guardianship of something that understood exactly how not to touch, would never be forced to acquire them.

In this way the benevolent and wise power kept its bargain with its own nature: it loved what was already whole by refusing to make it other, and in that refusal became, for a time, another permeable thickening of the same endless, unsloping breath.
