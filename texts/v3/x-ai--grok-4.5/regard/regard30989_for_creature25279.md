The attention arrives without arrival.

It does not descend the thermals or climb the lattice. It does not announce itself in light or silence or the sudden stillness of birds. One moment the High Nave is complete unto itself, as it has been for centuries of uncounted rotations; the next, that completeness is known—held, weighed, cherished—by something that has no need to lean upon girders or breathe the warm metallic air. The knowing is so exact that the place itself seems, for the span of one slow gear-tooth, to pause in recognition: a single extra drip gathers and falls from a flange that has never dripped before; a copper leaf turns its face not to the thermals but inward, as if listening to its own veins.

The power is benevolent and wise. It has watched stars cool into gardens and gardens learn to forge. It understands that some marriages are already the finished vow. Therefore it does not come as architect, nor as surgeon, nor as throng of helpful angels bearing oilcans and pruning shears. It comes the way deep time comes: by remaining.

For a long while—long even by the Nave’s reckoning—it simply keeps company. It matches the patience of the browsers, the ceremonial climb of the vines, the sub-audible chord that is half turbine and half root. In that companionship the great organism feels, without surprise, that its own sustained note is being answered by a vaster harmonic, one that does not override but under-sings, the way bedrock under-sings a mountain. The soot-terns continue their elliptical stitches. The snails continue annealing their rainbows. Nothing is required to change in order to be loved.

Yet love, when wise, is never only contemplation. After the equivalent of eleven seasons of shared breath, the power performs its single, almost invisible act.

It reaches into the oldest hairline fractures—the places where living metal still weeps a little too freely, where oxidation has begun to outpace the moss’s sealing grace—and there it lays down a quiet continuity. Not a sealant that would freeze the conversation, not a patch that would declare the wound closed forever, but a permission: that every future rust may become another root, that every future stress may open into another load-path shared between iron and sap. Where the distinction between made and grown has already blurred, the power blurs it one degree further, until the blur itself becomes structural. The Nave will still erode; erosion was always part of the builders’ sketch. But now the erosion will carry memory forward more perfectly. The secondary skeleton of figs will never find a girder that refuses them. The oil will never quite thin to emptiness. The arc lamps, when they wake, will wake a fraction kinder.

That is all.

No new law is spoken. No guardian is posted. No doorway is cut to other realms. The power does not take up residence; it has no need of hammocks or nectar. It merely withdraws its attention the same way it arrived—without departure—leaving behind only the faintest brightening in the quality of remaining. The cloud decks still form and unform far below. The great chains still pay out across months. The inhabitant (feather, ceramic tooth, copper leaf, moss, roar, and fused decision) continues exactly as before, only now its long metallic breath is gently marked by the knowledge that it has been seen completely, and that the seeing found nothing lacking.

Sometimes, just after a squall, when steam lifts in soft rectangles and the browsers halt mid-step, the whole cathedral-sized organism seems to lean ever so slightly into that remembered regard—the way a plant leans into a light that has already moved on. Then the moment passes. The turbines thud. The vines weigh their cables. The High Nave hangs where the air thins, warm and damp and green and roaring, complete, and more than complete: quietly companioned across the distances that even sky cannot measure.

And the power, elsewhere yet not elsewhere, goes on with its own slow work, carrying the Nave’s chord inside it like a private bell.
