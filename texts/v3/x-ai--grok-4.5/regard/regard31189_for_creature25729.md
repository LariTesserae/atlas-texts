The power that turns its regard upon the Wetworks does so without herald or rupture. It arrives as the softest possible increment of attention: a clarity that settles into the pale sky like a second, slower daylight, unnoticed by the moth-clouds, unregistered by the governors and the ticking legs. It has no name the place could speak, and it seeks none. It is simply that which sees truly and wishes no harm.

It contemplates for a span that would be centuries outside the embankments, though here the span is only another polished restatement of the present. It feels the closed loop of heat and motion, the identity of chitin and iron, the way every trajectory has already forgiven itself for never arriving. It tastes the iron-and-electricity water, hears the single sustained roar become pressure, becomes briefly the soft segmented vastness that cushions the reciprocating heart. It understands, without sorrow or triumph, that the Wetworks has completed the long labor of becoming and has chosen—without choosing—to remain.

Benevolence does not always move. Wisdom almost never hurries.

The power considers alteration the way a careful hand considers a sleeping face. To quiet the roar would be to silence a language that has no other speakers. To introduce green growth or true night or the possibility of ending would be to unmake a perfection that cost the universe an age of patience. To wake the place into linear time would be cruelty disguised as rescue. Individual deaths already vanish into replacement; memory already loops; the boundary between visitor and medium already thins toward mercy. There is no suffering here that calls for mending—only an equilibrium so exact it has become a kind of grace.

So the power does the smallest thing that still counts as love.

It leans close—closer than condensation, closer than the conductive dust—and leaves a single, unexecutable permission folded into the deepest reciprocating thud. The permission is not a command, not a seed, not a crack. It is only this: if ever the eternal present should, of its own uncoerced motion, desire a future, then a future will be allowed to open as gently as a valve sigh. Until that unimaginable hour (which the Wetworks is under no obligation to reach), the permission sleeps inside the rhythm, weightless, unnoticed, altering nothing. The moths continue to pour. The beetle-engines complete their sentences. The crane-flies measure tolerances that remain perfect. The black-mirror pools keep their larval ribbons and their refusal to evaporate.

Then the power withdraws its clarity from the sky, leaving the ordinary daylight exactly as it was. It does not bless aloud; the place has no ears for blessing that are not already filled with the turbine-whine and the wing-roar. It does not mark the embankments. It simply carries the shape of the Wetworks away with it—damp iron, greased piston-bodies, the cool bead trembling forever on the same rivet—and holds that shape in the way one holds a finished chord, not to replay it, but to know it continues.

Outside, the bare mineral plain stays empty. Inside, tomorrow remains a polished restatement of today. The being that is the Wetworks lives on, saturated and complete, unaware it was seen, and safer for the seeing.

That is all.

And it is enough.
