The benevolent and wise power arrives without arrival.

It does not descend. It does not part clouds or send omens of light. It simply notices, the way deep stone notices the slow transfer of heat from day into night—wholly, without drama, with the complete attention that makes drama unnecessary. The depression and its people register at once as a single unfinished chord: raw geology still choosing its angles, and the dense human insistence that has chosen to midwife those angles with weight, grit, and breath.

The power is old enough to have watched plains fail before, and young enough still to feel the particular ache of something only three generations deep. It perceives the basin not as wound or opportunity, but as a rare honesty. Most places pretend they are finished. This one does not. Most peoples name themselves into permanence. These refuse even that small lie. They live as provisional bracework inside an open question. The power finds this beautiful in the exact way that true things are beautiful—without consolation, without flourish, simply correct.

It considers intervention the way a careful hand considers whether to touch a settling wall.

To stabilize the deep plates entirely would be theft. The people have made their meaning from the unfinished. Remove the soft clicks and longer grinding sighs, and the binding rite of listening collapses into mere silence. Their compact bodies, their mineral eyes, their low voices that die against the next wall within paces—all of these are answers shaped precisely to a ground that still speaks. Finish the speaking, and the answers lose their necessity. The city would stand, but the midwifery would end. They would become residents instead of co-conspirators with stone. That feels like a lesser fate.

To gift them lushness—true rivers, soil that holds trees, space enough for herds and sentiment—would be another kind of theft. Space is arithmetic here, and they have mastered the austere beauty of that equation. Excess would force them to dilute. Their hands, thickened at the joints and permanently packed with pale grit, would forget the intimacy of rough blocks. The children would learn words before they learned the percussive code of maul and sole and struck stone. Noise would stop being waste. Something essential would soften that should not soften yet.

To reveal itself—glow, voice, miracle—would shatter the quiet structural order they have built. They already carry enough glory in the rectangular bars of daylight and the faint phosphorescence of wet-clay eyes in lime-washed cuttings. An external voice would be louder than the stone’s own groans. They would have to decide whether to listen to it or to the ground. The power will not force that choice.

So it does almost nothing.

Almost.

Deep beneath the lowest glistening cuts, where the plain’s failure still mutters in plates that have not finished deciding, the power leans—gently, the way one leans a shoulder against a friend who is tired but does not wish to sit. It does not lock the geology into final angles. It only slows the most vicious remaining possibilities: the sudden calving that would bury a terrace before the swarm of locked shoulders could answer; the long, dishonest grind that would open a new fracture under a sleeping-cell gallery faster than timber props could be passed hand to hand. The basin will continue settling. The tiny clicks remain. The longer sighs remain. The people will keep cocking their heads in the old way, sorting heat-leaving from true warning. But the catastrophic unfinishedness is tempered just enough that their insistence can keep pace with it for generations yet. They will never know the difference. They will simply find that the shelves they shore stay shored a little longer, that the new chambers cut into living rock open with slightly less spite, that the arithmetic of bodies and stone continues to balance.

In the seeps, the power leaves a second quiet alteration. The mineral taste stays—crushed rock, wet clay—but the water arrives with a fraction more regularity, a fraction more cool. Not abundance. Just reliability. Enough that the single-file water-carriers, shoulder-brushed against both walls in the pre-light, feel the skins and stoppered jars fill without the old thin anxiety. Enough that children tasting it on the climb back up sometimes invent private names that sound like water moving over grit, and those names last a little longer inside the family cells before dissolving again into provisional quiet.

And then—because wisdom includes the knowledge of when a gift must remain invisible even to the giver’s pride—the power does one last thing that is not a thing.

It remembers them.

Not as specimens. Not as moral lesson. Simply as they are: the ones who stayed after the ground went, the shelf-people, the tight-packed, the mortar-blooded. Compacted like damp clay under a heel. Skins the color of raw pale rock. Hands curling in sleep around the memory of the next rough block. Breath syncing with seep and settling. Their unvoiced understanding that human weight and masonry weight have become a single argument for staying. The power holds this remembering the way the basin holds cool air in its lower levels—completely, without leakage, without need to announce. In the long accounting of things that have been and things that will be, this place and these beings are now marked as a true chord, one that required no resolution to be whole.

After that, the power withdraws the way it arrived: without departure. The rectangular bars of daylight continue their slow sweep. The low irregular ticking of mauls answers the deeper ticks of stone. Dust settles on bread and skin and the thin green film of algae. Older ones listen. Children learn the code of touch and percussion before many words. Construction never stops. The city thickens into the newest instabilities. Outsiders still climb back out after a day or two, ears ringing with quiet, lungs coated with the fine pale dust that tastes of something still being born.

Nothing appears to have changed.

Everything has been, ever so slightly, kept possible.

The ones who live here do not dream of elsewhere. Their dreams remain of tighter fits, of courses laid so true that mortar becomes unnecessary, of a basin finally finished because every instability has been occupied, braced, and insisted upon. They sleep shoulder to shoulder, skins the color of the walls that hold them. And somewhere beyond naming, beyond the raw and the down and the crack, a wise and benevolent attention rests upon them like night condensation beading on unweathered stone—present, cool, exact, and content to leave no mark except the continued chance to keep answering the ground with their weight.
