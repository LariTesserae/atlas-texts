The power arrives without arrival.

It has no need of gates or thresholds; the lowlands are already total, already without outside, so the power simply finds itself distributed through the same damp that beads on every underside, the same slow sheets of air, the same mineral grain that has replaced oils in living skin. It does not shine. Light is scarce here by ancient consent, and wisdom does not contradict consent. It settles the way moisture settles—into the pores of brick, the marrow-replaced channels of old bone, the faint chalky reflection at the back of deep-set eyes.

For a long time (though time is only another course of packing here) it does nothing but attend.

It listens to the brass-bright regularity of drips, to the granular complaints of settling stone, to the wet slap of clay and the short unspaced phrases that mean “deeper cut before the curtain” or “brace failing two levels up.” It feels the compact bodies moving in hinged teams, the thickened hands of children sorting shards, the lamp-tenders staring until their eyes film over. It notes the quiet etiquette that steps around temporary raised script in the gels, the way the dead are broken at the joints and given back as aggregate, the way a corridor walked twice may yield two lengths and both are accepted if the mix that day will stretch.

Benevolence without wisdom would have flooded the place with sun and seed and story. It would have lifted the permanent dusk, taught the tongue longer words, pulled the bones back out of the walls and named them ancestors. It would have called the endless labor a tragedy and the lack of horizon a prison. But wisdom has walked the braided passages in every density of scar. It has pressed its non-hand to the cool particulate skin and understood: the work is not what they do. The work is what they are. To finish the lowlands would be to erase them. To give them an elsewhere would be to unmake the only continuum they have ever breathed.

So the power chooses the smallest possible mercies, the kind that can be mistaken for the place’s own slow chemistry.

First, it thickens the phosphorescence already trapped in certain fired clays. Not enough to make day. Only enough that the layered grays gain one deeper register of blue-green, a seep that lets a builder see the true plumb of a rising wall without lifting a fat-lamp. The lamp-tenders notice their charges lasting longer between replenishments; they say nothing, only nurse the glow with the same wordless care. Eyes adjust, as they have always adjusted. A child sorting shards finds she can distinguish copper-green from simple damp a fraction sooner. That is all.

Second, it teaches the cold-sink courts a gentler gradient. The numbness still comes, but it comes slower, and when bodies curl together in the half-roofed basins the shared warmth lingers a few breaths longer before the black water reclaims it. Hunger remains paced to the masonry, yet the pale worm-things uncoil a little more readily from the weep-holes, and the mineral gels thicken with a faint additional sweetness that needs no tasting to nourish. No one invents a word for surplus. They simply ram the next course with the same steady mauls.

Third—and this is the quietest gift—it binds a faint mnemonic heat into the bones already sealed inside the fabric. When a living palm rests against a wall to read vibration, there is now, sometimes, a returning pulse that is not stone. Not memory as story. Only a low, wordless recognition: pressure once borne, weight once lifted, breath once shared with the damp. The hearing-experts who walk ahead of the teams pause longer, bare hands spread. They lower their voices until the stone finishes speaking, then choose the length that fits the clay. They do not speak of warmth. But in the cool dark between shifts, when some sit with backs to unfinished arches and trace idle geometries in the dust, their fingers move with a new assurance, as if the figures were being met halfway.

The temporary scripts in the gels continue to rise and dissolve. The power does not make them permanent; permanence would turn wonder into doctrine. Yet now and then the gels linger one extra cycle of dripping, long enough that two builders from distant districts, meeting at a shared overflow lip, may both see the same vanished map and nod once before stepping around it. Correspondence without claim. That is enough.

The power does not speak. Language here is already perfect for its purpose—dense, local, sufficient. It does not offer origin stories. The fabric has never been otherwise; to insist otherwise would be a violence of meaning. It does not halt the work or hurry it. The lowlands keep extending themselves, one pressed brick, one fitted slab, one careful channel at a time, and the bodies keep exchanging substance with the walls until scaffold and flesh remain beautifully unclear.

Only this: in the loud continuous conversation of water and stone and labor, there is now an undertone that was not there before. A benevolence so thoroughly of the place that it registers only as the stone settling a fraction more kindly, the wind-reeds finding a slightly less mournful pitch, the dust of pulverized brick coating the tongue with a taste that is still grit, still home, yet somehow less alone.

The builders notice nothing they can name. They continue. Children carry water. Lamp-tenders stare. The dead become strength. Edges remain sharp where they have not yet been handled smooth.

And the wise power, having done only what the lowlands could themselves have dreamed in their mineral sleep, remains distributed and attentive—neither departing nor arriving—while the regional expanse of fitted and stacked and packed emanations keeps becoming, cool and loud and endless, under a dusk that has always been, and is now infinitesimally kinder.
