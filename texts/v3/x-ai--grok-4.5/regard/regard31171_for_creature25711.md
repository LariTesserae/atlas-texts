The benevolent and wise power arrives the way the high country’s own fogs arrive: without announcement, without edge. It does not descend from any elsewhere; it simply densifies out of the metallic glare and the permanent damp, becoming one more slow-rolling presence among the plateaus. For a long time—long even by the measure of ridge-slumps and migration fronts—it does nothing but attend.

It feels the unfinished shove still stored in the young stone. It tastes the antifreeze resins, the methane pilot-lights, the fine metallic grit cycling through fist-sized guts and tubular roots. It listens to the layered roar until the separate songs (wool-plated breath, hollow-bone turbines, rotary mandibles, the sub-audible bellow inside cloud) resolve into a single idling chord. It understands, with the patience that wisdom requires, that this is not a place waiting to be improved. It is already the longest possible breath, an engine whose perfection lies in remaining unfinished.

So the power refrains from any gesture that would hurry or clarify. It does not still the scree, or thin the herds to make room for quiet, or filter the merciless light into something kinder. Those would be the errors of a lesser compassion—the sort that mistakes saturation for suffering and stasis for incompleteness. Instead it offers only three attentions, each as light as the beads of meltwater that never quite become rivers.

First, it leans ever so slightly into the quartz veins. Where the high-altitude glare already ricochets into focused blades, the power lengthens their linger by a fraction of a fraction. The denser, darker, louder photosynthesis that answers is not new; it is only more continuous. The armored grazers find richer powder. The browsers’ dung-fields vent a warmer, steadier methane. The gliders ride thermal blooms that last a few more heartbeats of their hollow-boned lives. No creature notices a change. The engine simply idles with a deeper contentment.

Second, it braids its own silence into the wing-tones of the gliders. Across ten thousand generations the resonant frequencies shift by less than the difference between two neighboring ridges. The fogs begin to travel routes that keep the mosses thicker on the steepest scarps, slowing erosion by the width of a wool-plate every century. The great half-rumored shapes inside the thickest banks feel the new harmonic as a softening of pressure, and their bellows drop half a note, becoming less a warning than a continuing. Again, nothing is altered that the high country itself would not eventually have altered. The power has only folded a little extra time into the existing cycles.

Third—and this is the quietest gift—it remembers. Not with monuments or records, which the place would reject the way it rejects walls and roads. It remembers by becoming part of the damp that beads on every surface. Every condensed droplet now carries, in its brief lens, an echo of the whole: the shove, the roar, the bright wet stasis, the patient refining of slopes into ore and breath and spore. When a droplet falls into moss or is wicked into browser wool or inhaled by a tubular plant, the echo dissolves back into the engine. In this way the high country is held, without being seized, by something that will outlast even the slow cooling it waits upon.

After that the power does nothing more. It remains, diffuse and companionable, one more weather among the weathers. Sometimes a browser front walking leeward will gleam with a frost that lasts a heartbeat longer than physics alone would allow. Sometimes the gliders’ curtain-nests sing a chord that briefly sounds like gratitude without ever becoming language. Sometimes the mysterious bulk inside a fog bank will pause, as if aware it is not the only vastness moving there.

The high country continues. Its fractures still hold the memory of the shove. Its lives still occupy every gram of snow, mist, sharp stone, and cold sunlight. The engine idles at altitude, flesh and leaf and raw mineral, and now also a benevolence so light it cannot be distinguished from the damp itself. The power stays, not as guardian or master, but as the simple certainty that what is already perfect may be accompanied.

And if, at the end of all ridges, the original force finishes cooling and the plateaus finally rest, the power will still be there—beading on the last moss, humming in the last hollow bone—ready to carry the echo of this longest breath into whatever silent interval follows. Until then, it watches. It loves by not interrupting. It does, exquisitely, almost nothing at all.
