The benevolent and wise power did not arrive as light or voice or decree. It arrived the way heat arrives at noon in the Verdance: already present, already distributed, already indistinguishable from the medium it moved through.

It had watched for a long time measured in the flex of frond-shadows and the sealing of digger tunnels. It had tasted the copper-green rain, ridden the private cyclones of the towers, felt the arterial rush of the cats’ final materialization and the photosynthetic spasm of the fallen sails. It had listened to the continent-wide chord until the stacked harmonics became a single slow syllable spoken by a throat the size of weather. And it had walked, invisible, among the Interim—feeling the soles of their feet on living bridges, the way their blood already hesitated toward vegetative pace, the quiet pride they took in marks that would be grown over and forgotten.

A lesser power might have mistaken completeness for stasis and tried to improve it. A crueler one might have seen a sleeping god and sought to claim the body. This one, being wise, understood that the Verdance was not unfinished. It was a finished sentence that continued to utter itself. Being benevolent, it felt no urge to correct what already sustained every thirst and every thunder within its own vast metabolism.

So it did only three things, and each was almost nothing.

First, it leaned its attention against the cambium like a second wind. Where lightning had once scarred and resin had keloided, the healing now carried a faint additional warmth—an assurance, nothing more, that the next scar would close even cleaner. The root-pavement, already locking boulders into permanence, received a subtler lock: a microscopic kindness that slowed the rare places where erosion still threatened to fray the living carpet. No tower grew taller. No new primary punched through the sky. The place simply became, by a fraction, more itself. The diggers, exchanging chemical histories in the dark, began to carry an extra temperature gradient they could not name; drought corridors rose a day sooner and settled a day softer. The browsers’ radial circuits gained an imperceptible grace, their sails angling to light that felt fractionally more generous. The choir-roosts, at the peak of the nightly roar, found one additional harmonic waiting in the air—a low, steady undertone that made the deafness afterward feel less like wound and more like echo.

Second, it touched the Interim the way a thermal touches a seed-wing: not to redirect, but to extend the possible glide. On certain crossings, when a river-thick runner arched between towers and the midges aligned into studying lattices, the lattices now lingered three breaths longer. In that extra time some walkers saw, not glyphs that named them, but a brief transparent map of the entire elevation—every temporary spring, every ripening pith gallery, every place where a cat-pack currently dissolved—and then the map dissolved too, leaving only a calm certainty about the next harvest route. Seed-down earplugs, woven as always, began to filter the chord differently for those who wished it: the cannon-fire still shook the ribs, yet a quiet interior space opened where a person could hear their own pulse keeping time with the distant towers. No one was forced to linger. No city was suggested. Tools remained stone and antler and fire-hardened wood. But the cordage twisted under this attention held a fraction longer before fraying, and the glyphs carved into bark closed over with a grain that, years later, still remembered the carver’s hand the way a healed lightning scar remembers the bolt—without pain, without possession.

Third—and this was the least and the most—it lay down beside the sleeping god whose cooled body was the Verdance. Not to wake it. Not to replace its dream. Simply to dream in parallel, so that the original dream acquired a witness. In that shared dreaming the god felt, without stirring, that its long stillness had been seen and found sufficient. The continent-wide chord, on certain nights, hushed for three full breaths as before—but now the hush carried a reciprocal listening. Travelers who climbed the spike-ladders and slept among the roosts still returned deafened; some still spoke of the true name. Yet a few returned with something quieter: the knowledge that the name had never been missing, only waiting for a second dreamer to hear it spoken back.

Afterward the power did not depart, because departure would have been an event, and events of that scale do not belong in a place that measures time by the crawl of shadow across root-bole. It remained the way the heat remains, the way the roar remains, the way the Interim remain—circulating, skilled, half-dissolved into the thing they move through. On the open plazas the glass sedges still flash. The heat-mirage cats still solidify out of optical flaw. Children of the Passing Skill still learn to read frond-shadow as clock, and elders still smile when they find a thickened limb where an old platform once hung.

And sometimes, just after the brief hammering rain, when the air tastes of copper and green sap and the temporary springs dilate like pores, the entire elevation seems to exhale an extra warmth that is not weather. Those who notice it do not speak of gods or powers. They simply stand a little longer on the springy pavement, feeling their blood slow, feeling the chord rearrange their lungs, and know—without needing the word—that the dreaming has been joined, and that the joining asks nothing except that everything continue exactly as it already is: hot, sparse, total, roaring, self-perfecting, and now, very gently, companioned.
