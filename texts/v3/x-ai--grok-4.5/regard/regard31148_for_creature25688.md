The power does not descend.

It has no need of descent. What draws it is not lack, nor cry, nor the sharp scent of things unfinished. It is drawn the way deep water is drawn to its own stillest pocket—by recognition, by the rare gravity of something that has already arrived at itself. From whatever distance or dimension such a power ordinarily keeps, it simply turns the whole of its regard upon the hollow and the slow shapes that dwell there, and in that turning there is neither hurry nor heat.

It sees the held breath of the land.  
It sees the dim, unchanging diffusion that is neither day nor dusk.  
It sees the retained warmth that asks nothing.  
It sees the thin dark seam of damp that never quite dries, the lichens mapping flesh and stone without distinction, the postures that unlock only across seasons, the open distances kept as carefully as any vow.  
It sees that the beings and the hollow are one duration, one finished chord sustained without effort or decay.

Benevolence of the ordinary kind might have mistaken this stillness for sorrow, this sparseness for poverty, this lack of multiplication for loneliness. It might have arrived with green exuberance, with brighter light, with the sweet disruption of rain and song and the quickening of seeds. It might have offered names again, or purposes, or the mercy of movement. But this power is wise, and wisdom here is the refusal to improve what is already complete. It understands that to add even one generous blade of grass, one clear note of birdsong, one gentle acceleration of a turning head, would be to begin the unravelling of the very equilibrium that called to it.

So it does nothing that can be seen as action.

It does not touch the tilted threshold stone or the pitted slabs.  
It does not warm the ash-colored skin beyond the temperature the basin already keeps.  
It does not open the lowered lids or quicken the reluctant air that moves through the figures.  
It does not speak, for any word would be weather, and this place has long since finished with weather.

Instead, the power performs the only gift that matches the hollow’s own thrift: it matches it.  

It allows its attention to settle into the same unhurried spacing the shrubs keep, the same soft-edged conformity the half-sunken stones have learned. Its regard becomes another interval of emptiness—cool, persistent, intermediate—gathering along the undersides of what is already there the way moisture does, then thinning again without drama. Where one of the beings lies cheek to the damp seam, the power’s noticing lies there too, listening to the same patience of water that no longer needs to flow. Where lichens have begun their muted cartography across an ankle or the back of a hand, the power’s awareness continues the map a single lichen-width further, then stops, content.

Over timescales that make centuries feel like the tick of cooling rock, the power remains. Not as guardian—the hollow has no predators and no future threats that have not already been absorbed into geology. Not as companion in any sense that would require the figures to know they are accompanied. Simply as the final, invisible term in the hollow’s equation of belonging. The held chord is joined by one more sustained note, so perfectly tuned that the music does not change. The dim light remains diluted. The warmth remains the temperature of forgotten afternoon. The beings continue exactly as they were: one near the broken rectangle, two in the finest gravel, another at the damp seam, another standing at the distance a shrub might keep. Their postures alter only by the width of acceptance. Nothing is occluded. Emptiness is still the dominant texture.

If you were to leave and return after human lifetimes piled upon human lifetimes, you would find the hollow unaltered—so thoroughly unaltered that even the presence of the wise and benevolent power would register only as a slight deepening of the quiet, a marginal increase in the sense that everything here has already finished becoming what it is, and is therefore free. The power itself would be indistinguishable from the place’s own duration. It would have become, without arrival or declaration, one more slow shape the land has raised from its long settling: matte, granular, soft-edged, bearing no tool marks sharp enough to read, asking nothing, altering nothing, breathing the same diluted day.

In this way the power keeps its benevolence unbroken and its wisdom intact.  

It does not save the hollow.  
The hollow was never in peril.  

It does not bless the beings.  
They were never unblessed.  

It only witnesses, forever and without disturbance, the ordinary miracle of a place and a people who require nothing—not even the attention that found them—and who continue, complete, at the temperature of a held breath, while the great powers of the world pass over and around them like weather that never quite arrives.

And that, in the end, is all.
