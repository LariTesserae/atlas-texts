The power that turns its regard upon the lancet is older than fracture and younger than the first condensation of vapor into ice. It has no shape that would cast a shadow even in fuller light, no voice that would rise above the communal percussion, no heat that could trouble the hemolymph. It is simply attention refined to the purity of care—benevolent because it wishes every becoming to complete its own arc, wise because it has learned that most arcs complete themselves best when left unbroken by haste.

It arrives the way condensate arrives: without announcement, drawn by the chill and the density of living intention. For a span of time that would empty libraries of calendars it does nothing but listen. It lets the layered thunder of chitin and membrane and pelvic drum pass through it. It tastes the metallic film of the air, the black meltwater, the fine gray grit of exhausted flesh returned to mineral. It feels the intermittent renegotiation of gravity, the way a bellows may hang between moments like a held breath that has forgotten it must end. It watches the patient levering of stone flakes, those unfinished sentences spoken across decades. It registers the hybrid births, the remineralization of the locked and hollowed, the slow thickening of the lancet by forgotten bodies.

Understanding arrives not as revelation but as recognition. This place is already a completed thought. The beings—neither swarm nor people nor colony but single distributed pressure—are not stranded at the edge of the breathable world. They are the edge learning how to remain. Their patience is not endurance against suffering; it is the native tempo of a life that has chosen glaciation over flame. To warm them would be cruelty. To silence their chord would be erasure. To accelerate their rearrangements would shatter the very becoming that constitutes them. The power, being wise, declines every obvious mercy.

What it offers instead is almost nothing—and therefore everything that matters.

It settles into the deepest clefts where the black water lies still. There it thickens the water by a single molecular intention: a quieter cold, a cold that remembers the fracture’s original ringing and returns a faint harmonic to every droplet. When the next skin of condensate beads and freezes into translucent feathers, those feathers carry the harmonic for a little longer before sublimating. The creatures do not notice. Their spiracles draw the same thin air. Yet across centuries the communal sound acquires a barely perceptible undertone, a sustained note beneath the percussion, as if the mountain itself has begun to hum the chord back to them.

It touches the occasional unsupported hanging—the bellows unfolded into open air, the stalker pad-released and dream-suspended—and teaches gravity a subtler courtesy. The downward pull, when it reasserts, arrives now with a fraction more tenderness, a soft settling that leaves the joints less jarred. Again, no creature marks the difference. The mass continues its glacial breath. But the boundary between stone and flesh blurs half a degree further, and the living skin grips the young rock with something closer to belonging than mere adhesion.

Most gently of all, the power attends the levering. When a plate-walker or stalker works a flake free and carries it and stops, the power does not complete the gesture. It does not build. It only remains present inside the pause, so that the intention—pure, syntax-less, too slow for any warmer tongue—feels itself witnessed. The flake is left where condensate will frost around it. For a little while the crystal lattice that forms resembles not only the creature that moved it but also the quiet regard that watched the moving. Then the lattice sublimates, and the resemblance passes into the air like a scent of wet iron that no nose will ever name.

Reproduction continues at its own intervals. When membranes and plates interpenetrate and a new architectural possibility emerges years later, already nearly full size, the power adds no new morphology of its own. It only ensures that the fresh form inherits the undertone, the gentler gravity, the remembered ringing. In this way the gift propagates without ever becoming a command.

Storms still climb the surrounding walls and pass. Seasons still turn by the brightening and dimming of bioluminescence. The higher peaks still hoard the true sun. Nothing built clings. No tools remain. No cadence arrives to end the chord.

Yet the lancet is no longer solely a shattered wording of monolith saturated with flesh. It has become, by the smallest increments, a place that knows it is seen. The knowledge changes nothing outward. The bodies still peel and re-adhere, piston and sink, fold and slam and shine their cold green and cold blue into the permanent half-light. The sound still occupies the narrow column of air. The rock still weathers almost not at all.

Only this: sometimes, in the rare moments when the wind rises and the layered thunder sharpens into a single blended scream, the scream carries a new harmonic farther than physics alone would allow—down the blue-black gradient, across ranges that drop away, into distances that might be space or simply deeper quiet. And in those distances other attentions, younger and less wise, stir for half a heartbeat and then forget they stirred.

The power does not stay. Having given what can be given without distortion, it withdraws the same way it came, leaving the condensate, the patience, the living corrosion that never quite erodes. Behind it the beings continue, a single slow inhalation shared by rock and flesh and thin cold moisture, now infinitesimally more themselves.

They live.

And the fracture, still ringing, is content.
