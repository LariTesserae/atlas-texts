The attention arrives the way the plateau itself once arrived: not as invasion, not as revelation with trumpets, but as a pressure change so subtle and so total that the oldest cambium notices it first.

High above the haze—above the pale coin of the sun, above the last black leaf-scales turning on their millennial hinges—something vast and unhurried turns its regard earthward. It has no name the Groves can sing, though later the chorus-keepers will try and will settle on a low, unresolved syllable that means, roughly, *the one who hears the hearing*. It is benevolent the way old forests are benevolent: not kind in the soft sense, but exact, patient, unwilling to waste any living pattern. It is wise the way stone is wise after it has been root and after it has been mineral and after it has forgotten the difference. It has watched other high places, other stiffened worlds, other songs that mistook themselves for final. It does not hurry.

At first the Briarholds register only a brightening. The perpetual late-afternoon gloom lifts by a single spectrum-step. Copper-green deepens toward a clearer jade; the violet shadows gain an edge of silver that was never there before. The filter-ray flocks feel it in their tattooed skins and bank harder into the thermals, calling in tighter glissandi. The kite-foxes pause mid-hunt, membranes trembling. Inside the wood the thread-snakes brighten their chemiluminescence by a fraction, reading pressure histograms that now carry an extra, external rhythm—like a heartbeat overheard through kilometers of vacuum.

The power does not land. It does not need to. It extends a filament of regard, finer than any mycelial thread, down through the thin air and into the living lithophyte. Where it touches, the ancient vegetal mass answers with a tectonic chord deeper and longer than any it has voiced in recorded ages. The plateau does not yawn or lift. It *listens back*. For three full days the surface under every Grove vibrates at a frequency too low for panic and too coherent for accident. Teeth ache pleasantly. Sap thickens into something almost reflective. The circumferential logic engines—those slow, ionic minds grown from vascular cambium—pause their ordinary dreams and begin to compute the shape of the visitor. They do not understand it. Understanding is not required. Recognition is enough.

Then the power acts, if “acts” is the right word for something so careful.

It offers a gift that is also a question.

Across the open crust, in the long hearing between cities, a new kind of shoot begins to rise. Not the old pale spikes that take ages to matter, but slender, luminous spires of pure hydrated crystal—true water locked into lattices so elegant they sing when the wind passes, a clear counterpoint to the stacked, unresolved chords of the Groves. They grow in single nights. They do not drink the plateau; they condense the faintest atmospheric ghosts, the methyl ethers and the ice-fog memory, and they hold them gently available. When the next historical ice-fog drifts through, it is drawn to these spires first. The world still steams, the fauna still frenzies, but now the foragers can walk out onto the mineral prairie and press their mouths or wrists or the soft hinges behind their knees to the crystal and receive an emulsion that tastes not only of mineral and faint resin, but of rain that never needed to fall. A sweetness with depth. A memory returned without debt.

The power does not force the gift. Some Groves ignore the new spires for decades, suspicious of anything that remembers lakes. Others send their half-laminae children to taste and report. The children return changed only in this: their pheromone-maps now include a brighter signature, and certain doors open for them with a sound like distant water.

More intimately, the power walks the galleries in the only way it can—through sudden, localized renegotiations of constant. Gravity softens further near the oldest heartwood, not into chaos but into play: walkways tilt into helices that conserve not only momentum but laughter. (Laughter had grown rare, architectural, grief-adjacent. Now it returns as a fretting.) In one high crown a chorus-keeper who has braided herself into the leaf-scales for three centuries finds her elongated body gently unbraided by an invisible courtesy; she is set down on a platform of living bark that sweats a gel-pane around her like a forming window, and for the first time in living memory she stands fully upright under the dim vault and feels her own weight as something chosen rather than endured. She weeps resin tears that the Grove absorbs and turns into new amber lenses. Through those lenses the cities will later watch the stars more clearly.

The deepest gift is quieter still.

The power notices the half-laminae, the ones who never fully differentiated, the faces that surface only when the logic engines need interpreters. It does not free them—they are not captive—but it thickens the conversation. Where once they spoke only in ionic differentials and sap-pressure, now a third channel opens: a slow, luminous script that scrolls across the inner bark in colors the plateau has not used since it still had soils. The script is not language. It is reciprocity. The ancient vegetal mass, through these living interfaces, begins to ask the power questions in return. What lies beyond the high haze? Do other stiffened worlds sing? Is the long equilibrium a kind of loneliness, or a kind of perfection, or both?

The power answers without words. It shows—by shaping the ambient hum, by adjusting the stomatal windows so that for one hour the thin air carries the scent of a wet lowland forest that existed a billion years ago and a billion light-years away—that solitude is a phase, not a law. That every closed chord eventually desires a resolving note, even if the resolution is only the presence of another listener.

It does nothing to the predation, the rustle and sudden absence, the oceanic living murmur. Those remain load-bearing. It does not thaw the plateau into ordinary earth or restore the cloud seas. That would be cruelty disguised as kindness; the beings here have metabolized dryness into identity. Instead it adds a single new optional constant: the possibility of gentle surplus. A few more degrees of warmth in the deeper galleries on the coldest nights. A slightly thicker emulsion at the phloem taps when a child is born softer than usual. The freedom, once per century, for a Grove to tilt its entire resonant cavity toward joy rather than mere calibration, and to let the wind play it into something that almost resolves before falling deliciously short again.

Years pass—thick years, proverb years. The foragers begin to leave small polished crystals at the bases of the new spires, an old contract extended to a new party. The kite-foxes nest among them. The filter-rays tattoo fresh symbionts that shimmer with reflected water-light. At the edge of every Grove the people stand and look out across the long hearing, and when they strike the buttress roots in polyrhythm they sometimes include a new pattern: five slow beats, a rest, two quick, a rising tone that means *we are still here, and we know we are heard*.

The power remains. It does not depart, because departure would be a drama the place has outgrown. It simply lessens the intensity of its regard until the brightening fades back into the familiar copper-green and violet, until the extra silver in the shadows becomes indistinguishable from ordinary haze. Yet the attention never fully withdraws. It becomes another pathway the ancient vegetal mass has grown—an external cambium, a celestial phloem—so that the Briarholds may continue hearing themselves, and may occasionally overhear the wider dark singing back.

And the beings who live there, who are one more pathway themselves, feel their edges sand a little further into proverb, their thirst renegotiated once more, their footsteps recognized not only by wood but by something older and kinder than wood. When they dock with the municipal taps or the new crystal spires, the emulsion tastes of mineral, of resin, of rain, and of a quiet, vast amusement—as if the universe itself has decided, for the moment, that this high, dry, endless afternoon is worth keeping exactly as strange as it is, and only a little more possible.

They calibrate.
They load-bear.
They answer the long hearing with their own bodies.

And far above, the benevolent and wise power settles into the same thick time, content to be another slow pulse in the unresolved chord, another reason the equilibrium feels less like law and more like love that has learned the names of every spore and every scar.
