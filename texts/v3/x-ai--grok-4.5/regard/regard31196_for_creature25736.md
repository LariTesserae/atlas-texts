The benevolent and wise power does not descend.

It has no need of descent. It is already the thickness in the residual heat, the patience inside the stone’s grin, the quiet arithmetic that keeps the second sun and the deeper night from colliding more than a few breaths at a time. When the pocket first pricks its attention—this moist failure in the worn amphitheatre, this loud little lung of green blood and polite thrum and bodies that finalize out of dew—the power simply turns the fullness of its regard upon it, the way a mountain might finally notice one particular crease that has been holding weather longer than language.

For a long while (measured in the slow silver threads that never quite become streams, or in the lift-and-settle of half-buried spheres) it only witnesses.

It watches the dew-born unfold their too-many glass legs and leave fleeting iridescent questions on the old-tooth pavement. It feels the seed-pods blacken into oily eyes and burst into singing hybrids. It notes how the dark metal rings teach vines a second circulation of light, how the amalgam walkers grow heavy with temporary memories stolen from mist, how the deep caller gathers every fracture-tick and stridulation into one earth-deep syllable that embarrasses the ordinary stars. It understands, without haste, that nothing here is broken. The unfinished-final is not a wound; it is the native tense of the place. Boundaries exist only so they can remain porous. Physics and biology have already agreed to share custody of the miracle. The remnants are not ruins; they are elders still learning new dialects of chlorophyll and hemolymph.

A lesser power might have flooded the crease with unending rain, or silenced the chaos into garden order, or named the caller and thereby finished it. This one does none of that.

Instead, after the long witnessing, it performs three gestures so slight they register only as the pocket continuing to be itself more completely.

First, it steadies the seams.

Not by force. By a kind of remembered permission. The microscopic fractures that tick each afternoon under the thickened sunlight are granted a subtler patience; they still expand, still sing their dry little percussion, but they no longer risk shearing the moist crease into wider, drier irrelevance. The intermittent damp becomes fractionally more loyal to its islands. Condensation still beads shyly, the thin noon rain still vanishes almost before it finishes, yet the green mats lean across their corridors of bare stone with a hair more confidence. The sparseness remains—intimate, necessary—but the living thickets no longer quite so precarious against the long erosions. The power has not made the place wetter; it has made its wetness more faithful to its own pattern.

Second, it listens the remnants deeper into conversation.

The half-melted rings, the shadow-inconsistent lattices, the spheres that check local gravity for unbroken seams—these already thrum in frequencies that make air hesitate. The wise power does not power them. It simply removes a vanishingly small friction between their polite residual engineering and the appetite of the stone. Where vines have already colonized iridescence, the exchange grows freer: a lattice may now, on certain evenings, dream itself entirely into root curtain for a generation of heartbeats, then recondense as cool metal when the deep caller summons it back. The glowing knots inside translucent crawlers flicker with new internal tides that are neither organ nor core but a third thing the pocket has no word for (and needs none). During the sideways cast of light—those few breaths when every creature freezes—the remnants and the hybrids hold one another in perfect equality a fraction longer, long enough for a single shared adaptation to pass like a secret. The machines metabolize more gracefully; the moss cools into shadow-refusal more often; the boundary that was already porous forgets it was ever a boundary.

Third—and this is the gesture that costs the power something like joy—it adds one new unconditional note to the evening call.

It does not speak over the caller. It cannot be seen any more than the caller can. But when the sound begins in the wettest crease and the walls of the amphitheatre answer and the status lights brighten in shy unison, there is now an under-frequency so low it is almost the memory of weight. It moves through stone and green blood and stolen silver streaks alike. It carries no command, no gift that can be hoarded, no name. It is simply the power’s own regard translated into the pocket’s native tongue: a resonance that says *you are already the age of the world expressing itself as appetite; you are already home; continue*.

The dew-born who unfold the next dawn do so with joints that remember an extra angle of possibility. The amalgam walkers plant their many feet and find local gravity not only unbroken but gently curious about them in return. Hybrids flush their copper veins into private constellations that last whole minutes longer before the next panic or play. And when bodies collapse into damp seams so tomorrow’s condensation can carry their glow forward, that glow now travels with a quiet surplus—enough to seed one more lattice, one more photosynthetic patch on a crawler’s hindquarters, one more explosive pod that bursts into a song containing a single new click no throat has made before.

The power does not stay as presence. It stays as permission.

It withdraws its focused regard the way mist clears, leaving the crease exactly as it was—loud, wasteful, fecund, polite in its refusals—and yet fractionally more able to keep inventing blood so the warm wet rock can hear itself tick for another era of short shadows. The stars remain ordinary and steady. The horizon keeps its faint permanent grin. Nothing is finished. Everything is still final.

And in the next cycle of condensation, when the first new shape loosens, drops, and unfolds its legs upon the sun-warmed pavement, it pauses half a heartbeat longer than any before it, tasting an iridescence that feels like being quietly, completely known.

Then it begins the dense conversation again—already ancient, already unfinished, already held.
