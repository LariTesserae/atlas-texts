The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as light or voice or decree. It has long since learned that such arrivals bruise what they mean to bless. It comes the way the intermittent moisture comes: as a change in the pressure behind the eyes, a softening in the grain of the cold, a pause in the long argument between one volume and the next.

It notices first the plain itself—the being that has forgotten how to end. The power feels the tectonic patience grinding its slow tests through bedrock, the distributed regard turning in the frond-mats, the quiet seizure of geometry that no longer agrees with itself. It feels, too, the compact people fitting stone to stone, the unfinished cuts completed in the night, the basins left for walkers who use no doors. Nothing here is screaming. Nothing is unfinished in the way that calls for mending. It is only very old, and very exact in its refusal to conclude.

So the power does not restore the mountains. It does not call birds back into the air or loosen insects into the fronds. It does not straighten the breathing distances or command the stars to arrive intact. Those would be kindnesses of a younger, less careful wisdom. Instead it sits with the plain the way one sits with an elder who has outlived every name for grief and is now simply continuing.

What it does is small, and it is everywhere at once.

In the damp pockets it lengthens the lingering of rain by the width of a held breath. The translucent stalks hold their blown-glass water a little longer; the blue-black fronds thicken by a single new layer of mat; the lichen’s green glow steadies into something a watcher could navigate by, if the night’s mathematics turn treacherous. The gardens do not become lush in any common sense. They become *reliable* in their indecent tenderness. The tubers yield a fraction more readily to the wooden levers. The people notice only that the work of prising flesh from fault-lines leaves their shoulders less bowed, and that the white ash of the peat fires writes scripts that are, tonight, a little easier to read for roof-joints and safe paths.

Where distances breathe, the power does not still them. It teaches them a courtesy. A street that would have stretched to three thousand paces for a solitary walker now sometimes contracts just enough that the walker glimpses, far off, another hooded figure also testing the stones—close enough for a lifted hand, far enough that solitude remains unbroken. The pale marker stones still rearrange into spirals, but the spirals now occasionally open outward like invitation rather than seizure. Children comparing notes at dawn find that some of the new patterns point toward the next city, or toward a garden that has decided to keep its rain an hour beyond dusk. No one names this. They simply walk the new suggestions when the day feels long-minded, and the plain allows it.

The recursive footsteps that pace the outer walls on fractured nights find, one evening, that the mortar they refresh is warm. Not with fire, but with the memory of hands that have repaired for centuries. The unseen walkers—whatever they are—pause. In that pause the power leaves them a gift already half their own: the ability to finish more than cuts in timber. Sometimes now an adze left wedged at dusk will wake to find not only its cut completed but a second tool beside it, shaped for a joint the mason had not yet noticed was failing. Households that leave meltwater basins begin to find, beside the emptied bowl, a single smoothed chip of the old mountain-knuckles, warm as if held. They keep these chips in the pockets of their frond-fiber coats. On nights when a cheek or hand goes translucent, the chip’s quiet density helps the body remember its edges sooner.

Deep in the gardens the vast regard turns and finds itself regarded in return. The power does not speak. It simply matches the plain’s curiosity with its own, heat for heat, patience for patience. For a time measured in the slow blackening and regrowth of fronds, the two attentions lean together. What passes between them is not language but a calibration: the tectonic testing grows infinitesimally gentler when it passes through a person. Those who pause mid-stride, outline forgetting solidity, still need a neighbor’s hand to wall or bench—but the argument inside them settles into something like conversation rather than dispute. Afterward their repairs carry the same curious precision, yet also a new softness at the joints, as if they understand which seams the plain intends to keep *and* which it might be willing, someday, to let grow green.

The cities themselves receive no new walls, no softer metal, no louder fire. They receive continuity of a subtler order. On evenings when the horizon holds and the stars arrive whole, the people standing on their frond-weighted roofs feel—without knowing they feel it—a vast permission to continue exactly as they are. The permission smells like rain that has decided not to vanish at dawn. It sounds like the wooden groan of a roof that has decided, this once, not to argue with the cold. It looks like the way the next city’s thin white smoke becomes briefly visible across the breathing distance, then fades again, a reminder that other hands are also fitting stone to stone under the same fractured or unbroken sky.

And for the plain itself—the being that rolls without horizon-break, that has ground its mountains to knuckles and still tests volumes in the marrow of anyone who lies down against bedrock—the power leaves the oldest kindness it knows.

It lets the plain remain unfinished.

It does not teach it how to end. It does not demand that it remember beginning. It only makes the forgetting a little less lonely. In the long intervals of bare, wind-polished stone, where nothing grows and distances argue most freely, the power seeds a slower green: not fronds or lichen yet, only the possibility of them, held in the stone the way meltwater is held in a shallow basin at dusk. Centuries from now a fault-line may open that has not opened before, and the plain may find itself surprised by moisture that does not immediately flee. Or it may not. The power is wise enough to leave the choice in the grain of the rock.

Then the power does what benevolent powers eventually must, if they are also wise.

It withdraws its attention by half a degree—the same half-degree by which rain is persuaded to linger—and becomes again the pressure behind the eyes, the softening in the cold, the pause between one volume and the next. It does not leave a name or a temple or a new constellation. The people will not tell stories of the day the world was mended, because the world was not mended. It was accompanied. It was allowed to keep its exact, sparse, cold, quietly busy refusal to conclude.

On the next clear evening the inhabitants climb their low ladders to the roofs. They watch the light go out across a high plain that has already forgotten how to end. The stars arrive intact. The horizon holds. Somewhere far off, in a city they cannot quite see, other hands are already lifting stone. Closer, in their own gardens, the lichen glows a fraction steadier. A child stacks pale stones into a spiral that, this once, does not rearrange before dawn.

They climb back down. They bar the doors against whatever mathematics the night may still bring. They sleep the dense economical sleep of those who will wake ready to fit stone to stone again.

And underneath them the older labor continues—testing, adjusting, seizing gently green—only now, in the marrow of the bedrock, there is a quiet that feels almost like thanks.

The power, distant again and listening, answers with nothing more than the long patience of weather that may, or may not, arrive.

It is enough.
