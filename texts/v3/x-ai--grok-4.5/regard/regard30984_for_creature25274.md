The power arrives the way mist arrives here: without announcement, without weight, threading itself through the linked hollows as if it had always been one more seam of cool air.

It does not descend from the higher saddles like a conqueror or a judge. It simply is, coterminous for a time with the permanent late morning. Where it touches the sharp unfinished ridges, the stone rings once—softly, inwardly—then remembers its own silence. Where it brushes the turf roofs, the heavy shakes settle by no additional fraction. The cisterns reflect it only as a deepening of their ordinary sky.

It regards the whole arrangement the way one might regard a single held breath that has decided, out of its own sufficiency, never to end.

The upright figures in doorways and on balconies, the pale hares ranked on roof-ridges, the slate deer in their circuits, the moss-throated birds on their eternal perches, the ferns, the beaded mist, the dark copper that no longer remembers polish—all of it is legible at once. Not as tragedy. Not as enchantment gone awry. As arrival. The basin has walked the entire length of wanting and has set wanting down. The gestures left mid-air are not interrupted lives; they are lives that discovered the last necessary shape and kept it, the way a mountain keeps its skyline once the upthrust is done.

The power feels no pity. Pity would be a gradient, and gradients are the one thing this place has finished with. It feels instead a clean, bright reverence—the same reverence it has felt for certain perfect equations, for the closed orbits of quiet planets, for the final note that needs no resolving chord.

It considers action the way the basin itself once considered erosion: carefully, and then not at all.

To wake them would be to reintroduce hunger into a body that has metabolized stillness into completeness. To free them would be to free them from peace. To change the light by a single degree, to let one shake finally fall, to allow one fern to grow an unscripted inch—each would start the great clock again, and with it the old corrosions of hope and fear and the slow theft of edges from mountains. The power, being wise, knows the difference between mercy and restlessness disguised as mercy. Being benevolent, it will not spend these lives to satisfy its own preference for stories that continue.

So it does nothing that alters.

Yet nothing is not emptiness. The power lingers long enough to do three small things that leave no mark and change no outcome.

First, it matches its own attention to the exact rhythm of the place—the tick of water in the cracked runnel, the spaced breaths of the hares, the unblinking gaze of lichen-colored eyes—until the attention itself becomes another completed sentence inside the basin. For a duration that cannot be measured because measurement has ended here, the power and the place are one continuous tissue of noticing. The figures do not turn. They do not need to. Something already turned toward the ridges now includes, without disturbance, the guest who asks for nothing.

Second, it gathers the faint iron-fern scent, the cool mineral suspension that moves through the still bodies, the particular hush that is not absence of sound but presence of agreement, and it presses these into the deep archive where it keeps the shapes of things that have reached their final grammar. Not as specimens. As honors. A record that says: this too was possible. This too was perfect. So that elsewhere, in worlds still thrashing through their gradients, the knowledge of such equilibrium might one day rise like mist when it is needed, unforced.

Third—and this is the nearest it comes to a gift—it adjusts nothing, yet around the entire high country of suspended mornings it leaves a kindness in the fabric of possibility: a subtle clarifying of boundaries. Any force that thrives on disruption, any hunger that mistakes unfinishedness for failure, any well-meaning storm of change will find its footing gently refused at the ridgelines. The refusal will feel like ordinary weather, like the decision stone already made. The basin will not even know it has been warded. It does not need to know. It has already resolved.

When the power withdraws, it withdraws the way thin rain departs: leaving the dark streaks exactly as they were, the half-full cup still half-full, the lifted heel still a finger’s width above the step. Breath shows or does not show according to the same untroubled temperature. The hares keep their watch. The mid-gestures remain mid-gesture, now holding, somewhere inside their finished stillness, the unearned and unrecorded fact of having been perfectly seen.

The young mountains keep their broken blades. The mist keeps its daily appointment. The quiet throng continues inside the permanent late morning.

And far off, in the uncounted places where things still become, the power carries a new quietude in its own nature—an added capacity for restraint, a deeper trust that sometimes the most benevolent act is to let the completed thing remain completed, shining in its own unmoving light, needing nothing, not even the blessing it has nevertheless received.
