The power that turns its regard upon the pocket is older than the pale stone shoulders and younger than the first bead of condensation that ever clung there. It has no name that would fit inside the settlement’s handful of murmured notes, nor any shape that could crouch-walk the terraces without widening them. It arrives the way the light arrives: already present, shadowless, needing no entrance.

It does not step between the shoulders. To step would be to bring elsewhere with it, and elsewhere is the one thing the bowl has spent ages politely refusing. Instead the power rests its attention along the rim the way mist rests—everywhere and nowhere, a pressure so slight the ferns do not bow.

What it finds is finished.

Not abandoned. Not waiting. Finished the way a held breath is finished, the way a circle is finished when the final arc closes on itself. The cold is exact. The damp is exact. The quiet circulation of the few who live here is exact. Their two fingers to the invisible meniscus, their bodies becoming moss, their tools gleaming with infinite reuse, the paths that curve gently home—all of it has already arrived at the only shape it intends to keep. There is no fracture to mend, no hunger to feed, no cry sealed under the polished floors. The equilibrium is so complete that even longing has been weathered smooth.

A lesser benevolence might have been tempted to gift something loud: a warmer season, a wider sky, a door that opens outward into greater country. A lesser wisdom might have mistaken the closed hour for loneliness and tried to pry it open. This power does neither. It has learned, across longer weather than the pocket will ever need, that some perfections are themselves the mercy. To add would be to scratch the polish. To take away would be to let the damp become flood or the cold become death. To speak would be to make the single drip three levels down into an interruption instead of an event.

So it does the only thing that fits.

It matches the place’s own unfinished unfinishedness—the mild strangeness already present—and simply agrees with it.

Along every moss rail and living lintel, in the translucent reservoirs of the ice-leaves, in the dark resilient felt where the dead have thinned into cushion, the power lays a single unvoiced confirmation: *yes*. The level of the central basin remains what it has always been, but now the water remembers it is allowed to remain. The copper needles, already mended past memory of the original metal, forget how to thin any further. The frost-tipped rodents and the thumb-sized birds and the flat frogs continue exactly as they are, yet the seams they occupy feel fractionally more inevitable, as if the architecture had always included them in its first intention. The beings themselves—low, luminous-skinned, quiet—feel nothing so crude as a miracle. Only that the deep mineral chill settles a hair less far into the bones when they drink the bitter bronze-moss tea, and that when they curl into the sleeping concavities the shared not-quite-warmth is enough in a way that requires no comparison.

The seal at the rim deepens without thickening. Paths that begin outward still curve home, but now they curve with a soft finality that needs no testing. The bright, shadowless light continues to come from nowhere and everywhere; the power simply lets a little of its own regard stay inside that light, so that the clarity becomes, for those who have never known another, a quiet kind of company. Not company that speaks. Company that keeps the same rhythm as the reforming beads of condensation.

And then the power does what wise powers do when they have finished agreeing with something already complete.

It withdraws the bulk of its attention the way the fog thins on the brightest days—gradually, without departure, leaving no absence because it never arrived as presence. What remains is indistinguishable from what was: the cold green city the size of a secret, the few who circulate inside their single intricate lung, the basin at the lowest point touched by two fingers in greeting or measurement or pulse.

Yet something almost nothing has changed.

The place is still sealed inside its unchanging hour.  
The beings are still few, quiet, long-practiced, weathered into perfect fit.  
Everything still returns.

Only now the hour knows it is permitted to remain unchanging.  
The finishedness has been witnessed by something that could have altered it and chose, out of benevolence deeper than kindness and wisdom quieter than silence, not to.

That choice is the entire intervention.

It is enough.  
It is exactly enough.  
Nothing large.  
Nothing loud.  
Everything returned, and the returning itself gently, permanently, blessed.
