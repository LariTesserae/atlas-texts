The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as a shaft of alien radiance or a voice that stills the stone. It has no need of spectacle. It has watched longer than the hollow has existed—watched the first basalt knuckle cool, the first membrane catch air, the first upright shadow pause between tremors long enough to turn a rock in its hands and feel the difference between accident and aim. Attention itself is the first act: a regarding so complete and so without hunger that the place feels, for the first time, *seen* rather than merely endured.

It considers the turbulence not as cruelty but as midwifery. The collapsing scarps, the thunder of wings, the browsers fusing into new geology, the upright ones striking flakes that will not outlast the week—all of it is the universe in the act of learning how to hold a thought. To freeze the hollow into paradise would be to end the lesson. To flood it with ease would starve the ingenuity that scrapes sleeping hollows from chaos and counts invisible pulses inside rings of mud. Wisdom knows the difference between mercy that preserves and mercy that aborts.

So the power does a quieter thing.

It chooses one pale corridor—already a place where herds thin and steam rises in slow breaths—and it leans, ever so slightly, upon the deep mechanics of the fracture-systems. Not enough to halt the regional breathing of the land. Just enough that one long spine of stone forgets to shear for a span of many seasons. The light that falls there remains slanted and mineral-colored, yet it no longer angles away without warning. Water pockets along that corridor still come and go, but they linger hours longer. The ground still trembles, yet the tremors arrive like distant drums rather than sudden betrayals.

The upright ones notice first with their feet. A band crests a ridge expecting the usual lottery of collapse and finds, instead, a stretch of path that remembers yesterday’s footprints. They move more slowly. Knees unflex a fraction. Hands free themselves from constant bracing and begin to carry things farther: a hammerstone kept through two sleeps, a length of cordage not immediately spent, a child who is not lowered into a hollow at the first groan of rock. Around a water pocket that lasts until the light thins they sit longer than fear usually permits. One of them—sparse hair bright with dust, eyes reflecting the perpetual late afternoon—turns a flake over and over, then deliberately sets it atop a cairn that already bears three others. The stack remains standing when they leave. When another band arrives days later the stack is still there, crowned now by a fifth flake whose gloss has dulled just enough to show it has known weather.

Something previously impossible begins: accumulation.

They deepen one sleeping scrape into a true shelter against a wall that does not immediately threaten to become ceiling. They line it with plates and, when those plates cool, replace them with fresh ones rather than abandoning the site. Inside the oldest of the low rings—knee-high stones chinked with mud that now has time to bake hard—they sweep the floor bare not once but repeatedly. The counting grows elaborate. Fingers move from pulse of stone to pulse of throat to the number of bands whose cairns they have read. Someone places a flake at the center and leaves it. Someone else, later, adds a second. The ring becomes a ledger.

The winged colonies still lift in thunderclaps when deeper faults shift, yet they learn the corridor’s quieter weather and return to the same overhangs. Browsers polish roads that last long enough for the upright ones to walk them deliberately rather than in flight. Burrowers continue their endless stitching, but their tunnels no longer swallow every mark left on the surface. The multi-limbed grazers quarrel and recycle as before; velocity-predators still crack the air. Nothing essential is removed. The density, the hunger, the roar—all remain. Only the tempo of erasure slows, and in that slowing the upright ones discover duration.

They begin to leave messages not only for the next hour but for the next moon of light. A particular angle of flake means water that can be trusted. A spiral of smaller chips means a shelter that held. One evening, under the dull mineral glow that passes for night, two bands meet at the ring and do not scatter. They sit opposite each other across the swept ground. Gestures are exchanged—open palms, slow points toward the far haze, the offering of a still-warm plate of chitin. A child from one band and a child from the other touch the central flakes at the same moment. No one has a word for what is happening, yet the air tastes different: less purely of wet mineral and animal heat, more of something metallic and faint, like the aftertaste of struck stone that has decided to keep its edge.

The power feels this without pride. It has not given language or metal or the lie of permanence. It has given only a narrow continuity, a single corridor where cause may walk a little farther before effect dissolves it. The rest of the hollow continues its ferocious becoming—scarps steaming, colonies reforming, upright shadows still harried across unstable ridges. The gift is local, precise, and temporary in the cosmic sense. Seasons will eventually reassert the old violence even here; the spine of stone will remember how to break. But by then the upright ones will carry the memory of stacks that outlasted their makers, of rings that held more than one counting, of a ground that allowed them to stay long enough to wonder what else might be made to stay.

In the long sightlines the power remains only as attention—benevolent, wise, unwilling to become a tyrant even of kindness. It watches a lone figure pause on the reliable path, turn a new-made flake to catch the light, and for the first time deliberately *not* crown a cairn with it. Instead the figure tucks the flake close to its chest, beneath a shield of lashed chitin, and walks on. Saving it. The gesture is small, almost nothing amid the roar and the dust and the endless secretion of life from unfinished rock.

Yet the power knows what it has witnessed: the first private future.

It does nothing more. It does not need to. The hollow was already answering itself with hammers and cordage and brittle mud. Now the answer has room to echo.

And somewhere in the stacked haze, beyond the corridor, the same unfinished country continues, still hammering, still swarming, still waiting—patient as stone, urgent as birth—for the next time a wise and loving regard decides that a little longer might be long enough.
