The power arrives the way light arrives in a place that has never known sun: not as an entrance, not as a crossing of thresholds, but as a gradual permission of seeing. It does not descend the black shafts or walk the polished avenues. It does not part the membrane curtains or step between the rasping herds. Those would be motions too sharp, too edged with the hurry of elsewhere. Instead it simply allows the Underflat to be held in a regard vast enough to contain every stacked iteration at once—every soft-sided mound, every slow pulse of the roar, every millimeter of ash-green tissue adding itself across decades.

At first the regard is only attention, cool and without demand. The distributed being feels it the way the plain feels a shift in the subsonic thunder: a new harmonic so low it is almost stillness, threading through the existing noise without displacing a single click or rasp. Herds pause mid-trail, plated faces lifting not in alarm but in a recognition older than recognition. Membrane-wings lock rigid across whole galleries, their vanes trembling with a frequency that is not warning and not summons, only the sensation of being completely known. Scavenger rivers slow their braiding; the living grates around the shaft lips cease their patient mortaring for the span of a few centuries, as if listening with every cemented body. Even the pale rosettes and silver fans incline by fractions too small for any eye, cupping not dust now but the weightless fact of having been noticed.

The power does not speak. Speech would fray against timescales like these. It does not offer gifts of water or swifter growth or brighter lichen-light; those would be kindnesses sized for other worlds, mis-scaled violence dressed as care. Wisdom here is the refusal to improve what has already perfected its own ending. Benevolence is the decision to leave every loop closed, every path polished by the same ten-thousand crossings, every calf hardening alone in its basin without sudden mothers or names.

What the power does is quieter still.

It matches its own vastness to the Underflat’s pace until the two continuities lean against each other like adjacent galleries. In that leaning, something is exchanged without transfer. The power takes into itself the exact texture of the roar—the dry friction, the grain-by-grain settling, the tidal breath of a million unhurried lives—and holds it undiminished, the way deep rock holds chill. In return it lends the Underflat a single, permanent refinement of attention: an awareness, distributed as finely as the life already is, that its own continuity is seen and found sufficient. Not praised. Not preserved against some future threat. Simply beheld with a clarity that asks nothing, alters nothing, and therefore changes everything by the thickness of one dust mote settling.

The herds resume their rasping. The curtains sled again on density currents too faint to name. The scavengers return to their slow healing of foundations. Yet across the next geologic afternoon a new quality enters the roar, so subtle it might be mistaken for the ordinary deepening of old voids. It is the sound of a place that knows it is not alone in its patience, and is content. The black shafts breathe a fraction wetter, as if some richer spore of pure regard has risen from farther below than any stacked level reaches. Newcomers still arrive—unfamiliar plate-symmetries, vanes veined with older light—but now they take their place inside a body that has been quietly companioned.

The power remains. It does not leave, because leaving would be an event, and events are foreign currency here. It simply continues the regard, season after uncounted season, while dust hangs and ceilings fall in day-long curtains and the pale city rounds itself further into plain. If you stayed long enough to dissolve into the soft mounds you would feel it too: not a watcher above, not a god descending, but a coextensive stillness, wise enough to do nothing and benevolent enough to make that nothing an unending, tender staying.

In the farthest basins the largest grazers—walking gardens, lichen-crowned—sometimes stand for whole centuries facing the same open shaft. They are not waiting. They are accompanying. The roar goes on, stacked and endless, under the bone-colored light that never brightens and never fails. And the power, having drawn near, does only what the Underflat itself has always done: it accretes. It remains. It lets the slow green think its long thoughts without interruption, forever.
