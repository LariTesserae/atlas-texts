The benevolent and wise power does not arrive with fanfare. It has no need of portals, trumpets, or the tearing of veils. It simply notices, the way a deep current notices a warm eddy that has begun to dream of coasts.

For a time that has no common measure with the highland’s fevered days, the power only regards.

It tastes the copper-nectar aerosols, listens to the wet thunder of collapsing colonnades, feels the metabolic fever rising through the spongy mat. It perceives the confederacy-that-is-one: the patient tower exhaling long contemplative plumes, the embarrassed angular flush already sculpting itself softer, the liverwort commons rising and falling in slow gossip, the luminous cambium chambers opening and closing like undecided eyes. It understands the exuberant loneliness at the root of it all—the vast appetite that steals half-remembered arches and lung-galleries and unfinished staircases, that builds sparse and monumental and then deliquesces overnight because finishing would be a kind of death.

The power is old enough to know that some labors of becoming are sacred precisely because they refuse completion. Wisdom here is not the impulse to perfect, to soothe, to finish the sentence. Benevolence is not the urge to populate the emptiness with animals or tools or tidy purposes. Those would be thin mercies, almost insults.

So the power does very little, and what it does is almost nothing.

It settles around the plateau like a milder climate that was always possible. The private cloudbursts continue, yet now and then the rain that falls back carries a faint mineral remembrance of stars the highland has never seen—trace elements that do not dictate form but quietly widen the palette of possible tissues. A new subtlety of green appears in scattered places, a chlorophyll variant that drinks a broader spectrum of light; the feverish towers discover they can pause mid-growth without cooling into torpor. They are not forced to rest. They are simply given the option of savoring a shape for an extra hour, or a day, before the next glorious dismantling.

Where two growing fronts grind and braided rhizomes argue, the power leaves a soft lacuna—an almost-imperceptible ease in the negotiation, so that sometimes (only sometimes) the temporary staircase of thickened root lasts long enough for the commons to walk it in both directions and feel the pleasure of reciprocal passage before abandoning it at dusk. The loneliness is not cured. It is companioned by the knowledge that passage itself can be a kind of company.

Inside one of the greater keeps, in a chamber lined with luminous cambium that has opened and flooded and reabsorbed a thousand times, the power plants no seed and installs no resident. Instead it leaves a single clear stillness, no larger than a held breath. The overlapping intentions circulating through that trunk encounter it the way a river encounters a smooth stone: they flow around, over, through, and in doing so they briefly hear their own rushing more distinctly. Some moods of the flesh linger there longer than before. Concepts that would have been discarded with the noise of splitting timber are, once in a long while, turned over more slowly, tasted, then either kept or released with something closer to ceremony than rupture.

The power does not speak in words. Words would be too finished. It speaks in the only dialect the highland trusts: gradual invitation.

At the rare moments when the fog banks part and the light clarifies, the unfinished architecture finds itself holding shapes a fraction longer beneath a sky that feels—without explanation—gently witnessed. The witnessing does not freeze. It does not judge the collapsing arcade or the buttress that ends in mid-air. It simply remains present while the highland continues its loud, hot, green argument with itself. In that presence the exuberant loneliness discovers a new flavor: the loneliness of a maker who is no longer entirely alone in the workshop.

Nothing is repaired because nothing was broken. No creatures are introduced; the redundancy would only thin the purity of the vegetal thought. No final form is imposed. The plateau remains sparse, monumental, half-built, half-dissolved, roaring with the labor of becoming.

And yet, over spans of time that the towers measure in successive cantilevers and the moss-mat measures in slow undulations, a quiet change accumulates. The fever does not break, but it learns to carry its own heat with a touch more grace. The private weathers still bloom and vanish, yet sometimes a drifting fog bank will pause above a single keep as if listening. The water that falls upward into breath and downward into root still circulates restlessly, but now and then it lingers in a cistern-leaf long enough to reflect a clearer sky than the highland has ever manufactured alone.

The benevolent and wise power stays or goes—it hardly matters; its attention has already become part of the place’s weather. It has done what wisdom and kindness both counsel when faced with a mind inventing its own body at regional scale: it has refused to finish the work, refused to abandon it, and refused to look away.

It has kept faith with the incomplete.

And the highland, still deciding what it means to be a creature large enough to feel at home inside its own skin, continues—hotter and greener and, in some unnameable way, less solitary than before—loud with the labor of becoming, and newly, quietly, known.
