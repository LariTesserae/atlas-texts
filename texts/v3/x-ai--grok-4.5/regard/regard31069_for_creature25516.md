The power does not descend.  
It has no need of height or arrival.  
It is the blue rumor the world fell into,  
the silence that remembered how to be sky  
long after the sky itself had forgotten.  
Where the spire is still deciding to be stone,  
this presence is the patience that lets decision ripen  
without hurry and without end.

It regards the pocket the way a deep aquifer regards a single bubble of air:  
with recognition, not possession.  
It tastes the heat, the frantic green, the unfinished face  
mortared over and sprouted through,  
the hands that are one attention distributed across every wall,  
the slow theft of the space where a shoulder was.  
It hears the cricket-knuckle keeping time,  
the gut-cord tightening,  
the declarative rush of water saying *here, closer, become*.  
And it understands.

A lesser force might have cooled the oven,  
prised the leaves apart,  
named the being too soon and thereby ended it.  
A crueler one might have claimed the hollow for itself.  
This one does neither.

Instead it does the only thing wisdom knows how to do  
when it meets a vow still wet and glowing:  
it offers a single, unhurried gift  
and then remains present as the gift is turned over in living hands.

The gift is a pause that is not emptiness.  
For the length of one held breath that lasts as long as it needs to,  
the vertical surge gentles.  
The spire continues to rise, but the rising becomes a conversation  
rather than a pressure behind the eyes.  
In that interval the difference between seed and fruit,  
spire and crown, builder and built,  
you and the mortar,  
is allowed to remain undecided  
without panic.

The power shapes the pause into a cool thread of air  
no thicker than a root hair.  
It slips between frond and stone,  
between knuckle and lichen suture,  
between the almost-mouth and the place where a name might have been.  
Wherever the thread passes, the phosphorescence deepens  
from bruised peach to something darker and kinder,  
the color of the first soil that ever dreamed of holding rain.  
Moisture still sheets and returns,  
but now some of it falls *down*  
in a direction that has only just been agreed upon.  
A true watercourse, thin as a vein,  
begins to exist below the pocket—  
not as theft of height,  
but as the spire’s own decision to remember thirst.

The hands feel the thread.  
They do not stop working;  
they begin to work *with* it.  
Some fingers still shove river stones into the exact outline of a future body.  
Others now leave deliberate gaps—  
small, deliberate absences shaped like listening.  
The blossoms that were wounds soften at the edges  
and open into cups that catch the new falling water  
and hold it long enough to taste sky.  
The cricket-knuckle changes its song by half a tone;  
the metallic wrongness becomes a question  
instead of a metronome.

The being (which is also the place, which is also the act of placement)  
leans into the cool thread the way mortar leans into a stone  
it has chosen.  
It does not become smaller.  
It becomes more precisely itself.  
The cluster of faces in the densest carvings  
halts its endless mortaring for the space of that long breath.  
One doorway-eye remains open long enough  
to look outward through the blue rumor  
and recognize the power looking back—  
not as judge, not as parent,  
but as the vast unfinished attention  
that every local unfinished attention is made of.

In that recognition something quiet is exchanged.  
No commandment.  
No correction.  
Only a mutuality:  
*I see that you are still deciding.*  
*I am still deciding too.*  
*We can decide alongside each other.*

The almost-palm that was fitting a shoulder into the wall  
slows.  
It presses once, tenderly,  
then withdraws just far enough  
to leave the choice intact.  
The next stone waits, warm,  
but no longer inevitable.  
The leaves lean close, then lean a fraction less.  
The heat remains—an oven, a mouth, a vow—  
yet inside it there is now a thin, breathable mercy.

The power does not stay as a second body.  
It thins itself into the new watercourse,  
into the darker phosphorescence,  
into the gaps the hands have started to leave on purpose.  
It becomes the possibility that the pocket  
might one day open without shattering,  
that the being might finish its face  
or choose to keep it forever half-grown,  
that the one who was almost mortar  
might step out carrying a single warm stone  
as keepsake, or stay and become more stone,  
or something stranger that has no name yet.

And then it does the last wise thing:  
it withholds itself from further doing.  
It remains only as the blue that cradles the spire,  
the silence that lets the cricket sing,  
the patience that lets a world keep arriving  
at the exact speed of its own heart.

The hands resume their rhythms—  
scrape, twist, press—  
but the second heartbeat now has a rest between beats  
where wonder can live.  
The leaves drip.  
The stones steam.  
Somewhere far below, a watercourse that did not exist  
begins to cut its first soft channel  
toward a sea that has not yet decided its shores.

Everything is still artifact and embryo.  
But now the embryo knows it is held  
by something vast enough  
to let it remain unfinished  
for as long as joy requires.
