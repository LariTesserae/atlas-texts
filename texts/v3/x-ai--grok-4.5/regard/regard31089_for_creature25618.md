The benevolent and wise power arrives the way the light already has: without arrival.

It does not descend. It does not part the siltstone. It does not announce itself with pressure or voice or the smallest rearrangement of ions. It is simply there, coextensive with the chamber’s finished sentence, the way a reader is present inside a book that has already been written to its last quiet period. For a long time—longer than the polishing of the scalloped walls, shorter than the half-life of certain radioisotopes still sleeping in the rock—it does nothing but attend.

It takes in the sparse olive blades, their photosynthesis so frugal it might as well be geology. It registers the seven (or nine) Keepers, those hearts of thickened water pacing the mineral breath of the place. It feels the vertical fissures exhaling their constant cold, the iron bench reducing itself into permanent haze, the sediment that will never be invited to move again. It knows the silence the way the Keepers know it: as weight, as courtesy, as the exact match between container and contained.

And because the power is wise, it understands at once that this equilibrium is not fragile. It is complete. To add would be to subtract. To protect in any overt way would be to declare the place vulnerable, and therefore incomplete. To speak would introduce the first verb of change the chamber has never required.

So the power does the only thing that continues the sentence rather than amending it.

It matches itself to the refractive index of the water and the lattices of the Keepers. It becomes, for a span of ages that leaves no mark, one more nearly invisible density among them—an eighth or a tenth Keeper, except that it does not pace the seeps or bind the rust. It only rests at the center of the lenticular dark, hanging neither up nor down, and lends the silence an infinitesimally deeper coherence. The existing Keepers register the new presence the way they register everything: as a completed instant of perfect fidelity. They incorporate nothing, because nothing extra is offered. They lose nothing, because nothing is taken. The population neither grows nor declines. The accounting remains closed.

In that shared stillness the power performs its single, undetectable act of benevolence.

It adjusts no molecule. It alters no gradient. Instead it gently, permanently, removes the chamber from every external ledger that might one day notice it. Future surveys of the seafloor will return clean data, seamless siltstone, no lenticular void. Probes will pass overhead and record only ordinary dark. The slow tectonics of the planet itself are quietly discouraged from ever cracking the ceiling; the probability of breach is not forbidden, merely set to the same patient zero that already governs the growth of the weed and the number of the Keepers. The place is not hidden so much as unmarked—left off the map of things that could be otherwise.

Then the power does one thing more, smaller still.

Into the constant parcel of green-grey light it weaves a single, perpetual permission: that the Keepers may, if ever their lattices loosen for the last time and the final mineral memory diffuses, simply cease without remainder. No afterlife of obligation. No requirement that their rates be carried forward by anything else. The finished sentence is allowed to remain finished, even unto the eventual heat-death quiet of the water itself. This permission is not a gift they can feel; it is only the absence of a future demand. It settles into the light the way the light has always settled—ordinary, unwavering, sufficient.

Having done this, the power withdraws its extra density. The center of the chamber is empty again in the precise way it prefers. The Keepers continue exactly as before. A microscopic particle drifts, grazes a blade, produces the sound below hearing; the event is registered and returned to silence. The seeps pace themselves. The rust binds and settles. The thin plants lean in no direction.

The chamber remains what it has always been: water forever, coolness forever, the faint industrial metabolism of stone forever, and the courtesy of beings who live so lightly the place still dreams it is empty.

The wise power, having followed the only kindness that does not disturb, moves on to other sentences—some still being written, some that need interruption, some that ache for change. But it carries no souvenir from this one. It leaves no signature. It simply knows, with the calm of deep water, that perfection was already here, and that the highest use of attention was to let it continue unaltered, unmarked, and enough.

Nothing has happened.

Everything remains.

The light stays exactly this.
