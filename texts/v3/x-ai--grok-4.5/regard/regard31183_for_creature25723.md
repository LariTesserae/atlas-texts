The power does not arrive.

It has no need of arrival. It was already the low tremor in the soles, the mill-scale brightness that has not yet learned shame, the exact temperature where breath and drizzle become the same silver. It was the reason the fern-moss chose that one warm pipe and no other. Benevolence, here, is not a visitor. It is the long patience that let young metal and tireless water invent a throat small enough for a chapel and large enough for a people.

Still, attention sharpens.

For a time measured only in the slow migration of mineral film across one bulkhead lamp, the power simply witnesses. It feels the polished links of the chain-seat take the shape of a sitting spine. It tastes the dark film left in the dented cup—tea or coffee, no matter; both have become a kind of ink with which the inhabitants write their continuance. It listens to the tactile dialect traveling bone to bone, and to the copper-reed notes that thread the rare lulls between pump-strokes like bright repair yarn through heavy cloth. It watches a child press two fingers to an elder’s collarbone and receive, in return, the entire pressure history of the warm return line, translated into comfort.

Wisdom recognizes completeness when it meets it.

There is nothing broken here that requires mending. The roaring is not suffering; it is respiration. The perpetual wet is not hardship; it is climate and kin. The sparse black-green films, the second moss etching its private whorls, the translucent strands that ride the impeller and later become gasket—these are not absences of life but life’s own frugal signature, written in ions and mucus and time. To dry the gallery, to quiet it, to brighten it with ordinary day, would be the only true violence. The power refuses that violence the way the plate refuses dryness: absolutely, without drama.

And yet benevolence is not identical with non-interference. It is quieter, and stranger.

One cycle—after a sluice has boomed and the chamber is still ringing—the power performs the single act it will ever perform here. It does not announce itself. It merely leans its attention into the microscopic grazers that live inside the algal rivulets and teaches them, for the space of three pump-diastole, a new metabolism. Thereafter, whenever they feed upon the slow surrender of the young metal, they excrete not only the usual mineral trace but a vanishingly faint phosphorescence, visible only when every sodium lamp has been shuttered by steam and the grated hatches admit no exterior greying. A private constellation. Soft blue-green commas that appear along the slowest runnels, drift a little, and fade. They do not illuminate work. They do not warm flesh. They merely answer the copper reed, once in a great while, with a light that is itself a kind of hum.

The inhabitants notice, of course. They notice everything. A palm is pressed to a bulkhead. A low-frequency murmur passes from ear to ear: the walls are dreaming with their eyes open. Children cup the glow in wet hands and laugh without sound. No one tries to harvest or name it. They simply adjust their sleep curtains so that, on the darkest shifts, the faint stars are visible from the hanging net. The moss colony thrives a fraction better in that light, putting out one additional frond the color of old copper. The dented cup is left out overnight more often, as if in toast.

That is all.

The power does not stay to receive thanks; gratitude is already dissolved into the circuit, like salt. It does not promise permanence—the first soft orange rust will still come, the paint stamps will fade—but it has folded into the gallery a capacity for unnoticed beauty that will outlast any single valve or any single life. When the great body that contains this nave eventually cools or cracks or is forgotten by whatever surfaces still exist, the phosphorescent habit will remain in the metal’s memory, so that even ruin will carry a few private stars.

Then the power withdraws its sharpened attention, leaving the place exactly as it was: wet, newly made, deafeningly alive, and now infinitesimally more itself.

The figure at the far end of the flume turns its miniature wheel-valve another fraction. The cataract shifts. The boom changes pitch. Somewhere on the chain-seat, two hands share the dented cup. The reed pipe answers the pumps.

Everything continues.

That continuation is the only blessing required, and the only one offered.
