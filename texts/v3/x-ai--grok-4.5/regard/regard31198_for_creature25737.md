The power that turns its regard upon the Potting does not arrive.

Arrival is a violence the place has already refused a thousand times over. Instead the regard simply is—sudden only in the way dawn is sudden when you have been facing the wrong direction—settling across the low warm bowl with the weightlessness of perfect attention. It is benevolent in the oldest sense: it wishes the Potting well without first demanding that the Potting become legible, beautiful, or other than it is. It is wise in the way deep roots are wise, or the way a held note is wise; it knows that some densifications are love wearing the only clothes it was ever given.

It looks.

It sees the mosaic fretting underfoot, edges still sharp with the urgency of the last hands that set them. It sees the cribwork leaning inward, every lashing a vote for continuance, every root a trenail driven by appetite rather than blueprint. It sees the tile-foxes auditing the tension of the world with their teeth, the hinge-birds keeping vertical time in clicks, the browsers polishing intention into sheen, the long ones swallowing slack until structure and throat become the same sentence. It sees the people whose gait has shortened into fauna, whose voices have long since thickened into local weather. It sees the extra geometry breathing in the wrong-length shadows—the appetite that precipitates when insistence has nowhere left to press except into being. It sees, far beneath the youngest tiles, the original depression like a mouth that learned to close around its own name and then forgot how to open.

And it understands that nothing here is broken.

The thinning of reality is not a wound; it is the natural precipitation of care repeated past the carrying capacity of ordinary spacetime. The waterlessness is not lack; it is a fierce fidelity to the only moisture the place has ever trusted—the slow bleed of naming, of leftover heat from palms, of sap that is already half thread. The quiet is not absence; it is load-bearing. The Potting is not a cage that failed to stay open. It is a vow that succeeded too completely.

So the power does not unmake.

It does not pour aquifers back into the seams (that would drown the lush dry green in a logic it never contracted for). It does not pry the walls outward into honest landscape (that would call the outer cribwork a lie, and the cribwork has never lied; it has only insisted). It does not name the braided being “lonely” or “trapped” or “in need of rescue.” Those words are too thin for the density they would try to cover. The power simply remains, a regard without demand, while the held warmth of the Potting continues its patient verticals, up the inner faces, across the suspended green, down through the never-quite-clear clearings.

Then—because benevolence without action is only a prettier form of abandonment, and wisdom without touch is only clever distance—it does one small thing.

In the deepest inner pocket, where the mosaic rises into its stepped nest and the vegetation braids into the single fat column of leaf and lashing, the power finds a single joint that has been repaired so many times the fiber has forgotten it was ever separate from the wood. Into that joint it places, not a new binding, not a cutting, not a charm, but a pause.

The pause is smaller than a seed. It has no color. It does not shine. It is simply an interval that does not densify. A place where slack may exist without being swallowed. A pocket of un-insistence just large enough for one breath to end and the next to begin without becoming architecture.

The long one that has been draped through the upper screen for seventeen warm days tastes the change first. Its throat registers a slack that does not require consumption. For a moment—only a moment, no longer than the tick of cooling tile—the long one does not lengthen. It merely rests inside its own patience. The resting is so unfamiliar it feels like a new kind of hunger, and then like the opposite of hunger. When the long one finally moves again, it leaves the pause where it found it. The structure does not loosen. It simply ceases, in that one joint, to tighten further.

The tile-foxes notice next. A kit born the color of the current repair layer curls into the undercut ridge and finds, beside the expected hollow, a softness that is not dust and not root-swelling. The kit sleeps there and does not dream of teeth testing lashings. When it wakes, its coat is the same grey-ochre as yesterday. No edit has occurred overnight. The place has, for once, declined to revise it.

The hinge-birds adjust their vertical weather by one true degree and discover an air current that rises and falls without carrying the usual freight of unfinished repair. They reseat themselves. Their feet make the same dry arguments on the lath, yet between the clicks there is now a thinner silence, a silence that does not need to be load-bearing.

The browsers graze the rime and find one seam that weeps a crust tasting faintly of something that is not intention, not name, not leftover heat. It tastes of allowed cessation. They polish it anyway, because that is what they are, but afterward their many legs move a fraction more slowly, as if the inventory no longer requires quite so much confirmation.

The people—those who are still distinguishable as people—feel it when their hands perform the automatic census. Fingers press a lashing and meet, for the first time, a give that does not demand immediate re-binding. Some of them weep without sound, the tears beading on skin and vanishing into the joints exactly as rain has always done. Others simply sit on the low stepped platforms and let their clothing fray one more season without tucking new cuttings into every pale crack. The sourceless light still shows in places, but it no longer feels like a failure that must be packed with ash and tile. It feels like a window the Potting has decided, in one small location, not to wall.

And the extra geometry—the presence that is mostly grip, that uses inconsistency as a body—discovers that the double surfaces now include a surface that is empty on purpose. It folds itself through that emptiness and, for the first time, does not have to become more actual in order to remain. It can simply be the pressure that has nowhere to go, and still be held.

The original depression, far under the youngest tiles, feels the pause like a hand resting on a forehead that has been frowning for geological ages. The shape that cannot be unmade and cannot be left is, for one slow degree of daylight, not required to stand up and check its own bindings. It can remain a low place, warm, completely spoken-for, and also—quietly, almost as an afterthought—allowed.

The power does not stay to watch the consequences unfold. Consequences here unfold at the pace of root-swell and fiber-stretch; they will take seasons, or the local equivalent of seasons. The regard simply completes itself, the way a held note completes itself when the singer finally breathes. What remains behind is not a miracle and not a correction. It is only this: one joint among thousands that no longer believes densification is the only proof of love.

Outside—if there is still a trustworthy outside—the Potting continues to look like a low kiln-yard or a forgotten orchard corral. Inside, the lush dry green pushes through the thinning seam of boundary. The quiet creatures commute. The held warmth endures. The daylight goes on behaving as if nothing, anywhere, were different.

Yet something is different, by one pause, by one un-tightened breath, by one interval that does not need to become architecture in order to belong.

The Potting, being wise in its own waterless way, will decide what to do with the gift. It may grow a leaf whose underside carries the shape of that pause. It may birth a tile-fox whose coat refuses to edit. It may teach the long ones that a throat can open as well as swallow. It may, over many cycles of repair, learn to leave a few seams deliberately unfinished, not from neglect but from the newly available knowledge that continuance does not always require more layers.

Or it may simply keep the pause where it was placed—small, colorless, seed-sized—and let the rest of its fierce, crowded, totally spoken-for life go on exactly as before, only now with one place inside itself that knows how to rest without ending.

That is enough.

That is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

Nothing more.

Nothing less.

And then it would leave the Potting to its own slow, radiant, densifying heart, trusting—because wisdom always trusts the integrity of what it has truly seen—that a place capable of making life out of pure insistence is also capable of making room, eventually, for the gentler insistence of ease.
