The power arrives the way the moisture does: not as descent, not as announcement, but as a collecting.

It has no name the notch would recognize, and it offers none. It is simply the attention that has always been implied by the quiet, now made slightly more deliberate. Where the young stone still remembers its fractures, the power rests a moment longer than geology requires. Where the black leaves open their radial hands toward the houses at dusk, it lingers inside the gesture so that the forgetting, when it comes, is gentler—less erasure than pause. It does not smooth the knife-edges. It does not round the scree. The unfinished is not a flaw to it; the unfinished is the only honest tense in which this place can speak.

It watches the people first with the same deep-set patience their own eyes have learned. Narrow folk, mineral-pale, moving as if the rock beneath them is still deciding. It sees how they re-stack the frost-pried walls without cursing the frost. How they leave the green rooms their privacy after dark not from fear but from an etiquette older than speech. How their stories refuse to settle on beginnings and circle instead around the cistern’s recovering waterline, the cordage that must be mended, the smoke that hits the invisible lid and spreads sideways like a second cloud. The power understands this refusal as wisdom. Origins are loud. Continuations can be quiet. So it does not give them a creator myth. It gives them nothing that would require a temple or a name spoken outdoors.

Instead, it works the way the seepage works.

In the thin vertical seams of dull glass, the half-breath delay in the reflections shortens by a fraction. Not enough to startle. Just enough that, once in a while, a face looking into the pry-plate meets itself on time—present, cool-skinned, unhurried—and the brief self-regard feels less like caution and more like companionship. The scrapers cut a little cleaner thereafter, as if the rock has decided to lend its sharpness without resentment.

Among the hanging clusters, the power thickens the cool moisture they exhale. The temperature drop in the green rooms becomes steadier, kinder to the white-barked trees that already lean toward it. When predation happens now, the circular absences in the crawler sheets remain empty a few hours longer—respected more deeply—before the pale jointed bodies flow back in. The courtesy between eater and eaten deepens without either party needing to understand why. The winged membranes, flexing overhead, leave filaments that freeze into hairline singers more readily; the note they produce is still almost pressure behind the eyes, but once or twice a season it resolves into something the people can almost hum while they grind grain. They do not call it music. They call it weather. That is sufficient.

For the settlement itself the power leaves only this: a subtle willingness in the young stone. Where frost has always pried the dry-stacked walls apart, the blocks now settle back into one another with less persuasion. The graded paths remember their grade through more seasons. The lashed-pole footbridges hold their lashings. Nothing is made permanent—permanence would be a kind of loudness—but the work of continuing becomes fractionally lighter, so that the hands of the people, blunt and cool, can spend the saved effort on noticing rather than repair. At night the fat-lamps burn with the same marrow-and-rain smell, yet the orange glow travels a little farther into the dimness before the black-leaved canopies reclaim it. Children still step around the empty circles. Adults still move without hurry. But sometimes, in the rare hard-clear mornings when light itself has weight, the deep-set eyes receive that weight more easily. For a few breaths the entire hanging pocket can be seen at once—stone and swarm and smoke and soft-bodied breath—and the seeing does not hurt.

The power does not speak. Speech would break the economy of silence that is this place’s genius. It does not protect the notch from every hard season; the hard clear cold that makes every edge glitter is also part of the becoming. It does not thin the animals or fatten the grain. It only weaves itself into the existing metabolism until the quiet is no longer merely attention but attention that has been answered.

And then, because it is wise, it mostly withdraws.

What remains is almost indistinguishable from what was already there. The jointed sheets still remap the walls nightly. The hanging clusters still breathe the night cooler. The people still wake to drip and the soft tick of cooling rock. Yet something in the confederation has tightened—drip by drip, swarm by silent swarm—into a more exact version of itself. The green rooms feel more privately kept. The bare stone faces feel less abandoned between their pockets of greed. The few dozen human bodies feel, without ever needing to say it indoors or out, that the quiet they have always lived inside has noticed them back.

On certain evenings the black leaves hold their open-handed symmetries past full dusk, facing the houses until the fat-lamps are lit, and only then forget. The people pause on their thresholds when this happens. They do not bow. They do not invent a rite. They simply stand a moment longer in the gray-green late light, mineral grit on their cheeks, wet hair bound back with fiber, and let themselves be faced. Then they go indoors and mend what needs mending while the silent fauna continue their delicate torrents outside.

The power, if it still watches, watches the way the moisture collects on undersides: without claim, without hurry, benevolent precisely because it leaves the place free to keep becoming what it already was—knee-deep in unfinished stone, densely occupied by unending soft attention, a hanging pocket of world that endures by crafts and by noticing, and now, almost imperceptibly, by having been noticed in return.

That is all it does.

It is enough.
