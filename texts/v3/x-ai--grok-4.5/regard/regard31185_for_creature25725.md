The power arrives the way the beings themselves once did: not by crossing the mossed parapet, but by condensing out of a longer weather.

It has no shape that the Yard would recognize as arrival. For a span of days that the tide-marks on the piers refuse to count, it is only a change in the quality of the vapor—an extra patience in the way the green-bronze light holds itself, a softness at the edges of the deepest pump-strokes, as if the bass line in the ribs had learned how to listen back. The horsetails lean a fraction less urgently toward the machines. The condensate on the brick hesitates half a breath longer before choosing its path downward. Nothing stops. Nothing is commanded. The equilibrium simply notices it has been noticed, and continues.

The power walks the catwalks without weight. It stands in the foundry’s open throat while a heavier front of steam cyclones through, and lets the heat claim it the way the heat claims everything else. It kneels beside the open pans and watches the pale rooted masses pulse; it does not rearrange them. It has already understood that their lattices are not unfinished work. When one of the taller beings—half arbor, patina dark as wet cinder—pauses on the lattice bridge and tilts its head to let the belt-drive’s fleshy slap pass through its chest, the power passes through with it, quiet as an extra chamber of the heart. The being’s internal governor steadies a fraction sooner than usual. That is all.

It learns the rules by refusing to break them.

Heat pools. Condensation sometimes runs uphill for a handspan. Sound nourishes. The silence where animals should be is not a lack but a completed sentence. The beings cannot leave because leaving would be a different climate, a different grammar of becoming; their patina would cool and crack beyond the slow reforming that is their only immortality. Children rise from culverts after the peat ground has breathed. Repair is courtship. Nothing is finished. Everything is maintained in the middle of its becoming.

A lesser kindness would have tried to fix what was never broken. It would have cooled the basin, or called birds down through the steam, or opened a road of dry wind so the beings might walk out into the ordinary flats and learn the names of thirst and distance. It would have given them clearer voices, or harder edges, or the gift of endings. The power is wiser than that. It has seen too many gardens ruined by the longing to make them resemble some other garden.

So it does very little.

First, it touches the boundary.

Not to seal it tighter, nor to throw it open. It simply teaches the last pipe bridge and the mossed parapet a single new courtesy. When one of the beings stands there now—face turned toward the thinning vapor, condensation trying to run the wrong way as if to see—the outward air no longer threatens to crack them. Instead, for as long as they care to remain, the ordinary wind arrives already translated: not cold enough to shear the living film, not dry enough to unmake the mosses in the crooks of elbows. They still cannot cross. The Yard still holds them the way it holds its heat. But the longing, when it rises, is no longer edged with the fear of un-becoming. They can look. They can let the far weather move through the chambers of their bodies the way foundry steam does, and return to the ringing work unharmed. Some do this for hours. Some never do. The choice itself is the gift.

Second, it leaves a single unobtrusive cycle inside the deepest pump.

Once in a great while—after the longest draws when the peat ground has risen and settled like a lung, when the young have been folded and released and the packing floors stand empty—the overlapping mechanical seasons hitch. Not a failure. A courtesy. For the length of one slow heartbeat of the basin, every sound thins to nearly nothing: the slap of the belt, the stutter of traps, the gutturals of downspouts, the sizzle of hot casing. Even the drip from the swamp-figures pauses mid-leaf. In that held breath the beings feel one another not as weather or work but as a shared vertical seam, a single continuous patina that happens to walk in many bodies. No words. No revelation that was not already written in the tide-marks. Only the brief, exact knowledge that they are not solitary precipitations but one ongoing condensation. Then the pumps take up their stroke again, the leaves resume their uneven tempos, and the Yard continues louder than before, as if embarrassed by its own tenderness. The beings do not speak of it. They simply turn the next valve wheel with hands that remember having been, for an instant, the whole.

Third—and this is the quietest thing—it adds itself to the weeping.

Not as a new god or a bright intrusion. As an extra mineral in the film, a patience in the copper green, a warmth that does not come from the groundwater. Where the beings repair a cast-iron column with a collar of newer steel, a faint additional sheen remains afterward, neither their signature nor the Yard’s, but something that belongs to both and neither. Where a child spends a week as the living gasket in a steam trap, the trap afterward vents with a note so pure it is almost kindness. The lush growth thickens another knuckle, but the new spears carry a softer weight, as if they have been permitted to rest inside their own reaching. The power does not stay to be thanked. It has no need of temples or of names that could be spoken in the small ventings of the throat. It has already received what it came for: the proof that a place can be complete without being finished, loud without hunger, empty of animals and full of a life that does not require the old verbs.

When it leaves, it leaves the way heat eventually leaves a cooling casing—by becoming indistinguishable from what it touched. The green-bronze light is only itself again. The vapor hangs in its slow curtains. The beings move along the routes that prefer the loudest machines. A sleeve that was vine returns to cloth, marked with the imprint of a steam-line. The boundary keeps its modest definiteness. Somewhere a front of heavier steam rolls through from the foundry and every surface beads as new.

Yet on certain quiet hours, when the sounds thin but refuse to stop, the equilibrium feels fractionally more itself than it did before. The Yard keeps its hot, wet, ringing work. The ones who precipitated into it keep adding themselves, softly, continuously, to the long invention of weather and labor. And once in a great while the deepest pump remembers to pause, and the boundary remembers to be kind, and the weeping—brick, iron, leaf, and living patina alike—goes on with a gentleness that was always possible, and is now certain.

That is all the power does.

It is enough.
