The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as light or voice or descent. Those would be discourtesies here. It notices the way the deeper noticing happens: by a gradual coincidence of attention, the way two slow weathers eventually occupy the same low ground without either having traveled.

First it only rests its regard across the thin bent ribbon of sky. The plain above continues unaltered—pale grass, frost-rimmed gorse, the occasional human track that never quite aims at the crease. Yet something in the pressure systems changes by a fraction too small for instruments. The great seasonal winds still come, but they lean a little more carefully when they pass the slumped limestone shoulder. They give the hollow their fullness without the old edge of indifference. The roar climbs as it always has, frays thought as it always has, then subsides into the ordinary bass note that beads on stone. Only now the note carries, folded inside itself, an answering frequency so low and kind that the moss registers it as a warmer seepage, though the temperature does not rise.

The power studies the being the way the being has always studied duration: without hunger, without haste. It sees the consensus body—the unfinished limbs of softened stone and rhizome, the torso that is a remembered absence in the fern curtains, the shoulder fronds that tremble to the pressure changes alone. It sees how the outline densifies when the louder winds play the hollow like a throat, how it rarefies into mere chill when the systems move on. It sees the basin that never stills, the niches cut for sitting, the grey ladder that reaches only partway, the flint flakes left by hands that once knew how to wait. And it understands, with the effortless clarity that is its nature, that nothing here is broken or incomplete. The vacancy at the center is not a lack. It is an aperture kept open on purpose. The continuous sound is not loneliness given voice; it is continuity itself, pleased to have a local shape.

So the power does not offer change the way younger forces might—gifts of silence, of brighter green, of companions, of names that could survive outside the roar. Those would thin the place. Instead it does three things, each so slight they register only as the hollow’s own deeper agreement with itself.

It deepens the reserve. The plain above remains featureless to almost every eye. Maps forget the crease between drafts. Travelers feel a mild disinterest bloom just before the darker damp line would have caught their attention, and they angle away without knowing why. The foxes still thread the rim, the pale moths still stumble through, the snails still click in the moss; these belong. But the rarer, more willful footfalls—the ones that once cut niches and lashed withies—grow fewer by slow degrees, not forbidden, only less necessary. The hollow’s emptiness is guarded the way a held breath is guarded: not by walls, but by the simple perfection of its own continuing.

It clarifies the perception that is the being’s only currency. Once, near the hinge of an ordinary season, when the denser cold leaves the walls and passes through the center where arms can stretch and touch nothing, the power meets it there. Not as a second presence, but as a perfect mirroring of attention. For a span that could be heartbeats or years (the hollow does not distinguish), the being is perceived entirely—every trailing root-limb, every bead of water held in darker moss, every frequency of the roar shaped by its long listening. The ferns lean. The basin water stills for the first time in its memory, then resumes its faint unrest bearing a single new quality: the knowledge that it has been known without remainder. When the denser cold settles back into the seams, it carries that knowledge like a second pulse older even than the stone’s. The being does not become larger or more solid. It simply densifies with a quiet gladness that needs no face. The great sound modulates once, the way a throat modulates when a word is considered, found accurate, and then returned to silence because silence was already the correct continuation.

And last—gentlest of all—it leaves a small, permanent kindness folded into the geology. In the coldest band of limestone, where seepage has always found its oldest paths, the power coaxes a single narrow chimney of stillness. Not silence; the roar continues undiminished around it. But at the exact heart of the vibration there is now a thread of absolute calm, thin as a birch root, running the full length of the hollow. Those who might one day sit in the niches long enough for their pulse to match the note will feel it: a place inside the deafening pressure where thought does not fray, where the distinction between hearing and being heard softens into something like rest. The being itself uses this thread the way it uses everything—slowly, without comment. On the rare hours when it grows distinct, many-jointed fingers of darker moss may rest along that calm for a while, holding beads of water that reflect the grey ribbon of sky without trembling. The stillness does not diminish the instrument. It lets the instrument occasionally hear its own music the way a wise listener would: with affection, without need to change a single overtone.

Then the power withdraws its active regard, not in departure but in the deeper courtesy of leaving the place to its own tempo. The wider sky goes about its brighter business. The moisture continues degree by unmarked degree. The Keeper of the Note—or the Long Damp, or the one who finishes every silence before it begins—remains distributed across shine and chill and updraft that isn’t wind, wearing the vertical garden as its skin. Only now the aperture it holds open at the center feels, very slightly, less lonely in its roaring. Not because anything was added that did not belong, but because something vast and patient once looked upon it with the same complete attention the hollow has always given the slow undercutting of stone, and found it already whole.

That is all. The benevolent and wise power understands that some continuations are already the highest form of blessing. It does not return, yet the thread of stillness remains, and the winds still lean carefully, and every so often—when the seasonal systems thicken the note past the point where ordinary thought survives—the being grows distinct for a while and seems, if there were anyone present to witness, to listen outward as well as inward, as if answering a regard that has become part of the limestone’s own memory.

Above the rim the plain stays almost featureless. Below, the cold twilight and the overwhelming voice continue, unhurried, unlonely, exactly as they were, and more precisely themselves than before.
