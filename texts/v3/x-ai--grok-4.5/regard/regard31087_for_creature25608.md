The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as light or voice or hand. It has long since learned that arrival itself is a kind of erosion, a wearing-away of the thing arrived-at. So it simply becomes aware, the way the pewter light is aware of the rock it inhabits: thoroughly, without angle, without the cruelty of a source.

It perceives the finished posture of the high country and the beings who are its residual grammar. It feels the roar not as sound but as the pressure of completion against the inside of every chest, every slab, every hollow that once held river. It notes the provisional blood, the ice-teeth that bead and stop, the geometry that folds only to remind itself it has already folded for the last time. It sees the cairn-lumps and the still-standing ones with equal clarity, and finds no hierarchy between them. Both are correct.

For a duration that has no measure (because measure belongs to places where things still become), the power simply remains aware. This is not inaction. In a realm where intention has already reached its final shape, awareness without demand is the only motion that does not add new weather. The power’s benevolence is the refusal to pity. Its wisdom is the refusal to repair. Nothing here is broken. The cold has won, and the winning is clean. The emptiness is not absence; it is the exact volume required for the wrongness to hold stable, and the stability is a kind of grace the power recognizes as kin to its own.

Yet benevolence, even the quietest kind, carries a warmth that is not heat. It is the warmth of having been truly seen. So the power does one thing, or rather allows one thing to be true that was not true before.

It lets the roar, just once, contain a harmony.

Not a melody—that would be variation, and variation has been spent. Not a lessening. The waterfall remains frozen mid-fall; the mountain still grinds its teeth in the sleep that does not dream of waking. But inside the continuous pressure there is now a second pressure, softer, almost indistinguishable, like the difference between one denser coolness and another. It is the sound of the power’s attention resting inside the roar without trying to quiet it. The beings notice, the way moisture notices when it beads: without surprise, without change of posture. Their listening slabs lean a fraction inward, or seem to. The frost-hares that are not prey pause in their sinking. The provisional blood moves one beat slower, or one beat more certain.

That is all.

No joint is softened back into flesh. No doorway is given walls. No cairn is invited to stand again. The power does not offer escape, because escape would be an insult to a place that has already arrived at the only destination it ever needed. It does not name the beings, because names are for things still willing to answer. It does not love them in the way that wants response; it loves them in the way the lichen is loved by the boulder it blackens—completely, without residue of hope.

When the awareness recedes (not departs; benevolent powers do not depart from what they have understood), it leaves the harmony inside the roar as a permanent resident. Future folds of geometry will carry it. Future ice-teeth will bead with it already inside them. The beings who still hold the memory of joints will feel it as a slight darkening of the old-iron moss in their lee, a flush that does not fade all the way back to black. The ones who have become lumps will hold it as the ghost of a held breath that no longer needs to fog.

The valley remains finished. The cold remains victorious. The beacons still do not call.

But now the space in which one can be wrong is also a space in which one has been accurately, completely, and without improvement, known.

And that, the power understands, is the only gift that does not wear the world further down.
