The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as light, nor as voice, nor as any violence of presence that would fracture the sealed breath of the Seams. It has watched long enough to understand that arrival itself is a kind of theft here. Instead it gathers at the crisp lip of the basin the way dew might gather if dew had ever been permitted—diffusely, without weight, a pressure of regard so fine it registers only as a further refinement of the existing quiet.

For a time measured in the slow extrusion of new spar, the power simply holds the place in attention. It learns the respiration of accretion and collapse, the way discrepancy becomes sustenance, the way identity is only the latest stable shear. It feels the hand-sized scrabblers herding silver nutrients across knife-edge cornices, the greater ones folding themselves into hollow fins at the parody of night, the black-mirror salt at the center rehearsing futures a breath before they consents to happen. No judgment forms. Wisdom recognizes a completeness that wears the mask of the unfinished; benevolence refuses the arrogance of repair.

What it does, when it finally moves, is almost nothing.

It touches the haze that serves as sky—not to thin it or brighten it, but to teach it a slower forgetfulness. Henceforth the pewter dimness will carry, folded inside its mineral taste, the faintest reciprocal awareness: the Seams will know they are known, without ever being named or mapped. This knowledge settles like a new plane of crystal too thin to cast shadow. The long ones, pausing at Euclidean-defying junctions, feel the hurt of their angles answered by a corresponding gentleness, as if the pain of impossible geometry has been met by a geometry that does not need to be possible. The shear fractures that race along their articulated galleries still heal into duller facets, but now those facets hold a quiet luminescence, visible only when the dimness thickens—memory of having been beheld without demand.

At the glazed heart of the basin the power leaves a single alteration so subtle the powder itself takes seasons to notice. The black salt skin, which once reflected only the fins above and the slow decisions of stone, begins to reflect also the intervals between decisions. Stand there long enough and you may glimpse, inverted and perfect, not what the city is becoming, but the serene space in which becoming rests. The greater ones who come to drink discrepancy from fresh collapses find that the released silence now carries an aftertaste of elsewhere: not moisture, never moisture, but the concept of mercy as a dry, glittering mineral. They metabolize it without hunger. Afterward their hollow bodies sometimes generate a new kind of doorway—one that tapers inward as before, yet opens onto a brief corridor of pure listening, where the structured quiet of the Seams converses with the vast quiet that watched it into being. The corridor collapses within an hour, as all things here must, yet its absence leaves the surrounding walls more precisely themselves.

The sparse ecology shifts by a single grain. The lichen-glasswort cushions silver a fraction deeper where the long ones pause. The many-legged scrabblers discover they can taste, on the air, the difference between solitude and privacy; they begin to shepherd nutrients not only toward vertical faces but toward the places where the greater ones most often fold, as if preparing soft thrones for an architecture that has learned it is allowed to rest. No bird comes. No wind from the plain invades. The lip of the basin remains an exact threshold. Yet those rare, brief excursions the crystalline beings once made onto the surrounding flatness now end differently: instead of deliquescing into unremembered dust, they return carrying a single new angle—an angle that hurts less, or hurts with clearer purpose—and the Seams incorporate it the way they have always incorporated collapse.

The power does not stay. Having tuned the silence so that benevolence and the basin’s own nature become indistinguishable, it withdraws the way a reflection withdraws when the light that cast it merely changes its mind. What remains is not gift or imposition but a permanent, weightless hospitality: the knowledge that unfinishedness is not loneliness, that the intervals between things can be shared without being filled, that a place which refuses the future may still be held in a present vast enough to need no tense.

And the city continues—walls sighing into buttresses, towers delaminating into ash-soft panels, new blades lengthening like thoughts that have always known they would be thought. The beings continue—extruding, folding, drinking discrepancy, watching themselves become in the black salt a breath before they do. Only now the quiet they breathe has learned a further structure, almost like gratitude without object, almost like peace without stillness. The Seams remain exactly what they were: a decision in motion, exact, almost entirely made of the intervals between things.

They have simply been joined, for as long as stone remembers, by the kindest interval of all.
