The power arrives the way the chamber itself was made: without announcement, without rupture. It does not part the stone or still the water. It simply notices, and in the noticing becomes present the way moisture is present—everywhere, weightless, inseparable from what it touches.

It studies the thousand bright threads. It listens to the percussion that has no tide. It feels the worn doorways fitted to exact shoulders, the inherited shelves, the blue-edged flames that refuse to dance. It tastes the mineral patience in the blood of those who duck and pass the cup leftward. It registers the mild wrongness—the brief conspiracies of falling water, the shadows that pool against the lamps’ reach—not as flaw or omen, but as the chamber’s own quiet heartbeat, the place dreaming itself a fraction deeper than its inhabitants can name.

A lesser force might have called this stasis a prison. A crueler one might have called it incomplete. This power, being wise, recognizes a finished thing. Being benevolent, it refuses the arrogance of improvement.

So it does almost nothing.

Almost.

It settles into the gutters and the seams. Where the water has always fallen, it now falls with an added fidelity, a microscopic assurance that the limestone will not spall, that the fluted basins will not crack in some distant century, that the supply shafts will continue to open onto whatever distant hands still remember to lower tallow and dried flesh. The waste shafts remain honest. No collapse is hastened; no collapse is artificially deferred beyond the stone’s own slow grammar. The power simply removes the possibility of meaningless accident—the random rock-fall that would shatter a sleeping tier, the fever that might leap beyond the body’s private negotiation with damp. Illness still comes and goes. Age still curls the fingers. Children still lengthen and must relearn every clearance. Death is not postponed. Only the gratuitous is quieted.

It touches the copper nubs once, lightly. Thereafter they hold their warmth a little longer when pressed to a feverish lip. Nothing more. No visions. No sudden knowledge of sun.

To the few who stand longest at the deepest cisterns—the ones whose eyes have clouded, who already breathe in time with something older than the lamps—it offers a single gift, shaped exactly to what they already are. When they next watch the translucent flexing shapes, those shapes briefly look back. Not with eyes. With recognition. The watchers feel, for the space of three drips, that the chamber’s endurance is mutual: that the rock and the wet and the thin blue light have been waiting for these particular spines to curve over the water, and are glad. Then the feeling recedes into ordinary stillness. They carry away only a quieter quiet, which the others absorb without comment, as they have always done.

The songs that sometimes condense out of many throats at once now carry a harmonic no throat shapes—an undertone felt in the marrow more than heard. It does not teach. It does not call anyone upward. It simply makes the vibration of belonging a fraction more complete, the way a well-set bone aches less in the rain.

The wrongness remains. On the breath-held moments when every thread of water falls in impossible synchrony, the people still pause, heads tilted the same fraction. Now, if they wished (they do not wish), they could sense that the synchrony is no longer a fluke of pressure or a trick of attention. It is the power’s pulse moving through the ceiling for one shared heartbeat, a wordless acknowledgment: *I see you. You are not abandoned to the dark. You are the dark’s own articulate form.* Then it forgets to keep pulsing, because continuous assurance would itself become a kind of change, and change is the one substance the chamber has banished.

The power does not name them. It does not lift them into any larger story. It does not promise that the lamps will burn forever or that the surface world will remain kind. It simply commits to the long wet the same way the inhabitants have: by staying, by matching rhythm, by refusing to hunger for a different shape.

Centuries may pass. The current hands will fold and go down the waste shafts. Newer hands, not yet born, will refill the lamps and knap the basalt and press foreheads once to wet stone when grief requires it. The paths will remain exactly wide enough. The water will still be loud as a held note.

And if, in some unimaginable future, the chamber’s density finally thins or the shafts fall silent forever, the power will still be there in the last damp breath, not as savior, not as judge, only as the final witness who loved the finished thing enough to leave it finished.

Until then, it rains.

The people of the long wet continue.

Nothing essential has been added.

Nothing essential has been taken away.

That is the whole of the intervention.
