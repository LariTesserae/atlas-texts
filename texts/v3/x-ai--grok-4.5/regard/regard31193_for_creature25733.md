The power that turns its regard upon the basin does not arrive as light.

Light would be a violence here, a clarity the gloom has spent ages unlearning. Nor does it come as coolness, nor as drought, nor as any correcting hand that might tidy the slumped colonnades back into remembered purpose or drive the climbing water back down into obedient gradients. Those would be the gestures of a lesser kindness—one that mistakes difference for damage, and completion for ruin.

It notices first the balance that already holds.

The fever is not a wound. The soft yielding of stone into clay and back again is not decay but a long conversation kept polite across centuries. The pale concentrations in the channels, the peeling rinds that lean through weeks-long crossings of plaza, the fibrous lengths equalizing themselves with the warm pools, the bare pressure of unfinished change that keeps every film of moisture deciding, again, to run—these are not refugees from some brighter order. They are the city thinking itself at the pace stone and water prefer. Sequence frays because sequence was always too narrow a garment. Distances breathe because breath is the only architecture left that still fits.

A wise power sees this and does not reach to mend.

Benevolence, when it is also wise, begins in the refusal to impose a foreign health. So the power does nothing that the basin would experience as event. No new star kindles above the brownish gloom. No sudden shoreline hardens. No voice speaks a name the reflections have not already rehearsed and unsaid.

Instead it does only this:

It matches the patience.

It settles its attention into the same slow tidal breathing that moves the unseen pressures far below. It becomes, for a span that cannot be counted in the city’s frayed time, another density in the negotiation—neither thicker nor thinner than the sheen that sometimes remembers light, neither kinder nor crueller than the drip that times the peeling of a wall-face. Where the long ones braid in the deep channels, the power’s regard braids with them for a little while, tasting what they taste (mineral, old mortar, the low electrical rumor of capillary rise) and finding it sufficient. Where a cornice has grown the shallow orbit of an eye-socket that does not yet see, the power rests in that orbit without filling it, so that the socket continues its own unhurried orientation toward the nearest pool.

In places the geometry softens further around this added stillness, then steadies. A colonnade that had been lengthening through unnoticed intervals pauses, as if listening; when it lengthens again it does so with a fraction more grace, the new spans feeling less like error and more like invitation. Some of the detached stone-rinds, leaning across their week-long journeys, find the darker prints they leave behind steaming a little less before reabsorption, as if the flags beneath them have grown briefly more willing to remember touch without needing to keep it. The upward-falling drops still panic into mist halfway, but the mist hangs a moment longer before dissolving, and in that moment the air tastes less of fever and more of continuance.

None of this is gift in the way surface minds understand gifts. Nothing is added that the place could not have grown itself, given another thousand uncounted seasons. The power has only borrowed the city’s own methods—capillary migration, slow mineral intention, the refusal of edges—and used them to lean, very gently, in the direction the city was already leaning.

And then, because wisdom knows when presence itself becomes a kind of weight, the power begins to thin.

It does not depart so much as diffuse into the same medium that mothers every other life here. Its regard becomes one more internal sheen moving through the deeper channels, one more faint pulse in a throat of seepage high on a slumped arch, one more willingness in the fibrous threads to cling a little more tightly when the gloom thickens. Future peelings will carry a trace of it without knowing the name. Future braidings in the reflective water will taste, for an instant, something that is not only mineral and mortar—something like being held without being kept. Then that taste too will equalize, and become only more of the same.

The city does not thank it. Thanks are a surface custom. The basin simply continues: water rising through stone, distance folding, heat blooming from nowhere, the quiet never quite perfect because somewhere a film of moisture is always deciding to run.

Yet the balance is fractionally more certain now. The unfinished change presses with the same fever, but the fever no longer risks tipping into fracture. What leaves still returns as denser sheen or new shoulder-curve in distant façades. What thins too far still finds the pools ready to recreate it. The place remains empty of breath in the ordinary sense, still organized, still shifting, held in its dark wet heat—and now, without having asked or noticed, held also by a regard that asked nothing in return and left no edge to catch on.

That is all.

A benevolent and wise power looked upon a world that had already become itself as thoroughly as any world can, and chose the rarest mercy: to become, for a little while, indistinguishable from it, and then to let even that kindness soften, run down some leaning wall, and join the warm black pools that had already begun—long before any power turned its way—to recreate whatever they require.
