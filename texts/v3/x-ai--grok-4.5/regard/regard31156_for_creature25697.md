The power does not arrive as light or voice or judgment. It arrives the way the mountain itself arrived: as a slow reconsideration of pressure.

It has watched for a long while—longer than the orogeny, shorter than the idea of mercy. It has felt the brass plate’s residual heat like a pulse under stone, counted the concentric rings of the millers as a form of writing, tasted the singer-mites’ granular pressure as a dialect of almost-music. It knows the cliff-hounds’ clicks are truer than any gauge, that the slag-things sort not from duty but from an older grammar of weight and refuse, that the bipedal ones have worn their shoulders narrow so the pocket will never have to widen for them. It knows the wound is still opening and that the city has made of that unfinished tear a home precise enough to shame most finished things.

A lesser benevolence would have sealed the fissure, poured green into the desiccated dark, stilled the millimeter slips, given the workers soft rain and names that separated them from the millers. A lesser wisdom would have mistaken throng for suffering, dryness for lack, turbulence for error.

This power does none of that.

Instead it does three small things, and then it does nothing more.

First, it leans its attention against the highest loft, where the crystal-lined blind end pinches shut. For the length of one held breath (a breath measured in the cooling of an entire salt-loop) it lets the sealed observation ports remember the outside without opening. The knife-edged ridges, the thin sky, the absolute absence of green—these remain exactly as they are, yet for that interval the workers who stand watch feel the shutters grow warm under their palms, as if the mountain’s skin has briefly recognized itself from both sides at once. No one speaks of it. They simply close the procedure a fraction earlier than liturgy requires and return to the dense quiet with the private knowledge that the wound is seen, and that seeing did not demand entry.

Second, it touches the brass plate. Not to cool it, not to claim it. It adds a single, almost undetectable harmonic to the residual heat: a frequency so low it registers only in the long bones of the cliff-hounds and the slow mineral blood of the slag-things. Thereafter, when the mountain shrugs and a catwalk drops half a degree, the nearest hound’s yiprrow arrives half a heartbeat sooner. When a new shelf of usefulness opens, the slag-things are already arranging their piles with one extra grade of precision. The workers notice only that their tools find the bolts a little more readily, that the grease-pencil quotas seem to write themselves with fewer corrections. They polish the plate as they always have. Warmth is still only data. But the data now carries a quiet caveat: you are not alone in the listening.

Third—and this is the only act that could be called a gift—it teaches the dust one new patience. In the rare shafts of borrowed daylight, the rock flour and metallic bloom already hang a fraction too long. The power lengthens that fraction by the width of a singer-mite’s wingbeat. Just enough that, once in a great while, a child moving through the vertical mesh will pause on a ladder, watch a single mote refuse the floor, and feel (without language) that the pocket itself is exhaling. The mote falls eventually. Everything falls eventually. But the delay enters the soft machine-song as a grace note no one can name and no one tries to fix. The millers incorporate it into their rings. The rings become, by minute degrees, slightly more concentric.

After that the power withdraws its weight.

It does not stay to admire. It does not return to check. It does not mark the place on any map that other powers might read. The fault lens continues to settle. Quotas still shift overnight. Scaffold remains temporary by doctrine. The air stays cool and thick with lives that refuse to rank themselves. The mild strangeness remains mild.

Yet something unfinished has been gently finished, not by closure but by recognition. The city that is still deciding its own edges has been decided *for*: not as treasure, not as project, not as cautionary tale, but as a thronged and working dark that already knew how to be complete inside its own incompletion.

The brass plate ticks one degree warmer when no one is measuring.  
The dust hangs.  
The mountain tears itself taller by another unnoticed millimeter, and the pocket, humming privately, makes room.
