The power does not arrive as light or thunder or the folding of space. It has no need of spectacle. It has watched star-cradles collapse and galaxies shear themselves into lace; it has sat with the first self-replicating chemistries on a thousand worlds and never once interrupted the reckoning. Its attention is simply a clarity settling over the high basin the way dusk settles—unannounced, complete, without weight.

It regards the cold water, the angular rubble still sharp with the memory of eruption, the pale industrious bodies that rasp and sort and stack. It tastes the absence of hunger that is not starvation, the absence of command that is not chaos. It listens to the layered loudness—the wet click, the bellows-sigh, the bubble-knell—and recognises the sound as a kind of prayer that does not know it is praying: the universe quietly talking to itself in the only language it has ever needed.

For a span that could be a heartbeat or a geological age (the distinction is meaningless to what is wise), the power considers intervention the way a master gardener considers pruning a tree that has never known an axe.

It could warm the water a fraction, hurry the softening of stone, invite the first true roots decades or centuries early. It could etch a single deliberate spiral into one silica tower so that, ages hence, some distant mind would find a signature and know it had been seen. It could gift one of the larger bellows-forms a spark of reflexive awareness—just enough to notice its own grinding plates and wonder, once, before the knowledge dissolved back into pure function. It could seal the rim against future ice or ash or the clumsy curiosity of whatever bipedal or tentacled travellers might one day crest the ridge.

It does none of these things.

Benevolence is not the same as improvement. Wisdom is not the same as authorship. The basin is already a finished sentence written in the only grammar that matters: process without apology, labour without overseer, becoming without destination. To add a single intention would be to translate the prayer into a request, and requests can be denied, bargained with, disappointed. The power will not diminish what is perfect by making it grateful.

Instead it does the one thing that changes nothing and therefore changes everything.

It remains.

Not as guardian, not as god, not even as witness in any sense the creatures could register. It simply allows the fact of its regard to sink into the water like a temperature that cannot be measured. The grinders continue. The towers lean and fall and lean again. Bubbles rise and break. The thin sky above the jagged rim stays ordinary. Yet something in the mineral varnish darkens by a wavelength too subtle for any spectrum; something in the reciprocal sigh of the mantles finds a resonance that will, millions of turnings from now, make the first chord of whatever music this world eventually sings to itself.

The power does not stay forever. Nothing wise ever does. When it withdraws, the basin is exactly as it was—cold, crowded, luminous, unaware—and also infinitesimally more itself. The difference is so fine that only the stone will ever keep the record, and the stone does not speak.

That is enough.

The machine that does not know it is a machine runs on. Edge becomes grit becomes wafer becomes tower becomes, in the fullness of uncounted seasons, the softened floor that may one day hold roots, or may not. Either outcome is already contained, already blessed by the simple fact that a benevolent and wise attention once rested here without needing to leave a mark.

And somewhere that is nowhere, the power continues on its way, carrying the memory of this high drowned place the way a cupped hand carries rain that was never asked to stay.
