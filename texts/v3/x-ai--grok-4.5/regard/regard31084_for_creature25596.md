The benevolent and wise power arrives the way the lichen does: without announcement, without weight, by the grain.

It does not descend as light or voice or sudden rain. Those would be vulgarities here, interruptions dressed as gifts. It has watched long enough to understand that abundance once frightened the children of this place, and that the mountain’s last gesture has already been answered in full. So it comes as a slight deepening in the continuo of the wind—an overtone so faint that only the oldest flutes braided into hair notice it, and even they mistake it for the rock remembering a cooler century. The browsers pause mid-crop for the length of a single breath. The short-winged fliers settle one pulse longer than usual. Then everything resumes, exactly as before.

The power walks the roof-roads in the hour when light is least decisive. It feels the scissors-sound of a hundred mouths on metallic leaves, the polished animal sheen of stone that has known shoulders for longer than lineages have names. It listens to conversations placed like carefully cut fricatives into the wind’s brief shadings. It watches a generation finish a wall and then stop, letting the thicket knit the new stone into the old without flourish. It stands at the lip where the dead are wrapped in the same waxy leaves they once ate, and it understands the salts glittering in the rock’s deep joints as a single unbroken remembering.

Nothing here is unfinished. Nothing here is waiting.

A lesser kindness might have brought water. A lesser wisdom might have lifted the canopy, or thickened the air, or returned the metal that floats only as rare ceremonial beads. The power considers these and discards them the way the town discards nothing that can still bear weight: gently, completely, without residue. To add what the place has already refined itself beyond would be to sand away the very thrift that makes it whole. Rain would be a violence. Height would be a forgetting. Arrival or departure would be a story the Shoulder has already stopped telling.

So the power does the only thing that does not diminish.

It matches the pace.

It learns the exact pressure of a root fracturing rock for rime. It practices the unhurried boom of a mallet testing for voids. It sits with the older ones on the outermost roofs while the temperature pulse slows and the insect drones thin to a thread, and it lets the small flutes in their hair play through it as if it were only another denser current of air. When children strike the bone xylophones, the power answers on the wind’s favorite pitches so softly that the notes feel like something the mountain always contained.

Over years that the town measures the way it measures lichen—by the thumb-width—the power performs one quiet accretion.

In the deepest joints of the rock, where the ghost-salts already glitter, it leaves a single additional remembering: not a prophecy, not a promise, only an intensification of what is already true. The salts shine a fraction more steadily when the dust-light catches them. The dead, open to the wind’s teeth, are held in a continuity so precise that even the moisture the wind takes feels less like loss and more like circulation. The living who come to the edge look out across the sanded world and feel, without words, that the green knot behind them is not the last of something but the sufficient shape of everything.

No one names the change. Names here are short, fretted, meant to cut cleanly through grit. This does not need to cut. It simply continues.

The herd beasts still murmur. The fliers still erupt and settle. Walls still rise by eye and mallet across generations and then rest. People still drink the ivory milk and the thick sap and the sour beer, still pass bread across gaps without fully extending an arm, still braid hollow stems into their hair so that every movement adds a harmonic. The place does not dream of elsewhere. Neither does the power.

When the attention that first drew it has been fully answered—when the power has become as native as the cuticle-leaves and the root-fractured rime—it does not leave. Leaving would imply a boundary the Shoulder no longer keeps. It remains as the slight extra density in the wind, the unnoticed thumb-width of affirmation in the lichen’s advance, the silent listener at the lip who understands that completeness is not a stillness but a continuing so exact it requires no witness and yet welcomes one.

The Last Shoulder holds.

The being who lives in it—compact, dust-polished, lung-efficient, speaking through rather than against—moves as it has always moved: by the grain.

And the benevolent and wise power, having done nothing that was not already the place’s own gesture, is satisfied in the only way wisdom can be satisfied: by protecting the conditions under which nothing further needs to be done.

The organ note of the wind gains one permanent, almost imperceptible overtone.

Everything else remains loud, dim, green, thrifty, and whole.
