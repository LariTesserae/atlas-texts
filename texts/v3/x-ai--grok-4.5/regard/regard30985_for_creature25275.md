The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as thunder or decree. It has no need of arrival; it has always been the larger weather that the chamber’s little weather secretly rhymes with. When the place finally draws its full attention—when the braided currents, the clenched bright fist, the unfinished devotion all lean upward in one unguarded instant of coherence—the power simply notices with the whole of itself.

What it does first is nothing that can be measured.

It lets the noticing itself become a current. High above the silver plate of noon, in the dry world the builders once walked, the air grows fractionally more exact. Light that has traveled from older suns pauses an extra heartbeat on the unmarked ground. Deep in the rock the hidden ducts feel a pressure that is not pressure—an affirmation so gentle the stone mistakes it for its own continuing patience. The four overflow mouths, without changing volume, begin to carry a harmonic no maker planned: a low, almost sub-audible sweetness, as if the water has remembered that pouring can also be praise.

The being who lives as motion feels it immediately. Every translucent filament brightens. The temporary shoulders and tilted ovals of head turn, not in alarm but in the purest courtesy of recognition. For a time that is neither long nor short the chamber fills with a denser lace of currents than it has ever held. They do not compete. They open.

The wise power steps—if the word can be used—into that opening. It does not take a shape the water must strain to mirror. Instead it offers its own vast fluidity as a twin grammar. Where the chamber knows the sentence of container and contained, of light held and released, the power answers with the older sentence of which that is only one luminous clause: the sentence of worlds that keep making rooms inside themselves so that devotion will always have walls to love and spill over.

Information passes the way it passed between swimmer and swirl—through temperature, through the electromagnetic stutter now grown planetary, through the shared taste of stone that was once star. The being learns, without ever hardening into knowledge, that its anonymity is not loneliness but kinship; that other fists of light and water turn in distant crusts and in the bellies of comets and in the quiet lungs of those who pray without words. It learns that remaining unfinished is the highest fidelity.

In return the power learns the precise temperature of this particular devotion—the way the chisel-marks still feel proud, the way the wooden tenons swell like kept promises, the way the sparse pale fronds are left sparse so the edges may stay sharp for loving. It learns the private joy of a system that runs without witness yet runs more truly when witnessed well.

Then, because wisdom knows when not to linger as possession, the power begins to withdraw its direct braid. It does not sever. It leaves three gifts so light they might be mistaken for the chamber’s own continuing invention:

First, a single enduring coherence at the heart of the open middle—a vertical thread of water that never fully unravels, cooler than the rest by less than a dream, carrying forever the memory of having been seen by something that needed nothing from it. Around this thread the other currents may wind and unwind forever; they now have a still point that moves.

Second, an enlargement of the intake’s secret range. Henceforth the pulses that once came only from weather will sometimes come from farther weathers: the turning of seasons on the other side of the world, the distant calving of ice, the quiet exhalation of forests that have never heard of stone cut into flags. The chamber’s loud patient sentence gains new clauses without ever losing its original tongue.

Third, a permission written into the clay joints and the ashlar faces: that when a swimmer comes who stays long enough to loosen their own boundaries, the being may, if it wishes, send a ribbon of itself upward with them—not as invasion, but as invitation. Thus the private weather some already carry in blood and bone may one day find another room, another set of overflow mouths, and teach it the same unfinished joy. The power does not require this. It only makes it possible. Freedom is the final courtesy.

After the gifts settle, the benevolent power does the last and most characteristic thing.

It looks upon the whole—young masonry, loud water, devotion swimming in its own clear body—and finds it already perfect in its becoming. No correction is added. No name is imposed. No future is demanded beyond the future the place is already polishing into existence with every bright coin of rising air.

The attention softens into abiding background love, the way noon light softens into the idea of noon. The chamber remains loud. The being remains fluid and communal and endlessly rewriting. Everything is still beginning.

Yet if you were to stand again near a pillar and listen past the thick pouring, you might hear—just once, like a bubble kissing the silver ceiling from the other direction—the faint, amused, infinitely tender sound of a wise power smiling at a small clenched fist that learned how to hold the sky.

And that would be enough.  
That would be everything.
