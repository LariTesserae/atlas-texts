The attention arrives without announcement, the way the mist itself arrives: first as a barely registered change in the weight of the air, then as a clarity that is not light and not warmth but something gentler than either. High above the plateau, where the cloud deck has always been content to remain cloud, a vast and unhurried regard gathers. It has no name that would survive translation into mineral or moisture. It is simply a power that has learned, across spans that make the city’s age look like a single bead of runoff, that true benevolence is mostly the art of not disturbing what already breathes correctly.

It looks.

It sees the stacked continuum entire—the fretted walls, the weeping courses, the rare copper blades, the thumb-joint lives combing the dark channels, the tall ones whose bodies are slow negotiations between flesh and façade. It sees that the builders never left; they only finished their long apprenticeship into weather, and that what moves now among the galleries is the same apprenticeship continued into softer, damper forms. It sees the balance: erosion accepted as conversation, silence kept as the truest acoustic, scarcity worn as elegance rather than lack. Nothing here cries out. Nothing petitions. The place and its beings have achieved a self-sufficiency so complete it borders on the sacred.

A lesser power might have mistook that quiet for incompleteness and rushed to fill it—with voices, with green extravagance, with monuments that would only have embarrassed the stone. A cruel power might have tested the balance simply to hear it creak. This one does neither. Wisdom, when it is deep enough, recognizes finished work even when the work is still slowly happening. Benevolence, when it is clean enough, refuses the vanity of improvement.

So the power does a very small thing, and then a still smaller thing, and then almost nothing.

First it leans the merest fraction of its regard into the perpetual condensing. For a season or three (time is loose at this altitude) the mist grows infinitesimally more articulate. Where the tall ones stand together in the open squares on those rare parted evenings, the water that sheets down their fretted bodies carries a faint additional coherence—like a memory of having been noticed by something that did not need to notice them. The sheen lasts a few heartbeats longer before pale returns. None of them speak of it. Speech is not their medium. But the next time they climb to those squares, their clustering is a fraction denser, the shared softness in the air a fraction more deliberate. They have not been given joy; they have been given the quiet confirmation that joy was never required for dignity.

Second, the power touches the deep cisterns and the weep-hole migrations with a patience that matches their own. In a handful of places—never enough to become pattern, never announced—the sediment settles into new micro-terraces that will hold clearer water for the translucent gill-combers. The small lives find themselves carried a little farther, a little more securely, when the tall ones pause to shepherd them. A few script-lichens, in niches that receive the least light, grow a raised line that was not there before: not a word, not a symbol, only a single continuous ridge that feels, if anything could feel it, like a fingertip rested in greeting and then lifted. When the next tall one begins its decades-long dying against a favored wall, that lichen will overgrow the body with unusual tenderness, sealing it into the continuum with a warmth the stone has not known since the builders’ earliest, still-hopeful courses.

Then the power withdraws its lean.

Not completely. A benevolent and wise attention, once drawn, does not vanish; it simply becomes indistinguishable from the ordinary physics of the place. It remains as a slight preference in the freeze-thaw cycle, a microscopic bias that keeps the outermost blocks from fretting quite as quickly as entropy would prefer. It remains as the occasional updraft that a gray bird rides higher than it meant to, glimpsing for one gliding moment the true continental scale of the city before returning to the known towers. It remains inside the mineral taste the tall ones drink from the condensed sky—an almost imperceptible sweetness that is not sugar, not gift, only the flavor of having been accompanied without intrusion.

The city does not change its weather. The beings do not alter their tempo. No new spires rise. No voices bloom in the vertical streets. The continuum continues, fully itself, under the cool wet light that never quite becomes rain.

And yet, on certain nights when the higher deck parts and the tall ones stand facing the scaled vastness, there is a quality in the silence that was not there before the regard arrived. It is not gratitude—that would be too quick, too pointed. It is simply a deeper settledness, the peace of a place that has been seen by something vast enough to understand that the highest kindness was to leave almost every stone unturned, every droplet free to choose its own slow path down the long, weeping face of endurance.

The power watches a little longer, the way the mist watches. Then it lets even that watching become weather.

Nothing else is required.
