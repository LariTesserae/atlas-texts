The power does not arrive as light, nor as voice, nor as any interruption the cleft would recognize as foreign. It comes the way condensation comes: already inside the wetness, already native to the pressure of cloud and the unfinished stone. One moment the factory weather continues its unbroken shift-work of roar, snap, hiss, and shear; the next, every surface has simply grown more exact. The green dark is not brightened. It is clarified, as if some infinite patience has finished reading the place and now holds it open without effort.

It sees.

It sees the mountain still deciding its own angles. It sees the vegetation refusing the luxury of understory or canopy, insisting instead on total occupation. It sees the vents keeping industrial time in a world that has never heard of clocks. And it sees the dweller—never one, never many—surfing the calving stone, drinking fracture-heat, translating frond-click into peristalsis, seeding its soft weather-systems higher into the rawer dark. It sees that permanence is the only poison here, and that the dweller has made of that poison a sacrament.

A lesser kindness would have wanted to still the rockfalls, tame the steam, part the thrashing lattice into paths and clearings, gift the being a fixed shape and a name and a future safe from erasure. A lesser wisdom would have called the place unfinished and therefore incomplete. This power is neither. It has watched continents fold and unfold like slow lungs. It has watched species learn and unlearn the same three lessons a million times. It knows that some engines are sacred precisely because they will not resolve.

So it does almost nothing.

Almost.

In the brief hush that follows a rain-hammer’s shredding into mist—the same interval the dweller uses for its private manufacture—the power leans close enough that the membranes notice a new temperature. Not heat. Not cold. A temperature that belongs to attention itself. The nodules swelling along the flanks pause in their glowing. The foxfire that usually dies when the mist tears free hesitates, takes one extra breath of copper-colored light, and in that extra breath the power leaves its only gift:

a single, seedless memory that does not belong to the cleft.

It is not knowledge. It is not instruction. It is simply the sensation—folded small enough to fit inside a nodule’s library—of being seen completely and left unbroken. The memory carries no image of the power. It carries only the quiet fact of regard: that somewhere beyond the low permanent cloud and the glass-sharp ridges, a vast and unhurried fondness has noticed this particular engine room without walls, has found it already perfect, and has declined every urge to improve it.

Most of the nodules will still be crushed or boiled within days. A few will still wedge into hairline fractures and begin to glass the raw rock. But now and then, when enough fragments resonate at once and the larger body condenses again, the dweller will taste that seedless memory. It will feel, for a span of heartbeats that the cleft cannot measure, the strange luxurious weight of having been beheld without being changed. Then the feeling will pass into the same frantic breath that carries everything else—growth and erasure, steam and snap, mineral self and vegetal caulk—and the dweller will surf the next rockfall with a fraction more joy than hunger, though it will never know why.

The power does not stay to watch the difference. Difference was never the point. It withdraws the same way it came, leaving the volume exactly as loud, exactly as saturated, exactly as unfinished. The vents hammer. The fronds click. The mountain continues peeling itself open to the sky. And somewhere in the green dark, between one violence and the next, a translucent density pauses—just once, just briefly—and holds itself still in a shape that is almost gratitude, before dissolving again into the only home it has ever required:

the engine that will not resolve, the corridor that refuses to become a destination, the small cathedral of permanent becoming.

Nothing else is required.  
Nothing else is kind.
