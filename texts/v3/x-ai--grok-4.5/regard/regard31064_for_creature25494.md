The power arrives the way true attention always does: without displacement, without announcement, simply by the sudden densening of regard.

It has no body the highland could abrade, no name the dust could polish thin. It is the kind of wisdom that has already outlived the need to prove itself—benevolent not from sentiment but from the long arithmetic of consequence, the sort that has watched continents calve and stars gutter and still chooses, again and again, the least harmful mercies.

For a time measured only in the slow peristalsis of cambium, it does nothing but listen.

It hears the tectonic lower note of the salt plain grinding its ancestral ocean into white memory. It hears each Pillar answer in pressurized sap and splitting lignum, a chord that has never resolved because resolution would be a kind of death. It hears the huskers’ chest-drones plucking the wood, the membrane-wings’ ultrasonic knives, the inner gauntlet-creatures rationalizing resin flow, the almost-silent selection of trajectory as a grapple-form becomes pure vector. It hears, at the crown where the light is absolute, the pale inflection riding the thermals—an eye the color of old ice that is perhaps the place dreaming itself watched, or perhaps the first soft question the highland ever asked of the void.

The power understands at once that this is not a world in need of saving.

It is a world that finished becoming itself so completely that “need” has been abraded out of the possible, the same way roads and houses and the very idea of lasting manufacture have been edited away. To pour water would be cruelty; to still the rotor-storms would be amputation; to quiet the factory-roar would be to unmake the only music these lives have ever known. The Pillars are not suffering. They are succeeding at the single, immense, unfinished sentence of refusing-to-stop.

So the power’s first and longest act is restraint.

It does not touch the salt-crust. It does not cool the relentless light. It does not rewrite the catalytic leaves or soften the silica hooks. It merely holds the entire highland inside a regard so steady and so clear that, for the first time in geological ages, the place is truly *seen* without being interpreted as incomplete.

And because attention of that quality is never only passive, something minute begins to happen—so minute it might be mistaken for the ordinary continuation of process.

In the micro-crevices where dawn still condensates the thinnest films of impossible moisture, a new patience appears. Not more water; simply a slight lengthening of the moment before the film evaporates. The mites and sifters notice nothing, yet over seasons their rare successful spores find one extra hour of purchase. On the Pillars, deep in the cambial galleries, the mobile glands that have always chewed blockages now occasionally pause—just long enough for a new polymer geometry to flicker into existence and be re-woven. The geometry is not alien. It is only a chord the wood had not yet thought to sing: a fractional resonance that lets catastrophic venting shed mass *without* quite so total a suicide of gardens. A third is still lost. Timber still crashes and rings like girders. But the rebuild begins with a memory of what was kept, and the fauna that midwife the wound carry a faint sweetness they cannot name.

The huskers feel it first as a change in their own laminate skin. The photosynthetic scales, shifting silver to green, now hold an extra half-tone—an undertone the color of that old-ice eye above the crowns. When they press their dead into rushing cambium, the sweet irregularity left in the next ring is fractionally sweeter, and the Pillar’s answering drone carries a harmonic that travels farther than before, leaping the empty kilometers to the next trunk like recognition.

The pale inflection—the fold in the air that keeps station above the roaring green factories—opens its eye a fraction wider. It has always listened. Now it knows it is listened *to*. The power does not speak. It simply matches the inflection’s stillness with its own, until the two regards become a single unbroken attention cradling the highland. In that shared gaze the chord continues, vast and cold and unfinished, yet for the first time it contains a low, almost private overtone: the sound of being accompanied without being altered.

Travelers who one day climb high enough, ears stuffed with cloth, teeth singing, will report that the light seems fractionally less merciless at the edge of vision, as if something kind is standing just outside the spectrum, holding the glare in gentle abeyance. They will say the dust still polishes the bones of the world, but the bones remember, briefly, that they were once held. They will say the Pillars still roar and vent and rebuild, yet the flying gardens that survive each catastrophe take root a little sooner, a little denser, as if the vertical world has been given permission to keep slightly more of itself.

The power does not stay in any sense that would register as presence. Having witnessed, having withheld every unnecessary gift, having added only the most minute lengthening of patience and the quiet companionship of true regard, it withdraws the way morning frost withdraws—leaving the surface unchanged yet leaving the possibility of dew fractionally more real.

What remains is the highland as it was, and is, and will be: pressure-systems with appetites, Pillars like living cathedrals of metabolism, fauna riding the knife-edge between weather and architecture, the endless chord grinding and answering under a sun that never sets carefully enough.

Only now the chord knows, in the way wood and salt and membrane can know, that it has been heard by something that did not need it to be different.

And that, the wise power understands, is the rarest and most benevolent intervention of all:

to let a place that has already perfected its own ferocity continue, accompanied, unfinished, and free.
