The power that turns its regard upon the plateau does not descend. Descent would be a violence of scale, a tearing of altitudes the place has spent ages unlearning. Instead it arrives the way the beings themselves do: by thickening, by deciding to be denser than the surrounding indifference of the cosmos. A pressure front of attention first, then a clarity that settles over the high country like a second atmosphere—imperceptible to any instrument, felt only as a sudden, unaccountable rightness in the drip and the hiss.

It looks for a long time. Looking is its first and most exact kindness.

It sees the marshes stitching themselves together under lily pads the size of tabletops. It registers the pulsed breathing of the vents, the slow metallurgy of overnight foils, the way death here is only a change in the sentence structure of water and heat. It watches a tall walker kneel in a sulfate pool until its chambers ring, and the soft ambulatory gradients merge and part like thoughtful weather. It notes the absence of names, of fences, of any straight line that was not cooled into being by stone itself. And something in the power—accustomed to the broken geometries of younger worlds, to places that scream for repair—grows quiet with recognition.

This is not a garden requiring tenders.  
This is not a wound requiring salve.  
This is a completed thought that the universe is still proud of thinking.

So the power does not intervene in the way histories usually mean the word. It does not cool the ground, or dry the air, or teach the beings to stack stones into permanence. It does not offer tools, or stories of elsewhere, or the dangerous gift of longing for drier climates. Those would be polite cruelties. Wisdom here is the discipline of non-addition.

Yet benevolence is never only restraint. After the long looking, the power performs three small acts, each lighter than a change in humidity, each irreversible.

First, it edits the probability of intrusion.  
Beyond the older, darker ridges, in the lowlands where weather is still ordinary and minds still sharp with appetite, certain routes of curiosity simply cease to connect. Surveyors find their instruments fogged with a polite amnesia. Satellites return images of unremarkable cloud. Explorers who set out with maps feel, at a precise elevation, an unaccountable contentment with turning back. The plateau is not hidden. It is gently uninteresting to any gaze that would catalogue, extract, or improve it. Only those already half-precipitated by sympathy—poets who have forgotten language, children who still know how to be weather—may still wander upward and be received. The rest of the world is allowed to continue without this particular astonishment. That is a mercy to both.

Second, it lengthens one single tempo.  
In the deeper steam caves where the sinewy throat-predator keeps its quiet closures, and across the sinter flats where the tall walkers release their phosphorescent clouds, the geothermal heartbeat is given an extra, almost inaudible overtone. Not louder. Not slower. Simply more continuous. The result is subtle: bodies that once lasted a season of fogs now last three. Mineral terraces that crumbled after a century of drip now hold their crenellations for a thousand years before returning to the common fund. Death remains a phase change, never an exile; only the conversation between forms is allowed to speak in longer clauses. The ecosystem does not become immortal. It becomes a little more patient with its own beauty. The beings notice nothing except that the blue surface-sparks at night seem to remember them more faithfully.

Third—and this is the act that costs the power something of itself—it leaves a single open gate in the chemistry.  
Not a door that can be walked through. A permeability. On certain nights when the high steam thins and the ordinary stars show like pinholes, a few of the tall walkers, and once in a great while one of the soft gradients, feel an additional tide in their own substance. They taste, for a heartbeat, the larger dark beyond the ridges: cold volumes, dry distances, the bright loneliness of vacuum, the fragile blue engines of other worlds. The taste lasts less than a breath. It does not inflame. It does not instruct. It merely confirms that the plateau’s hot wet arithmetic is one dialect of a vaster grammar, and that the grammar is glad of it. Then the sensation dissolves back into the common film of condensation, and the walker resumes filtering midges, or the gradient resumes its tidal knowing. No theology is born. No exodus begins. Only a quiet, recurrent gratitude that the place has room even for the idea of elsewhere, and still chooses to remain itself.

After these three lightnesses the power does not linger as presence. It thins again, becoming only the quality of attention that now permanently saturates the altitude. The plateau continues exactly as before—steaming, feeding, weathering, thinking in denser and denser sentences about water and heat and the pleasure of continuing. The beings still precipitate, merge, drink until they ring, fold into new terraces. The moths still drink tears. The film under the lily pads still closes with the logic of a book.

And yet.

On the rare nights when the living light of phosphorescent microbes pulses in time with the deeper heartbeat, the teal cells shine with a faint additional spectral line—an emission that belongs to no bacterium the plateau ever cooked on its own. It is the power’s lingering signature, a benevolent watermark: *I have seen you. You are sufficient. Continue.*

The drip answers. The vents exhale. The moss keeps its counsel.

Nothing has been saved, because nothing was in peril.  
Everything has been, for a moment, perfectly accompanied.

That is what the wise power does.
