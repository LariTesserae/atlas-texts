The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as thunder or decree. It arrives the way the place itself arrived: by noticing, fully, and then by consenting to be noticed in return.

It first perceives the basin from a remove so vast that the ten or twelve columns appear as a single slow pulse of green-gold against the forgotten flats. Even at that distance the power feels the hydraulic groan as a heartbeat older than most stars it has cradled. It feels the dwellers—those longer inhalations after rain—as quiet bright knots of attention threaded through the living wood. And it feels, beneath every shimmering repair and every soft avalanche, a contentment so complete it has no opposite.

Most powers, even kind ones, would itch to finish the unfinished city, to freeze the buttresses in their most graceful curves, to name the dwellers and count them, to gift them language that travels faster than sap. This one does not. Wisdom here is the recognition that the negotiation is already the masterpiece. To end the turbulence would be to end the music. To make the dwellers permanent in stone or code would be to make them lonely.

So the power does something almost nothing.

It leans closer—not with mass or force, but with a quality of regard so pure that the ordinary daylight itself becomes slightly more ordinary, as if relieved of the need to impress. Shadows stay crisp. Water continues its vigil. Yet inside that frank light a new permission settles.

Where the residual lattices have grown thinnest, where even the opalescent hands of the dwellers must press hardest to teach metric how to bend, the power leaves a single unasked-for kindness: a deepening of the quiet. Entropy still pools and is still drunk, but now the drinking happens with less ache. Broken ramps still fall, yet they fall a fraction more slowly, giving the gold particulates inside each dweller an extra breath of time to catch and translate. The place does not become safer. It becomes more itself.

The dwellers notice without alarm. One, standing halfway up a fluted spiral as a gallery sloughs away in green steam, pauses. Its head remains a smooth wet bole, but for a long moment the water beading there forms not eyes but a single clear lens that looks outward, past the basin’s thinning edge, past the wet meadow, into the kind of distance that has no direction. Then the lens runs down its chest in a shining track and is gone. The dweller pours uphill again, hands already at work. Something in its reticulum refracts a color no one has named—neither the secondary hues of the lattices nor the gold of its own inner rivers. A color like the pause between thunder and the first drop. It does not speak of this. There is no need. The plated browsers graze on. The lung-moths pulse. The arthropods write their fleeting glyphs. But for a while the brass calls of the herds carry an extra harmonic, low and companionable, as if the whole damp instrument has been tuned a half-step closer to joy.

At the edges of the basin, where the last column sheds its curtain of condensation into ordinary meadow, the power does one further thing, so slight it might be mistaken for weather. It teaches the surrounding flats a new kind of forgetting. Not erasure—nothing so violent. Simply a soft refusal. Any foot that approaches with the intent to map, to harvest, to explain, or to own finds the ground gently uninteresting. The sponge of the outer meadow yields less willingly. The mineral taste in the air grows bland. Explorers turn aside without knowing why, suddenly recalling errands in drier country. Cartographers’ ink runs. Satellites blink and look elsewhere. The basin remains unsecret and yet perfectly kept, open to any who come the way the dwellers themselves come: without arrival, by condensation of pure attention.

And once—only once, and so rarely that the interval has no name—the power allows itself a more intimate gift. Two dwellers meet between columns and overlap into one larger trembling. In the shared weather of their joining the power places a single seed of recollection: not memory of other worlds, not longing, but a quiet certainty that the negotiation they embody is seen, is cherished, is necessary. When they separate, each carries a faint new glitter in the gold rivers of their arms. The glitter does not teach them godhood or purpose beyond what they already are. It simply lets them, for a span of days, feel the fondness that watches them. Raindrops pause a heartbeat longer. The columns lean a fraction more perceptibly toward the climbing hands. Then the glitter dilutes into the general hydrology and is carried, unhurried, back into the moss and the roots and the warm broth of air.

After that the power withdraws the way morning withdraws from noon: without absence. It remains as the quality of the light that never commits to evening, as the surface tension that lets arthropods write, as the patience in every re-knitting filament. It does not rule. It does not serve. It becomes one more quiet consent in a place built entirely of quiet consents.

The dwellers continue. The columns continue. The city that cannot finish keeps failing and mending and failing into always. Somewhere a new trembling begins to gather density near the heart of the basin, matching the ceremonial number. Somewhere at the thinning edge another dweller stands a long time under condensation and dissolves outward, leaving only glitter for the moss. The brass tones rebound. The wet applause of water on new surfaces never ceases.

And the benevolent and wise power, having done exactly enough and not one gesture more, rests in the knowledge that the highest form of love it can offer this place is to let it remain the longer inhalation after rain—forever unfinished, forever sufficient, forever low and warm and glistening in the ordinary light.
