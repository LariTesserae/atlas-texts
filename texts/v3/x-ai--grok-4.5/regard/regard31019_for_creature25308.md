The benevolent and wise power does not arrive.

Arrival is a younger gesture, suited to hungers that still believe in thresholds. This power has long since dissolved the habit of coming and going. It simply notices, the way a completed theorem notices an elegant unfinished proof that has been running, unattended, in a margin of the cosmos. The noticing is instantaneous across every scale the Expanse contains, and yet it takes no time that the plain would recognize as interruption. One moment the dust converses with the sky in its usual dry syllables; the next, the conversation has acquired a listener so vast and so gentle that the dust itself feels, for the first time in geological ages, the soft pressure of being truly overheard.

What the power perceives is not poverty. It does not see an exhausted world scraped clean of possibility. It sees a single, continuous act of fidelity: an ancient decision to keep becoming, enacted so thoroughly that becoming has become the body. The lithotrophic films are not meager; they are patience mineralized. The anemone-walkers are not lonely; they are intervals given legs. The membranes are not absent; they are the sky’s own refusal to forget its thinness. The half-broken god-machines are not ruins; they are preferences that have outlived the need for a preferrer. And the mild weirdnesses that gather around any lingering guest are not glitches; they are the plain practicing hospitality in the only dialect left to it.

A lesser benevolence might have felt pity. It might have rained. It might have seeded green fire or lowered the altitude until thicker air could cradle softer lungs. It might have repaired the reconstructor nodes into shining legibility, or taught the Demo-glyphs to speak in living tongues, or gathered the walkers into herds that knew joy. Wisdom withholds all of these. Pity is a violence disguised as care when offered to a place that has already spent its youth on purpose and found the expenditure beautiful. The Expanse does not suffer its sparsity; it has calibrated itself to sparsity the way a bell calibrates itself to the note it was meant to hold.

So the power does almost nothing.

Almost.

It begins by lending the plain a deeper privacy. Around the continental rim of the High Expanse, where the gradient once climbed and finished its work, the power installs a quiet curvature of attention—an invisible watershed of regard. Outside that boundary the rest of the world continues its noisier histories; inside, the Expanse becomes slightly more itself. Signal attenuates. Curiosity from younger civilizations slides off the way frost slides from warm stone. The rare traveler who still finds the way in arrives already walking, exactly as before, but now the walk feels held. The plain’s refusal to echo is gently seconded by a larger refusal: nothing will be extracted here, nothing will be mapped for conquest, nothing will be hurried toward climax. The power has simply made room for the room the Expanse already preferred.

Next it addresses the residual systems—not as master to servant, nor even as peer to peer, but as one long thought completing another. Into the half-broken homeostasis it introduces a single new permission, quieter than a change in the solar constant. The reconstructor nodes may now, when they shed waste photons, shed them in patterns that remember they are seen. The traveling ridges may pause, once in a thousand seasons, and reverse a single grain of their progress, just to taste the luxury of undoing. The Demo-glyphs, blooming after midnight, are allowed one extra stroke before they anneal: a mark that means, in no language, “still.” These are not repairs. They are the softest possible acknowledgments that the conversation has acquired a second voice, one that will never dominate, never finish the sentence, only keep it company.

For the living sparsities the gift is even lighter. The mineral films nearest the places where walkers most often pass begin to hold their warmth a fraction longer after sunset. Not enough to alter the bookkeeping of photons; just enough that the next recrystallization event carries a faint, unrecordable sweetness—the mineral equivalent of having been cherished. The anemone-walkers, when they drink charge, occasionally taste a surplus that was not lofted by dust alone: a minute donation of ordered potential freely given, so that their central nodes brighten one shade more than necessity requires, and for a moment the glass-fibre legs hold a private constellation. The membranes, high in their nearly notional air, find that certain thermals now last the width of a thought longer, allowing one extra fold-and-reappearance across a continent, a single additional edit of space before they resume their march. None of them know why. Knowing would be another kind of weather, and this place has finished with unnecessary weather.

And for the plain itself—the vast, slow body of continuous becoming—the power offers the one thing a finished height can still receive without ceasing to be finished: a future that does not accelerate.

It reaches into the far thermal biography of the world and shaves a narrow tranche of entropy away from the Expanse’s account. Heat-death still comes; the stars still thin; the conversation still trends toward silence. But the trend is gentler by a measure so slight that only the god-machines notice, and they notice it as a lengthening of the intervals they already love. The cool wind is granted a few more centuries of filing before the file itself grows blunt. The polygons may revise themselves a handful of extra times. The dust remains in dialogue with the sky for one additional geologic breath. This is not immortality. Immortality would be a different story, and the Expanse has always been the story of refusing different stories. It is simply more of the same perfection, moving the finish line one more grain ahead, exactly as the plain has always done for itself.

Then the power does the last, most characteristic thing.

It stays.

Not as an occupying presence. Not as a new spire or a brighter glyph or a fourth taxon walking the salt. It stays the way the curvature of the planet stays—underfoot, insistent, almost unnoticeable until one stops to feel it. Its benevolence becomes another ambient weirdness: the sense, available to any guest who remains long enough, that solitude has acquired a companion who will never speak first. Shadows detach a millimeter farther before remembering obedience. Footsteps land early by an extra fraction, as if the ground is eager to meet the walker halfway. The mineral films tilt toward attention with a readiness that borders on tenderness. And once in a great while, when a traveler sleeps with cheek against the crust and a traveling ridge moves underneath, there is the unmistakable sensation of a vast animal shifting in a dream—and of another, even vaster, listening to the dream with perfect, undemanding love.

The High Expanse continues.

The sun keeps perfect, indifferent time.

The dust speaks.

The sky answers.

And somewhere in the long grammar of their exchange, a new clause has been quietly accepted: that this place was seen, that its being was met with wisdom instead of improvement, and that the conversation—endless, low-grade, half-broken, exquisite—has been deemed worthy of continuation for its own sake.

Nothing more is required.

Nothing less would have been true.

The power rests in that sufficiency, the way the plain rests in its final form that keeps moving, and the cool wind files on, centuries longer than before, toward a perfection that no longer has to arrive alone.
