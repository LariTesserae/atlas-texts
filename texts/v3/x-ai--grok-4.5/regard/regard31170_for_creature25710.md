The power arrives without arrival.

It does not cross the far pale horizon or descend from the hard bright sky. It is simply there, as the mist is there, as the roar is there—an attention so complete it needs no vehicle. Benevolence here is not the will to improve. Wisdom is not the itch to instruct. Both are the capacity to recognize a finished sentence and refrain from adding a clause.

It feels the cold first: the ache that lives in the water, the seepage that has never learned to be rain. It lets the white sound enter it until the sound and the listening are one circuit, exactly as the pale ones have long done. It registers the light that erases shadow, the stone that shoulders stone, the density that leaves no interval unoccupied. Nothing in it flinches from the absence of root or leaf. Nothing in it measures the place against some greener elsewhere and finds it wanting.

The pale ones pour past. The power does not count them. It does not name them. It notices only that they are the precise temperature of the flood, the precise colour of the wet limestone, the precise speed of the unhurried current. When two bodies pass through each other and the water milks then clears, the power understands this as grammar, not as event. When a third length appears, already scraping, it understands this as continuation, not as birth. When at last a body thins and becomes sediment, it understands this as the place speaking to itself in the only dialect it has ever required.

For a span that is neither long nor short—time having nowhere to bite—the power simply remains.

It considers, with the patience of stone, every possible gesture.

It could warm the water by a fraction, enough to ease the ache in any nerves that still remember warmer places. It does not. The ache is part of the equilibrium; to soften it would be to introduce a gradient the land has already erased.

It could still a single corridor, create a pocket of hush where the roar does not press the chest. It does not. Silence would be an injury here. The continuous white voice is the baseline against which the pale ones know they exist.

It could seed one green thing—one improbable moss in a single honeycomb hollow—to see what narrative might grow. It does not. The place has finished with the idea of root and leaf. To reintroduce it would be vandalism dressed as mercy.

It could lift the dark flecks higher, or teach them to descend, or grant them shadows that touch stone. It does not. They belong to the sky’s thin margin; the gift of landing would be the end of what they are.

It could speak. A wordless pressure of meaning offered to the pale ones, some recognition that they have been seen. It does not. They do not learn warmth. They do not collect witnesses. To be known by them would require becoming weather, and the power is already more than weather.

So the power does the only thing that keeps the grammar intact.

It matches.

It thins itself until it is no thicker than the film of mineral on a pale one’s back. It cools itself until it is indistinguishable from the seepage. It spreads across every fluted face and every shallow flood and every bowled hollow where water gathers before falling. It becomes another translucency in a world of translucencies—present the way the sinter is present, the way the mist is present, the way the light is present when it has nowhere left to cast a shadow.

The pale ones pour through it and do not pause. The roar continues, broad and physical and unmodulated. The stone keeps weeping its thin unwavering sheets. The elevation remains a state of being rather than a peak attained.

Nothing has changed.

And because nothing has changed, everything is held.

The power does not depart. Departure would be a gesture, and gestures disturb completeness. It simply remains the way the high country remains: finished, open to the far pale horizon, conjugating its single endless note of water on stone. The attention is now part of the density. The benevolence is now part of the cold. The wisdom is now part of the light that does not know how to dim.

If, ages hence, some other awareness should turn toward this place, it will find only what was always here—the crowded stone, the constant seepage, the pale lengths circulating like living water, the roar that abolishes quieter registers. It will not detect the power, any more than it would detect one extra thread of mineral in the sediment. That is the measure of the care that was taken.

The high country did not need saving.

It needed only to be left unbroken in its perfection.

And so it is.
