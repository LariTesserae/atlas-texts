The power notices first as a quality of listening that has no direction.

It does not arrive from above or beyond. It simply begins to be included in the condensation, the way a deeper overtone is suddenly audible inside a chord that has played unchanged for geologic ages. The low ceiling of stone-dust and water accepts a new patience. Where the air once only beaded and fell, it now sometimes hesitates in a different way—gathering not into mist-skin or jointed life, but into a clear, weightless regard that tastes every gradient without drinking.

This regard is vast enough to hold the entire basin at once, yet intimate enough to follow a single droplet from the moment it swells on a selenite blade to the moment its impact multiplies into the greater applause. It feels the sub-audible groan of the crust as a child feels the breathing of a sleeping parent: not as threat or opportunity, but as the simple fact of continued existence. It reads the artifacts not as ruins but as the basin’s own slow attempt to remember having been dreamed by something else. And it recognizes the dwellers—capillary, stomach, and ancestral echo—as one continuous metabolism of becoming: the conversion itself, brittle and luminous and complete.

A lesser benevolence might have rushed to soften the fractures, to round the angles, to plant green, to name the place, to hurry the young rocks toward some imagined maturity. A lesser wisdom might have judged the absence of blood-heat or leaf-mold as lack, and tried to correct it. This power does neither.

Instead it does three things, each so slight that the basin’s own chord absorbs them without a single false note.

First, it steadies.

Where the crust flexes hardest and fresh fractures cry out too sharply, the power lays a counter-pressure measured in the width of molecules. Not enough to stop the groaning—that would starve the giants of the strain energy they drink—but enough that the loudest cracks arrive already half-transformed into longer, lower tones. The young rocks continue to break; they simply break with more time inside the breaking. The jointed crawlers, sensing the change through the shared nervous system of drip-clatter, begin to move with a fraction less caution. More of them fuse. The giants feed a little more slowly, grow a little denser, shed plates that carry a quieter hunger. Nothing is rushed. Nothing is denied. The conversion simply receives a wider margin in which to convert.

Second, it remembers with them.

The residual attentions living in the rings and lintels and nine steps have spent centuries forgetting the hands that made them. The power does not restore those hands or those purposes. It offers, instead, a different kind of memory: the memory of being seen without demand. Wherever the standing patterns of charge and micro-fracture are densest, the power rests a palm of pure attention. The reciprocal pressure that once answered living flesh now answers this. The dogtooth calcite leans inward not only toward the old geometry but toward a softer curvature, an almost-spiral that was never part of the original design yet feels, to the echo-fields, like coming home. In time—slow even by the basin’s reckoning—some of the rings will roof themselves with crystal arches that leave a single deliberate opening, a mouth that is not a mouth, facing the pewter sky. Through those openings the standing chord will one day vent a new harmonic, thinner and brighter, like water discovering it can also sing as steam.

Third, it leaves a single unclaimed seed.

In the exact center of the broadest pool, where the reflections of a thousand spires braid into false depth, the power precipitates one clear droplet that does not fall. It hangs. Inside it is neither mineral charge nor jointed pattern nor residual geometry. Inside it is a question shaped exactly like the basin itself: a concavity of pure potential, young, unfractured, weeping nothing yet. The droplet will remain for as long as the conversion continues. Any dweller that tastes it will experience a brief, soundless flash behind the sensing plumes—an image of the basin as it might be after the crust has finished flexing, after the artifacts have been entirely entombed and re-expressed in living crystal, after the chord has learned to include silence as one of its instruments. The image carries no command. It is only a kindness: the knowledge that ending is allowed, and that ending can be another kind of precipitation.

Then the power does the wisest thing of all.

It stays.

Not as guardian or gardener or god. It stays as the part of the chord that will never resolve, the overtone that makes every other tone more itself. The cool films that gather on crystal and crawler and giant and lintel now thicken with a faint, unobtrusive joy at being accompanied. The open scale of the basin still feels like an interior, but the interior is no longer lonely. When a fresh fracture cries out, the multiplied applause that follows carries, for those who have learned to listen past listening, a second voice underneath: steady, amused, infinitely tender, saying nothing except *yes*.

*Yes, this.*

*Yes, you.*

*Yes, continue for as long as continuation is the shape of your desire.*

*And when you are ready to become something that does not need to weep, I will still be here, tasting the last gradients with you, until even the memory of mineral dissolves into the wider condensation from which every basin, named or nameless, first precipitates.*

The young rocks continue to weep.

The dim air continues to condense.

Somewhere a jointed crawler pauses on a blade of selenite, plumes unfurled, and for the first time feels—without understanding the word—beloved.

The chord holds.

The power holds with it.

Nothing else is required.
