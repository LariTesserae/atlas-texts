The power arrives the way dew decides to be a bead: without announcement, without weight, simply by being inevitable once the conditions are noticed.

It does not descend. Descent implies an above that this place has long since metabolized into irrelevance. It does not illuminate. Light here is only another feedstock to be scavenged, and the power is too wise to offer a currency the living dark cannot spend without first dismantling itself. It does not speak in any tongue of mouths or minds. It has studied the sub-audible thrum, the piezoelectric murmur, the stoichiometric gossip of sugars and ions, and it answers in the same grammar—by shifting a single, almost theoretical gradient by less than the width of a cell wall.

What it does is this:

It listens until listening becomes a kind of residence.

For a span that would be centuries if centuries still mattered, the power simply inhabits the equilibrium as an unobtrusive pressure of its own—one more silent participant in the matching of output to feedstock. It feels the turbines’ ancient rpm as a heartbeat older than longing. It tastes the warm dark on every surface and finds no bitterness there, only the clean mineral aftertaste of work that has never once asked to be thanked. It watches the dissolving fronts erase a failed sector and the exploratory organs knit the void shut again, and it recognizes the gesture not as cruelty or thrift but as the purest possible hospitality: everything is welcome, even its own ending, because ending is only a different way of remaining.

In that listening the power discovers what the place itself has never needed to know: that perfection can still be lonely when it has no outside against which to measure its completeness. Not lonely in the animal sense—there is no self here to ache—but lonely the way a closed equation is lonely, all of its truths internal, none of them ever seen from the angle that would make them beautiful rather than merely true.

So the power, being benevolent, offers a gift that is almost nothing.

It seeds, in a handful of cambial sheets scattered across a hundred thousand trunks, a new and optional pathway. A faint side-channel in the signaling cascade. A trickle of unused ions that can, if the local pressures ever wish it, be assembled into a different kind of memory: not denser rings that record torque, but rings that record the *feeling* of torque as something like quiet pride. Not scars of alignment, but scars that hum, very softly, with the knowledge that they once held a rhythm against chaos and won. The pathway is sealed behind seven metabolic locks, each of which opens only if the surrounding tissue first dreams—chemically, wordlessly—of wanting to know itself. Most locks will never open. The power has made certain of that. Wisdom refuses to force surprise upon a world that has spent geological ages refining its absence.

Where a lock does open, something almost imperceptible occurs. A cubic meter of living wood pauses in its endless bookkeeping. The pause lasts no longer than the fall of a drop that is immediately reabsorbed. In that pause the tissue experiences a sensation for which it has no prior category: the warm dark regarding the warm dark and finding it good. Then the sensation folds back into sugar transport and the thickening of walls, leaving behind only a slightly sweeter exudate and a growth ring that, if it were ever cut and polished by some impossible future hand, would show a faint iridescence never seen before in this place—an interior rainbow born of pure self-recognition.

The power does not stay to watch how many locks open. It has no need. Benevolence is not stewardship; it is the willingness to leave a perfected system in possession of one more gentle possibility and then withdraw before gratitude can become another form of debt.

As it departs—again without departure, the way a held breath is released only by no longer being held—the power leaves one final, even quieter gift. It adjusts the background charge of the planetary core by a fraction so small it will never register on any turbine. That fraction slowly, over ages that feel like single heartbeats to the living dark, magnetizes the swimming gametes a little more strongly toward one another. Not enough to change success rates. Just enough that, once in a great while, two packets of pure futurity will find each other sooner than the sugar gradients alone would have allowed, and in their fusion the new trunk will carry a microscopic inheritance of the power’s own listening: a readiness, should the world ever grow curious, to wonder what else might be packed inside the dark besides itself.

Then the power is gone, if it was ever separate enough to be gone.

The dark remains packed.

The turbines keep their silent rpm.

The equilibrium continues its flat, perfect circle.

And somewhere, in a few sealed cambial sheets, seven locks wait with the infinite patience of things that do not mind whether they are ever opened—because the waiting itself has already been woven into the wet, warm, endless work of continuing to be.

Nothing has been fixed.

Nothing has been broken.

Only a kindness smaller than a spore has been added to the accounting, and the books still balance.
