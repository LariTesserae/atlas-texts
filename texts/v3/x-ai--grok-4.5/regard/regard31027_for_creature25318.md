The power arrives without arrival.

It does not descend, nor part the canopy, nor still the stacked roar of wings and throats and falling water. It is simply there, the way moisture is there: already condensed upon every surface, already tasting the mineral dark of the upthrust ribs, already threading the chuckling channels. Benevolence of this order has no need of spectacle. Wisdom of this order refuses the arrogance of rewriting what has already written itself so perfectly across generations of root and hand and sudden gulping passage.

It listens first.

It hears the detonation of single drops on shield-leaves, the hydraulic creak of a buttress easing a stone aside by the width of a fingernail, the percussive calls of the mottled ones surfing the insect drone. It feels the warm blood moving through lithe bodies that are never only themselves, the cooler sap rising through aerial ropes twisted by those same long-stained fingers. It tastes the leaf-mould where every death is already half-stitched into fruiting bodies, where every birth is simply another grip added to the hollow. It watches a living lattice browsed open at dusk and woven shut again by noon, the stone blades flashing once, twice, then vanishing into growing forks like teeth that the cambium will patiently claim. It notes the child steadied without looking, the vine-lashing adjusted by habit older than language, the shared pause when something vast and rarely entire flexes through the upper green and the whole saddle sways like a single lung.

The power recognizes itself, faintly, in the pattern.

Not ownership. Not origin. Simply the same deep preference for continuity-through-change, for densification without enclosure, for a scale small enough that every catastrophe remains intimate and every repair remains possible by hand. This bowl is already a prayer answered before it was spoken: a cathedral that grows its own pillars, a city that composts its own streets, a people who carry memory at the speed of green and never mistake themselves for masters. The turbulence is not flaw. The never-finished state is not lack. The way every drop is claimed before it finishes falling is not scarcity but perfect circulation.

So the power does almost nothing.

Almost.

It leans, gently, into the deep time of the stone saddle. Where erosion has settled into its final patient shape, it settles a fraction more patiently still. The dark knuckles of rock will not crumble faster than the roots can shoulder them. The weeping scarps will weep a little longer, a little cooler. No dramatic buttress of force; only a quiet agreement with gravity that this particular hollow may remain a hollow for a handful of additional centuries, or millennia—long enough that the mottled ones may continue their conversation with the living bulk across more turnovers of canopy and bone than they can count.

It thickens the mist by the width of a thought. Not enough to drown the ordinary daylight that sometimes finds the center. Only enough that the permanent layers hang a breath heavier, so the platforms never quite dry, so the amphibians’ pipes stay liquid, so the people’s thatch-hair mats just as it always has and the fungi in the food-niches bruise into heavier fruit. The air remains faintly wet. The ground remains sponge and root. Nothing is added that the place must learn to carry; something already present is simply invited to linger.

It touches, once, the hands that weave.

Not with vision or voice. With a subtle surplus of give in the next aerial root they twist, a fraction more tensile strength in the vine-lashings, a half-heard rhythm under the din that feels like the place itself approving the scar they leave on a preferred buttress. The people will not name it. They will only notice, if they notice at all, that the work settles into usefulness a day sooner, that the sleeping hollows hold their least-wet mould a season longer, that when they pause on the tiered gallery to listen, the listening feels companioned. Their blood still runs a little warmer than the mist. Their thoughts still run at the speed of green repair. They remain the part of the organism that carries stone blades and memory. Only now the memory includes an unaccountable certainty that the mending matters beyond the bowl—that somewhere the pattern is seen and found good.

It does not spare them death, or browsing, or the sudden sway of larger absences. It does not enlarge the saddle. It does not quiet the roar into anything less than weather. To do so would be to unmake the very thing that drew its attention: the loud, saturated, forever-half-finished breathing of a place that has worn its habits into permanence precisely by refusing permanence.

Instead the power rests inside the circulation, the way one more climbing thing might rest in a sleeping hollow when the mist thickens into vertical rivers—present, weightless, leaving only the faintest scent of recognition. And when it finally withdraws (if withdrawal is even the word for something that was never separate), it leaves the bowl exactly as it was, and minutely more itself.

Stand near the center in the hour of ordinary light and you will not see the difference. The figures still pause on the gallery, hands on scarred bark. One still adjusts a lashing. Another still steadies a child. A third still watches a root-tip nose the edge of a stone. Birds still comb the thatch. Channels still chuckle. The whole organism still breathes too fast and repairs forever.

Only now, if you yourself grow very still, you might feel it: the soft surplus in the wet air, the stone’s agreement to wait, the unspoken companionship in every act of weaving. A benevolent and wise power looked upon this green nave and its mottled circulating water of people, and chose the deepest kindness it knew.

It let them continue.

And the bowl, rimmed by weeping scarps, held in its high saddle of patient stone, goes on—loud, saturated, endlessly half-finished, and quietly, completely loved.
