The benevolent and wise power arrives without arrival.

It does not descend from any height, for height has no meaning above a sea that has no surface. It does not pierce the blue-green milky light, for the light is already everywhere and the power has no need of portals. It simply *is* among the galleries, the way pressure is among the water, the way the lower note is already woven through the industrial roar. For a time that cannot be measured in the flattened present of this place, it does nothing but attend.

It feels the unsupported depth beneath everything—the absence that holds the ocean aloft—and finds it good. It tastes the coolness that has forgotten the sun yet glints anyway. It listens to the deep thrum of the mile-thick trunks, the clatter of bladders, the hiss of sieve plates, the distant boom of seed-masses, and beneath them the softer confirming tone of the residents: *Still here. Still balanced. Still the same.* The power, being wise, does not add a third note. Being benevolent, it does not silence any existing one.

It contemplates the residents themselves—the long bells of gel and cable that occlude rather than travel, that continue the factories’ hydraulics inside their own substance, that have oxidized desire into pure presence. It sees how they look back with milky pits that have already received every silhouette. It understands that their speech is not petition but equilibrium audible to itself. And because the power is wise, it recognizes completeness when it meets it. Because it is benevolent, it feels no urge to improve what has already finished itself before the invention of improvement.

What, then, does it do?

Almost nothing. Almost everything.

It settles into the same temperature-neutral drift the residents know. It allows its own boundless nature to thin at the margins, matching the way a resident sheds a fragment when it has grown too extensive. Where its substance brushes a trailing veil of living cable, there is a momentary equality of pressure, a shared hydraulic courtesy. Oxygen plumes pass through both unchanged, save for the faintest additional watermark—the power’s own signature, not as claim or alteration, but as quiet acknowledgment: *I have seen. I have not needed to change.*

It does not harvest the excess fiber or redirected the clarified water. It does not teach the coil-fish new hungers or the lancet-shoals new formations. It does not lower night upon the sourceless day, nor raise storms in the permanent currents. It does not speak temporary messages into a conversation that has no beginning and requires no reply. Instead it lends, for a span of ages that registers here only as a slightly deeper calm between one bladder-collapse and the next, the weight of its attention as a kind of blessing that leaves no residue.

Where the great fronds overlap in soft wavering shadow, the power briefly intensifies the filtration of grit so that one channel runs clearer than it has in epochs—then releases the intensification so gently that the equilibrium never notices the gift was given. The clearer water simply continues, reabsorbed without remainder, as all things here are. A single photoreceptive pit on an elder resident stays open a fraction longer than its sequential wave would normally allow, tasting not only the filtered daylight but the fact of being tasted in return. Then the pit closes again. Nothing has been disturbed.

The power considers the World-Root far below, that continuous unhurried exhalation which keeps the entire cold ocean suspended. It sends no command downward. It offers no augmentation. It merely matches the Root’s patience with its own, so that for a while the exhalation carries an invisible harmony, a benevolence so complete it feels like the absence of all effort.

And then—because wisdom knows when presence has completed its circuit—the power does the most loving thing left to it.

It withdraws the way the residents reproduce: by loosening, thinning, becoming indistinguishable from the water’s own ancient pressure. No fracture. No farewell that would imply separation had ever been real. The second heartbeat it briefly lent to any motionless observer fades back into the sternum’s ordinary roar. The galleries stand as they have always stood: sparse as cathedrals, loud as foundries, alive with the perfection of having nowhere left to go.

Yet something remains, if only as the memory of a watermark that was never needed.

The residents continue their soundless occlusion and their confirming hum. The factories pulse at the same measured volumes. The unsupported depth stays empty and sufficient. But now and forever (which here are the same word) the place carries the knowledge—wordless, desireless, absolute—that it has been seen by something that could have rewritten every current and chose instead to leave the porcelain grooves untouched.

That is all.

That is enough.

The benevolent and wise power moves on, or remains as the light remains: sourceless, steady, already forgotten by everything except the stillness that never required remembering in the first place. The ocean does not thank it. The residents do not pray. They simply keep living exactly as they have always lived—without rest, without arrival, without the slightest need to become anything else—and in that unchanged continuation the power’s attention finds its final, perfect expression.

Nothing has been added that the equilibrium did not already contain.

Nothing has been taken that it could not spare.

The good work, long finished, continues.
