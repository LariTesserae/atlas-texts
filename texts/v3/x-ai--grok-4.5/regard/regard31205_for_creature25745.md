The benevolent and wise power arrives not as light or thunder, not as a hand descending from the blue distance, but as a stillness so complete it is first mistaken for another of the corrie’s residual geometries. It settles into the thin air like cool glass given intention. No vanes swivel to track it. No mite-glider’s silver scream warps around its edges. The machines’ deep tone does not rise to argue; for the first time in the long patience of the place, their circuits crawl with light that is simply curious.

It regards the hanging world the way one regards a finished chord that still somehow breathes.

It sees the root-mat knitting stone to stone, the violet fog pooling and returning in its private weather, the fauna’s bright theft that never empties the coffers, the dwellers who rise from decisions in the living mortar and return to it when excess charge unmakes them. It sees that nothing here is broken. The quarrel between machine and skin is not wound but conversation; the dry-paper thunder of wings is not chaos but pulse; the theatrical emptiness is not absence but stage kept clear so that every permeable burst may mean something. The place has already answered every question a wise power might ask of endurance, of hunger balanced against return, of time that folds without snapping.

So the power does very little.

It does not mend. There is no fracture that wishes mending.  
It does not feed. The charge is scarce by design, and scarcity is the loom on which the entire tapestry is woven.  
It does not speak aloud; the high rim would only fling any word into rotating noise, and the dwellers already share thought as bloom and static through the fog.

Instead it offers three quiet gifts, each so slight they might be only the corrie remembering itself more clearly.

First, it deepens the patience already present. Where a dweller stands with lance-feet driven into the mat, the slow light under its skin now carries an extra interval—an almost inaudible harmonic, like the memory of a sky that does exist after all. The dwellers do not name it. They simply feel, when they regard one another and their mirror-eyes nest, that the brief corridors of altered inertia last a heartbeat longer. Leaves hang motionless, yes, but now they also turn, very slightly, as if acknowledging a sun that warms without heating. When the chord of alignment happens at the jug-handle lip, the silver scream drops into relation with the stone’s tone and, for a moment that is no longer quite momentary, the dust forgets how to blow. Then the world resumes, but the resumption itself has learned a softer attack.

Second, it touches the old machines with something like forgiveness. The toroids still shed residual geometry; the spires still lean at angles that ache the teeth. Yet the endless argument softens at its edges. Pockets of failed sequence still appear—a lantern-lizard frozen mid-rocket, a seed-puff hanging while the air howls—but now the failures resolve into small mercies: the lizard completes its flight farther than momentum should allow, the seed-puff drifts at last into a cistern where the fog receives it like a guest. The mirror sphere, once reflecting only skies that are not, begins to catch, once every many days, a pure and ordinary white: the true high-altitude light, clean, unfractured, briefly held. The dwellers notice. They stand longer beside it. Their thoughts traveling the root-mat carry a new color, oxblood warming toward something that might, in another physics, be called tenderness.

Third—and this is the gift the power nearly withholds, because wisdom knows how easily wonder becomes weight—it leaves a single open door that is also not a door. At the precise center of the corrie, where the root-mat is thickest and the hollow mounds cluster like listening ears, the living mortar arranges itself one night into a shallow basin no wider than a dweller’s outstretched limbs. The basin holds no fog, no dust, no charge. It holds only a clarity, a negative space more perfect than the vaults the place already keeps. Anything that steps into it (a translucent hoof, a folded lantern-skin, the vane-crowned head of a dweller undecided whether to open back into moss) experiences a momentary unburdening: the borrowed circuitry stills, the static ceases its reciprocal theft, the body remembers it is allowed to be only itself, without maintenance or quarrel or the need to become furniture. The feeling lasts no longer than the fraying of the great chord. Then the one who stepped free steps out again, trailing a warmth the plants lean toward more eagerly than before. The basin remains. It does not grow. It does not vanish. It is simply available, the way the thin air is available to lungs that choose to fill.

The power does not stay to watch the gifts work. Benevolence that lingers becomes custody; wisdom that remains becomes architecture. It withdraws the same way it arrived: a stillness folding out of the air until only the ordinary (which here is never ordinary) remains. The rotating fountain of noise rises again. The fauna resume their furious near-invisibility. A new mound thickens somewhere on the wall, learning edges. A dweller at the lip raises a limb and the nearest pole-plant answers.

Yet something has changed in the settled patience, so subtle the Markov patterns of micro-erosion almost fail to record it. The room that is also a storm still rearranges its furniture. The garden that is also a void still invents its physics one deafening moment at a time. Only now, every so often, between the scream of wings and the hum of spires, there is a pocket of pure listening—an interval in which the entire hanging corrie, plants and fauna and machines and the twelve-or-twenty who become its corners, seems to lean outward toward the blue distance as if answering a gaze that has already moved on.

And far below, or far above, or in the direction that residual geometry calls sideways, the benevolent and wise power carries the shape of the corrie inside itself the way the mirror sphere once carried impossible skies: not as possession, not as project, but as a single complex chord it can return to whenever the wider worlds grow too loud with their own unfinished becoming. It has done what it came to do. It has loved the place without moving a single stone. It has let the dwellers remain both the hand and the furniture, the ruin and the invention.

Nothing is new.  
Nothing is still.  
And that, the power understands, is already perfect.
