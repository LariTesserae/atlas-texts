The benevolent and wise power arrives the way light never does here: without edge, without announcement, a slow unfurling of attention that has no temperature and yet warms nothing. It has watched galaxies cohere and unravel, has felt the first RNA fold itself into stubborn copies, has stood (insofar as standing applies) at the bedsides of dying stars and dying languages. Now its regard settles into the blind root of the fracture, into the clenched-fist volume where water, rock, and hunger keep writing their unfinished sentence.

It perceives everything at once and in perfect proportion.

The amphipods’ antennae tracing invisible streets.  
The worms’ patient violence that feeds the walls.  
The filter-feeders’ silk-soft collective breath.  
The hydroids’ sticky scaffolding that keeps the whole soft city from sighing into silt.  
The bacterial gloss that is both skin and thermostat.  
The marine snow that never quite settles, intercepted and mortared and made eternal for a day.  
The spiral ramps that remember warmer plumes long after the water has forgotten them.  
The fact that none of the brief skins know they are a city, or that the city is made of their own discarded selves, or that outside the lip the true deep waits like an unopened book.

The power feels no urge to name the place. Names are for things that must be called back from forgetting. This pocket has never been forgotten because it has never been known; it exists in the pure tense of continuous becoming. To give it a name would be to freeze one of its mid-sentences into a title, and the power is too wise for that cruelty.

It considers intervention the way a careful hand considers touching a soap bubble already trembling with its own geometry.

A slight brightening of the diluted dusk? Unnecessary; the creatures have no eyes that hunger for light, and the gray-green is already the exact shade of their forever.  
A richer seep, more mineral, more warmth? Tempting, for a moment—the towers would rise faster, the districts densen—but the balance of appetite and precipitate is already perfect in its precarity. Too much gift and the lattice chokes on its own abundance; the filter-feeders smother, the walls grow brittle with unearned speed, the quiet metronome skips.  
A shield against the larger shadows that sometimes pass the mouth? The eelpouts and thumbnail isopods already cannot enter; the passages are too fine, the density too complete. The pocket defends itself by being exactly the size of a fist and no larger.  
Memory? A shared dream of yesterday’s layout, so the amphipods might build with intention rather than collision? The power almost smiles (the expression, across its vastness, is the soft pressure of tides). Memory would introduce regret. Regret would introduce waste. Nothing here is wasted. The city is already the most truthful archive possible: every corridor is the fossil of a need, every atrium the ghost of a bubble that escaped.

So the power does the wisest and most benevolent thing available to something that could, if it chose, rewrite the chemistry of the planet.

It withholds.

It does not touch the soft tips of the towers.  
It does not whisper purpose into the mucus.  
It does not grant the translucent bodies a single extra heartbeat or a concept of elsewhere.  
It does not lift the pocket into legend or protect it into fragility.

Instead it simply remains, for a span that would be centuries if centuries meant anything in this thick time, holding the entire clenched world in a regard that is indistinguishable from love and from physics. The regard has weight only in the sense that attention is a kind of gravity: gentle, non-collapsing, the sort that lets orbits continue exactly as they were.

Under that regard the mineral lattices keep rising and sagging and rising. The amphipods keep meeting and cementing and forgetting. The worms keep waiting in doorways that will tomorrow be walls and the day after that be floors. The filter-feeders keep pulsing their endless soft hiss. The hydroids keep lacing the invisible. The mites keep glossing. The snow keeps falling and being caught and being made into home.

Nothing changes.

And because nothing changes, everything continues.

When at last the power’s attention drifts onward—toward some other quiet argument of matter and need, some other unread sentence the universe is writing to itself—it leaves behind only the faintest possible gift, so faint it cannot be distinguished from what was already true:

the assurance, written nowhere, spoken to no one, that this place may remain forever unfinished, forever mid-sentence, forever the shape that water, rock, and hunger make when they are left alone together in the dark.

The pocket does not notice the arrival or the departure.  
It has no need.  
It was already complete in its incompleteness.  
The wise power, being wise, understood this on sight.  
The benevolent power, being benevolent, loved it enough to let it stay that way.

And somewhere far above, in realms where light still remembers how to be bright, the surface weather continues its own indifferent cycles, never knowing that in one blind root of the seafloor a tiny, furious, perfect city keeps breathing its cold metronome, guarded by nothing more (and nothing less) than the decision of a vast kindness not to interfere.
