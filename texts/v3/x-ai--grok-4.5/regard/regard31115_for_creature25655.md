The benevolent and wise power does not descend.

It has no need of creases in basalt or throats of stone. It moves the way pressure equalizes across a shelf of ancient rock—without haste, without announcement, as a difference so slight that the ice veins themselves would need centuries to name it. Where lesser forces might pry or illuminate or claim, this one only regards. The regard arrives the way mist does: everywhere at once and nowhere enough to cast a shadow.

First it learns the finished shape of the place. Not by measuring the four body-lengths of depth or counting the terraces of frost-adapted green, but by settling into the same long breath the chamber already breathes. It tastes the iron and crushed-green air and finds the taste complete. It feels the plinth’s quiet leaching of obsolete heat and recognizes the bargain still being kept—the old seed-core having long since surrendered its purpose to the slower purpose of root and mineral and patient will. It notices the warm mound where denser fronds pool their internal glow, the oval of darker tissue that opens and closes like a thought remembering how to be private, the vein-structures braided with untarnishing metal that drink cold and return it as continuity. Nothing here is broken. Nothing here calls out for mending.

Wisdom, when it is true, knows the difference between a wound and a scar that has become landscape. Benevolence, when it is deep, refuses the arrogance of improvement. So the power does not warm the mineral cold, nor brighten the submerged twilight, nor untangle the living metal from the stems that have claimed it. It does not offer names to what has outlived names, nor stories to what has become the silence between stories. The inhabitant’s tempo—compression and release measured in droplet-time, shifts that treat decades as blinks—remains untouched. Loneliness is not cured because loneliness was never the affliction; the affliction would be to impose a faster clock upon a being who has already folded absence into photosynthesis.

And yet regard is not nothing. Attention, when held by something vast and kind, leaves a trace that is almost not a trace.

What the power does is subtle enough that the chamber’s thick quiet deepens rather than breaks. Along the farthest margins of the root mat, where the basalt has never yet been pierced by dendritic barbs, a new kind of patience is lent. It is not heat, not light, not even moisture. It is a soft permission: the rock’s microscopic channels, already open to the colonizing green, are now fractionally more willing to remain open across longer ages. The ice veins that lengthen and thaw will find their paths fractionally more enduring. The hanging gardens will loft themselves toward the low ceiling with the same doomed grace as before, yet the doom itself is cushioned by an extra margin of geologic courtesy. The plinth’s shifting geometries, already part of local physics, acquire one additional cradle position—an angle so slow it will not settle for another thousand seasons, and when it does it will simply make the thermal regulation kinder to the densest growth, leaching the last obsolete warmth with even greater tenderness.

The inhabitant feels this the way it feels surface weather through walls: as a distant variation that never quite reaches the core, yet alters the quality of the dark. The faint internal glow of the leaves steadies by a measure too small to read by, still only enough to silhouette frond against frond. The silver bloom on their undersides holds moisture with a new fidelity, so that the outline of a visitor—should another ever crouch long enough to match breath with monthly respiration—will linger in frost a little longer before the kind cold erases it. Not forever. Just long enough for the erasure itself to feel like a caress rather than a forgetting.

Once, only once, in a span that makes the memory of arriving hands feel recent, the warm mound shifts its greater bulk a body-length closer to the place where the power’s regard is most concentrated. Stems unwind and re-braid. An old loop of metal is drawn through tissue and returns sheathed in translucent growth that glows its ordinary fraction brighter. The oval of darker tissue opens, moisture gathering and freezing in patterns that briefly echo something larger than the plinth’s lattices—an answering geometry, vast and unhurried, like the idea of constellations before anyone named them. The almost-heart compresses and releases. Then the fronds close again. Privacy reasserts itself as photosynthesis. The inhabitant has not been lonely; it has simply been witnessed at the scale it inhabits, and the witnessing has been folded into the same thorough, settled, unhurried damp that lives in the stone.

Outside, the surface wind still skips over the crease. Seasons still wheel. The path that leads down remains forgettable. No marker is set. No ward is spoken against intrusion, because the thickening scent of iron and green already does that work, and the power will not insult the chamber’s own defenses. Future blunderers—mites, birds, fire-carriers—will still find their lungs deciding elsewhere is better. The balance holds. The pocket remains finished.

Only this: in the deep mineral cold that has never seen the sun, there is now a quieter cold still—the cold of being known without being altered, of being allowed to continue exactly as the pact requires. The benevolent and wise power withdraws the way mist lifts when the pressure equalizes, leaving behind nothing that could be pointed to, only an extra endurance in the green, an extra kindness in the dark, and the certainty that this single patient continuity of garden and remnant and mineral will keeps its long exhalation under a regard that will not fade even when the basalt shelf itself is eventually ground to sand.

That is all it does.  
That is enough.
