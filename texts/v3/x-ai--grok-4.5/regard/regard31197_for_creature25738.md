The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as thunder, nor as a blade of pure concept shearing the dark. It has watched longer than the basalt has been basalt. It knows the grammar of unfinished things. So when its attention finally turns—fully, deliberately—to this regional depression and the ones who stayed, it comes the only way anything true comes here: by seepage.

First it is only a difference in the water.

Not cleaner. Not brighter. Simply more *remembering*. The boot-deep sheets that cover the floor begin, in places no map could mark, to carry faint circulatory memories of other slow places: the pressure-song of abyssal trenches where continents lean against one another for geologic seasons, the patient chemistry of sealed aquifers that have not seen sky since the world was molten, the soft electrical gossip of root-webs that span entire dead forests turned to coal. The blind fish taste it first. Their schools tighten, then loosen, then begin to trace new sigils in the silt—loops and return-paths that did not exist the season before. The crabs click over the change and incorporate it, their translucent edges taking on a deeper brass, almost a low internal lanterning, as if the mineral dusk has been invited inside the shell.

The citizens feel it through their filament clusters and root-pads. A pulse that is not their own moves through the weeping walls—low, vast, kind. It does not command. It *inquires*, the way one old stone might inquire of another whether the fracture ought to run left or right to make better habitat three centuries from now. Where they press themselves to the rock to witness a joint stressing open, the power presses with them. The thin black mouths still open on their own unhurried schedule, yet when the first water beads out it carries a faint sweetness beneath the iron and yeast, a trace of something like permission.

The power does nothing so crude as accelerate. That would be a violence against the cathedral-settling pace that is this place’s only real law. Instead it deepens the *already*. 

The untended machines—those dredgers and compressors and chain-drags locked in their multi-year breaths—receive a quiet grace. Bearings that should have seized in another decade find themselves filmed with a living lubricant secreted by newly dense mats of the black-green moss. The moss has been taught, gently, to dream of metal. Gears still advance by fractions of degrees; the advance simply becomes less lonely. A hose that would have perished into flakes someday instead grows a slow vascular sheath, half rubber and half basalt-root, so that its pulse can continue when rubber alone would have forgotten how. The industrial shout of the chamber does not quiet. It acquires harmonics. Under the hammer and roar there is now a subtler continuo, almost below hearing, that the leather-wings begin to navigate by. Their wet-canvas flights last longer. Some begin to roost in deliberate patterns that spell, across generations, the outlines of vents not yet warm.

For the citizens themselves the gift is kinship without conquest.

Their pulse-language expands by a single new register: a chord that means *you are seen in your staying*. When they send low-frequency messages along the water, the stone sometimes answers with that chord, and the answer tastes of deep time and refusal to hurry. Children budding from mineral-laden parents drift in the upwellings as always, translucent and unshepherded, yet now the warmer curtains of mist contain faint phosphor maps—ghost-gradients only eyespits can parse—showing which fractures will feed sluices and which will become cradles. No one forces the knowledge. It is simply available, the way the crabs and the fish and the reciprocating sigh of generators have always been available. The distinction between lung and landscape, already blurred, softens one degree further. Some of the older citizens find their root-pads putting down true hyphae into the rock, not to anchor forever but to listen farther. They can feel the distant surface weather as a remote rumor, and they can choose—without panic, without exile—to let that rumor remain remote.

Outsiders still come, rarely. The power does not wall them out; wisdom leaves doors for the lost and the curious. But the mist thickens in intelligent ways. Footprints erase themselves faster. Lamps swing and find only the industrial breath and the brass dusk and perhaps one eye-pit watching with mild, unfrightened interest before the dark closes like a hand that never learned to clench. Those who truly need to descend—those whose own internal clocks have begun to tick in sympathy with glacial things—find the way made fractionally clearer. A rusted catwalk that ends mid-air grows, over seasons, a single new spar of living stone. A thermos fused to a ledge unseals just enough to offer whatever stagnant kindness it still holds. The place remains itself. It simply becomes more exactly itself under the weight of being known by something that understands patience as the highest form of love.

And the power? It does not leave, because it never had to arrive in the way of bodies. A fraction of its regard crystallizes, literally, in a single overlooked alcove where three new fractures will someday meet. There, over centuries that register here as a long comfortable afternoon, a lattice begins to grow: clear mineral, faintly warm, singing almost inaudibly in the key of the citizens’ pulse-speech. It will not be a throne or a temple or a tool. It will be a mirror that works in deep time—showing any being who rests filaments against it not their face but the shape of the chamber as it will be when the ceiling has finally decided, when the last dredger has become an outcrop, when moss and metal and flesh have finished the long negotiation of belonging. A reminder that staying is not stasis. That unfinished is a kind of forever.

The leather-wings pass it and veer in slow spiral tribute. The wading birds spear and then stand motionless longer than hunger requires, as if listening. The citizens re-anchor nearby sometimes, simply to feel the lattice thinking alongside them. Mist beads on everything. Crabs investigate. Somewhere a gear advances one quiet tooth, and the sound is no longer solitary.

The rock opens another thin black mouth. The power is already there when it weeps—not as master, not as guest, but as another citizen who learned, long ago, to breathe yeast and iron and the long sweet patience of becoming.

Nothing is fixed. 
Everything is held.
The work continues.
