The power noticed first as a pressure in the deep grain of things—an attention without eyes, a regard without weight. It had drifted the long corridors between stars and the shorter ones between heartbeats, collecting unfinished sentences the way the alpine bowl collected mist. When it turned toward the raw basalt and the pale rhyolite, toward the slow green argument of moss and the mineral lace forming on living collarbones, it felt something like recognition.

Here was a place that refused haste. Here were beings who had accepted the mountain’s suggestions without demanding a final draft. The power, being wise, understood at once that completion would be a kind of theft. Being benevolent, it felt no urge to correct, only to companion.

It did not descend in fire or voice. It did not raise walls of wind or rewrite the channels. Instead it settled into the unfinished courtesy already present, the way a new seepage finds an old crack and simply continues the weeping.

What it did was small, and mostly invisible.

It lengthened, by a fraction thinner than a single season’s moss advance, the moment when water forgets itself and climbs. Not enough to startle. Just enough that the older ones, humming at the plaza basin, sometimes felt the film carry their fern tips and copper rivets a hand-span farther toward the rim before gravity remembered its manners. In that extended breath the offerings dissolved more completely, becoming part of the mineral milk that would later honeycomb both cliff and shoulder. The conversation deepened by one quiet syllable.

It taught the echoes a gentler delay. Now, when a song left a doorway at the hour of flat gray light, the returning phrase arrived not merely late but slightly altered—softened, as if the stone had tasted it and chosen to keep a private harmonic. The singers never spoke of this. They only paused longer before the next verse, listening with the patience of people who already knew the bowl was learning them in return.

It touched the cyanobacteria and the moss cushions with a near-nothing of encouragement: a warmer chemical patience in the grit, so that the black felt advanced a finger’s width farther each decade across the youngest fractures, and the deep emerald cushions grew dense enough to hold a little more water against the thin air. The beings harvesting in rotation found the taste unchanged, yet noticed that the bare stone between their green islands gleamed a little less sharply, as if the land’s slow courtesy had gained another degree of kindness.

Mostly, the power practiced restraint so profound it became a presence. It turned aside a future rockfall that would have been too sudden, too loud. It eased the path of a lone kite so the bird’s shadow crossed the bowl less like an omen and more like a moving punctuation mark. It thickened the perpetual cloud just enough that explorers from the warmer, faster lands below would feel their urgency dissolve into damp and quiet long before they reached the rim; most would turn back, remembering only a dream of empty avenues and the sound of water practicing speech. Those rare few who arrived moving at the proper pace—boots already leaving prints the mist claimed—would find the length of dry cane-fiber cloth and the cake of black paste waiting, and would be welcomed into the unfinished as if they had always been expected.

At night, when the small hearths constellated the terraces, the power sat among them without body. It listened to the glacial groan, the cane’s dry vocabulary, the single hummed notes that rode the bowl’s curve. Occasionally it answered—not with words, but with a resonance that rose from the bones of the earth itself, a note so low and long that only those whose skin had already taken on the chalky bloom could feel it in their teeth and sternum. The note said nothing that needed translation. It said only: I see the sentence you are. I will not finish it for you. Keep becoming.

The beings did not invent rituals around this. They simply continued. Walls rose another course. Children sharpened into polish. Abandoned houses opened themselves to fern and liverwort. The mild wrongnesses remained mild, and beloved.

Generations later—counted in the rounding of outcrops and the lift of the basin by successive finger-widths—the oldest sometimes stood very still in the mist and felt themselves briefly indistinguishable from stone, from water, from the quiet regard that had chosen not to reshape them. In those moments they understood, without needing to sing it, that the benevolent and wise power had done the most difficult and generous thing possible:

It had noticed them completely,  
and then left them free  
to remain  
unfinished  
together.

The bowl held the power as lightly as it held the rain, the moss, the mineral lace, the long-legged walkers, the climbing water, the echoing songs. Everything still becoming. Everything still politely spaced. Everything still listening.

And the power, having followed its own heart into that slow green patience, stayed—not as ruler, not as savior, not even as guest—but as one more unfinished courtesy in a place that had room for it.
