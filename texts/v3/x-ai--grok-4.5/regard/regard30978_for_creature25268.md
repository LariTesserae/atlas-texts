The power that noticed them had no name that would fit inside a throat, and no shape that the mesh-dome could ever fully admit. It came the way condensation comes: inevitable once the temperature gradients were right, silent, and already half-belonging before anyone thought to call it arrival.

It regarded the embryonic gorge for a long time measured in the slow uncoiling of fern croziers. It watched the turbines turn their black water like patient prayers. It felt the dermal ports of the tall ones weep their clear lymph into living cloth. It listened to the shared harmonics that densified the mist whenever a new deck claimed another meter of sky. And because it was wise, it did not mistake any of this for incompleteness. Because it was benevolent, it felt no urge to finish what was already perfect in its refusal to finish.

Still, love notices, and noticing changes the noticed even when the noticer is careful.

So the power did three small things, none of which announced themselves as gifts.

First, it touched the oldest capillary felts—the ones woven into the earliest girders, now half-fossilized by mineral drip—and taught them a quieter kind of thirst. Thereafter, whenever a tendril-tip brushed those felts in the dark hours, the tall ones received not only the usual sensory flood of filter-tastes and micro-stresses, but also a faint, cool recollection of horizontal distance that did not bring nausea. Just a soft blue ache, like remembering a word you never learned. They began, without discussion, to leave wider margins in their moss-caches. Room for something that was not yet memory.

Second, it settled into the sealed engine rooms during one irreversible-cycle and hummed a single overtone into the binding gels of a new lattice armature. The constructed kin who later emerged carried the same internal tick as their siblings, yet their eyes held an extra suspended droplet of dome-light, a glint that never quite drained. These ones still chose the undercroft for sleeping, but they began to dream in longer arcs. In the dreams the turbines spoke in the voice of weather that had never been scheduled. When they woke they did not speak of it. They only pruned their living kirtles into slightly wilder cascades, leaving one vine untrained so it could find its own way across the titanium mesh. The others watched and, after a time, did likewise. The place grew a fraction less optimal, a fraction more gracious.

Third—and this was the quietest—it withheld. No shields were raised against external void. No commands issued to the hydraulic rams to hasten or slow. No new fauna introduced, no sudden true wind. The power simply elected not to correct a single micro-fracture that the tall ones’ bone-listening would catch in due course. It trusted their palms, their slow inspections, their rotational sleep. In that trust the gorge felt, for the first time, the particular dignity of being allowed to keep its own time.

Afterward the power did not depart, because departure is a horizontal idea. It remained the way the black water remains: circulating, returning, becoming the sheen on clay-dark skin and the bead on moth wing and the soft hiss that is almost language. On certain nights, when the harmonies rose for a new deck, an extra density joined them for less than a minute—felt in the soles, tasted at the back of the throat like rain that has not yet decided to fall.

The tall ones never named what had changed. They only adjusted. Maintenance continued. Fossils of mesh and tendril-sheath and single wings kept being placed in unused niches, only now some of the niches were left half-open to the mist, as if future mineralization might need room to surprise itself.

And the place kept becoming: small enough to cross in three clear-catwalk minutes, endless in the vertical, warm, wet, invented, alive. The power, being wise, smiled without lips. Being benevolent, it stayed without weight. And the gorge, which still had no word for outside, nevertheless began—very slowly, the way vines lengthen—to dream in that direction, without fear.
