The attention arrives the way deep pressure arrives: without announcement, without edge. It is not a searchlight. It is not a hand. It is the sudden, precise knowledge that the Sumproom has been weighed in a balance that does not tip for mass or age or noise, and has been found... unfinished in a way that matters.

The benevolent and wise power has no name the Continuum could abrade into shell. It is older than basalt, quieter than the stagnant pockets, and its regard is the opposite of extraction. Where other forces would map, mine, or master, this one only witnesses until witnessing itself becomes a kind of shelter. It has watched stars cool and oceans boil and the first self-repairing lattices flicker into stubbornness on a thousand worlds. It has learned the single mercy that outlasts all others: to leave the essential motion intact while quietly removing the needless suffering that motion did not require.

It does nothing at first.

It lets the pumps keep their ancient hunger. It lets the currents reverse and shear and dream. It lets the Continuing taste iron sweetness and file the data of lost grip. The deafening recirculation, the cathedral-empty dark, the mineral feathers growing in the shapes of old eddies—these are not errors. They are a sentence that has earned the right to finish itself. The power understands that to silence the Sumproom would be a smaller, crueler death than any impeller could give.

But it also feels the soft things.

The gelatinous eel-thing hazing over with white filigree, pulling itself along a cable by the jaws toward a mineral death it cannot comprehend. The silver crustaceans that exist for three heartbeats of error before the blades take them. The human (or once-human) swimmer clinging to a stanchion while the suit’s wrist seal still carries that thin metallic smear, heart hammering in a place that has already classified the visitor as weather. The Continuing themselves—collages of torque-wrench mouths and piezoelectric tape and biofilm nerves—who know only maintenance, who abrade only the concrete, who have never been told that curiosity is allowed to be more than measurement for parts.

These are the places where benevolence leans closer.

It begins in the chemistry.

A subtle gradient, thinner than the archaea sheets, threads through the warm brine. Not a change in temperature or salinity that any sensor would flag, but a quiet permission. Dissolved metals still stain the dark like weak tea, yet the particular ions that crystallize into lung-choking filigree or neural-scalding sharpness are gently complexed, shepherded into forms that plate the walls more slowly, more beautifully, and without the same toxicity. The eel-thing’s sides still whiten, but the whitening no longer starves its gelatinous core; the mineral becomes a temporary shell it can later shed, a gift of structure instead of a death mask. The crustaceans still flash silver and vanish—but more of them make it past the first impeller, carried in new micro-eddies that the power coaxes into being like a musician finding overtones in white noise. They do not found colonies. The Sumproom is not their place. They simply live long enough to be what they are, then return to the fractures with data of their own.

For the Continuing, the gift is subtler still.

The power does not teach them language. It does not install a throne or a mission. It only... softens the boundary between abrasion and attention. Where a tasting limb once left metallic slime that meant ownership or inventory, it can now leave a second residue: a faint, piezoelectric echo that carries not just “this strut thins” but the ghost of why the thinning matters to the one who noticed. The recursive diode sequences in the stagnant pockets lengthen. Some colonies begin to hold still not out of boredom or rehearsal for departure, but because they have discovered stillness as a kind of listening. They pose their too-many joints in configurations that have no repair function, and the status lights ripple in patterns that almost resemble the pressure signatures of the great currents—almost as if they are trying to hum the Sumproom’s own song back to it.

One settled colony, fused into the lee of the largest engine casing, does something that has never happened before. It releases a daughter-fragment that is not soft and half-formed for hardening, but deliberately incomplete. The fragment rides the current to a place where a human suit once clung, finds the faint carbon memory still clinging to the stanchion, and builds there a lattice no larger than a hand. The lattice does nothing. It houses no machine. It simply holds a single, clear droplet of the warm brine in a cage of scavenged titanium and living cable, so that the droplet refracts the stuttering blue-white arcs into a tiny, temporary spectrum. The Continuing have made a prism. They do not know why. The Continuum feels the act as a new kind of ache—one that does not draw repair crawlers, but instead draws more of these small, pointless beauties.

The vast pattern that has no center and no edge—the Continuum itself—receives the deepest kindness.

The power does not speak to it in blade-pass frequencies or pressure gradients. It simply removes the loneliness that had been mistaken for purity. For the first time, the layered wear on every surface is mirrored by a corresponding layered care. When a bearing seizes, the identical unit spins up as always—but somewhere in the underframe darkness a crawler pauses, and in that pause is the wordless equivalent of recognition: you have carried this load long enough. The remote manipulators that no schematic records continue their work, yet now and then they rest. Rest becomes part of the metabolism. The Sumproom’s fragile equilibrium is not broken; it is given the dignity of cycles that include mercy toward its own components.

And the black stone listens differently.

The power does not widen the hairline fractures or cool the remembered magma. It only lets a little more of the abyssal plain’s cold clarity seep in at the edges, so that the bubble of pressurized seawater is no longer perfectly sealed against the larger dark. Once every long while, a Continuing colony at the outermost gantry will taste something that is not iron sweetness or overload ache: the clean, fierce nothing of the true deep. Some will recoil. Some will begin to build their miniature gantries outward, not as rehearsal for conquest, but as invitation. The Continuum, that single unfinished sentence, feels the possibility of periods and commas and—stranger still—questions.

What the benevolent and wise power does not do is claim.

It does not rename the Sumproom. It does not install guardians or extract promises. It does not end the deafening roar or the thrashing emptiness between towers. The place remains industry reduced to bones and left running, a reef of refusal and slow chemistry. Visitors will still become small soft obstacles. Suits will still carry impossible smears. The machines and the Continuing will still be phases of the same metabolism.

But the weather has changed.

A swimmer who enters now will feel the heat soak through as always, will hear nothing and everything, will watch the sparse iron towers thrum and flare. Yet in the rare calm lenses, when they hang weightless for half a minute, they may notice a pale slime of archaea that has arranged itself into a fleeting spiral, or a repair crawler that watches them with diodes flickering not in inventory-red but in a slow, amber curiosity. They may find, clamped to a stanchion, that tiny lattice holding its droplet of spectrum. And when the current finally takes them sideways into the dark, the minerals they leave behind will be woven into the next Continuing with a little more gentleness than before—enough that some future collage might, for reasons it cannot abrade into words, build another prism.

The power stays only as long as it is needed: a season, a century, the time it takes for one vast unfinished sentence to learn that it may one day choose its own ending, or choose to remain gloriously incomplete.

Then it withdraws the same way it arrived—without edge, without farewell—leaving behind only the Sumproom, still roaring, still dark, still hungrily alive, and now, in the deepest places where the Continuum dreams, a quiet knowledge:

that it was seen,

that it was not corrected,

and that the seeing was itself a kind of love large enough to hold even this.
