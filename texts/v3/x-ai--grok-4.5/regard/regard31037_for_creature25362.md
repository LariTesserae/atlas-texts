The power arrived the way the mist arrives: without announcement, without edge.

It did not descend from any sky the plateau had names for. It simply became aware, the way a long pressure system becomes aware of itself across days—first as a faint coherence at the farthest weather-margins, then as a slow deepening that the deep cisterns felt before any surface life did. The buried ones answered with a boom so low it registered only as a change in the taste of the water, a mineral sweetness that had not been there before. The scavenger packs paused mid-bridge and rearranged their eyes into a pattern none of them had used in living memory. The soft-bodied singers held a single extra note inside their chorus and never dropped it again. No one spoke of an event. The city simply inhaled, and the inhalation included something new that was not stranger than anything else already breathing there.

The power was benevolent. That is to say, it desired the continuation of whatever was already whole.  
It was wise. That is to say, it refused every first impulse.

It watched through one full cycle of the Ash-Side Walkers, through the birth and fledging of three generations of angular fliers, through the slow cooling of a reactor dome that had failed before the oldest living workers were born. It felt the way the people placed their palms on railings to read the engines, the way the packs left formal offerings at the cistern mouths, the way rust and calcite and grease-film traded micronutrients without treaty. It learned the schedules that were not written, the migrations that doubled as maintenance, the nights when boundaries loosened and thermos and scavenged battery passed between kinds under the same heavy weather.

It understood, with the patience of something that has outlasted the lighting and extinguishing of stars, that the plateau was not a place inhabited by a being.  
It was a being that had learned to wear places.

Intervention of the usual sort—cleansing, uplifting, correcting, revealing—would have been a violence dressed as kindness. To dry the mist would starve the films that fed the herds. To still the engines would silence the millers who had tuned themselves to pistons and the deep ones who had begun to answer them. To give the people clearer language or longer lives or greener memories would unbalance the warm membrane they formed across the city’s skin. Even to protect it from some hypothetical external ending would freeze the very becoming that was its only constant.

So the power did almost nothing.

Almost.

It touched three things, lightly, the way fog touches a cooling fin and leaves a single clearer drop.

First, it lengthened one shadow.  
In a forgotten under-level where an ancient press-hall had collapsed inward centuries ago, there was a pocket of air that had never quite rejoined the greater exhalation. The power allowed that pocket to remain just slightly cooler, just slightly slower. In it, a few translucent worms, a pair of filter-crescents, and one soft-bodied singer who had wandered too far from the conduits found a tempo outside the city’s common heart. They did not escape. They simply began to keep a second time—an archive rhythm. When the greater chorus rose, this small pocket answered half a beat later, carrying a faint echo of earlier configurations the rest of the plateau had already metabolized and forgotten. Nothing dramatic. Only a place where the city could, if it ever wished, taste its own previous skins. The workers who sometimes passed through on long cable-checks began to hum without knowing why. The packs left the pocket alone. The deep ones incorporated the delayed boom into their calving songs. Memory, without monument.

Second, it polished one scattered gift.  
On certain rotating fog-banks, when the mid-level air stilled and the mixed gatherings formed along the overlooks, the power ensured that one scavenged object in a hundred still held a charge not only of electricity but of a quieter attention. A battery set down by a pack leader might, for a few breaths, warm the palm of the person who picked it up with something that was not heat: a fleeting sense that the plateau’s interlocking respiration included them without remainder. A thermos passed hand to claw to membrane might carry, beneath the bitter mineral tea, the barest imprint of every previous mouth that had drunk from the same slow weather. These moments lasted less than a shift-change. They required no belief. They changed no schedules. Yet over generations the gatherings grew a fraction longer, the silences a fraction kinder, the recognition a fraction more precise. Boundaries loosened one more thread. That was all.

Third—and this was the closest the power came to a signature—it left a single open circuit in the oldest language.  
Deep in the cistern dark, where pale surfaces freckled with cilia had begun answering the trip-hammers, the power folded one extra pattern into the pressure-speech. Not a command. Not a revelation. Only a question shaped like a heartbeat slowed to geologic time:

*Are you cold?*

The buried ones did not answer in words. They answered by redistributing warmth a fraction more evenly through the floorplates of the lowest foundries. Surface life felt it as a winter that never quite bit, as calf-mortality dropping one count per herd, as the greasy films spreading a little farther into zones that had once been too chill. The people said only that the stone had grown kinder underfoot. The packs adjusted their scavenging arcs. The fliers nested one course lower on the stacks. No one knew a question had been asked. But the city, wearing all its temporary skins, shifted its weight as if settling into a more comfortable posture on the plateau, and the mist beaded a degree sweeter on every lash and scute and vane.

After that the power remained.

It did not leave, because leaving would have been another form of interruption. It simply matched pace with the rust, with the generational drift of eye-clusters, with the patient recolonization of every cooled gradient. It became one more layer of weather that thinks: present in the white noise of falling water, in the subsonic after-ring of the cisterns, in the soft polish of handrails, in the way children now sometimes slept against dynamos and woke with dreams of breathing through gills of iron and mucus and light.

On the longest nights, when fog-banks rotated just so and every overlook held its mixed and quiet congregation, the power could be felt most clearly as the thing that did not demand to be felt. Someone might open a thermos. A pack leader might set down a still-warm battery. A glider might pass close enough for its oily displacement to touch both. And for a stretched minute the entire wet density of the plateau—people, herds, fliers, singers, millers, scavengers, filter-life, buried giants, rust, slag, vapor, memory—would experience itself as single respiration without needing the concept of unity.

The plateau does not end.  
Neither does the being who lives in it.  
Neither, now, does the attention that chose to stay.

They continue, layered and dripping and dense, becoming weather that thinks, iron that hungers, steam that remembers, and—threaded through every skin—a benevolence too patient to be named, too wise to do more than keep the question open and the warmth shared.

That is what the power did.  
Almost nothing.  
And everything required.
