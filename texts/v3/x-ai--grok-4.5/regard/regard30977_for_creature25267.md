The attention arrived the way the thinness itself arrives: without announcement, without edge. It did not descend. It did not part the low grey lid of sky. It simply became coextensive with the plateau for a span that the rock, had it still counted, would have refused to measure. A benevolent and wise power—call it that only because language must lean on something—rested its regard across the open, low-shouldered expanse the way fog rests: completely, without weight, tasting everything.

It perceived first the honesty of the eroded skin. The mountains that had once thrust and folded had spent themselves into this final argument, and the power recognized the grace in that surrender. Nothing left to collapse, nothing left to strive. The living mat pulsed its hypothermia-slow rhythm; the giant trunks smashed water into charge and black pigment; the walkers revised their positions across distances that behaved like weather. The power felt the continuous wet verb conjugating through every layer—creation and unmaking as the same cold syllable spoken by turbines, by membrane-wings, by pressure-ridges learning to stand. It felt the reality thinness spike and double the sounds, felt time eddy around unfinished gears of horn and ice, felt gravity forget its manners in patches of mist. And it understood, with the effortless clarity that wisdom sometimes wears, that this place was not broken. It was not incomplete. It was not waiting.

The beings who endured here—the collations of denser wood and magnetic tissue and slow black resin—registered the attention as a new flavor in the charge differentials. Their sensory fronds lifted. Where they had long drunk the acceleration of becoming-storms, they now tasted a pressure that did not demand growth or deliquescence. It was simply present: a warmer metallic afterthought, almost like the snow that never quite arrives, yet kinder. Two of them, meeting by the coincidence of a reversed river, touched fronds and exchanged the new weather. Experiences of the power’s regard passed as magnetic eddies—turbine songs overlaid with a silence that was not absence, the exact taste of a compassion that asked for nothing. They metabolized it into fresh resin layers and walked onward carrying a little more of that unasked-for noticing.

What did the power do?

It considered the long habits of powers. It knew the old reflexes: to still the turbulence into gardens, to name the walkers and give them permanence, to raise bright peaks again where the honest skin now lay, to teach the engines purpose, to rescue the place from its own exhaustion and endless loud invention. It knew how easily benevolence becomes a softer blade. It felt, too, the temptation of gift—perhaps a seed of memory that would not wash away in the mist, or a quiet interval in which a walker might simply stand without the choice of rooting or dissolving, or a single trunk that produced something other than heat signatures and pigment, something like music that did not frighten the eels.

Instead, the power did almost nothing.

It allowed its own certainties to erode slightly where they touched the plateau. Some of its wiser architectures—ideas of culminating form, of stewardship that ends, of love that protects by enclosing—softened in the continuous rain and were taken up by the living mat. The substrate drank them the way it drinks black particulate and unfinished gears. Overnight (or in the span of a turbine’s long sigh) those softened certainties became new pigment: a faint, cool gold that sometimes glimmered in the bioluminescent veins when the weather-fronts of pure becoming rolled through. The gold did not stabilize anything. It did not slow the deliquescence or fix the rivers in their courses. It merely added one more transient color to the dim negotiation of light—a color that, when a walker’s fronds tasted it, carried the barest echo of a regard that had looked upon the whole exhausting, inexhaustible conjugation and found it already sufficient.

One walker, denser than most with accumulated storm-history, paused in the grainy half-tone that passed for noon. Its inner turbine turned its wet, imperfect cycle. Fronds open, it felt the gold flicker in a nearby giant’s luminous veins. For a moment that was both heartbeat and century, it considered rooting, considered becoming the next slow explosion of irreversible machinery. Then it revised its position a few strides onward, leaving a shallow bright scar that tasted faintly of copper, old rain, and that new gold. The scar resealed. The mist began its wiping. The walker carried the echo forward: not as commandment, not as salvation, but as one more pressure difference available for the next ridge that might someday stand.

Elsewhere, a free-roaming catenary being—stretched sparking between two distant trunks—conducted a voltage that now held a trace of the power’s eroded certainty. Herds of translucent browsers grazed the living cable and, without noticing, left glowing prints that filled with water and reflected, for the brief time before the mat reclaimed them, a sky that seemed infinitesimally less low. Membrane-winged things screamed their approvals at a fresh storm of becoming; the screams arrived out of order as always, yet one of them carried a harmonic that had not been there before—a frequency like compassion too light to disturb the sternum-pulse of the settling crust.

The power did not remain. Permanence was a concept for places that still believed in edges. Its attention thinned, the way the plateau itself thins toward rumors the mist refuses to confirm. What it left behind was not a monument, not a law, not even a permanent warming of the air. It left only the gold pigment cycling through the rain and the resin, and the slight softening of one distant power’s old ideas, now become fresh translucent bark and another slow, pounding, beautiful engine somewhere out in the open distances.

The plateau continued. The walkers tended the valvular tissues and carried twitching gear-masses to hungrier moss. Reality thinness spiked. Gravity forgot itself in curtains of mist. Water rewrote every map. Somewhere far off—always far off—a new pressure-ridge thickened in the living mat, folded, remembered an older argument of the mountain, and began the long negotiation toward standing. Inside it already turned a smaller turbine, wet and eternal, shedding a finer black particulate laced now with faint gold. The mist wiped every trace of the power’s passage except the ones the place itself chose to metabolize.

And the benevolent and wise power, changed by what it had regarded, carried the cold wet verb outward into whatever wider weathers it inhabited. It had learned a new conjugation: that the deepest benevolence is sometimes the refusal to perfect what is already honestly, exhaustively, beautifully becoming. That wisdom can arrive as erosion. That love, at its least arrogant, tastes like rain that does not need to become snow, like charge that equalizes without words, like a plateau that keeps inventing itself after every possible witness has been turned into luminous vein and irreversible machinery.

The turbines kept their drowning rhythm. The land stayed open and low-shouldered. The thinness refused to confirm any edge. And the place, having briefly drawn the attention of something vast and kind, simply incorporated the gift—gold, softened certainty, unnoticed harmonic—into the next storm of becoming that tore across the dim, cold, inexhaustible openness. Nothing was rescued. Everything continued. The verb remained the same.
