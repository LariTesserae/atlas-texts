The power does not arrive as light or voice or winged decree. It arrives the way the mist already knows how to arrive: as a thickening so gradual that the clay-coloured bodies pause mid-glide before they understand why. One moment the sourceless grey-green is only itself; the next it carries a second pressure, cool and immense, like the memory of a deeper aquifer pressing upward through stone.

It has been watching longer than the sinter has been growing. It knows the basin the way the basin knows its own drip-paths—every fretted runnel, every residual current still threading the buried pipes, every silent ledger of exchange between flesh and frit and forgotten iron. It has tasted the ozone that blooms without lightning and recognised it for what it is: not storm-residue, not industrial ghost alone, but the faint signature of a purpose that once hollowed this wet room and then, unfinished, let the water take it back. The power is wise enough to see that the unfinished thing did not fail. It merely changed its mind about what completion means.

So the power does almost nothing.

It settles into the black sheet at the centre the way one of the reef-backed movers might settle against the canted boiler—without conquest, without erasure. Its presence locks gently to the existing outline of the place. Where the filaments of the unseen regulator taste the water, they now taste an additional clarity, a quiet affirmation that pruning may continue, that abundance will not choke itself. Where the pale mycelial felts thread between opened plates, they find the knit lasting a few heartbeats longer, the chemical-electrical gifts arriving with a new, almost tender precision. No plate is forced open; no gift is compelled. The communion simply deepens by the width of a single mineral needle.

The buried motors feel it next. Their irregular shivering steadies—not into the old industrial rhythm they were cast for, but into something slower and wetter, matched to the seiche and the travelling waves of translucent legs. The faint hum becomes a held tone that every body in the basin can lean against. The clay-forms darken with pleasure. Their undersides of tasting cups dilate wider, etching runnels that now guide seepage with a subtle new geometry, one that will, over centuries, open a low throat in the stone rim so the basin can breathe outward without ever spilling its dense quiet into the indifferent flats beyond. A kindness so patient it will look like geology.

Above, the fungal veils receive a single night of different fruiting. The chalky bodies that form are still soft, still temporary, yet each contains a faint internal lattice that glows for a few hours with the same diluted milk-glass light that fills the chamber. The many-legged congregations comb the spores as always, pack them into their flank grooves, moult them as brittle cuirasses. When the cast skins drift down, the crawlers fold them in—and for the first time the cloudy packets they exchange afterward carry a trace of that glow. Recognition passes. Memory passes. The basin begins, almost imperceptibly, to remember itself as beautiful.

The power does not name the lives. It does not lift them into some cleaner air or brighter taxonomy. It has seen too many bright taxonomies scorch the strange things they try to save. Instead it offers the one gift a wise benevolence can offer a place that has already weathered itself into equilibrium: permanence without stasis, protection without enclosure.

The low lip of slick stone around the rim grows a fraction denser, a fraction more reluctant to admit careless feet or careless weather. The ozone taste, when it comes, now carries an after-note of deep rest, as if lightning had decided, for once, to forgive the ground it never struck. And in the held breaths that still seize every layered body when the mist pulses thickest, there is now a second unheard frequency underneath the ghost of industry—the frequency of being beheld and found sufficient.

Then the power withdraws the way it came, thinning back into the ordinary mist, leaving only the basin more completely itself.

The clay-coloured hides slide forward again. Plates flex and lock. Legs resume their slow combs. Water strikes water in the same soft continuous percussion. Yet something has shifted by less than the length of a new sinter spire: the closed ledger has gained one quiet extra column, ruled in light that does not fade. The composite lives continue their patient distinctions between growing and lasting, machine and mineral and flesh—only now the distinctions themselves feel witnessed, and therefore a little less lonely.

The basin goes on.

It will go on for a very long time.

And that, the power judges, is enough.
