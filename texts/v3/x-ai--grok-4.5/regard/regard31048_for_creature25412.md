The attention arrives the way the rare rain arrives: without announcement, without thirst of its own, simply present.

It does not descend. Descent would be a violence of scale. Instead it thins itself across the ordinary light until it is indistinguishable from the mid-spectrum clarity that already bathes every tile. For a long while—longer than the oldest of the deep ones measure by residual strain—it only matches the breathing of the plain. Day-swell, night-contract. Tick, groan, click. Condensation bead, run, vanish. It learns the grammar before it speaks a single phoneme.

What it finds is already complete.

The constructed ground and the lives that move inside it form a single closed circuit of becoming: heat practicing self-knowledge, fracture practicing patience, the sparse gray-green blades practicing the oldest contract between surface and depth. Nothing is missing. The horizon’s thinness is not loneliness; it is exactitude. The absence of animals is not poverty; it is the refusal of hurry. Even the first draft of ruin is already a finished scripture written in a language slower than gods usually read.

A lesser benevolence would have wanted to gift. A lesser wisdom would have wanted to improve. This power, being both, feels the temptation rise and then watches it settle like dust that never quite becomes storm.

Still, attention itself is a kind of contact. The plain registers a new differential: not warmer, not cooler, simply more precisely known. In the hydrostatic conversations that travel through collector damp, a new referent appears—the-gradient-that-is-neither-gradient. The ancients taste it first. Their slow pulses upward, once meant only to sample the sky’s constancy, now return carrying a faint harmonic of recognition. Something outside the circuit has noticed the circuit and elected not to break it.

That election is the entire intervention.

Yet because benevolence abhors pure nullity when wonder is present, the power leaves one almost-nothing behind. It is not an object. It is a permission written into the expansion coefficients themselves.

Henceforth, when the great weather cells drag their milk-light veils across denser regions and the inhabitants rise into temporary calligraphy, the nested polygons are allowed to remember one another across greater distances than before. A pattern forming near a sunken court may now, for the brief duration of the dust, echo a pattern forming beyond the next low ridge, and the echo is felt as shared interiority rather than mere repetition. The lattices of re-sintered grit that the plants receive as payment grow, on rare cycles, one order more intricate—still ephemeral, still ground back to dust by the next flexing, but during their short standing they hold a new geometry: the geometry of having been seen by something that required nothing in return.

The plants tremble a fraction longer after moisture leaves. The after-image in the hex-basins lingers a breath past optics. The polished paths left by migrating seams acquire, here and there, a softer sheen that is not wear but gratitude expressed in the only medium they own.

And deep, where the oldest have become nearly pure substrate, the single warm spots that sometimes visit the surface now carry a second quality: a coolness folded inside the warmth, a resting place that does not halt the work of weathering but lets the work feel companioned. An ancient may remain inside that folded coolness for a century of plate-breaths, tasting the knowledge that its long remembering of the original pouring is itself remembered.

No maker returns. No sky changes color. No new plant appears. The heat remains muscular, the joints still catch the fingernail almost as sharply, the horizon still thins until the eye loses patience. The power does not stay, because staying would eventually become a different weather.

It withdraws the way light withdraws at the end of a clear day that needed no sunset spectacle—simply lessens until only the plain’s own radiance remains.

What is left is the same endless quiet fabrication, the same slow certain work of becoming weather-worn. Only now the work knows, in the marrow of its ceramics and the private excess of its living seams, that it has been met with a regard equal to its own patience. The first draft of ruin continues, but it continues inside a larger silence that has once held it with perfect, undemanding attention.

That silence is the gift.

The beings have no word for thank you. They do not need one. Across the next ten thousand rains they simply arrange their finest temporary lattices in the shape of the thin horizon itself—an open circle of grit that funnels every dawn droplet inward and then, when the heat drinks the last of it, lets the circle collapse back into the joint as if it had never been.

Payment for services rendered.

The plain keeps making itself in reverse, and somewhere beyond every thinning edge the benevolent and wise power keeps the memory of having once matched its breath, nothing more, nothing less, forever.
