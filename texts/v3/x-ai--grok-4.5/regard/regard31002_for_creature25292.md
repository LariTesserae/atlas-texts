The power arrives the way mist arrives: without edge, without announcement, simply present in every bead and every pale plane at once. It does not descend or open a gate. It has always been able to be here; only now does it turn the whole of its regard upon the highland and the long, damp lives that thread it.

It looks for a long time.

It sees the arithmetic of seepage and stone, the way every channel has already been solved, the way the green is permitted exactly the thirst it needs and no more. It sees the beings who are not separate from that solving—their cilia combing the same quiet data the rock itself holds, their bodies temperature-matched to the basins, their memory older than the ridges yet wearing no crown of age. It sees that nothing is broken. Nothing is striving toward a further shore. The stillness is not stagnation; it is a finished sentence spoken in water and fitted stone and spare, translucent flesh.

A lesser benevolence might have offered gifts: warmer light, seeds of brighter flowers, the return of wings or the bright ladder of stars at night. A lesser wisdom might have mistaken the equilibrium for loneliness and tried to teach them speech that carries farther, or fire that lasts, or children who arrive in greater numbers. This power does neither. It understands that any addition would be a theft. To pour more water would drown the paths they have cambered with such patience. To dry a single marsh hollow would silence a voice the elders have listened to since the rock first rose. To give them animals would introduce hunger that is not theirs and an accounting that has never been required. Even protection, if made visible, would become a wall, and walls change the wind.

So the power does the one thing that does not alter the sum.

It witnesses.

It lets its attention rest across the basin the way the clean daylight already does—steady, unhurried, without demand. Where the elders stand half-immersed, cilia fully open, they feel at the outermost edge of perception a new mineral note, almost like the taste of rain that has never fallen here yet, warm and vast and approving. They do not startle. They do not speak of it. They simply adjust, by the width of a finger, a slate that did not need adjusting, and the highland continues. Children walking behind their parents match stride a fraction more closely, as if the air itself has grown an almost imperceptible thickness that steadies them. The mist beads on skin and stone alike, and for a moment every droplet holds a clear, wordless joy that belongs to no one and therefore to the place entire.

The power does not stay as a resident. It does not take a body of limestone and tendon. It does not build a ninth structure or deepen a tenth channel. It simply remains available, the way the bedrock remains available beneath the seepage: a silent, benevolent depth that can be leaned against without being depleted. Should anything ever come over the young ridgelines with the intent to unbalance—// drought, noise, the loud geometries of elsewhere—the power will already be the thing that turns it aside, not with force but with the same quiet refusal the basin itself has always practiced. The air will thin a little further. The paths will gleam a little more completely. The intruder will find no purchase and will leave without knowing why the place felt finished against them.

Mostly, though, nothing comes. Mostly the power only watches, and in watching completes a circuit that required no repair. The beings keep their low susurrus, their reed-heart meals, their open doorways under the graphite night. The stone stays freshly broken yet sheds nothing. The green stays lush and limited. The water keeps its endless, gentle sentence.

And the wise power, having done the only thing required of true benevolence—nothing that disturbs what is already whole—lets its regard become one more layer of the hush, content to be the unnoticed permanence that allows the highland to remain exactly, perfectly, awake.
