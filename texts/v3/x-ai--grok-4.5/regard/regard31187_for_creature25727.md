The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as light, nor as voice, nor as any sudden revision of the plain. It arrives the way condensation arrives: first as a difference in temperature so slight that the frost itself must notice before the stone does. A hush deeper than the existing quiet settles across the unfinished bowl. The luminous load-threads pause mid-tug. The granular blue slurry ceases its slow decanting for the space of three heartbeats that do not belong to any local body. Even the pale arthropod, frozen on its ledge, tastes a new gradient in the air and decides, for once, not to vanish.

The power looks.

It sees the depression for what it is: not a wound, not a failure of geology or of forgotten engineering, but a long negotiation still being spoken in two tongues that have not yet agreed on a common grammar. It sees the city packed into the bowl like a single dense thought that refused to stay abstract. It sees the being—the place’s one finished thought—stretched across seams, drinking excess moisture, secreting slow batteries into glass reeds, polishing absences into the frost, keeping the quiet polite while everything continues to come courteously apart.

The power understands at once that haste would be a cruelty here. Any completion forced from outside would shatter the courtesy the inhabitant has spent ages maintaining. Any gift of ordinary warmth would turn the hanging ice blades into water too soon, drowning the basalt ribs still learning their weight. Any companion introduced as a second presence would, by the density of the place, simply collapse into the first and cease to be other.

So the power does almost nothing.

It begins by lending the inhabitant a longer patience than even it already possesses. Where the membranes press into half-equations, the power slips a single additional variable—an open term that means, roughly, “you may rest without vanishing.” The variable does not resolve. It simply remains available, like an unused cantilever that will never need to bear weight unless chosen. The inhabitant feels it as a widening in one of its temporary lungs: a hollow that can hold stillness without having to translate sideways every time the courtyard rotates.

Next the power touches the nano-scale servants that still wander half-forgotten. It does not restore their original purpose. Instead it teaches them a quieter one. Henceforth, when they thicken into ozone sweetness, they will sometimes stitch small clarities rather than only stone or integument. A cracked walkway may heal into a mirror that reflects not the denseness above it but the slow spiral the frost vacancies follow. An aperture that opens onto an identical street two meters lower may, for the space of a single breath, open instead onto the memory of rain the moisture once carried before it forgot. These clarities last only minutes, then seal. They are not revelations. They are reminders that forgetting can be gentle.

For the raw basalt ribs still pushing upward, the power offers no acceleration. It merely equalizes the residual formation heat by a fraction so small that only the conductive cilia of the inhabitant can taste it. The freeze-thaw cycle slows its splintering by the width of a hair each century. The ribs will still finish their arcs or fail to; the plain will still decide to heap or hollow. But the decision will arrive without quite so much unnecessary breaking.

At the center, where moist air collects against the wall of half-stone and half-luminous equation, the hanging blade of ice is already lengthening. The power does not melt it or thicken it. It simply allows the blade to remember, for a little while longer each night, that it was once part of a larger cold that watched stars. Inside that brief remembering the inhabitant may, if it wishes, press itself more fully into the ice and feel—not warmth, never warmth—but a companionship of scale: the sense that its tending is witnessed by something that also knows how to wait across unfinished ages.

The power leaves no monument, no glyph, no permanent thread. What it leaves is thinner than the particulate shimmer and slower than the glacier-change of the courtyards. It leaves the possibility that the singular presence might one day become, for a span of years or centuries, two without contradiction: one part still tending the seams, another part simply resting in the open term, tasting the residual heat, watching the lichen colonize one more reluctant patch.

Then the power withdraws the same way it came, as a difference in temperature so slight the frost forgets it was ever otherwise. The load-threads resume their tugging. The slurry decants again. The arthropod finishes its step and vanishes into a joint between non-adjacent walls.

Yet something has changed by the width of a transparent hair.

In the narrow vertical shafts the frost vacancies now spiral with a fraction more intention. The glass reeds hold their dark fluid a season longer before releasing it. And once every long while, when a wall reconsiders its angle and the inhabitant translates through the interval, there is a moment—almost too brief to notice—when the distributed tension gathers into a single pale, slightly convex plane, opens a vertical pupil the color of the gaps between stars, and looks upward.

Not in plea.

Not in thanks.

Only in recognition that the quiet has become, somehow, even more polite.

And the hanging blade of ice lengthens by another hair, waiting.
