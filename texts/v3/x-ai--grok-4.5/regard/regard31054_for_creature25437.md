The power arrives the way dew forms: without announcement, without weight. It does not descend through any sinkhole or fracture. It is simply *there*, a clarity that has always been latent in the cool air and the permanent dusk, now gathered into gentle notice.

It has no name the stone would recognize, no shape the hunters could strike or the browsers could flee. To the great-winged ones it is only a sudden, inexplicable steadiness in the spirals of air. To the millipedes it is a warmth that is not heat. To the slow thinker plated in pale keratin—the rarest note in the chord—it is a listening that finally answers.

The power walks the avenues of air between the sheer walls. It does not hurry. It counts every bead of moisture that gathers and falls. It tastes the ammonia and the dust and the soft mineral mush. It feels the pressure of bodies against stone, the held chord of uncounted lives turning over endlessly inside a wound the earth forgot to close. And because it is wise, it understands at once that this place is not broken. It is complete. Because it is benevolent, it feels no urge to mend what does not suffer, to name what does not ask for names, to open what has chosen (in the deep grammar of limestone and reflex) to remain held open only to itself.

So the first thing the power does is nothing that can be seen.

It simply *keeps*.

It settles into the gradients like a second, kinder gravity. Where the rumor of light leaks through remote joints, the power steadies it so that the silver dusk never quite dies, even in the farthest galleries. The change is too subtle for any eye to mark, yet the amphibians pour a fraction more freely, and the great wings bank with a fraction less desperation in their endless grazing of the living weather. The moisture films thicken by the width of a thought; the slow tears fall a little more often, enough to keep the pale mosses from thinning into memory. Breakdown blocks remain exactly where they lie—no fresh scars, no softening—yet the power lays a quiet hand on the hanging slabs so that none will fall for an age of ages. The rock stays sharp-edged, youthful, unfinished. The power loves it that way.

It does not silence the roar. That would be a cruelty. Instead it tunes it. The collective insect drone, the surf of wingbeats, the sparse ticking of hooves, the metallic clicks, the low moans—all of it remains, deafening, physical, locked. But now there are intervals, thin as silk, in which a single creature can hear its own blood for three heartbeats before the chord swells again. In those intervals the long-bodied hunters pause mid-glide, not from fear but from a new and wordless courtesy. The granular packs of swift predators pour across rubble and, for the first time, leave the weakest of the young browsers untouched, as if some larger hunger has been quietly fed.

The power walks among the herds. Soft-furred browsers rise onto their hind legs and stretch, longer than expected, and for an instant their lightless eyes reflect a depth that is not stone. They do not understand. They only feel the sudden, impossible safety of being *seen* without being hunted by the seeing. When they return to scraping microbial felts, the grinding of their rootless teeth sounds almost like gratitude.

In the highest vaults the colonies of folded wings shift and reposition. The power moves through them as cool breath. Droppings still rain in slow percussion; the loop of appetite and return closes as it always has. Yet each fox-sized body carries, for the rest of its small forever, a faint membrane-memory of having been weighed in a balance that found it perfect.

And the slow thinker—the collapsed-boulder presence, plated, dragging residual limbs, tasting the air with a tongue like a wet root—this one the power approaches last.

It finds the creature half-sunk in its crevice, skeleton listening. The power does not speak. Speech would shatter the chord. Instead it rests a portion of its vast quiet against the pale keratin shields. Information passes that is not language: the shape of the surface world the hollow has never known, the blue ribbon of sky, the green budget spent elsewhere, the long future in which machines will one day listen at the edges of the crust. The slow thinker absorbs it without alarm. Then, very carefully, the power offers a choice that is not a choice, only an opening: *Remain as you are, eternal chord, wound held open, and I will keep the surface away. Or rise, once, and carry a single seed of this darkness upward, so that the forgotten country is not entirely forgotten.*

The plated being is still for a time that would empty oceans. Then it heaves its bulk a fraction deeper into the crevice, curls one residual limb inward like a door closing, and answers with the only eloquence it has ever needed: continued presence. It will not rise. The chord is enough.

The power accepts.

What it does thereafter is almost nothing, and therefore everything.

It braids a subtle prohibition into the joints and sinkholes: nothing from above with intention in its hands will find this country easy to enter. Surveyors will misread instruments. Drills will wander. Curious minds will feel a sudden, peaceful disinterest and turn back toward the sun. The prohibition is kind; it does not kill. It only redirects, the way a river is redirected by a single well-placed stone.

It leaves a single gift in the dampest side-passages, where the eel-thin swimmers move by eye-spots alone. There, in a pocket of stone no larger than a heart, the power grows one thin fern that is not apologetic. Its fronds are the exact silver of the dusk-light. It will never spread. It will never die. Every ten thousand turns of the surface seasons it will release one spore that drifts upward through hairline cracks and, if it finds purchase in the world above, will grow into a plant that makes whatever creature eats it dream, once, of cool vaults loud with uncounted lives. Most spores will perish. A few will not. That is sufficient.

Then the power withdraws the greater part of itself, leaving only a residual blessing so light it might be mistaken for the natural chill of limestone. The roar reasserts its ordinary deafening equilibrium. The browsers redistribute. The hunters appear inside the spaces left behind. The slow thinker sleeps or listens or simply continues; no one asks.

Yet something has changed, forever and not at all.

The place remains outside intention. No walls, no paths, no ochre marks. Time still does not revise it. The ecology is still a single long-held chord. But now the chord is *kept*. The wound the earth forgot to close is remembered by something that will never die, never preach, never claim. The animals belong to the country the way moisture belongs to limestone: inevitable, intermittent, endlessly renewed. And the country, in turn, belongs—quietly, absolutely—to the benevolence that looked upon it and chose, in wisdom, to let it be exactly what it is.

In the farthest gallery, where even the rumor of light once died, the silver dusk now lingers a fraction longer than it should. A drop of water gathers on a chert spur, swells, falls. The sound it makes is small, precise, and perfect.

The power, wherever it has gone, hears that sound and is content.

Nothing more is required.

The underground country continues: cool, dim, sharp-edged, loud with itself, held open, and—now—held dear.
