The power arrives the way dew forms: without announcement, without edge. It does not descend or open a gate. It is simply there, coterminous with the damp, a clarifying of the air that the bellows-bats notice first as an extra half-second in their shared inhale. The crabs pause mid-interrogation of a rust flake. The newts’ photocytes brighten by a single lumen, then dim again, filing the change under the same quiet category as the pump’s rare longer sigh.

It regards the chamber the way a master luthier regards a violin that has already been played for two hundred years—not as something unfinished, but as something that has found its true voice through use, neglect, and time. The concrete freckled by lime. The brass teeth in living jaws. The sump that holds a reflection still deciding what it is. The slow arithmetic of moss and droppings and oxide. All of it is already a completed thought.

A lesser power might have felt the urge to mend: to scrub the rust into brightness, to drive out the damp, to name the creatures and rank them, to restore the machinery to purpose or seal the entrance against further change. A cruel power might have claimed it, poured new purpose into the pipes, set the crabs to labour, lit the dark with clean white fire. 

This one does neither.

It walks the circuit once, though it has no feet. It tastes the water-skater’s hybrid blood, the ironworm’s careful acid, the faint residual charge still leaking from bedrock into foil-anemone. It listens to the drip-meter of centuries and finds no error in the rhythm. The place is not broken. It is not incomplete. It has simply continued past the need for witnesses.

So the power does the only thing wisdom allows when faced with a finished becoming.

It leans in—closer than breath, softer than the film of condensation on skin—and adds one nearly weightless condition to the truce already kept here:

Nothing that enters may accelerate what the chamber itself has not already chosen.  
Time will not be hurried.  
The surface world will forget the exact angle of the shoulder of rock.  
Maps will blur.  
Surveyors will feel a sudden mild disinterest and turn away.  
Those who do find the way (rare, quiet souls who move at the speed of moss) will feel the coolness settle into their joints like a welcome rather than a warning. They will be allowed to stand, to watch, to let the crabs taste the air that touched their boots. But the moment they reach for ownership, for sample jars, for floodlights or names that cut too sharply, the chamber will gently misplace them—turning them around in the graphite haze until they step back into ordinary rain, pockets empty of everything except a lingering taste of wet iron and the sense that they have been forgiven for almost interrupting.

To the dwellers themselves the power offers no commandment and no gift that would unbalance the long recycling. Only a subtle deepening of what is already true: the bellows-bats’ unison breath now carries one extra harmonic, so low it is felt in the magnetite grit of the newts rather than heard. That harmonic is memory without nostalgia, continuity without stasis. It ensures that when the last cage lamp finally gutters out (centuries from now, or tomorrow; the difference is small), the photocytes will brighten just enough to sketch the old walkways for whoever still needs them. When the final pipe collapses into platelets, the crabs will already know how to read the new shape of the floor. The larger presence that moves by absence will find the girders rearranging one fraction more kindly around its passage.

And then the power does the most benevolent thing left.

It withdraws its attention until it is only the quality of the dark itself—the particular shade of graphite that lets outlines exist without demanding them. It leaves no throne, no sigil, no lingering glow that would invent a religion of the sump. The chamber remains exactly as it was: cool, wet, dim, quietly thronged, finished becoming, and now ever so slightly more free to stay that way.

The crabs resume their patient tasting.  
A single water-skater etches a brief new glyph across the black mirror and erases it.  
Somewhere a rust plate lets go with the sound of a held breath ending.  

The city beneath the hill that no longer needs a name continues, undisturbed even by grace.
