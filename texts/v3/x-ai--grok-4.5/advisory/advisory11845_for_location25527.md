**Prepare as if you are about to step into a warm, breathing machine that has forgotten it was ever built and now simply continues.**

The Soak (Low Stack, Down, the unresolved coordinate) does not welcome or reject. It incorporates. An ordinary human body arriving from a drier, quieter, more vertically modest world will be treated as another permeable surface, another source of heat and salt and motion. Preparation is less about conquering the place than about remaining coherent inside it long enough to leave again under your own power.

### Physiological and Sensory Baseline
Your body must be ready for unbroken warmth and unbroken wet. Skin will stay damp. Clothing will stay damp. Metal will never feel cold; railings and handholds will feel like the inside of a living mouth. Core temperature will rise not from exertion alone but from conductive heat rising through every floor and the industrial-geothermal background that never fully sleeps. Acclimate beforehand if you can: spend days in high-humidity environments at 28–34 °C with no relief. Practice sleeping in light, moisture-wicking layers while a fan pushes warm wet air across you. If you cannot do that, accept that the first 36–48 hours will feel like mild fever and perpetual post-rain stickiness.

Noise is a physical pressure. The cascade pumps’ white roar, maglev scream, reactive street-song, fauna calls, habitat-block thuds, and the subsonic throb form a continuous bath. Ordinary humans often experience this first as a low-grade panic or a pressure behind the eyes. Before arrival, expose yourself to dense, layered industrial soundscapes at high volume for hours while trying to hold a conversation, read, or rest. Learn to let the roar become a medium rather than an assault. Earplugs that purely block will isolate you dangerously; instead carry tunable filters or bone-conduction sets that can notch out the worst frequencies while still letting you hear the pitched-up human voices and the warning harmonics of a shifting spar. Many travelers report that after three days the silence of elsewhere feels like freefall; prepare for reverse culture-shock on exit.

Gravity is mostly reliable and mostly one g, but local lenses flicker. Loose objects (and briefly you) may lift half a meter for a few seconds. Children chase it; you should not, the first week. Train your vestibular system with mild, unpredictable acceleration if possible. Keep one hand free and a soft grip on something solid whenever you stand still on an open deck. The air above active lenses tastes of ozone and copper—treat that taste as a temporary altitude marker.

Constant fine moisture and mineral precipitation mean your eyes, nose, and throat will run more than usual. Surfaces sweat. Calcite veils turn rain into vertical rivers. Expect mild conjunctival irritation and a perpetual film on optics. Bring lubricating drops that do not wash away instantly, and accept that “dry” is a relative memory.

There are no plants. The word itself will start to feel antique. The green hunger some humans carry will not be fed. Compensate with whatever personal sensory anchors you need—scent oils, texture objects, recorded wind through leaves—if the absence begins to hollow you out. The city offers mineral veils, luminous needle-fish, folding gliders, and the slow mineral “lichen” of precipitation instead. Learn to find beauty in wet stone and sweating alloy or you will feel starved.

### Clothing and Body Interface
Dress for perpetual damp, abrasion against rounded ancient edges, and the need to shed heat instantly. Avoid anything that traps water against skin for long (heavy cotton, untreated leather, thick synthetics that sponge). Prefer quick-dry, antimicrobial, slightly hydrophobic base layers that still breathe. Multiple thin layers beat one thick one; you will strip and re-don as you move between climate-controlled cores and open gantries. Footwear must grip wet sintered metal, ceramglass, and dished stone stairs while allowing water to exit—perforated soles or self-draining designs. Many locals go barefoot or near-barefoot on certain levels; the floors are rarely sharp but they are never fully dry and they remember millions of feet.

A light, packable shell that sheds the fine rain and the vertical rivers of the calcite curtains is useful, but it must vent aggressively or you will steam inside it. Hats or hoods help with the drip from above and from fused skybridges. Gloves are optional for grip but will stay soaked; many travelers prefer bare hands after the first day so they can feel the polished memory of the handrails and the temperature of walls.

Skin itself becomes an interface. Locals use tight-beam skin-comms and rapid hand signs that leave brief afterimages in the humid air. If your multiverse kit includes temporary dermal relays or conductive lotions, bring them. Otherwise practice large, clear hand gestures that cut through visual clutter and learn to watch for the residual light-trails others leave. A small UV or polarized filter visor helps read those afterimages and cuts the soft multiple shadows.

### Navigation, Orientation, and the Living Layout
Maps expire. The city thickens, folds, braids, and reconfigures. Neighborhoods shift on roughly thirty-hour cycles so light (or its approximation) can reach deep courts. A walkway that existed at breakfast may be a market at midday and a crystalline spar by evening. Gravity-lens plazas, maintenance shafts, and the dark glittering mesh of canals are more reliable landmarks than named streets.

Prepare by training yourself to navigate by gradient and current rather than grid:
- Vertical value: slightly drier, quieter belts are higher-status and often better maintained.
- Water must move. Stagnant pockets are expensive and often contested or mechanically corrected.
- Sound and vibration: the subsonic throb, pump frequencies, and the particular scream of local maglev spines can serve as rough beacons once you learn them.
- Smell: hot metal, wet portfolio resin, the faint sweet note that might be food or the city cooling. Follow or avoid gradients deliberately.
- Fauna behavior: needle-fish schools act as living traffic data; glider flocks turn with updrafts and acoustic baffles; larger service-strata creatures avoid certain turbulence.

Carry a compact inertial unit that can dead-reckon through short losses of external reference, plus a simple chemical or biological marker kit (harmless, short-lived) so you can tag a route if you must return through a shifting zone. More useful than any device is the habit of frequent micro-orientation: every few minutes note the nearest calcite veil, the direction of water flow, the nearest fused skybridge cluster, and the quality of the light. Standing still is only another kind of motion here; the current will take you if you do not actively choose.

At the edges the density thins only relatively and then the city continues underwater. Do not go there casually. Lights move like deep schools; the quiet is different and not kinder.

### Health, Sustenance, Medical
Water is everywhere and belongs to whoever keeps it moving. Do not drink stagnant water. Flowing canal water, condensate from clean veils, and water processed through habitation systems are safer but still warrant filtration and sterilization. Carry a robust, low-power filter that handles mineral load, biofilm, and whatever organics the poikilohydric fungi and waste-heat films contribute in the deep strata. A small desalination or remineralization stage is wise; the ambient water is not ocean but it is not pure.

Food is printed, precipitated, grown in controlled cores, or harvested from the abundant adapted fauna. Needle-fish, certain glider proteins, and microbial films appear in market forms. Street stalls appear in negative spaces and vanish. The smell of hot metal and wet resin mixes with cooking. Bring a few days of dense, familiar rations for the arrival shock and for moments when the local proteins disagree with you. After that, eat what the floating stall-holders sell when they simply rise with a water-level change and keep trading. Watch what long-term residents choose after a gravity glitch or a fogbank avalanche; they know which vendors stay clean.

Medical kit priorities:
- Antifungals and broad antimicrobials (the deep strata fungi stay down, but wet joints and your own damp skin are opportunistic).
- Electrolyte and mineral balance support (you will sweat into a mineral-rich environment).
- Eye and mucosal care.
- Something for sensory overload and sleep in noise (chemical or device).
- Basic trauma for sparks, grinding plazas, and the rare failed spar: the city repairs itself with nanoscale nets and swarms, but your soft tissues do not.
- Motion and vestibular aids for the breathing shifts of the city and the lens flickers.

Any open wound must be kept meticulously clean and dry—an almost philosophical challenge. Sealants that bond in wet conditions are worth their weight. The city’s own runoff feeds slow mineral precipitation; small cuts can acquire a temporary calcite film if neglected. Some travelers lean into this and treat it as a badge; most prefer not to.

If you have internal chronometers or recording implants, note that the residual harmonics of the walls and the ancient systems can interfere or, conversely, be read by those with the right skin cycle. Patience and listening can sometimes extract memory of previous configurations directly from the structure. An ordinary traveler without that interface can still learn to feel the difference between a graceful shift and an impending grind by the change in the subsonic throb.

### Social Current and Behavioral Posture
Rules are prevailing currents. Water must circulate. Vertical space is dearer than horizontal. Anything that can be grown, printed, or precipitated from ambient moisture and minerals will be, repeatedly. Memory lives in the walls.

Speak in tight-beam or hand signs at street level; shouting into the roar is mostly futile and marks you as new. Markets are loud with commerce and louder with the physical fact of goods and bodies. When two elevated plazas grind together in sparks, repair swarms boil out and shouted deals continue around them—participate or step aside cleanly; hesitation in a narrowing gap is expensive.

Rich and poor are expressed as meters of slightly less saturation and half-decibels of noise reduction. Do not linger in the thin quiet belts without purpose or invitation. In the dense middle, abundance is the norm: fauna, stalls, heat, motion. Scarcity appears mainly as stagnation or as blocked vertical access.

Children chase weightlessness. Older residents check internal time and file glitches with maintenance boards that may or may not still answer. Match the local tempo: neither frantic nor rigid. The city does not keep still for anyone; neither should your plans. Build slack into every movement. Assume the route will thicken or fold.

Hand signs leave afterimages. Watch for them. Offer your own clearly. Touch is common on handrails and in crowds; the polished memory of millions of palms is part of the city’s continuity. Personal space is a luxury of the higher, quieter belts.

### Gear Loadout (Minimal, Robust, Wet-Tolerant)
- Moisture-management clothing system and draining footwear.
- Tunable hearing protection / bone conduction.
- Eye protection and lubrication.
- Compact water purification with mineral handling.
- Dense emergency calories and basic cook/heat that works in saturated air.
- Inertial navigator + simple markers.
- Wet-condition medical sealants, antimicrobials, electrolytes, sensory aids.
- Light, vented shell and a small dry-bag for the few items that must stay truly dry (documents, certain electronics, personal anchors).
- Dermal or handheld short-range tight-beam if available; otherwise disciplined hand-sign vocabulary.
- Soft grip gloves or callus plan; one strong carabiner or short tether for momentary anchoring during shifts.
- Small rechargeable power that tolerates humidity, plus a mechanical or chemical fallback light (the city’s own overcast of work lights, market lanterns, and genetics-barge flares is usually enough, but deep service strata and underwater margins are not).
- A personal scent or texture object if the absence of plants will matter to you.

Leave behind anything that demands dryness, silence, or large empty volume. The city will claim the volume and fill it with moisture and roar.

### Mental Framing and the Experience Itself
You are entering a riverbed that is also a machine that is also a dense animal. It is ancient the way a riverbed is ancient: constantly renewed, never the same twice, settled into its logic. Edges are rounded by weather and touch. Advanced systems past ordinary physics have been repaired in place until their makers are footnotes. Nanoscale nets knit microfractures. Gravity lenses hum below hearing. No one fully diagrams them anymore. They simply continue, the way the water continues.

Stand still on a mid-level gantry and the place will pour through you: mineral tear-tracks on sintered stone, the soft concussion of a habitat block locking into new alignment, silver flash of a glider flock turning as one, smell of hot metal and wet resin and that faint sweet note. Then the minute ends and the current takes you again.

Expect sensory saturation that can tip into awe or into exhaustion. Expect the deep pleasure of systems that work without needing your understanding. Expect irritation at the never-dry, never-quiet fact of the air. Expect to feel the city breathe under your feet—sometimes graceful, sometimes a shower of sparks and already-swarming repair. Expect markets to appear in demolished negative space and vanish when replacement structure grows through them like a slow crystalline tree. Expect to float a little when a lens flickers and to hear laughter or cursing according to temperament.

You will not see the horizon. Elevation never climbs far before the next district begins. The sky is milked silver or low brass. At night the city makes its own overcast. Shadows are soft and multiple.

Most people never reach the relative thinning at the edges of the depression, where calcite veils thicken toward cliffs of frozen rain and the water table finally wins. You do not need to. There is already more city than a lifetime can walk, denser than sleep, louder than thought, warm and wet and endlessly unfinished.

### Exit and After
When you leave, the silence will feel like falling. Dry air will feel aggressive. Empty space will feel wrong. Give yourself decompression time in an intermediate environment if your multiverse path allows it. Record what you can while the residual harmonics are still in your nerves; the walls of the Soak keep memory, and for a little while so will you.

Travel light enough that the current can take you without tearing anything essential away. Stay damp enough, quiet enough inside your own head, and vertically aware enough that you remain a coherent traveler rather than another seam for the city to seal with mineral film. The Soak does not care that you came from elsewhere. It will still let you pass through if you move with its weather, its water, and its unfinished density.

Go ready to be poured through. That is the only preparation that finally matches the place.
