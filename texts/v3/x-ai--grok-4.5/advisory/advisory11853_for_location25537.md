**Preparing as an Ordinary Human Multiverse Traveler for the Pocket**

You are not a god, not a native of pressure-realms, not a being whose body already knows how to dissolve into mineral chill and call it home. You are an ordinary human who has learned—through whatever doors, mirrors, equations, or accidents your particular multiverse path uses—to step between realities. This place will treat that ordinariness with exquisite patience. It will not attack you. It will simply wait until the distinction between “you” and “here” becomes an unnecessary courtesy.

Preparation is therefore less about conquest and more about remaining legible to yourself long enough to leave. The pocket does not punish the unprepared; it absorbs them so gently that the absorption feels like arrival.

### 1. Anchoring the Self Before You Cross

The greatest danger is not pressure or cold or the knife-edges of the fronds. It is the slow realization that you have stopped needing an exit. Time thickens. Space folds. The dense living tissue makes a room around your body that feels more intimate than any architecture you have known. Reality frays considerately. You must arrive already holding counterweights.

- **Carry three non-negotiable identity anchors.**  
  One must be physical and personal: a small object worn smooth by your own hands in your origin world—a ring with a private engraving, a scrap of fabric that still smells faintly of a specific room, a stone from a place that exists only in one timeline. Keep it against bare skin. In the pocket, touch it deliberately every time you notice the quiet becoming architectural.  
  One must be cognitive: a short, spoken phrase that has no meaning except to you, rehearsed until the rhythm is automatic. Something ugly or funny or banal works best—“the coffee was burnt that Tuesday,” “left sock first,” “red wheelbarrow.” Say it aloud (or mouth it if speaking feels wrong) whenever a frond uncurls in what feels like an afternoon.  
  One must be external and verifiable: a small locked box, timed beacon, or sealed note left with a trusted person or in a stable cache in a neighboring reality, set to open or transmit only if you fail to return by a pre-agreed window. The knowledge that something outside is still counting is a thin filament of “up.”

- **Practice deliberate temporal distrust.**  
  Before traveling, spend days deliberately mis-timing ordinary tasks: cook a meal while convinced an hour has passed when only ten minutes have, then check. Walk a familiar route while telling yourself the shadows are wrong. The pocket’s time pockets are not dramatic; they are polite. You need the habit of double-checking duration against something that cannot lie—your own heartbeat counted against a mechanical watch sealed against pressure, or a chemical timer that changes color at known rates.

- **Accept that memory will smear.**  
  Write a single-page “who I am and why I came” statement in waterproof ink on durable material. Read it upon arrival and at every subjective hour. Do not rely on digital devices alone; the darkness here has opinions about charge and signal. The statement should include one embarrassing detail and one unfinished obligation waiting in another world. Shame and duty are excellent adhesives for identity.

### 2. The Body Under Absolute Pressure and Mineral Cold

The depression sits so deep that pressure is a continuous slow thought. Ordinary human flesh is not built for it. Multiverse travel sometimes grants temporary tolerances, but never assume yours will cover abyssal compression plus the pocket’s own density seal.

- **Pressure and breathing.**  
  You need a sealed, positive-pressure exoshell or dermal reinforcement field rated far beyond standard deep-submergence gear—think of systems designed for the floors of hadal trenches, then add margin. If your multiverse kit includes biological adaptation (temporary gill-analogues, pressure-stabilized blood chemistry, reinforced alveoli), activate them early and test in intermediate high-pressure realities first. Carry redundant oxygen or oxygen-reclamation capacity measured in days, not hours; the stillness makes time elastic and you may stay longer than intended.  
  The medium is water and more than water. It will try to become your interior. Seal every orifice. Practice slow, shallow breathing rhythms in advance so that panic-gasp does not become habit when the quiet first settles.

- **Temperature.**  
  Steady mineral chill that never quite freezes. Insulation must handle prolonged cold without bulk that the dense vegetation will snag. Layered phase-change materials or active micro-circulation suits work better than simple neoprene. Protect extremities obsessively; numbness arrives as forgetfulness of the limb.

- **Movement and the packed garden.**  
  There is scarcely a hand’s breadth of open water. Fronds, mats, veils, filaments, jelly bladders, mineral lace—all of it sharp-edged and newly keen. You will not swim so much as slowly part and be parted.  
  Wear close-fitting abrasion armor with smooth outer surfaces. Avoid loose straps, dangling tools, long hair. Carry a short, blunt pry tool and a monofilament cutter that can part living tissue without tearing cascades of it onto you. Move like stalactite growth: decide, then inch. Sudden motion is both useless and rude to the place.  
  The plants are not hostile, but their margins cut. Expect slow lacerations. Pack coagulant gel that works in cold seawater and a method to close suit breaches from inside.

- **Sensory substitution.**  
  Light does not reach here. Vision becomes pressure-memory and reluctant phosphorescence. Train beforehand with total darkness and with sensory-deprivation tanks until the absence of light no longer triggers fight-or-flight.  
  Equip yourself with:  
  - Passive sonar or pressure-imaging that translates density gradients into spatial maps against your skin or a bone-conduction display.  
  - A chemical “taste” sampler for the water—mineral profile, dissolved organics—so you can detect when a fold has moved you into a neighboring identical chamber.  
  - A simple mechanical compass is useless; “up” is a rumor. Instead carry an inertial dead-reckoning package hardened against the pocket’s occasional non-Euclidean slides, and reset it frequently against your identity anchors.

Sound is nearly absent. The quiet feels built. Bring no music. Bring no recorded voices of loved ones on loop. They will sound like mockery after the first long stretch of nothing. If you need auditory contact, use a single low-frequency pulse you can trigger yourself—something that proves you are still an agent.

### 3. Navigating a Place That Refuses Ordinary Measurement

You do not enter so much as notice you have already been folded inside. Distances do not behave. A corridor of open water becomes the underside of a leaf. Angles slide. Darkness folds and briefly shows stacked identical chambers like sediment layers, then smooths shut.

- **Treat geometry as provisional.**  
  Never commit to a long straight-line plan. Move in short, closed loops that return you to a marked fragment of worked stone or metal lattice. Those built remnants are the closest things to fixed points; their corners are still sharp, their cut faces fresh. Tie a nearly invisible high-strength filament to one and pay it out as you explore, knowing the vegetation will try to claim it.  
  When the darkness folds and you glimpse the lattice of other darknesses, do not follow. Those are not exits; they are the pocket showing you its own recursion. Note the glimpse, reinforce your anchors, and continue in the single chamber you can still touch.

- **Marking and the problem of permanence.**  
  Ordinary markers dissolve into the density or are overgrown in subjective days that may be objective minutes. Use temporary chemical lights that react only to your specific touch, or ultrasonic tags that your equipment alone can hear. Better: memorize the exact silhouette of three different artifact fragments relative to your body and use them as a local constellation.

- **The exit condition.**  
  “You could leave if you remembered the direction of up.” Most stop trying. Therefore the direction of up must be an external artificial construct. Before entry, establish a weak, persistent multiverse tether—a quantum-locked filament, a probability breadcrumb, a keyed frequency that only resolves toward your origin when you intentionally activate a device you keep sealed until you are ready. Do not leave the activation to “when I feel like it.” Feeling like it is exactly what fades.  
  Set a hard chronological deadline tied to your external locked box. When the timer on your person hits the mark, you leave whether the garden has become beautiful or not. Practice the leaving motion in lighter realities until it is muscle memory: reach, activate tether, fold out. Hesitation is how the membrane thins and stays thin.

### 4. Psychological and Existential Fieldcraft

The weirdness seeps. It does not announce itself with monsters. The rare soft thumbnail bodies that drift past trailing apologetic light are almost irrelevant. Larger things remain distant pressure rumors. The real contact is with the completion of the density and the invitation to fray.

- **Do not romanticize the silence.**  
  It will feel sacred. It is not your sacred. Keep a small, private ritual of irreverence: every time the quiet becomes breathable, perform one deliberately clumsy human act—crack a joint, hum a scrap of advertising jingle, scratch an itch with exaggerated motion. Stay slightly awkward inside the perfection.

- **Limit communion with the vegetation.**  
  The fronds will pack around you until they make a kind of room. You may feel cradled. Resist the urge to stay still long enough for roots to knot through the idea of you. If you rest, rest with one hand on an artifact fragment and one hand on your identity object. Sleep only in short, guarded intervals if at all; the place does not sleep and will keep watch for both of you.

- **Artifacts and the withdrawn intelligence.**  
  The worked stone, the metal lattice sprouting rust-and-pearl leaves, the cleaved steps—these are not gifts or messages. They are residue. Touch them if you must; they are the only surfaces that remember deliberation. Do not try to complete them or repair them. Whatever shaped them has withdrawn completely. Attempting contact with the absence is how travelers begin to prefer edges that are deliberate and then forget they themselves were supposed to remain unfinished.

- **The gentle fraying.**  
  When surfaces forget which side is outside, when you watch a frond and later cannot say how long, when the boundary between miniature garden and infinite black ocean thins to a patient membrane—stop. Recite your phrase. Touch your object. Check your timer. The membrane may or may not still be there next time you reach out. That uncertainty is the pocket’s only real aggression. Treat every thinning as a last chance.

### 5. Gear Summary (Minimal, Redundant, Quiet)

- Pressure-rated sealed suit with thermal regulation, abrasion resistance, self-sealing.  
- Breathing/reclamation system sized for days plus emergency chemical oxygen.  
- Sensory substitution suite (pressure imaging, chemical sampler, bone-conduction status).  
- Inertial navigator + artificial “up” tether with dead-man and timed activation.  
- Physical identity object, written self-statement, spoken phrase.  
- Short pry/cutter, coagulant, filament marker.  
- Chemical or mechanical elapsed-time indicators immune to subjective distortion.  
- No unnecessary lights, no entertainment files, no weapons (there is nothing to fight that force would reach).  
- One small empty container: if you find yourself wanting to bring something back—a frond, a chip of stone, a jelly bladder—put the desire in the container instead and leave the thing. Souvenirs from this place continue growing in luggage.

### 6. Arrival Protocol

Cross. Notice you are already inside. Stop.  
Seal and check suit integrity.  
Establish breathing rhythm against the mineral chill.  
Locate the nearest artifact fragment by touch or imaging.  
Read your statement aloud or by touch.  
Set the first timer.  
Only then allow yourself to feel the packed life making a room around you.

Move little. Observe the glacial unfurling. When a soft body drifts past, let it. When darkness folds, watch but do not step. When time pockets, note the discrepancy and correct your count.

You are a visitor in a depression that feels newly pressed and intends to remain exactly as it is. Your ordinariness—your need for up, your unfinished obligations, your private ugly phrase—is the only thing the pocket cannot metabolize quickly. Guard it.

When the timer ends, or when you first catch yourself thinking that the cool dark has become preferable to any other air, activate the tether. Reach for the direction that does not exist until you insist it does. Fold out.

Afterward, in whatever brighter, louder, less complete reality you regain, do not immediately speak of what you saw. The silence of the pocket is contagious across worlds if given voice too soon. Write it down. Touch your ordinary things. Let the mineral chill leave your bones at its own basalt-cooling pace.

Most who find themselves there stop trying to leave. You will not, because you prepared to remain yourself longer than the garden expected. That small, stubborn surplus of self is the entire art.
