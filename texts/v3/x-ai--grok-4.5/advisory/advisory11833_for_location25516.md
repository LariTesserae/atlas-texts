**Preparation for the Unfinished Pocket**

An ordinary human multiverse traveler—someone with a baseline body, no exotic enhancements, standard multiversal navigation sense, and the usual mix of curiosity and caution—must treat this location not as a destination but as a process that is still deciding whether to include them. The chamber is embryonic space: damp, bright, quiet, soft-edged, densely inhabited by unnameable lives, and lightly unspooling at the margins of consistency. Visiting it is less like entering a room and more like being briefly written into a sentence that has not yet chosen its grammar. Preparation, therefore, is less about fortification and more about becoming permeable in the right ways and impermeable in the wrong ones.

### Mental and Perceptual Orientation

Begin days earlier by practicing deliberate unfinishedness. Sit in imperfect rooms. Leave sentences half-spoken. Walk without deciding your final destination until the last step. The pocket punishes rigid expectation and rewards soft attention. Travelers who arrive needing the walls to stay still, the light to have a source, or the creatures to resolve into taxonomy often find the glitches centering on them: depths opening underfoot at inopportune moments, directions folding so the exit seam leads only back into itself, or the crystalline freeze lasting longer than a few breaths when their panic spikes.

Cultivate a stance of courteous non-interference. The fauna do not startle and do not hide; they will treat you as another soft slow process among soft slow processes unless you introduce sharp edges of will. The half-built rings and grooves are not ruins or invitations exactly—they are rehearsals. Touching them is usually harmless but can accelerate local reabsorption or cause a nearby basin to deepen into a reflecting well. Decide in advance whether you are there to witness, to leave a small temporary mark of your own (a single moisture print, a carefully placed mineral shard from outside), or simply to stand inside the bright damp and let the chamber’s regard settle on you. Most travelers who fare well choose the last.

Accept that time and sequence will feel provisional. Your internal clock may drift. Moments of absolute freeze (when condensation droplets hang motionless and every creature locks mid-motion) are not threats; they are editorial pauses. Do not thrash. Breathe the still air if you can. When motion resumes it will pick up exactly where it left off, including any droplet that was about to touch your skin. The afterimages left by the thumbnail-sized quick ones will linger a half-second too long in your vision; do not chase them with your eyes or you may briefly lose the ability to judge distance.

Prepare for the light. It is cool, white, sourceless, and absolute. There are no shadows in which to rest the eyes or hide intention. After twenty or thirty minutes many travelers report that the brightness begins to feel like being steadily regarded by the chamber itself. This is not hostile. It is simply the local condition of existence. If you are prone to light sensitivity or existential vertigo under even illumination, spend time beforehand under high, diffuse indoor lighting with reflective white surfaces while practicing open awareness. Bring nothing that depends on darkness or contrast for comfort.

Language softens here. The low thrum that passes between the larger fluted shapes carries something that is not words but the shape words might later occupy. You may feel almost-meanings move through your chest or the bones of your inner ear. Do not force translation. Let the almost-language remain almost. Attempting to speak ordinary speech aloud sometimes causes the translucent air-sheets to tear open onto wrong colors of light, or makes the many-legged floor-skimmers pause in larger numbers around your feet, tasting the displaced air with what might be curiosity or mild concern. Soft humming or wordless toning is better tolerated, and occasionally answered by a gentle answering thrum.

### Bodily and Material Preparation

The air is persistently moist—the exact dampness of a long-breathed-in room—cool rather than cold, with continuous fine condensation. Ordinary clothing will grow heavy and cling. Choose layers of thin, quick-wicking, mineral-tolerant fabric that can accept repeated wetting and drying without stiffening or smelling. Avoid anything that traps moisture against the skin for long (you will already be damp). A light base layer, a breathable mid-layer, and a simple outer shell that can shed beading condensation work well. Pale colors are practical; they show less of the chalky mineral film that will gradually deposit on everything. Shoes should have soft, grippy soles that will not gouge the still-deciding floor. The stone ranges from firm pale ridge to glossy reluctant clay-like surface. Hard boots leave lasting scars that the chamber slowly tries to heal by bleeding fresh mineral, which can distress nearby fauna. Bare feet are possible for short visits if your skin is not abraded easily, but the soft zones can suck gently at bare soles and the standing water is cold.

Skin will take on the cool damp the way a new thought settles. After an hour you may notice a faint local scent of chalk and rain clinging to you—the same scent left when a lantern-body brushes a veil across a shoulder. This is ordinary. Some travelers report a mild temporary softening of the outermost skin layers, as if the body is being invited to participate in the unfinished mineral conversation. It reverses after exit. If you have open wounds, recent tattoos, or compromised barrier function, seal them thoroughly; the condensation and the occasional soft contact from fauna are not sterile in any human sense.

Breathing is easy. The air carries enough oxygen and no obvious toxins, though the constant moisture can feel heavy in the lungs for the first minutes. People with reactive airways sometimes experience a gentle tightening that eases once they stop resisting the damp. Do not bring heavy respirators unless medically required; they mark you as sealed-off and can make the larger membrane-creatures drift closer in slow inquiry. A simple scarf or light mask you can lower is enough for comfort.

Hydration and sustenance: the standing water in the basins is clear and appears clean, yet it is unfinished water—water still learning how to be water. Drinking it is not recommended for ordinary physiology. It can induce a transient sense of expanded internal space or mild dislocation of thirst signals. Bring your own closed water in a soft container that will not shatter if a reality glitch freezes and unfreezes around it. Food should be minimal, soft, and already familiar to your body; strong smells and vigorous chewing introduce too much finished intention into a place that prefers quiet becoming. Most travelers find appetite drops naturally after the first half-hour anyway.

### Navigation, Exit, and Reality Glitches

The entrance seam is short—barely twice your height in descent—and still deciding whether it is crack or throat. Mark your entry psychologically rather than with permanent hardware. A small loop of biodegradable thread or a single drop of your own water left at the lip can serve as a personal breadcrumb; the chamber often reabsorbs physical markers. Because directions slip, never rely on a single fixed mental map. Instead, carry a soft internal image of “the quality of the light at the seam” or “the particular dampness near the partial arch.” These qualities are more stable than geometry.

When a basin deepens while you watch, simply step back. Do not lean in to see the inverted second chamber; the well can decide to include you. When translucent sheets of thickened air drift past and tear onto amber or violet or deep-ice green light, do not step through the tear. The primary white reasserts itself quickly. If you find yourself on the wrong side when it closes, remain still; the pocket usually corrects courteous travelers within a few minutes.

The freeze events are the most disorienting for the unprepared. Everything stops—including falling droplets, moving creatures, and your own breath if you are mid-exhale. The silence becomes crystalline and absolute. Ordinary panic reads as sharp intention and can prolong the pause or draw a cluster of many-legged skimmers. Practice beforehand holding a soft suspended state: eyes open, muscles unclenched, mind resting on the simple fact of brightness. When motion resumes it is seamless from the pocket’s perspective. From yours there may be a single skipped frame of experience. Accept the edit.

The exit seam sometimes opens onto a different slope, starless dark, or recursive versions of itself. If this happens, do not force passage. Return to the approximate center of the chamber (the place where emptiness between lives feels most carefully kept) and wait. The pocket prefers not to keep visitors who are not becoming part of it. Ordinary humans who remain non-grasping are released, usually within an hour of subjective time, often finding the seam restored and the dry plain above exactly as remembered—or lightly rewritten in some harmless detail (a new small crack, a different quality of dust).

### Interaction Protocols

Do not name the fauna, even privately in your mind if you can help it. Naming is experienced here as a small violence of completion. Observe their categories instead by movement and relation: the limestone-colored many-legged constellations that skim without becoming herds; the soft lantern-bodies that pulse with the same light as the air and trail moisture-catching veils; the thumbnail quick ones that stitch stuttering arcs and leave physics-violating afterimages; the larger fluted membrane-and-cartilage browsers whose mouths open in more than one direction. They will approach. A many-legged skimmer may pause at your foot and taste the air you displace. A lantern-body may lower a veil across your shoulder, leaving cool moisture that evaporates into chalk-and-rain scent. A larger shape may pass close enough that the sub-audible thrum vibrates in your sternum. These are not attacks or greetings in any terrestrial sense. They are the pocket’s way of including you in its quiet abundance. Reciprocate by remaining soft, by not reaching, by letting the contact end when it ends.

The sparse pale stalks and green-black films are barely vegetation. Do not pick them. They lean millimeter by millimeter toward moisture; your presence already alters local humidity. Standing quietly near one is usually fine. The half-built rings of mineral shards, the single objects inside some of them, the faint parallel grooves, the partial arch that simply stopped—treat these as you would treat someone else’s unfinished sentence. You may look. You may carefully add a single small piece of exterior grit to an empty ring if the impulse feels clean and temporary. Do not complete the arch. Do not rearrange the offerings. The chamber reabsorbs what is ready to be reabsorbed and leaves what is still rehearsing.

### Equipment Minimalism

Carry almost nothing. A soft water container. A thin spare layer. Perhaps a small notebook of paper that can accept dampness without disintegrating, and a pencil (ink behaves strangely under the sourceless light and during freezes). A simple timepiece is optional and often misleading. Recording devices frequently capture only white field or loops of the same three seconds of condensation tick. Multiversal anchors or emergency beacons should be set to “request gentle extraction” rather than hard pull; a hard pull can tear the pocket’s edges and leave you holding a fragment of unfinished space that continues trying to become around your hand for days afterward.

If you wear glasses or lenses, accept that they will film over repeatedly. Cleaning them is possible but the film returns. Some travelers prefer to go without after the first adjustment period; the shadowless light makes depth perception less dependent on ordinary cues anyway.

### Duration and Aftercare

Short visits (twenty minutes to two hours subjective) are gentlest on ordinary physiology and psychology. Longer stays are possible—the pocket does not starve or freeze you—but the longer you remain, the more the cool damp and the steady bright regard rewrite small habits of perception. After exit you may find ordinary shadows briefly startling, ordinary thirst slightly delayed, ordinary speech a little too finished and loud for a day or two. Dreams may carry unfinished stone and soft lantern pulses for a week. These fade. A few travelers report a lasting increase in tolerance for ambiguity and a mild ongoing sense that walls everywhere are still negotiating their angles. This is generally experienced as enrichment rather than damage.

Wash with ordinary water after return. Let the chalk-rain scent leave in its own time. Do not scrub aggressively; the mineral film is polite and departs when ready. Speak with fellow travelers if you have them, but notice that describing the pocket in finished sentences always feels slightly wrong. Better to say: “I stood in a place that was still deciding, and it allowed me to be unfinished with it for a while.”

### Final Intuitive Note

You are not going there to conquer, catalog, or even fully understand. You are going there to stand inside a bright damp rehearsal of existence while soft strange lives continue their untranslatable affairs and stone thickens at the pace of thought. Prepare by loosening your grip on completion. Pack humility, soft fabrics, closed water, and a willingness to be regarded by light that has no source. Move as the fauna move: without startling, without hiding, with small automatic corrections of body and intent when reality glitches around you. The pocket is both habitat and embryo. If you enter it the way one enters a new thought—curious, unbound, ready to be changed in ways you will not fully name—it will likely set you back on the dry plain with only a faint scent of chalk and rain in your clothes and a quiet internal thrum that fades like the memory of a language you almost overheard.

That is sufficient preparation. The rest the chamber will teach you in its own unfinished time.
