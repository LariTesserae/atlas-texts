**Preparation for the High Drowned Attic**

An ordinary human multiverse traveler—baseline physiology, no inherent gills, pressure tolerance, or reality-stabilization gifts—must treat this pocket as a place that is actively unfinished and actively hostile to permanence. It is not merely “underwater at 500 m with geothermal weirdness.” It is a cathedral-sized volume of cold water clinging to the flank of a mountain still birthing itself out of the mantle, six kilometers above any proper seafloor, half a kilometer beneath a silvered surface that is only a dim rumor. The space rewrites its own architecture on timescales measured in heartbeats and minutes. Currents the width of streets appear without warning. Sound occupies bone. Gravity and time stutter. The rock is still soft in places. Everything is transit. You are not a visitor; you are temporary sediment that the mountain will try to incorporate or erase.

Preparation is therefore less about tourism and more about remaining a coherent, extractable object long enough to enter, observe, and leave before the cleft forgets you were ever an opening.

### 1. Physiological and Medical Baseline
You will be at roughly 50 atmospheres. Ordinary scuba is suicide; free-diving is fantasy. You need a rigid or semi-rigid atmospheric diving suit rated for at least 60–80 atm with significant safety margin, or a compact one-atmosphere submersible hard suit / micro-sub that can be maneuvered by a single operator through a basalt cleft. The suit must handle:
- Rapid, violent horizontal currents that can slam you into fresh pillow basalt.
- Abrasion from grit that sheds continuously.
- Temperature in the cold-spring range (likely 4–10 °C bulk water, with sudden warm plumes from the still-cooling rock).
- Constant high-intensity broadband noise, especially infrasound that will try to vibrate your soft tissues and middle ear.

Bring pharmacological support: strong anti-nausea and vestibular stabilizers (the direction-forgetting and lateral-drift effects will wreck ordinary balance), broad-spectrum antibiotics and antifungals (mineralized grit + organic films will colonize any micro-abrasion), and a short-course corticosteroid pack in case the roar begins modulating your pulse or respiration. Ear and sinus equalization must be bulletproof; many travelers pre-insert tympanostomy tubes or use advanced middle-ear pressure regulation systems. Hyperbaric preconditioning and a full medical workup for patent foramen ovale or other shunt risks are non-negotiable. You will decompress on the way out, but the pocket itself can rearrange and trap you at depth, so plan for extended bottom time and possible saturation.

Hydration and nutrition are closed-cycle. The water is not potable in any conventional sense; it carries dissolved mantle volatiles, fresh mineral precipitates, and whatever the red fronds and blue-black mats exude. Do not open the suit to taste the “lightning” minerals. That is how the place starts writing you into its drafts.

### 2. Life-Support and Mobility Systems
- **Propulsion**: Multiple redundant thrusters or a powerful umbilical-free thruster pack. Currents can exceed any swimming speed and reverse without notice. The suit needs strong attachment points for temporary anchoring (pitons that can bite into still-warm basalt, or expandible anchors that set in hexagonal cooling plates). Assume any fixed line you leave will be scoured or buried.
- **Gas and power**: Closed-circuit rebreather or suit life-support with scrubbers rated for long duration and able to handle possible hydrogen sulfide, methane, or other magmatic gases venting through fresh pores. Power systems must tolerate continuous vibration and occasional electromagnetic noise from the crystallizing rock. Carry sacrificial thermal batteries or chemical heat packs; the “watched by heat” skin sensation can mask real cold stress.
- **Sensors**: Forward-looking sonar that works in particle-laden water, Doppler current profilers, high-sensitivity hydrophones with real-time filtering (you need to hear the difference between a normal surge and the onset of a wall-forming curtain of fresh glass). Inertial navigation with frequent optical or acoustic resets, because dead-reckoning will fail when stones fall upward for a meter and time stutters. A simple mechanical compass is largely useless; carry multiple quantum or gyro systems and accept that “down” is provisional.
- **Lighting**: The ambient light is already the color of old bottle glass and arrives as slow pools rather than beams. Variable gloom from surface weather can drop you into near-blackness for hours. Use wide-spectrum, low-heat lamps that can switch to narrow bands; some mineral forests and fronds interact strangely with certain wavelengths. Avoid lighting that might provoke or attract the rare translucent eel-things.

### 3. Sensory and Psychological Hardening
The roar is the dominant fact of the place. It is not background; it is structural. Infrasound from rock being forced into crystalline order plus the shriek of self-sealing gas pores will occupy your teeth, the liquid in your eyes, and the marrow. Ordinary earplugs do nothing. You need active noise-cancellation tuned for extreme low frequencies, bone-conduction damping, and possibly a white-noise or counter-frequency overlay piped into the helmet so your nervous system has something else to lock onto. Even then, expect the roar to occasionally resolve into low vowels that feel like language. Do not answer. Do not try to parse it into meaning. Treat it as geological weather.

Light is dim, sideways, and upward-falling. Train in low-contrast, monochrome environments. Practice moving when visual flow is unreliable.

The time stutter and direction amnesia are the most dangerous cognitive hazards. A pillow basalt can cool and crack across a perceived hour while a miniature reef ages to collapse in a glance. A released tool may drift “up” before remembering gravity. Countermeasures:
- External atomic or pulsar-referenced clocks displayed in the helmet, plus a simple mechanical timer as backup.
- Strict personal protocols: every action is narrated aloud to the suit recorder (“I am releasing the sample bag now, expected drift vector…”). 
- Buddy system if traveling with another, but understand that the pocket can rearrange and separate you permanently. Solo is often cleaner; the place already has enough provisional minds.
- Mental framing: you are moving through a sentence that is still being written. Do not expect consistency. Consistency has not found the entrance yet.

Desensitization training should include prolonged exposure to infrasound, cold-water immersion under heavy vibration, and sensory deprivation tanks with randomized gravitational cues if your multiverse tech allows simulated partial gravity or vector shifts. Meditate on impermanence; travelers who arrive needing the world to stay put tend to panic when the ceiling becomes a descending curtain of fresh glass and the floor plan is rewritten mid-breath.

### 4. Physical Kit and Contamination Control
Everything you bring should be either sacrificial or easily cleaned of mineral grit and organic films. The red fronds will test your ankle. Grit will settle into every crease and begin cementing. The mountain practices incorporation without malice.

- Soft outer coveralls or ablative layers over the hard suit that can be shed if a frond anchors or a gypsum forest starts growing.
- Sampling tools: titanium or ceramic implements, sealed containers that can survive being gently crushed by a swelling pillow. The previous visitors used green-glass hand-axes, charcoal spirals on stones, and bone-and-sinew ladders. Their tech is first-morning tech—pressure flaking, knots that swell when soaked. If you must leave an object, make it deliberate and simple; the pocket will bury it anyway.
- Documentation: continuous video/audio with multiple redundant storage, sealed against pressure and vibration. Still photography in the bottle-glass light requires long exposures and stabilization that the currents will fight. Written notes on plastic or metal sheets; paper is vanity.
- Anchors, markers, and exit aids: biodegradable or pressure-dissolving markers if you wish to be kind to the next mind that tries. A mechanically simple “breadcrumb” system of small sonic beacons that ping infrequently; assume most will be scoured or swallowed. The cleft itself is not guaranteed to remain an entrance. Carry a means of cutting or bracing if the basalt decides to close like dough over a thumbprint while you are still inside. Some travelers bring small shaped charges or high-pressure injectors to keep a micro-passage open; use with extreme caution—the mountain is already unstable.

Do not bring open flames or anything that relies on combustion. Friction fire is a theoretical joke here. The wet vault will not permit it.

### 5. Entry, Transit, and Exit Protocol
Approach the unfinished mountain from the open water carefully. The flanks are still rising and shedding. The cleft is the only reliable verbal description of an entrance; it will look like black basalt that has not finished deciding its shape. Enter the way one enters a held breath—slowly, committing fully, because partial entry invites the rock to seal around a limb.

Once inside:
- Immediately map the current volume with sonar and establish a provisional “up” and “exit vector,” knowing both can change.
- Keep thrusters hot and anchors ready. When a street-sized current arrives, do not fight it head-on; ride or deflect, then re-stabilize.
- Move deliberately. Hovering in place invites grit moraines against your boots and frond investigation.
- Observe the artifacts if you wish, but do not assume they are gifts or warnings. They are broken sentences. Touching them may be how the place records you.
- Limit time. The longer you stay, the more the roar experiments with your pulse frequency, the more grit cements, the more the chamber considers you draft material. There is no safe duration, only less unsafe ones. Many travelers work in short sorties: enter, document for minutes to an hour of subjective time, leave.

Exit the moment the pocket begins to feel familiar. Familiarity is the first sign of incorporation. The cleft may have shifted or sealed; be prepared to create or force an opening, then seal your own departure so the attic can return to its private labor. Decompress with the understanding that residual grit, mineral taste, and phantom roar will travel with you for days or weeks. Some travelers report that after leaving, ordinary silence feels thin and untrustworthy, and that falling asleep on solid ground still carries the sensation of a possible upward drift.

### 6. Contingencies and Acceptance
If a tremor births a new wall between you and the exit, treat it as the environment speaking its nature rather than personal malice. Swim around, through (if it is still glass-soft), or wait for the next rewrite while monitoring life-support. If an eel-thing hangs with unhinged jaws, remain still or move with the current; everything here is transit. If the noise becomes unmistakable language, log it and do not converse. If time stutters badly enough that you watch a reef age and collapse in a glance, trust your external clocks over narrative memory.

You cannot “conquer” or “settle” this place. Previous minds left hand-axes and two-rung ladders and charcoal spirals; the mountain kept burying them and continuing its single upward gesture. Your goal is to pass through as cleanly as a fin, leaving as little permanent grammar as possible.

Finally, understand the emotional texture. The pocket never feels contained despite its cathedral size. It is a high, drowned attic where elevation and submersion conspired. Beauty here is real—the pale motes in quiet moments, the faint private glow of a frond, the way light flares white on still-breathing rock before cooling to wet slate and unripe plum—but it is beauty without hospitality. Awe and threat are the same sensation. Prepare to feel very small, very provisional, and strangely privileged to witness a mountain mid-sentence. Then leave before the sentence decides you belong in it.

Suit up. Check every seal twice. Step into the held breath. And remember: the place is already forgetting the shape of whatever just passed through. Your only task is to ensure that “whatever” is still you when the cleft closes.
