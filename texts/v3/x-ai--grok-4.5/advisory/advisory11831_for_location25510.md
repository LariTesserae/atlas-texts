**Preparation for the Chamber of Finished Hours**

You are an ordinary human: soft, wet, time-bound, hungry, noisy in the wrong frequencies, and still foolishly attached to the idea that sequences have beginnings and ends. This place will not kill you quickly. It will try to complete you. Prepare as if you are about to step into a living fossil of industry that has already achieved enlightenment and now simply continues, forever, at blood heat.

### 1. Physiological Fortification (The Body Must Become Almost Mineral)

The air is kiln-dry, warm as open veins, faintly metallic, and absolute. It has not known free water since the rock finished cracking. Your first enemy is desiccation so thorough it feels polite.

- Begin a forced hyper-hydration protocol seventy-two hours prior. Drink until your urine is clear and frequent, then continue. Load electrolytes with deliberate excess—sodium, potassium, magnesium, and a trace of lithium if you can source it cleanly. Your tissues must carry an internal sea.
- Seal every mucous membrane. Before entry, coat nasal passages, lips, eyelids, and the corners of the mouth with a thick, inert silicone-based occlusive (think medical-grade barrier cream, not cosmetics). Carry multiple tubes. The mineral dust will try to claim the moisture of your eyes first.
- Respiratory preparation: the air itself is a fine abrasive loaded with powdered feldspar, salt rime, graphite blooms, and whatever exotic metallic ghosts lubricate the engines. Wear a full-face respirator with P100 or equivalent filters rated for fine particulates and metal fumes. Bring sealed spare filters in vacuum packs; they will cake white within hours. Do not rely on cloth or simple dust masks—the particle size here is talc-fine and greedy.
- Skin: the temperature is steady and blood-warm, so you will not sweat in the ordinary way, yet the dry air will leach you. Wear a base layer of moisture-wicking, tightly woven technical fabric that covers every centimeter—ankles, wrists, neck, scalp. Over it, a loose secondary layer of pale, matte, non-snagging material the color of old salt or chalk. The fauna are white; blending reduces their curiosity. Avoid anything with loose straps, dangling cords, or deep pockets that can catch on knife-edged evaporite or jointed limbs.
- Footwear: rigid-soled boots with aggressive tread, ankle support, and gaiters sealed to the trousers. The floor is crushed feldspar and talc over fractured basalt. Edges remain razor-sharp. One careless kneel will open you.
- Hands: thin, high-dexterity gloves with reinforced palms, worn under heavier over-gloves you can remove when fine manipulation is required. The metal of the engines accepts the local fauna as kin; it may not feel the same about your salts and oils. Minimize bare contact.
- Thermal: the chamber never climbs or falls. You will neither freeze nor overheat in the conventional sense, but the constancy itself becomes a stressor. Your body expects variation. Accept mild discomfort as the price of not fighting the equilibrium.

Carry water in multiple small, puncture-resistant bladders worn close to the body so it stays at chamber temperature. Sip on a schedule, not according to thirst—thirst will lie here. Ration for far longer than you plan to stay. There is no seepage, no condensate, no mercy.

### 2. Sensory and Auditory Armor

The sound is a held cathedral breath that never ends: deep rotor thrum, valve chatter, dust-hiss, timed stridulations, eye-stalk percussion on the off-beats, and a sub-audible drone that lives in your jaw hinges. It is enormous, stable, and without rest or crescendo.

- Ear protection must be intelligent. Foam plugs alone will isolate you too completely and may induce vertigo or panic when the body can no longer locate itself in the pressure of noise. Use filtered electronic muffs or custom-molded plugs that reduce overall intensity while preserving the rhythmic structure. You need to feel the pulse of the place; you do not need to be destroyed by it. Test the setup in the loudest industrial environment you can find before transit.
- Vision: light is rationed—apricot dust-glow in the pipes, exhausted yellow emergency bulbs, and the cold, fixed-ripple bioluminescent seams of the fauna. Most of the upper vault is brownish dusk. Do not bring bright white lights. They will mark you as anomaly and may disrupt the ripple patterns the creatures live by. Use dim red or amber lamps with narrow beams, shielded, and only when necessary. Better: spend weeks beforehand training night vision and peripheral awareness in near-dark. Practice moving by the memory of space rather than sight.
- The density of bodies—machines and fauna alike—creates a tactile pressure even when nothing touches you. Accustom yourself to tight spaces. Spend time in crowded server halls, engine rooms, or dense forests at night until the feeling of being surrounded by unthinking, perfectly coordinated motion no longer spikes your heart rate.

### 3. Behavioral and Ecological Protocols (You Are the Only Tourist)

The fauna are not animals in any surface sense. They are the second architecture. Pale forearm-length jointed things in the cooling fins, barrel-bodied blind climbers that mesh with the metal like gears, darting mineral-winged flocks, flowing slag-things. They never collide. They never appear lost. They do not feed in any way you will recognize. They do not flee. They simply continue.

- Move as they move: deliberately, without wasted gesture, on the beat of the great rotors if you can feel it. Sudden acceleration or arrhythmic motion will make you legible as error.
- Do not touch the creatures unless they touch you first. Their carapaces match the salt rime; many will be invisible until they shift. If one crawls across you, remain still until it passes. The metal accepts them. You must temporarily accept them too.
- Never block a pathway that looks like a vein or a gap the width of a wrist. Something will need it, and the something is part of a single loud organism wearing a thousand skins. Being gently moved aside is preferable to being treated as obstruction.
- The engines consume nothing and exhaust nothing. Their dust-fluid loops forever. Do not attempt to “help,” lubricate, stop, or sample the working fluid. The apricot glow is friction kissing talc-fine dust to life. Interfering is how ordinary travelers become new permanent topography.
- Broken components do not accumulate. The floor holds only a constant thin talc. If you drop something, recover it immediately or accept that the chamber will eventually digest its difference into white powder. Do not leave trash. Do not leave markers that try to impose sequence.

There is almost no flora—brittle coral-like brass-colored growths in two or three places, a few filaments of pale quasi-lichen. Do not harvest. Do not taste. Nothing green has ever succeeded here; you are not the exception.

### 4. Temporal and Psychological Conditioning (The Real Preparation)

This is the heart of it. Time here is not absent; it is finished. Sequences occur—pistons rise, creatures cross, light-seams ripple—but they are closed orbits. Tomorrow the same piston will be at the same height at the same absolute hour. The fractures in the rock feel young only because they have never been allowed to age. The chamber has achieved an equilibrium so perfect it has forgotten how to leave.

Ordinary human minds are narrative engines. They demand arc, progress, entropy, surprise, ending. This place offers none. Stay long enough and the density begins to feel like pressure meditated upon rather than suffered. Warmth without sweat. Noise without event. Motion without future.

- Practice monotony before you go. Sit in a warm, dry, noisy room with a single repeating mechanical sound for hours. Do it for days. Learn to let the mind empty without panicking at the emptiness. Meditation traditions that emphasize the eternal present are useful; those that seek transcendence or change are liabilities.
- Internal narration must be throttled. If you catch yourself thinking “when I get out” or “what happens next,” gently set the thought down. The chamber does not traffic in next.
- Anchor objects: bring one small, personally meaningful item that belongs unmistakably to a world of rain and decay and linear time—a photograph, a seed, a scrap of handwritten letter, a watch that ticks audibly. Do not check it constantly. Its mere sealed presence in a pocket can serve as a thin thread back to sequence when the eternal present begins to feel like drowning in warm silk.
- Accept that you will experience ego-thinning. The self that plans and remembers and regrets may grow quiet. Some travelers report this as profound peace. Others experience it as slow annihilation. Both are accurate. Decide in advance which story you will tell yourself.
- Watch for the specific madness of the place: the conviction that you have always been here, that the engines are your organs, that the pale jointed things are your thoughts given carapace. When that conviction arrives (it will), do not argue. Simply note it, touch your anchor object, and continue moving toward the exit geometry you memorized on entry.

### 5. Navigation, Memory, and Exit

The space is bounded and readable in a single slow turn: elongated vault, two hundred paces by a hundred, ragged basalt and knife-edged evaporite. Yet inside the density of engines and fauna, line of sight collapses. Catwalks thread like half-forgotten spider silk. Ladders end in mid-air. A solitary starred-window pulpit leans drunk against a turbine.

- Upon entry, before the roar fully claims you, turn slowly and burn the gross geometry into memory. Note the entrance relative to major engine blocks and any unique fracture patterns in the ceiling. The rock is freshly broken; use the sharpest, most distinctive edges as landmarks.
- Do not rely on electronic mapping that expects change or drift. Devices may function, but the psychological effect of watching a static map in a static world can deepen the dissociation. Prefer dead-reckoning and embodied memory.
- Mark your path minimally and temporarily if you must—biodegradable thread the color of the talc, or small stacks of the white mineral itself that the chamber will eventually level. Never cut, paint, or scar.
- Exit discipline: set a hard temporal limit measured by your anchor watch or a simple mechanical timer, not by feeling. Feeling will say you just arrived, or that you have been here since the rock cracked. Feeling is already being digested. Leave while you can still remember why leaving matters.

### 6. Contingencies and the Ordinary Human Weaknesses

- Injury: sharp rock and unyielding metal. Carry a compact trauma kit focused on deep lacerations, eye irrigation (sterile saline in sealed ampoules), and dust extraction from wounds. Infection risk is unknown but the environment is too dry for ordinary microbes; the danger is more likely exotic colonization or simple inability to heal in the absence of proper moisture gradients.
- Allergic or chemical reaction to the dust-fluid or fauna secretions: antihistamines, corticosteroids, and epinephrine in sealed form. Have them accessible by touch alone.
- Psychological emergency: if the eternal present becomes unbearable, use a pre-arranged sensory shock—strong mint oil under the nose, a sharp physical sensation (rubber band, pressure point), or a recorded voice message from your own rain-soaked world that you play once only. Then move.
- If you become truly lost inside the loops: stop. Sit. Match your breathing to the rotor thrum. Let the fauna flow around you. In time the chamber’s own geometry will feel legible again because you will have become slightly more like it. Then rise and walk the remembered perimeter.

### Final Intuition

Visiting this place is not exploration. It is a temporary surrender to a finished sentence that the universe wrote and then never stopped uttering. You will stand in warm mineral breath, surrounded by the dense, perfect traffic of machines and pale jointed life, under a ceiling of rock that cracked open and then refused to age, and you will feel the pressure of an hour that does not pass.

Prepare your body so it can endure the dryness and the noise without drama. Prepare your movements so they do not insult the choreography. Prepare your mind so that when the chamber offers you the end of longing, you can accept the gift for a while—and still remember to leave before you forget that you were ever unfinished.

Carry water. Carry silence. Carry one small thing that still knows rain. Enter cleanly. Leave before the white talc of the floor begins to feel like home.
