**Preparation for the Unfinished Pocket**

An ordinary human multiverse traveler—someone with a standard terrestrial body, no innate heat immunity, limited oxygen storage, fragile skin, and a nervous system tuned to quieter biomes—cannot improvise this visit. The pocket is not a cave you enter; it is a brief geological inhalation still deciding whether to become architecture or reseal. Everything about it is provisional, hot, dry, dark, crowded, and indifferent. Preparation is less about conquest than about surviving long enough to witness one held breath of stone and bodies without becoming substrate yourself.

### Approach and Staging
The massif is young and still rising. Outside the pocket the air is already thin and merciless. Acclimatize for days or weeks at progressive altitudes if your baseline is sea-level. Carry supplemental oxygen for the final ascent; the shoulder where the blister cracked open will punish unprepared lungs. The entrance itself is recent and irregular—a fracture rather than a door. Expect sharp lips of stone that still weep pale dust, unstable scree, and temperature gradients so steep that the transition from cold thin air to hot thick air can crack untreated gear or shock the body.

Do not arrive alone if avoidable. A small team with redundant life-support and a designated exterior watcher is wiser; the pocket may decide to narrow or shed rock while you are inside. Mark the entrance with something that will not melt, corrode, or be immediately reworked by the local chemistry—high-temperature ceramic beacons or alloy pins driven deep. The “calendar is too short” here; what looks stable for an hour may spall.

### Core Life-Support Systems
**Thermal management is non-negotiable.** Heat radiates from below more than above—a buried engine that never quite cools. Ambient surfaces will be hot enough to burn unprotected skin within seconds to minutes. You need a full-body, actively cooled suit rated for sustained radiant and conductive heat, with insulated boots whose soles will not soften or outgas toxins onto the mineral. Passive insulation fails; you must reject heat continuously. Closed-loop liquid cooling or advanced phase-change systems with external radiators (carefully shielded so they do not become roosts or resources for the fauna) are mandatory. Expect your cooling load to spike near vents that freckle the gloom with dull orange. Carry spare coolant and power; failure means rapid hyperthermia followed by the fauna treating your body as fresh, soft mineral.

**Atmosphere and respiration.** Inside, the air is thicker only with heat and mineral haze. Free water is almost nonexistent; moisture floors instantly into salt lace and gypsum bloom on every surface, including your equipment if unprotected. Filter everything. Particulates, salts, and whatever volatile chemistry the vents exhale will coat lungs and eyes. A sealed helmet with positive-pressure filtration, moisture management that does not dump liquid water into the environment, and a rebreather or scrubbed oxygen supply is required. Do not rely on “thicker air” being breathable long-term; the chemistry is wrong for green life and may be wrong for you. Monitor CO₂, unknowns, and oxygen partial pressure constantly. The rust-brown gloom means your vision will be limited even with lights; infrared or low-light augmentation helps, but heat shimmer and haze will degrade every sensor.

**Water and metabolism.** Bring all water. There is none free to scavenge, and any condensate you try to collect will be saturated with minerals. Ration aggressively. Heat will drive massive fluid loss even inside a cooled suit. Electrolyte management and caloric density matter; this is not a place for leisurely meals. Food should be sealed, heat-stable, and require no open handling that might attract interest from mandibles already sorting rock.

**Mechanical protection.** Surfaces flake and re-grow in the same slow breath. Fresh fractures weep dust. Pillars of basalt and dark glass rise only halfway, unfinished. Wear abrasion-resistant outer layers over the cooling suit, a helmet that can take glancing spalls, and gloves that still allow fine manipulation. Kneepads and elbow protection help when you must crawl or brace. The rock has the dull shine of skin that has only just begun to scar—expect it to feel oddly organic underfoot, slightly yielding then sudden, with the risk of breakthrough into hotter seams. Traction is poor where gypsum blooms or where fauna have polished tracks.

### Sensory and Acoustic Reality
The dominant fact of the pocket is the fauna and the sound they make. Small, hard-bodied, heat-adapted things pack every crevice, every half-formed shelf, every cooling seam. Bodies click and grate against bodies. Mandibles work continuously at the soft new mineral—scraping, sorting, compacting. The noise is a layered industrial roar without industry: grinding, milling, conveyor-belt chatter produced by thousands of identical labors. It never peaks and never rests. Over hours the pitch shifts only by the slightest interval.

Ear protection is essential—high-quality active noise reduction or heavy passive attenuation rated for continuous industrial sound. Even then you will feel the vibration in bone and suit. The sound is not background; it is the medium. It rises and falls with the subsurface rhythm of the buried heat-engine (evaporate, deposit, crack, heal) on timescales of weeks, but your visit will catch only a slice. The fauna listen with their whole bodies; you will sense the volume of work as a physical pressure.

Light arrives only as slow leakage through hairline joints high in the roof, immediately diluted into brown dark. Shadows do not fall so much as thicken. Bring multiple independent light sources: helmet lamps with adjustable spectrum (avoid anything that might mimic a vent or heat signature the fauna key on), chemical lights that tolerate heat, and backup chem or battery units sealed against haze. Lights will attract coating by mineral bloom and possibly inspection by mandibles. Keep beams narrow and disciplined. The eye must push through the gloom; depth perception suffers. Pillars stop halfway; floor and walls are uneven and occupied. Move as if the space itself is scaffolding that is being continuously taken down and rebuilt a finger-width to the left.

### Fauna Protocol
They treat the unfinished stone as both warren and resource. Density is total—there is scarcely a hand’s breadth of unclaimed surface—yet the arrangement remains provisional. Migrations occur as dense carpets that occupy one cooling face until the heat gradient tips, then move as a single slow weather-front on the scale of days. In their wake: polished tracks and tiny spires of processed grit that the next geological pulse will bury or anneal.

You are large, soft, wet, and chemically novel. Assumptions:
- They may ignore you if you do not compete for immediate substrate or block a migration front.
- They may investigate with mandibles—expect scraping against suit, testing of seals, removal of any soft or loose material.
- Crushing them underfoot is inevitable in places and may trigger local density responses or simply be ignored as geological noise.
- Do not attempt to “clear” a path aggressively; the carpet reforms. Move with the gradients when possible. Step where polish already exists or where heat is less favored.
- Never leave tools, food residue, or shed material. Nothing they make lasts as artifact; everything returns to substrate. Your dropped wrench will be processed or annealed.
- Collect no specimens unless your mission absolutely requires it and you have containment that survives heat and abrasion. Removing individuals may be like removing grains from a dune—the system does not notice, or it notices as a change in local packing.

Observe first from a relatively less-dense shelf if one exists. Watch how the roar modulates with vent pulses. The call-and-response with the buried engine is the deepest clock here. Your presence is a temporary anomaly in a process measured in the rock’s own scar tissue.

### Movement, Time, and Psychology
Navigation is by dead reckoning, heat mapping, and memory of fractures. The pocket is cathedral-nave sized—large enough to become disoriented in brown gloom packed with moving bodies, small enough that the walls are always near. Pillars provide partial reference but stop short. Mark your route with heat-tolerant, fauna-indifferent tags if you must penetrate deeply. Time feels distorted: the constant roar erases quiet reference, heat slows cognition, and the provisional nature of every surface undercuts the sense of solid ground. Sessions inside should be strictly time-limited by coolant, oxygen, water, and psychological load. Fatigue arrives faster than expected.

Psychologically, the place is a single held breath. It is not hostile in the manner of a predator; it is unfinished and occupied. Many travelers report a profound sense of trespass mixed with awe—the recognition that this volume may close again and keep rising, sealing whatever it contains. The absence of green, the wrongness of the chemistry, the industrial sound without mind, and the skin-like scarring of the rock combine into an atmosphere of deep pre-biotic or post-biotic indifference. Prepare for that weight. Some find it clarifying; others find it eroding. Have an exit discipline: when indicators hit thresholds, you leave, even if the migration front is beautiful or the vent freckling hypnotic.

### Exit, Decontamination, and After
Exiting is the reverse thermal shock. Stage a cooldown and repressurization if needed. The mineral haze, salts, gypsum, and possible organic residues from the fauna will coat everything. Decontaminate thoroughly before removing seals—assume abrasive and chemically active films. Inspect suit integrity; micro-abrasions from mandibles or rock will be present. Monitor your own body for delayed heat stress, respiratory irritation, or any sign that something hitchhiked.

Document immediately if that is your purpose: heat maps, acoustic recordings (the pitch intervals matter), migration timings relative to vents, the feel of surfaces before they re-grow. Memory fades; the pocket does not care to be remembered.

### Minimal Viable Kit Summary
- Actively cooled, sealed, abrasion-resistant full suit + helmet with filtration and independent air.
- Redundant power, coolant, oxygen/water, lighting.
- Hearing protection tuned to continuous grinding spectra.
- Heat-tolerant marking and navigation aids.
- Trauma and hyperthermia response gear.
- Strict timeline and abort criteria.
- Mental preparation for sensory monopoly by heat, roar, gloom, and density.

Visiting feels like inserting a fragile, temporary vertebrate into a living kiln that is also a factory floor staffed by countless identical miners who are themselves part of the geology. You will sweat inside your armor while the rock weeps dust. You will hear the work of mandibles as the primary texture of reality. You will watch a carpet of hard bodies treat a cooling face as both home and ore until the gradient tips and they become weather. Surfaces will scar and shine under your gloves. The air will press with heat and occupation. Nothing green has ever tried to start here, and you will understand why in your bones.

If your preparations are honest and your windows short, you may step back out onto the thin-shouldered massif carrying the knowledge of a place that was never meant for you and is still deciding whether to remain open at all. That is the entire transaction. Move carefully. The breath is held only for now.
