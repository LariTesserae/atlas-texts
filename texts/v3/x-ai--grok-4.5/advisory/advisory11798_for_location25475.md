**Preparation Guide for the Forgotten Plain (Multiverse Designation: Eroded Industrial Metabolism / “The Unfinished Factory That Is the World”)**

An ordinary human multiverse traveler—someone with a standard baseline biology, no innate reality-hardening, and only the usual portable kit—must treat this location as a high-hostility environment that combines perpetual cold saturation, spontaneous semi-living machinery, predatory electrometallic fauna, and a locally exhausted physics where causality and object permanence have worn thin. The plain does not “attack” you in the conventional sense; it simply continues its ancient, noisy metabolism and will incorporate, short-circuit, crush, dissolve, or temporally displace anything that fails to match its rhythm. Visits should be kept short (hours measured carefully against your own subjective clock, never more than one local “day-cycle” if such a thing can be defined). Longer exposure risks gradual unspooling of personal edges.

### 1. Mindset and Psychological Pre-Conditioning
Understand first what the place *is*. It is not a ruin, not a battlefield, not a factory floor abandoned by makers. It is a completed ecology in which the industrial and the mineral reached final equilibrium long ago. Nothing here was ever “built” in the intentional sense; processes simply occur, cycle, soften, and re-occur. There is no quiet, no blank space, no seed-bed, no horizon that promises change. The constant racket (stamping without frames, turbines of vapor, inductive hum, wet valve-coughing, metallic ultrasonic cries) will fill your skull until your own thoughts start to sound like unfinished manufacture. Reality glitches—sound arriving before cause, double-exposed light showing the plain ten seconds ahead already rusted, pistons finishing strokes inside living cavities without contradiction—will erode the ordinary human expectation that objects stay discrete and time stays sequential.

- Spend several days beforehand in deliberately noisy, monochrome, wet industrial or coastal environments (shipyards in fog, battery recycling plants, freezing rain on flat salt pans) while practicing non-reactivity. Train yourself to flinch *after* the sound, not before.
- Meditate on permeability. Practice the mental exercise of watching two objects occupy the same space in imagination without resolving the paradox. You will need this when a forming gear-train assembles through your calf or a vapor-tower collapses across your timeline.
- Accept that you will not “explore” or “map” in any lasting way. The plain forgets height, forgets edges, forgets ends. Your goal is transient passage and observation, not conquest or settlement. Bring no seeds, no flags, no permanent markers; they will be metabolized or ignored.
- Expect a persistent low-grade grief or vertigo: the absence of green, of slope, of silence, of unfinished potential. The world here has finished becoming and is only busy staying. Ordinary humans often report a feeling of profound ontological unemployment upon return.

### 2. Physiological and Environmental Hardening
The baseline conditions are: near-freezing temperatures that never warm past a late-afternoon slate chill; standing water nowhere deeper than a finger-joint yet omnipresent and actively seeping upward; air that tastes of wet batteries and old snow (acidic, metallic, ozone-tinged); ground that crunches, reseals, and offers no reliable purchase; light that never brightens.

- **Thermal and moisture management**: You will be cold and wet continuously. Standard waterproof shells fail because the wetness is not only falling or standing—it weeps from the rock itself and condenses out of processes. Wear a multi-layer system with a vapor-permeable but chemically resistant outer membrane rated for battery acid and mineralized slurry. Under-layers should wick aggressively and include active heating elements powered by sealed high-density cells (the local charge fauna will attempt to feed on any exposed electrical potential). Insulate boots with rigid yet flexible soles that distribute weight; the mineralized fungus mats crunch like thin ice then reseal with a welding sound—repeated puncture can leave you ankle-deep in cold seepage. Carry chemical heat packs that activate without open flame or spark (sparks freeze mid-air here and may attract bat-analogues).
- **Respiratory and mucosal protection**: The air is a continuous aerosol of rust flake, supercooled vapor, bacterial iron, and microscopic metallic particulates. Full-face filtration with replaceable cartridges rated for metal fumes, ozone, and biologicals is mandatory. Eye protection must be sealed; the dim light double-exposes and the ultrasonic cries of the membrane-and-spark-gap flocks can induce headache and disorientation. Nasal passages and throat will raw quickly from the battery-snow taste—carry neutralizing saline rinses and a small sealed supply of plain water for mouth clearing (do not drink local water; it is industrial process fluid).
- **Skin and wound protocol**: Any break in skin becomes an invitation for mineralized fungus, iron bacteria, or transient micro-pistons. Pre-treat skin with a barrier cream that resists both hydrocarbon oils (from the browsers’ sludge) and electrolytic fluids. Carry rapid sealant gel and antibiotic/antifungal broad-spectrum that also addresses metal toxicity. If a spontaneous gear-train meshes through a limb, do not panic-pull; wait for the three-to-four revolution cycle to complete and the assembly to scatter into grit, then treat the crush and puncture. Tetanus and stranger industrial pathogens are theoretical risks—update all immunizations.
- **Nutrition and hydration**: No foraging is possible or desirable. The bacterial mats and oily sludge trails are metabolically alien and likely toxic or electrically active. Carry all calories in dense, non-perishable, low-scent form. Water must be sealed; atmospheric condensers will clog with metallic rime and may grow transient turbines. Plan for high caloric burn from constant shivering, noise stress, and uneven walking. Electrolyte balance is critical because the environment leaches ions.

### 3. Mobility, Navigation, and Ground Interaction
The flatness is a pressure in the knees. There is no rise, no fall, only continuation. Water sheets make every step a shallow wade; the interlocking litter (bruise-colored iron bacteria, overnight crystalline pseudo-leaves that shatter by “noon,” crunching fungus) creates a shifting, resealing crust.

- Footwear: wide-soled, aggressively treaded, puncture-resistant boots with gaiters sealed to the pant leg. Consider short stills or flotation-assisted sole attachments if you must cross denser seepage zones, but test them—anything that looks like a tool may be partially metabolized into the local machine-ecology.
- Gait: slow, deliberate, weight-distributed. Sudden vibration attracts the oxidized-copper skates that glide beneath the water film and fold upward into Faraday-cage traps. The bipedal condenser-coil crabs are less immediately lethal but will snip cooling fins (or fingers, cables, loose straps) to chew.
- Navigation: conventional landmarks do not exist. The horizon is only a thickening of gray wetness. Compass needles may spin or lock under local inductive hums. GPS analogues from your home universe will drift as reality double-exposes. Use a dead-reckoning inertial unit hardened against electromagnetic transient and temporal smear, plus a trail of ephemeral, non-metallic markers that dissolve after a set time (you do not want to leave permanent paths that the plain might incorporate into its gear-trains). Watch for the denser herds and taller short-lived smokestacks of pure cloud as temporary references, understanding they will collapse.
- Never stand still longer than necessary in one spot. Transient engines rise beneath feet. Pistons of fused sand and black ice thrust, cycle, and soften back into slurry.

### 4. Defense Against Fauna and Spontaneous Machinery
The fauna are not animals in the green-world sense; they are mobile extensions of the same industrial-mineral metabolism.

- **Bipedal crabs (condenser-coil + cartilage)**: Opportunistic snippers. Keep limbs close, cables secured, no dangling shiny or conductive objects. A sharp non-conductive prod can discourage them.
- **Many-jointed browsers**: Herds grazing bacterial iron, leaving warm oily sludge. They are not aggressive but will blunder through you. Their revolving drill-head mouthparts can core a boot or leg. Give herds wide berth; their sludge trails steam and are slippery plus chemically active.
- **Membrane-and-spark-gap flocks**: Dive on forming turbines to feed on charge release. They will also dive on any strong electrical potential you carry. Shield all power sources in Faraday cloth or mu-metal equivalents. Their metallic ultrasonic cries disorient—wear active noise-cancelling ear protection tuned to dump those frequencies while still allowing you to hear the deeper inductive danger cues. If swarmed, go low and inert; power down non-essentials.
- **Oxidized-copper skates**: The quiet predators. They wait for vibration then enclose prey in a living Faraday cage, forcing a death-cycle of tight revolutions. Minimize rhythmic footfall. If one rises around you, the cage is electrical and compressive—carry a sacrificial conductive decoy (a small sealed battery pack on a tether) to throw, and an insulated cutter for the rare case you must break a partial enclosure. Do not use your own body’s salt water as a conductor if you can avoid it.
- **Spontaneous machines**: Pistons, gear-trains assembling from rust flakes and shellfish-teeth analogues, condensation towers, frameless stamping presses. They are not hostile; they are simply occurring. Most will phase or complete their cycle through you if you do not resist the contradiction. The danger is mechanical (crush, shear) or thermal (supercooled vapor, sudden sparks that freeze). Move with the local rhythm when possible. If a tower forms overhead, step out of its collapse radius before the rain that is halfway to wire falls. Listen for the pre-sound; sometimes you are given a few seconds of warning that arrives too early.

Weapons: kinetic projectiles function but may attract attention or freeze mid-flight in odd ways. Directed-energy or strong EM emitters are magnets for the charge-feeders and may destabilize local processes around you in unpredictable cascades. Best defense is quiet passage, sacrificial decoys, and insulated cutting/prying tools of ceramics or advanced polymers. A collapsible non-conductive staff helps probe depth and discourage crabs.

### 5. Reality-Stability Kit and Temporal Hygiene
This is the most distinctive preparation. Edges forget to stay edges. A piston may finish inside your chest cavity; a flock may fly through a half-formed gear-house and emerge differently counted; light double-exposes the future rust; sound precedes source.

- Carry a small, sealed “anchor” object of high personal significance and simple geometry (a smooth stone from your origin, a plain ring) kept against skin. Many travelers report that a consistent self-referent helps personal boundaries resist smearing.
- Wear a heads-up temporal monitor if your multiverse kit includes one: a device that timestamps sensory input against an internal high-stability clock and flags arrivals of sound/light before cause. When the flag goes, freeze and wait for synchronization rather than reacting to the pre-echo.
- Practice “soft eyes” and delayed judgment. When you see two versions of the same stretch of plain, do not choose; walk the average until they collapse.
- If you notice your own count of fingers or the number of companions shifting after passing through a process, stop, inventory, and re-assert via the anchor object and verbal self-description (quietly—ultrasonic flocks listen).
- Exit protocol: pre-arrange a retrieval window or fixed transit bead keyed to your origin signature. Do not linger trying to “understand” the equilibrium; understanding is how the plain begins to wear your edges smooth. Upon return, expect residual auditory hallucinations of distant welding and valve-suck for days, plus a temporary difficulty with steep slopes or bright green.

### 6. What to Bring (Minimalist High-Reliability Kit)
- Sealed full-face respirator with spare cartridges
- Layered cold-wet immersion suit with active heat and chemical barrier
- Insulated, wide-sole boots + gaiters + spare socks in waterproof bags
- Inertial navigator + ephemeral markers
- Faraday-shielded power cells and minimal electronics
- Sacrificial conductive decoys
- Ceramic/polymer tools (prod, cutter, scraper)
- Dense rations, sealed water, electrolytes, medical sealant and broad-spectrum anti-infectives
- Noise-cancelling ear protection with selective pass-through
- Personal anchor object
- Compact recording devices hardened for EM and moisture (for observation only; data may arrive pre-event or double-exposed)
- One change of dry base layers in a hardcase for immediate post-exit

Do not bring: open flame, unshielded electromagnetics, seeds or biologicals from green worlds, permanent surveying stakes, anything you cannot afford to have partially metabolized or temporally duplicated.

### 7. Entry, Conduct, and Exit
Arrive low and slow if your transit method allows orientation. Spend the first minutes motionless (on a pre-placed insulating pad if possible) letting the racket re-emit through you until your nervous system stops treating every stamp and hum as threat. Then move. Stay light. Do not try to “fix” or “sample extensively.” Watch a full spontaneous cycle of a piston or tower if you can do so safely—it is the closest the plain comes to a heartbeat.

Speak little. The air is already loud with unfinished manufacture; adding language can feel like introducing a seed into a place that has no gap for seeds.

When your internal clock or anchor sense says the edges are thinning, leave. Do not wait for a dramatic event. The plain will still be there, denser at the limits of vision with the same late cold industry, forever busy at the work of staying. You will carry the taste of wet batteries and old snow on the back of your tongue, and for a while ordinary slopes will feel like arrogance.

This place is not evil. It is complete. An ordinary human can visit it the way one might visit a glacier or a deep-ocean vent or the interior of a working star—briefly, carefully, with full respect for a metabolism that has no need of you and will not notice when you are gone. Prepare thoroughly, move like a temporary fluctuation, and return before the plain teaches your knees that height was always optional.
