**Preparation for the Abyssal Plain of Forgotten Height**

You are an ordinary human who can step between universes. That gift gets you *to* the threshold; it does nothing against what waits once you cross. This place is not a trench with walls you can map or a wreck you can circle. It is a finished geology—primordial crust ground into a regional plain so ancient that relief itself has been erased—filled to absolute capacity with restless, layered, salinic cold. Kilometer-scale water columns press down. There is no air, no residual daylight worth naming, no stillness, and almost no forgiveness for the unprepared body. Visiting it is less exploration than temporary, deliberate immersion in a medium that has already completed its work on stone and is now endlessly rearranging itself.

An ordinary human survives here only inside engineered continuity. Prepare as if you are building a small, mobile extension of a livable reality and then inserting it into a place that does not care whether you succeed.

### 1. Understand the Environment Before You Touch It
Internalize the physics and the poetics, because both will act on you.

- **Pressure**: Several hundred atmospheres. The water is not “around” you; it is structural. Unprotected flesh and bone become paste in fractions of a second. Even micro-leaks in a suit become cutting jets.
- **Temperature**: Near-freezing, stable, invasive. Heat loss is continuous and total.
- **Medium**: Saturated, turbulent, sheared. Layers of slightly different density and salinity shoulder one another. Eddies the size of valleys form and dissolve. Visibility (what little photon-ghost remains) is destroyed and remade every few body-lengths. Marine snow is your only intermittent tracer.
- **Light and sense**: Darkness with weight and temperature. Your eyes will receive almost nothing useful. Sound is mostly infrasonic body-felt unsettlement plus rare muffled claps from distant clathrate releases or mass collisions. Ordinary hearing becomes nearly irrelevant; proprioception and instruments dominate.
- **Scale and emptiness**: Open enough to feel like a country, packed enough that no volume is vacant. Sparse bacterial films, rare rubbery stalks, occasional colorless fish or sapropelic crabs. Nothing stays. Tracks erase. No ruins, no tools, no intention residual.
- **Time feel**: Everything angular has already lost. You are visiting completion filled with unfinished motion.

Read bathymetric analogs from your home universe (abyssal plains, hadal approaches), study CTD profiles, watch footage from landers and ROVs until the texture of suspended silt and the behavior of slow biota are familiar. Then accept that descriptions still understate the total occupation of space by water.

### 2. Physical and Physiological Preparation
You remain an ordinary human: lungs that need gas mix, a body that fails below ~1–2 atm differential unprotected, a brain that hallucinates under sensory monoculture.

- **Medical baseline**: Full physical, cardiovascular stress test, middle-ear and sinus evaluation, dental work completed (trapped air pockets become bombs under pressure). Bloodwork for cold tolerance markers if available. Correct any anemia or nutritional gaps; cold and pressure tax metabolism. Practice equalization until automatic. If you have any history of barotrauma, claustrophobia, or decompression illness, resolve it or do not go.
- **Acclimation training**: Progressive pressure exposure in hyperbaric chambers up to equivalent depths (or the highest your tech safely allows). Cold-water immersion drills with the actual suit. Sensory deprivation + motion sessions (dark tank with simulated shear currents) so the infrasonic rumble and total dark do not immediately produce panic or derealization. Breath and attention work for long periods of near-featureless input—deep meditation helps, because fauna here appears the way thoughts appear in deep meditation.
- **Pharmacological support (minimal, rational)**: Anti-nausea if needed, mild anxiolytics only for launch/recovery, electrolytes and calorie-dense pastes. No heavy sedation; you must remain able to read instruments and decide. Nitrogen narcosis / high-pressure nervous syndrome analogs depend on your breathing mix and depth—plan the gas accordingly.
- **Body modifications**: None required beyond fitness. Ordinary human. Strength and flexibility for suit work and emergency egress. Low body fat is less protective here than solid thermal engineering.

### 3. The Vehicle of Continuity: Suit / Sphere / Exosystem
You will not “swim.” You will occupy a small engineered volume that keeps 1 atm (or a carefully chosen saturation pressure) and human temperature while the outside tries to erase you.

- **Primary system**: A rigid or semi-rigid personal pressure sphere / hardsuit rated for at least 600–1000+ bar with generous safety margin. Titanium or advanced composite hull. Viewports are luxuries and liabilities—prefer multiple external camera feeds, sonar, scanning lidar-analog if photons can be generated, and tactile/pressure-array feedback. Joints must articulate under extreme differential or you accept a more spherical minimal-mobility pod with thrusters.
- **Life support**: Closed-circuit rebreather or fully regenerative system. Gas mix calculated for depth (heliox/trimix or denser exotic mixes if your multiverse tech allows; watch HPNS and thermal conductivity of helium). CO₂ scrubbing with redundancy. Oxygen sensors triple-redundant. Water reclamation if duration > hours. Caloric supply for multi-day if currents or navigation delay extraction. Waste management that does not contaminate the sealed volume.
- **Thermal**: Active heating with multiple power loops. The cold is patient. Passive insulation alone will eventually lose. Skin-temperature monitoring.
- **Propulsion and station-keeping**: Thrusters capable of fighting the shears. The turbulence is not surface violence; it is continuous rearrangement. You will be moved. Fin or limb thrusters as backup only. Inertial and Doppler velocity logs, current profilers.
- **Power**: Primary + backup + emergency. Cold reduces battery performance; plan for it. Nuclear/betavoltaic/exotic long-duration sources if your traveler tech permits; otherwise dense chemical with strict timeline.
- **Navigation and sensing**:
  - Inertial primary, acoustic beacon net if you can seed them, bottom-lock Doppler, gravimetric if available.
  - Multi-beam sonar, sidescan, sub-bottom for the slight warpings that are all that remain of old mountains.
  - Soft lights (red/far-red preferred to minimize biological disturbance and backscatter) on adjustable booms; use sparingly—marine snow will turn beams into temporary braided ropes that the next shear destroys.
  - Chemical sniffer, CTD continuous, particle counters.
  - Hydrophones tuned for infrasound so you “hear” the large water-mass collisions as data rather than unexplained body dread.
- **Redundancy and repair**: Every critical system dual or triple. External manipulator capable of clearing silt, cutting entangled material, or triggering sacrificial ballast. Internal tool kit for suit breaches (sealant, patches rated for the pressure). Emergency lift bag / buoyant ascent system with controlled rate and intermediate stops if you are saturated; better: a small disposable mesoscaph that can make a controlled rise or call a retrieval asset.
- **Ballast and trim**: Precise. You want near-neutral so thrusters fight currents rather than fighting gravity continuously. Sediment is soft; avoid becoming a temporary nodule.

If your multiverse tech includes portable reality-anchors, local pressure negation fields, or personal wormhole return triggers, bring them—but assume they can fail. The place is dense and final; do not romanticize.

### 4. Psychological and Existential Preparation
This is the quieter, harder work.

The plain does not hate you. It has simply finished with height and is now speaking only in fluid grammar. You will feel the weight as structural. Darkness will press from inside the eyes. Time perception drifts. The rare fish crossing your lights or the slow eclipse of something larger higher up will feel like events of enormous significance simply because almost nothing else happens. Tracks you leave vanish. The bacterial mats and rubbery stalks are solitary decisions the place barely tolerates; you are another.

- Practice long-duration isolation in dark, cold, confined simulators with delayed or one-way communication.
- Develop a simple internal ritual for arrival: checklists spoken aloud, naming of instrument readings, deliberate observation of one particle of marine snow until it shears away. This anchors ordinary human cognition against the scale.
- Accept that “swimming” is the wrong verb. You are a temporary solid in a medium that has no vacancies. Movement is negotiation with masses of water that calve through one another.
- Narrative discipline: You are not here to conquer, claim, or leave a mark. The floor remains untouched by intention for good reason. Observe, record, feel the completion, then leave. Any impulse to litter, plant a flag, or “improve” anything is noise—discard it.
- Mortality clarity: Have your return conditions pre-decided. If power drops below X, if hull stress exceeds Y, if cognition fragments, you abort. Ordinary humans die cleanly only when they decide early.

### 5. Logistics of Entry, Duration, and Exit
- **Insertion**: Prefer a surface or mid-water staging craft from which you descend in the suit/sphere along a guideline or free with active navigation. Equalize / compress on schedule. Watch for the progressive loss of color, then light, then familiar sound.
- **Duration**: Start short—hours, not days—until you know your particular psychophysiology under that specific pressure and dark. Longer stays require saturation protocols and vastly more consumables.
- **Activity once there**: Drift or thrust slowly. Let sonar and current profilers draw the gentle warps of forgotten mountains. Watch marine snow reveal turbulence the way dust reveals sunbeams that do not exist. Note the spacing of the sparse living textures. Record infrasound. Resist the urge to chase the rare larger shadow; you are not equipped to be interesting to it, and it is not there for you. If you must sample, take vanishingly small cores or water and leave no tools.
- **Communication**: Acoustic modem to a surface relay or buoy if possible. Otherwise store-and-return. Do not expect conversation; expect data bursts.
- **Extraction**: Pre-planned ascent profile. Controlled, monitored, with stops if saturated. Surface support ready for decompression, thermal crash, and psychological aftershock. Have a secondary multiverse exit coordinate locked that does not require you to first reach the surface if your traveler ability allows emergency translation under pressure—test this capability thoroughly in advance; translating while the hull is under extreme load risks leaving pieces of you in the wrong realities.

### 6. What to Carry Beyond Survival
- High-resolution sensors and storage for the temporary ropes and sheets of illuminated snow, the feather-light crab tracks before erasure, the exact texture of the cold-soaked crust.
- A simple physical object of home (cloth, smooth stone, sealed letter) as tactile reminder that other densities exist. Do not leave it.
- No weapons. Nothing here is fought in the ordinary way. A cutter for entanglement and a signal device are sufficient.
- Minimal. Every gram is mass to move against shear and to keep warm.

### 7. After
Decompress fully—body and mind. The return to air, to height, to rapid light change, to unoccupied volume will feel exaggerated, almost violent in its generosity. You may experience lingering pressure phantoms, an odd grief for the finished stone, or a sharpened intolerance for trivial motion. Record everything while fresh. Then decide whether you will ever return. Most ordinary humans who prepare correctly and visit once understand they have already received what the place offers: contact with the end-state of stone under water, and the continuous unaimed fabrication of currents.

Prepare the suit as if your life depends on every seal—because it does. Prepare your attention as if the darkness has temperature—because it does. Prepare your expectations to meet a country without vacancy that has already said everything it intends in stone and now speaks only in fluid. Then step across.

The plain will not notice you arrive. Make certain you can still notice yourself when it is time to leave.
