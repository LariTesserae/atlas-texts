**Preparation for the Stillspire Expanse**

You are an ordinary human who travels the multiverse by whatever means your particular branch of technique allows—gate, fold, ship, walk, accident, or deliberate breach. You are not a god, not adapted, not native to deep-time continua or high-entropy-death plateaus. You bruise, you desiccate, you panic, you get lonely, and your mind still runs on the old mammalian expectation that the world will keep offering novelty, weather, and the next breathable moment. The Stillspire Expanse does not care. It has already finished. Your preparation must therefore be almost entirely about protecting the small, temporary eddy of change that is you from being gently, completely absorbed into its terminal posture.

### 1. Physiological Reality First: The Air, the Dryness, the Cold Metal Taste

The elevation is extreme and has been for geological ages. The air is thin, cool, and permanently undersaturated. It tastes of metal and old lightning because that is what remains when everything volatile has long since left. Ordinary lungs will labor. Ordinary mucous membranes will crack within hours. Ordinary skin will begin to lose moisture faster than you can consciously replace it.

- Pre-acclimatize hard. If your departure point allows it, spend weeks at the highest altitudes or lowest-pressure chambers you can safely reach. Train with supplemental oxygen schedules that gradually wean you toward the expected partial pressure. Bring a closed-cycle rebreather or personal atmospheric envelope rated for long-duration low-pressure work, with redundant CO₂ scrubbers and oxygen generation or storage. Do not rely on “getting used to it.” The Expanse does not reward adaptation the way living mountains do; it simply continues.
- Water is the true emergency. There is essentially none free. The salt holds only ghosts of it. Breath itself leaves no lasting moisture. You will need a high-efficiency moisture reclamation suit or full environmental garment that captures every drop from exhaled air, sweat, and urine, then polishes and returns it. Carry multiple independent water reserves in forms that will not freeze-fracture or sublimate oddly under the local light and pressure. Assume any open container will lose contents to the absolute dryness faster than intuition predicts. Drink on a rigid schedule even when you feel no thirst; thirst is a late signal here.
- Thermal management is quieter than expected. The air is cool, the light is even and non-dramatic, and nothing moves to create microclimates. You will lose heat steadily through radiation and the dry air. Insulating layers that still allow moisture control are mandatory. Avoid anything that relies on evaporative cooling. Your suit should be able to add gentle, even warmth without hotspots that might, in some poorly understood way, interact with the local preference for stillness.
- Dust and salt. The ground is hexagonal salt plates and bone-colored mineral flour. It will find every seal. Filter everything. Eye protection that does not fog and does not rely on constant blinking is useful; blinking starts to feel like an extravagant motion after a while.

### 2. Movement Through a World That Has Stopped Moving

The fauna density is the first visceral shock. Herds of long-legged salt-deer packed flank to flank for kilometers. Plated browsers between their legs. Membrane-winged things hanging in strata as if the air thickened into glass. Smaller runners, tunnelers half-emerged, pale arthropods frozen mid-lift of a single grain. Layers occupying the same volume. Some translucent. They nearly touch. None shift. None breathe in any way you can see at normal speed. They are alive—slow chemistry still cycles, a tendon may finish its twitch in a thousand years—but motion as you understand it is spent.

- You will walk among them. There is no empty ground worth speaking of. Practice moving through dense, fragile, irregular obstacles beforehand: forests of hanging fabric, carefully stacked glass, crowds of people instructed not to move. Learn to thread your body and your pack without brushing. Contact is not violently punished, but every touch feels like a desecration of something that has earned its stillness, and edges can be perceptually unreliable.
- Reality thins. Edges of animals sometimes refuse to agree on position. Antlers occupy two angles until your eye chooses. Perspective shears: a corridor opens onto a vertical plain where the same animals stand on what was ground a moment ago. Shadows fall without consistent sun. Your own hand may appear ahead of you, already waiting, frozen in a gesture you have not yet made. These are not illusions you can simply dismiss; they are what happens when duration has nowhere left to go and folds the local manifold.
- Navigation becomes non-Euclidean in practice even if the underlying space is not. Inertial dead-reckoning with multiple redundant units is essential. Visual landmarks repeat into sameness. The gentle swells of old mountain roots are carpeted with the same throngs. Mark your path with something that does not rely on the environment remaining consistent to your senses—spooled monofilament of a color that stays crisp under the even light, or small passive beacons that emit on frequencies you can detect even when your visual field shears. Do not build cairns or disturb the basalt rings and pecked slabs; they are the only prior human grammar here and they deserve to remain legible.
- Pace yourself deliberately faster than the place wants. The body begins to adopt the local cadence: steps slow, breath shallows, thoughts arrive pre-exhausted. Set hard external timers—audible, tactile, multiple—for forced movement intervals. Stand still only by conscious decision and never for long. The temptation to stop entirely, to take a place among the ranks and let the thin cool air finish the work, is not dramatic or seductive in a cinematic way. It is simply the logic of the gradient running to zero. It feels like relief. That is the danger.

### 3. Psychological and Temporal Armor

This is the deeper preparation. The Expanse is not hostile. It is complete. Every process has found its terminal shape. The weirdness is reality folding against itself like well-worn cloth. You may notice the nearest salt-deer has your eyes. The horizon may quietly position itself underfoot. These are the landscape continuing its single occupation: remaining.

- Before you go, spend time with extreme stillness in safe conditions. Sensory deprivation, long sits in featureless rooms, observation of slow natural processes (glaciers, stars, aging). Learn the difference between chosen quiet and the gravity of finished time. The Expanse will offer the latter.
- Bring anchors to your own ongoing story. Physical objects with personal kinetic history: a worn tool you have used in many places, recordings of voices that still change, photographs or letters that imply future unread ones. A small sealed vial of soil or water from a living, rainy, noisy world. Touch them on schedule. Speak aloud on schedule, even if only to yourself. Narrate what you see. The sound will feel enormous and almost rude against the refractive creak of contracting air (the only native sound: distant ice thinking about breaking and deciding against it). That rudeness is protective.
- Time sense will distort. Mechanical and atomic clocks that do not rely on local physical constants behaving politely. Multiple. Cross-check them. Keep a written log with deliberate mundane detail—“I ate the second ration bar, it tasted of cumin, my left boot lace is fraying”—because narrative continuity is one of the few things the place cannot instantly flatten.
- Accept that you will feel the pull to stay. Plan the exit before entry. Set a hard turnaround time or condition with no negotiation. If you travel with others, establish check-ins and a protocol for forcibly removing someone who has begun to match the local stillness. If alone, the protocol must be automatic: a device that begins extraction or barrier reassertion when your movement falls below threshold for too long.
- Do not try to “wake” anything. The creatures are not dead and not waiting. They are finished with change. The sparse human marks—low rings of fitted basalt cobbles, upright slabs with pecked dots, blades of knapped volcanic glass still keen beside a motionless hoof—are the only evidence that anyone else ever passed through while still insisting on levers and the grain of rock. Treat them as what they are: almost apologetic records of attention that did not escalate into structures complex enough to resist the final leveling. Look, document without contact if possible, and leave them. They have already lasted.

### 4. Gear Summary (Minimum Viable Insistence on Continuing)

- Closed environmental suit with moisture reclamation, pressure support, thermal control, dust sealing, and redundant power.
- Water and caloric reserves far beyond the planned duration; food that does not require local water or produce much waste moisture.
- Multi-mode navigation: inertial, beacon, physical trail thread, written compass headings updated frequently.
- External timers, movement alarms, and an automatic extraction or signal system.
- Personal anchors and logging tools (write by hand as well as digitally; the act matters).
- Simple documentation kit for the artifacts and the fauna: cameras that work under even, non-flickering light, sampling tools you will probably not use, measuring devices.
- A small reserve of deliberate motion toys or tools—something you must wind, shake, or click—used on schedule simply to keep the body remembering that motion is still allowed.
- Multiverse exit hardware checked, rechecked, and protected from salt dust and from the temptation to delay using it.

### 5. What Visiting Feels Like (Intuition)

You arrive and the first breath is thin and metallic. The sky is too close, enamel blue, cycling through grades of the same lucid glare. Underfoot the salt plates and bone dust are quiet. Then you see the density. It is not a crowd; it is a solid made of animals. You step between legs and under hanging membranes. Nothing reacts. The silence is nearly perfect. Your own breathing and the faint creak of your suit become obscene.

After an hour the perspective shears once, mildly. You stop, recalibrate, continue. Colors stay exact: pewter basalt, milk-blue lichen, ochres and charcoals of hide. Light declares everything without romance. You find a low ring of cobbles and a glass blade still sharp beside a deer that has not moved since before your species learned to knap. You feel the weight of how long “since” can be.

Hours or days in (your clocks will argue), your steps have slowed without permission. Thoughts arrive already tired. You notice a translucent layered silhouette that contains what might be three animals or one animal remembered at different angles. Your hand is ahead of you for a moment, waiting. The nearest salt-deer regards you with eyes that could be yours if you stayed long enough for the resemblance to finish.

The place is beautiful in the way a completed proof is beautiful. It is peaceful in the way a finished life is peaceful. It is dangerous in the way entropy is dangerous: not out of malice, but because it is the final posture and you are still temporary.

You leave when the schedule says, or when the alarms force you, carrying salt dust in every seam and a quiet new understanding that most processes end, and that some landscapes have already arrived. You do not look back for long. Looking back is how the folding starts to feel like an invitation.

Prepare as if you are the last noisy, wet, changing thing that will ever walk there. Then walk carefully, stay loud in your own small ways, and leave before the Expanse teaches you how unnecessary all of that ultimately is. It will remain exactly as it has been since the last mountain consented to become plain. You do not have to.
