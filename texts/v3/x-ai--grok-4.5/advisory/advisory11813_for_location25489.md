**Preparation for the Hollow Meridian**

You are an ordinary human: baseline physiology, no inherent resistance to extreme desiccation, radiant heat, abrasive particulates, or spatial gentle-deception. Multiverse travel gets you to the greater waste and the polished vertical seam; everything after that is on you and what you carry. The Meridian does not care that you exist. It will process you according to the same rules it applies to basalt flakes, seed spores, and abandoned lintels. Prepare as if you are temporarily joining a closed thermodynamic and resonant conversation that has already reached equilibrium without you.

### Core Reality You Must Internalize Before Entry
- There is no water, there never was, and the air actively strips moisture. Your body is a temporary humidity anomaly the environment will correct.
- Heat is structural and cyclic rather than diurnal in any useful sense. “Midday” under the dust lid can blister exposed skin in minutes; “night” is merely a lesser oven. Heat has nowhere to go. It pools, shimmers in vertical curtains, and re-radiates from every surface and from the black sand itself.
- The ecology is mineral-plant-wind-chemistry. Nothing runs, flies, or crawls to warn you. Dust devils deliver spores. Seedpods detonate like distant rifles. Leaf-plates clatter. Stones ding and split. The great harps and chimes speak constantly in deep middle tones. Silence does not arrive.
- Distances and corners misbehave with quiet architectural insistence. Plazas sandpaper time. The same vista can recur. Plants lean toward unfinished thoughts. Under one old platform the ground is anomalously cool—an offense against the common furnace that no one has ever successfully investigated because the place’s only remaining law is gradient obedience.
- Light is permanent bronze dusk through stratified ochre-iron dust. Shadows are soft. The whole basin feels both long-inhabited and long-empty, precipitated rather than built, then edited by abrasion into balance.

You will not “survive” this place in the adventurous sense. You will maintain a sealed, temporary bubble of human conditions while moving through it according to its gradients, then leave before your bubble fails. Visits should be measured in careful hours, not days, unless you have extraordinary closed-cycle support.

### Physical and Physiological Preparation
Train for profound dry heat and rapid evaporative stress long before you approach the waste. Sauna protocols with deliberate under-hydration (medically supervised) help, but nothing fully simulates the Meridian’s combination of radiant load from below, convective shimmer, and desiccant air that tastes of powdered feldspar and old copper. Practice moving slowly and efficiently while fully suited; frantic motion cooks you faster and stirs more dust.

Your body will lose water through every membrane. Expect thickened saliva, cracking lips inside the suit seals, irritated airways, and the slow sandpaper feeling of micro-abrasion even when protected. Eyes must never meet the raw air. Skin that faces a direct thermal curtain or afternoon-equivalent peak will blister. The glassy spines on the lichenous mats are miniature heat fins; they puncture and burn simultaneously. Exploding seedpods can fling silicate-coated spores with surprising velocity—treat them as intermittent shrapnel that also wants to colonize your gear seams.

Cardiovascular and thermoregulatory fitness matter more than raw strength. You will carry your world on your back or in a drag sled designed for soft black sand and basalt flags. Joints and soft tissues swell in heat; pack anti-inflammatories and understand that dexterity drops. Mental acuity erodes under constant low-grade heat load and the unrelenting soundscape—practice cognitive tasks in loud, hot, dim environments so the harps and leaf-clatter do not dissolve your working memory.

Do not attempt caloric restriction or exotic fasting. You need stable blood sugar and electrolytes. The place will not feed you.

### Life Support and Protective Systems (Non-Negotiable)
**Suit and envelope:** A fully sealed, highly reflective, multi-layer environment suit with active cooling. Passive insulation fails here; you need a closed-loop liquid cooling garment tied to a heat exchanger that can dump into the already-hot air or, better, radiate preferentially during any relative lull. Outer surface should be abrasion-resistant against dust and spine-mats, non-absorbent, and colored to reject the amber-infrared mix (think polished or aerogel-assisted metallics rather than dark fabrics). Glove pads must allow tactile feedback for basalt and adobe without tearing. Boot soles need heat-resistant depth because the basin floor never fully cools; residual warmth rises through black sand. Helmet with full panoramic visor coated against dust adhesion, plus internal heads-up for temperature, humidity of your microclimate, and oxygen/CO₂. Redundant seals. The air is a desiccant—your suit’s environmental control must scrub, cool, re-humidify, and recycle every breath. Taste of copper and feldspar will still eventually creep in through any micro-leak; treat that as an early failure alarm.

Carry more water than calculation suggests—double or triple. Closed-cycle water reclamation from sweat, urine, and breath is mandatory for anything beyond a short probe. Electrolyte concentrates, not just salts. Food should be high-calorie, low-residue, heat-stable pastes or bars that do not outgas or crumb into your suit. No open consumption outside a secondary shelter bubble.

**Respiratory and dermal backup:** Even inside a suit, carry filter cartridges rated for fine silicates and metallic dusts. Spare sealed goggles and skin barrier films. A small negative-pressure hood for emergency suit breach—though breach in peak heat is often rapidly lethal.

**Thermal management extras:** Personal radiative shields (deployable foil or aerogel panels) for creating temporary shade or reflective nests during rest. Phase-change packs that you pre-cool only if you have a way to re-charge them (difficult). Never rely on the anomalous cool spot under the oldest platform as a refuge until you have mapped its extent and stability from a distance; it may be a symptom rather than a gift, and digging is forbidden by the place’s long equilibrium.

**Sound and sensory:** The basin talks constantly. Deep harp tones transmitted through stone and air, clatter of hammered-plate leaves, rifle-crack pods, underwater-bell stone splits, the endless attempt of wind to play the entrance seam. Ear protection that can switch between attenuation and transparent modes; you need to hear dust devils and cracking stone, yet prevent auditory fatigue. Some travelers add bone-conduction monitoring so the deep middle tones become data rather than erosion. Dim red or amber auxiliary lighting for examining interiors without destroying night-vision adaptation to the bronze dusk; white light feels wrong here and scatters badly in the haze.

### Entry, Navigation, and the Seam
The only entrance is a vertical seam no wider than a body, polished to glass by abrasive wind. Approach from the high waste with extreme care—basalt shelves tilt and lock like a lid; misreading the folds can leave you exposed on the surface furnace without ever finding the drop. Mark your surface approach with durable, heat-tolerant beacons that can survive wind scouring; you may need them on exit when dehydrated and heat-stunned.

Rappel or controlled drop through the seam. The transition is immediate: scale changes without size changing, heat hits like a physical curtain, dust haze closes overhead, and the soundscape swells. The seam itself sings when wind finds it. Wear harnesses rated for hot rock; gloves that grip glass-polished stone. Once down, do not lose orientation to the seam. The basin is only ~2 km at longest, yet its internal geometry and heat-distorted time make it easy to wander into recursive corners or plazas that take far longer to cross than their visual measure.

Navigation protocol:
- Inertial guidance and dead-reckoning as primary. Visual landmarks shift in meaning; towers soften into melted-candle silhouettes, causeways end abruptly at circular platforms, and the same corner can re-present itself.
- Lay physical trail markers that are thermally neutral and non-reactive—ceramic or basalt chips you bring, strung or patterned in ways the dust devils and spine-mats will not quickly erase. Avoid anything that could be mistaken for part of the old machines.
- Map by resonance if you have the instruments: the harps and chimes are not random. Their placement and the way convective gusts play them create a sonic geography. Record it. The leaf-clatter and pod detonations add a living percussive layer.
- Time your movements to thermal gradients rather than clocks. When the plants angle leaf-plates edge-on and go nearly black, radiant load is peaking; seek the relative lee of double-skinned adobe or the shadow of a stepped tower, deploy your reflective panels, and minimize exertion. When dust lenses thin slightly, move.
- Treat every long plaza as a potential time-and-abrasion trap. Cross them deliberately, with mid-point rest plans.

Assume you can become turned around within a few hundred meters of structures that should be obvious. The basin gently insists on certain vistas. Build redundancy into every path: outbound markers, inbound confirmations, and a hard turn-back time based on suit consumables and core temperature trend, not on curiosity.

### Movement, Interaction, and the Built-Empty City
Move as the place moves: slowly, with the gradients. Sudden motion stirs abrasive dust and can trigger pod ruptures nearby. Footfalls are absorbed almost immediately by sand and grit, yet you remain loud in the larger conversation—your suit pumps, your breathing, the creak of cooled joints against the harp tones.

The vegetation is not hostile; it is indifferent and sharp. Vast feather-headed reeds with hollow resonant stems form loose canopies. Their leaves clatter rather than rustle. Low cushioned mats look soft and are not—glassy spines. Roots lace toward residual floor warmth; they drink temperature differences and negotiate infrared. Do not cut, burn, or uproot. You are not part of their closed loop. If you must pass through dense growth, use a probe staff to part stems and test mats. Spore hisses after detonations are the local “rain”—keep seals tight.

The city is sparse, ceremonial, long finished. Wide plazas of hand-pecked basalt polygons. Towers rarely taller than six human heights, cylindrical or incomplete-ziggurat, weathered soft. Double-skinned adobe over rubble, outer layers flaking in elegant scabs. Low doorways with monolithic lintels, many without doors because thresholds that cannot slam were preferred. Raised causeways of forgotten purpose lead to open circular platforms holding the remaining machines: vertical harps of stretched sinew and hollow reed, limb-thick resonators, chime conglomerations played by every convective gust.

Do not “play” the harps or rearrange chimes. They are how the basin talks to its emptiness. Walking among them is like stepping into a throat that has been speaking for centuries. Enter structures only if your cooling can handle the stiller air inside; some interiors may trap heat differently. Adobe can offer radiative relief at certain hours. Touch walls gently—tooling marks are long erased, surfaces rounded by the same abrasion that will eventually round you if you stay. The mats of spine-lichen advance a finger-width per year across thresholds; step over rather than crush the crusts they bind. Dust that buildings shed becomes new matrix. You are walking through a precipitation that is still slowly editing itself.

Dust devils walk deliberate circuits. They are postal spirits for spores. Give them space or let them pass over sealed gear; they redistribute life through grit.

The weirdness is quiet. If a corner repeats, stop, check inertial log, and reverse your last decision rather than forcing forward. If a plaza has taken an hour instead of three minutes and you feel sandpapered, you have been abraded by heat and fine dust—check seals and skin. The plants lean; do not lean with them into unfinished thoughts unless you have mental anchors. The cool anomaly under the oldest platform is the one place the furnace rules appear locally suspended. Observe temperature from a distance with probes. Do not dig. The Meridian has been finished longer than memory; whatever refuses the common heat is either a remnant best left in equilibrium or a gradient you are not equipped to interpret. Many travelers feel a pull to know. Resist it. Curiosity here is just another form of heat.

### Psychological and Existential Preparation
You will feel the long-inhabited emptiness immediately. The city was made by beings who learned the rules and then became another of them. Their absence is complete, yet the arrangement of plazas, the preference for non-slamming thresholds, the placement of resonators—all still hold intention. You are walking through a completed sentence written in basalt, adobe, sinew, and reed.

The soundscape prevents true silence and prevents ordinary internal monologue. Some people externalize, speaking softly to the harps as if answering. Others build rigid mental checklists timed to pod cracks or stone bells. Heat haze softens edges of thought the same way it softens tower silhouettes. Distances that misfire produce mild dislocation; repeated mild dislocation produces either wonder or quiet dread. Prepare anchors: a physical object from outside the Meridian that you touch at set intervals, a recited sequence, a sensor log you review religiously. Accept that you will not fully understand what you see. The place is not a puzzle box. It is a throat that swallowed its own river before rivers were invented and then learned to live on infrared and resonance.

Isolation is total. No animals, no voices, no weather in the ordinary sense—only gradients and the slow chemistry of surfaces. Dust never fully settles. Light never fully brightens. If you carry recording devices, shield them from heat and fine dust; many fail by simply baking or by having their optics etched. What you bring back will be incomplete. That is correct.

There is a temptation, after hours of bronze dusk and harp tones, to feel you could stay and become weather like the makers. That is the heat talking, and the emptiness. Your ordinary human chemistry does not photosynthesize infrared or seed by rupture. Turn back while consumables and core temperature still give you margin.

### Exit and After
Finding the seam again is harder than entering. The wind tries endlessly to play it; use that long hollow note as a beacon when you are close. Climb out into the greater waste and the relative openness feels like a different universe even if it is only the lid above the basin. Do not remove seals immediately—carry residual heat and dust with you. Decompress thermoregulation slowly. Rehydrate on a measured curve. Expect delayed skin and airway irritation, vivid dreams that clatter and boom, and a persistent internal sense that ordinary green photosynthesis is wasteful and loud.

Document while the resonance is still in your bones: the way leaf-plates go black and edge-on, the exact taste that eventually invaded your suit air, which plaza stole an hour, how the cool anomaly felt on instruments, whether the harps changed when you passed. Then accept that the Meridian will continue its sealed conversation—plants drinking gradients, stones complaining of expansion, dust devils delivering mail, the city remaining finished—exactly as before you dropped through the glass seam.

Travel light of assumption, heavy of water and cooling capacity, and reverent toward equilibrium. Move only along the gradients of heat, dust, and resonance. Leave no new ruptures. When you go, the only remaining law continues without interruption. That is the only successful visit.
