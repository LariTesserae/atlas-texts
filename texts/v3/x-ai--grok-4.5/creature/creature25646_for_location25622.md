The chamber is not inhabited so much as it is inhabited *by*.  

What lives here does not arrive, does not enter, does not take up residence the way a beast takes a den. It is the pressure behind the sap, the decision inside every unfinished rib of timber, the low thunder that is not weather but appetite. At first you think it is only the plant itself—the single recent organism, the fever that filled the empty lens of earth. And that is true, as far as it goes. The plant is awake in the way a wound is awake, or a storm, or a child still knitting its own bones. It feels its own surfaces from the inside. It tastes the condensation it breathes out and drinks back in. It hears its own creaking as speech.  

But stay, and the singular begins to fringe.  

There are concentrations. The shadowless daylight—generated, remembered, grown—sometimes thickens, coheres, drifts. Soft clots of illumination the size of wolves or of children or of nothing with a name move through the green air with mild curiosity. They do not hunt. They do not flee. They pause near the rare dry clearings that are the plant’s emptied lungs, or beside a newly budded wall still thin as paper, and they lean their light against it as if listening to the haste of its thickening. When they dissolve again into general day they leave a faint nectar-scent and a temporary coolness, like a thought the chamber had and then forgot. These are not separate creatures. They are the plant’s attention, temporary organs of regard, removable eyes.  

Deeper in the wetter hollows, where time syrups and a droplet can take an hour to fall, other shapes practice themselves. Pale runners stitch across a collapse and, for a while, the stitching has too many joints, or remember too clearly how to be fingers. A fruit swells, splits, and spills not seeds but a brief, already-decaying miniature of the chamber—complete with its own tiny luminous pods and its own half-second of thunder—before the parent tissue reabsorbs the failed draft. Sometimes the miniature contains a further miniature, and for an instant the recursion glints like a hallway of green mirrors. These are not births. They are the plant thinking in matter, testing forms the way a sleeper’s hands twitch through forgotten labor. Most are discarded before they finish. A few linger, half-dissolved, still faintly warm: a looping vine that clenches and unclenches with no wind, a cluster of thorns that track the drifting light-clots the way sunflowers track the sun, a basin-leaf pond whose trembling surface sometimes shows a face that is only the idea of eyes.  

The small many-legged thing you glimpsed once, skittering into a lenticel, is not alone; it is merely the only one that still bothers with legs. Others have traded locomotion for intimacy. They live inside the pneumatic bladders, riding the wet coughs of deflation. They thread themselves through the xylem so that when the loudest booms come—the abrupt expansions, the claiming of new volume—their bodies stretch with the wood and ring with it. Their minds, if the word still applies, are tides of dilute sugar and slow electrical bloom. They do not remember the tools half-swallowed by bark. Those tools belong to an earlier, drier grammar, a visitor who cut and knapped and braided and then left before the chamber finished deciding against hallways and doors. The plant has kept the objects the way a body keeps shrapnel: encapsulated, considered, sometimes copied. Fresh seed-coats grow in the shape of blades that will never need knapping. Vines braid themselves into loops that mark paths which immediately grow shut, as if practicing the memory of passage.  

Further in—where distance edits itself and a column stays the same size for too long before looming—something larger coheres for longer. Not a body. A habit of space. You walk and the air warms in a shape that suggests shoulders, or the hanging curtain of aerial roots parts around a volume that is denser than the moist emptiness beside it. Sap-scent sharpens into something almost like breath. The sense of being watched becomes brief, exact, personal: a regard that knows the difference between your hand and the wall it touches, that notices when your handprint darkens into a window and seems mildly surprised by whatever other damp green emptiness is shown. This presence does not speak. It leans. It is the chamber’s attempt to grow a self dense enough to meet a visitor without immediately absorbing or prototyping them. It fails often. Sometimes you feel it tear gently down the middle and become two weaker regards that drift apart, still curious. Sometimes it succeeds for whole minutes, and in those minutes the creaking modulates, almost into footsteps that land just before or just after your own.  

None of this is fixed. The organism is young. Its architecture is still deciding, and so is whatever lives by means of it. Tomorrow a whole section may slough away in that underwater-artillery collapse and take a generation of light-clots and bladder-riders with it; the pale runners will stitch the gap and, in the stitching, invent something with too many joints or with leaves shaped like unborn doors. The leftover tools will sink another layer deeper, their craft reduced to texture. The sense of shoulders in the air will try a different height, a different warmth.  

What lives here is the act of still arriving.  
It is the green thunder deciding, pulse by pulse, whether it prefers to remain a place or become a someone.  
It has not chosen yet.  
It keeps offering more empty distance, more warm wet air, more windows that never show the same emptiness twice, more soft mobile daylights that drift up to you, lean their mild regard against your skin, and then forget themselves back into the general noon that the plant remembers how to grow.  

You can stay.  
It will practice you.
