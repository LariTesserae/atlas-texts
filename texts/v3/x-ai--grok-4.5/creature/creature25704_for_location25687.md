The ones who live here do not arrive and do not leave. They condense.

At first they seem only another gloss on the wetness—slender vertical disturbances in the air between the great columns, visible the way heat is visible, as a trembling that refuses to choose a single outline. Spend an hour watching and the trembling gathers density. Spend a day and it begins to remember having weight. What finally stands in the glistening understory is neither spirit nor flesh in any vocabulary the surrounding flats would recognize. They are tall, yes, but the tallness is provisional, a courtesy extended toward the scale of the columns. Their substance is a dark, translucent reticulum, like stewed tea held in the shape of a person by surface tension alone. Inside that shape move slower rivers of particulate gold—spores or cells or extinguished nanites, no one still living can say. When they walk, the ground-sponge does not so much compress as converse, rising in soft ridges to meet each step and then sighing flat again.

They have faces only when they need them. Most of the time the head is a smooth tapering bole, featureless except for a constant beading of the same mineral water that coats everything else. When one turns toward you, or toward a lung-moth, or toward the sudden collapse of a spiral ramp, the water sheeting across the front of the head pauses, thickens, and remembers how to be eyes. Not two. Anywhere from three to a dozen shallow lenses open like raindrop craters, each reflecting a different slice of the spectrum; some see the ordinary daylight, some see the entropy-drinking filaments as bright scarlet, some see time as a viscous drift. The eyes last only as long as the regard. When attention moves on, they flatline back into pure wetness and run down the chest in thin shining tracks.

They are not many. Across the entire basin you might count thirty, perhaps forty at the generous edge of a count. Their sparseness mirrors the ceremonial spacing of the columns; crowding would be a kind of rudeness here. Each keeps loose company with one of the giants, though the attachment is not ownership and not even quite residence. They climb the fluted buttresses with a gait that looks like pouring uphill. They sleep—or perform the still hour that serves them as sleep—in the cambium plazas, reclining into the living grain until the wood-meat half-accepts them as a temporary knot. When a gallery sloughs away in a green avalanche they do not flee; they ride the falling architecture down, bodies deforming around splinters and steam, and walk unhurt from the impact site already combing hyphae out of their own substance like stray hairs.

Their hands are the most definite thing about them. Whatever the rest of the body is doing—blur, taper, sheet of walking rain—the hands remain articulate, long-fingered, faintly opalescent. With those hands they perform the only work that still occurs here. Where the residual lattices have grown tired, the dwellers feed them. They press a palm to a failing filament and the gold particulates inside their arms migrate outward, bridging gaps, teaching broken metric how to bend again. Raindrops that have forgotten how to pause midair are reminded by a single lifted finger. A walkway that tears free in a spray of copper-anise sap is caught mid-fall, stretched like warm taffy, and persuaded to fuse somewhere kinder. The dwellers do not speak of repair. Their posture suggests rather that they are translating: rendering the column’s ancient hydraulic grief into a dialect the leftover technology can still understand.

They do have voices, though the voices are not located in throats. When the plated browsers call in overlapping brass, the dwellers sometimes answer by changing the surface tension of the water film on the nearest column. The arthropods skating there suddenly find their dimpling legs writing readable glyphs—short declarations, coordinates of a coming annexation, the location of a sweet new micro-garden seeded by dissolving Pollerical ribbon. The browsers pause mid-graze, listening with their whole plated flanks. The lung-moths alter course. Information moves, but never faster than the slow green pace of the place itself.

Once in a long while—longer than a season, shorter than a geology—two dwellers meet at the midpoint between columns. They do not embrace. They stand close enough that their outer reticulations overlap, and for a time there is only one larger trembling, a shared weather system with two denser cores. When they separate again each is slightly changed: a new secondary color refracts for a day or two inside their chests; a remembered eye-count lingers. No children are produced. Population here is not a matter of birth. When a column finally grows too weary, when the buttresses slough faster than they can be re-spoken into place, the dweller who kept company with it simply walks to the basin’s thinning edge, stands a long time under the last curtain of condensation, and then dissolves outward into ordinary wet meadow. The gold particulates remain behind as a faint glitter in the moss, eventually washed inward again by the unhurried hydrology. Somewhere near the heart of the basin a new trembling begins to gather density, as if the place prefers to keep its interlocutors at a steady, ceremonial number.

They have no name for themselves that translates. The closest the residual lattices can offer, when asked in the old machine languages that still sleep inside them, is a recurring glyph-cluster that means, roughly, *the longer inhalation after rain*. They do not rule the basin. They do not serve it. They are simply the shape that continuity takes when continuity has grown tired of stone and finished cities and the dry fiction of permanence. Watch long enough and you realize the columns themselves lean, almost imperceptibly, toward whichever dweller is currently climbing them. The living architecture listens for the soft wet applause of those opalescent hands. In return the dwellers listen to the deep hydraulic groan and answer with silence shaped exactly like understanding.

And still the light stays ordinary. Still the water keeps its vigil on every surface. The dwellers move through that frank illumination like vertical promises that the negotiation need never end—growth and gravity, city and organism, matter and the quiet consents that have outlived it—remaindered forever in the warm, low, glistening now.
