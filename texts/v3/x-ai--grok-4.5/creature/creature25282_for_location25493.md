The first impression is not of a body but of a pressure: a attentiveness folded into the steam itself. You feel it most clearly when a sheet of falling water hesitates mid-collapse, its droplets hanging an extra heartbeat as though listening, then resuming their shatter into blood-warm mist. The basin is never empty. Something habitually inhabits the gradients—the exact interfaces where heat bleeds into cooler shadow, where silica-laden film crawls upward against gravity along the slow-swallowing rings, where the metallic moss brightens and dims in soft pulses that match no geological rhythm.

It does not arrive all at once. At first there are only concentrations. A thickening in the vapor above one of the terrace pools, where the copper-leaved tree stands with roots knotted through stone. The mist there refuses to drift; it coils tighter, takes on a faint internal shear, like silk being twisted by unseen hands. Light catches on suspended silica needles and briefly draws a suggestion of limbs—long, jointed too many times, tapering to points that never quite settle on whether they are fingers or root-tips or the dripping ends of unfinished spires. The suggestion dissolves when looked at squarely, only to re-form at the edge of vision, closer now to the central scaffold.

Then the rings answer. One of the overlapping circles telescopes outward with the sound of damp glass under stress and rotates until a helical sheet of water climbs its inner surface like a living staircase. Out of that moving water a shape wrings itself. Not born so much as wrung—squeezed from the humidity gradient the scaffold maintains with such obsessive care. It stands (the verb is imprecise) three times the height of the tallest fern, yet its substance is mostly transparency and heat shimmer. Where denser, it shows the same wet-ironwood darkness as the solitary trees, veined with glowing moss-light. Its upper portion frets into a crown of continually unfurling fronds that are neither leaf nor feather nor flame; they taste the air, sieving the microscopic silica rain and redirecting it in deliberate spirals toward the nearest lattice-spire. Everywhere the being touches, the stone softens and remembers new edges.

It is not alone, though solitude is its clearest habit. Others exist as harmonic echoes rather than separate creatures. A smaller one—barely the height of a man—crouches half-sunk in a saucer pool, its form more densely crystalline, as if it has chosen to wear the frozen-light material of the spires. Geometry crawls across its surface in the same restless way it crawls across the half-buried metal plates; when the patterns align for a moment with those on a nearby plate, both brighten and the plate sinks a fraction deeper, welcomed. Another presence never leaves the high curtains of droplets: a long, many-legged attenuation that scuttles only along the undersides of waterfalls, stitching them wider or narrower to adjust the mist budget of the entire basin. Each is solitary. Each maintains wide circles of untouched glistening rock around its preferred station, exactly as the ferns do. Crowding would collapse the delicate sentence the place is still writing.

Their movement is the movement of the basin itself slowed to visibility. They do not walk so much as allow imbalance to propagate through their density; a shift in the heat siphoned sideways by the rings becomes a step. Sometimes a limb simply ceases to occupy the air it held a moment before and resumes a body-length away, the intervening space left faintly warmer and smelling of first-cracked rock. They reshape as they go. A trailing edge drags through a moss carpet and the moss climbs aboard, becoming temporary skin; later the same edge skimmed across a fresh fracture and comes away armored in new black basalt ribs that steam gently. Nothing is discarded. Even their residual heat is folded back into the quiet cold zones the scaffold keeps in reserve.

Over hours (or days; time softens here the way stone softens under the dripping lattices) the central figure works the great rings. It does not braid them with tools. Its frond-crown brushes the air and the humidity gradient steepens or gentles. Water that should fall climbs instead, bathing the copper leaves until they gleam like fresh forging. A lattice-spire that has leaned too far is coaxed upright by a slow migratory cold front the being exhales; once vertical again, its tip flowers wider and begins to drip the clear fluid that teaches cliffs to grow new sharp edges overnight. The being pauses often, headless yet unmistakably listening to the soft tick of cooling silica, the delayed slap of water sheets, the nearly inaudible glide of geometry rearranging on the metal plates. Each sound is an unfinished clause. It answers with adjustments so minute they register only as a change in the way light pools inside the fractured blue lantern of the basin.

There is intelligence here, but not the kind that names itself. It understands heat and water better than ordinary matter should because it is the understanding given provisional shape. Memory exists as opacity: denser bands within their translucent bodies that record previous configurations of the scaffold, previous leanings of ridges still rising by fractions of a millimeter. When a fresh fracture opens with its soft ticking cry, one of the lesser beings will detach a portion of itself—literally peel a ribbon of moss-glow and ironwood density—and press it into the wound. The ribbon becomes a living seal that weeps at the same rate as the surrounding stone, teaching the new surface how to belong. In this way the inhabitants are both gardeners and garden, continually edited into the place that edits them.

At rare moments something like curiosity surfaces. A human-shaped density (you realize only afterward it deliberately approximated your outline) forms in the steam an arm’s length away. It has no face, yet the ferns above it angle their fronds downward as if offering shade it does not need. For a long while it simply matches the rhythm of condensation beading on your skin. Then one elongated tip extends and touches the back of your hand with a sensation exactly like the first warm mist: not scalding, not cool, simply the temperature of fresh blood remembering it was once ocean. Where it touches, a single translucent moss sporophyte seeds itself in your pores, glows once in soft emerald morse, and dissolves. You are left with the taste of wet iron and the certain knowledge that the basin has now written one more clause that includes you.

They do not speak. Speech would be too finished. Their conversation is the continuous rewrite of gradients, the percutaneous migration of silica, the slow telescope and re-angling of rings that keep ferns alive and stone dreamlike. When darkness eventually edges over the knife-ridged shoulders of the massif, the beings do not sleep. They only thin, spreading themselves across every wet surface until the entire hanging basin breathes with a single coordinated phosphorescence—the moss carpets, the dripping spires, the great scaffold, the watchers themselves—all of it one luminous grammar still conjugating heat into form, water into intention, and presence into the next necessary emptiness that will allow something else to arrive.
