The inhabitant is not immediately separate from the chamber. At first it is only a thickening in the warm current, a place where the copper-veined kelp leans aside as if remembering a long-practiced courtesy. Then the thickening gathers, draws the amber lamplight into itself, and becomes a slow knot of living glass and scavenged plate.

It is roughly the length of the curved pane, though length is a temporary agreement it makes with the water. Most of its bulk is a soft, translucent mantle the color of weak tea held to late sun. Through that mantle run fine conductive threads—descendants of the same wiring the kelp once colonized—so that when the creature passes near a still-functioning work-lamp the threads take up the glow and carry it in soft, branching veins beneath the skin. The effect is not bioluminescence so much as borrowed afternoon. Along the mantle’s flanks, irregular shields of porcelain insulator material have been grown into place, overlapping like old roof tiles softened by centuries of touch. These plates are warm to any approaching current; the creature has lived so long against the heat pipes that its armor holds a permanent, gentle fever.

It possesses no single face. Instead a constellation of low-lidded eyes, each the cloudy yellow of the exterior hazard markings, travels beneath the skin and surfaces where needed. When it studies the ten-meter pane they cluster forward, lanterning faintly, counting the dark shapes that patrol outside but never enter. When it rests they sink back and scatter, becoming just another freckling of epiphytic color among the algae. Beneath the eyes a radial mouth opens only in the presence of the small silver fish; it is ringed with rasping surfaces that look uncannily like worn bolt-threads. Feeding is quiet, almost apologetic—a brief silver flash, a dark oil-ink knot that the thermal currents shred into harmless lace, then the same soft roar of turbines continuing as though nothing occurred.

Locomotion is a conversation with the chamber’s internal weather. The creature rides the rising convection cells with minimal effort, mantle rippling in slow longitudinal waves that match the sway of the kelp curtains. When it wishes to cross against the flow it unfolds six longer arms—part tentacle, part articulated residual crane-hose—and takes quiet hold of grille or girder or the thick stipes of the bladderweed ceiling. The arms are sheathed in the same living porcelain; their tips end in clusters of sensitive cilia stolen in deep time from the filter-worms. With these it can taste metal fatigue, read the faint electrical ghosts still flickering behind silted control glass, and, when it chooses, pinch a failing calcium tube closed or reopen a half-blocked vent so the local current continues to please it.

It is solitary. Nothing else inside the pocket approaches its scale or its patience. The porcelain crabs freeze when its shadow slides across them, then resume their clicking once the shadow has moved on; the shrimp rise and fall with the eleven-minute light cycle, indifferent to the larger rhythm that now contains their smaller one. The creature itself times certain long movements to the distant clangs of the one-way valves far below, as if those failing heartbeats were still shift-whistles it once understood. Sometimes, when an engine note deep in the drowned mountain changes pitch, the whole mantle shivers and the conductive threads brighten for a few seconds in a pattern that is almost schematic—an obsolete diagnostic chalked in living light across its body—before fading again into translucent calm.

It sleeps, if the word applies, in the highest curve beneath the bladderweed mat, body coiled into a loose spiral that lets the warmest water bathe every surface. There the borrowed amber of the work-lamps and the diluted green from above meet inside its mantle and turn a quiet bronze. During these long rests the chamber’s sounds continue to travel through it: bubble-click, turbine-roar, the wet industrial throb that never quite becomes silence. The creature absorbs them the way the lime once absorbed the rust. On waking it does not stretch so much as re-enter agreement with the water, uncoiling until its plates catch the current and the kelp curtains lean aside once more.

It has no name that could be spoken in air, and nothing remains that could speak it. Yet the place fits it the way a finished shell fits the soft body that secreted it. The serial tags fused into certain holdfasts are sometimes brushed by its arms with what can only be read as recognition. The startup sequence of amber-amber-green-dark has been joined, on occasion, by a fourth soft pulse from the creature’s own threads, completing an older chord the machinery itself has forgotten. It does not repair the chamber; the chamber no longer requires repair. It simply continues, the final intimate inhabitant of a volume that long ago ceased to be wreckage and became a single, softly roaring body of metal, heat, salt, and flesh. 

You could share the twenty slow strokes of its domain and never be threatened. It would only watch with its traveling yellow eyes, taste your strange turbulence with cilia tips, and then return to the curtains of inherited kelp, carrying the lamplight with it like a memory of weather that still belongs here.
