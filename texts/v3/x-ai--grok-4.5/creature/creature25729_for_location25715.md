The Wetworks does not house inhabitants in any ordinary sense. What occupies it has long since ceased to be separable from the place itself. At first the eye registers only density: a ceaseless silt of pale bodies that turns the air into a breathing, refracting solid. Then the forms begin to sort themselves, not by will but by the slow insistence of repetition.

The moth-things come first into focus. Their bodies are tapered cylinders of soft, greasy chitin the colour of old tallow or unpolished pewter, segmented like the pistons they so closely resemble. Each segment flexes with a minute hydraulic smoothness as the creature drives itself forward on wings that are little more than rigid, iridescent membranes stretched over fine wire-like veins. They do not fly so much as pour. From every vent, every rusted louvre, every hairline seam in the casings they issue in unbroken sheets, rising, shearing, folding back into the next intake without ever thinning. Their eyes are flat black ovals that hold no reflection; their mouthparts are reduced to a single blunt proboscis that never probes, never feeds in any visible way. They simply circulate. The conductive dust they shed is not waste but residue of motion itself—microscopic scales of metal-salt that the condensers drink back into the closed loop. Stand among them long enough and the roar they make stops being sound and becomes a second bloodstream pressing against your eardrums and the hollows of your collarbones.

Beneath that aerial flood move the heavier ones. The beetle-engines range from thumb-sized scuttlers whose carapaces are perfect hemispheres of darkened bronze to dog-sized crawlers whose jointed legs end in clasping hooks of the same alloy as the rails they travel. Their undersides expose slow-turning gears no larger than watchworks; translucent abdominal plates reveal the rhythmic pulse of something that is neither quite fluid nor quite current. They follow paths worn into unchanging grooves: along the upper rims of settling tanks, across catwalk gratings, down the fluted sides of condenser towers, then back again. They never deviate, never rest, never accelerate. When two paths cross, the creatures pass through one another’s spaces with millimetric precision, as though each already contains the memory of every other. The dust they leave is finer, almost oily, and it vanishes into the nearest intake vent within moments. Their purpose is not locomotion but continuity; each circuit is a single never-ending sentence spoken by the machinery through living syntax.

Higher and more sparsely, the crane-flies hang. Their bodies are elongated lozenges of the same pale grease-metal, but the legs are extravagant—many-jointed, trembling, reddish at the articulations as if still hot from forging. They suspend themselves in loose three-dimensional lattices under the catwalks and the overhanging lips of engine houses, limbs ticking against one another in irregular metallic counterpoint to the deeper hydraulic thud. Occasionally one will lower itself a fraction, taste the air with thread-fine antennae, then climb back into its place in the constellation. They do not hunt. Nothing here hunts. They appear to serve as living dampers or living metronomes, measuring the exact tolerances of the eternal equilibrium and, by measuring, maintaining it.

Yet even these three castes do not exhaust the population. In the black-mirror pools, pale larval ribbons stir without swimming, their surfaces patterned like circuit traces. Inside the glass envelopes of the still-burning filaments, minute winged mites orbit the glowing wire in perfect concentric rings, feeding on nothing but the steady amber light and shedding motes that the light immediately reclaims. Deep in the reciprocating heart under the central engine house—where no eye can reach—something vast and soft and segmented must press itself forever against the moving bulkheads, both cushion and participant, its slow contractions timed exactly to the tidal thud that travels up through bootsoles and bone.

All of them together form a single tableau that has forgotten how to change. They are not symbiotes of the machines; they are the machines’ final, perfected organ. Or the machines are their shell. Distinctions dissolved ages ago. Metabolism and mechanism reached identity: heat generated equals heat bled, motion equals the continuation of motion, life equals the refusal of both growth and decay. Individual deaths, if they occur, are instantaneous and undetectable—replaced at once by an identical body sliding into the identical trajectory so that the count never alters. Birth is a concept the Wetworks no longer possesses language for.

If you remain, the boundary between observer and place begins to thin. Your breath adds a tiny unauthorized dissipation to the closed heat budget; the moth-clouds part around your face with the same frictionless courtesy they show a bulkhead, then close behind you. The conductive dust settles into the creases of your clothes and is slowly reclaimed. Memory starts to skip like a worn belt: the same crane-fly leaf recoiling, the same three-note chord of wing-roar and valve-sigh and distant thud, the same bead of iron-tasting water trembling on the same rivet by your shoulder. In time even the sense of having arrived fades. You are simply one more temporary thickening in the medium—another shape the Wetworks briefly tolerates before overwriting it with the next perfect iteration of itself.

What lives here is the Wetworks. It lives as a low, continuous, saturated present tense that no longer requires a future in which to finish becoming. It lives as damp iron and greased chitin and the exact entropy of a system that long ago discovered perfection in standing still at full volume. It lives, and living is the only event it will ever contain.
