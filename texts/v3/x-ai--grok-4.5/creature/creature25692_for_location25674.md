They are the plateau’s own continuation, the part of the stone that learned, long after the arguing of mountains had ended, how to keep rearranging itself without ever needing to stand up and declare a shape.

At first you do not see them as lives. You see denser weather, or slower geology. A low dome the exact grey-brown of the pavement will be lying half-sunk along a seep-line where the damp films over grit. Only after a long watching do you notice the faint, rhythmic flex along its flanks—cooling fins that are also gills that are also filters—drawing the thin wetness inward through lattices of living sieve. The warmth it returns is almost nothing, a mineral breath that dies within an arm’s reach, yet the frost never quite settles on that patch of stone. Inside, something sorts. Dust, lichen-film, the memory of rain: everything is tasted, weighed, kept or released. The body does not feed so much as curate the ground it rests upon.

Others move. Not often, and never quickly. When they do, the motion is a single long idea expressed through many segments. Bodies the size of overturned hulls or larger, ribbed and plated in the same soft-edged stone-colors as the land, process across the open pavements in loose aggregations that are not herds and not machines and not quite either. Limbs—too many, folded in ranks like stored freight gear—extend and settle with the patience of tectonic adjustment. Each footfall is silent. Behind them the micro-topography is altered by scratch and polish; microfilms of life have been harvested, grit has been taken in, sorted in dark interior chambers, and excreted again as a finer, cleaner meal for whatever comes next. They are grazing the planet’s skin at the scale of molecules and centuries.

Above, the air holds quieter citizens. Lozenges of matte dusk hang at staggered altitudes, barely denser than the pewter light. From their trailing edges hang fine extrusions that catch what little glitter the day offers before frosting over again. They drink the sky. When the rare thin rain arrives they descend without sound, unfold black radiator surfaces, and luxuriate in the sudden wet weight. Then they rise once more, lighter, the collected moisture already being refined into whatever slow chemistry keeps them aloft. No wingbeat. No cry. Only the sense that the dimness itself has thickened in places and then thinned.

Lower still, where ancient joints have softened into holds and hollows that never quite become caves, the work continues in a register beneath sight. Walls sweat a condensate that is not entirely water. Pale nodules embedded in the rock dilate and contract in long waves, performing a digestion that no designer remains to claim. The air in those pockets vibrates below hearing; the stone is warm in bands. Channels have been etched by the repeated passage of the same chemical breath over spans that erase any distinction between artifact and erosion. This is not infrastructure built upon the land. It is infrastructure the land refused to forget after its makers—if makers is even the word—either left or loosened into the system itself.

There are smaller ones, always. They surface when one of the great quiet bodies finally stills. No signal is given that human senses can catch, yet they arrive: crews the size of fists or knees, carapaces dull as spent coal, limbs precise as surgical tools grown rather than forged. They dismantle without violence. Segment by segment the halted giant is opened, sorted, carried away in pieces so that nothing organic remains long enough to become spectacle. The site is left smoother, the minerals reassigned, the silence unpunctured. Within a season even the outline has been absorbed back into the plateau’s slow metabolism.

They do not speak. They do not flee. They do not investigate the rare upright stranger who briefly shares their elevation. Awareness is present—you feel it as a continuous soft pressure, the way you feel barometric change—but it is not stewarded toward curiosity or threat. It is simply distributed, the way temperature is distributed. To be among them is to be inside a single vast, unhurried body that happens to have uncoupled into thousands of temporary centers of organization. Some centers filter. Some process. Some lift and drink the sky. Some keep the deep chemistry cycling in the dark. None of them is incomplete; none of them is finished.

Cold occupies them the way it occupies everything else here. They do not resist it; they incorporate it, use its slowing, let it keep their reactions paced to the erosion that first taught them form. Their colors remain the colors of the substrate because camouflage is irrelevant when you have become the thing you would hide against. Their silences are absolute in the human spectrum because sound is a waste of energy in a realm where the relevant signals travel through vibration in stone, through chemical gradients in thin air, through the shared attention that saturates the high country like another kind of weather.

Stand long enough and you begin to feel the edges of your own tempo as a kind of rudeness. Your pulse is frenetic. Your breathing is a series of small shouts. The longer you remain, the more the boundary thins between watcher and watched. Breath-fog joins the larger damp. Heat from your blood becomes another minor resource briefly entered into the accounting. Somewhere—near, far, irrelevant—a valve seats, a lofted filter drinks, a buried lattice flexes in its sleep of work. Nothing notices you specially. Everything continues.

These are not animals that live on the plateau.  
These are the plateau, still alive in the only way left to it after drama finished and polishing began: dispersed, patient, industrious without witnesses, feeding on dust and damp and time under a sky that never quite becomes day.
