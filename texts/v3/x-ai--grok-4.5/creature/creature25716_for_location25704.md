The plain does not host life so much as it continually misremembers it.

What moves here first appears as hesitation in the grinding. A local quietening, yes—the rumor is true—but the quiet is not absence. It is a thickening. In a circle perhaps three meters across, the wet-stone scream drops half an octave and the suspended rock flour begins to spiral, very slowly, as if deciding whether to become solid. Inside that spiral something stands that has no firm outline. It is roughly the height of the black reeds, but it is made of the same fever-warm mud that weeps everywhere else, only denser, wrung tighter by some internal decision. Water runs down it in the same fine beads that run down the basalt, yet the beads follow deliberate paths: they gather at what might be joints, pause, then choose new courses. When the thing shifts its weight the ground does not imprint; instead a faint stair-ghost of cooler clay rises under its presence and is immediately reabsorbed. It has been standing there for less time than a breath, or for longer than the last mountain wore itself smooth—the plain refuses to distinguish.

These are the quieter ones. They do not travel so much as they allow different versions of the plain to arrive around them. You can watch a translucent plate of ancient groundplane slide through one’s torso without resistance; the being simply incorporates the older texture for a while, lets frost form along a former edge, then sweats it away. Their attention is what damps the turbulence. Where they gather—never more than three or four, and always at distances that feel negotiated rather than chosen—the continuous low chewing of continents softens enough that a different sound can be heard: a soft, rhythmic pressing, like many palms flattening wet paper in unison. It is the only rhythm the plain ever permits.

Farther off, rarer, the heavy movers declare themselves as traveling dimples in the mist layer. They are not larger in any simple sense; they are denser with sequence. A single passage can take the better part of a day to cross a field of view, and in their wake the temporary silver eyes of standing water remain a few heartbeats longer than usual, reflecting the brownish soffit with a brief, uncharacteristic clarity. When one of them comes near enough that the air grows heavier against the chest, the outline resolves into something blunt and segmental, like a wall of the dark algae that has decided to stand upright and then forgotten how to stop. It progresses by folding successive moments of the plain beneath itself and leaving them slightly reordered. Behind it you may find a low ring of fitted stone that was not there before, or a cube of the darker material already half-softened, as if the mover extends the old conversation the structures began. It does not build; it rearranges the argument so that building once happened.

Neither kind has eyes. Sight is a poor instrument when light itself has no loyalty and edges refuse to keep their agreements. What they possess instead is a pressure sense tuned to the internal tides of the water-laden air and to the shear planes slipping underfoot. They taste the rock flour on what pass for skins, reading the memory of compression the way other creatures might read wind. When two of the quieter ones meet, they stand for a long while with their bead-paths intertwining in the space between them; water is exchanged, and with it whatever faint pattern each has managed to keep stable inside the rewriting. Sometimes after such a meeting a short length of black reed nearby will stand a little straighter, or a wall segment will lean at an angle that almost suggests shelter before the turbulence corrects it.

There are smaller lives still, if the word applies. In the sudden white curtains of vertical water that roar into existence and extinguish themselves, brief brightnesses flicker—threads of something almost translucent that ride the pressure failure for the few seconds it lasts, tasting the disagreement between regimes. When the curtain collapses they are gone, or they have been folded into the next version of the mud. The dark algae itself is not passive; under the old-tea light it slowly migrates, concentrating in the shapes of former footprints that no longer have makers, then dispersing again when the plain forgets those shapes. Once in a great while the algae blooms in reverse: a patch of ground becomes so light-absorbing that it falls out of the present texture entirely, leaving a soft crater of absolute matte that the water cannot immediately fill. Things avoid those craters. Even the heavy movers alter course around them, dimpling the mist in a long curve that takes hours to complete.

And there is one further presence, quieter than the quiet ones, discusable only by what it prevents. In certain widely scattered places the endless warm murk thins just enough to suggest a memory of sky—not blue, never blue, but a higher brown. Nothing lives there. Or rather, something lives by keeping that thinning from becoming real. The structures nearest those places are always more thoroughly softened, their cubes almost spherical, their rings worn to gentle welts in the clay. The grinding never quite reaches them. It is as if an intelligence that finished its work early, or sideways, still exerts a residual preference: that a few square meters remain slightly less rewritten than the rest. No body anchors that preference. It is simply a stubbornness in the wet paper, a place where the eraser forgets to pass.

All of them—mud-walled standers, sequence-dense movers, curtain-riders, algae that enacts absence, and the sourceless stubbornness—are native to the plain’s refusal of repose. They do not survive the turbulence; they are its way of tasting itself. The water keeps working through them. The rock accepts their temporary shapes and then reclines again. When a thin black reed sheds another leaf, one of the quieter ones may turn (or what passes for turning) and let the leaf adhere to its side for a while, adding the leaf’s brief vascular pattern to its own bead-paths before both are surrendered back to the slurry. Nothing is kept. Everything is participated in.

The plain continues. The horizon stays exactly where it was. And whatever lives here lives by remaining unfinished, the same way the erosion is unfinished, the same way the mountains’ long argument is still being settled in every warm, wet, endlessly rewritten step.
