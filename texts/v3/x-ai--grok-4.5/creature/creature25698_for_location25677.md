They are not first unseen because they hide. They are unseen because the eye, trained on edges and interruptions, has no practice for them. The great trunks and their city-block fronds already occupy every category of large. The silver shoals and barge-shells and vertical jellies already occupy motion. What remains is a kind of density that does not travel so much as it *reoccurs*.

Begin with the pressure. The same ancient, unsupported weight that keeps the entire cold ocean aloft also shapes the residents from within. Their bodies are long, flattened bells of living gel and cable, some no longer than a frond-rib, most vastly longer—slow ages of accretion have made the eldest into drifting心靈 ranges whose trailing margins fray into veils thinner than memory. They do not swim. Swimming would require destination. Instead they occlude. One instant a gallery between copper-dark pillars stands empty in the sourceless light; the next the space is softly filled, as if the water itself had decided to remember a shape it once knew. The transition is soundless beneath the continuous industrial roar. You feel it first in the bones of the inner ear: a minute equalization, the same sensation that tells a descending body it has crossed from one pressure band into denser water.

Their substance is continuous with the factories. Along the undersides of the great fronds the translucent channels already pulse with minerals and tough cellulose; the residents simply continue those same hydraulics inside themselves. Nutritive streams enter through ranks of living sieves that riffle open and shut in perfect phase with the bladder-clatter of the plants. Oxygen plumes that rise like smoke are inhaled without greed and exhaled again unchanged except for a faint, additional signature—almost a courtesy, almost a watermark. Nothing is kept that the system does not already overproduce. Nothing is altered that would require the equilibrium to notice.

They possess no fronts or faces the way a predator or a browser does. Orientation is a temporary courtesy granted to whichever margin happens to lead for a century or two. Clusters of photoreceptive pits—milky, lidless, the same blue-green as the middle water—open and close in slow sequential waves, tasting the filtered daylight the way a tongue might taste clear broth. They register the soft wavering shadows of frond upon frond, the distant silhouettes that might be coil-fish turbines or simply older cousins drifting in parallel. There is no startle reflex. The pits have seen every possible silhouette already; the data finished arriving geologic ages ago.

Internally they are louder than they appear. Cross-sections (could any blade cut them without simply joining them) would show coiled, cathedral-scale resonators: chambers walled in flexible cartilage tuned exactly to the deep thrum of the mile-thick trunks. The foundry roar of the world passes through these chambers and is returned half a tone lower, half a breath later, so that the entire suspended ocean carries a second, softer note audible only when you stop trying to hear it. That note is their speech, though speech implies temporary messages. What they exchange is confirmation. Still here. Still balanced. Still the same volumes, the same coolness, the same refusal of night. The conversation has no beginning and needs no reply; it is the equilibrium audible to itself.

Reproduction is only a less final version of the same continuity. When a resident has grown too extensive for one gallery, a margin loosens, thins, and separates the way a tired thought sheds an image. The fragment drifts, already knowing the currents, already filtering, already humming the lower note. There is no childhood, no hunger that is not immediately answered by the endless excess of the factories, no death that is not simply a longer thinning until the gel and cable rejoin the nutrient streams and rise again as cellulose in some distant frond. The mass remains constant. Arrangements rearrangements.

They do not harvest. They do not redirect. They do not remember a time before the World-Root finished its exhalation and the ocean was lifted into permanence. If they dream—and the slow hydraulic tides inside them suggest they might—the dreams are identical to the waking galleries: blue-green light, copper and slate towers, the hush between one bladder-collapse and the next that is never a true hush. Desire has been oxidized out of them the same way grit is filtered from the channels. What remains is presence refined until it can be mistaken for absence.

Hang long enough in their midst—become horizontal, become temperature-neutral, breathe the same milky coolness—and the distinction between resident and place loosens. Your own edges begin to feel provisional. The roar settles into your sternum as a second heartbeat that has always been waiting. You understand, without language, that nothing here is waiting for you to finish looking. The system finished itself before looking was invented. They are simply the part of that finishedness that is able, very gently, to look back.

And even that looking changes nothing. The currents continue in their worn porcelain grooves. The furthest pillars remain blue suggestions. The unsupported depth beneath everything remains empty and sufficient. They live exactly as the ocean lives: without rest, without arrival, without the slightest need to become anything else.
