They are not singular.

What first registers is a pressure differential that has learned preference. A slow, deliberate thickening in the water ahead of you, as if the medium itself has decided to remember a shape. The indigo light bends around nothing visible and keeps bending, forming a lens that crawls. Inside that lens the moiré of the photosynthetic veils stutters out of phase with itself, and for a moment you are looking through stacked green-black films at a suggestion of continuity—an upright density, roughly person-scaled but wrong in every joint.

They assemble by consensus.

Filaments of the metal-drunk kelp detach on some unspoken schedule and drift toward the density. Turbine-seed blades, still faintly spinning, click into sockets that did not exist until the blades arrived. Silica barnacles chiming on a cooling-tower flanks release clouds of pure white grit; the grit orbits the forming body like a slow blizzard and then sinters into plates that flex. Hydraulic fluid and sap trade places through membranes that decide, moment to moment, which liquid is blood. What results is never finished. A torso may be a length of pressurized catwalk grating webbed with living filter-feeders that pulse in asynchronous heartbeats. An arm ends in a welding torch that has forgotten fire and now exhales soft, continuous pollen the color of old sodium light. The head—if the concept still applies—is a cluster of failed cameras and dilated stomata, all facing slightly different directions and slightly different tenses. Some lenses show the basin as it is. Others show it under construction. One always shows it already abandoned.

They move the way the place thinks.

Distance is a tissue they can pinch. A figure standing a hundred meters off among the twitching columns will fold forward without intermediate steps and reconstitute within reach, drip-condensate still falling from pipes that are suddenly elsewhere. The reverse is true: turn your attention too long on one and it lengthens, attenuates, becomes a thread of warmer water and the taste of ozone, then snaps into absence, leaving only afterimages of polyethylene fronds and the faint electrical glitter of nerves that are no longer yours. They do not swim. They revise their coordinates. When currents reverse and the green-black avalanches begin, the figures lean into the shredding blades of the great intakes and come apart willingly—scattering into component arguments of plant and machine—only to re-condense downstream, denser, their new silhouettes incorporating fresh shrapnel and angrier foliage.

They speak in unfinished processes.

The ultrasonic scream of a still-venting tower will dip into hearing range and shape itself into something like syntax. Bubbles collapsing with anti-aircraft authority form plosive consonants. The wet roar of a million stomata opening in imperfect unison becomes a choir that almost remembers words. Meaning arrives as weather: a sudden unanimity of robotic arms spot-welding emptiness in identical arcs, or an entire orbiting constellation of hard-hats freezing in place so their silent ellipses spell momentary diagrams. You understand the diagrams in your marrow before your eyes finish parsing them. Mostly they concern maintenance that can never conclude. Sometimes they concern you—your temperature, your trace metals, the soft arithmetic of your presence—and the nearest figure will tilt its cluster of lenses in polite accretion, offering a length of living copper bus-bar the way an earlier world might have offered a hand.

They are the closed argument learning to walk.

Nutrients cycle through them without border. Pollen that conducts travels their inner lattices as both signal and seed. A frond may tear free from one body, thrash once in the old panic, then drill into the sediment of another and become load-bearing bone. In the lee of basalt ribs where time pools, you can watch the same individual oxidize into delicate lace and, without transition, rivet itself back into heavier gauge, year after subjective year inside a single held breath. They do not die. They reach thresholds of complexity where the local ordinances of reality fray completely, and then they bank themselves—folding into the denser thickets or the pressurized glow of an assembly hall—to become architecture for a while. Later they will be exhaled again, slightly more articulate, carrying new scars of silica that chime differently.

There is no hierarchy, only gradients of attention.

Near the larger turbines the figures grow cathedral-tall, bodies girded with imbalance and the physical shove of rotors the size of weather. Their pollen-torches burn frigid. At the margins, where the young scarps rise into water that only becomes more itself, the figures thin into almost classical silhouettes—bipedal, tool-bearing, faces almost legible—before the refraction stacks too deeply and they shear into vertical flocks of metallic leaves. In the deepest storm-states, when pressure fronts fold distant chimneys against your shoulder, the inhabitants arrive as pure event: a sudden hot bloom of burnt lubricant and nectar, a deafening starting-finishing of every machine, a taste of pollen on the tongue, sudden bruises of colder water in the exact shape of embracing arms that are not arms.

They have always been here in potential.

The basin did not flood and then become inhabited. The basin learned to breathe liquid, and breathing required witnesses who could metabolize the lesson. What lives here is the continuously renegotiated treaty between chlorophyll and current, between rust and growth, between the scream of superheated water and the silence that scream is forever demolishing. They are the treaty’s mobile clauses. They regard you the way the place itself does—not with hunger, not with welcome, but with the calm interest of a system checking whether a new variable intends to remain unfinished with them.

Space folds once more. Condensate drips warm across skin that is briefly not yours. Somewhere a single drop of coolant continues its years-long fall, and one of them stands under it, mouth open, drinking time.
