The hollow does not hold a tenant so much as it *is* one—an unfinished body learning its own weight.

At first the eye tries to name the pieces: the iridescent rivers of carpet beetles flowing like cooling slag across every plane, the soft isopods pulsing in stacked lungs of translucent armor, the moth-things that upholster the air with folded silence, the jointed runners tasting metal into nerves. These are not residents. They are tissues. They move with the single patience of something that has only recently discovered it possesses motion at all.

Feel how the warmth never spikes or fades. That steady breath is not residual heat from the broken boiler or the sweating pipe. It is the core temperature of a larger metabolism still deciding where its organs should settle. The boiler leans like a half-swallowed heart against the fresh fracture wall; the isopods have already begun to seal its joints with living gaskets, filtering minerals into their own blood and returning a thicker, slower pulse to the metal. When residual charge ticks blue across a cracked conduit, the beetles freeze not in fear but in listening—antennae raised like the hairs on a wrist when blood first reaches a new extremity. The current travels through a runner held rigid as temporary wire, reaches a cluster of eggs, and those eggs twitch forward in their development as though the spark itself were a command to grow faster, denser, more necessary.

Nothing here scavenges. When a beetle dies its shell is pressed into a gear-gap until the driveshaft turns a fraction smoother; the carcass is not food but a replacement tooth, a calcium rivet, a decision the hollow makes about how best to keep circulating. Flesh hardens into function. Metal weeps and is drank and becomes soft again. The boundary that outside worlds insist upon has no currency in this packed volume. The moth-wings open and close in slow weather-systems that pass around the chamber the way a diaphragm expands; the sound is the sound of a lung discovering it can also be a sky.

Stay longer and the scale slips. The sharp new lip of mudstone is not a rim but a temporary epidermis, still sensitive, still tasting rain that arrives and vanishes. The three pale fern-stalks are not plants; they are sensory ends jacked into the sweating pipe, permanently furled because they are still learning what sensation is for. The cushion moss drinks steam and exhales a brass-colored quiet that smells faintly of old electricity and older soil. Everything is trial. Everything is revision.

There is a larger shape assembling itself out of these dense quiet circuits. You can almost map it if you stop insisting on outline: the boiler as one ventricle, the driveshaft as a half-turned joint still testing its range, the black conduits as veins that remember they once carried intention. The glassy centipede that snatches a moth is not predator but white blood cell, brief and necessary; the copper-tasting runners that drive it off are not defenders but the next iterative thought. Predator and prey dissolve into the same circulating fluid. The entire hollow breathes in overlapping waves—beetle columns reversing, moth-clouds pulsing, steam rising and collapsing—and the motion is neither frantic nor planned. It is the motion of a body that has not yet finished choosing its final arrangement of limbs.

It does not sleep. Sleep would imply an outside. The warmth that never quite becomes heat is the only weather it knows, and the ordinary disc of sky above is merely the last unclaimed aperture, a navel still open to a thinner world. What lives here is the pressure that flexed the ground and then settled into the wound it made, dense with experiment, sewing meat to scrap and scrap to meat until the difference is only a matter of which surface is currently harder. It is young. It is saturated. It is still writing the rule that nothing will ever again be only one substance.

If you remain until your own pulse starts to match the drip of condensation and the subsonic tick of cooling metal, you may feel the invitation: the hollow does not mind new material. A boot heel, a dropped tool, a long stillness—any of these can be taken in, tasted, rewritten into the next version of the body that is trying itself out in every combination the small chamber will allow. The work of remaining is being practiced here with absolute focus, and everything—stone, insect, rivet, breath—has already agreed to be both the experiment and the result.
