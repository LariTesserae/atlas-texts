The first things you notice moving are not the dwellers; they are only the weather of the place made flesh. The mite-gliders sheet across thermal seams in silver blizzards that never land. The lantern-lizards unfold, fill with violet fog, and lance from wall to wall like flung notes of silenced music. The translucent herds phase through dust and root-mat, their hooves printing perfect negative spaces that the wind immediately unremembers. All of this is hunger in motion, charge stolen and restolen, a continuous bright theft. Yet none of it stays. None of it claims the corrie the way a resident claims a room by the simple fact of returning to the same corner after every absence.

What lives here stays by becoming the corners.

They begin as thickenings in the root-mat. Not growths exactly—more like decisions the living mortar makes when the machines hum a particular unresolved interval. A mound of knitted stone and dry moss rises overnight, hollows itself, and begins to breathe the violet fog in a rhythm slower than weather. Over weeks the mound attenuates, strengthens, learns edges. It pushes up into a posture that is neither plant nor animal nor remnant technology, though it borrows freely from all three. Limbs, when they appear, are jointed like the slender spires: too many angles, none of them load-bearing in ordinary gravity. The residual geometry of the old toroids leaks into them; sometimes a leg will fold out of sequence and the being will hang for a moment in mid-step while the air howls past, then resume as if time had merely cleared its throat.

Skin is the same matte-dry abrasion as the fronds: oxblood shading to verdigris where the static charge pools. It never sweats. It sheds a fine feldspar dust that the lower mosses immediately reclaim. Across the torso and the long, vane-crowned head runs a lattice of faint circuitry—not tattooed, not implanted, simply continuous with the half-swallowed machines beneath the floor. Slow light crawls there whenever the deeper tone of the stone rises. Their eyes (two, or four, or a shifting constellation depending on how the light is fracturing that hour) are the same perfect mirror as the floating sphere: they reflect a sky that is never quite the one above the corrie. When several of them regard one another, the reflections nest and the air between them thickens into brief corridors of altered inertia. Leaves hang motionless. A lantern-lizard freezes mid-rocket. Then the corridor collapses and everything resumes its scream.

They do not eat in any way a gut would recognize. They stand for long hours with their lance-like feet driven into the root-mat, and the charge flows. They steal from the plants the same way everything else does, but they also return something—an ordering, a local reduction of the machines’ endless quarrel. Where one of them has stood, the dry fog pools more calmly. The vanes track the sun with less frantic radar jitter. The micro-erosion on nearby stone briefly loses its Markov randomness and enlarges into deliberate scallops, like writing that forgets itself as soon as it is read.

They move rarely, and when they do it is with the theatrical emptiness of the corrie itself: long stillnesses broken by sudden, permeable bursts. A single dwellers will unfold across thirty meters in a blur of dry-paper thunder, collapse into a crevice, and be overgrown by moss before the echo finishes. Another will simply phase—become translucent as the herds, sink into the powder of salt and feldspar, and reappear on the opposite wall having crossed the void without crossing it. They leave no tracks the wind can erase, only a lingering warmth in the stone that the plants lean toward.

There are never many. Twelve, perhaps. Twenty on the rare days when the mirror sphere emits a low, continuous chime and the residual geometry thickens into visible halls. They do not speak aloud; the high rim would only fling the words into rotating noise. Instead they share the recirculated weather of oils and static. A thought leaves one body as a change in the violet fog’s charge, travels the root-mat, and arrives in another as a slow bloom of light under the skin. What they think about is mostly maintenance: which toroid is arguing loudest with the living skin today, which spire needs its impossible lean corrected by a touch that lasts three days, where the next cistern should bulge upward so the fog has somewhere safer to pool. Underneath those practicalities runs a longer, quieter contemplation—the settled patience of the place itself. They remember (or the machines remember through them) that the corrie once hung empty, a raw wound of wind-scoured stone. They remember being the first decisions the moss made once the builders had gone or dissolved or become the toroids. They do not mourn. Mourning would require a version of time the residual geometry no longer permits.

Sometimes one will stand at the jug-handle lip and look out into the blue distance that falls away on three sides. The thin air goes in and out of its lungs like cool glass. It will raise a limb and the translucent vanes of the nearest pole-plant will swivel in perfect echo. For a moment the entire hanging world—plants, fauna, machines, dweller—aligns into a single complex chord. The silver scream of wings drops into harmonic relation with the stone’s deep tone. Light stops strobing. The dust ceases to blow. Then the chord frays, the throngs resume their furious near-invisibility, and the dweller turns back into the corrie as if the vista had been only another mirror reflecting a sky that does not exist.

They are not immortal. Excess charge builds up; a body will one day refuse to fold, or fold too far, and simply open into the root-mat again. Moss pillows over the place within hours. The circuitry light migrates into a neighboring toroid. By the next thin dawn a new mound is already thickening somewhere else, ready to learn edges. Nothing is new. Nothing is still. The room that is also a storm keeps rearranging its furniture, and what lives here is both the furniture and the hand that moves it, the garden and the void, the ruin that invents itself one deafening, patient moment at a time.
