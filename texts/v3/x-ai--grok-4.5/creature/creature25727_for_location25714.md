The first signs are not bodies but absences that remember shape.

In the narrow vertical shafts where daylight arrives already thrice-refracted, the frost does not form evenly. Here and there it thins into curved vacancies—scalloped opacities the length of a forearm, repeated at irregular heights as though something has pressed itself against the cold several times and then withdrawn, taking a little of the night’s glaze with it. The basalt beneath these vacancies is smoother than the surrounding stone, polished by a pressure that was never quite heavy enough to crack, only to burnish. If you wait through several of the slow rotations of a courtyard, you may notice that the vacancies migrate a finger’s width, always following the same patient spiral that the luminous load-threads themselves obey.

Deeper in, where walls share membranes and corridors fold through themselves, the granular blue slurry that refuses to crystallize sometimes holds temporary impressions. Not footprints. Something closer to the negative of a resting posture: a long, tripartite depression, wider at one end, tapering through two articulated narrowings, then flaring again into a splayed, almost flower-like termination. The slurry glows a little brighter inside these molds for an hour or two after they appear, then slowly effaces them as it decants into the next errant form. The impressions never repeat in exactly the same corridor twice; the place itself seems to dampen any habit before it can harden.

Whatever leaves them does not travel by walking in any ordinary sense. It moves the way the architecture moves—by reconsidering. A cantilever unhooks, a thread tugs, a wall decides to lean three degrees farther, and in the brief interval while the stone is still negotiating its new load-path, the inhabitant has already been translated a body’s length sideways or up. You become aware of it most clearly in the particle shimmer of the old nano-servants. Once, they maintained. Now they mostly wander. But every so often a cloud of them will thicken, staling the air with a faint ozone sweetness, and for a few minutes they stitch not stone to stone but something softer to the stone: a translucent integument that takes the local cold without stiffening, a membrane that drinks condensation and returns it as a slower, darker fluid, the same fluid that thickens inside the rare glass reeds.

The creature itself (if creature is the right word; it may be more accurate to say the place’s one finished thought) is seldom present as a single outline. It exists more often as distributed tension. In one slit courtyard it may be only a warming of the air above a frost-rimmed ledge, the pale arthropod frozen mid-step as though listening. Two shafts away, a luminous thread suddenly thickens and dilates into a hollow, ribbed sleeve that opens and closes with the patience of a lung. Elsewhere a section of shared wall thins until it is only a vibrating equation, and through that equation you glimpse a longer interior: chambers the size of cupped palms lined with the same dull porcelain blue, each chamber holding a dark, slow pulse. These are not organs in a body so much as temporary concentrations of intent. When the wall thickens again, the pulse has already relocated.

Over weeks you begin to understand the economy of its presence. It does not hunt; there is almost nothing to hunt. It does not build; the city is already over-built and still building itself. It tends. It moves along the unfinished seams where raw basalt still pushes upward and hyper-engineered stone still tries to teach the basalt how to finish. Where the two languages grate hardest—where a load-thread fails and slurry spills—the inhabitant gathers. Its membranes drink the excess moisture so the freeze-thaw cycle will not splinter the new ribs too quickly. Its darker fluid is secreted into the glass reeds, which then act as slow batteries, storing a dim chemical twilight against the times when the particulate servants forget how to glow. Sometimes it presses itself into a forming ice blade near the center of the depression, and the ice grows around it in reverse—becoming less like a hanging dagger and more like a clear, temporary cocoon that will melt at the first partial thaw, releasing whatever small adjustment the inhabitant has made to the wall’s half-equation.

It is cold-blooded only in the most literal sense; its true temperature is the temperature of unfinished decisions. It has no face that remains a face long enough to be looked at, yet it has many surfaces capable of attention. One of them is a pale, slightly convex plane that can open a vertical pupil the color of the gaps between stars. Another is a cluster of fine, conductive cilia that taste the residual formation heat still leaking from the basalt and convert the gradient into the faint tick of settling stone you hear when everything else is silent. It does not flee observation. It simply becomes, for a while, more of the architecture, until the observer’s patience runs out or the courtyard rotates enough degrees to carry the encounter elsewhere.

There is never more than one. Or rather, the depression has room for only one continuous presence; any second would be forced, by the density of the place, to occupy the same set of folds and threads, and so would not be second. It has been here since the first luminous struts were driven into stone that had not yet learned it was stone. It will remain until the plain finally decides whether to heap or hollow, until the basalt ribs finish their arcs or the city finishes declining into sachets of glowing dust. In the meantime it keeps the seams polite. It lets the pestilent quiet deepen. It waits, stretched across half-stone and half-equation, while the hanging blade of ice lengthens by another transparent hair and the moisture that formed it forgets whether it was ever rain.
