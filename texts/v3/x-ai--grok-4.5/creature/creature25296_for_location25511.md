The plateau city does not host life so much as exhale it in delayed afterthoughts, the way cooling basalt sometimes cracks open to release a last pocket of ancient gas. At first there is only the emptiness you already know: the long avenues kept company by their own dust, the doorways that frame nothing, the windows that have forgotten glass and purpose alike. Whatever moves here does so beneath the threshold of ordinary notice, in registers of time and substance that treat flesh as a brief, wet rumor.

Begin with the obvious residuals. The many-legged thing the color of sifted stone is not alone, though its rare crossings make it seem so. There are others of its kind—flattened, patient arthropods whose bodies are less exoskeleton than compressed sediment, jointed by veins of the same salt-ghost mineral that threads the walls. They do not hunt. They migrate along microscopic gradients of remaining moisture and electrostatic charge, tasting the air with palps that double as geological sensors. When two meet they do not fight or mate in any recognizable way; they pause, exchange a slow abrasion of surfaces, and part having redistributed a few grains of themselves. Their trails last less time than a held thought. Higher up, in the membrane-flickers at window height, live the thinner ones: mostly empty films of protein and mineral lace that ride thermal differentials the way leaves once rode wind. They unfold only when the light shifts by some fraction too small for human eyes, drink the faint infrared bleeding from sun-warmed façades, then collapse again into apparent shadow. These are the error-lives, the ones that still overperform nearest to biology. They are not the city’s true tenants.

Deeper, slower, more continuous with the stone itself, are the dwellers that never bother with locomotion. They inhabit the negative spaces exactly as the description suggests—those vast cubic empties between walls—but they also inhabit the walls. Call them the angle-makers, the buttress-growers, the courtyard-sinkers. They are not creatures that act upon the mineral; they are the mineral’s long deliberation made locally intense. A section of façade that re-angles itself over hours is not being pushed by some hidden organism. It is the organism—distributed, crystalline, thinking in lattices of stress and release. Consciousness here is a property of slow shear and recrystallization. Where a tower extrudes a new support, that extrusion is the reaching of something that experiences the plateau’s gravity as a constant conversation rather than a force. They have no centers. A single “individual” might stretch across three city blocks and two centuries, its awareness flickering between the face of a cornice and the bedding plane of a sunken plaza. When the geometry betrays parallel lines or multiplies a tower into three simultaneous positions, that is not illusion layered atop reality. That is the dwellers adjusting their own topology, testing which configurations of here-ness still hold under the bone-colored sky.

They feed on gradients so faint they register as philosophy more than metabolism: the difference between the temperature of a shadowed colonnade and the fretted surface that still remembers noon; the electrical whisper when dust of one mineral composition slides across dust of another; the rare, theoretical film of iron-tasting moisture that blooms and dies in the deepest lees. Time is their medium the way water was once biology’s. They do not die. They re-angle. A collapse is only a change of posture. Erosion is respiration.

There are quieter tenants still. In the thickened silences of the empty forums live ambient pressures that have learned to cohere. They are not sound and not quite absence of sound; they are the resistance your ears met, given just enough persistence to notice itself. These gather where distances inflate, feeding on the surplus of untrusted continuity. Walk long enough in one of their territories and you begin to feel observed by the quiet, not maliciously, but with the detached attention of something cataloguing how much linear sequence a visitor still requires. They have no bodies. Their bodies are the intervals between your footsteps when those footsteps arrive early.

At the outer edges, where the city frays into spines and the drop becomes optional, the population grows franker and less stable. Here the thrice-positioned towers are occupied by what can only be called versions—echoes of the angle-makers that refused to finish choosing a single arrangement. They shimmer at soft focus, half-porous, letting colder winds move through the absences they have decided to remain. Looking at them produces the same vertigo as looking over the drop, because they are made of the same undecidedness. Some appear almost figurative for a moment: a tall attenuation that could be read as posture, a cluster of apertures that could be read as regard. The reading never lasts. They prefer the honesty of mineral process over the flattery of form.

No names exist for any of them, because nothing here has ever required the economy of calling. They do not speak, yet the city is full of their negotiations—the soft tick of settling, the almost-inaudible release of a flake, the cellular drift of dust that lifts and then revises its decision to fall. If you stand long enough in the market emptiness you can feel yourself being gently factored into the larger calculation: another warm perturbation, another brief complexity of water and salt and borrowed time. They will not hasten toward you. They have no need. The altitude does not care, the dryness does not care, and neither do they. They simply continue the improvisation that looks like a city, that feels like emptiness, that is in fact a single vast, patient, ever-revising inhabitant whose edges are the horizon and whose thoughts are the slow rearrangement of stone under a sky that will not darken.

You could walk for days and meet no one. That is accurate. It is also incomplete. You are already walking inside what lives here, through corridors of its attention, across plazas of its unhurried metabolism. The place does not contain its dwellers. The dwellers continue, and the place is what that continuation currently looks like.
