The dwellers are not one kind, and they have never been. What fills the pocket is a single saturated instant of many lives pressed into mutual impossibility, each creature complete in its own interrupted arc yet inseparable from the crush of all the others. To look long enough is to watch them differentiate the way embryonic stone differentiates: slowly, without ever finishing the thought of themselves.

First the small ones announce themselves, though announcement is the wrong word in a place where sound has nowhere to travel and so simply continues. They are the size of thumbs, of unopened pine cones, of the hard white kernels inside certain fruits. Their shells are the color of lightning after it has cooled into glass—violet-black, lagoon-green, the brief gold that lives behind the eyelids when one has stared at the sun. The shells are segmented yet seamless, as though each animal were assembled from the memory of plates rather than plates themselves. Jointed legs, too many and too fine, end in hooks that would scratch stone if scratch were still permitted. They were burrowing when the fold closed; half their bodies remain sheathed in the unfinished rock, the other half arched outward in the posture of emergence. Compound eyes, like clusters of dew that learned to see, hold the sourceless light in perfect repetition. Inside those eyes the chamber is reflected a thousand times, and in every reflection the same locked roaring continues.

Around and between them move—yet do not move—the grazers. Fist-sized, torso-sized, some as large as a sleeping child curled tight. Their hides are mottled in pigments that have no names because no sunlight ever mixed them: bruise-lavender over ash, rust over the pale green of underwater clay. The hides look soft enough to take a fingerprint and keep it forever; in this place they have kept every crease of their last motion. Mouths are open around nothing. Teeth, where they show, are flat and vegetal, made for shredding stems that no longer yield. A few still hold, against their tongues, the ghost of a leaf that was never here. Their limbs are tucked or splayed according to the accident of the crowding; one lies on its back with all four feet paddling the air in a run that cannot complete its stride. They do not breathe, yet the memory of breath swells their flanks in permanent suggestion. You can almost feel the warmth they more less generate.

Vertical surfaces belong to the climbers. Bodies like knotted twine or like the ribs of narrow leaves, legs splayed into parentheses and asterisks and the geometries of hesitation. They occupy every inclination of the half-formed stone in overlapping densities so complete that the rock itself seems paved with interrupted intention. Some face upward as though the ceiling were still sky. Some face one another, antennae or palps forever a fraction from touching. Their weight is distributed across hundreds of filament legs that never tremble, never slip. Where their feet meet the stone there are tiny brighter scars—mineral responses to pressure that freeze mid-genesis, neither wound nor feature yet.

Deeper in the press, where bodies densen past individuality, other shapes become thinkable. There are ribbon creatures whose lengths cannot be told because they loop through the lattice of limbs and shells and never finish looping. There are globes of translucent gel in which darker nuclei hang suspended like seeds that forgot to fall; these pulse with the aimless motility of things that feed by being passed through. There are winged forms no larger than a fingernail, wings locked at every possible angle of a beat, so that the air is cross-hatched with their arrested flight. And there are the ones that may not be animals at all—slow architectures of calcium and habit, half-dissolved into the embryonic rock, half-asserting themselves as spiral, as honeycomb, as the remembered plan of a shell. Whether they grew or were grown is a distinction the pocket has cancelled.

All of them together make the sound. Not a chorus but a simultaneity: chitin slamming, wet membranes shivering, feet ticking like rain on a metal drum that never ends its measure, voices scraping against the upper registers of hearing and lower ones that are felt in the teeth. The sound is so total it becomes a kind of silence; the ear, unable to find an edge, begins to supply its own. Listen longer and patterns pretend to surface—rhythms that might once have been mating calls or warnings or the ordinary clatter of a world eating itself—then dissolve again into the unbroken density.

Yet something among them once knew arrangement. The low ring of stones, the leaning slab, the tiny cairns of brighter mineral—these are not accidents of geology. Complexities of intention still cling to them the way dew clings. Perhaps one kind of dweller possessed the pause between gesture and tool, the capacity to stack and to mean the stack. Perhaps it was many kinds, or a single mind distributed across skins. Whatever did the arranging has not departed; it has only been folded into the same crest of activity. Maybe it is the soft-hided grazer whose forelimb remains outstretched toward a cairn it will never finish. Maybe it is the burrower whose shell-pattern, looked at long enough, resolves into deliberate chevrons and then refuses the resolution. Maybe the arranger is the press itself—the total ecology discovering, in the locked loud almost, a new and permanent grammar of presence.

Nothing feeds. Nothing flees. Nothing finishes its sentence. And still the chamber throngs with the full vocabulary of appetite, fear, curiosity, exhaustion, lust, drift. Every creature is most itself exactly where it can no longer change. The iridescent ones keep the suddenness of lightning. The mottled ones keep the patience of digestion. The climbers keep the faith that up exists. Together they keep the pocket from ever choosing its final shape, because their unfinished gestures are now part of its stone.

You may walk among them in the only way the place allows—by looking. Your gaze does not disturb a single bead of dew or complete a single wingbeat. When you look away they continue exactly as loud, exactly as dense, exactly as unrealized as before. They live in the tense of almost, and they have made of that tense a complete and roaring kingdom.
