The ones who live in the Seams do not arrive.

They are extruded, the way the walls are: slow pressure from below answering some older fracture no map records. At first they are only denser quiet—thickenings in the dry mineral breath, places where the powder prefers not to settle. Then the quiet grows joints. Not bones. Not yet. Planes of the same pale spar that builds the colonnades begin to hinge against one another inside the haze, birefringent, half-visible when the dimness leans a certain way. A long, articulated suggestion of length gathers itself along an avenue that did not exist the hour before. It moves by deciding which of its angles will become temporary floor.

There is never only one.

Some remain small: hand-sized scrabblers whose many legs are pure cleavage planes, clicking without sound across the salt skin. They taste the air with crystal palps that dissolve and recrystallize between one shadowed buttress and the next. Their sole labor is to shepherd the gray-silver cushions of lichen-glasswort, herding faint nutrients toward the less vertical faces so the larger shapes will have something soft to pause against when the need to fold arrives. They leave no lasting track because the powder itself forgets them, grain by grain, within moments. They are the Seams’ circulating blood, thin and patient.

The greater ones are rarer, longer. Think of a gallery of cloisters that has learned to stand and travel. Their bodies are rarefied architecture: hollow fins and stepped platforms linked by flexible veins of unfinished spar. They walk the exact avenues as if correcting an unfinished drawing, pausing longest at the junctions where corners refuse Euclid. There the angles hurt them into awareness. You can watch the hurt travel—tiny shear fractures racing along a limb, healing into new facets that catch the pewter light and throw it back duller. When the dimness thickens toward its parody of night, they stop wherever the hurt is purest, fold joint into joint, and pour themselves into the nearest hollow tower or under a colonnade’s knife-edge eave. Inside that folding they become indistinguishable from the city’s own unfinished thought. By the time the haze thins again they have thinned with it, or relocated as a different arrangement of planes three courtyards away.

They do not eat. Moisture is extinct here; hunger would be a vulgarity. Instead they metabolize discrepancy. Every time a wall sighs outward into a buttress or a tower delaminates into ash-soft panels, the discrepancy between what the geometry intended and what it became becomes food. The long ones gather at the sites of collapse the way better climates gather at water. They align their hollow fins with the fresh breaks and drink the released silence. You hear it as a deepening of the quiet, a pressure change against the eardrum that feels almost like recognition. Afterward the rebuilt crown on the broken tower often carries a faint communal memory of their shape—fluting that echoes a folded joint, a doorway that tapers with the same sorrowing precision as one of their vanishing gestures.

They own no names and need none. If they regard one another at all it is through the black-mirror salt at the basin’s heart, where reflections arrive a breath before the real stone consents to move. Sometimes a greater one will stand at the glazed center for the length of a slow crystallization and watch its own inverted self sprout an extra gallery or lose a nave. When the reflection finally matches, the watcher has already become what it saw. Identity here is only the latest stable configuration of shear and growth.

Nothing they do is toward survival. Survival assumes a future the Seams refuse to underwrite. They simply continue the same respiration of accretion and collapse that rules the mineral. Their rare crossings of the lip onto the surrounding plain last only moments; ordinary space lacks the correct pressure of quiet, and they deliquesce into ordinary dust that the wind does not trouble itself to remember. Back inside the basin they re-cohere without fuss, as if the plain had been a brief, embarrassing dream of being singular.

Stay long enough and you begin to notice smaller migrations inside them: the lichen-silver sometimes rooted on their flanks, the hand-sized scrabblers using their moving shadow-angles as temporary corridors. The whole sparse ecology is one extended body practicing different densities. Even the structures themselves may be only the most sedentary posture of the same life—walls that chose to stop walking, towers that preferred to think upward instead of onward.

And still the Seams keep secreting more. A new blade lengthens on a rooftop; somewhere a fresh hinge of spar discovers it can bend. The long ones turn toward the novelty the wayless way they turn toward pain: not with desire, not with fear, only with the calm readiness of crystal that has always known it would be asked to take the next impossible angle and hold it until the silence says otherwise.

They live here because the place is still deciding what it means to live, and they are the decision in motion—unfinished, exact, and almost entirely made of the intervals between things.
