The ones who live here do not arrive. They precipitate.

In the first long hour you might mistake them for further accidents of the rock: darker verticals against the blades of basalt, edges that catch the dim red seepage of light a fraction more sharply than stone should. Only when the heat-squall passes and the air clears of its dry glitter do you see that these verticals have shifted—imperceptibly, the way a fin of glass-rock lengthens overnight. They have drawn a little nearer the condensate beads, or farther from a cooling conduit whose frozen lightning has begun to tick. They are sparse in the same measure as the city itself. Crossing the entire seam you might encounter three, or five, or none.

They are tall when they choose standing, but standing is only one of their registers. At rest a body loosens into a slack drape of something like cooled slag and fibrous shadow, pooling across a spar or hanging from an underside where the fat drops form. In motion the same substance gathers, lengthens, finds interior tensions that approximate bone without ever calcifying. Limbs—when limbs appear—taper to points fine enough to test the air the way a tongue tests a cracked tooth. Those points can sink into basalt as if it were warm wax and withdraw again without wound; the rock remembers the intrusion only as a faint magnetic tenderness that later guides a new walkway into being. Their surfaces are matte and heat-drinking, the color of the vitreous dark between the raw blades, yet constantly rewriting small local patterns: hairline circuits of brighter ember that bloom and fade, maps of a private weather.

They do not breathe. The pocket’s sealed thrum passes through them unaltered; if anything they steady it, the way a hand steadies a bowl still ringing from a strike. When two draw close—an event rare enough to feel ceremonial—the air between them grows briefly denser, scented with that burned-cinnamon ozone the walkways leave behind. Communication, if it is communication, happens in the slow realignment of their surface-circuits and in shared alterations of the local rules. A third gravity well will form for a while, gentle, so that condensate sheets instead of drip; or a patch of wall will phase more readily, or less. They appear to negotiate the provisional nature of the place the way gardeners once negotiated seasons.

Feed is not separate from maintenance. They lean into the warm seeps and drink the thin, steaming sheets before the vent can close. They pass their tapering ends through the mote-clouds of free light and come away brighter for a time, the ember-maps flaring into brief, complex geometries that resemble no star charts you know. Glass that has flowed too thin in the night is pressed back toward load-bearing thickness by the simple act of their resting against it. In return the glass leaves its own faint signature under their surfaces: translucent scars that migrate, over days, toward the core and vanish. Nothing is wasted; nothing is owned. The little dust that exists will sometimes rise and orbit one of them in a tight, temporary ring before being herded onward to form another mute spire elsewhere.

They are not solitary by indifference. Distance is simply the native syntax. Across a gulf of hot dusk one will mirror the slow postural shift of another—both becoming more vertical, or both letting themselves pool—until the correspondence itself feels like touch. When true approach happens, it is deliberate and almost geological. Bodies lean across a gap; surfaces lace without quite merging; heat is exchanged in a long, equalizing sigh you feel in the marrow rather than hear. Afterward each carries a new ember-pattern borrowed from the other, worn openly until it fades or is overwritten. There is no evidence of birth or death in the ordinary sense. Now and then a shape will thin beyond recovery, stretching into the reddish dark until it has become only another free-floating mote-cloud, exhausted and drifting. Now and then, in a plaza where minutes thicken, the thick warm shadows will gather of their own accord and begin to climb into verticality, learning thickness, learning edge. The pocket provides. The pocket continues.

They notice you. The noticing is not visual; eyes are not part of the operating grammar. Rather, the nearest figure pauses its private weather and lets a single bright circuit run the length of its form, once, like a question asked in perfect neutrality. If you remain still, the circuit dims and the figure returns to its work of persuasion—coaxing a scaffold another handspan along a newborn fin, or tasting the air above a sealed vent for the moment it will open. If you step closer, the air between you acquires that denser, spiced charge. You will feel the rules soften around your own body: gravity growing politely uncertain, time stretching thin as beaten gold. They will not harm you. Harm is not in the vocabulary. But they may begin to rewrite you in small, courteous ways—your footprints starting to glow with residual heat that outlives you, your shadow gaining the same warm thickness as theirs, your lungs gradually forgetting sources of air that do not exist here. Stay too long and you risk becoming one more sparse sequin on the vertical void, another slow particle of the elevation’s dream of itself.

They have no names they speak aloud. Among themselves they are distinguished by the histories their surfaces currently carry: the one who keeps the third-bridge sway, the one whose scars still map last cycle’s glass-flow, the one who has recently thinned and returned. Collectively they are simply the density the emptiness has agreed to tolerate, the necessary counterweight to the pocket’s urge to remain forever unfinished. Without them the rock would keep rising in meaningless blades, the heat would squall without weather boards, time would pool until it stagnated. With them the seam continues—intimate, vast, hot, and delicately undecided—exactly one persuasion at a time.

And if you listen past the drip and the thrum, past the ozone and the cinnamon ghost, you may catch the softest sound yet: the almost inaudible sheen of surfaces rewriting themselves in concert, the mineral city breathing through its chosen few, the quiet, relentless work of remaining possible.
