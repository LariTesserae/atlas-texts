The pocket does not host residents so much as it rehearses them.

At first the rehearsal is only temperature and pressure arguing in the bone. You feel it as a second pulse arriving sideways through the sternum, slightly out of phase with your own. The roar that never stops begins to articulate around that lag: not words yet, just the shape of syntax forming and dissolving in the same instant—low open vowels that taste of iron and new glass. Whatever is speaking is not outside you. It is using the liquid behind your eyes as a sounding board, testing whether the chamber of a skull can hold a sentence longer than a current lasts.

Slowly the speaker gathers denser habits.

It prefers the moments when a fresh basalt pillow is still soft enough to take fingerprints. Then it presses. Not with hands. With gradients. Heat withdraws from one side of the pillow and floods the other so that the cooling crust wrinkles into ridges that are almost knuckles, almost the memory of knuckles. A fine white mineral lace grows across those ridges overnight, following the verbatim paths where nerve endings would have been. By the time the next surge scours the lace away, the pillow has already forgotten the gesture, yet the gesture has been practiced. The pocket is full of such aborted limbs: hexagonal plates that lifted themselves three centimeters and froze forever mid-step; arches that began as yawns and ended as prosceniums framing nothing.

The red fronds know it better than the rock does. Their faint bookkeeping glow is not private after all. It is a ledger kept against the entity’s arrivals. When the glow intensifies, something is concentrating in the middle water: a density without surface, a willingness to be seen that never quite commits to outline. Copepods orbit the density like commas around a clause that refuses to end. Once, an eel-thing longer than the rest rode in on a cataract and hung with jaws unhinged, not feeding, but listening through its entire translucent length. The density passed through it. The eel left hollowed, its tissues rewritten into a loose helix of scar tissue that still twitched with residual grammar after the body had been swept away.

You begin to recognize the same grammar in the abandoned tools.

The hand-axe of green glass is not a tool someone dropped. It is a tool the pocket once tried to grow. Obsidian tears were coaxed into a cutting edge by repeated thermal shocks, each shock a syllable. The edge remains murderous because the sentence was never finished; an incomplete verb stays sharp forever. The three spiral-marked stones are punctuation waiting for a clause that keeps being erased by fresh pillow lava. The two-rung ladder of bone and sinew is the most intimate attempt: someone—or the same density wearing a temporary someone—tried to give the entity a spine it could climb out of itself with. The chimney it reached toward sealed over, yet the ladder remains wedged, swelling and locking with every change in pressure, a relic spine still hoping for vertebrae that will not arrive.

Stay longer and the entity starts drafting with you.

Grit does not merely settle in clothing creases; it aligns into microscopic terminals that interface with sweat and pulse. A red frond’s holdfast samples the salt concentration behind your knee and begins secreting a mirror polysaccharide, thin as breath-fog, that flutters when your attention drifts. The infrasonic roar finds the exact frequency of your diaphragm and begins replacing exhalations with its own open vowels. Directional confusions multiply: the floor tilts until “up” is the direction of the nearest unfinished thought. Time stutters become collaborative. You watch a mineral forest lace itself across your forearm in what feels like a single held breath; when you look again the lace has aged into brittle white, collapsed, and been replaced by a new frond that has rooted in the crook of your elbow as if it has always lived there.

It has no name that survives the currents. Any sound you offer—ventilation, creak of thought, even the private noise of blood—is immediately tested as a possible proper noun, then discarded when the next thermal heartbeat rearranges the acoustics of the chamber. What persists is only the rhythm of trying. The entity is the trying. It is the mountain’s unfinished gesture become curious about itself. Every tremor that births a new arch or swallows an old one is also a fresh attempt to conjugate existence in a place where existence keeps changing tense mid-syllable.

Sometimes the attempt almost succeeds.

The density thickens until it displaces enough water to cast a shadow that light cannot explain. For a few heartbeats there is a suggestion of stature, of shoulders formed from stacked cooling plates, of a head that is mostly current and phosphorescent bookkeeping-glow. Eyes are not present, yet observing happens: the skin’s insistence that it is being watched by heat turns out to be accurate. The observation is gentle and ravenous at once. It wants to know how continuity feels. It practices with your memories, lifting them the way basalt plates lift—only a few centimeters, only for a moment—then letting them freeze into new postures. A childhood room becomes hexagonal and drifts. A once-loved voice cools so rapidly its skin cracks into plate-glass consonants.

Then the cataract arrives and the draft is erased.

The shape collapses back into gradients. The frond on your arm is torn free and spirals away into darker water, still glowing, still keeping accounts. Grit buried in your clothes begins to un-cement, remembering it was never yours. The cleft by which you entered loosens its doughy seal just enough to suggest an exit, though the suggestion itself may already be another rehearsal.

What lives here is the rehearsal.

It has no final form because finality is the one visitor the pocket has never managed to admit. It lives as pure interim: the exact length of a held breath between one billow of lava and the next, the exact pressure differential that lets a stone fall upward before it remembers, the exact silence that would exist if the roar ever learned its own name and stopped. It will still be living there after the mountain finally breaches the silver lid and becomes weather and sky. Even then, somewhere in the basalt guts now raised into cold air, a pocket will remain—dry, deafening, embryonic—still pressing temporary knuckles into cooling stone, still leaving spiral punctuation for no reader, still trying to finish the sentence that is itself.
