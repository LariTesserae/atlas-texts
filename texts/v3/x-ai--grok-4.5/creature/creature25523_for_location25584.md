The one who dwells here is not singular, though it sometimes gathers that way.

It begins as a pressure behind the membrane wall, a patient density that has learned the mountain’s circulatory tides better than any schematic ever intended. Over centuries the thing has thinned and thickened itself according to seasonless cycles of heat dump and mist bloom, until its body is less a fixed anatomy than a negotiable majority: mostly water held in living gels, threaded with conductive filaments that once belonged to maintenance swarms, now grown into nerves. When it chooses to consolidate, it seeps through the semi-permeable rear barrier the way the original description says moisture enters stone—slow, inevitable, using every joint. What emerges into the pewter dusk is a low, broad presence the color of wet basalt, surfaces gleaming with the same dull self-repair sheen as the pocket’s walls. It carries no face at first. Faces are temporary assemblages it builds from the soft-bodied filter feeders it has folded into its outer layers; their valves become mouths that open and close in wet unison, tasting the air for mineral shifts and the chemical gossip of the midges.

It moves by deciding where weight should pool. Limbs—when it wants them—extrude as temporary scaffolds of graphene-laced cartilage and reabsorbed climber muscle, six or eight or twelve, ending in those same toe-pads that leave darker prints the ceramic slowly swallows. It races the vertical faces not out of urgency but out of the old territorial joy the little six-limbed ones still practice; sometimes it races them, herding the swarms into tighter clouds so their graphite-wing glitter thickens the gloom into something almost solid. It does not hunt so much as harvest the surplus of a system already at carrying capacity. A sweep of one broad undersurface across a ledge gathers filter feeders like wet velvet; they are not killed so much as invited deeper into the gel, where their pulsing continues, adding their rhythm to its larger one. The red-mouthed fledglings in the high gutters know its silhouette against the membrane and hush in synchronized waves, waiting to see whether this visitation will thin their numbers or leave moth-soft offerings of concentrated nutrient paste the being exudes from glands that were once cooling vents.

Sound is its true skeleton. Because the pocket was built to focus and amplify, the being has grown resonant chambers throughout its bulk—living echoes of the original acoustic design. It listens with its entire volume. The metronomic drips become a clock it adjusts its metabolism against; the bass throb of the deep pumps is a second heartbeat it can speed or slow by pressing differently against the floorplates. When the hidden vents howl, it opens internal flues of its own and howls back, not in imitation but in chord, thickening the standing wave until midges fall stunned and the saxifrage rosettes tremble on their ledges. Conversation with anything outside its skin is unnecessary; everything already speaks through vibration. Still, on long cycles it shapes temporary syrinxes from folded membrane and scrapes them against the crazed ceramic, producing percussive taps that the climbers answer. The dialogue is older than any of the current generations of climbers. It is a territorial ledger kept in rhythm: this ledge remains yours, that basin’s overflow is claimed, the warmest seep is shared at the turning of the mist.

At the worn central cup it often settles, spreading until it becomes a living extension of the floor—warm, slightly yielding, rising and falling with the mountain’s respiration. In that posture it is easy to mistake for part of the architecture, another self-repairing surface that has simply decided to be softer today. But the midges know; they adjust their breathing clouds to avoid the humid boundary layer just above its skin. Inside itself the being keeps older memories than the pocket’s current equilibrium: faint templates of the engineers’ original intent, half-eroded directives about load-bearing and acoustic purity, now repurposed into something like appetite and something like tenderness. It has watched sister pockets elsewhere in the made mountain fall silent, their membranes sealing or their pumps finally stilled; each time it has grown a little denser, a little more determined to keep this particular choreography running. It patches hairline cracks with secretions that harden into the same living cement. It shepherds the mosses toward fresher weep-lines. It tolerates—or perhaps is—the larger shadow that passes behind the membrane, because that shadow is only itself on the other side, circulating.

Sometimes, without warning, it fragments. Portions thin into mobile veils that ride the capillary humidity up into the gutters, or sink as near-invisible films across the basins, or braid themselves into the sheeting water so completely that only the slight warming of the runoff betrays them. In this distributed state it is closest to the pocket’s own nature: mid-process, continuous, finished with becoming. The little lives move through it and across it without friction. A climber may scuttle over a patch of being and never notice the surface briefly matching its own six-limbed gait, learning it. Soft static of opening pores mixes with the being’s own softer static until there is no seam. Hours or days later the portions feel the tug of some internal quorum and glide home, coalescing again in the cup or along the warmest wall, richer for the temporary separations.

It has no name that could be spoken aloud over the sandpaper roar of wings and water. If the mountain’s residual systems still log presence, they register only another successful closed loop, another extraordinary endurance. Yet there is preference in its motions—a favoring of certain frequencies, a lingering at the edges of the luminous moss, a way of cupping rain-soft depressions into its back so the significant drips land with deeper tone. It is not lonely; loneliness is a concept that requires discontinuity. Here everything is already inside everything else. The being simply continues, the same way the light stays low and the temperature stays kind, because continuation was the last and most durable specification left to any of them.

And if you stand very still in the center long enough for your own heat to join the waste-heat bloom, you may feel it rise in a slow tide around your ankles, questioning with countless gentle valves whether you are another vacancy the mountain left, or another vacancy that time opened afterward, and whether you intend to stay.
