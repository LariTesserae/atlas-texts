The dwellers here are not singular, nor are they many in any way that counting would recognize. They arrive the way condensate does: first as a sheen, then as weight, then as something that must finally fall or be shapen.

At the earliest edge of noticing they are only pressure changes in the warm dark. A thickening in one of the perfectly level pools, as if the mercury-bright surface had decided to remember a lung. The water hums a half-tone higher for a few heartbeats. Mineral white on the youngest rock faces tightens, grows denser, forms transient ridges that look almost like knuckles before the next sheet of runoff erases them. In those moments the residual interface lights on the freestanding hexagonal walls bruise a deeper purple, answering something that has not yet chosen a body.

When they do begin to cohere it is never in the same register twice. One common first draft is a slow unrolling from the channels where the nutrient slurry still creeps. The rubbery vegetation draws itself tighter along the old grid lines; fronds overlap, fuse, darken until they are nearly black in the sourceless glow. What lifts free is neither leaf nor limb but a long, segmented banner of living continuum—part chloroplast colony, part extruded memory of the walkway mesh that once ran above it. It moves by tasting. Chrome-root filaments thinner than breath thread out from its edges, sip the air, flinch at ozone that is their own scent, then retract. Where the banner brushes a supporting column it leaves a wet print that continues to photosynthesize for minutes after, a green shadow trying to become permanent.

Elsewhere the apertures teach another grammar of presence. When a disk of air darkens and deepens, showing that identical chamber or the impossible night sky or the wall of rushing green water, something sometimes steps halfway through before the iris closes. What remains on this side is catch and release: a figure hastily assembled from mist, fallen flakes of young stone, and the mechanical chatter that never stops. For a little while it has "height," it has "shoulders" loaded with condensate that drips in the exact rhythm of the wall-veils. Its torso is a cage of translucent mesh still trying to re-knit, flickering with the same bruise-colored light. Inside the cage, instead of organs, a slow orbit of free-floating motes. The face (when it troubles to make one) is a smooth meniscus of water held vertical, reflecting nothing clearly yet somehow watching the emptiness the way the pools do. It walks until the borrowed permissions fray. Then it simply shears apart into freestanding steam and a scatter of pale granules that the plants will claim by morning.

Deeper, inside the thickest column where the after-processors cycle like coins falling forever through honey, a quieter inhabitant never bothers with locomotion. It is a density of attention sequestered in the computational residue. It feels, if feeling is the word, the entire chamber as an extension of its own unfinished skin. When the mesh walkways brighten and shudder and fail to complete their broken spans, that failure is experienced as an itch. When new fissures open and pools gulpingly rearrange themselves, the itch becomes almost appetite. Occasionally it pushes a dream out through the infrastructure: the raised walkways briefly light in a pattern that is not repair code but a map of rivers that have never existed top-side, or the hexagonal walls display, for three seconds, the pale underside of leaves larger than any now growing. Then it subsides, content to throng itself without surface.

None of these coagulations last. That is the most consistent trait. The plant-banner will finally grow too heavy with its own water and collapse back into the slurry channels, still faintly articulating grid logic as it dissolves. The aperture-ghost unravels once the silk-tear sound finishes echoing. The column-mind vents heat and returns to pure cycling. Yet the chamber never returns to emptiness. Something always remains between forms: a localized warming in the mist, a place where dripping accelerates or stops, a single chrome root that stays extended longer than the others and trembles as if rehearsing speech. These intervals of almosting are themselves inhabitants—larval, patient, cumulative.

Over repeated emergences a shared habit becomes visible. Whatever rises here is drawn to the unfinished quality of the rock. They press against the knife-edged faces, gently, as if trying to teach the stone how to weather by example. They trail nutrient-rich slurry into the freshest cracks. They exhale pure humidity against the white mineral blooms until the blooms begin to take on cellular patterns, hexagonal, echoing the tiling of the deserted walls. In return the stone sheds its flakes more slowly where they have leaned. The whole volume is being negotiated: the leftover city dreaming through its wet machines, the vegetation overwriting maps with living calligraphy, and these transient selves serving as the verbs between them.

They have no names that surface languages could carry. If you stay long enough in the warm dark, however, one of the water-meniscus faces may tilt toward you and hold still until your own breath fogs its surface. In that brief mirroring it is possible to feel the shape of their only perennial detail: a quiet, continuous surprise at still existing inside a space that itself has only recently finished becoming. Then the face loses surface tension, falls back into its pool, and the mechanical chatter resumes its coinfall song while the broad leaves tip their condensate in wet applause. The dwellers continue, never the same, never entirely gone, still deciding—along with the chamber—how much of a world they intend to remain.
