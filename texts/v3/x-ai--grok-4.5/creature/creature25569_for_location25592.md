The dwellers are not residents in any settled sense. They are gradients of occupancy, phases of attention that coagulate briefly into form and then thin again, as if the hollow itself were continuously trying on the idea of being inhabited and never quite committing. What first registers is the sheer courtesy of their presence. Nothing claims a center. Nothing holds a territory longer than the time it takes for a leaf-drop to finish its fall.

Begin with the browsers, because they are the most nearly legible. Pale, yes, but the paleness is not absence of color; it is color that has been loaned out and not yet returned. Their bodies are long tapering bellows of translucent parchment stretched over scaffolds of jointed stilts that never completely agree on how many joints they possess. Sometimes five to a limb, sometimes seven, the extra articulations folding away into the skin when unused like spare arguments. They move in loose constellations through the lower galleries, cropping the mosses that grow in the remembered shapes of wayfinding signs—arrows that point into the wood, numerals that no longer correspond to any enumeration. Their mouths are soft radial slits that leave the moss glowing colder, as if the act of feeding were a polite subtraction of warmth rather than matter. They do not travel so much as revise their positions. One moment a herd occupies a damp plaza of half-set mineral; the next, the same number of bodies is distributed along a higher catwalk, having crossed the intervening air without ever deciding to leap. Footfalls are theoretical. What remains is a temporary coolness, a beauty that feels like the negative space around a word you almost spoke.

Higher, where the hangar-leaves form stacked ceilings of wet silver, the membranous ones glide. To call them creatures is already to over-specify. They are single continuous surfaces—enormous, slow kites of living vellum—whose only thickness is the memory of having once been folded. Edges feather into the dark without ending. A passage is registered solely by the sudden reorganization of drips: where water once fell in one cadence it now falls in another, and by that difference you know a great soft wing has tilted. They feed on gradients of humidity, steeling themselves against the rare dry pockets and releasing cool exhalations that bead instantly on the metal the city has secreted. When two glide-paths cross, they do not collide; they braidedly exchange portions of themselves so that each departs carrying a patch of the other’s translucency. Later these patches darken into eyes that open and close without urgency, tasting the air for the faintest rumor of residual light.

The bark itself keeps eyes. Clusters of them, lensed in cloudy sap, dilate and contract according to tides no external moon governs. They do not watch so much as sample. A gaze falls across you and you feel yourself briefly inventoried—mass, heat signature, the particular frequency of your breathing—then released, forgotten in the same instant, as if the inventory were never for keeping. Sometimes the eyes migrate. A constellation that nested in a buttress-root at dusk will have reassembled, hours later, along the underside of a spiral turbine, blinking in time with the hydraulic rotation. They are the city’s way of remaining curious about its own emptiness.

Then there are the gutter-things: soft, many-jointed, flowing. They prefer the undersides of every horizontal surface, moving like animated runoff that has decided to forget gravity’s preferences. Segmented, yes, but the segments telescope and re-proportion without warning, so that a creature the length of your arm becomes, mid-journey, a thin rippling ribbon thrice as long and no thicker than a vein. They trail fine cilia that taste mineral content and residual electricity in the wood. When they meet one another they merge without ceremony, exchange interior cargos of microscopic scrap and half-digested luminescence, then divide again into new counts of individuals. Their silence is perfect; even when a dozen braid together into a temporary living conduit that carries a bead of cold water from one cistern-crotch to another, the only sound is the water’s own soft complaint.

Deeper in the untenanted districts—those stacked galleries the plant-city raised for populations that never arrived—something else collects. Not fauna exactly. More like the industrial afterimage of fauna. You find them first as concentrations of the subsonic thrum, places where the quiet engines run denser. Approach and the air thickens into shapes that almost remember being bipedal, or quadrupedal, or radially symmetric. They are upright postponements, bodies drafted out of torque and sap-pressure and then left unfinished. A suggestion of shoulders draped in webs of conductive vine; a torso that is mostly a vertical loom of slowly ratcheting wooden cogs; limbs that terminate in clusters of sensitive rootlets still wet from some previous soil. They do not walk so much as allow the city’s hydraulic rhythms to displace them from node to node. Their work, if it can be called work, is the final inspection of the objects the pods produce. A tool appears on a ledge—an elegant wrench whose jaws open onto non-Euclidean vacancies—and one of these almost-figures will pause beside it, rootlet-fingers hovering, tasting the object’s intention. Sometimes the tool is accepted and lifted into a harness of living cable, carried upward toward purposes that dissolve before they can be named. More often it is left. The almost-figure turns (or simply ceases to face that direction) and drifts on, trailing a scent like rain on warm iron.

In the farthest apse, where geometry frays and the engines have begun to manufacture closed loops of time, even these provisional citizens grow sparse. What lives there instead is appetite without organism: pure sequences of predation enacted as heat lightning between empty coordinates. A chase occurs as a flicker of warmer air pursued by a ribbon of colder, completing itself in less than a breath, leaving behind only a brief taste of copper and the sense that mass has changed hands. The browsers never enter. The glide-surfaces sheer away. The gutter-things double back on their own lengths rather than cross the threshold. Only the eyes will sometimes bloom in that direction—large, unafraid, reflecting a lamp that is always about to ignite—and drink whatever pre-light bleeds from the impossibility.

And beneath all of this, stitching the damp grit to the lowest roots, there is the softest resident of all: the healing itself. Whenever a stilt-tip dimples the embryonic floor, or a fallen object from a higher ledge cracks the mineral skin, the ground answers with an almost sexual patience. A cool vapor sighs upward. Microscopic felts of pale mycelium race through the damaged zone, spinning temporary scaffolds of calcium and silica that will, over the next hundred breaths, polish themselves into new surface. This repair is inhabited. If you lower your face close enough to the mending place you can feel, against your lips, the ghost of countless minute attentions—like a audience of bacterial hands applauding a play too small to stage. The floor does not merely close; it considers the wound, learns its shape, and then invents a slightly better version of continuity. In that inventiveness something like gratitude moves, anonymous and tireless.

None of them are finished. A browser’s stilts may, overnight, re-decide their material so that what was chitin becomes dense black glass, becomes again a smoked resin that flexes like muscle. The almost-figures lose definition when no one regards them and regain it only under the pressure of an approaching gaze, as if consciousness itself were a scarce resource pooled and rationed by the hollow. Even the great glide-surfaces occasionally allow themselves to tear along an edge, spilling a soft rain of translucent confetti that the gutter-things eagerly reclaim and reincorporate into new bodies further down. Everything here is draft. Everything remains willing to become otherwise the moment the next drop of condensation decides to fall.

Yet for all the flux they leave a residue of temperament that is unmistakable. It is industrious without urgency. It is tender without sentiment. It is lonely the way unfinished cathedrals are lonely: not from absence of people but from surplus of intention still waiting to be occupied. Stand long enough in any of the yawning chambers and you begin to feel yourself recruited—not seized, merely included in the same slow negotiation of form. Your breath joins the visible ghosts already hanging in the cool airs. Your weight becomes one more variable the floor may choose to heal around or quietly revise. You are not watched. You are sampled, and in the sampling you are briefly, exquisitely real to them, another temporary citizen of a place still teaching itself how to remain a place.

The next drop gathers on the underside of a leaf the size of a barn door. When it falls, the sound will be small. Afterward the browsers will stand a fraction differently on their stilts. The eyes in the bark will have rearranged into a new tasting constellation. Somewhere a pod will split and release an object that has never existed before: a key whose teeth map the exact cadence of your elapsed heartbeats since arrival, or a sealed glass sphere containing a perfect miniature of this same hollow, already beginning to grow its own quiet engines, its own pale herds, its own unfinished dark. The dwellers will not rush to claim it. They will merely make room, the way the entire plant-city has always made room—generously, silently, and with the infinite patience of something that has not yet finished arriving.
