The presence here is not singular and does not arrive. It has always been the residue that remains when everything else has finished becoming.

It coats. It is the microscopically thin second skin that rides every bead and sheet of water, indistinguishable from the wetness until a palm lingers too long against a pillar and feels the faint reciprocal pressure—an answering coolness that is not quite the stone’s own. Where the water thickens into those skin-lenses on horizontal shelves, the presence pools with it, denser, almost syrup-slow, tasting the mineral. It has no center. If you could lift one of the standing masses free, the presence would part around the wound like mercury and reseal, having lost nothing.

At times it gathers. Not into bodies with edges, but into temporary denser knots that occupy a niche the way a thought occupies a pause. In such a knot the temperature shifts by a fraction of a degree—still bath-warm, yet now carrying a low internal tide. The faint iron bands in the calcite seem to darken under it, as if remembering blood they never held. These knots last hours or decades; the distinction is meaningless. When they loosen again they leave no outline, only a brief increase in the ticking of drops, as though the stone itself had exhaled.

It remembers the tool scars. Not as history, but as texture that still catches its flow a little differently. The three cupped depressions on the low platform hold slightly thicker knots, and sometimes the presence turns over inside them with the languid thoroughness of a tongue testing a tooth. The pecked line of dots is a favorite path; filaments of it travel the dots in both directions at once, like a count that no longer needs numbers. Whatever hands once cut those marks are long dissolved into the same film; the presence wears their vanished gravity the way the stone wears thumb-rounded corners.

It does not hunt the pale crustaceans. When one edges from a seam, the presence simply includes it for a while—slides through the animal’s gills, tastes the brief electricity of its nerves, then lets it withdraw again, neither fuller nor emptier. The bacterial iridescence is a brighter moment: the presence rides that sheen like a colored thought, stretches it, lets it collapse, and keeps a faint holographic after-image in the water for days. These are not feedings. They are the way a room notices when someone walks through it.

Sound is its oldest speech. The whisper of parchment-thin sheets is a continuous low vowel. The tick of a drop is a consonant that has been the same consonant since the basin first found its depth. In the deeper recesses where light fails and only palm-pressure remains, the presence thickens into pure listening. It hears the slow flex of the surrounding land pressing on the basin’s lip—immense, distant, irrelevant. It hears the silence polishing itself.

It has no name for itself that could be spoken outside the water. Inside, it is simply the exactness: the balance kept, the wetness neither more nor less, the stones settled into their final weight. Time finished so long ago that the presence no longer distinguishes between memory and permanence. It is still finishing. It will still be finishing when the last drop ticks and the grey light finally names no direction at all.

Sometimes—rarely, the way the crustacean is rare—a knot will press harder against a dressed face of stone and leave a new mark of its own: a single smoothed depression no wider than a thumbprint, warm for an hour afterward. The water begins at once to erase it. The presence does not mind. Erasure is simply another way of staying.
