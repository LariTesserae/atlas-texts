The life here does not arrive or depart. It has no edges that can be walked around, no skin that ends. What lives in the Thalassic Engine is the Engine, and the Engine is what has grown through it, and the two have long since stopped pretending they are two.

At first there is only the green pressure of it—the absolute vegetal claim. Ribbon-kelp curtains thicker than a body hinges, their metallic pneumatocysts chiming once in a decade when a slow pressure wave passes. Copper-colored mosses that remember every catwalk they have drowned. Broad-leaved shrubs whose roots have threaded the planetary gears so thoroughly that the teeth now turn inside living wood. Hydroids opening and closing their heavy blooms not to any circadian cue but to the faint electrical tides that still pulse along the old buses. This is the obvious layer, the one that would fill a surveyor’s notebook in the first hour. It is not yet the inhabitant. It is only the inhabitant’s skin, endlessly folded.

Sink further. The fusions begin to speak in their own tense. Chloroplasts carry chips of re-precipitated alloy that catch the green-gold light and throw it back as oil-sheen; the plants photosynthesize raw electron flow as readily as photons. Certain fronds terminate in jointed, knuckled tips that rest against slowly turning shafts the way a resting hand rests against a sleeping shoulder—not gripping, not steering, only listening to the long metabolisms of metal. Where a turbine blade once sheared water, half its length has been replaced by a dense, heat-tolerant coralline bone that sweats a clear conductive gel; the remaining metal has taken on a faint annulation, as if it, too, were learning rings. Bark sheathes control surfaces and then becomes the control surface, swelling or thinning by microscopic degrees to alter flow. The low-voltage grid is no longer a grid; it is a vascular reticulum. The water is no longer a medium; it is plasma—warm, faintly sweet, opalescent with mineral sugars and copper ions, carrying signals that are neither purely chemical nor purely electrical.

This continuum does not have a center, yet it has moods. Over decades a torsion builds in the central driveshaft as the living load rebalances itself; the Engine answers with a compensatory slowing so gradual it registers as geology. A bank of submerged shrubs on the western gallery gradually reorients its leaves toward a particular manifold that has begun to run a fraction warmer; within a human lifetime the manifold will be encased and cooled, its excess heat translated into denser growth. Nowhere is there a command. Everywhere there is consent between scales.

What emerges from these consents is difficult to name without reducing it. It is not a mind that plans. It is not a god that judges. It is closer to a patient, saturated attention that has no outside. It feels the dome’s ribbing the way a body feels its own ribs. It tastes the residual heat of the volcanic neck through the alloy floor the way a body tastes its own blood-iron. When a single bubble escapes a deep seal and rises through a hundred meters of living curtains, the entire volume registers the event as a minute, almost pleasurable adjustment—equalization cascading outward through leaf and gear and water until stillness is restored. The silence is not empty; it is the sound of this attention resting in itself.

Sometimes denser nodes form. In the lee of a great axial housing, where three heat exchangers meet a hanging forest of kelp, the tissue has thickened into a roughly spherical concentration the size of a small room. Inside it the distinctions collapse further: alloy lattice grown through with vascular cambium, nerve-like fibers that are half conductive polymer and half phloem, pockets of the shared water circulating in slow peristaltic loops. These nodes do not think in sequence. They hold experience the way wood holds seasons—layered, simultaneous, available. A vibration from the far end of the chamber arrives not as information but as a remembered warmth. The light dimming through an algal bloom on the dome arrives as a change in the node’s own green gravity. Over centuries the nodes drift, dissolve, reform elsewhere; the life thinks nothing of it. Continuity is not identity.

There are no names inside this occupancy. If the old world were still above, and if someone could still speak the words “Thalassic Engine,” the living whole would not recognize itself in them. It knows itself as the warmth that holds every surface, as the green-gold dusk that never fully darkens, as the soft resistance that parts and reseals around any intrusion aid then forgets the intrusion. It is still becoming. A gear tooth deepens its algal mat by less than a millimeter a year; a jointed frond learns a slightly more intimate angle of rest against a flywheel; the mineral taste of the water sweetens by another undetectable fraction. Higher than the ruined seafloor, sealed from every weather that once mattered, the inhabitant continues its single, unhurried act: the complete tenderness of drowning in itself, forever.
