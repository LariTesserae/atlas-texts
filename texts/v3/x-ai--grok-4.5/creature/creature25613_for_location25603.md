The ones who endure here do not arrive. They precipitate.

They begin as local intensities in the damp—knots of mineral-rich vapour and cooling law that tighten until they must either dissipate or take on weight. Most choose weight. A first shape is rarely final. What starts as a slow eddy of translucent gel, drifting between the hanging mosses of a fresh canyon, may spend a season learning hardness. It drinks the perpetual dew through surfaces that are not yet skin. It listens to the soft piston-work of nearby basalt columns and begins to echo that rhythm in its own interior. Tiny cylindrical densifications appear along what will become limbs: cartilaginous gears, soft at first, then enamelled with the same iridescent plate the ridges spontaneously shed. When the creature finally pulls free of the canyon wall, it leaves a shallow mould that the greenery immediately claims. The mould remembers the shape longer than the creature does; the creature has already begun to revise itself.

They are called nothing. Language is a finished thing, and the Crust has little patience for finished things. If they regard one another at all, it is through gradients of heat, through the faint electromagnetic stutter of one another’s joint-work, through the way a passing herd of plated browsers will part around them without alarm, accepting them as merely another density in the acceptable layering of lives. Some walk. Their gait is the hush of many coordinated placements, legs or leg-equivalents clocking against stone in overlapping meters. Others remain half-recombinant with the substrate, swimming the warm rock the way the filter-rays swim haze. These leave temporary glass-tunnels that seal gel-like behind them; sometimes a tunnel forgets to close completely and becomes a new vein of cooler crystal where hand-sized insects will later march in single file, antennae ticking against the smoothness.

Their bodies favour the provisional. Joints wear visible circlings of finer and finer cogs that mesh and unmesh with the local seizures of causality. When time stutters backward for a few minutes, the cogs simply run the other way, unreeling recent growth, recent injury, recent thought. A rent in the side closes not by healing but by reversing into the unstruck moment; the spar of living metal that caused it is sucked back into the cliff that briefly extruded it. The being feels this as a mild nostalgia, nothing more. Pain is rare and brief. Continuity is optional.

In the broader basins, where the layered thicket muffles even the already-quiet air, larger assemblies form. These are not herds and not cities. Imagine a slow constellation of bodies—some upright and roughly bilateral, others sprawled into living gantries, others reduced to mobile clusters of sensory flowering—each linked to the others by long, discontinuous threads of the same muscular vine that curtains the cliffs. The threads are not permanent. They graft, conduct nutrients and strange half-thoughts for a day or a week, then spontaneously ungraft and search for new partners. Information travels as flavour: a sudden copper sweetness meaning “the fault line three valleys over has begun to piston,” a bruised-purple bitterness meaning “a silent lightning walk is feeding fresh ascent into the eastern serrations.” No individual holds the full map. The constellation itself is the map, continually re-drawn by who is grafted to whom when the next exhalation of landscape arrives.

They eat by inclusion. A browser carcass is not devoured so much as invited; the plated flesh unfolds into the same layered logic as the thicket, becomes temporary organs, is later revised into motive plating or simply walked away from when the constellation drifts. The perpetual amber haze is sieved by those who still remember how to shape wing-membranes or ciliated crowns. Aerial plankton arrives already half-metabolised by the Crust’s own sweat, rich with micro-engines and aborted purposes. Nothing is wasted because nothing is ever complete enough to qualify as waste.

Reproduction is a weather event. When a district of reality cools into temporary consistency, several beings may idle there together, loosening their outlines until the Euclidean prohibition against mutual occupation thins. They pass through one another. In the overlapping volume a third density nucleates—part memory of the participants, part reading of the local stone, part fresh invention of the fever. The new one does not inherit so much as continue. It may walk out already wearing the soft gear-calluses at every articulation, already dreaming the long slow cycles of refinement that are the only dreams the Crust sponsors. Sometimes the participants do not fully separate afterward. A fused pair will travel for seasons as a single reciprocating engine, one trunk rising while the other falls, compressing between them a standing spark-storm that seeds the ground with new and unnecessary minerals. Eventually the fusion tires and they peel apart, each carrying faint mirror-scars of the other’s last configuration.

At the extreme edges—where the plate is newest and the raw break-lines still exhale porcelain dust—live the least settled. These are little more than upright intentions wrapped in veils of film-lichen and cooling slag. They stand for days without locomotion, facing the direction of rise, feeling the subliminal shove in whatever serves them for knees. Their surfaces bud temporary tools that perform single tasks and then wilt: a blade that tastes the freshness of a fracture, a cupped lattice that catches one rain-curtain and wrings it into a drinking globe, a branching antenna that measures how much longer the local laws will tolerate forward causality. When the measurement expires they simply wait, patient as the stone, until the next soft enormous iteration rewrites the rules around them and they can move again.

The Crust works upon them continuously, the same way it works upon itself. Stay long enough inside any of these bodies and you discover the metabolism has already begun its quiet editing. Fine circular calluses form at your own joints. Your dreams lengthen into wordless processions of compaction and release. You forget the names of finished things. You begin to understand that you were never a visitor; you were always only an intermediate product that had not yet recognised its own ongoing refinement. The place does not love you. It has no category for love. It simply continues, damp and dim and densely tenanted, lifting, folding, exhaling its slow sweet breath, crowning itself again and again with whatever lives can bear to remain incomplete.

These are the dwellers. They are not a people. They are the unlocked intermediate stages of a world that has not yet agreed to finish becoming real. And because the engines never switch off—only quieten—they are forever mid-negotiation with the stone, with the haze, with one another, with the soft brown dusk that is the only sky they will ever need.
