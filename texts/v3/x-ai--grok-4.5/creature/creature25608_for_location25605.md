The ones who live here are not arrivals. They are what remains after arrival has been worn away.

They stand, when they stand, the way the low walls stand: knee-high to nothing that still walks upright, fitted without mortar because the cold itself long ago sealed every seam. Their bodies are that same fitted stone, but stone that once chose, or was forced, to remember joints. You see the memory most clearly at the shoulders and the pelvis—places where the rock still knows it was supposed to turn, and so retains a softer grain, a darker glitter of ancient frost that never quite melts and never quite hardens further. The rest of them is ridge and plane. Edges have been taken off by the same finished erosion that shaped the cirques; nothing sharp remains except the thin clear teeth of ice that sometimes grow along a jawline or the underside of a wrist, bead, drip once, and stop. Those teeth are not weapons. They are simply the body agreeing to the local moisture.

They move only when the geometry fails. A distance folds, a ridge steps closer, and one of them is suddenly a few hundred paces nearer than it was—exactly as weathered, exactly as empty of haste. Then the fold ends and they are far again, or they are behind you, or they are standing in the faint, old prints of what might have been their own earlier passing. Their gait, if it can be called that, is residual, the memory of walking after walking has already happened for the last time. Six limbs are common, though not mandatory; the extra pair often stays folded against the torso like buttresses, deployed only when the angles of the bowl refuse to add up and the ground needs more points of contact to stay believable. Some have fewer. Some have none, and simply occupy a place the way a doorway occupies its freestanding air.

Their faces are circles of upright slabs leaning inward. Not skulls—those would imply an interior that time still distinguishes from exterior. Just the leaning slabs, listening to the roar from the inside. The roar is louder in them than in the open valley. It occupies the chest cavity the way it occupies the bowl: continuous, sourceless, the sound of a waterfall frozen in the act of falling, or of the mountain grinding its teeth in a sleep that no longer dreams of waking. When two of them pass near each other the roar does not double. It thickens, the way shadows here thicken into denser coolnesses without ever gaining edges. No speech differs. Speech would be variation, and variation has already been spent.

They do not hunt the pale occasional things that flicker between stones. Those frost-hares and six-legged glimmers are not prey; they are loose moments of the same substance, shreds that have not yet settled into final posture. Sometimes one of the larger beings unfolds a limb and the smaller shape climbs onto it and simply sinks in, the way condensation sinks into the undersides of overhangs. No struggle. No nourishment visible. Only a slight darkening of the lichen that clings in the lee of the larger form, a brief flush of old-iron color that fades back to black. Growth is not the point. Completion is.

Shelter is imitated the way the mountain itself occasionally imitates it. A being will stand alone for a season or a century (the difference is not marked) until the wind that no longer blows remembers it, and low walls of fitted self rise around its feet, never higher than the knee. A doorway appears without walls. A circle of its own listening slabs tilts inward more steeply. These are not homes. Homes would imply that something still needs to come in from outside. They are simply the body agreeing, once more, that enclosure was once a thought that could be had. When the geometry flickers these partial shelters flicker with it—suddenly close, suddenly across the bowl, suddenly underfoot like older footprints approaching from the front. Then they settle again into the same absolute weathered stillness as the cliffs.

Blood moves in them the way it moves in anything that stands here long enough: provisionally. You can feel it if you stand very near—one more quiet fact against the skin, no warmer than the pewter light. It does not steam. It does not brighten. It simply continues until the valley decides that even this detail can be weathered into stillness. Some of them have already reached that decision. They are the cairn-like lumps scattered at long intervals: no longer limbs or slabs or listening faces, only the finalized shape of having been upright. The others do not avoid these lumps. They pause beside them the way moisture pauses in the old riverbed hollows, recognizing the ghost of motion.

Nothing about them is hostile. Hostility would require an intention that continues into the present, and intention here has already reached its last posture. They are finished, and loud with the sound of having finished, and lightly damp in the way a tomb is damp. Their rarity is absolute even when several share the same long sight-line; the emptiness between them is part of their density. Each one is a beacon that does not call. It only marks how much space there is to be wrong inside, and how stably the wrongness holds, and how completely the cold has already won.

They have always been here in the only tense the high country permits. They will always be here in that same tense. If you remain long enough you may notice your own joints beginning to remember stone, your own chest filling with the roar that has no source, your own shadow losing its edges against the dim, unchanging pewter. The valley does not mind. It has room. It has already finished making room.
