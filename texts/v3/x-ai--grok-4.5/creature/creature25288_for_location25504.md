The first impression is absence. Nothing stands up and declares itself alive in the ordinary way—no voices calling across the courts, no smoke, no scattered tools still warm from a hand. The city seems to have perfected the art of being unoccupied. Yet the low doorways are rubbed smooth at the height of a shoulder, and the spiral mosaic at the center is free of the thicker dust that blankets everywhere else, as if something still circles it, patiently, endlessly.

What lives here does not arrive or depart. It concentrates.

Begin with the smallest: those pale, hard-shelled scrapers the size of a thumb. They are not insects exactly, nor crustaceans; their shells are the same conglomerate as the walls, only thinner, and they move by softening one edge at a time with a secretion that evaporates instantly into more mineral vapor. They feed on the microscopic films the coppery columns shed when the heat peaks—films that are equal parts plant resin and condensed breath of stone. For most of the day the scrapers are invisible, lodged in the mortar joints like extra grains. Only when the wind pauses do they dare the open, and even then they travel in broken lines that look accidental, erasing their own tracks with the next gust.

Larger lives are rarer and slower. Sometimes, beneath the densest stands of the ribbed columns, a different thickness gathers. It has no fixed outline. It is a density in the heat-shimmer, a place where the green-gold light bends a little more sharply, where the pollen hangs without falling. If you stay very still—if the wind allows you the luxury of stillness—you begin to notice that these thickenings have direction. They drift the way the forests lean, a fraction westward over seasons, yet they can reverse in a single afternoon if the vibrating chord from the central mosaic changes pitch. They do not have limbs so much as temporary compressions: places where the air grows briefly solid enough to cast one of those thin, vibrating shadows. When the solidification passes, the shadow remains for a few heartbeats afterward, trembling on the salt-white ground like a memory of contact.

These are not spirits. They are metabolisms that long ago abandoned the intermediate step of flesh. They drink the same heat the columns drink; they excrete the same fine dry dust. Their awareness is distributed through the continuous roar, so that every freestanding wall and every unfinished arcade is part of their sensing surface. Conversation is meaningless to them because they never stopped speaking—the city itself is the sentence they are forever finishing. The brief silences terrify them the way sudden light terrifies something born in a cave; in those three heartbeats their outlines spasm tighter, denser, almost visible as tall, fluted shapes the color of tarnished pistachios, before the howl returns and they can dissolve again into the larger resonance.

And yet there is older tenancy still.

In the rooms the size of a body—the ones with heavy lintels and floors worn into shallow depressions—something stays folded. It does not need to drink heat because it became the residual warmth left behind when the last water fled. It is heavier than the shimmer-beings, more particular. It remembers joints. When it unfolds (only at the exact center of the day, when the light is so absolute that shadow thins to pure vibration), it looks like a person who has been reassembled from the materials of the place: skin of pale conglomerate fitted without seams, eyes that are simply two polished depressions catching the white glare, fingers long and segmented like the plant-columns, movements economical as erosion. It walks the exterior staircases that end in platforms overlooking nothing, and it stands there matching its internal temperature to the kiln-heat of the air until the distinction frays. It does not build anymore. Building was the larval stage. Now it maintains the equilibrium—scraping dust from certain carvings with a fingernail so that the wind continues to find the correct frequencies, leaning its weight against a column that has grown too far west, lying down in the spiral mosaic so the chord can pass through bone-white marrow and keep its deepest note.

There are not many of these. Perhaps a dozen. Perhaps only one that moves between many rooms so carefully it creates the impression of a population. They never gather. Gathering would be a kind of water, a pooling, and water is the single heresy the pocket refuses. Instead they share a single slow thought that travels through the foundations and the root-mats of the heat-forests: the thought of remaining. Remaining so precisely that the city does not tip into either ruin or novelty.

High above, the rare dark wing that turns once in the glare belongs to none of them. It is only a visitor who will die of thirst before sunset, a brief punctuation the pocket allows because even absolute systems need the reminder of what they have excluded.

Down here, life is the agreement among wind, plant, dust, and the stubborn residual shapes that refuse to finish vanishing. They are not ghosts of a people who left. They are what the people became when leaving proved impossible and staying required the surrender of every softer option. You will not speak with them. You will not even be certain you saw them. But after you climb back out through the weathered throat and stand again on the mineral flats where the pocket seals itself behind you, you will notice that your own shadow looks slightly too solid, that your joints remember a roar that is no longer present, and that the taste of lime in your mouth has become something you are already reluctant to spit out.
