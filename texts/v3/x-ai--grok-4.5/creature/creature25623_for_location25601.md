The streets and gantries offer no one. That much is immediate. Catwalk gratings ring under no foot that is not scheduled; the black canals below them accept only the drip of condensate and the occasional slick of oil that has escaped a gasket older than memory. Sealed trams slide their routes like beads on a frozen abacus. Maintenance frames stamp past in press-gangs of six or eight, articulated limbs ticking through the same interval every time, chassis beaded with the same sweat the basalt weeps. They do not look up. They do not vary. Whatever directive once animated them has long since worn smooth into pure periodicity, and so they register as weather—another pressure system, mobile and metallic.

Yet the cities are not vacant.

Go higher, into the turbine halls where the great rotors keep their unending station, or lower, into the foundry undercrofts where the light is only the blue stutter of arcs and the glow of metal that has never been allowed to cool. There the dwellers keep their shifts. They are not many. A single complex may hold fewer than a hundred, dispersed so widely among the vertical dens of piping and crane-roads that days can pass without one crossing another’s sightline. They move only in the pocket silences between the stacked industrial notes—those brief troughs when a safety valve has finished lifting and has not yet reseated, or when a cage-elevator has docked and its cables still sway with residual travel. In those troughs the dwellers pass like afterimages, gray on gray.

Their bodies have been shaped by the same refusal of change that locks the moss at crown. Limbs are long, jointed with an economy that echoes the leafless wands outside the blast walls; the torsos are narrow, ribcages reinforced by slow deposition of mineral taken from the sweating stone itself, so that the chest clicks faintly when breath is drawn. Skin is a matte, translucent pale, the color of drip-ice before it decides whether to freeze or fall. Beneath it the veins run dark and viscous, carrying a blood that has thickened against the cold until it moves with the same deliberateness as the peat-water in the hollows. They do not shiver. Thermogenesis has been surrendered; the ambient chill is simply the medium in which they remain visible. When they exhale, the breath hangs in place exactly as the plateau insists all breath must hang, and then invents no further history for itself.

Faces are open and nearly featureless by any softer world’s standard—nostrils reduced to flat valves that open only on the metered schedule of the plant’s own ventilation cycle, eyes large and shadowless, adapted to the unvarying gray-white that might be morning or afternoon. The eyes do not blink so much as iris-down in slow diaphragmatic pulses timed to the distant stamp-hammers. They have no use for names that imply sequence. Each is known, if at all, by the sector and function it has always held: Ladle-West Continuity, Stack-Four Resonance, Ore-Gate Interval. Speech, when it occurs, is spare and already half-mechanical, phonemes cut to fit between the teeth of the great gear-trains. A full sentence might take the length of a descending elevator’s travel; half of it will be numbers that have never needed updating.

They eat little. What sustenance they require is drawn from the same cycles that feed the furnaces—mineral slurries decanted from the black canals, protein chains cultured in the warm effluent jackets of the converters, occasional scrapings of the bronze moss that the outer walls permit to creep through unsealed expansion joints before the truce asserts itself again. The moss does not grow inside; it is metabolized before it can remember how. Waste heat from the bodies is kept deliberately lower than the machines’, so that the dwellers do not appear as anomalies on any thermal survey that will never be performed. They sleep standing, locked into spare harnesses between coolant risers, eyes open and iris-down, while the chord of the city continues through them. Sleep is not restorative; it is merely another mode of staying exactly on frequency.

Their intelligence is not conversational. It is diagnostic and minute. A dweller will stand for an entire watch beside a single bearing housing, palm flat to the metal, feeling for the tremor that is not yet a failure, the heat that is not yet an imbalance. When correction is required it is performed without ceremony—an oil port tapped, a set-screw advanced a fraction that no gauge would register, a condensed organ-pipe of ice cracked free before its hum can drift sharp or flat. They never log the act. To write it down would be to admit that a before and an after once existed. The plateau does not traffic in such distinctions. Sometimes, during the rare dimming of a weather-cell, their skin answers the moss’s single blue pulse with a faint reciprocal fluorescence along the mineral-ribbed chest, a brief pulse-code no one has ever troubled to translate. Then the light levels again and both the green and the gray continue as before.

They do not leave their cities. The open plateau, with its kilometers of bare sweating stone and its forests of clicking wands, is not forbidden; it is simply off-rhythm. A dweller who stepped beyond the outer blast wall would carry the city’s chord in the chest for a while, a private engine idling against the vast silence, until the cold lushness drowned the frequency and the body, no longer calibrated, would slow into the same locked green sleep that holds the moss. None have tried this within the deep present. The notion of trying belongs to a gradient of time that does not obtain here.

Children are not born. When abrasion or a rare cascade failure finally stills one of them—after intervals that might be measured in the rising of whole new fist-ranges—another is released from the gestation vats sunk into the deepest undercrofts. The vats themselves are part of the original clamping of city to cliff; they have always produced replacements at the exact rate that replacements are required. The new dweller climbs out already adult, already elongated, skin already beaded with basalt sweat, memory already stocked with the single continuous shift that has never begun and will never end. It takes its place at the vacant harness. The chord does not hitch.

Thus they persist: quiet vector-points of attention inside the louder eternity of belts and arcing current. They are the reason the weirdness remains equilibrated—mid-drip ice vanishing back into drip, bioluminescent pulses subsiding without sequel. They are not masters of the machines, nor servants. They are the biological component of the same bare tolerance that keeps the young stone from eroding, the weather-cells from migrating, the green from advancing or browning. Inside the blast walls they keep the oil-shine wet and the gears meshed. Outside, the moss holds its crown. Between the cities the deep note continues to sound in the ribs of anyone who still possesses ribs. Nothing is coming. Nothing is leaving. The dwellers walk their rounds in the troughs of the noise, and the plateau, vast and self-contained, does not notice that it is being maintained.
