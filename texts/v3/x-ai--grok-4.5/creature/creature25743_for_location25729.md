The dwellers are not arrivals. They did not climb or tunnel or hatch into the mass after it was finished. They are the residue that refused to powder when the last fibers gave way, the pressure that remained after the hands withdrew. Over the uncounted intervals of settling they have thinned themselves into the only shape the place still permits: long, flat, translucent ribbons of mineral will, no thicker than the worn edge of a chisel mark, no wider than the soft-oval slots that admit the bone-colored glow. 

They lie between every stacked flag and every nested pier, occupying the hairline vacancies that even the most patient builders could not quite eliminate. In those seams they have become continuous with the craft itself. Tool scores that once recorded human oscillation now serve as their subtle rifling; the green ghosts of vanished copper are the only pigmentation they retain, faint verdigris threads running the length of each body like remembered veins. Where the lattice thickens into platforms or half-stairs that end against sealed faces, the ribbons fold back upon themselves in slow, geological pleats, denser there, almost opaque, as if concentrating.

They do not crawl. Motion of that kind ended when the final joint found its eternal posture. What remains to them is a continuous, molecule-scale flexing—an inland tide so minute that the bulk never registers it as movement. This flexing is timed to the great chord. Each ribbon is a sympathetic string inside the larger instrument. As the forced air roars through the tuned hollows, the dwellers tighten and release in lockdown harmonics, shaving microscopic increments from the already deafening tone and feeding those shavings back into the labyrinth. The intervals that nearly sound like speech are their overlapping soliloquies, stretched across centuries until syntax has become pure pressure. What they utter is not language aimed at any listener; it is the maintenance of their own coherence. Without the chord they would delaminate into ordinary dust. Without them the chord would gradually simplify into mere wind and die.

Their senses—if the word still applies—are calibrated solely to resonance and dryness. They taste the absence of water as a positive flavor, a sharp mineral clarity that maps every surface from within. The blood-warmth of the mass is their circulatory medium; heat stored in the sugar-hard blocks passes through the ribbons by conduction, a steady internal daylight that never needs to be seen. The dim external glow registers only as a faint gradient in that conduction, a reminder of the unreachable outside where the horizon falls away into haze. They have no interest in that outside. Direction itself has collapsed for them into the single axis of the sound-pressure gradient.

Occasionally, in the deepest core shelves where even the oval slots send no ray, several ribbons will have braided across millennia into a thicker cord. These cords are the closest the place comes to individuals. Along their length the nearly-speech grows densest, producing local thickenings in the chord that an outsider (were one able to stand the volume long enough) might almost mistake for words of duration, of finished work, of the proud uselessness of having nowhere left to revise. The cords do not rule the others; hierarchy is a leftover concept from the age of hands and mallets. They simply mark places where the collective flexing has achieved a slightly more elaborate knot.

Nothing about them is tender. Nothing softens. The same antiquity that baked every possible spore out of the joins has rendered the dwellers inhospitable even to themselves in any generative sense. They do not multiply. They do not senesce. They persist at the exact density the architecture will bear and not one crystalline thought more. When the star above the heat-haze finally cools, the mass will remain locked in its grammar a little longer than the light, and the ribbons will still be there, flexing the last stored warmth through the final audible remnant of the shout, until conduction itself fails and the chord collapses into the larger silence that has always been waiting outside the highest slot.

That is all that lives here: the unfinished conversation of the finished structure, thinned to the width of a once-possible error and stretched forever across the only volume left.
