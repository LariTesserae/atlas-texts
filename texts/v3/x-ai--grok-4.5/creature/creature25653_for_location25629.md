The ones who live here are few, and they have grown sparse in the same proportion as the stone. They are upright, bipedal, recognizably like the mountain-climbers who once wandered into this fold by accident and never quite left; yet the resemblance is something the place itself has edited. Skin holds a persistent sheen, as if the perpetual film of water has taught it a slower evaporation. Pores have narrowed. Nails and the edges of teeth carry a faint mineral hardness, the slow incorporation of sulfide dust and dissolved basalt. Their breath, when it leaves them, joins the mist without protest, becoming one more deliberative pulse among the others.

They move singly. You will not see two of them cross the same plaza at the same hour unless some shared maintenance requires it, and even then they keep the width of a tower between their bodies. Conversation is rare and low; when it happens it sounds almost like the lattice walkways contracting—short, precise syllables that do not travel farther than they must. Their eyes have adjusted to the long blue shadows and the sudden thinning of haze at noon; irises lean pale, pupils quick to open in the wet gloom inside the towers. They do not squint. They have forgotten how.

Clothing is less worn than grown or secreted. Thin layered membranes of the same self-annealing ceramic that skins the buildings cling close to the body, vented along the spine and under the arms so that the capillary systems of the architecture can handshake with the capillary systems of the flesh. Heat is shared without ceremony. A person stepping from an interior into the weeping air does not shiver; the garment simply tightens its gradients, borrowing a few degrees from the wall as they pass. Footwear is almost unnecessary on the bare mineral, yet they wear thin articulated soles that click once, softly, against stone—echo of the jointed pale rumor that sometimes crosses above them. Some of them have begun to wonder whether that rumor is an escaped tool, an earlier draft of themselves, or a visitor that learned the city’s rules faster than they did. No one has caught it long enough to ask.

Inside the towers the rooms are spare and exact. Surfaces remain slightly damp to the touch except where deliberate dry pockets have been held open for sleep or for the slow work of thinking. They sleep in short, deep intervals timed to the twenty-minute weather cycle, body temperature dipping just enough to match the exterior cold so that waking is only a gentle reassertion of the interior gradient. Dreams, when they speak of them at all, are full of faceted growth and the sound of frost flowers sublimating. They wake already knowing which wall has thickened overnight, which channel has rerouted.

They eat little that a lowland body would recognize. Nutrient films are cultured on the same dendritic patterns that crawl the outer stone—translucent plates harvested before dawn shatter, tasting of cold electricity and distant iron. Water is taken directly from the weeping rock after the heat-exchange matrices have gentled it; it carries a faint sweetness left by the crystalline ecologies. Hunger itself has been edited toward efficiency. A person can go days on the residual salts in the mist if the towers are busy with larger repair.

Children are uncommon and quiet. When they appear they are already half-adapted, skin pearlescent, fingers paired with fine tools that look grown rather than made. They learn the city by walking its lattices until the sub-audible hum feels like an extension of their own pulse. They are taught early how to step upward in the brief loosenings of gravity, how to stand still while a beetle-drone swarm flows around their ankles unmaking a fallen shard, how to listen with their teeth for the deeper re-phasing vibrations. By the time they are grown the mountain’s knife-edge feels like a natural horizon and the idea of open wind is faintly nauseating.

Yet they are not machine. Warmth still pools in them. They keep small private objects that serve no municipal function: a polished conchoidal flake carried in a palm for the pure pleasure of its fracture lines; a stopped vial of last winter’s frost flowers that never quite sublimated; a length of crystalline film trained to grow in a spiral around one wrist like a living bracelet, cracking and reforming with the hours. Sometimes, in the central plaza, one of them will stop and turn a full circle simply to feel the entire enclave arranged around a single pair of lungs. The air tastes of wet iron and distant electricity. They breathe it and become, a little more completely, the place that breathes them.

At the rim, where the mist is woven thicker, they have learned to damp their own thermal signatures without thinking. The city remains hidden because they remain hidden—each solitude a deliberate pixel in a larger camouflage. They are young, as the towers are young. Their edges have not yet learned to be kind. And still, hour by hour, cycle by cycle, they continue the quiet work of living here: adjusting, secreting, listening, becoming sharper in the only climate that will have them.
