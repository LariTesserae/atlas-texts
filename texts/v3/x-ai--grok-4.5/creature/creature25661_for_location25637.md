The first impression is that nothing lives here that is not already the place itself. You watch a curtain of aerial roots for an hour and realize the curtain has relocated three handspans leftward, not by walking but by withdrawing from one set of anchors while secreting another, as if the concept of location were optional. The rare softmoth drifts past—a pale, pulsing scrap that looks more like a detached bract than an animal—and even it settles onto a leaf only long enough for the leaf to begin assessing its chemistry. Then the moth is gone, or absorbed, or simply reclassified as localized turbulence in the green.

Yet duration alters the reading. Stay longer, let the green-gold pressure work on the eyes, and other rhythms declare themselves. There are consolidations. In the temporary plazas where canopy giants have called their uneasy truce, the air thickens with a different intent. Spores and pollen and abraded cuticle do not merely drift; they braid. Over the course of a rainy exhalation they knit into loose, upright lattices that hold a suggestion of posture—four peicendes that might be limbs, a central mass that might be torso, a tapering that could be read as head if one were lonely enough to need the word. These figures last only as long as the humidity gradient that forms them. When the fog is drunk back by the stomata, the lattice unravels into separate motes again, each mote already forgetting it ever participated in a silhouette. They are not born and do not die. They are weather that briefly learns anatomy.

Deeper in the corridors, where the rhizome plate has heaved into low ridged ramparts, another kind of presence keeps steadier shape. Here the twiners have grown so densely that their combined vascular systems have begun to share pressure waves. A pulse travels the length of a fused gallery and, at certain nodes, blooms outward into a swelling the size of a kneeling body. The swelling is warm, faintly phosphorescent with the internal weather of too many chloroplasts working at once. If you set your palm against it you feel not heartbeat but the slower tidal surge of collective decision: open stamens here, seal vessels there, abandon this storey, corkscrew a new one toward the thicker light. Sometimes the swelling extrudes a temporary sensory surface—a palm-sized patch of tissue that is all stoma and fine root-hair—and the surface tastes the air, or your skin, and then retracts with the soft wet sound of a decision rescinded. These nodes have no names and want none. They are simply the places where the argument of meristems has grown complex enough to buffer a self for a while.

Still farther, in the zones where reality has grown porous, the living become harder to separate from the failures. A fold in space that returns you to your own footsteps will sometimes return you accompanied. The companion is made of the same green, yet it walks a half-second out of phase, its weight falling through the rhizome plate in a different year. You see its leaves flash the ultraviolet home-signal of a species that has not yet evolved, or that evolved and was already written over. When the fold seals, the companion remains for a time, disoriented, its tissues arguing whether to lignify into permanence or dissolve back into the continuum. Most choose dissolution. A few linger, becoming the quiet courtyard anomalies: moss that remembers street grids, ferns that keep trying to grow at right angles to gravity, a single upright stem that flowers in sequences no pollinator will ever recognize because the sequence is a message addressed to a geometry that no longer obtains.

There are older things yet. In the sinklands where the plate has torn and shown its white fracture planes, something like memory pools. Not personal memory—nothing here keeps a self long enough for biography—but the accumulated pressure of every collapsed vault, every abandoned plaza, every agreement between giants that failed. The memory is viscous. It seeps upward as a clear gel that hardens, under the green light, into translucent panes. Behind the panes shapes move with deliberate slowness: vast, patient rearrangements of leaf and root that have been practicing the same slow gesture for longer than the current canopy has stood. They are not trapped. The panes are their chosen interface, a way of slowing the surrounding roar so that a single sustained intention can be completed. What the intention is cannot be read. It has to do with the horizon, with the cooler uplands where the pure plant regime begins to fray, with the coast the water never quite reaches. Sometimes a pane softens and a long root-tip extends through, tasting the ordinary air like a tongue testing a tooth that no longer hurts. Then the root withdraws and the pane firms again.

And then there are the ones who almost have faces. They form in the brief hours after a major architectural mass loses coherence and settles. The sudden mulch releases a cloud of volatiles—alarm chemicals, growth regulators, the sweet wreckage of ruptured sugar stores. In that cloud the spores of a certain epiphyte, normally solitary, germinate in frenzy and lace themselves into thin free-standing screens. The screens catch the green-gold light and throw it back as shifting planes of brighter color. Look long enough and the planes organize into the memory of eyes, of mouths mid-sentence, of hands lifted in a gesture that might be warning or welcome. The faces last less time than it takes for the mulch to cool. While they persist they seem to watch the living density with something like recognition, or nostalgia for a speed of thought that flesh once had and green has surpassed. When they go, they leave only a finer dusting of green on the nearest surfaces, already being assessed as substrate or rival.

None of these are inhabitants in the way a mind raised on animal continuity would mean the word. There is no private interior, no sealed skin, no sleep that is not also digestion. What lives here is the continuous negotiation itself, sometimes thickened into a posture or a pulse or a remembering gel, sometimes thinned to a moth-like scrap that has learned to travel between arguments. The plants do not host them. The plants are them, under different tempos of attention. Stand still and the damp beads on your skin; the nearest surface begins its slow chemical inquiry. You feel the inquiry as a warmth that is almost affectionate and almost hungry. In that warmth it becomes possible to understand that you, too, could be lived through—that your edges could soften into curtains of root, that your thoughts could be shared out as pressure waves, that you could join the great spelling and finally be fluent.
