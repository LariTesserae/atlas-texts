The ones who live here do not arrive all at once in the mind’s eye. They appear first as absences given shape: the distance between one tower and the next is calibrated to their stride, the height of a cantilevered garden to the easy reach of an upraised arm, the hush in certain plazas to the pitch of their resting breath. You notice the accommodations before you notice them.

They are tall, but not uniformly. Some stand three meters at the shoulder when they choose to; others compress, folding long bone and tendon into compact resting forms that occupy little more space than a seated child. The change is not transformation so much as preference—an ongoing negotiation with gravity and mood. Their limbs are many-jointed in the quiet fashion of the larger shape that sometimes moves among the outer ridges; elbows and knees are suggestions rather than fixed hinges, so that a single gesture can arc through more degrees of freedom than an old-world body would allow. When they walk the packed-earth paths the sound is soft, almost apologetic: bare feet or something like feet, arched and slightly translucent, leaving damp prints that the warm air erases within moments.

Skin—if it is skin—holds the same fine condensation as every other cool surface. It beads, runs in thin clear rivulets along the collarbone or the long line of a forearm, then vanishes when they move into a warmer microclimate or when the air itself is politely edited around them. In the dim light the surface is neither matte nor glossy; it carries a slow subsurface iridescence, like the towers, as though liquid metal or cool latticework lives a millimeter beneath. Colors shift with bloodflow or whatever serves them for blood: deep jade at rest, warming toward copper when they quicken, cooling to the blue-green of the luminous moss when they stand long and still among the spiral trees. Some wear garments grown rather than woven—thin membranes that breathe with them, parting and resealing at collarbones or wrists so that skin can meet air or rain without ceremony. Others go uncovered; the damp never seems to chill them.

Faces are the last thing to resolve, and even then they refuse a single template. There are eyes—large, dark, adapted to the wet-slate sky—but the number is not fixed. Two is common; three or four appear when depth or spectrum must be parsed more finely. They can seal completely, leaving only smooth planes, or open into broader photosensitive bands that run back toward the temples. Mouths exist for speech and for the quiet tasting of air; they are modular, able to narrow to a precise instrument for a single clear note or widen into something almost avian when laughter (rare, soft, like water finding a new channel) moves through a small gathering. Hearing is distributed: fine feathery structures, reminiscent of the hare’s translucent ears, rise and flatten along the skull and neck, tracking the dual drip of condensation, the almost-imperceptible slide of a transit capsule, the chemical conversations humming through root and stone.

They do not crowd. A dozen may share a floating habitat for a season and still leave most of its internal volume empty, occupied only by slow tides of light and the occasional cultivated vine that has been invited indoors. When they gather it is usually in the low gardens or on the higher new ridges, standing or half-reclining at distances that feel, to an outsider, like solitary concrete. Yet information moves among them constantly. A fingertip brushed along a walkway’s grown-glass rail leaves a temporary mnemonic; the nanoscale relays overhead pick it up, braid it into the larger weather-and-data process, and set it drifting. By the time another of them passes the same rail hours later, the memory is already half-familiar—taste of recent rain, angle of a particular stone’s extrusion, a half-formed question about whether the bamboo thicket on the eastern flank needs thinning. Speech, when it happens aloud, is spare and musical, full of shared referents that never need explaining. Much more is conveyed by the brightening or dimming of the mosses at their feet, by minute shifts in the iridescence beneath the skin, by the way a spiral trunk’s leaves turn to track a departure.

They are builders, but gentle ones. A new joint grows into a walkway that has risen a millimeter over a decade because one of them noticed the stress and sang a low corrective frequency into the material until it remembered how to flex. Doors consent because the inhabitants long ago stopped thinking of thresholds as barriers; a surface simply recognizes the approach of a known pattern of warmth and gait and softens its refusal. They edit the air in plazas not for spectacle but because certain frequencies of quiet make thinking easier, and because skin that never quite dries and never quite chills stays in better conversation with the larger damp. Their technology is not worn or carried so much as inhabited—an extension of the same slow chemical dialogues that already run between vine and stone, root and channel. Some of them are more metal than meat on a given day, internal lattices brightening until the body becomes a temporary node on the city’s violet-pulsed network. Others reverse the ratio, spending seasons almost entirely biological, lying among the luminous mosses while microscopic grazers groom their skin and root networks share sugar-data across the thin membrane of contact.

Young ones—though “young” is an imprecise word here—are rarer than the land’s own newness might suggest. They appear as smaller, brighter nodes of iridescence, often riding the shoulders or hip-joints of an adult, large dark eyes open to every spectrum at once. Their education is the city itself: the patient business of water and leaf, the way a tower façade opens like a flower when the air is right, the courtesy of surfaces that remember and then politely forget. By the time they unfold into fuller height they already know how to listen to the almost-imperceptible further extrusion of young stone, how to ask a walkway for covenant rather than command.

There are loners. One may take a low, many-jointed gait into the outer ridges for months, mosses brightening in acknowledgment as it passes, returning only when some internal tide turns. Another may climb into the highest spiral canopy and become nearly indistinguishable from the pale bird with too many primaries—watching, not calling, feathers or feather-analogs stirring in the absence of wind. Yet even these carry the network with them; solitude here is never severance. The long conversation continues through them.

They eat sparingly. Broad jade leaves are harvested with ceremony and grown back within days. Nutrients are drawn directly from the damp channels or from the slow internal tides of the towers. Pleasure in food is real but refined—taste is another data stream, another way of remaining in dialogue with the place. Sleep is lighter still: a standing rest among the trees, iridescence dimmed to moss-light, while the body continues its quiet exchanges with air and root.

Time moves differently on their skin. A decade lifting a walkway one millimeter registers as a single comfortable stretch. The city’s “beginning” is their continuing present; they do not speak of founding or of arrival so much as of a gradual thickening of presence, the way condensation gradually beads until it must run. Some remember older rules—of weight, of growth, of wall and weather—and treat them as respected cousins rather than discarded parents. The strangeness they inhabit is simply the next clause in an unfinished sentence.

Stand long enough on one of the higher ridges and you will see them: a tall form unfolding from a translucent façade, condensation sheeting down one arm as it reaches to adjust a garden vine; two smaller figures crossing a catenary walkway in companionable silence, ear-structures tracking the same distant drip; a many-jointed silhouette pausing at the edge of vision while the mosses flare cold blue-green in greeting. They do not demand notice. They simply continue—lush and under-occupied in their own bodies, newly made and already deep in the long conversation that is both their city and themselves. Everything works. Everything has room. Everything, including them, is still, in some essential way, beginning.
