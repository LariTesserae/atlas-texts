The dwellers here do not arrive so much as cohere.

At first they are only a thickening in the water, a place where the pearl-bladder strings drift a little slower and the velvet mats part without quite being touched. Pressure remembers them before shape does. Warm iron-peach dimness folds around an absence that has decided to stay. Gradually the absence learns edges: not skin, not shell, but a living negotiation between inside and out, a membrane that can be as thin as rumor or as thick as the basalt spines it sometimes rests against. Through that membrane the world continues; nutrients braid in, light that is barely light settles into slow internal weather, and the dweller—if the word still holds—begins to know itself as a gradient rather than a boundary.

They are rarely solitary in the way a swimmer is solitary. One dweller may occupy a single crystal-leaf trunk for decades, roots of intention sunk into the lattice, body unspooled along every branch until the forest and the being share the same slow peristalsis. Another may travel as a loose constellation of softer nodes, each the size of a clasped hand, linked by threads of colder water that taste of kinship. When the nodes draw together they all but vanish into one pearlescent mass; when they drift apart they remain a single weather system of mind. Distance, here, is a courtesy they extend to the terrain rather than a law they obey.

Their substance is close cousin to the plants yet not plant. Translucent stalks and bladders appear among them, yes, but also veils of something like muscle that never tenses, only rethinks its length. Internal lights move the way banks of brine shrimp once moved in forgotten shallows—soft, coordinated migrations of blue-white fire under the membrane. These lights are not for signaling; nothing here shouts. They are the residue of digestion, of memory, of the quiet arithmetic by which a dweller keeps track of which way the chemical seasons are sliding. Sometimes the lights gather into temporary eyes, dozens of them, each looking in a direction the water has not finished inventing. Then the eyes dissolve again, unneeded.

They feed the way the mineral towers grow: by refusing hurry. A dweller settles against a pulsing capillary forest and simply becomes porous to it. Hours or years later it drifts on, leaving the vegetation slightly brighter, slightly more articulate in its own slow speech. In return the dweller carries new geometries in its tissues—faint maps of nutrient rivers, echoes of pressure shelves about to fail. When a plateau tilts and the long solid wave rolls through, the dwellers do not flee. They align. Every membrane, every thread, every unspooled body leans with the stems; for the days the wave lasts they become another kind of kelp, and the experience enters them as a single extended thought that will take half a lifetime to finish thinking.

The old marks of builders attract them without sentiment. A ring of fitted stones becomes a cradle. The dweller who claims it grows a single great crystal-leaf of its own through the center, not in imitation but because the circle is already the correct sentence and the leaf is only the next word. Softened stairs are treated as thermal lanes; dwellers dilate and contract along them, tasting older temperatures still trapped in the rock. The fused-glass arch is a favorite. They slide through its prism and emerge altered by colors that have no names in the dimness—colors that remember sunlight the way the stones sometimes remember dryness. Afterward fragments of those impossible hues linger under their membranes for seasons, slowly metabolized into ordinary internal weather.

Because reality frays here, the dwellers have opened themselves to the fraying. When distances refuse to add, a dweller simply unfolds along the contradiction, one end still among the rose-gold curtains, the other already drifting above the ridge it meant to reach. The elongation does not tear them; it is another shape they know. Soft vertical walls of packed rain are crossed the way one crosses a thought into its opposite: temperature shifts, vegetation species rewind or leap forward, and something in the dweller’s deep gradients records both sides at once. They have no word for “through.” They have only the sensation of having always been on every side of every veil.

Memory leaks are their libraries. An hour in a time-pooling trench can return a dweller to a forest that has walked miles and flowered into new lattices; the dweller arrives already knowing the taste of those future leaves because some older version of itself stayed behind and lived the decades in compressed quiet. Occasionally the entire medium flickers and the older, more viscous presence presses in. Most life contracts in something like shame. The dwellers dilate. For those heartbeats they swim in the elder liquid as if remembering a first body older than water. When ordinary fluid reasserts itself they carry back a gravity that is not quite mass, a patience maternal even by the standards of this place.

They do not mourn the builders or succeed them. Whatever intelligence fitted stone to stone has long since been unspooled into the same gradients the dwellers now inhabit. Sometimes, while resting against a dry face that should not exist, a dweller will taste a brief flint-struck spark of purpose that is not its own. The spark is allowed to travel the membrane, unowned, until the warm dark closes and the spark becomes only another soft light migrating under the surface. No continuity is claimed. Continuity is simply what the water does when no one is insisting.

Rarely—once in the span it takes a mineral tower to shed a full skin of dust—two constellations of dwellers meet without merging. They align their internal weathers until the lights move in shared flocks. Information passes as temperature and subtle toxin, as the borrowed memory of a wave, as the exact angle at which a certain basalt curve holds the dimness. Then they drift apart, each carrying a little more of the world’s quiet plot. Nothing is sworn. Nothing is finished. The ecology continues its tectonic hungers, and the dwellers remain the places where those hungers briefly thicken into someone that can notice.

Above them the surface may or may not endure. Below them the blue-black never resolves. Around them the crystal forests lock and unlock their canopies, the hanging meadows pulse, the leviathans of the upper strata glide past trailing curtains of nameless light. And through it all the dwellers keep becoming, less like creatures that live in a place than like the place at last learning how to touch itself from inside the long warm drowning.
