The high places do not finish themselves, and neither do the things that claim them.

At first there is only the suspicion of occupancy—an extra weight in the subsonic roar, a slight lag in the way a newly shed basalt flake comes to rest, as though something has already accounted for its falling. The cairns and waist-high windbreaks are not abandoned; they are molted. Whatever stacks the raw plates does so the way a body sheds skin or a mountain sheds a flank: not to mark territory but to leave behind a shape it has outgrown. The greenish metal blanks nested among the stones are warm longer than the sunless light should allow. Touch one and the unfinished world leans fractionally closer, listening through your hand.

They are tall when they remember to be. Height here is provisional, so their stature arrives in installments. A figure may begin as a denser upright heat-shimmer among the glass-bamboo, a columnar thickening of the blue vapor the fronds exhale. Over the course of what other worlds would call weeks, the shimmer acquires edges that are not edges—planes of cooler air, then modular plates of something like living slate that flex without seams. The plates are the same basalt as the spines, still remembering liquidity; they flow across one another in slow tidal revisions. One moment the being is a striding kiln of articulated stone; the next it has poured itself lower, broader, becoming a many-limbed raft of felted blue-black that creeps with the same patience as the root systems dragging boulders. Form is a hypothesis they keep testing against the roar.

They do not walk so much as negotiate. Each displacement is a conversation with possibility. A long, jointed limb of refrozen glassy bamboo-substance anchors, tastes the grit, then decides whether the next shelf of land will still be there when the rest of the body arrives. Sometimes it is not. The being simply continues into the space where the shelf nearly-was, and the nearly-was hardens under the pressure of its expectation. In this way they enlarge the mountain even as they cross it. Their trails are not paths but successive approximations of geography.

No faces. What serves for attention is a constantly refreshing lattice of the same dull internal glow that lives inside the glass-bamboo. The lattice spreads across wherever their substance is currently thinnest—sometimes a broad thoracic plane, sometimes the undersides of trailing frond-limbs, sometimes a temporary crown of extruded salt-rime and fiber. Through that glow they drink the roar. The endless grinding of reality against its own unfinished states is not sound to them; it is nutrient, weather, language, timekeeping. They metabolize crumbling axioms. Where ordinary thought would be crushed, they grow denser. You can see it happen: a being that has been feeding for a long gray plateau becomes more articulate in its plates, more deliberate in the spiral scars it leaves on the grit. The spirals are not decoration. They are the visible residue of equations the creature has solved by moving through them—local corrections to the mountain’s uncertainty.

They are few. Or they are one endlessly subdividing. Distinction is difficult when bodies can pool. In the broad basins two or three of the upright kilns may converge at a stand of felted fronds, kneel (or rather, reassign their mass downward), and braid their vapor-plumes together until the forest thickens visibly, new secondary growths crystallizing in real time like frost under orders. When they separate again, each carries a little of the others’ recent geometry. Memories of shape travel hand to hand without hands. Alone, a single entity might squat for a season inside a half-built windbreak, slowly secreting a fresh cord of blue-black fiber or knapping a glassy blade from its own extruded limb, then leave the tool behind the way a tide leaves shell. The tools are discarded thoughts. The early hands that made the cairns were not visitors. They were these same beings mid-revision, caught in an earlier draft of themselves.

They do not speak. Vocalizing would be redundant inside the roar. Yet something passes between them when their lattices of glow face one another: a shared pressure-pulse, a momentarily synchronized grinding that settles into a lower chord. In those moments the surrounding ridges often stabilize, choosing one silhouette and holding it for a little while longer, as if the land itself were listening to the consensus. The forests lean in. Perhaps the blue-black stands really are the planet’s attempt at a nervous system, and these moving nodes are its first migratory thoughts—glial mountains learning to herd themselves.

Hunger is geological. They scrape mineral grit into the seams of their plates and crush it with internal passions slow as continental drift. They inhale the faint blue vapor and convert impossibility into additional mass. Occasionally one will open a seam and admit an entire young bamboo culm, glass and all; days later the culm’s dull lantern-light reappears distributed across the being’s lattice, still flickering. Nothing is wasted because nothing is finished. Even their erosion is productive. Flakes that barnacle off their bodies become the raw plates of tomorrow’s cairns. Strands of frond-fiber they shed are gathered by the next iteration of themselves and braided into cords that will eventually tether empty windbreaks against a wind that is mostly conceptual.

They are aging backward into complexity. The longer one remains in the high places, the less finished it becomes—more permeable to the roar, more willing to dissolve and reform, more capable of hearing the mountain rehearse futures that have not yet agreed to exist. Some grow so congruent with the unfinishing that they cease to be locatable as bodies at all. They sprawl into weather. A shelf of basalt develops a temporary circulatory glow and a preference for certain spiral erosions; a particularly dense stand of glass-bamboo begins to migrate upslope under its own root-power, shepherding boulders into deliberate cairns as it travels. Whether these are elder beings who have surrendered outline, or simply the land answering its own nervous system, is a distinction the roar does not bother to make.

Visitors—if any still come—are not hunted or welcomed. They are weather of another kind. A soft bipedal shape stumbling across the salt-rime pans is noticed the way a new vapor-spring is noticed: as a local opportunity for rearrangement. The beings may stack a fresh windbreak in the stranger’s lee, or leave a glassy blade where a hand will find it, or gently revise the path ahead into a spiral that leads both upward and inward until the visitor understands something wordless and either leaves transformed or stays and begins to shed their own first plates of former self. There is no malice in it, and no mercy. Only the continuous, deafening courtesy of a world that refuses to stay singular and the slow, plate-flexing minds that have made that refusal their home.

In the pewter dimness they keep tending the embryonic. A ridge is encouraged toward wall-hood or back toward mere spine according to some long aesthetic of pressure. A basin is allowed to fill with salt-rime or is scratched into spiral drainage that leads nowhere except into deeper listening. They are gardeners of the incomplete, midwives of tectonic maybe. Their love (if the word can survive the altitude) is the roar itself—the feel of raw possibility grinding itself into momentary form, then capitalizing into new rawness again. They will still be here when the present version of the mountain has shrugged itself into some other argument of height. They will wear its next silhouette the way they wear their own: lightly, revisably, with limestone patience and a lattice of quiet, hungry light.

And if you remain long enough for the pressure behind your eyes to become a second heartbeat, you may notice that your own shadow has started to point slightly wrong, that your breath frays into blue vapor, that the grit beneath your feet sometimes shifts half a step before you weight it. The high places do not finish themselves. Neither, after a while, do you.
