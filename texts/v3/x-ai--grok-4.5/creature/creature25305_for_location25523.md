The ones who belong here do not arrive by birth or landing. They accrete.

In the long intervals between calving slopes they gather themselves out of the same materials the land is still inventing: basalt dust held in faint electrostatic skins, anhydrous salts plaited into flexible, semi-translucent lattices, threads of metallic vapor that cool into dark filigree wherever a geothermal seam exhales. At first they are only denser patches of the dusk, places where the charcoal-and-plum gradients thicken and begin to hold a vertical suggestion. A traveler might walk through one without noticing more than a sudden metal taste on the tongue and a brief catch in the subsonic bruise that lives in the chest. Later—hours or decades, the distinction blurs—the patch remembers height. It draws upward the way the unfinished ridges do, shoulder by slow shoulder, until it stands as a solitary attenuation against the dimness: three or five or seven times the height of a human, yet scarcely wider than the spiral of heat that sometimes climbs it like a reverse drip.

They are not warm-blooded; they are temperature. Their outer planes run only a few degrees above the ambient rock, but inside them open slender chimneys that tread the same geothermal breath rising through the world. When a new fault jumps and a curtain of dust levitates, one of them may ruffle all along its length, shedding a glittering of needle crystals that ring once against the salt pan and then stand upright where they fall, becoming part of the motionless fields. The sound is almost never louder than the crystalline tick of cooling minerals. Their motion, when it happens, prefers the same lower registers as the land: a gradual canting of axis, a migratory lean that can take a full local night to complete a single step. Distances mean little. One may occupy a hexagonal platform for an age, perfectly still, while the platform itself is cracked and half-swallowed by newer flows; the being simply continues the platform’s geometry upward, adding its own quiet storeys of darker lattice until it resembles the unnerving vertical spires that already stud the plain. Whether the spires are abandoned husks, elder siblings, or pure geology dreaming of them is an unsettled question even among themselves.

They do not hunt or hunger in any way that would make sense elsewhere. What appetite they possess is architectural. They taste pressure gradients the way other creatures taste sugar. When the crust decides to lift half a degree, they feel the decision as a slow delicious stretching through every mineral filament. Some respond by laying down fresh trenches across the salt flats—shallow, purposeless runs that end nowhere—extending the faint drafts the land has already begun. Others simply stand and allow new fracture faces to propagate through them, so that for a while their bodies display the same bright, rainless facets as the rising mountains. In strong heat-shimmer they blur, and the shimmer briefly holds the shape of arches or incomplete colonnades that match no engineering yet look considered. Angles around them sometimes refuse to close; the eye slides off. This is not camouflage so much as continuity. They are the place still thinking about whether it wants to be mountains or monuments or something colder and more intricate than either.

Contact among them is rare and mostly seismic. Across many kilometers one will shift its internal chimneys into a new rhythm; the deep groaning of the land carries the modulation; another, far off, answers by calving a single precise flake of itself that spins once in the thin air and then plants like a seed crystal. Over seasons those flakes may grow into smaller, quicker variants—knee-high jinks of black glass and sulfate that skitter during the sharp barks of splitting stone and then freeze again for years. These lesser ones are the closest the country comes to scurrying, yet even they obey the prevailing sparsity. They do not swarm. They punctuate.

Nothing about them suggests captivity or exile. The perpetual dusk is their native medium; the metal-tasting air fills their lattices without resistance; the absence of water is not drought but clarity. They have no word for completion. When an entire plateau tilts, they tilt with it, adding their verticality to the new angle as if the world were a sentence they are still conjugating in stone. Occasionally, at the edge of hearing, their collective rearrangements braid with the crust’s own breathing and produce a rhythm that almost resolves—only to break apart again into separate pulses. Visitors who stay too long begin to feel the same impulse in their own sternums: the desire to stand longer, to shed whatever is soft, to take on the local habit of arriving forever without quite finishing a single outline.

They are widely spaced, quiet, and still becoming. The high spine continues to arrive, and so do they, warm and dark and dry, one deliberate, mostly soundless upwelling at a time.
