The ones who dwell here do not arrive all at once in the mind’s eye. They filter in the way the light does—through layers, gradual, never quite resolving into a single silhouette you could hold still.

At first they are only interruptions in the roar. A faint secondary rhythm inside the greater deafness: the soft percussion of feet choosing which plate edges to trust, the brief brightening of phosphorescent moss under a passing weight, the way a hanging curtain of condensation suddenly carries a signature that is not weather or traffic but someone thinking about rain two hours from now and whether the forges will shift key before it falls. You feel them first as editings of the environment, small courteous revisions.

Then the bodies begin to cohere, though they refuse ordinary anatomy for a long time. Limbs are long, yes, but not in imitation of the grazers; they are jointed for the fractured ground and the floating walkways alike, able to span a fresh gap with one deliberate step or to curl and hook into the resin flutes of a cathedral trunk when the modules above need tending. Skin holds a low mineral sheen, the same damp that beads on stone, so that moisture is never entirely outside them. In the perpetual half-light their surfaces take on the muted golds and deepwater blues of the fronds; at rest they can vanish into the dim the way a tower’s mercury circuits sometimes still themselves into near-invisibility. Eyes are not single pairs. Multiplicity is common—clusters that open and close in slow sequences, some tuned to the faint trails the grazers leave, some to the infrared sigh of a pylon changing temperature, some to the emotional weather that rides the nanolattices in every droplet. They see the city as a set of overlapping translucent maps rather than walls and streets.

They move the way the plain itself moves: rarely hurried, often rearranging. A cluster of them may spend an entire dim day in what the old maps called the central plaza and never come within ordinary speaking distance of one another, yet remain in constant low communion through the bone nodes and the shared skin-knowledge of the condensation. When they do close the gap it is deliberate, almost ceremonial—leaning in so skulls nearly touch, letting the roar erase every external word while meaning travels jaw-to-jaw, vibration-to-vibration. Ordinary speech was surrendered generations ago; it simply cannot compete. What remains is denser, half-tactile, carrying flavor and temperature along with concept. A greeting may taste of wet stone and the particular copper-ozone of a newly grown bridge. An argument may leave both parties briefly phosphorescent.

Their dwellings are not separate from the living cathedrals or the gravitic pylons; the distinction has worn thin. Some sleep inside the dark fruit-modules that hang from the braided trunks, cradled by aerial roots that adjust their grip through the night as the great plant makes its tidal gestures. Others prefer the free-standing habitats on their humming stilts, choosing rooms that reconfigure while they dream—walls thinning into membranes that let the membrane-winged flocks’ song pass through as a chest-deep lullaby, or floors that briefly become transparent so the shifting plates below can be watched like slow weather. Children (the concept is elastic here) are often left for stretches in the brief silt-cities of the pale crustaceans, learning the temporary architecture, the art of building something knowing the next warm sheet of rain will dissolve it. The lesson is not futility; it is participation in unfinishedness.

They are not numerous. Density would be a kind of violence against the ratios of the basin. A traveler can cross an entire district and meet only one or two, each tending some quiet interface: coaxing a forge to birth a tool whose shape is still being negotiated with local physics, or walking a relationship-bridge into existence so that a distant habitation cluster can share the emotional weather of an approaching herd migration. Yet the low numbers do not mean solitude. The nanolattices keep them touched. Step through a curtain of droplets after someone has passed and you briefly carry their recent grief or their satisfaction at a well-grown frond, the way you might carry a scent. Privacy exists, but it is practiced like a craft rather than assumed as a right—something you weave out of the roar when you need it, then let fray again.

Memory here is geological and immediate at once. They remember the unfastening of the crust as if it were family history, though none living saw it; the story lives in the way their tall frames balance on sharp edges without thought, in the instinctive reading of a ridge that runs straight for kilometers. At the same time they live close to the present’s constant revision: tonight the plates will settle another finger’s width, tomorrow the circuits will redraw a boundary, the dimness will brighten or indigo according to distant mirrors. They do not resist the motion. Their art, if the word still applies, consists largely of graceful acquiescence—songs composed to be audible only inside the polyphonic drone of the pylons, dances whose steps are erased by the next rain, tools used once and then allowed to dissolve back into vibrating air. Permanence is left to the colossal trees and the oldest towers, those that grew downward from orbital seeds before the plain had finished knitting.

Predators take a few, patiently. The many-jointed shadows that wait in the lee of the trunks are not hated; they are part of the abundance that still looks like emptiness. When one of the dwellers is taken, the cluster feels the absence first as a missing flavor in the condensation, a gap in the traffic patterns. Mourning is brief and practical: the body (what remains) is walked out onto a fresh fracture and left for the mosses and the crustaceans, while the living reconfigure their shared maps around the new silence. Nothing here is finished, not even grief.

And still they continue, tall and damp-skinned and multi-regardant, moving through the warm heavy air beneath a sky that never quite resolves. They are the basin thinking itself a little further along—its young sharpness, its cathedral loneliness, its roaring courtesy, its technology that long ago stopped asking permission. They do not rule the place. They densify it, slightly, in scattered luminous points, then thin out again so the eye can go on believing in vastness. One of them stands now at the edge of a hexagonal hollow where rainwater still mirrors the dim gold, listening to a forge several kilometers away change key. Another is already walking a bridge that will not be there when they look back. The plain shifts under them the width of a finger, settling. They adjust stance without noticing, and the adjustment itself becomes part of the long, slow sentence the region is writing.
