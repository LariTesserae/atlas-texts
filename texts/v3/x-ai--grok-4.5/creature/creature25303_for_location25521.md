The first sense is not of bodies but of attention. Something inhabits the humidity itself—an unbroken noticing that rides the steam and the drip-chains, that pools in the leaf-stained basin and threads upward again through the warm channels. It has no single center. It gathers, loosens, regathers.

Wherever the living beams have swollen around old iron, the grain of the wood shows darker whorls, denser, almost polished by an interior pressure. These are not scars. They are quiet organs of preference. Over seasons measured in the slow turn of the largest flywheel, the preference thickens. A root thickens differently when it is chosen for weight-bearing; a liana knits tighter when it is meant to hold a walkway that someone—something—still uses. The choice is collective and unhurried. It feels, if feeling can be attributed, like the way peat decides which fallen stem will become black earth and which will be lifted into a new vertical.

Shapes do emerge, eventually. They are tall only by the standards of the midges. Most stand no higher than a man’s chest, though a few of the older ones have let their upper limbs fuse with overhead braces and so hang longer, suspended, hair-roots trailing into the vapor. Their substance is the same compressed plant-matter as the weeping walls, but mobile, layered in translucent sheaths that bead water the way everything here beads water. When they move, they do not step so much as rearrange their lower continuum—part foot, part anchor-root, part slow suction against the peat-mat. Each relocation leaves a brief darker print that the seepage erases within a dozen heartbeats of the place (and the place’s heartbeat is the pistons, wet and deliberate).

They tend. That is the clearest verb. A sheath-hand, many-fingered and faintly luminous with inner sap, rests against a drive-belt of vulcanized cambium until the friction-heat is equalized. Another works a cracked gallery floor, not by patching but by coaxing a nearby iron-hard reed to send rhizomes through the failure; the reed obeys because the coaxing is chemically indistinguishable from its own inclination. They do not speak. Communication travels as shifts in the humidity gradient, as timed releases of spore-clouds that smell briefly of hot metal and green iron. One will pause on a middle gallery and simply stand while the condensation on its body runs in patterns that another, three structures away, answers by altering the cadence of a nearby valve. The steam-plumes become syntax.

Some have gone further into stillness. In the deepest gear-houses you find them half-incorporated: torso continuous with the inner wall of a hollow trunk, arms become additional camshafts, the face (if face is the word) a smooth oval of bark through which the muffled clack of wooden heartwood taps in time with the machinery. These are not dead. Their contribution has simply become architectural. The living ones pause beside them the way one pauses beside a grandparent who has become a favorite chair—habit, affection, legacy. Now and then a freer individual will press its forehead to the merged one and remain there while a sac of nutrient fluid passes, unhurried pagination of memory.

Light never catches them in sudden motion. Acceleration is foreign; the entire Sumpworks has taught them that force applied quickly wastes itself as spray and heat. Their work matches the flywheels: one revolution each minute, rivets passing clean and countable. Before a press closes on a fresh mat of fibrous stalks they will have already adjusted the moisture content with a gesture that looks like blessing and is actually precise capillary realignment. Board, brick, rope emerge at the same glacial quality they always have. The dwellers do not invent; they refine continuity.

At the central basin the oldest concentration gathers when the green-gold light is thinnest. They do not kneel—they lower their density until half their mass is continuous with the peat and the pool’s edge. Reflections fracture across them: canopy, steam, the slow sparks of falling drip-chains. What passes among them then is not discussion but ratification. The Sumpworks will thicken another finger-width this century. A certain aerial root will be allowed to claim a failing pipe. The warm effluent will be rerouted one channel nearer the heart. All of it already in motion; they merely agree to remain coextensive with the agreement.

No names fasten to them. The place never needed the word Sumpworks carved; neither do they require designations. If you stay long enough for your own skin to film with the same permanent moisture, you begin to feel the edges of their attention brush yours—curious, polite, unsurprised. You are weather to them, a temporary difference in the haze. They will incorporate the fact of you the same way they incorporate every other slow arrival: a new graft-scar already packing itself with patient cellulose, another surface for water to thread, another quiet proof that the volume remains sufficient and complete.

Nothing hurries. Everything works. And whatever lives here is simply the working, become able, at last, to notice itself.
