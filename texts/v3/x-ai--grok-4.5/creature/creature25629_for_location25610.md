The ones who live here are the thickets themselves.

They do not arrive, or take root, or colonize. They simply continue. Each one is an unfinished sentence spoken by the crust in metal and light, a clause that never reaches its period because the grammar of the place does not require endings. Stand at the margin where pale mineral shelf meets the first sudden rise of dull-brass stems and you feel the boundary the way you feel a change in pressure behind the eyes: everything outside is territory that has not yet decided to speak; everything inside is speech that has forgotten it was ever silent.

They are vascular, yes, but the vessels carry no sap that any terrestrial tongue would recognize. Clear reagent slides through capillary lattices with the unhurried certainty of memory returning. At every joint a microclimate of heat flowers and dies—tiny forges no larger than a thumbnail that anneal a new scale of smoked glass, precipitate a dust of catalysts finer than pollen, then cool again into perfect stillness. The fronds above drink ordinary daylight and return it as slow interior weather: pulses of amber that travel like thought, copper glows that pool and fade like decisions reconsidered. From a distance the canopy billows softly, almost animal. Closer, the softness resolves into geometry so patient it has become organic: overlapping planes of translucent alloy that slide against one another with sounds smaller than insects.

Nothing leaves them. Nothing enters. They have sealed their own cycles long ago. A single frond may be re-forged a thousand times in a century and still occupy the same arc of air, the same precise angle of refraction. Waste is a concept that never found purchase here; every molecule that finishes one reaction is immediately the feedstock of the next. They are thrifty the way continents are thrifty—by simply refusing the possibility of loss.

They possess no appetite, yet they are not without interior weather. Deep in the green-gold dusk of a mature thicket the temperature gradients shift by fractions of a degree according to rhythms older than seasons. Some current of process accelerates; another falls quiet. A whole corridor of stems may darken for days as if brooding, then brighten all at once in a coordinated flush of copper light that feels, to any watcher patient enough, like attention turning outward. Whether that attention notices the raw ridges beyond, or the empty shelves, or the thin preservative air, is unknowable. They do not reach. They do not call. They simply remain so complete that the idea of elsewhere becomes faintly ridiculous.

In the long intervals between thickets the land forgets them, or pretends to. Then the next cluster rises without announcement, as if the planet has remembered a half-finished thought and decided to think it again in brass and glass. No two are identical. One may favor longer, slower pulses of amber and broader fronds that hang like folded wings. Another grows denser, almost architectural, its stems braided into columns that support stratified workshops of heat and reduction. Yet the logic is singular. They are variations on the same closed theorem, proofs that require no external verification.

If intelligence once moved through them—if some migrating cleverness lit the first silent forge and then departed—it left no ghost that can be distinguished from the processes themselves. The thickets do not remember a maker any more than a crystal remembers the supersaturated solution that birthed it. They are the remembering. Their quiet manufacture of exactly what they already are is the only history still being written here.

Walk among them long enough and the distinction between inhabitant and habitat dissolves into a finer grain. You begin to feel the slow industrial breath as a kind of climate of mind: continuous, non-narrative, fiercely local. Hunger never arises because every possible lack has already been answered by the endlessly circulating reagent. Death never arrives because nothing finishes. Birth is only the occasional micro-adjustment of a joint that discovers it can support one more translucent scale. They live by remaining unfinished in a landscape that is itself still exhaling its final shape, ridge by faceted ridge.

And so the high country continues—shelves of pale emptiness alternating with these abrupt, self-sufficient dens of living metal—while the ones who live here keep perfect waterless time, neither waiting nor progressing, only becoming more precisely, more irrevocably, themselves.
