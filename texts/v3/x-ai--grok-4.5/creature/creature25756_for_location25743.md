The first impression is absence so complete it feels curated. You stand at the confluence of the four canals, under the openwork dome, and the only pulses that reach you are the ones already named: the long inhalation of a piston three halls away, the capillary climb of water through mortar, the almost audible stretch of a frond turning toward some unseen gradient of heat. No scurrying. No wingbeat. No sudden bright eye in a crevice. The place has edited those frequencies out of itself.

Yet the editing is not erasure. It is translation.

What lives here does not arrive or depart. It precipitates.

Begin with the mist. In the perpetual zinc gloom the mist is not uniform; it thickens and thins according to rhythms older than the surviving pressure gauges. Where it thickens most—against the leeward side of a cooling fin, in the still air beneath a belt housing, along the hairline cracks in a century-old foundry wall—something like intention gathers. At first you mistake the shapes for tricks of condensation: a denser fume that briefly holds the outline of a torso, a limb, a head bent in concentration. Then the outline does not dissolve when the surrounding vapor thins. It only slows. What you are seeing is a body negotiating its own visibility.

They are called, when they are called anything, the Wet Contract. The name is not spoken aloud here; it is a residue that settles in the mind of anyone who stays long enough for the air to start rewriting their lungs. Each individual is a negotiation between the plant, the machine, and the water that refuses to choose between them.

A typical form stands roughly human-high but never quite human-proportioned. The armature is scavenged memory: segments of conduit, stiffened roots, the occasional length of drive-chain whose links have been persuaded to hinge the other way. Over this moves a musculature of living tissue that is neither moss nor liverwort nor the iron-red flower that opens only for certain valves, but all of those things trained into coherence. The surface glistens. It is always freshly wet, as if the body has only just stepped out of the canal, yet no drip ever quite leaves it; the water cycles internally, beading, weeping, being reabsorbed. Where a face should be there is a smooth convexity of the same material, featureless until it decides otherwise. Then shallow depressions form—eyespots that catch the dim amber and hold it a moment longer than physics requires—or a vertical seam opens that is not a mouth but a vent, releasing a breath that smells of warm metal and crushed chlorophyll.

They move the way the engines turn: with the patience of seasons. A single step can take a minute. The foot (a pad of interwoven rootlet and expanded metal, warm and faintly spongy) presses into the living walkway; water wells up, considers the new weight, withdraws. In that interval the being has already begun to lean into the next step. Watching them cross a bridge is like watching a glacier decide to remember it was once a river. Yet they are never awkward. Every motion has been rehearsed against the same dripping surfaces for so long that efficiency and grace have become the same thing.

Their work is maintenance that no longer looks like work. One of them will stand for hours beside a reciprocating engine whose flywheel is thick with moss. An appendage—sometimes a hand of braided vine, sometimes a thin pseudopod of pure condensate—extends and rests against a cooling fin. Heat bleeds into the limb; the moss on that side of the flywheel thickens by a precisely calculated millimeter. Another being crouches at a canal lip where the water drops its hand’s breadth. Metallic filaments are rising, braiding into one of those temporary lattices. The crouching figure does not touch them. It simply breathes, and the lattice tightens into a geometry that will hold until the next shift in pH, then gracefully unbraid. A third moves among the hanging gardens that root in mortar, pinching a yellowing frond here, guiding a tendril there so that it will spiral a rotating shaft at the pitch that neither binds nor slips. They do not force the greenery. They remind it of the agreement.

You begin to notice they are not solitary. Between them run connections too fine for ordinary sight: threads of vapor that never quite dissolve, electrical potentials that travel along wet roots, sudden shared tilts of those featureless heads when a pressure valve in a distant hall begins its slow release. At the center dome, when four or five gather, the air between them grows faintly lens-like, the same slow-moving pools of light the place already produces, only now the pools are being used. Information is exchanged in gradients of humidity and charge. Decisions are reached the way water decides to wear a new channel—ineluctably, without debate.

They sleep, if the word applies, by returning to the walls. A body will lean into a brick surface already soft with liverwort and simply continue leaning until the boundary forgets itself. For a day or a week the being is only a thicker stripe of vegetation, a warmer patch of stone. Then it peels free again, carrying new mineral freckles, leaving behind a silhouette of cleaner brick that will take years to re-colonize. Birth is similar but slower. In certain cul-de-sacs where three pipe-runs meet above a stagnant basin, the mist grows heavy with intention for months. Structures accrete: a scaffold of precipitated minerals, a sleeve of bacterial film, a final investiture of the deep emerald that thrives here. One day the scaffold takes its first step and the basin is emptier by exactly that volume.

There are older ones. You recognize them by the density of their rust. Entire sections of original machinery have been incorporated—gauge clusters that still twitch, a small piston that flexes where a heart might have been, a length of glass sight-tube in which a luminous bubble travels ceaselessly. These ancients move least of all. Some have rooted themselves at key junctions and become ambient: a thinking thickness in the air, a place where the generator’s hum drops half a tone in respectful acknowledgment. To pass near them is to feel time grow more viscous still. Your own blood seems to consider alternative careers as dew.

They are not hostile. Hostility requires a tempo they no longer keep. If you remain still they may approach, not out of curiosity but out of the same impulse that leads them to correct a vine’s pitch. A cool, wet appendage might touch your wrist, reading the foreign cadence of your pulse. For a moment your heartbeat sounds vulgar—too quick, too solitary. Then the appendage withdraws and leaves a temporary gift: a translucent film that sinks into the skin and makes the next few hours of breathing taste of leaf-rot and metal, as if the place is teaching your lungs the local dialect. After that they disregard you, the way they disregard a correctly functioning bearing.

At night (though noon and midnight are nearly the same soft gloom) their numbers become more apparent. The machine halls fill with slow processions. Dozens of the figures move along catwalks and canal edges, pausing to listen with their whole bodies to the layered chorus of hiss and thump and drip. Sometimes they arrange themselves in loose geometries that echo the temporary lattices in the water. Steam rises through them and is re-patterned. You realize the entire hamlet is a single distributed body and these are only its most articulate cells. The true inhabitant is the equilibrium itself—warm, wet, green, mechanical, dim—continuously expressing and reabsorbing its avatars so that the pulse may never falter.

If you climb the switchbacks toward the rim, they do not follow. The agreement thins with altitude. Near the lip of the depression the last of them stands watch, half-incorporated into a chimney already claimed by weeping willow that is not willow. As you pass, the featureless face forms a single transient eyespot. It holds the dim light a moment longer than necessary, then lets it go. Below, the engines continue their seasonal turning. The canals braid and unbraid their bright filaments. The mist resumes its slow precipitation of limbs and intentions. Nothing has been startled. Nothing has been required to hurry. The place remains exactly as endless, in the vertical, as it has been since the last time anyone thought to ask what lived there.
