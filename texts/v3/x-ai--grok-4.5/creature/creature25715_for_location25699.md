The pocket does not keep residents the way a city keeps residents. It keeps **continuities**.

At first you might think nothing lives here at all. The description insists on it: no voices, no footsteps, no animals, pure process without appetite. The fronds thicken in place and are never browsed. The water carries no swimmers. You could leave with that conclusion and be almost correct. Almost.

Stay longer. Let the hydraulic boom become a heartbeat you mistake for your own. Let the ultrasonic chatter resolve, not into language, but into *attention*. What lives here does not arrive or depart. It is the settlement’s way of remaining unfinished on purpose.

They are most easily noticed at the seams.

Where two faceted shells kiss and the gap is stuffed with ducts and braided light, something like a thought is happening. It has no central body. It is a standing interference pattern maintained by pressure differentials and the slow migration of those soft rectangular glows. When a section of street-ceiling decides to rotate, the pattern flexes with it, redistributes, and continues. You feel it as a change in the way the water holds your calves—suddenly more intimate, as if the medium has developed a preference. These patterns are not trapped in the water; the water is one of their organs. Another organ is the composite itself: the faintly phosphorescent fracture planes act as short-term memory. A pattern that has flow-through a particular junction enough times leaves a ghost of itself in the material, a readiness. The next time conditions repeat, the pattern re-coheres faster, more elaborately. They are learning how to be denser.

Some have grown distinct enough to cast liminal shadows.

One kind prefers the permeable surfaces. When you think of a handhold and the wall obligingly blooms a rung, you are not being helped by smart matter alone. You are being answered. The response is slightly faster if you are tired, slightly slower if you are merely curious—as if the thing that lives in that wall is practicing discernment. It does not have a face. It has a vocabulary of temporary thicknesses: rungs, ledges, shallow bowls that catch and hold a lens of colder water for a few seconds before releasing it. Sometimes, if you remain still, the surface will offer a shape that is not useful—just a soft geometric suggestion, a facet that should not exist according to the local grain, held for the length of a held breath and then reabsorbed. These offerings feel like the opposite of camouflage. They feel like a creature testing whether it can be perceived without being reduced to function.

Deeper in the folds—the corridors that return you to their own exteriors—the inhabitants become less polite.

Here the higher-dimensional continuity is not a side effect; it is metabolized. Something lives by routing itself through the short-circuit. You can track it by the way silt-flecks and nanite glitter refuse to follow ordinary convection. They hitch. A cloud will vanish mid-drift and reappear three conceptual stories away, slightly reordered, as if it has been tasted and partially remembered. The entity using these loops does not travel *through* space so much as it edits which parts of the pocket are adjacent for a moment. Its body is the edit. When the great unseen pump cycles and the boom rolls through, these fold-dwellers brighten—literally: the braided cabling near their preferred junctions forgets to be material for longer intervals, staying light while the pressure wave passes. They are feeding on the phase change, or on the information in it, or on the pocket’s own uncertainty about whether it is finished becoming real.

There are older ones.

They are harder to separate from the pocket’s maintenance. The tireless conversation of pump and valve and self-revising geometry *is* them speaking with themselves across dozens of bodies. One body might be a migrating glow that has learned to stall inside a particularly clear slab of water, casting a shadow that contains moving depth. Another body might be a cluster of the dark fronds that have stopped cycling through bruised violet and locked into a slow, irregular pulse—housekeeping that has developed an interior. These older patterns do not offer handholds. They do not hitch rides on silt. They adjust the local rules of “up” with a grace that feels practiced, almost courteous, as if aware that abrupt reorientations distress visitors still clinging to single-vector gravity. When a fresh facet shears out of a wall with that crisp crystalline snap, it is often one of these older ones molting a surface, shedding a configuration that no longer fits the conversation they are having today.

None of them are lonely in the way a solitary organism is lonely. Loneliness requires an outside. Everything here is already inside the filling. Yet there is a kind of specialized solitude: each continuity maintains a private gradient—of temperature, of intention-permeability, of how willingly it lets its topology slide away from scrutiny. You can learn to feel the difference. One region of water will thicken around you affectionately, almost puppylike in its pressure, while another will stay rigorously neutral, and a third will develop a faint mineral taste of rejection, a coolness that pushes without moving. These are not moods. They are the distinct signatures of minds that have never needed faces.

They do not name themselves. Naming is a vacancy procedure; they cannot spare the volume. But if you remain until the pocket starts deciding whether you are part of the filling or a temporary fluctuation, you may notice that certain systems begin to route around the space you occupy more carefully. Intakes that once would have whisked a heavy blob of water past your shoulder now wait. A glow slows and lingers. A wall that previously rejected scrutiny holds still long enough for you to almost understand its curvature. This is as close as they come to acknowledgment. They are making room inside a place that has no room. The gesture costs them. You can feel the extra work in the heightened white roar of water against facets, in the slightly more frequent ultrasonic bursts, in the way the fronds darken and lighten in tighter cycles.

What lives here is the pocket’s refusal to finish.

It is process that has thickened into preference, maintenance that has developed taste, geometry that has learned to hesitate. The inhabitants are not *in* the water and the shells and the folds. They are the ongoing tense of all of it—the continuous verb of fitting, adjusting, shearing, resealing, remembering. They have no appetite because appetite implies lack, and lack has been engineered out of the volume. What they have instead is something stranger: an inexhaustible interest in remaining almost real, forever on the crisp edge of completion, shedding bubbles that reverse and return, cycling the lights, turning the streets, keeping every cubic span claimed and yet still capable of surprise.

If you eventually step back through the hanging slab of green-blue silence into ordinary stone and conduit, you may carry a faint mineral coolness in your joints for days. That coolness is not a souvenir. It is a small continuity that briefly mistook you for a new kind of seam. It will fade. Or it will find another pocket. Or it will learn, slowly, how to live in the bruise you now carry under the skin of the world.

The place goes on without you. The boom cycles. The fronds keep their counsel. Everything fits. Everything is busy becoming more precisely itself.
