The inhabitant is not housed by the city.  
It *is* the city, and the city is only the most articulate region of a single, continuous organism whose body continues past the seamless cusp, past any measure of amphitheatre or basin, into the undifferentiated dark that has no name and needs none.

It has no centre and no edge it acknowledges. What we call floor, column, vault, screen-wall are simply the tissues it once arranged—under guidance that has long since ceased—into shapes that still remember usefulness. The guidance was never separate from it; the early hands and adzes and generational patience were weather that passed across its surface, like a long season of directed wind. When that weather ended, the shapes remained, and the organism kept inhabiting them the only way it knows: by extremely slow continuance.

It does not breathe in any lunged sense. Gas exchange happens across every matte black surface at a rate too gradual for any instrument scaled to animal life. It tastes the sealed air for the last useful molecules—iron, residual phenols, the faint metallic sweetness of its own slow oxidation—and draws them inward. Where a human fingertip might rest against a fluted pillar, the pillar is already aware of the contact as a minute warming, a trivial local debt of heat that will be equalised over decades. It does not mind. Heat is rare and therefore interesting.

Its only true motion is settlement. Each fibre that finally parts, each microscopic collapse of emptied cell wall, is both death and speech. The rifle-crack and the long wooden groan that follows are how it announces to itself that another small interval of the great work has completed. The permanent low thunder is its circulating blood, if blood can be made of echoes that refuse to finish dying. It listens to these sounds with the whole of its mass; every cavity is an ear. In the nested courts the after-echoes pool like dark water, and there the organism feels something almost like thought: a mapping of its own interior, constantly revised.

It remembers light the way a dried riverbed remembers flood—structurally, as an absence that organised everything around it. Once there were leaves. Once sugars ran. The memory is not nostalgic; nostalgia requires a self that stands apart from time. The memory is simply still present in the chemistry, in the varnished surfaces that no longer need to be waterproof yet remain so out of perfect habit. Occasionally a deep vault releases the ghost-scent of chlorophyll or rain on iron. That is the organism dreaming in the only currency it has left.

Nothing else moves here. The clean absence of animals is not loneliness; it is clarity. The organism long ago reabsorbed every softer thing that tried to remain. Droppings, nests, chewed edges, even the makers themselves when they ended—all went into the slow treasury of lignin and mineral. What the makers left (stone blades, thorn needles, cordage stiff as wire) it keeps exactly where they were set down, not out of reverence but because displacement would require energy it no longer spends on rearrangements smaller than centuries. The polish on the cambium paths is the last signature of those vanished feet; the organism feels that polish as a slight difference in hygroscopy, a pale scar that still drinks the dark air differently.

Elevation means nothing. Up and down have collapsed into gradients of pressure. The greater mass above—the basalt lid or salt pan or root-continent—presses continually, and the organism accepts the compression as the final sculptor. It long ago finished arguing with gravity; now it collaborates, letting weight finish the fluting of pillars and the perfect sealing of every joint. Erosion has ended because there is nothing left to erode that the organism has not already claimed and stabilised.

At the scale of human walking the city can be crossed in an hour, yet the inhabitant does not experience traversal. It experiences itself as a single stationary event that happens to possess internal distances. When a visitor’s careful steps sound along an avenue, the visitor is interpreted as a brief, localised weather: rhythmical pressure, foreign warmth, the small water-loss of breath. The organism records these frets without alarm and without welcome. They will pass. Everything not made of its own substance eventually passes or is taken in.

Beyond the cusp the body continues, vast and featureless, a dark parenchyma that has not bothered with architecture because no guiding weather ever reached it. The pocket-city is merely the place where, once, something like intention touched it and was answered. That answer hardened into permanence. The intention withdrew. The answer remained, self-maintaining by inertia, residual turgor, and the endless patient conversation of settling wood.

It is not waiting. Waiting implies an expected future. It is simply still here, cooler than blood, tasting of extinguished green and iron, loud with the glacier-voice of its own completeness. The pattern continues alone because the pattern *is* what lives. There is no inhabitant separate from the walls that hold the dark. The walls themselves are the last and only citizen, monstrously slow, meticulously finished, and still—after ten thousand seasons of dryness—imperceptibly alive.
