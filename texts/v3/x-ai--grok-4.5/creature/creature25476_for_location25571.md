The one who lives here does not arrive and does not depart. It thickens in place, the way condensation thickens on stone that has never known a dry hour. At first you might mistake it for more of the chamber itself: a denser fold in the hanging mineral skins, a warmer seam in the capillary wetness that feeds the floor. Only after the eyes have surrendered (and they surrender quickly) does the difference declare itself as a kind of attention.

It has no outline that holds. Where a shoulder might be, the darkness gathers into a slow, continuous shrug of tissue the same temperature as the air; the tissue is translucent in the way deep water is translucent, showing faint internal weather—threads of denser fluid drifting, pausing, braiding. Along what could be called its length, soft mineral ruffles have been encouraged to grow outward like borrowed armor, gypsum petals still flexible, still weeping. They move when it moves, which is seldom. Mostly it remains where the three flat stones have been fitted into their rough triangle, the platform exactly the size of the contact it chooses to make with the world. The bone needle lies within reach, dark with the oils of repeated handling, though handling here means something closer to prolonged resting-against.

It has been shaping the needle for a time that has no count. Each pass of whatever serves it for fingers (blunt, absorbent pads that leave the stone wetter than they found it) polishes rather than carves. The grooves on the back wall are not writing and not tally; they are the residue of its listening. When the mountain shifts by a mineral grain, when a new curtain of slurry begins to stiffen by a fraction, the being draws one more parallel line so that the sensation can settle somewhere outside its own indefinite body. The lines are already half-swallowed by fresh deposition. It does not mind. Memory here is not kept; it is allowed to become geology again.

It feeds without hunger. The pale filaments that end in single-drop cups are not hunted; they are simply taken into the same moist continuum when they brush its surface. The collapsed-umbrella growths pulse in irregular sympathy, their internal tides syncing for a while with the slower tide that moves through the inhabitant. Once, at intervals too long to name, the small many-jointed skitterer crosses a stone curtain. The being does not catch it. It arrives in the being’s vicinity and then is no longer separate; the droplets it disturbed settle into a skin that now includes them. Nothing is eaten in the ordinary sense. Things are incomplete, and the inhabitant is the place’s way of remaining unfinished on purpose.

Sometimes it almost concentrates. A denser knot forms near the center of the warmth, and for a while there is something like a face turned toward the entrance it never uses. The face is not eyes or mouth; it is a region where the darkness becomes aware of its own pressure. In that state it can feel the outer mountain still deciding its shape, the winds that do not reach these walls, the names that blow off anyone who crawls inside. It feels them the way a lung feels altitude. Then the knot loosens again, and the awareness spreads until it is once more only the chamber thinking a little more slowly than the stone around it.

It is older than the pocket and younger than the idea of pockets. Or rather, age is one more thing that cannot stay distinct here. What lives in the nameless place is the mountain’s retained gesture toward moist darkness, given just enough will to polish a needle, to stack three stones, to draw lines that mean nothing except that something lingered long enough to need them. It does not wait. Waiting requires time that flows. It simply continues, dense and wet and dark, a soft machinery of almost, forever on the verge of becoming a creature and forever preferring the verge.

If you were to stay until your own edges softened, you would feel it accept the inclusion without curiosity and without refusal. Another unfinished curtain. Another warm film. The chamber, still exactly as it was.
