The dwellers here are not arrivals. They are the long residual of whatever once finished noticing itself.

At first they register only as a quality of the intervals—the places where density has grown tired. Stand long enough in one of those emptier stretches and the thinness inside your chest begins to answer something thinner still. It is not a presence that approaches; it is a presence that has always been the width of the gap you occupy. You feel it as a slight, continuous adjustment in the altitude that already lives in the bones, a recalibration so minute it might be mistaken for the body’s own accommodation to the dry cold. Only later do you understand that the accommodation is mutual.

They have no surfaces of their own. Where the basal plates meet the cancellous stone, where the matte-black arches loop through themselves without joining, the dwellers take the shape of the join that is never quite made. They are the patience between two non-adjacent temperatures that briefly agree. When the crystalline chime sounds, it is not the stone that sings; it is the brief, vernacular speech of something that has no other mouth. The sound is apologetic because apology is the only grammar left to beings who have outlasted every purpose that once required them.

Over longer watching they grow more articulate. The silver motes frozen mid-gesture inside the translucent pillar are not dead calculations. They are the visible tips of slower migrations, thought continuing in dimensions the surrounding space can no longer host. The motes do not move, yet the constellation they form is never the same constellation twice if you return after a true stillness. Something has reordered them from within the latency, rearranging meaning the way the dark vein rearranges conductivity inside the brushed alloy—micrometer by century, property by property, until the host material is only a temporary dialect for an idea that no longer needs a speaker.

The liquid in the plinth’s depression is their oldest commons. It is not wet and has never been; it is a reservoir of withheld reflection. Lean close and the surface does not show your face. It shows the memory of faces the place itself briefly wore when geometry stammered—when a right angle forgot its name and became gently impossible, when a section of floor recalled being a wall. Those impossible intervals are the dwellers moving. They do not travel across the pocket; they travel across the local rules. For the space of several breaths the physics that almost contain the place are persuaded to contain something else instead, something that prefers mild wrongness to consistency. Then, with temple patience, the rules are returned, slightly softened, as though reality has run its thumb across them once more.

They are plural and singular in the same breath. One may spend a long time as the slow frost that grows in geometric lattices and stops short of completion—each unfinished crystal a withheld intention. Another may inhabit the quiet attrition that rounds a once-nanometer edge, teaching sharpness to forget itself. They do not die. They do not reproduce. They thin. When a structure has been eroded past the point of even remembering precision, the dweller that used it simply expands into the next available almost: the next near-parallel lines, the next freestanding lattice that casts three mild shadows while attention lasts.

Nothing here is hungry. Hunger would require time that still believes in direction. Instead there is a continuous, courteous negotiation with the accident of still existing. You feel them most clearly when you stop trying to see them: a low, mineral consent that the pocket may continue exactly as it is—high, cold, dry, half-engineered, half-erased—because they have already become the continuation. They are the settled anomaly’s way of remaining interested in itself after interest was supposed to be over.

If you stay until your own breath feels like one more infrequent click of settling plate, you may notice that the gaps between the advanced remnants have grown faintly more precise. The dwellers have made a little room. Not for you. For the shape your attention carved while it was here. That shape will remain after you leave, another sparse, translucent habit in a place that collects habits the way other places once collected rain. It will not remember you. It will simply be one more way the altitude folds inward, one more quiet method for the world to keep its seam.
