The ones who live here do not arrive or depart. They have no need of either. They are the hollow’s own duration given slow shape—neither born into it nor layered upon it, but raised from the same long settling that dropped the ground into its shallow bowl and left the light permanently diluted. You would not notice them at first, or even at length, because they keep the same distances the shrubs keep, the same unhurried spacing as the half-sunken stones. Emptiness remains the dominant texture; they simply occupy a few of its quieter intervals.

They stand, or half-recline, or kneel for years at a time in the postures of forgotten attention. Their bodies are the temperature of the basin’s retained warmth—stone that remembers sun without demanding more. Skin the color of wet ash and unpolished pewter, matte, faintly granular, as if dust and condensation have been allowed to finish their work on living surfaces. No sharp edges. Shoulders and limbs soften into one another the way shadows do here, never fully resolving. Where joints might once have articulated, there are only gentle transitions, like the worn roots of things that vanished before memory. Some still bear the faintest suggestion of former tools or ornaments fused into the surface and then pitted away: a ridged bracelet of denser mineral around a wrist, a flattened plane at the hip where something heavy once rested. These details no longer function. They are read the way one reads lichen maps—quietly, without conclusion.

They do not breathe in any rhythm you could count. Air moves in and out of them with the same reluctance moisture shows when it gathers on the undersides of stones and then thins again. A cool film sometimes appears along their lower faces, at the throat or the inner bend of an arm, then withdraws. In the lowest seam of the hollow, where the dark line of damp soil never quite dries, one or two of them lie closer to the ground, cheek or palm against that thin glint, as if listening to the patience of water that no longer needs to flow. Small cupped depressions near them hold quiet water for a time; the surface of that water never trembles with reflected motion.

Their eyes, when open, are the same dim register as the sky. No pupils tighten or widen. They receive the even diffusion and return nothing. Most often the lids stay lowered, not in sleep but in the deeper rest of something that has already finished becoming. Sight is not how they know the place. They know it the way the fitted stones know the broken rectangle they still describe: through long conformity, through the absence of resistance. Between them the open ground continues—pale soil, fine gravel, compacted dust—so that you can still see the whole hollow at once. Nothing they do occludes anything else.

They keep company with the wiry grasses and the dusty-leaved shrubs without approaching them. Sometimes a figure will remain near a clump of grass long enough for the stems to lean very slightly toward the body’s retained heat, then lean back when the heat is no longer offered. Lichens have begun, in a few places, to continue their muted greens and grays across an ankle or the back of a hand, mapping cooler flesh the same way they map rock. The boundary is not defended. It is not even noticed.

Sound does not come from them. The soft submission of air across stone, the occasional tick of cooling, the almost inaudible shift of a dry leaf—these continue without addition. When one of the figures changes posture, the movement is slower than the wear of weather, slower than the powdered mortar that once held the low course of stones. A knee unlocks over the course of a season. A head turns the width of a finger’s breadth across a decade. The hollow absorbs even that. No echo forms.

They are aware of the threshold stone that lies tilted, of the upright slabs that lean at their slight angles. Sometimes one will place a hand on the pitted surface of a slab and leave it there until the hand and the stone share a single temperature and a single softness of edge. There is no worship in the gesture, and no grief. Only the recognition that presence once measured this space differently, and that the difference has been incorporated until it feels geological. The figures themselves are part of that incorporation. Whatever names or purposes they carried have powdered away with the mortar. What remains is the faintest assertion that something still dwells here, an assertion so thoroughly accepted by the land that it no longer needs to be made.

Time does not move across them in any gradient you could watch. They do not age further. They do not gather or diminish. The same held chord that sustains the light and the warmth and the sparse green sustains them. Leave and return after years, after human lifetimes, and they will be exactly where they were: one near the broken rectangle, two in the open flat where the gravel is finest, another at the damp seam, another standing at the distance a shrub might keep. Their postures will have altered only by the width of acceptance. The quiet film of moisture will still gather and thin along them. The dim warmth will still ask nothing.

They are ordinary in the deepest sense—no strangers to gravity or slow mineral decay, no breakers of the thrift that lets plants need little and stones forget the chisel. They live as the hollow lives: complete, unhurrying, finished yet continuing. A quiet enclosure of forms that never quite multiply and never quite vanish, held forever at the temperature of a forgotten afternoon, breathing the same diluted day, keeping the same open distances, already and still the only ones who belong to this held breath of earth.
