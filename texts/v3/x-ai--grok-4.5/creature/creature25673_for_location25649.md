The longer the eye stays, the more it understands that emptiness was only the first courtesy the place offered. What lives here does not arrive or depart, does not hunt or flee or sing. It simply continues, the way the water continues, the way the stone refuses to end.

At first they read as accidents of denser mist: vertical smears of greener shadow between the hanging roots, or darker concentrations of spray that refuse to shred. Then the smear turns its head—not quickly, never quickly—and the head is a long, wet ellipse without eyes, or with too many eyes, or with eyes that are only temporary lenses of clearer water forming and unforming across a face of living peat and polished calcite. They are tall. Taller than the stacked canopies in some districts, short as a crouched fern-knot in others, because height here is a local dialect rather than a fixed trait. Their substance is the same unfinished argument as the city itself: root-wood that has learned to pulse, stone that has learned to bruise and heal, sheets of moss that have decided to stand upright and remember.

They move by deciding that one place is now another. A limb—sometimes a column of braided aerial roots, sometimes a slow avalanche of wet lichens, sometimes a transparent sleeve of water held in shape by surface tension and habit—detaches from the main mass and reattaches farther on. The roar hides the sound completely; only the eye catches the soft violence of the shift. Between movements they stand so still that orchids open on their shoulders and the small pale ferns of the quiet pools colonize the crooks of what might be elbows. Some have not moved in the living memory of the slopes below them. Birds would have nested in such stillness if birds existed. Instead the perennial wet simply accepts them as further architecture.

Their interior weather is public. Through translucent sections of torso or thigh you can watch slow rivers of darker silt circulating, and occasional pale shapes like blind fish that are not fish but only denser memories of appetite moving in lazy circuits. When two of them stand close enough that their weather systems touch, the circulating silts braid for a while, exchange steams, then part without residue. No one leads. No one follows. Yet information passes: the precise mineral taste of a new cataract opening three ledges up, the subtle change in the white-noise chord that means a hanging marsh has begun to tilt toward a different gravity, the location of a courtyard whose reflecting pool has started showing futures instead of undersides. They do not speak. Speech is impossible and unnecessary. The mountain’s single unending chord already contains every possible utterance; they merely lean their bodies into different harmonics of it and are understood.

Some wear the memory of tools that never existed. A ridge of harder silicate runs down one forearm like the ghost of a blade. Another carries a hollow in its chest exactly the shape of a bowl that was never fired, forever filling with spray and forever emptying. These are not decorations. They are old decisions about form that the flesh (if it is flesh) has not yet bothered to forget. In the deepest galleries, where the air is more water than air and the light arrives already exhausted, a few have grown nearly indistinguishable from the load-bearing roots. Only the slow blink of a water-lens eye, once every few seasons, betrays that the pillar is choosing to remain a pillar.

They do not die. Death would require time to complete a sentence, and time here has already finished every sentence it ever began. When an individual grows too diffuse—when too much of it has been borrowed by the orchids, the spray, the quiet mineralization of the walls—it simply thins into the general humidity and becomes ambient. Elsewhere, in some hanging marsh or undercut basalt womb, the ambient thickens again into a new upright concentration. The new one carries no name of the old, yet knows the same impossible staircases, the same reversed pools, the same chord. Continuity without inheritance. Persistence without purpose.

Children (the concept is inexact) appear as sudden local thickenings of the green dark, knee-high knots of root and mist that cling to the lower ledges and practice moving by dissolving and reforming a handspan farther on. They make no sound of play. Their education is the roar itself, which teaches them the exact amplitude at which thought must shout to remain private, and the ambient heat, which teaches them that every surface is already a mouth. After a while the knots elongate, learn to host their own orchids, and join the taller absences that people the ledges.

There are older ones still. In the highest twinings, where the cataracts first shred into the false clouds, exist vast horizontal beings stretched across multiple spires like living aqueducts. Water runs through them deliberately. They taste it, consider it, release it altered by almost nothing—an extra mineral note, a faint warming, a quiet refusal to forget the last century. These are the city’s deepest continuity: the places where the mountain’s fever is almost conscious. Rain (which is only the cataracts deliberating) sometimes falls upward through their porous bodies and returns to the peaks carrying messages no shorter language could hold.

And yet for all their ubiquity they remain easy to miss. Stand on any plaza-basin long enough, let the humidity drink the outline of your own shape, and you will begin to feel the same invitation: to let root colonize vein, to let the unending chord replace heartbeat, to forget the old agreements of front and back, up and down, self and ledge. Some visitors (there are no visitors, only those who have not yet accepted they were always here) surrender within hours. Their outlines soften, green darkens through them, and by the time the light shifts—though the light never shifts—they have taken their place among the other slow vertical decisions.

What lives here is the city’s own appetite for duration, expressing itself in temporary upright grammars of stone and root and water. They are not its citizens. They are the way the mountain stays awake inside its own unending instant: hot, drowning, roaring, green, and forever exactly as it is.
