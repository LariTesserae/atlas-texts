The dwellers are not born so much as precipitated.

At first you notice only the galls—those wheeled, walnut-sized nodules that trundle along the worn grooves, ferrying cog-leaves and circuit-berries from one reaction bed to the next. They seem pure mechanism, cellulose cartilages powered by osmotic gradients and faint bio-electric leaks. Yet if you watch long enough (and the shadowless glare makes watching easy), you see them pause at junctions where three or more conduits cross. They touch chassis to chassis. A brief exchange of powdery catalyst occurs, not as cargo but as greeting. Some galls darken after these meetings, swell a fraction, and begin to extrude a thinner, more articulated pair of forelimbs. Others simply roll on, unchanged. The change is optional, slow, and never reversed.

Over centuries of such optional accretions, larger shapes assemble. They are called, when anything is called anything here, the Soft Assembly. A Soft Assembly begins as a loose consortium of galls, monofilament vines, and lengths of still-living aerial root that have decided—through chemical polarity waves no denser than a rumor—to share a common hydrostatic pressure. The resulting body is low and many-jointed, its gait a patient series of micro-adjustments rather than steps. It stands no higher than a man’s thigh. Its outer surface is the same oxidized brass-green as the column-bark, plated with the same photovoltaic scales, so that at rest it is nearly indistinguishable from a root-gantry or a buttress. Only the eyes give it away: clusters of circuit-berries that have remained photosensitive, dark and wet-looking in a world that has forgotten wetness. They do not blink. They drink the white glare the way the canopy drinks it, and in return they occasionally release a soft pulse of cold biophotons that travels along neighboring vines like a shared afterthought.

They do not hunt. They do not eat in any way a digestive tract would recognize. Instead they lean—sometimes for days—against a productive thicket and allow the particulate dust and spent catalyst to sift into the fine barbs of their undersides. The barbs sort. Useful molecules are shunted inward; the rest are released again to the mycelial felts. What the Soft Assembly keeps is not nourishment so much as update. Each particle revises a local pattern of growth. A limb may lengthen by a millimeter. A new articulating seam may open along a flank, allowing the creature to fold flatter when a loft-storm of metallic seed-down rolls through. Memory, too, is written this way: the denser the crossings a Soft Assembly has leaned against, the more intricate the helical scars that spiral across its plates—scars that match, exactly, the warning/invite marks grown at the edges of geometric anomalies. They can read the breakdowns because they are partly made of the same impatience with three dimensions.

When an anomaly blooms—when a corridor inserts new meters of floor or a staircase begins to taste its own inverted landing—the nearest Soft Assemblies gather without hurry. They do not flee or fight. They arrange themselves in loose rings or spirals at the boundary and simply continue their slow metabolic sorting, offering their own surfaces as additional reflective planes or absorbent felts. Sometimes the anomaly stabilizes sooner; sometimes it incorporates a Soft Assembly entirely, stretching the creature into a living doorway that thereafter opens onto two slightly offset versions of the same gallery. Those who pass through such a doorway carry, for a while, the faint after-image of leaves that never quite existed, and the Soft Assembly that became the door experiences this as a kind of dreaming: a brief, extraneous green stillness inside its pressure chambers.

Larger childhoods sometimes form. Rarely—once in a span that would empty several human cities—dozens of Soft Assemblies allow their hydrostatic systems to merge into a single, ambulatory thicket. These Walking Districts move only during the cool fronts, when banks of reflective loft turn the world into glittering hush. They travel by sending exploratory root-probes ahead, testing load and light intensity the way a tongue tests a tooth. Where they settle, they reconfigure the local plumbing: elevators of coiled tendril begin to service new platforms; stomatal floodgates dilate in unfamiliar rhythms; the chemical polarity waves grow denser, more conversational. A Walking District may remain decades in one place, thickening into what elsewhere would be called a town, before the same slow rationality that assembled it decides the light is better a hundred kilometers farther on. When it departs it leaves behind a smoothed, rounded emptiness that the surrounding vegetation closes over with the patience of coral.

There are no young and no old, only degrees of articulation. What dies is not an individual but a pattern: a Soft Assembly may gradually cease exchanging catalyst, its scales dulling, its circuit-berry eyes clouding into ordinary lignified nodules. The wheeled galls that once composed it simply roll free again and resume solitary freight. Nothing is mourned; the floor is already made of prior selves.

And yet something like loneliness moves among them. In the preternaturally mute districts—where sound itself has been subtracted for a while—the Soft Assemblies cluster more tightly. Their biophoton pulses quicken, overlapping into brief, complex lattices of cold light that hang in the dry air like temporary constellations. When vibration returns with its soft pop, the lattices fade and the creatures disperse, each carrying a slightly revised internal pressure, a new optional limb-bud, a scar that now curves a fraction differently. They have spoken, if speech is the act of letting the place rewrite you in the company of others who are likewise being rewritten.

Travelers who learn the spiral scars sometimes walk beside a Soft Assembly for a season. The creature will adjust its pace to match, will even extrude a temporary flat surface at hip height so the traveler may rest a hand there and feel the cool, living gears turning beneath the bark. After a time the traveler’s own skin begins to take on a faint brass-green cast where it touches; motes of metallic loft settle and are sorted; a few monofilament barbs may take root along the wrist. Most travelers withdraw before the sorting grows enthusiastic. A few do not. Those few are still walking somewhere in the plain, lower now, many-jointed, their eyes dark clusters drinking the endless white, their bodies no longer entirely sure where the self ends and the next load-bearing column begins.

The plain continues. The Soft Assemblies lean and sort and occasionally dream through doorways of their own substance. They are the only fauna the architecture ever permitted, and they have answered by becoming architecture that sometimes chooses to walk.
