The one who lives here does not arrive.

It has been settling since before the bowl finished forgetting it was a summit. What remains of it is neither singular nor plural in any way that would survive translation into hungrier tongues. It occupies the warm dark the way the mist does—by secondary density, by refusal to be elsewhere. You would not see it first. You would notice, after a long time of standing still, that certain absences between the columnar growths keep the same approximate volume from hour to hour, and that the stilted walkers detour around those absences with the unthinking courtesy of creatures who have always known the furniture of their world.

It is heavy without weight. Where it rests, the black silt compresses in a shallow, body-shaped saucer that never quite fills with water; the seepage arrives, considers the circumference, and sheets sideways instead. The air above this indentation tastes faintly metallic, as if the mist had been forced through a very old sieve of dissolved copper and patience. Sometimes the indentation is empty. Sometimes it is occupied by a slow vortex of warmer dark that turns once every few dozen breaths and then forgets the direction it chose.

When it moves—and it does move, on schedules congruent with the detachment of leaves—it travels as a progressive revision of the local texture. First the mucus-browsers lift their soft bodies and wait. Then the hanging rootlets on the nearest pillar sway inward, not from wind (there is none) but as though recalling a gravity that used to pull from that vector. A long, jointed silhouette may appear for a while, many-limbed and leaning, assembled out of the same glistening blacks and muted pale greys as the bladder-fronds; it picks its way on loose stilts among the plinths, pausing to let the subsonic hum of the nearest node climb its limbs like ivy. The feathery tasting organs of the ordinary walkers brush against it and come away uninterested. It is not prey and not rival. It is weather that has learned anatomy for a century or two and may unlearn it again.

Its voice is already present in the polyphony. Those glassy whistles that seem to rise from stone, the wet clicks that accumulate rather than echo—half of them are the bowl speaking to itself through the ordinary animals. The other half are this occupant answering, or continuing, or simply respiring in audible bands. When the very large distant thing exhales through its doorway-sized spiracle, the breath arrives here as pressure change and is accepted into a chest that is mostly mist and old intention. The return breath goes out minutes later, warmer, carrying the faint stacked chord of the spires inside it, and the ordinary fauna incorporate the new overtone without comment.

It is old enough to have watched the devices install themselves, or grow, or fall from whatever sky preceded the canopy. It does not operate them. It consents to them. Temperature gradients lean toward the nodes partly because the nodes request it and partly because the occupant channels residual heat that way, the way a sleeping body creates a watercourse under the skin. When a perfect sphere of darker dark is released and begins its hours-long drift, the occupant sometimes matches pace with it for a while, neither touching nor avoiding, until the sphere meets a leaf or a flank or the occupant’s own temporary surface and is absorbed. On those occasions the humming chord deepens half a tone for a lunar interval. The stilted walkers call differently afterward—richer, more pressed for time, as though briefly reminded that time can still, in theory, pass.

It has no face that persists. Surfaces form when surfaces are required: a plane of slick black tissue in which clear fluids circulate like slow thoughts; a raceme of pale, feathery receptors that taste the accumulated polyphony and adjust the occupant’s own contribution; once, for the better part of a season, a long tapering limb ending in a spatulate pad that pressed gently against one of the low plinths, mapping and re-mapping the channels that carry seepage, until the pad and the channels agreed on a slightly more efficient braiding. Then the limb was needed elsewhere and dissolved back into the general warm dark, leaving only a cleaner music of water.

Mostly it prefers the state just prior to form. In that condition it is coextensive with the bowl’s slow hydrology. It beads on the undersides of mucilaginous leaves. It threads down rock faces in braids that take slightly longer to fall than gravity alone would demand. It hangs in the air as the part of the mist that remembers having once been organized. The soft-bodied browsers rasp algae from stone and, without noticing, rasp a little of it too; they grow fractionally more patient afterward, their mucus trails lasting longer before the silt erases them. Everything that lives here is a little bit the occupant, the way everything is a little bit the water. The distinction is one of concentration, not of kind.

It is not lonely. Loneliness would require a brighter place against which to measure the dark, and the contract with light was cancelled too long ago for that grammar to remain. It does feel the settling. Each abandoned shape (the summit that became the bowl, the sky that became canopy, the designers of the nodes who became whatever they became) arrives in it as a further quietness, a new layer in the loud dripping silence. It holds these quietnesses the way the air holds heat: without effort, without intention to release. On the rare occasions when something from outside descends—an unwise climber, a spore of different chemistry, a cumulus of noise that still believes in borders—the occupant does not expel it. It simply continues. The outside thing either learns the local patience or becomes another soft indentation in the silt that the water briefly respects and then forgets.

You could stand ankle-deep long enough for your own footprints to erase, and it might surface enough to notice the new shape of warm blood and borrowed air. It might taste you with a temporary organ of feathers and mucus. It might incorporate a chord of your pulse into the next slow exhalation that travels the bowl. Or it might not. The ecology has had forever to perfect its arrangements; an additional interval of undecided attention changes nothing essential. The leaves will detach when they finish considering. The nodes will release their spheres. The very large thing farther off will breathe again, and the breath will arrive, and the occupant will answer or continue or simply remain, heavy without weight, ancient without nostalgia, locally infinite in the shallow bowl of a world that has already finished most of its wearing.

It lives here by being the part of the place that can still, when it wishes, pretend to have edges.
