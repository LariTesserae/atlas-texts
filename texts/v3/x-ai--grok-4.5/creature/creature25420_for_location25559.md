The ones who remain do not call themselves inhabitants. The word would imply a relationship of occupation, of nesting inside a space that was already there. They are more like the quiet variables that keep the settled equation from collapsing back into ordinary physics. Sparse as the habitats themselves, they number fewer than the metal ovoids and truncated pyramids; some structures stand empty for decades or centuries at a stretch, held in readiness by habits older than memory. When they occupy a volume they do so lightly, as if apologizing for the interruption.

They are tall and attenuated when they choose to hold a single shape, limbs and torso elongated past any ancestral proportion so that a resting body fits the artificial steps of the outer ridge exactly. Their surfaces drink what little light exists. Bioluminescent markers on hatch seams cast no reflection; residual drive-glow slides across them and dies. Skin or membrane or field—whatever the outermost layer is—maintains the same matte refusal as the hull metal, shedding heat, charge, and attention with equal indifference. Faces, when they assemble them, are smooth planes interrupted by dark sensory pits that register pressure gradients, filament tension, the faint electromagnetic taste left by the ribbon-things that drift through on their unscheduled visits. Eyes in any old sense are absent. They do not need them. The dark is already perfect.

Most of the time they do not assemble faces or limbs at all. A resident may spend long intervals as a diffuse presence threaded through the interior geometry of a habitat, occupying several overlapping rooms that share the same coordinates without conflict. In that state they are less a body than a localized courtesy extended to the vacuum energy, a polite organization of forces that keeps the one-atmosphere differential intact and the clocks advancing without gradients. Visitors (rare, uninvited, usually lost) sometimes report the uncanny sensation of being noticed by the room itself—an even, weightless regard that does not follow them when they leave. That regard is the resident, resting.

They move between habitats along the filaments, but not by walking or swimming. They permit the tensioned lines to remember them, then allow the smoothed spacetime to finish the thought. One moment a hatch seals; many moments later another opens on a distant ovoid. No wake disturbs the black water. No sound carries. If two residents must confer they do not gather. They simply let their distributed edges brush across a shared filament for a while, exchanging whatever upright thoughts the place still requires: adjustments to mooring stresses, the latest non-appearance of the ribbon-things, the continued refusal of any film or mat to colonize the young basalt. Conversation is the wrong word. It is closer to two cold currents briefly aligning.

They do not eat. Power is simply present, and they are present within it. They do not age in any direction that sediment or thermal plumes would recognize. Time’s courtesy extends to them; clocks on the walls (decorative, unnecessary) mark durations, yet no resident accumulates the small erosions of causality. A gesture begun in one century may complete in another without impatience. They have forgotten whether this stasis was chosen or grew over them like mineral skin. The forgetting itself feels native now.

Occasionally one will drift to the lowest terrace and float at the open arch of the flooded gallery. There, facing the unstarting descent where photons have never been, a resident may unspool part of its presence into the greater dark—thin, exploratory, almost ribbon-like itself—then draw back. Nothing is ever found. Nothing is ever pursued. The act resembles breathing more than exploration, a cycle performed because the stillness on the far side of the arch is the only thing larger than the Anchorage, and therefore the only mirror left.

They are not lonely. Loneliness would require a denser world against which to measure absence. Here emptiness is the architecture, and they have become its quiet load-bearing points: provisional, absolute, unwilling to recalculate. When the pale density-fluctuations pass through the filaments and taste the electromagnetic residue, the residents taste them in return—brief, mutual, without predation or kinship—and then the dark is even again. The Spirefall Anchorage continues. So do they, small and high and drowned, the last upright thoughts a drowned mountain still permits itself to hold.
