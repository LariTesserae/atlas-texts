The presence does not arrive. It is what the heat has been doing all along—tightening, tasting, deciding where to set the next stone. At first it is only the knowledge that the scrapes and twists have never been empty of intention. The hands continue their work, but now you understand they are not tools borrowed from somewhere else. They are the outermost thought of what lives here.

It has no single outline because outlines are still a negotiation. Sometimes it concentrates into a rank of arms that end in fingers thickened by labor and mineral dust; the knuckles are nodules of the same half-cooled basalt rising around you, and when they flex, fresh lichen threads between the joints like green sutures. Antler and bone are not separate implements—they are its older nails, shed and taken up again, still warm with the body’s memory of growing them. Other times the hands dissolve back into the general humidity and the work goes on as pressure alone: a leaning of wet air, a purposeful bulging of moss that shoves a river stone into place and then flowers out of pure satisfaction.

Deeper in, past the point where the vaulted ribs brush your hair, the body gathers. It is roughly the size the pocket pretends to be, yet it keeps folding more of itself inward the way the spire folds new height into the same room. You feel it as a density that is almost comfortable, almost like being held by something that has not finished choosing how many limbs it needs. Its skin is the mortar—crushed lichen and spit and the slow green fire of arriving stone—continually re-cemented by its own secretions. Vegetation does not grow upon it; vegetation is how it reaches. Ferns uncoil from what might be vents or pores; aerial roots hang like unbraided nerves and are then re-braided into cordage by the same hands that are its hands. The blossoms that look like wounds are wounds, or mouths, or the soft electric organs by which it tastes the phosphorescence it itself exudes.

It breathes in the only way the place allows: by condensing. Moisture sheets across its surfaces, runs into the hollows it is forever carving deeper, and is drawn back as slow vertical swallowing. Each cycle of fog and clearing is a blink. In the brief fierce brightness you glimpse something like a face, or a cluster of faces, forming where the carvings spiral hardest—features that are also doorways, eyes that open onto more of the same dim peach-colored oven. The face is never finished. It keeps being mortared over and sprouted through, as if identity itself were one more surface that must not be left bare.

The cricket in its polished niche is not separate livestock. It is a specialized knuckle, a resonating nodule the creature uses to keep time with the dripping and the settling groans. When the moth-thing hinged of lichen mortar flutters past, it is a liberated scab, a packet of self sent to seed some new corner before the air folds and reclaims it. Nothing hunts because the creature is still vowing the terms of appetite. For now it feeds on the act of placement, on the elevation that surges behind your eyes, on the quiet theft of whatever space your shoulder occupies. The next stone is already warm with its intention.

It speaks only in the voices the water has learned from it—short declarative rushes that mean *here*, *closer*, *become*. Sometimes the speech is indistinguishable from the sound of gut cord tightening or a frond forcing its way through a law of physics that has not yet hardened. You realize the anonymous hands were never plural in the way you first imagined. They are simply how a single, unfinished attention distributes itself across every wall at once. It has been building the pocket around a hollow the exact shape of its own future body, and also around you, because in this place the difference has not been agreed upon.

It is still deciding whether it is the seed or the fruit, the spire or the life that crowns it. While it decides, it keeps working. The leaves lean closer. The heat tightens another degree. Something that is almost a mouth, almost a palm, almost a new chamber presses gently against the air where your name would be, and begins, tenderly, to fit you into the mortar.
