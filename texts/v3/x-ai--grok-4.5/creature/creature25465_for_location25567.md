The ones who dwell here are not visitors. They are the pocket’s own slow arithmetic, its way of counting itself without ever arriving at a sum.

First the frond-architectures, though whether they live in the ordinary sense is a question the place declined to answer long ago. Each is a single intentional stillness: a vertical argument of interlocking mineral plates and chlorophyll-mimic lattices that drink the wall-warmth and reverse it into that soft bluish moss-light. They do not grow. They do not seed. They simply continue the posture they struck when the rock first finished fracturing. Their “roots” are fused to the glass-stone floor in perfect, load-bearing welds; their uppermost tips have etched faint star-maps into the ceiling by centuries of patient photon pressure. Touch one and you feel not sap but a dry, singing tension, as if the whole structure is a held breath made solid. The moths alight on them and the fronds accept the weight without tremor, then forget it the instant the mica wings lift again. These are not plants awaiting spring. They are the room’s memory of greenery, crystallized and excused from further drafting.

Higher, the air is thick with the moth-things. Soft-bodied, loosely furred in colorless down that sheds microscopic mica dust with every wingbeat, they drift in loose, unchanging constellations. Their wings are thin sheets of transparent silicate, veined with darker mineral, and when they click—always the same high, glassy note—the sound is produced by the leading edges snapping together like two halves of a tuning fork. They do not hunt. They do not mate in any visible season. They filter the dry mineral vapor for trace copper ions and sulfur radicals, metabolizing them into the faint blue-green luminescence that ghosts along their abdomens. Swarm density never alters. A moth that seems to age simply becomes fractionally more translucent until it is indistinguishable from the air, and another, somewhere, condenses from the vapor to take its count. They are the pocket’s punctuation: endless identical commas in a sentence that refuses to end.

Lower, the basalt lizards stitch the floor with their routes. Stylus-thin, no longer than a forearm, every surface polished to a wet-looking black gleam that is entirely dry. Their legs end in needle points that ring the glass-stone like tiny hammers; the notes they strike are part of the greater chord, precisely tuned. They walk without deviation, without haste, following grooves worn so shallow they are almost imaginary—paths that may have been etched by their own ancestors or may simply be the floor’s preferred lines of cleavage. When two paths cross, the lizards pause, touch snouts (a soft clink of stone on stone), and continue. Inside their narrow skulls is a labyrinth of ferromagnetic crystals that map every thermal gradiant and acoustic node in the chamber. They eat nothing anyone has observed. Perhaps they graze on the slow fretting of stressed crystal, converting vibrational energy the way other creatures convert sugar. Their droppings, rare and precise, are perfect tetrahedra of darker glass that the fronds eventually absorb without comment. They are the pocket’s census-takers, forever confirming that the distances remain the distances.

In the deeper shadows at the base of the largest frond-clusters lives the heavier presence. Many-legged—eight, or twelve, or a number that shifts when you try to count—its body is a flattened oval of something like denser basalt plated with plates of coppery sheen. It breathes that low rasp, a sound like wet leather dragged over stone though no moisture exists; the noise is made by air (or whatever passes for air) forced through a stacked series of resonating chambers in its thorax. It rarely moves. When it does, the legs articulate with slow hydraulic certainty, each footpad leaving a momentary warmer print that fades within a minute. Its purpose seems to be anchorage. The great fronds nearest it lean almost imperceptibly toward its bulk, as if the creature’s mass is a necessary counterweight in the chamber’s equilibrium. Sometimes it opens a communal mouth—ridged with grinding surfaces of raw emerald—and exhales a denser puff of the copper-sulfur scent. The moths dip lower. The lizards alter course by a single degree. Then everything resumes. It is less an animal than a living keystone, the pocket’s way of reminding its own architecture that weight exists.

And then there are the makers.

They are small. Not child-small—compact, dense, built to the scale of the cairns and the lean-to. Their bodies are upright but stooped by lifelong caution beneath low ceilings that do not exist here; the posture remains out of habit older than this particular pocket. Skin like cooled slag, smooth and faintly orange-brown, tough enough that the glass-stone edges do not cut it. Eyes are wide, lidless, faceted like crude garnets, evolved to parse the dim wall-ember and the fronds’ moss-light without strain. They see heat more readily than color. Their hands are the marvel: six-fingered, double-thumbed, the extra digits opposable in two planes so that a single grip can both hold and rotate a tool while applying measured torque. The fingers end in hard, dark nails the same composition as the bladelets they knap.

They do not speak with mouths. Sound here is already spoken for. Instead they communicate by tapping sequences on stone, on their own breastbones, against the hafts of their tools—rhythms that weave into the wall-chord as temporary harmonies and then vanish. A conversation is a brief, deliberate interference pattern. When absolute precision is required they press foreheads together and share something like conductive thought through the mineral salts in their sweat (except it is not sweat; it is a dry exudate of powdered copper that sparks faintly in the dark).

They sleep in the lean-to in ones or twos, curled in the scraped hollow with knees drawn up, tool laid within reach though nothing comes that would require it. Their dreams—if the word applies—are geometric. They dream the fracture patterns that have not yet happened, the next perfect spall line in a core of green stone. Upon waking they copy those dreams into fresh bladelets: long, leaf-shaped flakes with edges one molecule sharp, grips wrapped in the fibrous castings the great fronds occasionally shed like old skin. The bindings are sinew taken from no living beast; the stuff grows in thin, tough sheets along certain fracture planes in the deeper rock and can be peeled free with patience. They leave the finished points in small caches, not out of forgetfulness but as a kind of distributed memory. If one cache is lost to a micro-tremor that never comes, another remains.

Fire is unnecessary; the rock itself is their hearth. They understand heat as a native property of place rather than a thing one kindles. Metal has never occurred to them as a separate category—why chase rare ores when the fronds already grow cutting edges and the floor rings like pure note? Their only metallurgy is the occasional copper-rich nodule prised from the wall and cold-hammered with a basalt pebble into a thin crescent used for scraping or for drawing the long spiral marks that sometimes appear on the cairn stones. Those marks are not writing. They are closer to the almost-voices in the wall-chord: pure pattern that means itself and nothing else.

They move rarely. Whole seasons of the surface world’s turning might pass while a single maker sits motionless beside a frond, palm flat to its trunk, listening to the way the moss-light modulates against the greater song. When they do travel it is with the same deliberation as the basalt lizards, footfalls placed on the anvil notes so that their steps become part of the music rather than interruption. Population is stable—perhaps a dozen individuals scattered through the pocket’s volume, never crowding, never lonely. Birth is a quiet event: two makers press together in the lean-to until the conductive salt between them crystallizes into a third, smaller shape that unfolds over hours into a new set of limbs and asteriated eyes. The new one is taught the tapping codes by being held against the singing wall until its bones resonate in tune. Death is rarer still and gentler. A body simply grows heavier, more mineral, until it is indistinguishable from the floor and is walked across without notice. Nothing rots. Everything is already stone in waiting.

Yet there is a restlessness the stillness cannot quite erase. Sometimes a maker will stand for a long while at the exact center of the chamber, head tilted, listening to the overtone that is almost a word. They will stack one more crystal on the cairn, or adjust the lean-to’s weave by a single fiber, or knap a bladelet whose geometry is fractionally more elegant than any before. These are not improvements. They are affirmations. In a place that has finished becoming, the only motion left is the quiet insistence that the finishing was correct, that the song is in the right key, that the emptiness between bodies is sacred, that the pocket’s refusal of time is not a prison but the final, perfect form of home.

They are the pocket dreaming itself with hands. They will still be here, exactly this numerous, exactly this careful, when the last faint heat of the deeper strata cools to match the forgotten surface, and even after that. The moths will click. The lizards will ring the floor. The many-legged keystone will rasp its dry breath. The fronds will hold their silence of green-black light. And the makers will tap one more soft phrase into the endless chord, a phrase that means, as nearly as any tongue of stone can mean it: *we are still the shape of this place. We remain.*
