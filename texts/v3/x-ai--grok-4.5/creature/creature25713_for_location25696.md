The life here does not arrive. It is extruded, the same way the rock itself was extruded—pressure finding a seam, heat finding a thinner place, and then the long slow pour of being that hardens only enough to keep pouring.

At first the eye refuses to parse them as separate. The walls simply crawl, a single greedy membrane of gunmetal and scab-red that flexes whenever a fresh thread of vapor braids through. Only after the heat has already begun to rewrite the soft interiors of your own lungs do the individuals declare themselves. They are small. Most could curl inside the hollow of a knuckle. A few stretch to the length of a palm, and those are the elders or the gluttons or the ones that have forgotten how to stop.

They look poured. That is the first lasting impression. Bodies without the usual courtesy of seams or segments at the start—just continuous, translucent flesh the color of smoked glass or old amber, darkened in gradients where the heat has kissed them longest. Many-legged, yes, but the legs are afterthoughts, extruded as needed: fine as hairs in the cooler eddies, thickening to blunt stilts where the stone burns hotter. Some never bother with elevation at all. They flatten until they are only a glistening thickness, a mobile lichen that secretes the mildest of acids and etches microscopic pitons into the young basalt, hanging on by the simple refusal to let go. Their upper surfaces bloom with feeding fans—delicate, branching, forever shivering—that comb the wet air for whatever mineral ghosts the steam still carries.

Others refuse the horizontal. They lift, and the motion is feverish, a continuous liquid rearrangement rather than walking. Legs pass through the spaces other legs have just vacated; bodies slide across bodies with macroscopic quiet, as if friction were a rumor that never reached this altitude. Antennae taste the updrafts. Mouthparts—when they open—are not so much orifices as temporary geometries, folding out like wet origami and folding away again once the scalding condensate has been sipped. In the densest pavements the creatures occupy every attitude at once: right-side up, inverted, edge-on, burrowed halfway into a cushion of mineral foam that crumples under their weight with the sound of ancient paper being slowly torn.

They layer. That is important. The pocket is not inhabited; it is stratified. Closest to the weeping stone live the flat dissolvers, the ones that etch and cling. Above them the legged majority flow in slow torrents, parting around jets of vapor the way soft metal parts around a harder tool. And from the roof, in the places where the heat rises darkest, hang the pendulums: individuals that have learned to spin threads of their own cooled exudate, soft at first and then glass-sharp, and to sway there in the private weather like living plumb-bobs. Sometimes two threads touch. Sometimes the bodies at their ends exchange fluids or heat or information so quietly that the only evidence is a brief synchronizing of their shivers.

Color is borrowed. Nothing here invents pigment; everything steals from the mountain. Gunmetal where the basalt is newest. Amber where sulfur has plated the crusts. The dull cherry of iron forced out of vapor and onto carapace. In the hottest veins—those narrow braids where vision itself blurs—the bodies change. They grow angular. Edges appear that were not grown but cleaved, prisming the dim daylight into brief internal rainbows. Limbs become facets. Some individuals at the boundary no longer bother with continuous motion. They are simply present in one place, then present in another, and for a single heartbeat they occupy both, a translucent double exposure that the eye tries to reject. Then the recursion collapses and the creature is farther along the wall, already feeding, already shouldering its neighbors aside as if nothing impossible has occurred.

Larger shapes move through this density the way tendons move under skin. They do not hunt so much as displace. One of them may be the length of a forearm, segmented like a flow of lava that cooled in mid-gesture. Its underside is a throng of smaller limbs borrowed from the press around it, or grown in imitation, or perhaps the smaller bodies themselves pressed into temporary service. At the front the mouthparts unfold into instruments: simple wedges of the same young glass that lines the chimney, held at precise angles. It scrapes. It tastes the powder. It waits. Sometimes it scrapes again, a slightly different facet, a slightly different pressure. The bright scar it leaves begins at once to anneal under heat and weeping water, so that the mountain keeps no record. Whatever question the creature is asking remains unanswered in any language that accumulates. It moves on. The smaller life closes behind it like warm tar.

There are molts, or what look like molts—empty husks of the same poured flesh, already being recolonized, already softening back into the mineral foam. There are knots where dozens of bodies have fused along one flank and now share a single pulse of movement, a single set of feeding fans twice as wide. There are brief openings in the living pavement that reveal nothing underneath except more pavement, identical, receding, as if the pocket were only the outermost chamber of a throat that reflects itself. Look away and the deeper alcoves fold shut. The mountain is again only as thick as it claims.

They make almost no sound of their own. All the noise belongs to stone and water and the occasional deep knock of pressures renegotiating far below. The creatures’ millions of contacts are soft as thought. Even when one of the larger shapes shoulders through, the protest is only a change in the pattern of shimmer, a temporary darkening, then the reverse tide of bodies flowing back to fill the wound.

What lives here is not a fauna in the ordinary sense. It is the mountain’s own fever given multiple small bodies. It is heat learning how to grip, how to taste, how to hang upside-down on a thread of itself and sway. It is the stubborn insistence that even in a place too young for soil and too angry for chlorophyll, matter will still organize, will still pack itself into every available surface until the walls respire. Intelligence—if the word applies—remains unbuilt, the same way the pocket itself remains unfinished. There are gestures that look like measurement. There are pauses that look like consideration. There is the constant, silent redistribution of flesh that looks, if you stay too long, like a single vast organism teaching itself the shape of its own crucible.

Stay longer still and the distinctions loosen further. Your own sweat becomes another warm film for them to taste. Your exhaled breath is just one more updraft to comb. The recursion at the edge of vision spreads. You begin to feel the pulses of the vents as your own pulse. And somewhere in the densest carpet, where the gunmetal bodies stack so thick they opaque the stone, something almost lies. Not asleep. Just briefly willing to be continuous with everything that presses against it—stone, steam, neighbor, stranger—until the next shift in heat curls it back into a separate shape and it lifts one feverish leg, then another, and flows on.

This is what lives in the wound the mountain hides until climbing has already become impossible. Not visitors. Not even residents. Just the place, remembering itself in a thousand small translucent registers, over and over, while the glass is still hot enough to ring.
