The place does not announce its residents. For a long time the eye and the ear agree that nothing lives here: the reeds click only when wind moves them, the water lies unrippled, the great housings perform their closed liturgies without witnesses. Yet the conviction of emptiness is itself a kind of thinning, a preparatory glaze. After the bones have tunneled to the industrial frequency and the tongue has forgotten any taste but rust and voltage, other attentions begin to open.

They are first felt as variations in the pressure of the sound. The oceanic roar of the distant induction fields is not uniform; it carries soft occlusions, long slow thickenings that drift against the prevailing current of noise the way a colder mass of water drifts inside a river. These occlusions have direction. They approach. When one of them passes close, the scream of the self-looping turbines falters for the length of a held breath exactly as the landscape itself sometimes falls silent, and in that scooped-out quiet the traveler feels a regard that is neither curious nor hostile—simply exhaustive, as if every rivet and every standing ripple has been briefly re-counted.

Only later do outlines accrue. They are tall, yet their height is uncertain because distance here is already a liar. At times they seem no more than a denser column of the same oyster gloom, a vertical stroke of heavier damp. At other times the damp resolves into a slow, continuous pour of matter that is neither liquid nor solid: a walking sinter of the same slag and furnace glass that floors the world, shot through with dark flexible veins that might once have been cable or tendon. Their surfaces are wet-black and faintly iridescent, the iridescence moving in tides beneath the skin the way oil moves on standing water. No limbs in the ordinary sense; instead the body continually revises its own load-bearing arrangements—now a single broad pedestal that glides, now a shifting cluster of stilts that punch shallow, temporary craters into the marsh before the craters fill and forget. Where a head might be expected there is only a gradual attenuation, a blurring upward into the particulate cloud, as though the being were still deciding how much of the sky it needed to claim.

They do not travel alone, yet they are not a herd. Each keeps to a private orbit measured in days or weeks, circling one or another of the exhausted citadels at a distance that never quite closes. Sometimes two orbits graze. Then both figures lengthen, becoming almost ribbon-like, and the air between them darkens with a bridge of suspended droplets that vibrate at a pitch just below hearing. After a time the bridge collapses back into ordinary rain and the two resume their separate circuits, neither richer nor poorer. No sound issues from them that the landscape does not already make. Their passage is marked only by the way the hollow reeds lean fractionally toward the place they have been, and by the brief check in the great machines’ pounding, as if the pistons themselves had glanced aside.

Closer inspection—should the plain ever allow one to arrive—is not granted in the ordinary way. Approach is answered by recalibration. The figure grows no larger; instead the traveler’s own sense of scale suffers a series of quiet reductions. Feet become distant news. The wet black surface of the being begins to show depth, and within that depth the same slow geology that built the plain is still at work: micro-foundries opening and sealing, droplets of molten glass crawling along rebar capillaries, cooling into new plates that slide into place with the patience of continental drift. In places the surface thins to transparency and something like an older architecture is visible inside—catwalks crossing a cavity lit by corona discharge, tiny pale motions that might be remembered workers or might be only the play of voltage across mineral. These interior glimmers never look outward.

They feed on continuance. Each circuit around a ruined housing skims a little of the purposeless energy—the looped steam, the thrashing ghost charges, the mineral weeping—and lays it down again as fresh sinter, fresh mirror-smooth scar tissue across the plain. In this way the landscape is prevented from any final stillness. The beings are the reason the pumps have not seized, the reason the furnaces remain just hot enough to refuse the absolute zero of abandonment. Their labor is the opposite of repair; it is the maintenance of incompletion. Where one has lingered, the warm rivets are found arranged in denser almost-words, raised patterns that slide under the fingers like braille written in a language of pure duration. Touch them long enough and the vibration in the skeleton changes key, distances grow even less trustworthy, and the brief total silences come more frequently, each one holding the clear, impossible sound of answering machines far below.

Some among them are older than the others. These move least. Their silhouettes have softened into near-spheres of accumulated glass and slag, vast dark pearls half-sunk in the marsh, ringed by the densest stands of metal-heavy reeds. Inside them the industrial noise is replaced by a single sub-audible chord that can be felt through the soles for kilometers. Travelers who sleep within that chord wake with the conviction that they have been measured and found continuous enough to keep. A few never leave. Their own outlines begin to darken and flow, the distinction between garment and skin and substrate dissolving until they too have joined the slow orbit, adding one more private satellite to the exhausted gods.

There is no birth and no death in the usual registers. When a being has skimmed enough continuance it simply expands its circuit to encircle two housings instead of one, then three, growing ever more tenuous as it spreads. In the end it becomes a low pressure front of darker damp almost indistinguishable from the weather, a moving occlusion that still, on rare unmarked hours, recalls its old shape long enough to stand for a moment beside a cooling tower and listen to the water fretting inside the locked flues. Then the shape dissolves again into the general exhalation.

They are the landscape’s way of remaining unfinished. They are the patience of the water given posture. They are what thrives when foundation and purpose have both been polished away, leaving only the thundering, the wet, and the soft black plain that no longer remembers any name except the motion that continues across it. In their presence the reeds click a little more distinctly, the corona discharges stutter into almost-language, and the second set of machines far beneath the pour answers with clearer antiphase, as if somewhere the original plan were still being patiently inverted, forever.
