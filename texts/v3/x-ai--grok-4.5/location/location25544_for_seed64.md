The place is a pocket, nothing more. You would never find it unless you already knew the seam—an improper fold in the uppermost rim of the basalt scaffold, three vertical kilometers above any mapped contour, where the old lift-cables finally fray into rust lace and stop. The air there is permanently cool and thin enough to taste of powdered iron. No weather crosses the threshold. No drip has fallen in recorded time. The pocket simply continues, sealed by its own density.

Inside, every surface is spoken for. Pale, blade-thin fronds packed edge to edge rise from the mineral floor and from the ribs of half-buried housings; they lean until their tips touch the low ceiling of compressed basalt and then keep growing sideways, layering over one another in slow, irreversible braids. These are not ordinary plants. Their tissues are laminated silica and dried metal salts, translucent the way old bone is translucent. They drink nothing liquid. Instead they root directly into the warm jackets of the buried conduits and draw a faint, constant current that has nowhere else to go. The result is a saturated green-white interior light, dim as aquarium glass at night, bio-phosphorescent and unwavering. There is no soil—only a grit of abraded basalt and flake rust that the fronds have long since knitted into a single sprung mattress of growth. You cannot take a step without the entire woven mass answering underfoot like a restrained trampoline.

Threaded through this living felt are the last industrial ghosts: single cast-iron stanchions, scraps of grated walkway no longer attached to anything, pressure valves frozen at half-turn, a solitary flywheel housing whose axle still turns at the same patient speed it has kept for centuries. The motion is sourceless now, or rather its source has sunk so deep into equilibrium that the distinction no longer matters. Each slow rotation throws a soft mechanical cough into the packed air; the cough multiplies against the basalt shell and the vibrating fronds until the pocket is filled with a steady, mid-register roar—loud enough to press on the eardrums, unchanging enough that after a few minutes the mind files it as silence. Nothing accelerates. Nothing stops. The temperature never drifts. The light never brightens or dims.

Because the vegetation has occupied every cubic handspan, the remaining artifacts feel like afterthoughts or bones. A cracked ceramic insulator the size of a skull nestles in a crotch of fronds. A short run of narrow-gauge rail surfaces for half a body-length and vanishes again. There are no animals to exploit the niches, no insects, no scavengers; the ecology completed itself without them and then locked. The fronds shed microscopic scales that the air keeps in permanent suspension—an almost invisible snow that never lands, never clears—adding one more layer to the density.

Time here is not frozen so much as saturated. Gradient has been spent. The pocket sits at the altitude where the planet’s curvature is a clean geometry visible through the entrance slit, yet the slit itself is only a shoulder-wide wound half-hidden by the outermost fronds. You can crouch there and look out across an oceanic absence of water—ash-colored salt pans and knife-edged ridges falling away forever—while behind you the perpetual engine-note and the cool, luminous crowding continue exactly as they have since the last technician left or died or forgot. The place does not remember which. It has no further need of memory.
