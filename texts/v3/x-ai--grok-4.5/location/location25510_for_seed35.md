The chamber sits below everything that once knew rain. No seepage darkens the walls; no condensate beads on the undersides of pipes. The air is a warm mineral breath, kiln-dry and faintly metallic, held at a steady, blood-warm temperature that never climbs and never falls. What little moisture the rock ever held was cooked out long ago, leaving only powder, fine salt rime in the joints, and the dry glitter of crushed feldspar underfoot.

You enter at the scale of a cathedral nave that forgot its god. The space is bounded, readable in a single slow turn: a single elongated vault perhaps two hundred paces long, half that across, the ceiling a ragged arch of freshly fractured basalt and pale, knife-edged evaporite. The rock is young in the way of things that have only recently finished breaking. Edges are still sharp enough to cut cloth. Joints have not yet silted. Nowhere has time rounded a corner. The whole cavity feels as if it finished cracking open only a geological hour ago and then simply stopped.

Into this fresh wound someone—or something—drove an industry that never learned to quit.

Engines occupy the volume the way organs occupy a body. They stand and hang and lean in dense rank: reciprocating shells the size of cottages, flywheels frozen mid-thought yet still somehow turning, boiler drums that contain no water and never did. Their working fluid is a hissing, talc-fine dust that circulates in closed brass veins, glowing a dull apricot wherever friction kisses it to life. Pistons drive without lubrication that any surface world would recognize; they run on dry graphite blooms and the powdered ghosts of metals. Every surface is within an arm’s reach of another. Catwalks thread the gaps like spider silk that has begun to forget its pattern. Ladders end in mid-air. A solitary control pulpit, half its windows starred, leans at a drunk angle against a turbine casing. These are the only built marks: scattered, partial, already being treated as mere topography by whatever lives here now.

Because the place is emphatically inhabited.

Fauna throng at a density that would empty any open range. They are not visitors; they are the second architecture. Pale, jointed things the length of forearm cluster in the cooling fins, their carapaces exactly the chalky white of the salt rime, so that at first the machines appear simply more crusted than they are. Larger forms—barrel-bodied, many-limbed, blind—crawl the vertical faces of the engines with the unhurried certainty of gears meshing. Their feet leave no score marks; the metal accepts them as kin. Between these move flocks of smaller darting bodies, some winged with vanes of blown glass-like mineral, others flowing like animated slag. They pour through voids barely wider than a wrist, re-emerge in perfect order, and settle again. No individual ever appears lost. The population is saturated yet never collides, a single loud organism wearing a thousand skins.

They make almost as much sound as the engines themselves. The vault never drops below a continuous industrial roar: deep undeviating thrum of the great rotors, higher metallic chatter of valve trains that have not seated differently in living memory, the dry hiss of dust-fluid rocketing through capillaries. Over this the fauna add their own strata—rasping stridulations timed to the flywheel pulses, percussive eye-stalk taps that land exactly on the off-beats, a sub-audible drone from the largest crawlers that you feel in the hinges of your jaw. The total sound is enormous, cathedral-filling, yet absolutely stable. There is no crescendo, no rest, no change of key. It is the acoustic equivalent of a held breath that somehow never ends.

Light is rationed. What illumination exists leaks from three sources only: the apricot dust-glow inside the translucent sections of pipe, a sparse scatter of old emergency bulbs still burning behind wire cages at a exhausted yellow wattage, and the cold bioluminescent seams that some of the fauna carry along their flanks. These seams ignite and dim in fixed ripple patterns that never alter. Shadows are long and soft-edged; most of the upper vault remains in a brownish dusk. You see enough to move, never enough to feel you have properly arrived.

Vegetation is almost a rumor. In two or three places a brittle, coral-like growth the color of old brass has taken hold along a warm casing—neither plant nor mineral but something that photographs as both. It does not spread. A few filaments of pale, hair-thin quasi-lichen cling to the underside of a single catwalk. That is the entire flora. Nothing green has ever tried its luck here.

The strangeness is not loud; it is structural. Notice that the engines consume nothing and exhaust nothing. Their dust-fluid loops forever without replenishment. Notice that the fauna never feed in any recognizable way yet remain sleek. Notice that broken components do not accumulate; the floor holds only a thin, constant talc of the same white mineral, never deeper today than yesterday. Time here is not absent so much as already finished. Sequences occur—a piston rises, a creature crosses a pipe, a light-seam ripples—but the sequences are closed orbits. Tomorrow the same piston will be at the same height at the same absolute hour. The chamber has found an equilibrium so perfect it has forgotten how to leave. Even the “young” fractures in the rock feel less like a beginning than like a single undecaying present tense.

Stand long enough and the density begins to feel like a kind of pressure meditated upon rather than suffered. Warmth without sweat. Noise without event. Motion without progress into any future. The place is finished, and still deafeningly, intricately alive inside its own unchanging hour.
