The cleft is no longer than a short walk and scarcely wider than the span of outstretched arms. It opens high on a mountain still unfinished—ridges glass-sharp, faces raw where they sheared free only seasons ago. Crystalline edges catch nothing, for almost no light reaches this far. The sky above is a low, permanent pressure of cloud, and the opposing walls lean together until the space between them becomes a vertical dusk. What little illumination filters down arrives already green and spent, strained through the living mass that fills every available inch.

Moisture never settles into pools. It travels. Condensation beads on basalt still warm from its own fracturing and runs in cold threads. Brief rains arrive as vertical hammer-blows, then shred into drifting mist before they can pool. The air stays cool enough that the wetness feels personal, like breath against skin. Everything glistens without ever becoming swamp.

Vegetation has answered the cool damp with extravagance. Moss and liverwort stairs rise in thick spongy tiers. Ferns shoulder against one another until their fronds alone fill the volume; they stand man-high and packed edge-to-edge, clicking whenever the wind moves. Twisted shrubs root in hairline cracks and throw branches across the gap, weaving temporary scaffolds that sag, snap, and are replaced within days. The density is absolute—leaf against stem against hanging root against new unfurling tip—so that the cleft feels less like open air occupied by plants and more like a single organism that has learned the shape of a corridor. There is no understory, no canopy. There is only saturation.

Sound never pauses. The cleft funnels every gust into a solid roar that flattens fronds and then lets them spring back with wet, rubbery snaps. Rock shears continuously from the young walls; chips and slabs clatter downward through the green in irregular volleys, breaking stems, tearing curtains of moss, ringing against lower ledges before vanishing into the murk below. From deeper fractures comes another voice: geothermal vents pulse with clockwork regularity, jetting steam that smells of hot metal and wet cloth. The vents hiss and hammer like distant boilers, industrial and tireless, layering a mechanical rhythm beneath the organic thrash. Together the noises make a continuous factory weather—loud, layered, impossible to ignore.

The entire volume is in violent negotiation with itself. A saturated ledge lets go and drops an entire vertical garden into ruin; within hours pale new shoots already thread the scar. Mist floods the cleft until vision shrinks to an arm’s length of thrashing green, then tears away again to reveal fresh white rock and hanging wreckage of plants. Wind arrives as a shove, rearranges the living lattice, and departs. Nothing here is allowed to finish. Growth and erasure occupy the same frantic breath. The place is small enough that its turbulence feels intimate, almost indoor—an engine room without walls, a machine made of stone and stem and steam.

No animal moves. No structure stands. The only manufacture is geological and vegetal, driving itself at industrial volume in the dark.
