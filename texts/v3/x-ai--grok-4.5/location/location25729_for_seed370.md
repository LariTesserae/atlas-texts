The place is a single dense mass worked by countless hands long ago into what can only be called a chamber, though the word fails. It sits high, where the air thins and the horizon falls away on every side into heat-haze that never lifts. No rain has ever reached it. No dew. The materials themselves reject water: blocks of pale, sugar-hard mineral cut from some vanished salt-vein or baked clay so old it has forgotten plasticity. Everything here was stacked, fitted, wedged, and lashed with fiber that has long since powdered into the same dry body. There is no substrate beneath the construction. The floor is flag after flag of the same stuff, walls rising immediately, ceilings pressing down or vaulting only so that more walls can rise again inside the vaults. Natural rock, if it ever existed, has been sheathed or replaced until the distinction is meaningless.

Scale is intimate and absolute. You could walk the longest unbroken path in four hundred paces if any path were long. Instead the interior has been filled—shelves, piers, nested alcoves, half-stairs that end in sealed faces, pillars thickening mid-height into platforms that support yet denser lattices of the same material. There is almost no empty volume. Bodies could not pass one another without turning sideways and breathing carefully. The air itself feels occupied.

Light enters only as a dim, directionless warmth the color of old bone. High slots, worn into soft-edged ovals by whatever ages the place has endured, admit just enough glow to keep total darkness at bay. The temperature is steady and blood-warm, as if the mass itself stores heat and refuses to release it. No gradient. No cooler corner. The warmth is the same against every surface.

Nothing grows. Nothing moves of its own will. The mineral surfaces carry no lichen, no smear of life. Whatever simple organisms once might have claimed the joins have been baked or starved out of existence; the environment is sealed against them by dryness and antiquity. The craft is everywhere visible and everywhere finished: tool marks still readable under the polishing of deep time—chisel ranks, mallet flats, the slight OSCillation of hand-sawn cuts. Primitive work, patient, endless, performed without metal or with only the softest copper that long ago oxidized into green ghosts in the crevices. Every joint has settled. Every angle has found the posture it will keep until the star above this place cools. The erosion is complete; the structure has reached its final grammar and will not revise it.

And through every aperture, every hollow, every carefully left gap between stacked forms, the sound pours without cease. It is not wind in the ordinary sense. It is the air being forced at constant pressure through a labyrinth tuned, whether by design or by the long compromise of wear, into a single deafening chord. The tone is enormous, low enough to feel in the teeth and high enough to needle the inner ear, layered so thickly that individual notes can no longer be separated. It does not swell or fade. It does not pulses. It simply is, at full volume, forever. The packed geometry acts as both instrument and amplifier; every new pier and shelf added in the original building only tightened the resonance until silence became physically impossible. The sound saturates. It leaves no quiet pocket. Standing anywhere inside is like standing inside the throat of a vast, dry shout that began before memory and will not end.

Slightly off, beneath the barrage, is the sense that the chord is not quite empty. It contains intervals that suggest speech slowed past recognition, or the grinding of some enormous mortar that has no pestle left to turn. The mind keeps nearly grasping a pattern, then loses it again in the sheer pressure of noise. Nothing changes to confirm or deny the suspicion. The equilibrium is perfect. The light stays dim. The warmth stays even. The mineral bulk stays locked in its ancient, handmade density. High above whatever plain or void surrounds it, the place continues exactly as it is—packed, sound-riven, finished—without punctuation.
