The high basin holds no soil. What would have been a floor of scree and bare alpine rock has been unmade and remade so thoroughly that the original lie of the land exists only as a remembered slope beneath the work. Every surface is placed stone—flag, course, curb, step, channel, lip, terrace, and the narrow standing sheets that serve as walls without ever quite becoming architecture. The pieces lock by weight and cut alone. There is no mortar. The craft is handwork: pounding, grinding, fitting, the patient correction of edge against edge until water cannot find an easy seam and must instead be invited along deliberately scored paths. The tools that shaped it have left their signatures in shallow dimples and drag-marks still bright under the damp.

Water is everywhere and never hurried. It films the paving in continuous sheets that bloom and break with each faint shift of air. It beads along the underside of every overhang and falls in slow, even threads. It well up between fitted blocks as if the whole construction floats on a shallow lens of groundwater forced high by the surrounding ridges. In the lower runs of the basin the channels braided into one another until the walking surfaces become intermittent—long platforms that step just clear of the black slow current, connected by slabs no wider than a stance. The air itself is thick with fine cold moisture that condenses on any cooler face and runs. Brightness pours through this wet atmosphere without mercy; the light is high, direct, winter-clear, glancing off every filmed plane until the basin seems lit from within as much as from above. Shadows are few and bluish, and they move only when the thin clouds do.

Nothing grows. No lichen has found purchase on the sharp new edges. No moss softens the joints. The mineral world is complete and closed: schist, quartzite, a dark volcanic stone hauled from somewhere lower and set in patterns that have no ornamental purpose beyond drainage and load. The density is nearly total. Where one terrace ends another begins at once, half a handspan higher or lower. Standing walls rise only to shoulder or chest before dissolving again into pavements perforated by drains. There are no courtyards large enough to feel empty, no plazas; the open scale is achieved by repetition and extent rather than by void. From any rise you see the same saturated fabric continuing—kilometers of it—until it climbs the lower flanks of the containing ridges and finally frays into unfinished courses and stacked blanks still waiting for placement. Out there the work is younger still. Edges glitter. Fresh fracture planes have not yet been rounded by so much as a season of wind-driven grit.

Sound is almost absent. The water makes a continuous but subdued presence: a myriad of soft impacts, the hush of sheets meeting one another, the occasional hollow note when a larger drop finds a deeper cistern. No wind finds purchase long enough to speak. No creature crosses the stone. The quiet has weight; it makes the brightness feel louder than it is.

The construction continues, or rather it is never finished. Small gangs of work—never many, never hurried—move along the outer margins carrying hammerstones, wooden levers, drag sledges of lashed sapling. They speak little. Their labor belongs to the same slow cycle as the seepage: days measured in the seating of a single long slab, years in the completion of one more descending arcade of spillways. The basin does not erode so much as it is endlessly re-trued. Rain and melt would like to cut grooves and undercut lips, yet every season the same hands return to grind the lip true again, to lift and reset a settled course, to keep the young sharpness that defines the place. The geometry itself harbors a mild strangeness. Sightlines that should close instead slip sideways; a channel that falls for a hundred paces somehow delivers its water to a pool slightly higher than its origin, as if the whole pavement tilts through a dimension the eye cannot name. No one remarks on it. The water accepts the path it is given, and the light continues to shatter across the wet stone in every direction at once.

At the center of the basin the density tightens further into a shallow stone sink, tiered and perfect, where all the gathered waters pause before sinking through graded grates into whatever depth lies beneath the made world. The surface there is black glass under the sun, barely wrinkled. Standing at its rim you feel the altitude in the thin cool air and in the way sound fails to return from the surrounding maze of walls and walks. The place does not echo. It simply holds the light, the slow water, and the endless patient fitting of stone to stone, as though the basin were less a site than a single continuous object still being persuaded into existence.
