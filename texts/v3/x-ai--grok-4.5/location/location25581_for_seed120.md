The pocket is no larger than a sleeping body’s curl, yet it occupies the tip of a spire so high the rest of the world has already fallen away into blue rumor. You do not climb to it; you discover you have always been inside it, the way a seed discovers it has been inside the fruit. The spire itself is still arriving—raw geology in the act of deciding to be stone. Layers of half-cooled mineral peel upward like slow green fire, knitting themselves into walls even as the walls are already being finished by hands.

Those hands work without metal. Fingers and antler wedges, polished bone chisels, cordage twisted from the same vines that immediately take root in the fresh cuts. Every surface is made: fitted blocks of still-warm basalt, mortared with a paste of crushed lichen and spit, then immediately overgrown. There is no substrate that was not placed. The floor is a mosaic of river stones carried one by one from a watercourse that does not yet exist below; they steam where they touch. The ceiling is a low vault of interlocked ribs—petrified saplings bent while living, lashed with gut, already sprouting new leaves that press damply against your hair.

Heat saturates. It is not the dry blaze of open sun but the close, wet oven of a mouth. Moisture condenses on every plane, runs in sheets, collects in the hollows of carvings that are still being deepened. The carvings themselves change while you look: spirals become doorways that open only into more spiral. Water does not fall so much as well sideways, beading along the underside of leaves the size of hands, then leaping to the next in bright, audible threads. The air is thick enough to drink. Breathing feels like drowning slowly upward.

Vegetation is total and frantic. There is no bare made-surface for more than a few heartbeats before mosses the color of old bruises quilt it, before ferns uncoil through the mortar joints, before aerial roots descend like extra ropes and are promptly spliced into the binding by those same anonymous hands. The plants are lush to the point of excess—swollen, dripping, flowering in dimness with blossoms that look like wounds or ears. They grow in every direction at once, including into the spaces where rules of up and down have not finished settling. Sometimes a frond pushes through a patch of air that was solid a moment earlier; sometimes the air folds around it like cloth.

Light is a memory of light. It seeps from the rock itself—an interior phosphorescence the color of bruised peaches—filtered through layer on layer of wet leaf and steam. Shadows do not fall; they pool and then slowly climb. In this dimness the density becomes overwhelming. There is no gap. Every volume is occupied: leaf against stone against root against the humid press of your own skin. The air is so crowded with growing and making that sound itself has weight. Water strikes leaf, leaf strikes stone, stone settles with a groan as new mineral arrives beneath it. The hands keep working—scrape, twist, press—rhythmic and loud as a second heartbeat. Occasional deeper knocks sound from inside the walls, as if something larger is being framed farther in.

Fauna is almost absent, which makes the few presences immense. A single pale cricket the size of a thumb joint lives in a polished niche and stridulates in time with the dripping; its song is metallic and wrong, as though the air forgets how to carry vibration and then remembers too hard. Once, something like a moth but hinged passes through; its wings are scraps of the same lichen mortar, and it leaves a trail of wet dust that immediately sprouts. Nothing hunts. Nothing needs to. The place is too newly vowed to itself for hunger.

Time here is a weather. Cycles turn in minutes: a sudden dense fog of condensation that tastes of iron and nectar, then a brief fierce clearing in which the phosphorescence brightens and every surface shows the tool marks still fresh, still warm. The spire continues to rise under you—you feel the elevation as a slow vertical surge, a pressure behind the eyes—yet the pocket remains exactly room-sized, folding the new height into itself like a sleeve. Reality has not finished agreeing on its laws. Edge and center trade places. A corner becomes distant; distance becomes something you can cup in your palm and set carefully into the mortar. Sometimes the loud water speaks in the voices of the hands that placed it. Sometimes the hands are only the shapes that water takes when it decides to build.

You are inside the moment the world decides to have an inside. Everything is artifact and everything is embryo. The heat tightens. The leaves lean closer. The next stone is already being fitted into the space where your shoulder is.
