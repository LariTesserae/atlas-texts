The place is no larger than a forgotten courtyard, though no walling hand ever set its bounds. It sits low, cupped in a shallow depression where the ground has only just decided to become ground—soft seams of clay still hardening in slow breaths, mineral crusts flaking upward like thin lids lifting from sleep. Nothing here has finished arriving. The rock is embryonic, crumbly at the edges, slick in places with the last residues of whatever fluid pressed it into shape. You could break a shelf of it with the pressure of a palm and watch new grains sift down, rearranging themselves into almost-floors.

Warmth holds the hollow like a held breath. Not the dry heat of open sun, but a dim, even warmth that rises from the forming earth itself and lingers in the air, soft against skin. Light filters imperfectly: a diffuse pewter glow that never fully commits to day or dusk, as if the sky above this pocket has not yet been told which hour it is. Shadows pool in the raw hollows and cling to the undersides of leaves.

Moisture is intermittent, almost shy. Condensation beads along the cooler faces of the half-formed stone and drips at irregular intervals, each drop sounding once and then vanishing into silence. Occasional thin rains pass over, more mist than water, leaving the vegetation beaded and the air briefly heavier. Small pocket wetlands form in the lowest seams—quiet, dark mirrors no broader than a handspan—then dwindle again, leaving darker stains that the warm earth slowly drinks. There is no standing river, only these temporary wetnesses moving through a larger dryness that is itself still young.

Vegetation thrives in the warmth and damp, yet keeps its distance. Broad-leaved plants push up through the raw substrate in scattered clusters, their stems already thick and glossy, fronds unfurling with slow deliberation. Mosses soft as old cloth colonize the damper ledges. Thin climbers lace themselves across gaps, bridging one fragile outcrop to another. The growth is lush where it occurs—deep greens, occasional wine-dark undersides, leaves that look almost overfed—but the overall field remains sparse. Wide intervals of bare, pale mineral separate the thickets. The plants do not crowd; they hold court in their own small territories, as if aware the ground between them is still negotiating its own existence.

What gives the hollow its urban character is not stone cut by tools (there are none) but the patient architecture of living bodies. Termite-like builders, sightless and tireless, raise fluted towers and hinged galleries from the same soft clay the landscape is still becoming. Their spires lean and join, creating multi-level walkways, chambers stacked like rooms, arches that frame other arches. Ants of several sizes maintain parallel corridors, packing moisture into walls that harden overnight. Larger fauna—soft-furred mammals the size of fists, iridescent beetles with shovel jaws, pale amphibians that never fully dry—have enlarged and refashioned these structures into dens, lookouts, communal courts. The result is densest at the center: a low skyline of living mound-work, perforated, chambered, faintly warm to the touch. Pathways of packed silt connect outlying nests to the core. Nothing is permanent; a heavy rain can melt a facade overnight, and by the next quiet morning the builders are raising it again, slightly different, slightly taller. The settlement is always under revision, always embryonic like the land that hosts it.

Fauna is everywhere yet never loud. Things move constantly at the edge of hearing: the dry whisper of mandibles packing mud, the faint tick of claws on new crust, the wet shift of something turning over in a damp hollow. Wings open and close without buzz. A small predator and its slower prey complete their ancient circuit across a span of open mineral no wider than a stride, then vanish again into separate thickets. Life here is abundant in number and variety, but spaced, polite with emptiness. The air between bodies stays mostly empty. When rain beads the leaves, dozens of hidden throats drink in near-silence. When the condensation drips, ears that are not human turn toward the sound and then away.

The whole pocket operates under quiet, continuous motion. Clay continues its slow crystallization. Roots test new seams. Builders extend a ramp. A patch of moss advances one thumb-width overnight. Weather cycles are brief and local: a veil of mist, a pause, a warmer exhalation from the ground, another slow drip. Nothing here is finished, and nothing is still. The city of mounds breathes. The raw geology invents itself underfoot. The lush, spaced greenery drinks, waits, and reaches. All of it happens at a scale small enough to be missed by anyone walking the harder lands above—hidden the way a single room can be hidden inside a larger, indifferent house.

Stand in the center long enough and the dim light, the warm damp, the faint percussion of living work begin to feel like the only climate that ever existed. The place does not announce itself. It simply continues becoming, quietly, lushly, unfinished.
