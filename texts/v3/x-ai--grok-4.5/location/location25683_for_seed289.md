The pocket is no larger than a chapel crypt, and it has no entrance you could find twice. You do not descend into it so much as notice, quite suddenly, that the ordinary ground has folded around you and sealed. Above is rock that still remembers being soil; below is rock that has not yet decided to be floor. The whole place exists in the grammatical tense of almost.

It is cool here, a damp that never quite becomes wet. Beads of condensation stand on every surface as if the air itself has paused mid-exhale. They do not run. They do not fall. Where two beads should merge they simply occupy the same curvature forever, bright as glass. The moisture comes from nowhere continuous—no spring, no seepage vein—only these suspended arrives of water, like rain that forgot the rest of its fall.

Light saturates the chamber without source or shadow. It is the hard, shadowless brilliance of overload, as if the air itself were white-hot yet cold to the touch. Every facet of the half-formed stone glares. The stone is the strangest material: raw, embryonic geology caught in the instant before it chooses a shape. There are suggestions of strata that never stacked, crystal lattices caught midway through deciding their angles, soft mineral buds that look as if a fingertip could smear them—yet nothing smears. The rock is perpetually under construction and perpetually finished. No grain ever shifts. Erosion has not begun; formation has not ended. The place is locked in the equilibrium of becoming.

Into this bright unfinished stone the life is packed so tightly there is almost no interval left between bodies. Vegetation is sparse—only a few pale, leafless stems that rise like forgotten punctuation marks, their surfaces beaded with the same unmoving dew—but the animals leave no gap. They fill the volume the way liquid fills a sealed flask. Creatures the size of thumbs and the size of fists and the size of quiet thoughts press against one another, against the rock, against the straight stems. They are abundant past crowding: iridescent burrowers whose shells look cast from cooled lightning, soft mottle-hided grazers that chew nothing because there is nothing left to chew, thin-legged climbers that occupy every vertical face in overlapping densities. Their motions are fully articulated and completely frozen. A wing remains mid-beat. A jaw stays open around a mouthful of air. A dozen compound eyes reflect the sourceless light in perfect, unblinking arrays. They do not breathe. They do not settle. The entire ecology is held at the crest of its own activity, loud with the memory of sound.

And the sound is tremendous. It has nowhere to go and so remains. A continuous, overlapping roar of calls, clicks, wingcases slamming, foot-tips ticking stone, fluid moving through phantom throats—every noise the packed fauna would make if time still consented to carry it. The chamber shouts with the volume of a throng preserved at full cry. The noise does not echo because there is no after; it simply occupies, dense as the bodies themselves.

Here and there, almost lost in the press of living stillness, stand the scattered marks of something that once arranged material. A low ring of fitted stones no higher than a hand, its circle incomplete. A single upright slab leaning as if it meant to be a doorframe and then forgot. Tiny cairns of brighter mineral, deliberately stacked and then abandoned mid-gesture. No tools remain. No metal, no worked edge, no device. Only these faint geometric disagreements with the raw rock, like sentences begun in a language the place no longer speaks. They are partial, occasional, already half-recomposed into the embryonic stone around them.

The rules are simple and absolute. Nothing changes. The light does not dim. The droplets do not fall. The creatures do not finish their gestures. The rock does not choose its final face. Hot and cold fronts, growth and decay, ascent and collapse—all gradients have been cancelled. The pocket exists in perfect stasis, a bright loud crowded almost beneath the ordinary world, forever arriving at itself and never quite completing the journey. You may look as long as you like. When you stop looking, the place will still be exactly as loud, exactly as full, exactly as unfinished as the instant you found it.
