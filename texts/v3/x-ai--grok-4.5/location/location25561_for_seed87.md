The pocket is no larger than a clenched fist, and it sits in the blind root of a fracture where the seafloor has not yet decided which way to split. Cold water holds it the way amber once held insects: complete, unmoving at the edges, furiously busy at the center. Light arrives only as a diluted memory of surface weather, a gray-green dusk that never brightens or darkens. Everything here is the color of wet slate and bruised bruise.

What first reads as architecture is precipitate. Mineral-rich seep water, slightly warmer than the surrounding dark but still cold enough to ache, is leaking upward through raw basalt that has not finished cooling from some older fracture. As it meets the frigid brine it drops its dissolved load in delicate, accelerating lattices. Towers thinner than needles rise, branch, fuse, collapse under their own fresh weight, and rise again. In places the growth has already reached the density of a city block seen from altitude: avenues of hollow tubes no wider than a hair, plazas of overlapping plates, sudden open atria where a gas bubble once lodged and then escaped. The structures are still soft at the tips, translucent, more intention than stone. Touch them and they smear like wet chalk; leave them a day and they will have sheathed themselves in a second, harder skin.

Sparse life clings to the unfinished walls. Thread-thin hydroids, almost invisible, wave in the micro-currents. A few pale, rootless algae drift past without settling, torn free from brighter zones far above. That is nearly all the vegetation the pocket will ever hold. Everything else that grows here is animal.

The inhabitants are dense as breath in a closed room. Specks of translucent crustacean, armored worms the length of eyelashes, completely blind amphipods whose antennae map the corridors by touch alone. They do not merely occupy the rising city; they hurry its construction. Each animal trails a faint mucus that traps fresh mineral crystallites and cements them into place. Where two creatures pass, they pause, antennae interlacing, and a new strut thickens between them. Colonies of filter-feeders have claimed the upper terraces and pulse water through lattices they themselves secreted last week; the only sound in the entire pocket is the almost-inaudible click of tiny jaws and the softer hiss of water forced through living sieves. Silence, really. You feel the motion more than you hear it.

Above the highest unfinished spire a slow snowfall of marine snow never quite settles. Current threads weave between the towers like invisible streets, carrying ground-up carcasses and shed exoskeletons that the residents intercept before they can bury the growing city. Predation is intimate and constant: a worm half the size of a grain of rice will wait in a half-formed doorway until a smaller mite drifts within reach, then exist only as a brief soft thrashing that ends without audible complaint. The emptied husk is quickly mortared into the next wall.

Nothing here was designed. There are no tools, no memories of tools. The order is an emergent accident of chemistry meeting appetite meeting cold. Yet the layout has begun to rhyme with itself: spiral ramps that follow the path of least resistance for circulating water, cul-de-sacs that collect detritus for later eating, open courts where three or four species can feed without colliding. Walk the length of the pocket (a journey of ten slow centimeters) and you pass through zones that already feel like districts—denser growth near the seep, quieter residential galleries along the colder outer wall, a chaotic industrial fringe where new mineral is still raining out of solution and the animals work fastest.

Time here is thick. Structures rise and are remodeled by the same mouths that feed on them. A tower that stood this morning may be a low wall by evening, its material scavenged and re-deposited three streets over. The city is embryonic and already senescent, forever unfinished, forever mid-sentence. Outside the pocket’s mouth the true deep waits, featureless and immense. Inside, the dense, quiet, cold metronome of growth and consumption continues, illuminated only by the faint, internal glow of whatever biochemistry keeps these bodies from freezing solid. No one named this place. It does not need a name. It is simply the shape that water, rock, and hunger make when they are left alone together in the dark.
