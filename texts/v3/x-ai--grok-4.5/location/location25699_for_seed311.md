The pocket does not announce itself. You find it the way you find a bruise under the skin of the world: by pressure, by a wrong coolness that settles into the joints. One moment the corridor is ordinary stone and conduit; the next the floor has already given way to a vertical sheet of water that does not spill but simply continues, a hanging slab of green-blue silence that you step through without ceremony. Gravity loosens its manners. You are inside.

It is not large. The entire volume would fit inside a modest civic atrium, yet every cubic span is claimed. Buildings do not stand so much as tessellate—angular shells of pale composite grown or extruded in sharply faceted planes, their edges still crisp, barely weathered, as if the whole settlement were poured and set only weeks ago. They interlock at impossible angles, floors becoming walls becoming ceilings mid-span, so that “up” is a local and provisional decision. Between them the water is not emptiness but a second architecture: dense, cool, faintly mineral, moving in slow deliberate sheets and sudden rips. There is almost no free volume. Bodies of liquid press against panels of tech-glass, against lattices of filament that thrum with currents you feel in the teeth more than hear. Where two structures nearly touch, the gap is packed with translucent ducts, with boiling knots of cabling that sometimes forget to be solid and briefly become braided light.

Light itself behaves ordinarily—bright, even, shadow-casting—yet its sources are coyly hidden inside the water. Soft rectangular glows migrate through the denser strata like silent elevators. You can read by them. You can watch suspended flecks of something almost like silt flash and vanish. Vegetation is scarce and deliberate: thin dark fronds cling to a handful of edges, their fronds slowly cycling from near-black to a bruised violet and back, never growing outward, only thickening in place as if housekeeping rather than living. Nothing browses them. Nothing swims. The ecology here is pure process without appetite.

Sound is constant and layered. A deep hydraulic boom rolls through the pocket every few heartbeats—some vast unseen pump or tide-gate cycling—while closer in the water itself hisses and knocks against the facets, a thousand small impacts braided into one continuous white roar. Inside the canals and service throats, sensors or valves or things that are neither chatter in sharp ultrasonic bursts that the water helpfully translates into felt vibration along the bone. The place is always adjusting itself: panels iris open and reseal, allowing thick lenses of colder water to slide through; a whole section of street-ceiling rotates ninety degrees with a graceful hydraulic sigh, reorienting the local “down” so that what was a companionway becomes a drip-shaft. Droplets—though there is no air to hold them—still somehow bead and race along invisible tension lines, gathering into heavier blobs that are then whisked away by hungry intakes.

The materials feel new. Edges have not rounded. Where the composite has been cut or grown, the fracture planes are razor-clean and slightly phosphorescent, as though the act of formation left a afterimage. Low elevation is absolute; there is no horizon climb, only denser water below and slightly less dense water above, the whole pocket nested in some greater drowned foundation you never see. Temperature stays just cool enough that the water feels like a held breath. Occasional clouds of microscopic glitter—nanite factotums, perhaps, or the spent dust of whatever physics keeps the hanging interfaces stable—drift past like slow reverse snow and are reabsorbed by the nearest surface without sound.

Strangeness lives in the seams. Look too long at a junction and you notice the buildings are not merely adjacent but continuous through a higher-dimensional fold; a corridor you follow will, without ever turning, return you to its own exterior three meters away and one conceptual story down. Some walls are permeable to intention: think of needing a handhold and the surface blooms a temporary rung of thickened water-glass. Other surfaces reject scrutiny entirely, their topology sliding away from the eye the way a word slides away from the tip of the tongue. The settlement is occupied only by its own maintenance. No voices, no footsteps, no animals. Only the loud, tireless conversation of pressure, pump, and self-revising geometry.

Stay long enough and the pocket begins to feel less like a place you entered and more like a place that is currently deciding whether to finish becoming real. The water thickens almost affectionately around the calves. A fresh facet shears out of an existing wall with a crisp crystalline snap, shedding a sheet of bubbles that immediately reverse and suck themselves back into the newborn surface. Somewhere deeper, the great boom cycles again. The light migrates. The sparse fronds darken, lighten, darken. Everything is busy. Everything fits. There is no room left for anything that is not already part of the filling.
