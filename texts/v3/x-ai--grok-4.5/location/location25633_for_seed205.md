The high mesas of the Verdance do not rise from stone. They are stone’s long aftermath—eons of root and mycelial acid that unmade entire mountain chains and rebuilt them as living plateaus. What remains is a single, continent-spanning organism whose body is geography. From any vantage the eye first registers emptiness: pale-gold distances, heat shimmer, the slow drift of cloud-shadow across surfaces that look like baked clay. Only after minutes does the clay resolve into compressed leaf-litter and root-pavement, warm underfoot, faintly springy, veined with darker seams where moisture still travels. Vegetation is total yet sparse; the biomass has simply grown so large that its individual members stand leagues apart, leaving abyssal prairies of low, heat-tolerant mosses and glass-bladed sedges between them.

Each primary is a buttressed tower hundreds of meters across at the base, bark plated like cooled magma, rising until it punches through its own weather. Their crowns are separate skies—layered galleries of leathery fronds that clap and boom when the thermal winds hit them. Condensation runs in sudden silver sheets down fluting trunks whenever a temperature gradient sharpens; the water never pools for long. It hisses into vapor again before it can become a river, or it is drunk immediately by the carpet of epiphytes that sheath every vertical surface. Rain, when it comes, is a brief hammering that leaves the air tasting of copper and green sap; within an hour the only wetness left is the perpetual sheen inside leaf-axils and the dark circles of temporary springs that open at the feet of the giants like dilated pores.

The heat is not cruel so much as omnipresent, a thick medium one swims through. At this elevation the sunlight is undiluted, normal in color yet relentless; it drives convection cells between the towers so that each mega-trunk owns a private cyclone of rising air. Dust rotates in lazy helices. Seeds with translucent wings ride those helices for days before choosing a crevice. The sound never drops below a roar. It is compounded of a thousand strata: the bass flex of wood expanding in heat, the white-noise hiss of millions of stomatac pores venting pent-up vapor, and above all the fauna.

Animals here are not visitors. They are the moving part of the same total biomass. Filter-swarms of iridescent midges turn noon air into sheets of living foil that scream at the edge of hearing when they veer. Herds of tall, jointed browsers—keratin and photosynthetic sail-skin—move in slow radial patterns around each tower, their calls a metronomic thunder that travels farther than sight. Predators do not stalk so much as weather; packs of heat-mirage cats appear first as optical flaws, then solidify into corded muscle and recurved teeth, their hunting cries a serrated shriek that can panic an entire plaza of browsers into stampede. At dusk the volume climbs further when the tower-roosting choirs begin—creatures like elongated bats whose throat-sacs amplify ultrasonic clicks into audible cannon-fire. Deafening is not metaphor; prolonged exposure without the wax-plugs woven from seed-down leaves a traveler’s ears ringing for days, a private tinnitus that mimics the landscape’s own pulse.

Over centuries the giants have achieved a settled morphology. Lightning scars are healed over with resin keloid. Wind-pruned limbs have become permanent cantilevers. The root-pavement has locked the last free boulders into its matrix so that erosion now operates only as gentle polishing. Everything feels finished, self-maintaining, middle-aged on a geologic clock. Yet the system is emphatically alive. New runners the thickness of rivers sometimes surface overnight, arch a hundred meters, and plunge again, redrawing the sparse geometry of the plain. Colonies of entrepreneurial vines may bridge two neighboring towers within a season, creating temporary aerial highways that the browsers learn, then abandon when the vines lignify and become just another spar of architecture.

Scattered across this living desert of plant-mass are the marks of tool-users. They are slight. A ring of fire-cracked resin stones where someone rendered pith into edible starch. A ladder of driven hardwood spikes ascending a trunk to a mid-level gallery where woven platforms sag under the weight of drying sail-skins. Occasional free-standing structures—low domes of bent rib-wood sheathed in stretched leaf leather—crouch in the lee of root-buttresses, their doorways hung with chime-strings of hollow seed-pods that clatter frantically in every breeze. The craft is pre-metal: stone flakes set in antler, fire-hardened spears, cordage twisted from vascular fibers so strong it can check a panicked browser mid-charge. No city has ever cohered; the distance between towers and the overwhelming appetite of the swarms discourage permanence. People pass through, harvest, etch a few symbols into bark that will grow shut within years, and move on. Their legends say the Verdance is the cooled body of a god who lay down to sleep and dreamed a forest so complete it replaced the need for any other world.

Stand long enough in one of the great plazas and the place begins to edit ordinary senses. Depth loses reliability; a tower that seemed a morning’s walk away resolves into a three-day traverse once the heat-haze is mentally subtracted. Time dilates to vegetative pace—one learns to watch the slow flex of a frond-shadow across a root-bole as if it were an hour-hand. At night the stars appear between the crowns in unfamiliar densities, and the deafening choir shifts register into a single continent-wide chord that seems to breathe. Some travelers report that the chord contains languages, or that the midges briefly align into glyphs that name the viewer before dispersing. Whether these are hallucinations born of heat and infrasound or the genuine strange cognition of a mind distributed across a million square kilometers of linked cambium remains an open question. The Verdance does not clarify. It simply continues—hot, sparse, total, roaring—weathering itself into ever more perfect versions of the same endless, living elevation.
