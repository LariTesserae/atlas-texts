The basin has no proper name that holds. What names cling to it slide off in the wet, or get swallowed by the same slow dark that keeps the place. It is low country, barely a crease in a larger flatness, freshly opened—as if the ground only decided to sag open a short while ago, geologically speaking. The edges are still sharp where mud meets something harder. Fresh scarps, unsoftened. Young water has nowhere old to go yet, so it sheets and pools and weeps out of every cut face.

There is almost no light. Not night, not overcast, not eclipse. The dark is simply the given. It has thickness. You feel it on the skin the way warm mist settles: continuous, slightly greasy, carrying a faint metallic tang from somewhere industrial and far, or close, or dissolved. Temperature stays warm, body-warm, as if the dark itself exhales. Sound is sparse—occasional soft plips of water detaching from overhangs, the muted shift of something heavy adjusting its weight in mud, once in a long while the distant, slow bang of metal cooling or settling. Conversations, if any occur here, happen in the chest more than the air.

Water is everywhere near the surface. The ground is a memory of rain that never quite finished. Marsh-edges blur into shallow sheets that reflect nothing because there is nothing to reflect. Rocks—if the low ridges of half-consolidated slurry can be called rocks—weep steadily. Condensation beads on every upright surface and runs in thin, patient threads. You wade more than walk. The wet is warm too.

Vegetation keeps itself scarce. What grows does so in thin, deliberate clustering: pale, fleshy stems that feel more like cartilage than plant, low mats of something that might once have been moss and now only remembers the shape. They do not reach. They wait. Between them the ground stays open, slick, busy with other traffic.

That traffic is dense. The darkness is crowded. Fauna moves in layers, near-touching, a saturation of bodies and almost-bodies. Soft shapes shoulder past at knee height, higher shapes drift with a wet-silk sound, lower things string themselves through the mud in slow braids. Some are warm-blooded enough that their passage is a brief sensible bloom of heat. Others seem continuous with the water, thickening it into temporary limbs or curtains. Eyes are rare or useless; the knowing here is pressure, displaced water, the faint electric prickle when two presences brush the same cubic meter. There is abundance without frenzy. Everything is patient. Cycles turn on long axes—digestions that last seasons, migrations measured in the gradual lean of the whole basin toward some lower discharge that has not yet finished deciding where it is.

Scattered through this are the built things. Not a town. Not ruins in the romantic sense. Occasional hard geometries: a trapezoidal housing half-sunk and still faintly warm to the touch, the way old transformers stay warm. A gantry half-collapsed into spasm, its crane-arm forever beginning a lift it will never complete. Lengths of pipe that appear and disappear into mud as if the ground is still negotiating whether to accept or reject them. At long intervals a larger shape resolves—factory wall, cooling tower truncated, the ribcage of a dredge—edges still crisp with that same youthful sharpness the landforms have. Industrial memory without industry. Some of the machinery still turns, or tries to. Deep inside drowned casings, shafts rotate a fraction of a degree each day. Somewhere a dynamo hums below hearing, a vibration more than a sound, feeding nothing, or feeding the dark itself. Electrified fences stand in places with no property left to mark, their wires sagging, occasionally webbing a unnecessary blue-white spark that dies before it can become light.

Scale opens outward. You can walk for a long time and the character of the place remains the same and yet never repeats: new clusters of the cartilage-stems, new constellations of half-sunk engines, new densities of the soft-bodied traffic. Ridges of young mud angle away and are lost. Sheets of water widen into temporary proto-lakes whose far shores exist only as a change in the way the dark presses. It is regional. It has weather, of a sort—slow pulses of warmer wetness, rare colder slicks that slide through at ankle depth and take a day to pass. It has horizons that refuse to resolve.

And the weirdness is not an addition; it is the medium. Reality thins here the way metal thins when it has been flexed too often. Directions braid. A pipe you passed an hour ago stands again ahead, bone-dry inside while everything around it weeps. Fauna leave tracks that reverse while you watch, or fill with a different water that has different weight. Sometimes the dark develops folds: you step through a warmer curtain and for a few breaths the industrial hum becomes a choir of overlapping factory whistles in languages no longer spoken, then the quiet returns, thicker. Edges of objects forget to be edges. A gantry’s shadow (though there is no light to cast one) peels free and drifts a little way as an independent dark, denser than the resting dark, before it tires and settles back. Time is not exempt. The slow rotation of those drowned shafts sometimes stutters, leaps, and you feel in your teeth that a century tried to happen and then thought better of it.

Yet the overall motion stays glacial. Nothing here is in a hurry to finish becoming. The basin continues its minute, endless settling. Water continues finding newer, lower skins to slide along. The crowded lives continue their long assimilations and slow dispersals. The scattered machines continue their private, uncompleted industrial dreams. The dark continues to be absolute and warm and populated. If you remain long enough, you begin to suspect the place is not broken so much as unfinished—an early draft of a landscape that has decided it prefers the draft stage, and has the patience to stay there while every other process in the world exhausts itself elsewhere.

That is the country. It does not care whether you map it. The maps dissolve anyway.
