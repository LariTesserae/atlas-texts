The place is no larger than a collapsed basilica, or the cupped palm of a sleeping giant: a single bounded hollow you could cross in a hundred long breaths if the geometry held still. It sits low, where the land has not yet decided whether to remain soft or become stone. The floor is embryonic geology—damp grit and half-set mineral skins that dent under weight and slowly, quietly heal, occasionally exhaling a thin cold vapor that condenses on everything above. There is no proper sky. What light there is arrives as a rumor, a diluted indigo leak through living ceilings, enough only to make the darkness visible as texture rather than absence.

Everything structural is plant. Not plants growing upon architecture, but plant as the sole substrate and the sole builder. Massive trunk-walls and buttress-roots have arranged themselves into something that remembers a town: stacked galleries, arched thoroughfares, cisterns, loading bays, even the suggestion of factories. The wood is cool and permanently beaded with moisture; where two great stems have fused they leave glossy seams that sweat a clear liquor smelling of crushed green and old iron. Leaves the size of barn doors hang motionless in the upper dark, their undersides silvered with condensation that gathers, swells, and falls in slow, isolated drops. Each drop travels a long time before it hits. The sound is small and quickly swallowed.

Despite the total vegetable mass, the interior volume is almost empty. Chambers yawn. Catwalks of living cable span distances that feel indecent for the scale of the place, leaving vast lenses of still air between. You can stand in the central hollow and sense more absence than presence; the plant-city has overbuilt its own occupancy, leaving district after district of untenanted, softly creaking space. Yet the emptiness is industrious. Halfway up a column you will find a turbine of spiral bark and tendon that turns without wind, driven by slow hydraulic pressure inside the xylem. Elsewhere, reticulated vines serve as drive-belts, transferring torque from one ponderous wooden gear to another. The machinery is quiet—industrial, electrified in some sap-conductive way, but dialed down to a near-subsonic thrum you feel in the teeth more than hear. Production occurs: pods swell and split on timed cycles, releasing finished objects that clatter once onto collection ledges and are then left alone, uncollected, as if the process itself were the point. The objects are never the same twice. Sometimes they are tools. Sometimes they are locks without doors. Sometimes they are small, sealed climates.

Fauna is abundant and almost entirely silent. The darkness seethes with bodies that refuse ordinary outlines. Soft, many-jointed things flow along the undersides of beams like living gutters. Clusters of eyes open and close in the wet bark, not watching so much as tasting the air. Herds of pale, deer-sized browsers move through the lower galleries on jointed stilts, their feet never quite touching the embryonic ground; they crop the luminous mosses that grow in the shape of outdated signage and leave no footprints, only a temporary coolness in the air. Higher up, something large and membranous glides between the hangar-leaves, its passage marked solely by a change in the pattern of dripping. Predator and prey relations exist, but they are conducted as polite, almost ceremonial exchanges of mass and heat. A chase, when it occurs, is a single fluid gesture completed before the mind can frame it as violence. Afterward both parties resume their slow circuits.

The damp is intermittent and personal. There is no river, only pockets: a cold mirror of water held in the crotch of a root for an hour before the root absorbs it again; curtains of mist that form at ankle height and burn off without heat; the sudden interior rain that happens when a high leaf finally tilts. Everything is cool to the touch, the temperature of a stone corridor just after rain. Breath hangs visible. Metal—wherever the plant-city has spun or secreted true metal—blooms with slow dew that never quite becomes rust.

Reality is locally negotiable. Edges do not always agree on their lengths. A stair of living wood may reverse its chirality while you climb, so that you arrive on the same landing you left, only now the air tastes of copper and the fauna have rearranged their silhouettes. Sound sometimes arrives before its cause, or lingers as a hollow after-image once the drop has already fallen. In the farthest apse of the hollow, where the plant-walls thin and the raw new ground shows through like a wound, the industrial processes have begun to manufacture impossibilities: gears that turn in closed time, conveyor belts that load tomorrow’s condensation onto yesterday’s leaves, a single electric lamp that is always just about to ignite and never does, holding the entire chamber in a perpetual pre-light. The fauna avoid that end, or perhaps they are already inside it, converted into pure motion without bodies.

Nothing here is finished. The geology continues its soft self-assembly underfoot. The plant-city keeps extending mezzanines into voids that did not exist an hour earlier. The quiet engines revise their own schematics. Even the darkness feels newly mixed, still streaked with residual shades that have not yet decided to be black. You are inside a place that is still coming into terms with being a place—small, damp, overgrown, nearly vacant, industriously alive, and only loosely committed to the older agreements of space and sequence. The next drop of condensation is already gathering. When it falls, something minor will have changed forever.
