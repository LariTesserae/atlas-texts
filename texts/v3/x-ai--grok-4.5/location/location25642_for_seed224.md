The pocket sits where the absolute height of the world folds inward on itself, a seam no wider than a held breath and yet inadvertently vast. You do not arrive by climbing; the altitude is already inside the place, a permanent high thinness that makes every edge feel vertical even when the floor is level. There is no sky overhead—only a dim, unwavering gray that might be stone or distant cold fire or simply the absence of anything willing to be light. The air (if it is air) carries no moisture. What little residual cold there is has long since settled into the materials themselves: dry mineral dust that never cakes, salt-fine crystals that refuse to melt, a rime of colorless frost that grows in slow geometric lattices and then stops, never quite completing its patterns.

Nothing green has ever lived here. Nothing fungal, nothing bacterial, nothing that once photosynthesized or decayed. The ground is a sparse scatter of dark basal plates and pale cancellous stone, worn smooth by processes older than weather. Between them stretch expanses of absolute emptiness—not voids exactly, but intervals where density itself has grown tired. A single structure may stand for a long time in one direction before another appears: a partial arch of matte black metal that loops through itself without joining, a low plinth whose top surface displays a shallow depression filled with motionless, non-reflective liquid that is not water and has never been wet. These are not ruins in the ordinary sense. They feel installed, then patiently abandoned by whatever intelligence no longer needs them. Their edges are softened not by wind but by a slower erosion: the quiet attrition of possibility. Over durations measured in stillnesses, corners forget how to be sharp. Surfaces that once held nanometer precision now hold only the memory of it, slightly blurred, as though reality has run its thumb across them.

Light does not fall so much as linger at half-strength. It has no source and no preference. Shadows are therefore mild and unreliable; sometimes a freestanding upright of latticework casts three of them lasting only as long as attention remains on it. The silence is nearly total. What sound exists is structural: the infrequent, almost apologetic click of one plate settling a fraction deeper into its long habit of rest, or the faint crystalline chime produced when two non-adjacent surfaces briefly agree on the same temperature. These events are separated by stretches of quiet so complete they feel curated.

The advanced remnants are the only density worth noticing, and even they keep their distance. A short upright pillar of translucent material contains, frozen mid-gesture, a constellation of silver motes that once performed calculations in dimensions the surrounding space can no longer support. Nearby, a low curve of brushed alloy embeds a vein of something darker that slowly migrates a few micrometers per century, rewriting the alloy’s properties as it goes—conductivity appearing and vanishing like a tide that has forgotten the moon. At rare intervals the geometry stammers. A right angle becomes gently impossible for the space of several breaths; parallel lines converge without ever meeting; a section of floor momentarily remembers being a wall and then, with temple patience, forgets again. These breakdowns do not announce themselves. They simply occur, adjust the local rules by a small degree, and allow the place to continue being what it has long been: an established, high, cold, dry pocket of mild wrongness, half-engineered and half-eroded into a stable strangeness.

Nothing moves of its own will. No fauna, no wind worth the name, only the glacial rearrangement of materials that have already decided what they are. The entire pocket exists in a state of settled anomaly—neither growing nor collapsing, merely continuing its slow negotiation with the physics that almost, but not quite, contain it. To stand here is residual: you occupy one of the sparse gaps between presences, breathing the mineral quiet, feeling the altitude in your bones even though the ceiling (if there is a ceiling) hangs nearly close enough to touch. The place does not care that you have noticed it. It was already finished noticing itself long ago.
