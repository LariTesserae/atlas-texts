The cavern does not begin as a cavern. It begins as a decision the plants made.

Far below the sealed crust—where the surface world keeps its weather and its oceans and all the wet bargains of ordinary life—there is a regional dark that has been colonizing itself for a long while. Not a maze of tunnels, but an open holding: a buried plain the size of a valley system, multi-featured, with distances that behave like landscape rather than architecture. The air is cool and thin of moisture. What little water exists is locked inside living tissue or bound as crystalline hydration in the mineral dust; free liquid is a rumor, a technical memory, something the factories still model in diagrams that no one prints anymore.

Underfoot, between the settled places, the floor is a pale salt-and-silica pavement, dry as a kiln shelf, faintly chalky, whispering under soft boots or pads. It looks lifeless until you watch long enough. Then the pavement shows its youth: unfinished seams, embryonic ridges, raw upwellings where something vegetative is still deciding how stone should feel. The geology here is not weathered so much as premature. Pre-formation. Basins have edges that fade into haze rather than cliff. Low mesas of compacted dust wear crowns of root-hab that have not yet agreed on a final silhouette. The place is mid-birth, and birth, underground, is quiet.

Light arrives as a continuous daytime without a sun. The upper vault—too high to be called a ceiling with any intimacy, unfinished in places where vine-scaffolds still hang like unfinished cathedrals—hosts sheets of translucent canopy tissue. They photosynthesize on residual heat gradients and on the industrial flue-glow siphoned upward, and they radiate back a steady, ordinary brightness: the color of overcast noon in a cool latitude. Shadows fall cleanly. You can read by them. You can lose a tool in them and find it again. Nothing flickers unless a growth-pulse moves through a district and the canopy dimples for a quarter-hour, the way a lake dimples under rain that never comes.

The city is grown and spaced.

It is urban at settlement scale—districts with names scraped into bark-plate, process halls, transfer yards, sleep-galleries—but the density is deliberate and low. Between structures there are wide mineral commons, empty enough that sound frays and dies. A factory will stand alone like a buttressed tree-cathedral, its outer walls laminated living wood bonded with ceramic resins the plants themselves secrete, and a kilometer off, another will rise with its own swarm of aerial roots rasping slowly into new footings. Scaffold is not temporary here; scaffolding is a life stage. Young industrial organisms arrive as seed-packets and rhizome-dumps dropped by slow cable-walkers, then swell through successive architectures: frame, envelope, gland, engine.

The engines are the strange heart of it. They do not burn wet fuels. They grind dry chemistry—phosphate grit, atmospheric nitrogen fixed in membrane towers, powdered organics scraped from older die-back zones—and they turn that work into motion, heat at a carefully throttled cool, and more plant. Pistons of compression-wood flex in silence polished by generations of design; gear-forests mesh with a sound more like continuing than like machinery. Belts of living cambium carry loads. What would be smoke in another world is here a fine productive dust, unwatered, that the understory fauna comb from the air with feathery mouthparts and return to the nutrient loops. Occasionally a pressure-valve venturi unseals and the cavern receives a long, soft exhalation—almost a sigh—then hush again.

Because the whole ecology is plant-as-substrate, “building” and “forest” are continuous categories. Streets are braided root-lanes kept pruned to a walkable hardness. Walls leaf in slow seasons that do not match any surface calendar. Windows are grown as translucent callus and kept clear by symbiotic grazers. The power grid is a vascular grid; pulse-statons regulate turgor the way transformers regulate voltage. At night-cycle—when the canopy dims to a lower, bluer working light—you can see the sap-current as a muted internal weather moving through translucent trunks.

Fauna are abundant without crowding. They have learned the sparse grammar of the place. Herald-moths with mineral-dusted wings ferry pollen and spores between factory-crowns, their flight almost silent, only a dry clocking of wing-cases when they settle. Drift-hares with elongated cooling ears nibble spent leaf-litter and the soft edges of still-forming landforms, their teeth adapted to silica as much as cellulose. In the mid-levels, something like a lank primate—jointed, thoughtful, tool-using in a modest continuous way—moves along the cable-walks servicing gland-valves and listening with bone-conducted attention to the health of a beam. Lower still, in the coolest gutters of the salt pavement, armored isopods the size of clasped hands farm algae-films so xerophytic they scarcely count as wet; the isopods click very softly when they approve of a patch. Predation exists as a paced, almost ceremonial circuit: stalkers that hunt by interrupting secondary roots and waiting for the panic-chemicals, and several species of kite-bird that ride the rare thermal columns above active engine-halls and take the moths in clean, economical stoops. Nothing shrieks. Kill-sounds are brief; the cavern swallows them.

Atmosphere is the long cool of a root-cellar crossed with a machine-shop that has decided to be polite. You smell cured lignin, warm dust, a faint sweetness of terpenes engineered for pest-guidance, and sometimes—near a new founding—the sharp green of ruptured growth tips, like a greenhouse arguing with a desert. Air currents are managed more by canopy flex and district respiration than by weather. True weather is rare and local: a sudden shedding of seed-scales that fall like dry snow and erode the youngest pavements into temporary dunes; a phenolic fog released when two rival expansion fronts meet and negotiate by chemistry rather than by timber-war.

The rules here are botanical-industrial and still soft at the edges.

Ownership is maintenance: if your roots keep a hall alive and productive, the hall is yours until the commons network pulses a reclamation signal through the vascular grid. Expansion is a civic duty and a private hunger; map-rooms show the front as a glowing irregular hem eating the unfinished dark, and work-crews (humanoid and otherwise) travel out with seed and with engines-in-larva to pin the next sparse node. Water is sacred in the way absences become sacred—hoarded inside bodies, counted, never wasted on spectacle. Light is a commons. Silence is a design constraint: loudness means inefficiency or injury, and both are corrected.

Weirdness does not announce itself with impossible geometry so much as with processes that feel half a step sideways from sense. Some engine-trees dream—or the local language has no better word—in the form of overnight growth-spurts that rearrange internal stair-spirals according to equations no planner drafted. Certain districts audible only to the elongate-eared fauna broadcast infrasound maps of future construction; the hares will not nest where the map already sketches a road. At the farthest active margins, where pavement is still more idea than fact, you can watch plant and mineral switch roles for an hour: a ridge of salt crystallizes in the precise dendritic pattern of a leaf, hardens, and is later mined as structural grit, as if the abyss were rehearsing botany in stone before committing to the living draft.

Walk far enough across one of the open commons at mid-light and the city becomes well-spaced punctuation rather than enclosure. Behind you, a cluster of factory-crowns fir each other with flying buttresses of braided liana. Ahead, the pavement runs toward a haze where new mesas are lifting in slow motion, raw, pale, faintly green at the compressive cores. A cable-walker crosses the high middle distance, tiny, purposeful. A moth-cloud turns as one organism and settles into a gland-spire, and the spire’s output note—already nearly too low to credit as sound—deepens by a fraction. Somewhere a press closes on a dry charge and opens again. The cavern takes the small violence of production, folds it into cool air, and returns to the larger quiet of a place still deciding the final names of its hills.

This is not a ruin and not a paradise. It is an underworld industrial garden in the act of finishing its own ground, sparse by doctrine, total by material, dry as a held breath, lit like ordinary day, and busy in the long roles that do not need to shout.
