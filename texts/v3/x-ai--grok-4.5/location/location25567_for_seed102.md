The pocket sits a little way beneath a surface you will never see. It is no larger than a banquet hall, if banquet halls were hollowed by sudden geological accident and then left alone. The rock around it is warm to the touch and faintly orange in its own right, as if the heat of deeper strata has been trapped here and forgotten how to leave. There is no drip, no seep, no damp. What passes for air is dry mineral vapor and a slow, scented breath of sulfur and copper. Whatever chemistry runs this place does so without water; everything that lives here learned another solvent ages ago, or invented one.

The floor is a young fracture-plate of pale glass-stone, still sharp where it sheared. Edges have not yet been blunted by anything. From it rise the plants—if that word will serve. They are tall, sparse, and lush only in the local sense: each one is a dense, layered architecture of translucent fronds and knotted stems that look half vegetable, half slow crystal. They grow in wide isolation from one another, dozens of paces of empty floor between them, so that the eye travels far before it finds the next green-black silhouette. Their surfaces drink the dim wall-glow and return it as a softer moss-light, bluish at the tips. Nothing wilts. Nothing advances. The fronds hold the exact same attitude they held when the pocket first finished cooling; the place has already reached its equilibrium and intends to keep it.

Sound never rests. It is loud enough that you feel it in the teeth. The walls themselves sing—a sustained, refracted chord produced by heat stress locked forever into the crystalline grain—and the fauna add their parts without variation. There are many of them. Soft, moth-bodied things with mica wings clog the higher air in loose constellations, clicking on a single high note. Lower down, stylus-thin lizards of polished basalt walk deliberate paths between the plant-stalks, their feet striking little anvil notes from the glass floor. Something heavier and many-legged keeps to the shadows at the base of the largest growths and breathes out a low, wet-sounding rasp that is not wet at all. The chorus does not swell or fade. It is the same measure, the same volume, the same stacking of tones, whether you stand still for a minute or a year. Time, here, has nowhere to go.

Scattered among the open spaces are the marks of hands. Not many. A ring of fist-sized crystals stacked into a knee-high cairn. A few bladelets of flaked green stone left as if someone meant to come back for them. Against the far curve of the wall, a shallow lean-to woven from the fibrous cast-off skins of the taller plants; inside it a sleeping hollow scraped neat and a single tool—a haft of dense root-mineral bound with dried tendon-stuff to a cutting edge. The craft is careful and early. Whoever made these things understood fire only as the warmth already present in the rock, metal not at all. The objects show no dust, no decay. In a room that refuses change, even abandonment looks fresh.

Light stays dim by choice or by law. What illumination there is comes from the walls’ own slow ember and from the cooler phosphorescence of the plant-tips. Shadows are long and soft-edged. No shaft connects this pocket to any sky; if the surface world still turns above, the news has not arrived. The warmth is constant, a dry cross between fever and hearth. You could map every temperature with your palm and find no gradient worth mentioning. The air does not stir. The fronds do not tremble except when a mica-moth lands and then they settle again into their original angle, as if embarrassed by the interruption.

There is a strangeness under all of it that the eye keeps trying to name and failing. Distances inside the pocket sometimes feel longer when walked than when looked at. The plant-shadows fall in directions the light does not fully justify. Once in a while the chord in the walls acquires a momentary overtone that is almost a voice, almost a word, then folds back into pure tone so cleanly you cannot swear it happened. The fauna never leave and never arrive; they simply continue, abundant yet never crowding, as if the emptiness between them were part of their bodies. The little lean-to waits with the patience of a place that has already forgotten the difference between occupancy and vacancy.

This is the whole of it: a sealed, freshly broken chamber under warm rock, dry as orthodoxy, green where green should be impossible, loud with a song that will not develop, populated and marked by small intelligences that work in stone and fiber, and held in a stillness so perfect it reads as permanence. Nothing here is going anywhere. The pocket has finished becoming itself and is now content, forever, to remain exactly this loud, exactly this sparse, exactly this strange.
