The pocket is no larger than a cathedral nave, though no one built it and nothing like a cathedral will ever stand here. It sits high on the shoulder of a young massif still lifting itself out of the plain, a sealed blister of stone only recently cracked open to its own interior. Outside, the altitude is already thin and merciless; inside, the air is thicker only with heat.

There is almost no free water. What little moisture exists hangs as a mineral haze, floored instantly into the porous walls as salt lace and gypsum bloom. Surfaces flake and re-grow in the same slow breath. The rock itself is still deciding its final texture: surfaces have the dull shine of skin that has only just begun to scar. Pillars of basalt and dark glass rise and stop halfway to the roof, unfinished. Fresh fractures still weep pale dust. The place is under construction by processes that do not hurry.

Heat soaks every surface. It radiates from below more than above, a buried engine that never quite cools. In the near-dark the air receives a dull orange freckling only where vents breathe; elsewhere the pocket holds a thick, rust-brown gloom that the eye must push through. Nothing green has ever tried to start here. The chemistry is wrong, the calendar too short.

What fills the volume is fauna—so much of it that the air itself feels occupied. They are small, hard-bodied, heat-adapted things that treat the unfinished stone as both warren and resource. They pack every crevice, every half-formed shelf, every cooling seam. Bodies click and grate against bodies. Mandibles work continuously at the soft new mineral, scraping, sorting, compacting. The sound is industrial without industry: a layered roar of grinding, milling, conveyor-belt chatter produced by thousands of identical labors. It never peaks and never rests. Over hours the pitch shifts only by the slightest interval, the way a factory floor might change shift without ever falling silent.

Their migrations across the chamber take place on the scale of days. A dense carpet of them will occupy one cooling face until the heat gradient tipps, then move as a single slow weather-front to the next. In their wake they leave polished tracks and tiny spires of processed grit that the next geological pulse will either bury or anneal into the wall. Nothing they make lasts as artifact; everything returns to substrate. There are no tools left lying about, no chambers enlarged beyond the needs of the hour. The density is total—there is scarcely a hand’s breadth of unclaimed surface—yet the arrangement remains provisional, like scaffolding that keeps being taken down and rebuilt a finger-width to the left.

Light arrives only as a slow leakage through hairline joints high in the roof, diluted immediately into the brown dark. Shadows do not fall so much as thicken. Somewhere deeper beneath the floor the buried heat-engine continues its patient cycle: evaporate, deposit, crack, heal. The fauna listen with their whole bodies; the volume of their work rises and falls with that subsurface rhythm, a call-and-response measured in weeks rather than minutes.

This is not a landscape that frames a view. It is a single held breath of stone and heat and bodies, sealed against the thinner world outside, still deciding whether it will become a lasting chamber or simply close again and keep rising.
