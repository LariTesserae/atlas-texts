The basin has no name that holds. It sits low, a broad concavity recently pulled open in the crust, its edges still flaking in thin glassy sheets that have not yet learned to round. Everything here is young in the geological sense: fractures clean, angles unsoftened, surfaces retaining the bright skin of fresh break. Water does not so much fall as continually arrive. It beads from the air itself, a cool condensing breath that never quite becomes rain and never quite stops. The rocks weep. Every face of every slab and blade and honeycomb issues a slow bright film that gathers, thickens, and threads downward into shallow pools whose bottoms are only more of the same fractured mineral, layered so tightly that depth is an argument rather than a measurement.

There is no green. There is no brown of leaf-mold or the black of true soil. The palette is wet pewter, night-blue quartz, the pale sulfur of new crystals still deciding their habit, and a milky translucence where silica gel has not finished hardening. What grows here grows as mineral. Dense forests of blade-thin selenite and dogtooth calcite rise shoulder to shoulder, their points almost touching, so that walking between them (if walking is possible) means turning sideways and feeling cold edges score the air beside your ribs. In the thicker stands the crystals have anastomosed, fused into cavernous walls perforated by smooth weeping tubes. Sound moves through this density like a living thing. Every droplet’s impact is multiplied, handed from facet to facet, until the entire basin carries a continuous bright clatter, a glassy applause that never climaxes and never rests. Under it runs a deeper tone: the slow flex of the young crust, a sub-audible groan that occasionally surfaces as a sudden crack, loud as a tree splitting, though nothing here was ever wood.

Light is a dim pewter weight. The sky, if it can be called that, is a low ceiling of suspended water and stone dust; illumination seeps rather than falls. Shadows are soft-edged and permanent. In this half-light the crystals perform small treacheries of reflection: a distant spire appears close, a near pool opens into false depth, and sometimes a plane of cleavage catches the dimness and throws it back as a brief, soundless flash, like lightning remembered rather than seen.

Scattered through the densest stands are marks that do not quite belong to the geology. Low rings of fitted stone, each block identical in size and beveled with unnerving patience. Single lintels spanning gaps between crystal clusters, their undersides polished by nothing that lives here now. A narrow staircase of nine steps that begins in a pool and ends against a sheer face, leading nowhere. These artifacts are few, worn not by weather but by a kind of absent attention, as if the hands that set them had already begun to forget the purpose. No tool marks remain sharp; the tech that made them has withdrawn completely, leaving only the mute geometry.

Fauna is a rumor with occasional proof. Once in a long while a pale, many-jointed something uncoils from a water tube, tastes the air with feathery mouthparts that look like frost feathers, and withdraws. Larger shapes sometimes shoulder through the crystal forests at the edge of hearing—slow, heavy, their passage marked by a temporary silencing of the droplet-clatter as water redistributes across their backs. They leave no tracks that last; the weeping erases. The place does not seem predatory so much as indifferent. Its hunger is mineral and patient.

Change happens, but on cradles of time. A crystal lengthens by the thickness of a fingernail across what would be a season elsewhere. A pool rises, overflows its sharp lip, and begins to cut a new channel that will take centuries to matter. The loudness itself is constant only in the aggregate; each individual sound is a tiny, finite event spreading into the next. The overall impression is of a single held chord played on something vast and brittle, sustained by innumerable microscopic hammers. Stand still long enough and the chord enters the body. The coolness settles behind the eyes. The density of dripping faces presses close. The open scale of the basin—its far walls lost in mist, its floor a repeating argument of pool and spire and pool—begins to feel like an interior. You are inside something that is also endless. The young rocks continue to weep. The dim air continues to condense. Somewhere a fresh fracture cries out once, sharply, and is absorbed into the greater sound.
