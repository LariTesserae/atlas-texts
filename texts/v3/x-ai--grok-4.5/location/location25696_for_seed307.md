The pocket reveals itself only when the climb has already become impossible—when the black serrated fin of rock has risen so abruptly from the greater massif that the world below is a vertical rumor, and the sky is a hard pale lid. You do not find the place by searching; the mountain simply opens a vertical wound the width of a body and half again as deep, and the heat pours out like breath that never cools.

It is a chimney no taller than a standing person is long, a raw vertical alcove still shining with the glass of its own birth. The rock is young: every edge is a fresh fracture plane, unsoftened, still ringing faintly if struck. Basalt and something darker, almost vitreous, fold around one another in knife-edged shelves and overhangs so sharp they seem unfinished. Water does not fall here so much as weep. It beads from every plane, forced out by internal pressure, skating downward in thin, scalding sheets that flash to steam before they can gather. The air is a wet oven. Mist does not drift; it condenses on skin and stone in the same instant, re-evaporates, condenses again—an endless private weather small enough to cup in two hands.

There is no green. Nothing photosynthetic has ever claimed this cut. The only soft textures are mineral crusts—sintered white, sulfur-yellow, a bruised violet iridescence where metals have plated themselves out of the vapor. In the deeper recesses the crusts thicken into spongy mineral foam that crumples under a fingertip with a sound like distant paper.

And yet the pocket crawls.

Fauna is not an occasional presence; it is the density of the place. Bodies occupy nearly every surface that is not actively jetting steam. They are small—knuckle-sized, palm-sized, rarely larger—and they layer. Translucent, heat-blackened, many-legged things that look poured rather than grown press against one another in living pavements. Some are flat as lichens and cling by dissolving microscopic holds into the glass. Others lift free on feverish legs, antennae or feeding fans shivering through the wet air without ever quite touching. A few hang inverted from the roof on threads of their own cooled exudate, swaying in the updrafts like living pendulums. Their colors are the colors of the stone and the steam: gunmetal, amber, the dull red of overheated iron. When they move it is with a continuous, almost liquid rearrangement, individuals flowing past and through the spaces left by others so that the walls themselves seem to respire.

They make almost no sound. The overwhelming life of the pocket is conducted in near-silence. What you hear is only the mountain’s own quiet vocabulary: the tick of cooling glass, the soft tearing whisper of water turning to vapor, an occasional deeper knock as some internal pressure adjusts far below. The creatures’ millions of limbs and plates slide with macroscopic quiet, as though friction itself has been negotiated away.

Heat is not uniform. Threads of superheated vapor braid through cooler eddies; step half a body-length and the air shifts from merely scalding to a temperature that blurs vision. In those hotter veins the fauna thins, or changes. Bodies become more angular, more crystalline, edges prisming the dim normal daylight that filters from the slit above. Sometimes a creature at the boundary simply ceases to occupy successive positions and is instead present in two places at once for a heartbeat, then collapses back into single occupancy farther along. Depth becomes unreliable. A recess that should be shallow opens, while you watch, into a throat of repeated identical alcoves stacked like reflections, each occupied by the same dense carpet of life, each weeping the same water. Look away and the recursion folds shut again, leaving only the original tight space and the sense that the mountain is thinner than it ought to be, or thicker, or folded through itself.

Occasional larger shapes move through the press—predators or molts or something less classifiable. They do not walk so much as shoulder the smaller bodies aside in slow surges. One such form, long as a forearm and segmented like cooled lava, pauses at the rim of a steaming vent and unfolds mouthparts that are also geomantic instruments: simple wedges of the same young glass, held and angled with deliberate care. It scrapes a fresh edge against stone, tastes the resulting powder, then moves on. There are no workshops here, no caches, no lasting marks beyond the momentary bright scars that the heat and the weeping water begin at once to anneal. Whatever intelligence or instinct guides the gesture remains as unbuilt as the pocket itself.

Light enters cleanly enough from the slot above—ordinary daylight, neither dim nor searing—yet inside the chamber it behaves with mild treason. Shadows fall in directions that ignore the single aperture. A bright mote will sometimes detach from a sunlit edge and drift against the updraft, casting its own small umbra before winking out. Colors bleed at the periphery of vision: the violet mineral sheen leaks a few millimeters into adjacent air and hangs there like a stain that has not decided whether to be matter or merely wavelength.

Time inside the cut has a pulse. Cycles establish themselves without clocks. A vent that has unloaded its mist for a long minute suddenly quiets; the fauna that had parted around its heat-flow contracts inward again, sealing the scar. Elsewhere a fresh fracture clinks open with a sound almost too soft to credit, and new water begins its scalding descent, drawing a dark rivulet of creatures after it like iron filings. Breathing becomes a negotiation with air that is too hot, too wet, too thin from altitude and too thick from vapor. The body understands, without language, that this is not a place one remains. It is a momentary aperture in an extreme elevation still deciding its own shape, an intimate furnace where stone is young, water is constant, life is a silent crushing density, and the ordinary rules of location and succession have begun, politely but unmistakably, to loosen.
