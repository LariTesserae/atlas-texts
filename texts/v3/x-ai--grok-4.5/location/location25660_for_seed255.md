The lowlands here are not land at all, not in any older sense. What stretches outward in every direction is a single continuous fabrication: a regional expanse of fitted and stacked and packed emanations, hand-wrought from whatever could be broken, ground, fire-hardened, or pressed into shape. There is no substrate beneath it. Dig and you only find earlier courses of the same work—older bricks, denser ramming, bones set as reinforcement, plates of simple hammered copper gone green-black in the damp. The whole plain, valley-wide and farther, is artifact. It has never been otherwise.

Cool air moves through it in thick, slow sheets. Moisture does not fall so much as condense and migrate. It beads on every underside, gathers in the shallow runnels cut between courses, drips with a brass-bright regularity into cisterns and overflow lips and deliberate steeps. Occasional heavier rains arrive as sudden vertical curtains that last only minutes; afterward the entire fabric steams and glistens, dark stone and ceramic drinking what they can before the surplus is led away in carved gutters no wider than a forearm. Pockets of standing water form in the lower courts and the half-roofed basins, black mirrors that rarely reflect anything because there is so little light to borrow.

Light itself is a scarce visitor. What illumination exists seeps rather than shines: a mineral phosphorescence trapped in certain fired clays, the dull orange of rare open hearths, the occasional oily smear of fat-lamps carried by whoever still moves here. Most volumes remain in layered darkness—grays stacked on deeper grays, edges known more by touch and echo than sight. The eye adjusts, or it does not; either way the place keeps its permanent dusk.

No plants root or climb. The chemistry of the place refuses them, or the builders long ago purged every spore. In the persistent damp one finds only the simplest films—mineral gels, colorless smears that thicken on north faces and in the permanent shade of overhangs—nothing that photosynthesizes, nothing that flowers or seeds. The ecology, such as it is, is slow chemistry and rarer flesh. Animals appear as abrupt facts: a many-legged scavenger the size of a hand clicking across a wet ledge, a pale worm-thing uncoiling from a weep-hole, once in a long while something larger and cavern-bodied that drags itself through the wider corridors leaving a trail of cooler air. They are infrequent enough that each sighting feels like an intrusion into the true business of the place, which is mineral, manual, and endless.

That business is loud. Sound saturates the dense packing of walls and vaults and half-levels. Water never stops its percussion—single drops striking cistern lips, thin sheets sliding down leaning faces, sudden gurgles as levels equalize through buried channels. Stone settles with deep granular complaints; courses shift half a finger’s width and the grinding travels farther than any shout. Wind finds every unsealed joint and turns it into a reed or a flute or a low continuous moan. And under and through all of this runs the work itself: the wet slap of fresh clay being beaten into molds, the rhythmic knock of stone on stone, the dry rasp of abrasion, voices calling measurements or warnings in short, practiced phrases. The fabric is still coming into being. Edges remain sharp where they have not yet been handled smooth. Dust of pulverized brick and stone flour hangs in the cooler pockets and coats the tongue. Whole districts feel embryonic—walls raised only to shoulder height, timber (rare, scavenged, precious) still bracing unfinished arches, geometrical figures scratched into floors as guides for the next lifting.

Density makes the openness paradoxical. You can see a long way when the gloom thins, across roof-terraces and the upper lips of courtyards, yet every volume is near-touching the next. Passages braid, stack, double back. One person’s ceiling is another’s floor a half-level away; sound and moisture and scarce heat migrate through shared masonry. The scale is regional—walk a day and the character remains the same even as local patterns of vault and channel invent variations—but it never thins into wilderness or empties into horizon. There is always another wall, another collected darkness, another place where water is being taught to run.

The strangeness is quiet and structural. Angles that should close sometimes refuse; a corridor walked twice yields different lengths. Certain courts collect cold the way others collect water, becoming sinkholes of temperature that numb the hands in minutes. Sounds arrive from directions that have no openings. In the deepest damp, the simplest films sometimes arrange themselves into temporary raised script or maps that dissolve when touched. No one claims to have made them. The builders—those who still raise and repair—treat such occurrences the way they treat rare animals: noted, stepped around, left outside the pattern of the work.

Everything here remains under the hand. Simple levers, mauls, wooden forms, fire, the weight of many bodies moving in sequence. No engines, no distant power. The place grows and maintains itself only at the speed of muscle and daylight borrowed from the phosphors and the lamps. It is always becoming, never finished, a total made-environment whose rawness has not yet been worn away by centuries. In the cool dark, under the continuous loud conversation of water and stone and labor, the lowlands keep extending themselves—one pressed brick, one fitted slab, one careful channel at a time—across a world that contains nothing else.
