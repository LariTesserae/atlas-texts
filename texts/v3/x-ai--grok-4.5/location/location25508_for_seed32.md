The place is no larger than a room that has forgotten it ever needed a door. You do not enter so much as find yourself already inside it, the way a grain finds itself inside a closed fist of stone. There is no outside from which to approach; the pocket simply is, low and complete, pressed into whatever larger darkness surrounds it and offering no seams.

Everything here has been made. Not assembled—there are no tools, never were—but secreted, precipitated, stacked molecule against molecule until the volume filled. Pale mineral walls meet at angles that feel decided. Naves narrower than a forearm, vaults that finish an inch above the crown of a head, colonnades of hexagonal prisms fused side to side so that no gap remains larger than a hair. The architecture is total: floor is ceiling’s mirror after five generations of slow accretion; every surface is load-bearing; every emptiness is shaped rather than left. There is no bedrock, no original ground. Substrate was used up long ago, converted into the structure itself.

It is dry with a dryness older than thirst. Salt blooms in microscopic roses along the joints, never growing, never shedding. The air holds nothing that could condense. Coolness sits in the hollows the way light sits in glass—unmoving, evenly distributed, neither rising nor pooling. Brightness arrives from everywhere and nowhere: the mineral itself is translucent to a depth of several thumbs, and a white radiance occupies it the way water might occupy sponge if water were light. No shadows lengthen. No faces darken. The light simply is, high and constant, turning every edge into a thin blade of glare.

Sound fills the remaining volume so thoroughly that silence has been crowded out. It is not wind—there is none—and not machinery. It is the standing resonance of the entire packed mass, a chord locked into the lattice at some forgotten frequency and left to ring forever. Loud, close, bone-conducted. It does not swell or fade. It does not travel. It simply occupies, the way the walls occupy.

Vegetation is an afterthought of chemistry. In the tightest seams, where two faces failed to fuse completely, a few colorless filaments thread outward—more mineral than plant, stiff, unbranching, never taller than a fingernail. They do not seek light; light is already total. They do not drink; there is nothing to drink. They simply persist, sparse as mistakes that were allowed to remain.

No animal has ever lived here. The density leaves no corridor wide enough for even a mite’s pilgrimage. Nothing eats, nothing dies, nothing turns the dry surfaces into soil. Equilibrium was reached and then polished by nothing more than the passage of a time that has no weather. Edges are softly worn where crystal once rubbed crystal in the last microscopic settling. After that, stillness absolute.

The weirdness is mild and architectural: corridors that terminate in perfect mirrors of themselves, stair-forms that climb three steps and become ceiling, chambers whose volume feels larger from the inside than the outside geometry should allow. Mild contradictions, held without strain, because nothing here is required to make sense to a traveler. There are no travelers. There is only the place, packed full of its own finished intention, bright and loud and cool and dry, sealed against every future change.
