The plateau was stamped into existence all at once, or nearly so. That is the oldest story the place tells about itself, though no one remaining can confirm the date. What stands now is a single continuous fabrication the size of a highland valley system: a mid-range country of riveted decks, cold-rolled ridgelines, and vaulted service trenches, all suspended several thousand metres above whatever substrate once lay beneath. The original ground has long since been plated over or dissolved into feedstock. There is no unworked stone here. Every slope is a formed panel, every summit a capped pressure dome or an unfinished gantry. The edges of things are still crisp. Tables of metal drop away at true right angles. Fresh cut-marks show where giant shears parted a bulkhead last season or last century; the difference is hard to feel. Erosion, such as it is, proceeds by deliberate human-scale operations—panel replacement, stress-fracture trimming, the slow unbolting of failed spans—rather than by wind or ice. The landscape therefore keeps the look of something only recently broken free from its moulds.

It is cool in the way of high industrial interiors left unheated. Breath condenses. Moisture does not fall often as rain; instead it beads continuously on the undersides of catwalks and the leeward faces of cooling towers, then drips in irregular curtains that never quite become streams. The water gathers in shallow stamped basins and in the corrugated gutters that serve as rivers. These channels is rarely more than damp; they shine under the lights and give off a faint mineral smell of rust inhibitor and algae that has learned to colonise glycol residues. Vegetation is sparse and almost entirely opportunistic: mats of blue-black adhesive lichen along expansion joints, a few stands of brittle, aluminium-tolerant scrub that roots in the grit of worn brake linings, and long pale streamers of filter-floss moss that trail from overhead pipe racks whenever the air hangs still. Nothing forms a canopy. Growth is measured in decades.

Light arrives from every direction at once. The sky itself—if the term still applies—is a luminous overcast of permanent sodium-mercury glare reflected and re-reflected by the polished upper works. There are no true shadows, only cooler gradients. At ground level the illumination has the flat brightness of a foundry floor at full shift, yet the sound never rises to match. Engines turn, but slowly. Most of the plant operates on long-cycle maintenance modes: compressors that inhale once every few minutes, conveyor belts that advance a single pallet in the course of an afternoon, cooling fans whose blades can be watched as they rotate. The prevailing acoustic is a low, almost sub-audible thrum threaded with the occasional drip or the soft scrape of something living moving across grating. Conversations, when they occur, are held in ordinary voices and carry surprising distances.

Density is the organising principle. Structures stand shoulder to shoulder and then stack above themselves. Stair towers rise through seven and eight stacked process halls. Pipe racks cross and recross until the air becomes a three-dimensional diagram of flow. Walkways lace through the gaps at every elevation; some are original design, many are spontaneous accretions of decking welded into place by whoever needed a shortcut. The same spatial saturation applies to the living. Fauna is abundant and thoroughly at home. Colonies of pale, armour-plated insects nest inside junction boxes, feeding on dielectric grease. Flocks of angular birds with turbine-blade primaries roost in the mouths of decommissioned flues and launch themselves in near-silence across the open reaches. Larger things move in the deeper trenches: slow-bodied grazers that scrape biofilm from the walls with keratinised shovels, and lean, jointed predators that specially favour the stairwells. There are also the assemblers—semi-autonomous maintenance frames that have slipped their original programming and now wander the decks rearranging struts according to private, multi-year aesthetic logics. None of these populations is sparse. They pack the available volume the way the pipes do, near-touching, layered, thick enough that a long view across the valley always contains motion even when the machinery itself is nearly still.

The weirdness is not an overlay; it is a property of the density and the completeness of the build. At certain interchanges the geometry simply fails to remain Euclidean. A corridor cut between two reactor shells may measure thirty metres from outside and three hundred from within, the extra length appearing as repeated, slightly variant sections of the same bulkhead art. Stairs ascend to landings that open onto the same stairs again, or onto catwalks that can be seen, from a distance, to be already occupied by one’s own approaching figure. Time dilates around the larger heat-exchange batteries: workers who step inside to inspect a valve sometimes return having aged only hours while the surrounding decks have advanced a full maintenance cycle. Objects forget their materials. A handrail under the palm will momentarily feel like packed earth or warm hide before it remembers it is extruded alloy. These breakdowns are patient. They do not shatter the environment; they accumulate the way condensation does—quietly, layer upon layer—until whole districts operate under local physical consensus that differs, gently but irrevocably, from the consensus a kilometre away. Navigation therefore relies less on maps than on repeated traversal and the oral transmission of which spans are currently trustworthy.

Across longer spans the entire highland continues to grow, though the process is almost geological in its pace. Outlying fabrication halls still extrude new decking at the margins, bolting fresh territory onto the hanging edges of the world. Older central sectors are slowly dismantled and returned to melt for re-use. The cycle takes generations. No one hurries it. The prevailing mood of the place is one of unfinished permanence: everything is clearly made, nothing is entirely final, and the quiet bright air keeps circulating through the same valves and lungs, year upon uncounted year, while the fauna rearrange the grit and the moisture continues its intermittent silver pilgrimage down the cool metal faces.
