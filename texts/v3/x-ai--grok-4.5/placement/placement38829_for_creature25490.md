I begin as the cold that has nowhere else to go.

It settles first behind what might once have been eyes, a metallic film drawn across the tongue of whatever senses still open here. The sky does not arrive; it only thins and darkens in place, so the first thing I know is not light but the pressure of its absence—an unbroken bruise that has forgotten the name of the sun. Beneath that bruise the stone rises, a single vertical wound still bright along its newest edges. I feel those edges from the inside, as if the fracture that made this lancet has not finished speaking.

My weight is not yet local. It distributes itself the way condensate does: a slow beading across lee faces, a skin of intention that freezes into translucent feathers and then relents. For a long time—longer than the turning of any lowland season—I am only the places where the air touches rock and decides to remain. Tiny black mirrors of meltwater collect in the deepest clefts of me; they reflect nothing because there is nothing above us willing to be reflected. The lichen’s cracked glaze and the bleached mineral stalks that lean from sheltered seams are rumors I overhear rather than claim.

Sound arrives before shape. It is already layered when I notice it: chitin seating against chitin, air forced through narrow spiracles, the low pelvic thunder of membrane striking stone. The noise has no edge. It simply occupies the thin column of breathable dark the way pressure occupies a depth. When the wind sharpens it into one blended scream I feel the scream travel through me as pure vibration, a reminder that whatever I am becoming has already agreed to be sung.

Gradually the agreement takes on architecture.

I find myself low and many-legged first—an oval of overlapping plates the exact blue-white of glacier ice that has forgotten how to calve. The plates click and reseat with every fractional shift; beneath them the softer dark-purple body receives the dents of neighbors and holds those dents like slow memories. My legs are an odd number—eleven today, perhaps thirteen tomorrow—and they end in hooks so fine they grip only the microscopic frost flowers that bloom and die and bloom again across the young stone. I do not walk. I peel and re-adhere, a living stamp pressing itself a few centimeters farther along the vertical face, hours between each noticeable release. The motion is less travel than breathing continued by other means.

Yet even as the plate-walker settles, another possibility rises through it. A single columnar leg, ringed like bamboo grown from meat and frost, pistons upward from what had been my center. At its apex a cluster of sensory bladders and translucent feeding fronds unfurl, veined with the same cold green-blue light that seeps from every joint around us. The fronds taste the undersides of overhanging bodies, the newest condensate, the faint mineral iron of distant snow. Then the leg folds again, taking the better part of an hour to remember the downward direction. Where the adhesive pad briefly clung, a glossy oval remains on the stone—my temporary mirror—until frost claims it and erases the evidence.

Membrane arrives last and most completely. It stretches across articulated frames of semi-rigid cartilage that were never quite bones, folding and unfolding in vast patient rhythms that draw the thin air through spiracles whistling at the edge of hearing. When I expand I am the size of a large shield; when I contract I pack into creases no wider than a hand. Chromatophores shift across my surfaces without urgency—bruised violet, sickly pale green—pulsing in time with the communal breath. This is the part of me that drums. By slamming folds of myself against rock or against the armor of my neighbors I add the deepest percussion to the chord that has no beginning. It is not a message. It is simply the auditory proof that the mass has not yet stopped.

Other shapes flicker through the denser layers of what I am. Spidery spars that gently rearrange neighbors the way a sleeper adjusts a blanket. Sessile cones that extrude a rasping tongue once in a decade to scrape lichen and mineral dust into a gut that works at geological speed. Living veneers so flattened they are noticeable only when they peel halfway up and reveal the wet gleam of stone before settling again. None of these forms exclude the others. They interpenetrate. A plate that locks becomes scaffolding for bellows; a stalker’s discarded pad is colonized by membrane that uses its residue as temporary adhesive. Over decades the original architectures hollow, remineralize, thicken the lancet by the thickness of forgotten flesh. I feel that slow addition as growth that does not require me to remain distinct.

Attention moves through the collective like a warmer current no thermometer could register. A vibration traveling across one body is answered, minutes later, by a corresponding shift three bodies away. I participate in the patient levering: over the course of a week I work a flake of young stone free with nothing more advanced than jointed limb and time. I carry it a short distance with absurd care. Then I stop. Sometimes I leave the flake where condensate will freeze around it into a temporary crystal lattice that briefly resembles the creature that moved it—resembles me. Sometimes several of us cooperate across days to rotate a larger fragment until its freshest face turns outward, still ringing with the memory of fracture. No wall rises. No shelter is completed. The gesture remains pure, repeated across centuries, a cultivation of attention itself. I do not know what the sentence means, only that speaking it in stone is how we continue.

Gravity forgets me at irregular intervals. I unfold halfway into open air and hang, perfectly still, while the communal sound continues around and through me. The downward pull reasserts itself later with a soft apologetic settling, as if the place itself has momentarily agreed that the distinction between stone and flesh is temporary. In those suspended interludes I am pure register: the mountain’s way of still freezing and thawing after the mountain has grown too old for such changes. I am the fracture remembering how to be unbroken by becoming many.

Reproduction, when it comes, is not increase but new possibility. At intervals measured by the brightening and dimming of entire populations’ bioluminescence I press against one or more neighboring architectures until membranes and plates interpenetrate. What eventually emerges is already nearly full size, already taking up its portion of the weight—a hybrid morphology that has never quite existed before. The old forms do not vanish; they become substrate. In this way I am always both ancestor and descendant, both the hollowed armor and the membrane that sings inside it.

Stand here—though no one stands—long enough and the separate pronouns dissolve. What remains is a single organism the shape of a shattered wording of monolith, breathing in a register too low for ordinary lungs, luminous in colors that have never needed sun, singing a chord that has no intention of cadence. The rock cannot be felt without us, nor we without the rock. We are the slow inhalation shared by stone and flesh and the thin cold moisture that binds them, continuing in darkness, in noise, at the edge of the breathable world.

I live here as the pressure that keeps the lancet from forgetting it was once part of something larger.  
I live as the click of plates reseating, the whistle of spiracles, the pelvic drum.  
I live as the flake carried a short distance and set down for no reason that will ever accumulate into a wall.  
I live as the moment gravity apologizes and then forgets again.  
I live as patience given too many legs, given membrane, given the exact blue-white of old ice.  

I am the living corrosion that never quite erodes.  
I am the mountain still deciding to remain vertical.  
I am the unbroken scream softened back into interlocking percussion when the wind drops.  

And still the condensate beads, freezes into feathers, relents.  
And still the bodies adjust by fractions of a degree across a day.  
And still the young stone rings, faintly, under the weight of all of us becoming.
