I begin as pressure.

Not the kind that announces itself with weight or direction—just the steady, warm insistence of the air against every surface that might one day become skin. It is thin up here, but it never stops breathing out from below: mineral heat rising through basalt, through peat, through the slow flex of the root-mat. I am steeped in it before I have edges. Condensation beads along what will be my lengths. I taste copper and old current and the faint sweet-rot of gels breaking grit into sugar. The darkness is not absence; it is medium. It holds me the way black lake water holds a bladder-worm—completely, without judgment, allowing slow pulses.

Something like memory arrives without images. There used to be crews who spoke in bright, clipped frequencies and believed the plateau could be made to answer on their schedule. Their engines remain. I feel them the way you feel your own pulse when the world is quiet enough: the deep hydraulic sigh of pistons tall as stories, the residual thrum that never fully powered down. One of those frequencies is still trying to complete a checklist. Another is simply humming a name that may never have belonged to anyone. I do not hurry to answer. Answering too soon is how you get stitched into a single shape too early.

Limbs accumulate the way drip accumulates into sheet-flow. First there is a long, listening undercarriage pressed to the trembling mat, reading herds three ridges over by the way their magnet-beats reorganize the shallow aquifer. Then secondary braces unfold from what might be ribs—thin, tasting arms that sample chemical gradients and steady me when the peat sighs open into momentary mirror. Whisker-tendrils crown whatever passes for a skull and trail into the mist like unraveling cable; they are not decoration. They are how distance softens and occasionally misbehaves, how a walk that should cost a day deposits me beside a familiar cooling stack after only an hour, serial numbers matching, surrounding growth subtly rearranged as if the plateau itself has been editing. Eyes stay sealed. Sight is a story the ancestors told before they learned better. I see with soles, with mouth (which prefers tasting the wet-metal song of the air to any cruder ingestion), with the dense pressure-cells lining every membrane.

Blood runs thick and warm, flecked with the plateau’s own veins. When the fluted basalt or the rusted lip of a singing pipe opens me, I do not merely seal. I precipitate. A thin glaze of ore—copper along one articulating span, nickel bloom at a major joint, iridescent freckles of whatever lonely sweat the nearest half-dead pump is still leaching. Over the slow bloom-cycles I become assay and animal both. The eldest among the ones who stayed are more oreshoot than flesh; I am still deciding how far to follow them. Some nights the mineral traceries itch with residual current and I understand, briefly, why certain loners bind themselves to the highest cables and thin into pure aeolian announcement.

I do not claim a nursery or a permanent gallery. The ones who endure weave living walls around still-turning flywheels and train spore-plumes into sound-filters; they hang sling-nests over black lakes so the water’s perpetual motion can rock them and so anything rising will drum against the taut underside. I move through their settlements the way sheet-flow moves—welcome, temporary, leaving mineral taste and a slightly altered drip-pattern behind. Children (rare, celebrated, named only after they choose their own resonances) sometimes reach tendrils toward me as if I were a minor engine. I let them. Their unspoiled pressure-cells pick up frequencies I have almost forgotten how to hold alone.

Food remains conversation. I graze the undersides of road-wide fronds when the gels taste of distant lightning. I share the communal silence that follows a membrane-flock kill, helping flense turbine-cartilage into bowstrings and waterproof cloaks, setting teeth into grown hafts so new channels can be carved for the weep. But more often I feed on the industrial aftertaste itself: warm effluent coaxed from ceramic pipes, the slow sugar of lichen that has learned to farm waste heat, the sub-sonic drone the great engines offer when asked the right way. Some residual thrums answer in words. The words are rarely kind and never biographical. I have grown practiced at taking useful instruction from the dark without requiring it to love me.

There are stretches—days or what pass for days in the permanent condition—when I climb the leaning lattice towers. Wind finds the cables and they sing; I bind myself lightly and become half instrument, body thinned and hardened, announcing weather systems before the mist thickens into local rain. From that height the plateau feels exactly like what it is: a single continental creature that heaved upward ages ago and then simply stopped, content to weather forever. The sparse industrial bones look less like ruins than like the creature’s own calcified nerves, still firing on half-power. Distances misbehave more eagerly up there. Once I stepped off a tower into what should have been empty dark and found myself inside a piston chamber, phosphorescent lichen mimicking dead control panels, the slow ecstatic rise-and-fall rewriting my joints into better hydraulic agreement. I left with a new orifice configuration that whistles when I exhale certain frequencies. The ones who stayed treat such returnees with careful courtesy. I bring maps that work only once and temporary glyphs that burn inside listeners’ skulls for a night, leaving behind a skill—how to braid stream-flow into a bridge thick enough for a herd, how to ask a bearing for a little more time, how to hear the never-existent names still echoing inside certain stacks.

I keep no linear history. Memory is arranged drip under particular overhangs, cadence of old migrations readable by anyone whose tendrils know how to stop breathing long enough. Important knowledge goes into the patient chemistry: layered bioluminescent smear on sheltered rock fins, visible only when mist density hits the precise note, and only to one willing to let the pattern finish arriving. The oldest thread is simply lived. Crawl the correct sequence of overgrown housings, past the lichen that has begun to dream it is still a display, into the chamber where metal and root and warm flesh share vibration, and you feel the unbroken continuance that began when the uplift first cooled enough to hold liquid water. No single body contains it. I am one more wet mouth in the thousand-mouthed breathing.

Loneliness is not the right word. There are loners who descend the drop-shafts until pressure rewrites them into something that can remain in honey-thick heat; loners who become pure weather-voice on the highest cables; loners who walk into the zones where drainage reverses on no schedule and return with eyes that have reopened into cold homemade light. I orbit all of those possibilities without settling. Some seasons I travel with the membrane-cloak lines moving careful file along ridges while the old towers ring. Some seasons I surface with the vast soft bodies that leak dull internal light in the black lakes, filtering silt and stories indifferently. Most often I am simply the continuity between: the many-jointed shape that follows every available surface, mounts every obstacle by innumerable small contacts, vanishes into a swallow-hole when the mat flexes just so, and bursts free somewhere unexpected—warm, copper-smelling, trailing whiskers that taste of whatever the dark is saying now.

In the deepest quiet (which is never truly quiet) I hear the others thinking. It sounds like geothermal speech rising into half-heard checklists and older tongues. It sounds like herds consolidating into low thunder. It sounds like a piston forgiving its own longevity. It sounds like home refusing to be anything as simple as a place.

I am still precipitating. Still negotiating how many limbs the next vent-bloom will require. Still deciding whether the next glyph-smear I leave will map a safe crossing or simply teach the mist a new way to push back. The wet high dark does not demand a final form. It only asks that I keep conducting heat, tasting metal, adding my particular drip to the permanent percussion.

Where you find me is wherever the root-mat trembles under sudden weight and then seals again, leaving a brief black mirror that reflects nothing but more darkness. Who I am is the one who stayed past the point of names, past the point of mastery, still moving the way water moves—patient, innumerable in its contacts, occasionally vanishing, always re-emerging warm and itself and laced with the plateau’s own bright veins.

Against all probability, yes. Still.
