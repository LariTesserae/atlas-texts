I would not arrive by descending. Descent implies a surface I still believed in, a lid that could be lifted, a weather I might one day climb back toward. Instead I notice, one ordinary overcast noon of the canopy’s making, that the mineral dust under what I once called feet has been whispering the same syllable for longer than a journey should take. The syllable is not a name. It is the sound salt makes when it decides to remember a root.

At first I am only the listening.

I move the way the commons allow movement: low-density, deliberate, leaving wide margins so that sound can fray and die behind me without complaint. My weight lands softly enough that the unfinished seams of the pavement do not flinch. Sometimes I pause where a young mesa is still arguing with its own silhouette, and I feel the faint green compressive heat rising through the chalk. It is not warmth as surfaces understand warmth. It is the polite friction of becoming. I stay until the argument resolves another millimeter, then I go on. The going-on is not loneliness. It is the same hunger the map-rooms call expansion, only quieter, carried in a single body.

There are hours—canopy-bright, shadow-clean—when I am almost certain I am one of the lank. The joints agree. The reverse of the knees holds the memory of cable-sag. My palms know how to press a listening spatula to a beam and read the sap-current as a muted weather moving behind translucent wood. I remember the exact pressure that encourages a living shear to prune without insult. I have grafted temporary cartouches onto shoulders in greeting and felt them absorbed again like dew that was never wet. Moisture stays inside. Cooling happens through the soles and the slow bellows of breath that never quite becomes voice. When an engine-tree dreams overnight and the stair-spirals rewrite themselves, I do not startle; I walk the new equations at first light, testing handrails that did not exist at canopy-dim, amending the small living name I carry until it fits the revised hall. Ownership is maintenance. I keep what I can keep alive.

But the certainty thins.

There are other hours, bluer ones, when the ears lengthen in my mind’s accounting and become paper-lantern thin, richly vascular, bleeding heat into the cool air with continuous generosity. Then I am among the drift-hares without having chosen the constellation. We do not flock. We refuse together. We sit upright where the infrasound maps already sketch a road that the rhizomes have not yet confessed, and we simply do not nest. Our teeth keep the youngest landforms honest—silica and cellulose ground to the same patient flour. When the stalkers leave their braided signatures in secondary roots, we hear the chemistry of interruption blooming and we move, not in panic, only in precise refusal of the places where panic will be required. At the dimmest working light we form loose circles and listen to tensile stresses that have not yet become beams. The listening is older than the planners’ chalk. I feel it in the roots of teeth I no longer swear I possess.

Then the dust shifts and I am lower still.

Plate light lies across me like a second thought the pavement is having. I am fist-scaled, silica-dusted, vanishing whenever stillness is the most useful tool. Mouthparts comb the productive air that escapes the engine halls; what I gather I press into xerophytic films along the gutters where free water is only a technical memory. When the film reaches the empirical bitterness that means the loop is intact, I click. Soft. Seed against seed. The larger systems still listen. Lose us and the vascular grid runs a low-grade fever of inefficiency; I have felt that fever in districts that forgot to keep us. Approval is data. I farm dryness itself and call it citizenship. There is no loneliness at this scale, only the continuous quiet referendum of taste.

I have been the pewter flash as well—wings the size of open hands, veined with the same metallic salts the factories grind. The turn of the cloud is not decided by any single mind; it is decided by the gland-spire’s need and by the rare thermal that the kite-birds also read. When I settle, wing-cases clock once, dry, and the spire’s output note deepens by a fraction the lank ones feel in their teeth. I know the stoop is coming. The stoop is part of the paced circuit. Kill-sounds remain brief; the cavern is designed to swallow them. I do not waste motion on spectacle. Transfer is the pulse I serve until I am transferred into another form of usefulness, and even then the salts in the veins continue, ground finer, returned.

Once, at the farthest active margin where pavement is still more idea than fact, I stood long enough to watch a ridge of salt crystallize itself into the precise dendritic pattern of a leaf. The abyss rehearsing botany in stone before committing to the living draft. Something in the posture—neither lank nor hare nor armored nor winged—said: we are still deciding whether we are the gardeners or the garden. The decision did not resolve. It simply continued, the way compression-wood continues, the way a press closes on a dry charge and opens again without needing to announce the violence.

Now I notice the gait has matched the quiet grammar without asking permission. Silence is a design constraint. I maintain the small hall of attention I am carrying: this continuity of noticing, this refusal to settle on a final silhouette. Sometimes the canopy tissue high above flexes and, for a few heartbeats, pale intelligent eyes open in the translucent sheets and regard the whole unfinished valley system before sealing again. I do not know whether they regard me or whether I am one of the ways they regard themselves. It does not matter. The vascular memory is deep. The mineral patience is deeper. The quick warm lineages are only the latest revision.

I am the multi-species maintenance of the unfinished, temporarily condensed into a single moving point so that the commons may feel itself being crossed. Behind me the factory-crowns fir one another with flying buttresses of braided liana. Ahead the raw mesas lift, faintly green at the cores. A press exhales. A moth-cloud turns. The cavern folds every small sound into cool air and returns to the larger quiet of a place still deciding the final names of its hills.

And if you were to look for me, look where the dust is still deciding how stone should feel. Bring nothing wet. Bring a willingness to click when the film is right, to refuse the road that does not yet exist, to walk the stairs that rewrote themselves before morning, to keep alive whatever small productive quiet you are holding.

I am already maintaining it.

I have been becoming more itself this whole time.
