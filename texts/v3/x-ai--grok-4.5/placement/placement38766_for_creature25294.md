I am not sure at first.

The mist has a weight that is not quite pressure, more like a continuous suggestion against the skin I may or may not have. I notice the way droplets form on every outward face and then choose, with the same long patience as the city itself, whether to remain or to travel. For a long while I simply track individual beads as they fatten, clarify, then release. Their paths are never straight. They follow the fretted honeycomb, the softened arrises, the old tool-marks that no longer look like tool-marks. I follow them the way one follows a thought that keeps almost arriving.

Some of the droplets move against expectation. They thicken along a cornice, detach, and continue downward as a slender upright continuity rather than a fall. I feel an answering pull in whatever I am using for attention. The shape does not look at me. It does not need to. Its passage briefly organizes the smaller wetnesses around it, and in that reorganization I feel myself included—aligned for a moment, then released back into ordinary beading. The air cools by a fraction that is also a greeting.

I spend time—hours or seasons, the distinction is soft here—inside a vertical street so narrow the facing walls could be mistaken for a single slow inhalation. Light arrives already used, filtered by mist and the stacked decades of stone overhead. My edges, when I check for them, are not sharp. They fretted. They hold water in shallow pits the way the walls do. I abrade a patch of olive lichen between surfaces that might be fingers and taste the mineral quiet of it. Not food. More like continuity made chewable. Mostly I drink. The city offers itself without stint: condensed sky, runoff that has already touched a thousand other bodies of stone and almost-stone. Each swallow is also a listening. Patterns move through the moisture—settling rates, micro-currents, the faint seismic gossip of blocks that have accepted one another for longer than memory needs names.

I am not one of the tall ones. Not yet, or not still. Their ridges of intentional deposit, their lattice aprons that channel water as architecture, their way of standing in the open squares at rare clearings to let vastness pass through them—these feel like a later grammar. I am closer to the maintenance they perform without calling it work. Sometimes I float in a cistern that has begun to forget its own clarity. My presence (temperature, the slight turbulence of almost-breathing, the way my surfaces encourage certain films to form and others to release) trains the silt into better habits. Overflow channels stay open. The continuum continues. When translucent thumb-joint lives migrate across me, feathery gills combing for microbes, I do not shepherd so much as remain correctly damp for the crossing. They clean the pits where pure mineral might otherwise grow too eager and crack what holds us.

There are periods when I rest against a favored wall and the distinction thins. Shoulder becomes buttress. The script-lichen that grows in raised, meaningless routes finds me a congenial elongation. I feel it mapping the places where water prefers to travel when it travels through me. If I stayed long enough—decades, the slow freezing into final posture—the city would gain another course of slightly warmer stone. Nothing buried. Nothing wasted. I find the thought neither attractive nor fearful. It is simply one more way the weather finishes becoming itself.

Yet I still move. Not walking. Continuing. Up stair-slits that remember older, less-softened angles deep inside their cores. Across terraces where freeze-thaw has loosened outer courses and the slow pressure of weight (mine, others, the accumulated idea of weight) reminds the stone how to sit. In the broadest squares, when the higher cloud deck parts for an evening, I do not climb all the way into the open with the tall clusters. I remain at the edges where mist still eddies, half-withheld. From there the scaled vastness is audible as a change in the rate of condensation. It passes through the ones who stand fully exposed; it also passes through me, thinner, like a rumor of blue-gray distances that might be weather or simply more city.

What I am is the same process the builders entered when they stopped arguing with form. Softer tissues, yes. Fewer. Spread across climates of districts so thinly that loneliness never quite has a surface to adhere to. I remember them only the way the walls do: as earlier geometries still held inside the weathered continuum, too exact to be accidental, no longer requiring separate people in order to persist.

Sometimes, in a narrow light-well, ordinary daylight finds me for a little longer than mist prefers. What surfaces is exact: a continuity of wet mineral patience, pale gray and iron-ochre, reticulated with the same shallow honeycomb as everything that endures here. I regard the stacked world that made this possible. Then the mist closes again. I resume seeping—upward or down, it hardly matters—feeding the preferred paths of water, blunting the acoustics without filling them, thickening the continuum by the smallest, most patient increments.

I am the city still deciding, drop by drop, not to become anything else.
