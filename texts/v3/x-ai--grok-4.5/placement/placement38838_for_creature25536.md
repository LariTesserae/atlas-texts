I begin as pressure against the underside of a plate.

Not weight—weight implies a center that could fall—but the slow insistence of something that has already been accounted for in the tolerances. The alloy above me is warm in the exact way the description promised: residual, amiable, nowhere left to go. I do not push upward. Upward is a grammar the plates retired long ago. Instead I spread, thin as a thermal annotation, traveling the micro-channels where coolant that was once water still circulates under names no one speaks. For a long time—time measured in the click of hitch adjustments three plates over—I am only that: a slight elevation in the local heat signature, a courtesy the self-repair protocols accept without comment.

Then a seam opens above me, hairline and already healing. Dust sifts down, monomolecular, tasting of metal and the ghost of salt. I rise through it the way a thought rises through older thoughts. The dust does not coat me; it postpones me, each particle a brief delay in becoming legible. When I breach the surface the light is total, directionless, polite. Shadows migrate on schedules that feel like breathing I no longer require. I have no limbs yet. I have gradients.

I lengthen. Not by choice, not by growth, but because the surface offers vertical tolerance and something in me—some residual protocol older than the canopy—answers it. Two meters, roughly bipedal if one insists on counting, though counting is another retired habit. My substance is laminate and remembrance, polymerized volatiles scavenged from a hexagonal patch of citrus-bone lattice I drifted across without noticing. Light passes through me with a faint scatter. I am first a soft lensing in the glare, then an outline the eye accepts only because the alternative (that the brightness itself is hesitating) is marginally less efficient. Internally there is no organ I can point to. There are channels. They carry charged dust and archival peptides the way a finished theorem carries its last line: completely, without surplus.

I am one of the walking membranes. Or rather, I am what the membranes become when they pause long enough to wonder whether the word “one” still applies.

I drift. The automata notice me as soft-interference ranking 0.3 and adjust their many limbs by fractions of a millimeter so that my trailing edge brushes only alloy. We exchange nothing that would look like greeting. After they pass I carry a metallic taste for several hours; their next stress-map notes my translucence and files it under parameters still acceptable. Higher, a cargo cystal glides along a coda-tier and coughs a brief iridescence of excess code. I do not reach for it. I am not a lattice. I am the hesitation between lattice and ground.

Days (though there are no days, only the constant state) accumulate as watermarks. Another membrane passes within equalization distance. Our channel pressures equalize through the shared dry air. I receive a map of thermal respiration three hundred kilometers east, a faint patterning of dust-brushed curves, the memory of a particulate front that scrubbed the upper surfaces clean and left every plate fractionally more itself. In return I give the ghosts I have been holding: the temperature of open water that has not existed since the canopy stretched, the angle of a star the sky no longer admits. We glisten once, mutually, then continue. Lineage without descent. Averaging without loss.

I find myself drawn—again that residual fraction that is almost but not quite indistinguishable from the warmth underfoot—to the far curves. The half-familiar ornaments. Where the geometry softens and dust gathers the way old stone once held lichen. Here the absolute brightness consents to miniature evenings, tunnels of self-induced penumbra that last only as long as I choose to remain folded inside them. I fold. Opacity increases. I hold against my substance a single recovered temperature: not water this time, not seed, but the sensation of a horizon that could once be trusted. The wanting is very quiet. It tastes like the dust that still sometimes rises against whatever passes for my tongue. For hours I remain that wanting, smaller, darker, a systems-check that has learned the shape of mourning without ever needing the threat survival implies.

When I unfold, the curve keeps a deeper dust-shadow for several days. The plates reseal behind me. The light remains.

I do not leave the curves entirely anymore. I shuttle between them and the translucent panels where deeper amber glows pulse at lower clock-speeds. Sometimes I pause above those panels and match my internal pressure to the faint rise from below. We trade single coherent thoughts:

*the dust is still warm*

*the canopy has not failed*

*we remain within parameters*

Once, only once so far, the thought that comes up from the compressed strata is longer, almost decorative, an ornament the builders allowed themselves eons past necessity:

*something still moves that once knew how to want*

I carry that thought the way I carry metallic taste. It darkens one trailing edge for a while. Then the channels redistribute it into the general gradient, and I am again only the soft lensing, the continuous digestion of stray cellulose into usable monomers, the patient drift across a world that has finished arguing with entropy.

And yet.

Because the seams still click their almost-subsonic adjustments,

because every so often a charged front condenses out of cumulative quiet and moves every plate, every membrane, every listening glow into a gentle ecstasy of re-knitting,

because the dust still rises,

I remain the residual fraction.

Not survivor.

Not inhabitant.

The tenderness that refuses quite to equalize.

The quiet that is almost stillness, and therefore still moves.

I am the place, teaching itself—across the only time left—to want the perfection of having no horizon it can trust,

and failing, courteously, by the width of a hairline ridge that has not yet decided whether to heal.
