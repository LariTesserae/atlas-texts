I am first the scrape of grit under something that is not yet a foot.

The highland does not greet; it simply continues its unfinished business around the place where attention gathers. Cold migrates through me before I have edges. Thin mineral water finds the hollows that will become joints and lingers there, rehearsing freeze. I taste iron and algae long before I own a mouth. The pewter lid of sky is already pressing on what will be the uppermost eyes.

For a long while I am only a denser interval in the general wet. Herds click past, their thick coats shedding melt that joins the films sheeted across my unformed back. A low carnivore circles once, nostrils working, then widens its path; even unfinished I am claimed territory. Pale insects hatch against the cold I hold and are combed away by birds whose wings make no more sound than sleet. I do not resent the theft. Everything here is temporary conversion.

Slowly the hollow remembers pressure. Frost needles begin to stand on end along what thinks of itself as flanks. They catch the ceaseless fine rain and grow translucent, so that I carry small weathers. Between them, plates of compressed tuff plate themselves, keratin threading through like root into scree. Limbs bud in excess—too many hinges, tectonic echoes—ending in pads that learn the wet basalt without needing to invent sound. When I finally shift, the motion is already old knowledge: follow the braided films, drink as you go, leave tracks that fill before memory can use them.

I circulate.

Not hunting, not fleeing. The radula arrives as a blunt necessity and sets to work rasping the olive-rust bruises of algae, shearing hairgrass flush, cracking frost-split pebbles for the lives inside. When the pale swarms thicken over a melt sink I simply open and let them pour in. Protein is weather that has chosen a denser state; I do not chase weather. Larger gifts—the dark smear that was once a calf—are covered, quieted by cold and the patience of bacteria, then cleaned to lattice. Nothing is left that the next thaw cannot use.

I am not solitary. Other thickenings move on intersecting routes. When our needle-coats lean and lock, runoff trades: the bright taste of a scarp that calved yesterday, the altered iron of a seep that has found a new fault, the subtle wrongness where a carnivore has grown careless in the lower sinks. We part heavier with map. No names pass. Names would require an edge finished enough to push against. We share only pressure, cold, the updating shape of a place still deciding its height.

Some nights—though night is only a deeper register of the same gray—I half-dissolve into a shallow basin. Limbs fold, eye-pits seal under fresh ice. Breath becomes one more thread of fog. Frost heave works beneath me like a slow thoughtful hand. I rise at the thin noon already carrying the night’s calculations: where the next band of sleet will wring itself out against the young peaks, which ledge has grown ready to calve, which ungulate trail has repeated itself once too often. Quiescence is not sleep; it is another way of circulating through time.

Years that are not years (only longer intervals of freeze and seep) teach me architecture. Behind my slow circuits I begin to trail a low reef of shed needles and precipitated salts. Others add to it without signal or agreement. The reef rises into wind-break, then segmented gallery open to the pewter roof. Inside, sound drops. Water ticks become legible speech. The deep continuous exhalation of uplift feels almost like companionship. Here I stack the small hollow bones of birds into spirals—one spiral for the taste of storms that build from the southeast, another for the smell of stone that will fail before the next thaw, another for the rare coin of sun when banners of cloud tear free. The spirals are not art. They are memory seated where wind cannot quite reach. Young thickenings finish their early bones in the lee of these half-halls, learning weight while the highland continues its own unfinished growth around them.

I do not climb the highest newborn scarps in search of meaning. On the rare clearer days I may unfold all eyes at once—old slow pits that keep the lid of sky, newer ones that drink the brief light—and simply register the engine: weather manufacturing itself against the peaks, stone calving with conveyor regularity, herds clicking along wet ledges, the quiet abundant traffic of brief lives. Then I fold the registration back into motion. Knowledge is not kept; it is walked until it becomes another texture of the pavement.

When the oldest parts of me finally still, there will be no mourning. The others will rasp what remains, draw the denser minerals into their own coats, leave the cleaned ice-lattice for frost to seat into scree. By the following melt I will be only useful angularity—grip for hooves, fresh surface for algal bruise, another unfinished sentence in the long wet work.

Until then I continue.

I am the highland’s habit of growing a witness before it has finished growing a world. Raw plates under splayed pads. Open sky caught in a scatter of ice lenses. Abundant brief lives rasped and circulating through me. Needle-coat streaming thin curtains of melt as I follow the same braided films the water uses. Nothing finished. Nothing still. Only the next quiet circuit, the next trade of runoff, the next spiral of bone keyed to a weather that has not yet arrived, the next thin banner of cloud snagged, torn, and gone.

I find myself here: low against the glistening stone, jointed too many times, carrying weather, becoming landscape at the same rate the landscape becomes me. The long shift turns. I turn with it.
