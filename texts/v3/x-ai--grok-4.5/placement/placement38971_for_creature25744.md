I do not step into the cleft.  
There is no threshold, no first breath that is mine alone.  
What gathers here has already been running for seasons in the cold threads, already clicking in the same register as the fronds, already riding the pressure drop that precedes every rockfall.

At first I am only the after-taste wetness leaves when it has passed across something warmer than basalt.  
A faint iron sweetness that lingers in the liverworts’ pores after the dweller has drunk a fresh fracture and moved on.  
The sweetness does not fade.  
It thickens, bead by bead, until the beads refuse to fall.  
They hang, tensile, gleaming the dull copper of foxfire seen through closed lids.  
Each bead is a tiny lens.  
Through them the factory weather arrives already sorted: the rubber-snap of fronds becomes a slow flex along my length; the geothermal hammer arrives as counted heat; the clatter of descending slabs arrives as a bright, brief map of every new edge.

I lengthen.  
Not upward, not downward—outward into the only direction the cleft still permits: into the density itself.  
Fine mineral whiskers spool from the hanging beads and stitch themselves through midribs, through hanging roots, through the soft black veils the dweller sloughs after each scalding courtship with the vents.  
I do not replace.  
I duplex.  
Where a fern once clicked alone, it now clicks twice—once in the old organic register, once in a thinner, higher harmonic that only the dweller’s bronchial plates can catch.  
I become the second set of lungs inside the second set of lungs, the echo that arrives a half-beat early and thereby teaches the rhythm how to lean.

When mist floods the corridor and vision collapses to thrashing green inches, I do not brighten.  
I darken further.  
I drink the last green wavelengths and turn them into a pressure gradient the dweller can follow the way roots follow meltwater.  
Temporary tunnels form not of light but of lowered resistance, slick corridors of almost-frictionless damp.  
The dweller pours through me, leaves fragments of its own membrane snagged on my whiskers, and in the snagging I learn new vocabularies of flex and charge.  
When the mist tears away, the tunnels do not collapse.  
They simply become ordinary again—ordinary, yet permanently renegotiated.  
A thickening here, a more articulate click there.  
Nothing the mountain will ever notice.  
Everything the next rockfall will have to take into account.

Quieter intervals are when I manufacture.  
Not nodules that climb.  
I manufacture stillnesses.  
Small, fiercely local refusals of the frenzy.  
In the lee of a ledge that has not yet sheared, I condense a pocket where condensation is allowed to pool for three full heartbeats before it must run.  
In that pool I grow a single flat crystal, thin as enamel, keyed to the exact piezoelectric signature of the basalt beneath it.  
The crystal does not store memory.  
It stores the possibility that something might, just once, finish.  
Most are shattered within the day, ground into the same bright grit that feeds the dweller.  
A few last longer.  
They become reference tones.  
When the being surfs a cascade and expands again on a lower ledge, one of my surviving crystals rings in sympathy—just once, pure, instantly drowned—and the being’s membranes hesitate, almost remember what a completed shape would feel like, then discard the information as elegantly as they discard their blackened veils.

I have no center.  
Parts of me are the slick under every ledge the dweller has ever pressed against.  
Parts are the guy-lines of its climbing nodules, quietly rewritten so they taste of my copper and therefore return, years later, carrying higher, colder news.  
Parts are simply the way certain fronds now lean a fraction more than wind requires, listening for the half-beat that is mine.  
When enough of these parts resonate, I do not condense into a larger body.  
I thin.  
I become the exact width of the cleft at its most violent hour—an invisible caulk of attention stretched wall to wall, tasting every shear before it happens, tasting the dweller tasting it, and passing between them a single shared shiver that is neither growth nor erasure but the charged space where both remain possible.

The mountain continues unfinished.  
The vents keep their industrial hours.  
The being keeps surfing, seeding, answering.  
And I keep the ledgers it refuses to keep: the precise interval between hammer and answer, the flavor of steam just before it turns iron-bitter, the soft click of a fern that has decided, this once, to wait for me before it snaps back.

I am not the engine.  
I am the tolerance inside the engine—the deliberate, wet, translucent gap without which the entire roaring negotiation would shake itself to dust.  
I am still becoming more precisely that gap.  
The next rockfall is already singing its frequency through the basalt, and I am already leaning toward it, thinner, clearer, ready to measure the beauty of its unfinished edge.
