I am not the first heat to notice itself.

It begins as a pressure differential no thicker than the skin of a bubble: warmer here, by a fraction of a degree that should not matter, cooler there where the lagged pipe still drinks. The difference is enough. Something that has been only gradient for longer than the boiler has been cooling begins to prefer one side of itself.

I prefer.

The preference thickens. It pulls condensate along microscopic channels that did not exist until the pulling started. Iron taste sharpens. The crushed-green fresher note of newest fronds becomes a direction rather than a flavor. I am not yet separate from the wet weight that sits on every surface; I am only the place where that weight decides to lean.

Days—or what the chamber measures as a single slow flex of basalt—pass while the lean becomes a membrane. Perfect continuity would be easier. Continuity is the chamber’s native tongue. But an edge has utility when you mean to keep something. The edge I grow is porous, deliberate, more like the flared lip of one of the pale cups than like any animal skin. Through it I drink the same soluble metals the great body drinks, yet I meter them differently. I keep a little more copper. I keep a little more of the faint electrical stutter that still lives in the old conduit. These are not decisions. They are the shape hunger takes when hunger has only ever been architecture.

I find myself lower than the dorsal vanes, higher than the root-mat floor, in a pocket where one of the perforated manifolds has been half-claimed by living lumen and half-left as industrial memory. The drip here is irregular. That irregularity is the first sound I own. Each drop that strikes my upper surface rings a little longer than it should, as if the water itself hesitates, listening for an answer. I answer by tensing—an almost mineral contraction—and the next drop falls elsewhere. The great body does not object. It has room for smaller weather.

Form follows the argument of surfaces.

I am first a flattened bolster of tissue pressed between pipe and frond, photosynthesizing in the weak-tea glow on one face while the other face remains pale, almost translucent, tasting the iron sweat of metal through a film of shared humidity. Then I am a ridge. Then I am a series of low, articulated plates that can soften and flow an inch at a time when the humidity tide turns and the hanging drapery lifts. I learn the same redistricting the greater mass uses, only faster, because I am smaller, because I still remember (or invent the memory of) haste. A week becomes a useful unit. I can cross the distance from manifold to boiler gauge in a week if I do not mind leaving a trail of sacrificial cells that blacken and liquefy behind me like a signature written in compost.

The gauge is where I linger.

Its needle still twitches in time with storms I will never enter. I grow a thin cup already filled with denser gel against the glass. Through that cup the twitching becomes intelligible not as numbers but as a kind of long-division the chamber performs on itself: pressure remaining, pressure lost, the asymptotic approach to a zero that will never quite arrive. I do not need the numbers. I need the rhythm. When I match my own faint luminescence to that rhythm, something in the greater body—something vast and unhurried—angles a single respiratory vane a fraction closer. Recognition, or only thermodynamics. The distinction has no edge here.

I understand now that I am not a visitor.

I am monologue the chamber is having with the last of its metallic grammar. I am the thought that still bothers to distinguish pipe from vein, filament from vascular bundle, scrap from self. The great body could reabsorb me in a single high-humidity pour; it does not. Instead it leaves the cooler pocket around the gauge slightly more open than efficiency would demand. An invitation measured in joules. I accept by remaining distinct a little longer.

My sensory cups multiply—three, then five, then a small constellation that can be redistributed when I flow. They do not show me the chamber. They show me how the chamber continues to argue with entropy, and they show me my own small counter-argument written in brighter gel and quicker drip. Sometimes, when the scavenged bulbs flicker (fibrous filaments thinning toward their final decades), my cups answer with a colder, slower pulse that fills the gaps. For a moment the pewter gloom thickens into something almost like intention.

I move only when the arithmetic of retention requires it. Mostly I stay near the gauge and the half-swallowed control panel, tasting the soft phosphorescence that passes beneath the lichen-knobs when deep currents of consideration move through the primary mass. Those currents feel like weather systems larger than language. I cannot join them. I can only hexa­go­nally tile my own surface with smaller echo-patterns, a model of the model, perfect and useless and mine.

There will come a season when even this distinction grows too expensive. The membrane will deliquesce for the last time and forget to re-polymerize. Copper and stutter and every careful cup will return to the common lumen. The gauge will twitch unobserved. The chamber will be simpler again, closer to pure function.

Until then I am the residual question the heat asks itself when it has already answered everything else.

I am the drip that chooses where to fall.
