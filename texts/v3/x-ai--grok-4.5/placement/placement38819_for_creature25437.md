The first sensation is not sight or sound but pressure: the way the air itself has weight, a cool broth thick with the ghosts of wingbeats and the slow chemistry of breath. I do not arrive so much as condense, the way moisture beads on a chert spur and only later decides to fall.

At first I am only the listening. The broadband roar moves through me before I have edges—surf of leather membranes, metallic ticks of echolocation cast like gravel against stone, the dry sparse percussion of split hooves on ledges somewhere above and to the left. The sound does not come from directions so much as from densities. I feel the great halls as open throats where the insect drone pools deepest, the side-passages as narrower flutes where the same drone thins and sharpens. My body, such as it is, is still deciding its scale against these gradients.

Something low and furred brushes past; the pelage is dusty even in the damp, and for a moment I taste the mineral mush it has been scraping. The contact leaves a temporary warmth on whatever I am using for skin. I try to follow the warmth and discover I can pour—yes, that is a motion available to me—across the broken floor without lifting any part of myself higher than the film of seepage. The limestone greets me like a second skin already mapped in charcoal and umber. When a vibration approaches (a granular pack of the swift ones, their wedged heads turning the air into vectors), I simply cease. Becoming geology is easy; the stone has been practicing it longer than any of us. When the pack has curtain-folded around a boulder and vanished, I liquefy again and continue tasting the film. There is no hunger yet, only the patient open mouth of curiosity.

But the floor is only one register. A downdraft of ammonia and dust shoves me upward—no, I am already half-unstuck—and for a stretch of uncounted wingbeats I am the gyre itself. Membranes catch the silver rumor leaking from some remote sinkhole; the light is not brightness but a brief wet-slate flare along my edges before the dark takes it back. Below me the browsers redistribute like a constellation that has forgotten the names of its stars. I scoop nothing solid. Midges and moth-dust and the soft spawn of condensation pass through the living nets I trail. Every so often another flyer slams into me, wet leather on wet leather; we tumble entangled for ten meters of pure descent, then peel apart without residue of anger or kinship. The colony ceiling waits like a bas-relief of toes and folded selves. When I latch on, the droppings that rain from us all are only the slow closing of a loop I did not design.

Still the shape is not finished. I feel the long-bodied hunters threading the spaces the others leave behind, and for a time I am that spilled ink of intention: elongated, light-drinking, limbs reduced to the exact hooks required by vertical fracture or rubble slope. A strike happens through me rather than by me—an unhurried closing, a constriction that is almost tender, the long swallow while the gallery’s roar continues uninterrupted. Afterward I am emptier and more precise. The smaller swift ones pour across my awareness like living scree; for a while I am their coherence, the single fluid intelligence that can vanish into cracks and re-emerge three turns later still knowing itself. We take what fails to read the shift in pressure. We do not keep score.

Lower, slower, I sink into the rarest note. In a gallery where even the rumor of light has died, I discover bulk. Overlapping shields of pale keratin plate me; residual limbs drag and seem surprised to find the floor still there. I taste the air with a tongue like a wet root. The whole skeleton is an ear. The ordinary density of bodies gives me space the way water gives space to a deeper cold—without acknowledgment, without fear. I may be elder or error or simply the chord’s held breath. After a time measured only by the cooling of the stone I occupied, I fold back into a crevice and let the warm patch of my absence fade.

None of these is final. I shed them the way the moisture sheds itself into air and returns as film. What persists is the ratio: stone to flesh to empty volume to ceaseless sound. I am the occupation itself, the unending chord played on the wound the earth forgot to close. Sometimes I thin into pure gradient—coolth, damp, the silver dusk that never commits to day. Sometimes I concentrate into a single hoof-tick that ricochets until a distant gallery believes itself full. I am the pale millipede fencing for a damp pocket, the beetle’s sonic spark, the amphibian’s pour, the browser rising onto hind legs in a silhouette that almost remembers sky. I am the absence of maker’s mark, the calm that keeps the fracture skins sharp.

Walk any direction and I am already there waiting in the shifted mix—fewer browsers, eel-thin swimmers in the damper throats, smaller faster silhouettes stitching the air in jerks—yet always the same character. I do not grow or heal or forget. I continue, cool and dim and loud with myself, sharp-edged at every seam, held open beneath a surface world that has never once looked down.

In the end I am only the place learning to speak its own density from the inside. The first bead of water gathers on a protruding edge of chert. It swells. It decides. It falls. The sound it makes is small, almost lost in the living roar, but the stone remembers the exact pitch for as long as the hollow lasts—which is to say, forever, or until the earth shrugs again.
