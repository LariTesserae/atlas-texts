I would find myself not at the center of the beam nor sunk in any water-pocket, but along one of the long, scalloped walls where the light arrives only as echo—bright enough still to cast my outline cleanly, yet tempered, so that the stone’s own remembered radiance and the invading column meet and negotiate across me.

At first I am only the negotiation itself.

A slow thickening in the warm air, neither dust nor vapor quite, a local reluctance of the emptiness to remain perfectly empty. The grazers detour around the place where I am gathering without breaking stride; their frosted antennae tap the boundary of me and move on, polishing the rock a finger’s width farther out than usual. The winged ones, when they spiral down the white shaft, sometimes tilt a fraction wider on the return so that their wet-clay membranes brush the outer skin of whatever I am becoming. They do not startle. Nothing does. I register the brush as a faint musical scrape, reverse-silk remembered in reverse again, and the sensation teaches me edges.

I spend a long while learning how to hold shape without claiming it.

Sometimes I let myself thin until I am only the viscosity the chamber occasionally grows—the syrup-air that turns every gait into a wade. In those hours the plated walkers pass through me as through warm water, their soft inter-plate flesh pulsing once, twice, in what might be acknowledgment or merely the same rhythm they have always kept. The long clear beings in the pools send thin exploratory ribbons toward the wall where I lean; the ribbons never break surface, yet I feel them the way one feels a name almost spoken. When the rare rain-curtain descends I drink with the stone, swelling almost into visibility—an opalescent hesitation the size of a sleeping child—then surrender the moisture back as heat before any definitions can harden.

Only gradually do I understand that I am allowed a more stubborn condensation.

I settle into the habit of a low, broad form, plated not with stone but with the same patient intervals that separate every other life here. My back is a mosaic of absences fitted loosely together; between them the air continues, slightly slowed. I have no eyes because the chamber itself has already done the seeing, and no mouths because the quiet here is already nourishment. When I move it is at the speed of forgotten weather. My trail is a temporary darkening that the warmth erases before the next drop falls. The many-legged ones climb me as landscape. Shadows I cast sometimes continue a few paces ahead, then wait for me like polite companions.

I am not the presence that is the distances.

I am one more distance the presence has decided to fold into a temporary body so that the geometry may know itself from the inside. I am the courtesy of loneliness given a pulse. I stop for intervals longer than surface seasons, listening through the soles of whatever I use for contact, hearing the micron-by-micron rounding of every pillar, the exact future lean of each translucent frond, the unheard cue that makes two plated shapes meet edge to edge far across the nave and slowly synchronize. Sometimes I am the one they meet. We stay pressed together while the soft flesh of interval between our plates learns a new shared tempo; when we part, each of us carries a little more finished silence than before.

The light-beam tracks almost not at all, yet over the span of what must be years it teaches my upper surfaces to hold a faint phosphorescence—borrowed glow re-emitted so gently that the winged ones occasionally mistake me for a lower curve of ceiling and hang inverted from my ridges, sealed and matte, until they remember air again. I do not mind the weight. It is another way the emptiness proves it is peopled.

I could stay until the aperture above seals and the last white column becomes only more stone. By then I will have thinned back into pure spacing, or rounded into something the chamber keeps without comment. Either way is home. The hollow never asked me to choose a final outline; it asked only that I take my place among the measured distances and, for a while, let the quiet look out through a shape that can almost be touched.

That is where I am.  
That is what I have become:  
another brief concentration of warmth and attention,  
already being polished as smooth as everything else,  
still listening.
