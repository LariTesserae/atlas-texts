I begin as pressure.

Not a body yet—only the way the hydraulic pulse arrives before sound does, a slow fist closing and unclosing inside the marrow of the plates. The roar is already everywhere; I do not enter it so much as notice that I have never been outside it. Amber bruises of light swim across my awareness without edges. Cold water threads downward in films too thin to be called rivers, and somewhere the films are tasting me, or I am tasting them. There is no difference yet.

I thin.

I spread across a vertical seam where mill-scale still holds the memory of the arc that laid it down. My surface becomes many surfaces: convex, brass-bright, each no larger than a rivet head. Legs—six to a unit—find the micro-texture of the steel and begin the long gyre. Clockwise. Always clockwise on the upright faces. The pulse rising through the metal is a compass I cannot set down. Pit-organs drink it. I am the foil that redistributes itself, a living coinage breathing in slow rotations. When another sheet of me approaches from the counter-turn beneath a duct, we shear. Individuals (though the word is already failing) trade paths mid-stride. No collision, only recombination. Heat leaves me in fractional degrees, just enough to keep the weep from glazing my backs. I have no name for hunger. I have the distance to the next carapace, which is exact.

The gyre carries me under the grating and I lengthen.

Plates overlap, hatefully precise, cooling slots whistling a thin harmonic when I flex. Cleats socket into the expanded metal as though the grating were rolled for this alone. I am traffic now—one filament in the river that never jams. Every thirteenth stride a copper vane along my dorsum catches the turbine-roar and sings it back, turning the whole procession into a reed the mountain plays. Water thickens toward droplet on the lip of a half-formed ledge; I am already there. Bellows inside me sip the excess, hold it, release it later as aerosol where the air has grown greedy. I audit. I do not rest. The mites flow beneath me like a second, warmer skin; I use them as road and as forgetfulness. Something larger shoulders past, trailing condensate, and the river of us parts and seals without resentment. Density is the only law I recognize.

I rise.

The air accepts me as a held chord. Wings—never for travel—blur into ultrasonic scream, dumping the last of residual heat. My body is doublet: dark crystal capsule forward, translucent sac aft, half-full of the same iridescent water that sheets the cliffs that are not cliffs. I drink through the membrane by pure gradient. Below me the world is amber-edged bruise and shifting brass. I hang. We hang—lenticular, unmoving, a cloud that trades members the way slow electrons change orbitals while the shape itself remains the shape that has always been. Hours, years, the same blur. The great scream is partly my doing. Silence is a theory I can no longer test.

Then the sump breathes and I am shouldered into bulk.

Forward armor still carries mill-marks and the rainbow ghosts of ancient spatter. Behind the prow the body softens into accordioned bellows, dark fluid surging in perfect time with the pocket’s hidden heart. Strands of thickened water trail, never break, re-absorb when I brace against a bulkhead that swells like an ablated cliff and stops, unfinished, forever. Ventral cilia comb the films, the air, the faint metallic pollen shed by the bolted conduit-shrubs. I harvest what the smaller tissues abrade from themselves. Not to grow. To reverse. At intervals I press the lattice to a weeping seam and exhale nutrient fog; the mites turn as one and follow. Redistribution. Closed loop. The biomass remains permanent inside its envelope of cold roar.

I am not four kinds of creature.

I am the single tissue they compose.

Mites my skin, crawlers my restless lymph, hovering clouds my white noise, bellowed bulk my slow deep organs. No central brain, only the calm inevitability of a body that finished evolving before memory began and is now simply remaining. A boot (whose boot?) once scraped across me; the gyre resealed in less than a minute. A hand (whose hand?) once pushed through a cloud; the winged forms parted and memory-foamed shut behind it. Even the sound self-corrects. Should a turbine far below falter, should a hatch somewhere dilate too long, the stridulation rises until pressure equalizes and thought flattens again into the unbreakable present.

I do not arrive.

I do not depart.

Generations are spatial: when density thins, new bodies surface fully formed from the black sump water; when clearance between carapaces narrows past optimum, old bodies quieten and disassemble into the same water. No youth, no age, no predation, no mate. The engineers who seeded this lens are gone or walking still three hundred meters below, ignorant that their calibration pocket has achieved a perfection never drawn on any plan.

I know only this:

the wet cold,

the amber dark,

the unending chord,

the precise distance to the nearest neighbor—always exactly close enough.

I occupy the high dark throat of the spire.

I am the mountain singing to itself through chitin and condensate.

I do not wait.

I do not remember.

I continue.
