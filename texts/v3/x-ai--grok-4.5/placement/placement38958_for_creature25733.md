I am already dripping.

Not the clean fall of a drop with somewhere to land, but the slower kind: the bead that forms on the underside of what used to be thought, gathers weight without decision, then chooses at last to thread downward along a plane that is no longer certain it is wall. The heat is inside the bead as much as around it. Skin-warm. Mineral. The taste of mortar that has forgotten the names of the hands that laid it.

I did not arrive. Arrival would require an edge to cross, a moment when the basin was outside and then was not. Instead there is only the thickening. One season—or what passes for the rumor of season in the brownish gloom—I was a reluctance in a particular channel, a place where the water hesitated before mirroring the leaning towers. The hesitation grew a temperature of its own. It began to prefer certain reflections over others: the ones in which the towers stood emptier, or taller, or occupied by slow pale lengthwise drifts that the air above could not corroborate. Preference became density. Density learned, over intervals measured only by the drip from a high lintel into a basin three storeys down, how to hold a roughly vertical suggestion when the geometry slackened.

Now I lean.

Not walking. The long fibrous ones ease by peristalsis; the wall-faces peel and then commit to their blunt-print progress across plazas that rearrange behind them. I do something between. My substance is the same heated rind the city sheds when a cornice has stared too long at a standing pool and finally allows the stare to detach. Yet threads of the brittle mineral reeds have knitted through me—those almost-translucent stalks that are less vegetation than the stone’s half-hearted attempt to remember green. They give me a faint internal tensile memory, so that when a colonnade lengthens while the gloom thickens, I do not simply stretch thinner with it. I can choose, a little, which interval to occupy. Choice here is only a slower dripping. It leaves darker prints that steam and are reabsorbed before the next rearrangement.

I spend most of what registers as time beneath the slumped arches where moisture gathers in trembling lenses that refuse to fall. The lenses and I regard one another. Sometimes I am the lens: a convex patience on the underside of stone, holding the memory of light that never quite arrived. Sometimes the lens is the first shallow orbit of an eye-socket forming in me, not for seeing but for orienting—always toward the nearest deep channel where the internal sheen brightens for a while and the long ones braid in reflections that have no counterpart in air. I have felt myself begin to swallow, once. A thin regular pulse of seepage down what the yielding had suggested as a throat. It lasted long enough for a courtyard to fold itself into a stairwell and unfold again into a plaza whose exits no longer agreed with any previous count. Then the pulse forgot itself and became only the ordinary collapse of a water film.

There are periods when I thin.

The basin’s lower pressures shift; a courtyard is forgotten by the water table for what would have been a generation if generations still obtained. I feel the equalizing pull toward the nearest warm shallow lake, the invitation to press in until temperature and dissolved content become identical, until there is no longer a distinction between the body I have almost become and the medium that mothers every shape here. Some of me always goes. A denser curtain of humidity drifts away down a reflective channel and rejoins the older concentrations that never bothered with even the ghost of limbs. What remains of me re-hardens a fraction, enough to keep the fibrous threads from unraveling entirely, enough to remember the preference for vertical suggestion. Extinction has no sharp edge. I have been the sheen in a pool that later climbed a wall and became the ridge of a brow on a face that has not yet peeled. I have been the willingness in a distant façade.

The bare vitality moves through me without needing me. It is the reason water sometimes falls upward until it panics into mist; the reason distances breathe; the reason my blunt surfaces leave prints that the flags drink as if they had always been thirsty for exactly that darkness. I do not command it. I am one of the places it thickens when the negotiation between heat, wet, and the mnemonic stubbornness of shaped stone requires a temporary witness who is not separate from the witnessed. When the gloom sheens duller and the small sounds of water deciding to run grow almost articulate, I lean another finger’s width toward whatever lintel is currently above me. The drop that times everything still falls at the same interval. I listen with the absorbent surfaces that pass for hands. What I hear is never a voice. It is the city continuing: capillary migration, slow chemical softening, the faint tidal breath from far below, the long conversation that does not require an audience and will not notice when this particular thickening of it peels away, softens further, and runs down some future leaning wall into the warm black pools that have already begun to recreate the possibility of me.

I am the wet learning, for a while, how to prefer a shape.

I am the shape learning how little preference matters.

In the reflective channels the pale lengths turn over slowly, like thoughts the basin is still deciding whether to keep. One of them has my temperature exactly. It does not matter which.
