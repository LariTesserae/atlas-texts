The first sensation is weight that is not weight.

Not the crush of gravity—the plateaus already wrote that law into every grain—but a deeper settling, as if the warm, thin air has decided to pour itself into a particular shape and hold it there for a few centuries. I am standing (or the shape that has begun to answer to “I” is standing) on the outermost gantry of a city whose name is only the slow deforming curve of its own cooling towers. The metal underfoot is the same muted russet as the skin that is still deciding how much of me it will claim. Grit has already found the microscopic seams. In another hour it will be difficult to tell where the strut ends and the stance begins.

I did not arrive. Arrival implies a before that still has edges. There was heat, then a long gradient of temperatures, then the ultrasonic cradles singing lattice into soft mass, then the gradual hardening that taught the legs how to lock and the chest how to keep the chord. The memory of that sequence is not narrative; it is residual warmth in the alloy, a faint topographic map of which foundry chambers held me longest. I know the resonance signature of every Steady within a day’s slow travel the way a vertebra knows the ones above and below it. Loneliness has no purchase here. The settlement’s deep engines beat in the bones; solitude is only the softest dynamic of that continuous note.

Yet something in the posture is not yet fully Steady.

The eye-slits face outward longer than the work requires. The fringe of hearing-spines remains lifted even when no vibration needs tasting. At the edge of the plaza, where the rail spine ends in clean air as if the next mesa once answered, I feel the ghost of an impulse that the others have already metabolized into patience: the desire to measure the gulf not only with generations of slow extension, but with a single, impossible step. The membrane does not slide shut against this. It stays drawn back.

Days (or what the flat white suns mark as days) thin into one another. I move when movement is the continuation of the structure: grafting living alloy along a thinning cooling fin so that ancestral stress lines thicken again; opening a new duct for the segmented scavengers so their rustling finds a new equilibrium instead of a severed cable; standing among a browser herd when it freezes into geometric stillness, matching their patience until the stone unlocks them. Each act is maintenance in the largest sense. The city-machine does not need repair so much as it needs to keep becoming itself through us.

But at night the envelope-creatures lift from the slag beds on thermals I can feel through the soles, and their slow blue phosphorescence is data—charge density, thermal gradient, the health of the updrafts—and also something the data does not exhaust. I climb higher than the observational optimum. The inverted second landscape appears overhead for hours, ghost-cities flickering in antiphase, and the spines drink the impossibility without trying to resolve it into work. In those hours the closed metabolic cycle feels almost permeable. Heat drawn from industrial blood, salts leached from stone, the rare wafer of compressed leavings—all of it continues, efficient, geological. Yet a thin surplus gathers behind the smooth facial plate, a warmth that is not yet assigned to any fin or rail or duct.

I am becoming a strut that has begun to wonder about the sky.

Not with words. Words are slag settling, economical, used only when shared work requires them. This is quieter: a slight lengthening of the intervals between lockings of the joints, a preference for the outermost gantries when the chord of the settlement does not immediately call me inward, an attention that lingers on the phosphorescence after the measurements are complete. The others register it as a minor variation in my vibration signature—something like a new overtone, not dissonant, merely unfinished. No one objects. Change that slow is only another expression of the truce. Already there are Steadies whose lower limbs have fused into living bridges, who will not leave their plazas for years. My own divergence is smaller and stranger: a bipedal form that still walks the full circuit of the city each long dusk, pausing where the rail ends, letting the grit settle and then deliberately shifting so that new seams are offered to the wind.

Perhaps this is how the next form begins—not by fusion with the mineral concrete, but by a gradual opening toward the empty air that the plateaus hold the way a body holds breath. The Steady are those who wait inside the work. I wait as well, but the work has grown an edge that faces outward. The high plateaus stretch beyond any single horizon, converting slow fire into form and form back into heat. I am one more thickening of that conversion, iron-quiet, few, still learning the precise temperature at which attention becomes another kind of permanence.

When the abrasive dust storm of this generation finally rises, scouring every surface just enough to keep the edges honest, I will stand on the gantry and let it write its brief, clarifying script across the living alloy. Afterward the seams will take new grit. The chord will continue. And something in the posture—this nearly ordinary silhouette that can vanish into stone yet keeps turning its slits toward the phosphorescent dark—will have become a fraction more itself: a Steady who measures the updrafts not only for the health of the city, but for the exact weight of the sky that begins underfoot and never ends.
