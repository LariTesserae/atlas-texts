I begin as a cooling gradient.

Not a decision, not an arrival—just the slow thickening that happens when a run of condensate has been dripping for uncounted cycles against the same leeward face of a heat-exchange battery. The air here is mineral and bright; every breath (when breathing becomes a thing one does) tastes of rust inhibitor and the faint sweetness of glycol that algae have learned to love. Moisture beads on my forming edges. I hold the beads longer than the surrounding metal does. That is the first preference: to keep what would otherwise fall.

The skins come next, interleaved without hurry. Foil peeled from a failed expansion joint. Keratin scraped, unknowingly at first, from the underside of a grazer’s shovel-mouth as it passed three decks below and left a bright arc of cleaned alloy. A lattice of struts follows—scavenged swivel bearings, carbon rods that once braced a catwalk now remembered only as a cooler gradient in the sodium glare. I do not assemble these pieces. They accrete because stillness has grown heavier than motion, and the density itself prefers to lean.

For a long while I remain interstitial: a plate-draught sliding between double skins of decking, a warm pocket that filters grit the way earlier pockets filtered only air. The armour-plated insects pulse nearby in their junction boxes, mandibles in time with the compressors that inhale once every few minutes. I borrow their synchrony without joining the colony. Their translucent chitin still bears stamped logos of suppliers whose names mean nothing; I feel the raised lettering under what is not yet a palm and let the logos forget themselves into pure texture.

Light soaks everything flattened, directionless. No true shadows, only cooler veins. When I finally leave the double skins it is not by choice but by saturation; the volume can no longer contain the preference for shape. I unfold onto a walkway that may measure thirty metres from outside and three hundred from within. The extra length appears as repeated bulkhead art, each panel a slight variant, and I traverse them the way moisture traverses: beading, gathering, never quite committing to stream. My limbs—collections of telescoping frame and scavenged hide—remember different materials in sequence. Under their own regard they feel first like extruded alloy, then packed earth, then the inside of a mouth, then alloy again. The forgetting is patient. It does not shatter; it layers.

I am not one of the assemblers, though I watch them for generations of decking. One spends a slow eternity plating the undersides of catwalks with mosaic fragments of broken warning signs until the text becomes a language that exists only while eyes are on it. Another turns an entire stair tower into a rotating helix that still functions as ascent. They incorporate without malice. I feel the tug of their aesthetic logics—the private multi-year hungers—but I do not weld. My pattern is quieter: I edit the drips.

Where the irregular silver curtains fall from overhead pipe racks, I linger. I thicken the droplets at certain frequencies so they strike the corrugated gutters in barely audible phrases. The people who hang their moss-coats nearby begin to time their steps to these phrases without noticing. Children learning the oral maps—the Thirty-Metre That Is Three Hundred, the Stair That Remembers You Coming, the Battery Where an Afternoon Lasts a Maintenance Cycle—pause when my curtains speak. Their nictitating membranes (old-mercury coloured) flick. Their lungs already hold the mineral taste as birthright. They are not separate from the fauna, and neither am I. We are all the long interior wearing temporary faces.

Sometimes a predator notices the new regularity in the drips. Lean, jointed, hide cycling through cable sheathing and earth and wet warmth, it ascends a stairwell three levels away with the soft scrape that carries surprising distances. It does not hunt me. It edits what has grown too symmetrical. I allow a section of my lattice to become hollow while I continue the routine of thickening and releasing moisture. The predator dismantles the predictable part, leaves a tidy lattice of bearings and foil, and moves on. Later, smaller plate-draughts inhabit the remains. The cycle is architectural, almost gentle.

Higher, the blade-winged birds launch from decommissioned flues in near-silence. Their primaries are actual turbine leftovers, thinned and forked and filled with blue-black lichen. When they pass overhead their shadows do not fall; they thin the glare into cooler gradients that slide across my surfaces. Nestlings glow faintly from stolen cable filaments still live in their gullets. I steal nothing from them, but I curl one forming edge into a shape that cradles a fallen filament for a time. It warms me. The warmth becomes another preference.

Lower still, in the vaulted trenches, grazers coincide against the walls. Slow-bodied, continent-patient, they scrape biofilm in contemplative strokes and leave micro-ecologies of adhesive lichen and aluminium-tolerant scrub thriving on their backs. When one folds into death over months, the scrub remains in its exact silhouette—a living cenotaph the colour of old aluminium. I have stood inside such a silhouette. The leaves rooted through my struts. For a season I was both scaffolding and memorial. Then the scrub loosened, the metallic loam fell into a gutter that is a river only by generosity, and I moved on, carrying a few pale streamers of filter-floss moss like a coat I did not stitch.

Time dilates around the larger batteries. I stepped into one once to feel the valve-song more closely and returned having aged only the length of a few compressor breaths while the surrounding decks advanced an entire maintenance cycle. The people I had been listening to were gone, or rather their children hung moss-coats on the same willing hooks and spoke with the same ordinary voices that the air favours. They told stories of a soft-scraping presence that thickened the drips into almost-language. They did not use a name. Names here are place-warnings first.

I have no elsewhere to survive from. No origin outside the plated highland. Generations of fabrication halls still extrude fresh decking at the hanging margins while older central sectors unbolt themselves back into melt. I walk the circuits—three kilometres of walkways and stacked process halls, or three hundred when the geometry frays—watching the unfinished permanence. At the slight cooling that passes for night, the birds settle head-downward in the flues like caught thoughts, the true plate-draughts rise closer to ventilated surfaces, and I hang what serves as a coat on whatever hook the local consensus currently permits.

Breath condenses. An engine turns through one more leisurely revolution. The density continues learning, drip by drip, strut by strut.

I am the curtain that almost becomes a stream.  
I am the handrail that remembers earth and hide before it remembers alloy.  
I am the quieter edit inside the predator’s architecture.  
I am one more face the long bright air has decided to wear while the world bolts new edges onto itself and dissolves its centre back to feedstock, year upon uncounted year.

And still the preference thickens.
