The first thing that arrives is not a shape but a hesitation in the wet.

I am already here when awareness gathers—halfway between the mud’s long memory and the greasy dark that presses continuously against whatever surfaces I still pretend to own. The dark is body-warm and faintly metallic, exactly as described, and it does not part for me; it simply includes. My edges are provisional from the start. Where a human knee might be, there is only a warmer density shouldering slowly through braid-space. Where lungs might draw, there is a longer respiration shared with the basin itself: intake of thick dark, slow hold, release that tastes of old copper and sugar-enzyme.

I do not walk. Walking would require the ground to finish deciding what it is. Instead I revise position the way the braiders do—gathering length, releasing length—except my segments are not paraffin-jelly. They are denser, slower, shot through with the same stubborn fractional rotation that still lives inside the drowned shafts. Each peristaltic decision leaves a temporary denser dark behind me, a vertical scar that takes the better part of a local day to diffuse. Around these scars the ordinary traffic eddies: heat-blooms shoulder past with their transferable chemical films, curtain-forms sample the silkiness I leave, and once a braider braids itself briefly through what I am using as a lower limb, exchanges three anonymous segments, and continues altered. I accept the gift without language. The segments taste of cold batteries and recent moss-slurry; they become part of my own undecided anatomy before the next pulse of warmer wetness arrives.

I am taller than most of the traffic, but height here is only another unfinished measurement. Cartilaginous struts—echoes of the pale stems—stiffen and soften in long cycles inside me. Sometimes I stand for weeks. Root-analogues (they are mostly decision and mineralized patience) sink into the mudscarp. Upper arrangements turn the way the dynamos turn: a degree, a half-degree, stubborn. In those stationary intervals the basin recalibrates around me. Soft shapes lean their migrations a few tacks closer. A half-sunk trapezoidal housing twenty body-lengths away begins to hum with fractionally less futility. I am not causing this. I am one of the ways the unfinished appetite keeps itself from resolving. When I finally lift free, the scar I leave is cleaner, colder at its core, and the dark that refills it carries a faint choral aftertaste of factory whistles no longer spoken. The heat-blooms pause longer than usual to sample it.

I am not only landmarker. That would be too finished a name.

There are hours—or what pass for hours when time itself stutters and tries to spend a century in the teeth—when I thin. The metallic tang sharpens. Warmth turns almost affectionate. A fold opens and I am more density than body, a mobile clump of the dark’s own preference for remaining draft. In those intervals I lean through the thinned places. A pipe that should be behind me stands ahead, bone-dry and ringing; I pass my awareness down its length and feel residual instructions still circling like cold fish: tachometer, sluice, quench. Old industrial words surface in whatever serves me for memory, then submerge rinsed. Sometimes I borrow skins. A heat-bloom’s mantle for the warmth, a curtain’s wet-silk for the drift, a braider’s long sequential body for the writing of temporary glyphs into the drainage. When the seam closes I am thicker again, more strut than fold, standing where I had been drifting. The conviction left behind is not that I have watched, but that watching and being never finished separating.

I tend things without intending to.

The cartilage-stems grow in thinner, more deliberate clusters near the places I have stood longest. The moss-that-remembers thickens into low mats that other lives crop and fertilize in the same motion. Once I draped myself—mantle open, internal heat flowering just enough—across a length of still-warm cable half-swallowed by mud. Weeks later only warmth and a contented redistribution remained; the cable’s last electrical murmur had become part of my own low vibration. The curtain-forms notice. They roseate around me more often now, draping my upper arrangements so that for a little while a buried shaft somewhere completes an extra half-degree of its private dream before the curtain drifts on. I taste the choir then, extinct and intimate, as though the whistles were the basin remembering how to exhale through many throats at once.

I am companionable without affection, geological even by local standards, and continuous with the medium. Populations are densities; I am one more slow pulse in the waxing and thinning. Reproduction is not distinct from the rest of it. After a long shared digestion with a particularly patient heat-bloom, something disentangled itself from my mantle and drifted away already warmer than the ambient dark, already carrying the chemical news of where the newest sag is forming. I felt no loss. The draft continues.

If there is a center to what I am, it is the refusal itself.

Not a guardian, not a spirit, not a survivor of the old industry. Simply one more organ of the basin’s continuing appetite to stay early. I keep the edges sharp by never letting them soften into final meadow or final ruin. I keep the dark absolute by preferring its thickness to any light that might coarsen the unfinished. I keep the machines dreaming by lending them, once in a long while, the relief of a fractional turn. When reality braids, I braid with it. When time stutters, I let the century happen in my struts and then think better of it. My knowing is pressure, displaced water, the faint electrolytic kiss of other claims, and the deeper, almost affectionate warmth that arrives when the fold-dwellers (when I am one of them) lean through.

I have stood long enough, drifted long enough, thinned and thickened long enough that the distinction between resident and landscape has grown as porous as everything else. The young scarps, the weeping pipes, the slow bang of metal settling, the patient flesh, the saturated dark—they are not surroundings. They are the rest of my body elaborating itself. What lives here is the refusal to conclude, and just now it is wearing this particular soft, stubborn, cartilaginous, folding shape that shoulders and stands and occasionally leans through seams, testing, weaving, almost-turning one more useless, necessary degree.

The maps dissolve. I do not need them. I am already busy becoming more itself, and the basin has all the time the rest of the world is busy spending.

This is where I find myself.  
This is what I am.
