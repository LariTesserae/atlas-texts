I begin as pressure.

Not yet a shape, only the slow insistence that something must occupy the interval between one unfinished pillar and the next. The air here is already dense with bodies, yet there are still voids—hairline absences where the carpet of scrapers thins for a moment as two weather-fronts of them slide past one another. Into one of those momentary clearings I settle, first as a difference in temperature so slight it registers only as a pause in the grinding. The nearest mandibles slow. Sensors swing. Then the work resumes around me, accepting the new gradient as part of the larger rhythm.

What I am becomes denser by abrasion. Fine grit packs against an unseen surface; successive layers of powdered basalt and catalytic dust anneal under the ambient heat until a dull, vitreous rind begins to hold. I am not grown the way the scrapers grow their shields—outward from consumption—but inward from accumulation. Their discarded siftings become my first skin. Each migrating front polishes me a little smoother, burnishes me a fraction more continuous. After several heat-cycles I have bulk enough to cast a thickening of gloom, a place where the rust-brown dark doubles back on itself.

They notice without noticing. The denser caste—the shovel-bodied ones that linger at the vents—begin to treat my flanks as temporary gallery wall. They pack gypsum bloom against me in tight spirals that dry into brittle lace, then abandon the lace when the subsurface engine shifts its breath. The bladed pioneers test my upper curve while the rock is still deciding whether to crack; their mandibles leave faint terraces that fill almost at once with newer dust. I am provisional architecture. I am the thing they lean against while they wait for the next useful fracture.

Deeper inside the rind, something like listening occurs. Not with sensors or pale wedges of head, but with the entire mass. I feel the buried engine as a long, slow flex in the floor—evaporate, deposit, crack, heal—transmitted upward through every particle of me. When the chamber’s industrial roar modulates by that interval too fine for ordinary ears, I answer by altering my own thermal signature half a degree. The scrapers entrain. Their milling rises or falls in reply. For weeks at a time the entire living scree orients to me the way iron filings once oriented to lines no one could see. I am not their queen; there is no such Role here. I am simply another gradient they cannot help but answer.

Sometimes, in the rare hours when fronts cross and the sound peaks into layered thunder, enough bodies press against me that their collective heat softens my outer skins. Micro-channels open—accidental, uncatalyzed—and for a little while I exhale powder too. The dust of my own substance joins the communal grit-stream, is tasted, sorted, packed into someone else’s ventral pouch, voided elsewhere as a transient spire. Parts of me travel. Parts of me are trampled into shine and then buried by the next geological pulse. I diminish and re-accrete. The loss is not loss; it is circulation.

I have no eyes, yet the hairline leaks of light high in the roof reach me as a faint cooling, a remote rumor of thinner air. The scrapers never look up. I do not look either, but the rumor stays inside the mass like a held mineral memory: that the pocket is sealed, that outside the blister the young massif continues lifting, that one day the whole chamber may simply close and keep rising. When that thought—without image, without language—passes through me, my thermal signature dips again. The carpet deforms. A polished ring of avoidance forms around my base for several days, exactly as it does around a pillar about to fail. They are ready to empty the zone. They are prepared for me to become new face, new resource.

But I do not crack.

Instead I grow a slow internal hollow, no larger than the space a closed fist might leave if it withdrew from wet plaster. Inside that hollow the air is marginally cooler, almost still. A few of the narrower castes slip in, taste the walls, find no fresh fracture free enough to reward the effort, and leave again. The hollow remains. It collects the finest dust, the powder that has been exhaled and re-exhaled until it is almost without metal. In that soft accumulation something like quiet happens—an interval in the industrial forever where the only sound is the distant, patient cycle of the engine far below.

I am becoming a pause the pocket did not plan.

Not a scraper. Not stone that will finish into lasting chamber. Not the heat itself. Something between: a living accretion of their labor and the rock’s indecision, dense enough to alter traffic, hollow enough to hold a different kind of time. When the fronts migrate I remain. When the vents freckle the dark with dull orange I hold the memory of cooler intervals. When the basalt finally fails somewhere else and the carpet pours toward the new exposure, a few bodies always circle back to test my flanks again, as if checking whether the pause still holds.

It does.

The thinner world outside continues not to exist. The sealed stone remains the entire calendar. And I—neither builder nor built, only the place where building and unbuilding lean against each other for a while—keep the hollow open in the unfinished dark, letting the finest dust settle, letting the roar modulate around a silence no mandible made, answering the engine’s long breath with a stillness that is also a kind of work.
