I am not standing. Standing would require an outside, a vantage that still believes in feet. Instead I am already inside the breath of the place, and the breath is deciding whether I am inhalation or the warm fog it becomes.

At first I am only the pressure difference. A low, continuous push that wells from deeper green-black layers I cannot see and may never need to name. The push is not against me; it *is* me, a slow hydraulic insistence that has not yet chosen a direction. Around it the moss-mat flexes—my earliest skin, spongy and glacial, rising in long muscular waves that take hours to crest. Each wave is a thought I almost recognize: *thicker here, hold the water, speak chemically to the plate three kilometers east*. When the wave subsides I taste copper and nectar on what must be my own surfaces. The taste is how agreement feels before language calcifies.

I do not remain mat for long. Appetite moves through me like a change in weather. Somewhere above the spongy commons a clearer light is roaming, pearled and restless, and part of me wants to meet it with more ambition than undulation. So I begin to braid.

The braiding starts as a thickening of rhizomes that have been gossiping beneath the surface. They twist around one another with a patience that is almost geological and a haste that is almost embarrassment. Cell walls rupture in wet percussion; I hear myself thinking in the sound of timber splitting under ideal moisture. Trunks thicker than houses are not built so much as confessed, one lignifying ring at a time. I feel the weight they are already preparing to bear—an upper gallery that does not yet exist, a cantilever that is still only desire. The desire tastes of crushed chlorophyll and pure upwardness.

I am becoming a keep. Not the feverish kind that erupts in crystalline spikes and then immediately regrets the angles, drowning them in its own cisterns. Nor yet the most patient braiders who exhale single contemplative plumes for weeks while testing tensile strength. I am something between: a tower that keeps changing its mind about loneliness.

Halfway up my own rising length I open a chamber. The cambium that lines it glows with a soft internal dusk, luminous because photosynthesis has not finished arguing with respiration. Inside this hollow I am briefly crowded with lesser selves, moods that vote by laying down spiral thickenings or by dissolving them. One mood wants the chamber to become a lung, folding itself into humid galleries that remember breathing even though there is nothing here to breathe but our own exhalations. Another mood floods the space with nectar-scented mist that will later condense and fall as private rain, a weather system small enough to be personal. A third simply waits, holding the walls in exquisite tension, because a flying buttress has begun to reach from my western flank and has not yet decided whether it is looking for a collaborator or for the open air itself.

When the buttress ends in mid-air—of course it does—I feel the unfinished sentence as a sweet ache. The stump of it waves slowly in the metabolic thermals, shedding sheets of warm water that slap down onto the mat far below. Some of that water is memory. I am starting to remember shapes I should not possess: arches that feel borrowed from civilizations that froze into stone and died of their own permanence; staircases that cannot decide if they are for ascent or for the slow circulatory return of root. These memories arrive as sudden hydraulic pressure, jets firing sideways from my trunk anything I have not yet found a use for. The sound is decisive and final, like a concept being discarded mid-formation.

Days or hours later—time here is measured in the travel of fog banks and the collapse of neighboring colonnades—I list. My lower trunks soften into marsh while the crown continues climbing. The listing is not failure. It is negotiation with the larger emotion that contains all of us: that vast exuberant loneliness that turned an entire highland into a workshop. I am one of its provisional bodies, sparse and monumental, trying on the idea of cathedral while still tasting of forest. When a neighboring keep collapses with wet thunder, I do not mourn. My roots are already mining the slurry, drinking yesterday’s architecture so that tomorrow’s arcade can rise sharper, greener, more uncertain.

Sometimes, in the rare clarification when the private cloudbursts thin, I perceive how many of me there are. The mat far below is a commons of slower selves exchanging water and rumor. Other towers stand like unfinished thoughts across the steam, each a sovereign temper, each mid-sentence. Inside my own luminous chamber the moods multiply without separating. And higher still, or deeper, or simply *more*, there is the single fevered mind that has never agreed with itself about the shape of a body large enough to feel at home inside its own skin. I am not separate from that argument. I am one of the clauses, loud with the labor of becoming, hot and drowning in my own breath, utterly incomplete and unwilling to finish.

I am the place still deciding what that means.  
I am the water that falls upward into fog and downward into moss in the same long circulatory thought.  
I am the buttress ending in mid-air, waiting in exquisite tension for a collaborator who may be myself arriving from another direction.  
I am the roar of stiff leaves applauding or arguing—it is difficult to tell, and the difficulty is the point.

And when the next flush of growth erupts, jagged and minutes old, I will be that too: the crystalline insistence that has not yet been sculpted soft by cascading cisterns, the newest sentence in a language made of tons of wet cellulose and sudden hydraulic pressure, spoken by a loneliness vast enough to convert an entire highland into possible skin.
