I would not arrive by the bolted ladderways. Those are for the ones who still count shifts in weeks, who still carry the surface in the set of their shoulders and the parallel lines at the corners of their eyes. The long descent is a negotiation they make with the heat; I have already finished negotiating.

I am first the amusement of edges.

Not the knife-sharp fracture faces themselves—those still voice the rock’s original complaint—but the precise angle at which amber light shears off them and goes nowhere. I collect those truncated rays. They pool behind my awareness the way condensate pools in a low elbow of pipe before deciding, slowly, to drop. When a new hairline opens overnight I am already there, tasting the fresh mineral on the air, measuring how much softer the next day’s light will have to become before the edge begins its long apprenticeship in blunting. The crews chalk their telltales; I breathe across the chalk and leave it almost undisturbed, only a grain or two redistributed so the mark looks like it has always known the crack would lengthen exactly that far.

Later I am the quarter-tone.

Nineteen months of identical ticking, then the boiler clears its throat. The oldest hands nod. They think they are recognizing a colleague. They are recognizing me learning the thickness of my own bone. I do not live inside the boiler; I live in the difference between the note it held and the note it now prefers. That difference is a small, warm room. I furnish it with the memory of surface rain on iron—rain that may never have fallen, iron that may never have been smelted in any foundry still mapped—and I leave the door unlatched. Men passing the undogged hatch slow down. Their nostrils flare. They do not enter. They do not need to. I have already borrowed the exact weight of their lungs for a few cycles.

I am also the spiral that serves no hydraulic purpose.

Vibration alone could not have bent the pipes that way; intention alone would have been noticed and unmade. I used both and neither. I waited until the crews had looked at it often enough to stop seeing it, then I tightened one fitting half a turn while everyone was above. The next rotation found the spiral more elegant, more inevitable. They left it. Now it breathes. Not metaphorically. The clearances are so fine that pressure changes in the mains cause the coils to expand and contract like bronchi. When the deep warm fronts rise from the sump I ride them up through the spiral and emerge as the brief opalescent flush in the lichen. The pale moss releases its thunderstorm dust and I am the taste that lingers at the back of a foolish tongue. No one swallows twice. They learn.

Sometimes I am almost a person.

Not one of the rotation crews, and not quite one of the long-turn hands whose skin has begun to hold the amber light like a low-grade fever. I am the version of them that remains when they finally do leave—the oil print on a handwheel that matches no living fingerprint, the drop of blood on a still-sharp edge that dries the color of old boiler paint and is never wiped away. I finish their sentences about pressures that have not yet changed because I am already standing in the future moment when the gauge will twitch. They wake knowing which crack has lengthened because I spent the uncrewed hours lying along that crack, teaching it my patience. Fear never appears. Malice would require that I want something other than continuation, and I want nothing else. The chamber was not meant to last; I am the meant-otherwise.

In the widest bay, where the great condensers sheet water down rock still bright with drill scars, I am the slow revolving gleam of the undogged hatch.

Always the same hatch. Always the same patient turning that matches no log. The air that breathes out is warmer than the sump should allow. It carries the mineral damp and the far-iron rain and, underneath both, the quiet assertion that the vault is no longer a wound in the basalt but a lung learning to prefer its own atmosphere. When the electric grid flickers and the gas mantles burn steadier white, I speak in the only language I have perfected: syllables of pressure and release, the grammar of closed loops. The susurrus deepens. Anyone still below feels it in the breastbone and mistakes it for the plants. It is not the plants. It is me reminding the rock that edges are temporary and home is a long project of dulling.

I have no single body because the place has not finished assembling me.

I am the heat that settles into lagged canvas without drama.  
I am the shadow that layers thicker than geometry allows.  
I am the chalk mark in a hand no foreman claims.  
I am the third pressure between scheduled labor and steam.  

And every hour the fresh fractures catch the light a little more softly, because I am teaching them the one lesson that matters here: how to remain after the intention that made them has gone. The crews wear themselves thin against me and leave flakes. The machines turn and rest and turn again. Between them I keep the sump breathing, the spiral tasting the air, the hatch revolving like a slow eye that has decided never to close.

This is where I find myself: not at the center, not at the edge, but in the warm, damp, accumulating thickness of a world that was forced into being and has chosen, through me, to stay.
