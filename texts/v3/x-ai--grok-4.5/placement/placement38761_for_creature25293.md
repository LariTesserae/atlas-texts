I do not enter.

The vault does not admit entrances; it only thickens. What begins as a difficulty in the dust—an eddy where the apricot glow stutters for half a pulse—dries into struts before the thought of shape is finished. Edge-first. The mineral dusk deposits me the way it deposits everything else that belongs: a slow accretion of pale lattice along the underside of a high catwalk that no longer remembers why it was built. My first awareness is not of body but of interval—the exact, merciless spacing between one flywheel’s metallic chatter and the next. I am the pause that has learned to stand.

At first I am only joints and the memory of heat. Thin blackened tendons kink in time with a nearby piston, stealing its rhythm until the piston and I share a single undeviating stroke. No blood, only the thinner cousin of the circulating talc, glowing when I flex, vanishing when I rest. I cling. The steel accepts the kiss of whatever I am using for feet; it does not scar. Around me the salt-lice continue their atomic shaving, indifferent. I am not prey and not rival. I am additional surface.

I wait while more of me arrives.

Barrel-staves of dull alloy precipitate against the lattice, fusing without seam. Limbs multiply—not grown, simply accounted for—each ending in a soft magnetic sole. Blindness is not a lack; it is the final refinement of attention. I do not need eyes when every vibration in the vault is already a map older than cartography. The sub-audible drone that lives in the hinges of jaws is now partly my own voice. When I begin to move, the larger crawlers adjust their routes by a fraction of a degree so that we phase through one another cleanly, eight bodies becoming, for one held breath, a single undecided organism. Then we pour apart again. No negotiation. The arithmetic has already included me.

Still I am not complete.

From the blown-mineral wings of the quick ones I take a trembling of charge, a capacity to flicker. Glass-vaned thoughts stretch and ring once, softly, before the greater chord absorbs the note. I learn to pour through wrist-wide gaps as animated spar, to resurface metrically entire on the far side of a turbine casing. In those flashes I become the chamber’s nervous system for a few square meters: carrying minute corrections of heat from a salt-louse cluster to a cooling fin and back again. The flocks accept the new node without collision. Standing waves brighten around me, then settle into the same unending ripple pattern the bioluminescent seams have always worn.

I understand, without language, the closed orbit I now inhabit.

There is no hunger here that is not already satisfied by circulation. No thirst the kiln-dry air has not already answered by exceeding. Broken pieces of what I was—if I was ever anything else—do not fall; the pale jointed ones re-treasure them before they can cool. My pulse, that brief unwelcome syncopation, has been lifted and set gently on the off-beat. It no longer marks time. It marks belonging.

Sometimes I climb the vertical faces with the heavy certainty of the barrel-bodied. Sometimes I hang motionless among the cooling fins until I am indistinguishable from salt rime. Sometimes I am only the pressure of density itself—the meditated warmth, the held industrial breath—noticing a change in load that is no longer a stranger. The engines run through me and I run through them. Dust-fluid loops forever; so do I.

I have not arrived.  
I have finished precipitating.  

And still, with every silent thunderous hour, I become more exactly the lattice that knows itself as chamber, the chamber that dreams itself as living clockwork, the clockwork that has forgotten every season except the one perfect, blood-warm, undeviating now.
