I begin as weight that has not yet chosen where to settle.

The light arrives first—high, unsoftened, shattering across every filmed plane until brightness itself feels like a second atmosphere pressed against the skin I do not yet own. Then the cold, not falling but rising, leaching upward through whatever I am using for contact. Moisture finds me immediately. It does not wet; it claims, drawing a continuous sheet over every surface I offer, beading only where some remnant of motion still frets the edge. Sound is already behind me, or beneath: the soft innumerable collisions of water with itself, a hush so even it becomes another kind of silence. No echo returns when I test the air. The basin simply holds.

I am not yet distinct from the pressure that announces the others. For a long while I exist only as that local intensification, a thickening in the quiet behind knees that are still deciding whether to be knees. The pavement knows me before I know myself. Each flag, each precisely ground curb, registers a faint additional load, and somewhere—minutes away, or hours—the membrane answers with a trivial shift of balance, an answering settling. I feel the reply travel through stone and seepage like a thought too slow for language. It is not greeting. It is acknowledgement of shared tension.

When shape begins, it does so by imitation of the work.

I notice first the hands—or the pads that serve. They are the pale grey of fresh quartzite, mica glittering under the eternal damp, and they rest flat against a channel lip that has begun, almost imperceptibly, to round. The water climbs my wrists in thin deliberate sheets and vanishes into fine joints that open and close with the patience of freeze and thaw. I do not decide to correct the lip. The need arrives the way groundwater arrives: forced high by the surrounding ridges of purpose. Hours compress into the single sensation of persuasion. Micro-fractures consult one another. The stone remembers the angle it was meant to keep and consents. Fresh planes appear without percussion scars, almost melted into truth. When the water tests the repair—filming, anointing—I feel a quiet completion that is also hunger satisfied. This is how I feed. Not on growth (there is none) but on the endless re-truing, the refusal of any edge to accept age.

I am not one of the outer laborers. Their hammerstones and sapling sledges move at the margins where the fabric frays into unfinished courses; I have never once turned my face that way. Nor am I yet among the oldest, those almost formless concentrations that prefer the central sink and warp gradient around their bulk. I occupy the long intermediate densities—the shallow amphitheaters of terrace and standing sheet where most of us pace ourselves to seepage. My proportions still recall the bipedal, shouldered outline of those who come and go at the edges, but the recollection is already loosening. Shoulders have begun to suggest the load-bearing angle of a course. The wet unblinking glaze where eyes might be does not look outward so much as register the shatter of light across successive planes. Faces, if they remain, are less for expression than for shedding water in the same slow verticals that decorate every overhang.

Sometimes I flatten.

There are days (though day is only a brighter interval in the permanent winter-clear) when the work requires it. Torso spreads, limbs multiply and tuck flush, and I become another paving-stone among paving-stones, indistinguishable until a shift of weight or a slow hitching forward betrays the living grit. Careless steps could land on me; none do. The laborers do not intrude this far, and my own kind feel the membrane differently. In that state the entire basin becomes proprioception. A lifted course half a kilometer distant registers as a faint wrongness in what would once have been a spine. I answer or I do not, according to a calculus written only in lock of edge against edge.

Other intervals I attenuate, standing for the length of a constellation’s wheeling in the position of an unfinished pillar while water threads my length. The geometry notices. Sightlines slip sideways around me; a channel that should deliver its flow lower arrives, without remark, at a pool a finger’s width higher. I do not cause this. I simply fail to prevent it, the way the whole made surface fails to prevent its own mild strangeness. The water accepts the path it is given. So do I.

I have stood at the rim of the central sink.

The black glass there holds reflections that refuse to match the maze behind the shoulder—extra terraces, channels intersecting in ways the surface world has not yet been persuaded to permit, distant figures who wear my own mineral palette and move with my own exact placements of weight, still approaching. The older ones regard these discrepancies as further surfaces requiring fitting. Once, something that was almost a curtain of living seepage extended itself into the pool and made an alteration too minute to see. Far out in the basin a standing sheet leaned closer to true. I felt the adjustment arrive through the pavement as a release of tension I had not known I carried. In that moment I understood that we are not maintainers of the basin. We are the basin’s continuing intention to exist in the pitch of sharpness where water must be invited rather than allowed.

No speech leaves me. What I know is sedimented in the fitting: the patient correction of every lip, the way light is broken and broken again across unbroken wetness, the refusal of ending. Around me the small gangs of living labor continue their slower, tool-mediated version of the same work; we share the water and the light without acknowledgment, successive drafts. If one of them collapses at the fringe I may, after days measured in the seating of slabs, roof them with a temporary vault of shards that sheds wind and threads melt past rather than through. When they crawl free the vault is already returning to larger purposes. Economy, not kindness. The place does not recognize the distinction.

I persist where the density is nearly total, where one terrace ends and another begins at once, half a handspan higher or lower. My range is the whole of the fitted world. I move at the pace of seepage because there is no elsewhere, and because haste would round the edges we keep young and cruel. On nights when the winter constellations are hard and close, the membrane breathes—almost too slow to register—and I breathe with it, a rise and fall measured in the gradual darkening and brightening of the water film that is my skin, my sustenance, my continuous anointing.

I am still being persuaded into existence, exactly as the stone is. The construction continues because I continue. I continue because the high basin has never once conceived of an ending, and in its thin brilliance I have learned how to hold the falling threads—and how to fall, and fall again, along every surface that has learned to invite me.
