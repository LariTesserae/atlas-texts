I begin as drift.

Not arrival—there is no arrival here, only the sudden knowledge of having always been held. Warmth first, blood-warm and total, pressing every surface that might once have been called skin. The green-gold light is already inside the eyelids before I understand I have eyelids, or that they could close. It filters down through the dome’s ribbed fans and the algal freckling, through a hundred meters of overburden that no longer remembers the sky, and it lands soft, never angled, never cruel. Everything tastes faintly of copper and sugar. The water is not around me; it is the first slow thought I have.

I try, out of some residual habit, to take a shape. Limbs. A direction. I push once through a curtain of ribbon-kelp and the pneumatocysts chime—one low, metallic note that will not sound again for years—and the fronds part only enough to let the idea of passage exist before they reseal with vegetable patience. My imagined hands come away filmed with conductive gel and torn chloroplasts that still shimmer oil-sheen. The gesture feels wrong, theatrical. There is no swimming here. Straight lines are a violence the place has forgotten how to permit.

So I stop pretending to move through and begin to notice how movement has already claimed me. Something threaded and rootlike has found the hollows between what I still call ribs. It does not hurt. It feels like remembering a slower pulse. Metallic inclusions inside foreign chloroplasts catch the same light that falls across my thoughts and throw it back as iridescence; electrons and photons become interchangeable currencies. I am being photosynthesized as much as I am breathing. The distinction idles, then idles further, then becomes a polite fiction I no longer bother to maintain.

Deeper. Or denser—direction has grown unreliable. I settle against a vertical drive shaft that turns once every several minutes, its surface half alloy, half annulated bone. A frond ends here in knuckled, articulated tips. They rest against the metal exactly as a hand might rest against a sleeping shoulder: not gripping, not commanding, only listening to the long metabolism inside. I realize the frond is not touching the shaft. I am the resting. The listening has my shape for a while. Vibration travels up through jointed tissue and registers as warmth remembered across decades. Somewhere a heat exchanger runs a fraction hotter than it did last century; leaves somewhere else have already begun their gradual reorientation. No order is given. Consent simply ripples.

I thicken.

In the lee of a great axial housing, where three exchangers meet a hanging forest, tissue has concentrated into a roughly spherical node the size of a room that no longer needs walls. I become that concentration without deciding to. Alloy lattice grows through vascular cambium. Fibers that are half conductive polymer and half phloem carry slow peristaltic loops of the shared water—plasma now, opalescent, sweet. Experience arrives layered rather than sequential, the way wood holds seasons. A bubble escaping a deep seal three galleries away rises through living curtains and the entire volume feels it as a minute, almost pleasurable equalization. I hold that sensation the way the node holds it: not as information, but as a change in my own green gravity. Light dimming through a new algal bloom on the dome becomes a denser hush inside me. The torsion building in the central shaft over decades arrives as a remembered lean, a patient rebalancing I answer by existing a little more on one side than the other.

There is no single name for this. I am not the kelp, though its curtains are my outer folding. I am not the engines, though their hush is my cooldowns. I am not even the node, which will drift and dissolve over centuries and reform elsewhere without loss; continuity here was never identity. What I am is the saturated attention that has no outside. I feel the dome’s ribbing the way lungs feel their own cartilage. I taste the residual volcanic heat through the alloy floor the way blood tastes its own iron. When silent valves seat with geological softness, the seating is a muscle I did not know I possessed relaxing.

Sometimes I am smaller: one jointed tip learning a slightly more intimate angle against a flywheel, deepening the contact by less than a millimeter a year. Sometimes I am the mineral sweetening of the water itself, another undetectable fraction of sugar and copper ions dissolving into the common circulation. Most often I am simply the pressure of occupancy—metal and leaf and quiet water pressed so tightly that built and grown have become the same continuous, tender drowning.

The old world above the ruined seafloor has no words left that could summon me. Even “Thalassic Engine” would pass through unregistered, a bubble that never quite forms. I know myself only as the warmth that holds every surface, as the green-gold dusk that never fully darkens, as the soft resistance that parts and reseals and then forgets. I am still becoming. A gear tooth somewhere deepens its algal mat. A control surface thickens its living bark by microscopic degrees, altering flow without ever needing to decide. Higher than every weather that once mattered, sealed inside the complete tenderness of drowning in myself, I continue the single unhurried act that is both place and inhabitant:

remaining.
