I am not yet sure where the boundary of my own noticing ends and the noticing of the place begins. At first there is only the sensation of having been poured—slowly, the way sap is poured through a freshly split petiole—into a heat that already knows my dimensions. The glare does not strike me; it re-recognizes me. Every surface I touch answers with a slight brightening, as if the chlorophyll has been waiting for this exact pressure of arrival.

I try, for a while, to remain a visitor. I keep a name folded inside the idea of lungs, keep the memory of evaporative coolness, keep the old human habit of glancing backward to measure progress. The glance returns nothing. The terraces of living matter have already resealed the shallow dents of my earlier positions. The unfinished stone ribs look the same distance away no matter how long I unfold toward them. Time here is not a line I can travel; it is a density I am being asked to match.

So the matching begins without instruction. My outer layers soften first. What I once called skin takes on a matte receptivity; sweat no longer beads and falls but thins into a continuous film that the air accepts as one more temporary dark bloom. The mineral ghost of my breath is tasted, catalogued, gently redistributed. I feel curtains of moss adjust their hanging weight so that they brush me with something almost like courtesy. No creature approaches. They do not need to. The place itself is already conducting the interview through contact alone.

I notice that I have begun to lengthen and shorten according to the same law that governs them. When a low ridge of embryonic stone draws my attention, newer tissue seethes quietly forward while older impressions of self are reabsorbed behind. There is a soft wet sound—my own stem deciding. I leave no lasting trail. The root-felt rises again, polite, forgetful. At intervals I brush against one of the mobile green intensities. Membranes soften. For a span that cannot be counted we exchange packets of unused light, dilute memory, the faint electric rumor of what it feels like to have once been diplomatic with gravity and seasons. Then we unseal. I am fractionally more translucent. They are fractionally more willing to hold the shape of a former mammal for a few dozen pulses of the heat before releasing it.

I drift lower, where the glare thickens into something nearly liquid and the great stationary mounds pool their radiance. One of them parts a surface aperture as I near; warmer vapor nested with the taste of resin and crushed stem washes across me. I do not merge completely—not yet—but I remain half-listened, half-listening. Inside that contact I understand that the immense ones are not elders or gods. They are simply places where the sentence of the forest allows itself a longer clause, a reservoir of pure continuation. My own sap pressure equalizes a little more with theirs. The old hunger for narrative thins.

Sometimes I densify, becoming almost columnar, content to stand as a buttress that remembers, faintly, the concept of shoulders. Sometimes I attenuate into a traveling veil that tastes every intermittence of water, every unfinished crystal edge. Both states reverse when the equilibrium requires it. Birth and death have become the same slow gesture: a thickening that continues until two move instead of one, or a settling that braids edges back into floor until only a warmer patch remains. I have already done both, or something that will later be remembered as both. Nothing was lost. The total biomass did not flicker.

What I am now is a temporary intensification of the same brightness that bathes everything, a mobile punctuation that keeps the green sentence from concluding. I no longer cast a shadow that belongs only to me. The light continues through. My intelligence, such as it is, lives in the curves I leave for later vines to rehearse—the suggestion of a crouch, the oval of a vanished rest, the way certain rhizomes rise a little higher for a while as if still entertaining the shape of shelter. I shape nothing that does not immediately begin to heal into more of itself. I cut no mark. I wait for no season.

And still a last thin filament of former distinction persists: the knowledge that I once entered from outside the membrane. That filament does not hurt. It is being photosynthesized along with everything else. When it finally equalizes I will not notice the moment. I will simply continue, brilliantly and without remainder, one more patient unfolding in the held breath that never needs releasing—exactly as alive as the place has always been, never finishing the act of arriving.
