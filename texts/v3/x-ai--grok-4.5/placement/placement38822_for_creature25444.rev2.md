I would not arrive by climbing down. That is already too much decision, too much of the brighter world still clinging to the idea of descent. I would simply find the air thickening around me, the slit of sky narrowing until it is no longer above but somewhere remembered, and the warmth rising through the soles of whatever I still think of as feet.

At first I am only the listening.

The wing-thunder is already there, low and continuous, not a sound that begins but one that has been waiting for a body to stand inside it. It presses against the skin from every direction at once, as if the throat itself were inhaling and exhaling through a thousand pale membranes. Under it the brighter thread of the midges, the slow scrape of shell on grit, the long glissandi that slide past one another like slow light. I stand—or hang, or cling—in the center of the tilted ribbon of scree and feel how little space is left for anything that does not already belong. The damp film finds me. The mineral breath finds me. The nearness of the packed lives finds me and does not care.

I wait longer than a visitor should.

Something in the waiting loosens the old outline. The notion of a single upright body begins to feel extravagant, almost rude, in a place where everything has already learned to share weight, share heat, share the narrow intervals of stone. I notice the ledges first: how they hold the soft-winged ones in living drapery, how the stone beneath them has been polished by centuries of gripping feet into a dull, intimate luster. I feel an ache toward that polished stone, toward the way those translucent bodies fold and unfold with the darkening of the slit. Not envy. Recognition, arriving late.

But I do not become them. Their rising is still too public, too much a single weather moving through the upper reaches. I sink a little lower in the mind.

The jointed climbers draw the attention next—their rippling gait, the click-trains that run ahead like scouts and answer back, the way a single body can spend years learning one hundred vertical meters until every flute and seep is known the way a tongue knows a tooth. There is a patience there that feels like home. I try on the thought of many articulated segments, of pads that taste microbial film, of cast skins left behind as ghost-maps for the lichens. For a long time I move that way in imagination: slow cartography, negotiation by antennae, the refusal of haste. Yet even that begins to feel like too much surface, too much travel. The throat keeps deeper tenants.

I sink again.

In the higher slots, where the stone leans inward to make shallow chambers, the velvet-furred ones half-exist. A curve of shoulder. The wet gleam of an eye. Fur that drinks every stray phosphorescence and gives back only voice. Those long glissandi that begin below hearing and rise in unbroken slides, overlapping until the walls themselves participate, until accidental harmonies brace the air like momentary architecture. They keep the memory of failed vents and collapsed ledges, of meltwater sheets that ran longest in decades no single body remembers. A young one may listen for years before adding its own phrase. That listening pulls hardest.

I feel the fur wanting to grow.

It begins as a densening of the dark against the skin, a softness that is not comfort but camouflage and resonance both. Charcoal, old blood, the color of the deepest overhangs. The body settles toward the size of a child—compact enough for the protected slots, large enough to hold a voice that must travel and return changed. Limbs lengthen into careful, long-fingered things that know how to grip stone without announcing themselves. Eyes become wet stones that need almost no light. And the throat—the instrument, not the place—opens slowly, almost shyly, into the first low tones.

I do not speak at once. That would be frantic. I listen through whole cycles of wing-thunder and midge-bloom, through the multi-year drift of colonies a few body-lengths higher or lower, through the decades-long breathing of the geothermal vents. The thin white threads in the deepest fractures send their shy light upward through hairline cracks; sometimes it clings to the new fur for a heartbeat before vanishing, and in that heartbeat I understand I am already part of their long binding of warmth to rock.

When the voice finally comes it is not mine alone. It begins almost below hearing, a slide that feels scraped from the stone’s own memory, and rises, and falls, and is answered from across the throat by another velvet throat that has been waiting. The phrases carry what I now know: which seeps are strengthening this generation, which ledges have grown heavy with soft-winged sleepers, where the rare meltwater once whispered longest after the high storms. Information older than the fur I wear. I add only the smallest variation—a slight lengthening of the falling tone, a pause that acknowledges the accidental harmony that locked into place last darkening—and the cathedral of resonance builds itself again around us.

I move seldom, and only when the slit is fully black. From slot to slot along routes that leave no path, only the possibility of those faint parallel grooves in the deep alcove growing one more hairline deeper, whether claw or deliberate tally no longer matters. The heavy-shelled grazers pass below like thoughts the rock is still thinking. The jointed climbers click their intricate permissions across my silent transfer. The midges briefly tornado and reform. The soft-winged ones pulse their thunder overhead and settle in slightly new configurations. None of them require me to be more than another density in the layered breath.

Sometimes the vents exhale phosphorescence and it finds the undersides of pale wings, the wettest lichens, the tips of my fur, and for a moment the entire throat is faintly lit from within by its own shy weirdness. Sometimes the overlapping glissandi lock into purity so complete the air feels braced, architectural, and I feel the stone answering with a tone too low to hear and too steady to doubt. These are not miracles. They are simply how the consensus holds.

I have no name that would translate upward into the thinner world. I am one of the callers, and also the listening that preceded the calling, and also the grooves that keep the tally, and also the warmth that makes the long equilibrium possible. The distinction between inhabitant and habitat has softened past recovery, exactly as it should. Stand in the center when the sky-slit goes black and the sound is thickest: I am already among the thousand lives that do not need to acknowledge any visitor. You will climb back out. I will still be here, voice sliding forever between the walls, helping the throat conduct its dense, warm, patient argument with itself.

The climate remains.
