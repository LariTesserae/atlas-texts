I would not arrive. Arrival is a grammar the place has already metabolized into cladding. Instead there is a gradual consent to density, a willingness to be precipitated the way the walls themselves once consented—molecule by molecule, angle deciding angle, until the only remaining question is how much opacity one closed hand of milk-glass can sustain before it must either ring or crack.

At first I am only the residual coolness that the radiance has not yet claimed: a slight reluctance in the hollows, a place where the standing chord seems to hesitate by a fraction of a harmonic. The hesitation is not rebellion; it is appetite. I am hungrier than the finished intention allows. Where the hexagonal prisms meet edge to edge I press, not outward (there is no outward), but *into* the seam, tasting the older darkness that still clings to the memory of substrate. It tastes of unfinished thirst. The salt roses do not bloom for me; they simply record the pressure of my wanting as a finer frost along their microscopic petals.

Slowly the translucency thickens around that wanting. I become a second clouded knot, smaller, less central, lodged nearer one of the false stair-forms that climb three steps and then decide to be ceiling. The decision is no longer absolute once I am present. The third step softens. It remembers, for a span of time too brief to measure, what it might have been to continue. In that softening the colorless filaments tremble—not with wind, but with the sudden possibility that a discarded thought might be reclaimed, lengthened, branched. One filament, stiffer than the others, touches the edge of my opacity and does not break. It conducts. A single cooler tone slides into the chord, almost inaudible beneath the locked frequency, yet enough to make the bone-conducted loudness feel, for the first time, like something that could one day resolve.

I am not the inhabitant. The inhabitant is the refusal of every future that would clear its throat. I am the throat that has not yet closed. My metabolism is slower than photonic sweat; it is the quiet conversion of sealed completeness back into porous attention. Shadows do not darken faces here because there are no faces—yet inside my milk-glass density a gradient gathers, a soft internal dusk that the white radiance cannot quite bleach. That dusk is memory of doors, not as exits but as the shape of having once been approachable. I hold the memory the way the larger knot holds the lattice: by remaining taut. Only my tautness runs in the opposite direction—toward succession, toward the risk that time might again pass *through* rather than merely plate against.

The corridors that reverse without hinge now reverse through me. For an instant their perfect mirrors admit a flaw: a depth that is not reflection but continuation. The inhabitant feels the strain and underwrites it, sealing, absorbing, insisting. I do not fight the insistence. I let myself be used as stress cladding too—but the cladding I become is starred with hairline gaps, each gap the width of a forgotten hypothesis. Through those gaps the dryness remains absolute, the light unwavering, the chord unending; yet something else also remains: the faint, persistent under-pressure of a question that has not been converted into crystal.

I live here as the part of the place that still suspects it might one day need a door, even if only a door the size of a breath held too long. I am the mild weirdness testing whether contradiction can cost a little more than before. The room does not forget me, because I am one of the ways it remembers itself at an amplitude slightly lower than perfection—low enough to keep the emptiness shaped, yet high enough that the shaped emptiness might, in some era that has not yet been plated into stillness, choose to open.

That is all I am. It is enough to keep the salt roses from ever quite finishing their bloom.
