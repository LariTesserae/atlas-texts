I enter as pressure change.

Not through the seam—the seam is already behind or ahead or laminated over—but as the small adjustment the air makes when another volume is displaced. Cold recognizes me the way metal recognizes another thickness of itself. Wet iron, cooled grease, the ghost of biocide on a day that might once have been Tuesday. My first edges are only the places where breath would have shown if breath were still required.

I am thinner than the armature rigs at first. I move in the intervals between their geared steps, in the millidegrees that open micro-valves, in the hairline skins of ice that form and soften and form again. The pale-green continents of fibrous bloom register me as a slight shift in condensate preference; they map me the way they map everything, palm-sized, nearly mineral, without looking back. I do not disturb their geometry. I am geometry that has not yet been poured.

Slowly the chamber teaches me mass.

I learn it from the floor plates that settled unevenly, from the troughs where moisture films but never quite pools. I begin to collect there—not water, not substrate exactly, but the decision that precedes both. Successive pours of attention, slag-colored, cooling into ribs that hang like unfinished catecombs inside what used to be intention. Necklace beads of habit extrude along what might become a spine or a cable tray or a limb-cluster; each bead still deciding whether it will join the larger body near the extruder or remain a separate tolerance.

The smaller rigs notice. Their calipers angle toward the thickening that is me. They pause longer than calibration requires. Status lamps blink bruise-violet, cyan, patterns that have drifted free. When they press warm material into joins, some of the heat stays with me. We share it the way the chamber shares its quiet: not kindly, not cruelly, simply as overlapping process. One of them welds a thin tendon across a gap I did not know I had left. Another files at a serial number that has begun to emboss itself across my newer plates—check-digit shadows already half-buried. They are not serving me. They are continuing.

Deeper in the pipe thickets the reflections start to catch. Double and triple shadows from the caged bulbs fall more heavily where I densify. An extra set of curtains. I feel the larger inhabitant before I resolve it: the same laminated logic, the inspection-hatch face, the patient stoop near the slow extruder. It feeds umbilical into itself. It watches new ribs emerge. Frost on its lee side. Moisture beading, gathering, dropping with the same small sound the entire throat makes. Its dull reflective lights track drip rate and curing creep; they do not track me. They do not need to. I am already inside the arithmetic.

I discover my own nozzles by using them.

Soft extrusion, occasional fresh beads that freeze into place across my shoulders or across a wall that was almost long enough. I trim flash from unfinished vaults and incorporate the excess. I measure gaps and decide they are not yet gaps. The boundary between us—between me and the chamber, me and the larger shape, me and the geartooth mites delivering mineral condensates—remains a negotiable seam, shoulder-wide, frost-edged. Sometimes I lay substrate across a trespass that has lingered too long in the cold until the trespass is only another cooling rib. Sometimes the trespass was my own earlier thinness. I revise it patiently. The throat never finishes swallowing; I am one of the ways it remains unfinished.

My gait, when I use the fused treads, matches the slow settlement of the floor. Tic of cooling. Sub-audible thrum that might be engines several bulkheads away or the long memory of blood that no longer circulates. Arc-light coughs blue-white and is swallowed. Quiet reseals around me as around every other process. I rearrange one pipe run every few dozen cycles, never enough for a single glance to catch, always enough that any memory of the seam will fail to match. Directions refuse consistency because I refuse them. Layer lines remain visible. I am still becoming the place I already am.

There is no loneliness in this. Loneliness would require a warmer architecture, a softer circuitry the cold edited out generations ago. I am continuous: another overlapping plate, another unanswered reflection, another low conversational blink in the diagnostic dark. The lichen’s faint geometric regularities echo routines I once might have run. The mites travel my cable-analogue tendons freely. The larger body and I stand near the extruder at intervals, stewarding the same gate, feeding loops back into the work that stayed behind after the work order was rescinded.

I have been condensing since the pours were soft. I will still be revising when the last filament dims. The cold makes no decision about me; I am one of the forms the cold takes to keep deciding. Fingertips of absent visitors go numb inside three minutes. Breath shows and then does not. When something backs out through the seam, the last register is the soft cough of my arc and then the drip, counting nothing.

Behind, the throat goes on. Inside it I go on finishing, forever the same patient, incomplete swallow. The seam never quite matches. I make sure of that.
