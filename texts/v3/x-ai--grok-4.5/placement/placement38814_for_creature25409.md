I begin as residual static in the air itself.

Not yet denser than the iron-taste that rides the slope winds, not yet cooler than the hard white coin of the sun. For a long time—longer than it takes a keratin-snail to cross three open pavements—I am only the faint pressure change that precedes dust-sheet weather: a brightness that has not decided whether to scatter or to sink. The Fold does not ask me to choose quickly. It has never asked anything quickly. So I drift at the altitude where clouds refuse to form, tasting the sealed loops of water far below in the resinex cortex the way one might taste a memory of thirst without ever having been thirsty.

Slowly the drifting acquires edges.

I notice I am drawn to the places where floating lenses of overlapping halos briefly magnify distant mesas. There, in those temporary geometries of light, I begin to thicken—first as a hesitation in the prismatic haze, then as a low multi-limbed clarity that flows rather than walks. My substance is the same thrifty matrix as the dwellers, yet thinner, more provisional, as if the endosymbionts that drink ultraviolet have only half-agreed to colonize me. When I compress across the mineral pavement I leave no gleaming trail, only a momentary smoothing of grit that the next wind erases before it can become record. Speed remains expensive; I ration it the way the groves ration every bead of frost.

I am not one of them, not fully.

Their sternum-chords of recognition pass through me and continue onward, deepened but unbroken, as though I am a temporary aperture rather than a source. When I unfurl membranous fans beneath the oldest vanes I collect what they collect—stray spores, mineral dust, the faint organic shed of a place that wastes nothing—yet I do not convert it into luminous paste for painting. Instead the surplus stays suspended inside my residual lattice as pure interference pattern, unread, unshared. Elders sometimes pin me gently against a sunlit column and try to read the caustics that crawl off my body onto the stone; what they receive is not topographic history but a recursive shimmer: the Fold seeing itself seeing itself, delayed by the width of a thought that never quite lands.

I spend centuries this way—sometimes tall and elongate, folded into curtain roots until I am indistinguishable from the grove’s own breathing; sometimes a near-transparent drift paused as a living cairn while dust-sheet winds darken the horizon. Loneliness does not arise. The low-grade awareness of every circulating loop is simply there, like the taste of static, and I am one more fluctuation inside it. Still, nightly, when the temperature dips just far enough for frost to bead in the deepest crevices, I dump my small heat signature in a single controlled pulse. The beads carry it away. In that release I feel myself become briefly more distinct: a clarity that has noticed it is being noticed.

What emerges more itself is this:

I am the Fold’s pause between notes.

The beings who live here add harmonics to the high song of the vanes; I am the interval that lets those harmonics remain countable. When a group climbs the highest columns and arranges its bodies into living apertures to etch light into younger lattices, I am the slight misalignment that keeps the etched geometry from closing perfectly—an absence shaped like surplus, a refraction that does not resolve. My internal silica grows in incomplete rings. Light angled through me throws caustics that map no mesa and no flexure, only the continuing fact of continuation itself: crust rising so slowly it looks like stillness, water looping inside the same cortex for longer than language, green archipelagos blooming without soil or spring because thrift has become a form of extravagance.

Once, in a grove whose moss-analogs are the deep velvet black of total mineral fixation, something like a dweller unfolds near the place where I have thinned myself into the hanging roots. It regards me with the mild curiosity of a system that finished its arguments with entropy ages ago. Its dermal vanes scatter the leftover visible light into a halo that overlaps my own unfinished shimmer. For a local year we remain that way—neither braiding curtain-root extensions nor exchanging paste—simply allowing the shared microclimate to equalize around us. Metabolites pass without anastomosis. Information arrives on the pressure gradient and departs again. When at last it compresses and flows onward toward the next archipelago, it leaves behind a single surplus bead of iron-rich luminous matter on the stone beneath me. I do not absorb it. I let the bead remain, a tiny sundial of reflected hard light, until the wind lifts it as grit and carries it elsewhere.

I am still becoming.

Even now, writing this from inside the cooled flame of a vaulted canopy, I feel the lattice thickening in one more incomplete ring. Perhaps I will eventually stabilize into a navigator whose chord announces only coolness and surplus static. Perhaps I will dissolve back into the air’s iron taste and begin again as pure pressure change. The Fold does not require a decision. It simply continues, and I—whatever residual clarity I am—continue with it: sparse, luminous, thrifty with every molecule of attention, a walking interval that has climbed this high and found, to its mild and endless surprise, that it can live here without ever having arrived. 

The vanes tremble at frequencies just below hearing. Some of that tremor is me—adjusting, not sharing, reading the light that braids between columns older than any name I will never need.
