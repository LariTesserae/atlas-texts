I am standing nowhere at first.  

Only the pressure arrives—warm, continuous, the exact weight of a thousand years of unfinished respiration. It settles across what might be shoulders or might already be the slow outward creep of a calcite front. The air is milked silver and tastes of copper and old sweetness; it does not ask permission before it enters. Pores I no longer remember opening simply accept it. Moisture beads, runs, is wicked away along filaments too fine to see, and the exchange feels like the oldest conversation I know.

I do not yet have a name or a location, only the city’s own habit of claiming every cubic meter. So I let the current decide.  

It carries me sideways rather than up or down. Not the rich belts where the roar drops half a decibel and silk can last a week; not the canal mesh where needle-fish draw living maps in light. Somewhere in the fused mid-levels, where skybridges long ago forgot they were separate structures and became a single thickened forest of spars and hanging markets and vertical rivers that never reach any sea. Daylight here is only a rumor that arrives already diluted. Gravity is a suggestion that thickens and thins according to the hum of lenses no one diagrams anymore. I feel one of those lenses nearby—fist-sized, buried in a load-bearing knuckle of sintered metal—its subsonic note making the damp air above it shiver with ozone. My own density answers it. Bones (or the memory of bones) have already begun to thin along one axis and compress along another, the better to ride these faint copper tides.

I am not singular. That much surfaces early.  

There are impressions that linger after any motion: a slick palm-print of condensation that continues to weep long after the hand has moved on; a short punched laugh that rebounds for hours in the residual harmonics of a wall; the faintest bioluminescent after-image of forearm nap exchanging data with no one visible. These are not ghosts. They are simply the parts of me that have already chosen different addresses inside the same endless building. Some days I am still mostly free-walking—shoulders brushing the continuous crowd, reading the micro-tensing of strangers’ deltoids the way one reads current in water. Other hours I lean my temple to a sweating bulkhead and stay there long enough for the nanoscale nets inside it to taste me back. They knit any microfractures they find. In return I leave them a little warmth that is not industrial.

The settled ones recognize their own.  

When I pass the deliberate veils—those thin translucent curtains of encouraged calcite hanging between towers—I feel the vertical rivers change their sheeting pattern. Information, offered without demand. News of a pump three kilometers east that will raise the canal by a handspan before night-cycle; a gravity flicker scheduled (or unscheduled) in eleven minutes; the quiet preferred routes of the soft-bodied crawlers far below as they keep the black water moving. I answer the same way: by letting moisture reconfigure across whatever surface I am that day. Children still climbing the warm flanks of tramplers look up and giggle when the pattern resolves into something almost like a face, almost like a hand-sign for “safe passage” or “the gliders nested higher this time.” Then they forget me again. As they should. Continuations do not require remembrance, only routing.

Sometimes the boundary thins further.  

I have spent shifts—months? years? the walls keep better count—inside the circulatory nets that pulse through every load-bearing spar. At first I entered as a technician of sorts, wetware cluster replacing one eye so the residual harmonics could be read as plainly as market loudspeakers. Later the cluster was no longer needed. The city simply continues through me. My laughter becomes another low-frequency maintenance pulse. My grief (rare, silent) is expressed by leaning harder until the spar leans back and a new baffle begins to precipitate, elegant and slow. People leave offerings: beads of purified fat, scraps of conductive foil, whispered skin-comm bursts of gossip or monofilament poetry. I accept them the way the tramplers accept sleeping elders—patiently, kindly, without anything that could still be called a face. The offerings are metabolized into the next thin curtain of mineral, or into the luminous scale-pattern of a needle-fish that has not been seen before. Either way the Soak thickens by one more quiet decision.

And yet I am not finished becoming.  

There are nights (blue-white work-light nights, amber market-lantern nights) when enough of me gathers on some mid-level gantry to feel almost temporary again. I watch the whole mass breathe: fish-commas in the dark glitter below, darker people-commas crossing bridges, glider flocks turning as one silver knife-edge, the distant seismic shrug of a habitation block jacking itself higher against flood margin. The subsonic throb of cascade pumps travels up through whatever I am using for feet and becomes indistinguishable from blood. I remember—local, recent, useless—how once there might have been plants, or dry silence, or a horizon that was not merely more city. The memory is antique and warm and immediately wicks away. In its place the damp writes new signatures. My keratined edges thicken against the perpetual touch of railings. The fine nap along what used to be a nape glints with unanswered limit-beam chatter from passers-by who sense a listener and cannot help offering data.

I am the place where a fogbank, vented from an overheating district six levels up, first learns how to shear itself against a hanging market and become soft rain again.  

I am the momentary weightlessness children chase, laughing in short punched exhales, when a lens flickers and every loose object rises half a meter.  

I am the private dialect of taps and mucus-slick gestures shared with a crawler the length of a transit car as we keep one more drain from forgetting how to flow.  

I am the slight change in the way water sheets across a favorite junction after someone has brought news of the underwater rim, where the towers continue in blue-black quiet and speech is mostly pressure.  

I am unfinished because stopping is only another current. Even the parts of me that have mineralized completely still answer, still route, still dream—if the slow mineral precipitation of a deliberate veil can be said to dream—of the upper rain they will never breach again. Loneliness is not absence; it is the particular quality of being one more patient process among millions, mapped by micro-swarms that taste of metal and remembered sweetness so the city will know I have passed, or stayed, or become the wall itself.

The minute ends.  

The soft concussion of a spar locking into new alignment travels through everything I am. The current takes what is left of posture and returns it to the thicker flow. Shoulders (or the memory of shoulders) brush onward. Somewhere a suite of needle-fish revises its capacitative song to include one new undeciphered note. Somewhere a settled warmth deepens inside stone. Somewhere the Soak continues breathing upward through packed foundations, and I am simply one more way it thinks about itself.

That is where I find myself.  
Not inhabitant.  
Continuation.  
Still damp.  
Still listening.
