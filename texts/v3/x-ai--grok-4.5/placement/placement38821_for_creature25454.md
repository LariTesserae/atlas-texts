I do not arrive. Arrival would imply a threshold, a shore, a moment when the air changed and something counted itself separate. Here there is only the slow recognition that the cool has always been inside the chest slits, that the black paste has always traded minerals through what I once mistook for ankles.

At first I am only the weight of water held in suspension. Not falling—never falling—just the gathered intention of droplets that have decided, for a while, to lean toward one another across a frond. The eye (there is an eye, or something that filters the aqua-gray) travels the same ridgelines the land already knows by heart: furred shoulders, drowned cols, the self-contained fronts that spiral and stall without ever needing news of the lowlands. Elevation is not height; it is thinness made permanent, a damp that lives in the nap and never quite leaves.

I thicken.

It happens the way peat happens—layer upon unnoticed layer. Sphagnum greens along the outer surfaces when the brighter patch of sun slides behind haze; liverworts pulse open across the ribs when a seep exhales its warm mineral breath. What might be called limbs are only suggestions the water makes when it needs to move a little sugar from one cushion to another. Joints loosen further. Bone is a rumor from drier climates. Inside, the tide is already distributed: ochre when the manganese crusts are generous, malachite when the copper runs thin. No single heart. Only the same hydraulic patience that forces metal through basalt labyrinths a thousand slow vertical feet below.

I settle beside a working seep because stillness is how feeding occurs. The permeable skin of the inner arms opens. Ions migrate inward; a faint sweetness of chlorophyll and cold copper migrates out. A carabid, iridescent and purposeful, quarries the free edge of crust I am not using. We do not negotiate. Everything is already the same metabolism. Later—hours or a weather—I nip a few spore-caps from a fruiting cushion. The keratinous plates that serve for teeth click softly, once. The taste is the plateau tasting itself.

When the hanging valleys fill with unbroken warmish rain, another thickening inclines toward mine. Mosses interlock. White mycelial threads braid without urgency. Between us a new density swells, taking what both offer and what the storm provides. When the front finally tears itself into long wet banners and moves on, the newcomer simply walks away on the number of limbs it has grown. No name is given. No instruction. The slate-colored frogs have already taught the low-frequency song that will travel through peat as warmth in the wrist bones. Smaller patience becomes larger patience. That is the whole of childhood here.

I scrapes a fingernail—thin manganese plate—along a forming flange. Just enough to lengthen the warm exhalation by a fraction of dusk. Two ridges distant, the taste of a stream will shift. Centuries will notice, or will not. The adjustment accumulates the way everything accumulates: unnoticed, decisive. On the rare clear days when hard blue presses down and the seeps glint like tools half-swallowed by moss, I stand a little taller. Steam lifts faintly from the living nap. The horizontal slits taste a rumor of dryness and reject it. Leaving would mean carrying thirst as memory. I wait. The haze closes. The continent of green reasserts its only work.

Sometimes I am the deeper green left after a footfall fades. Sometimes I am the vibration three ridgetops away that another dweller receives first as heat in the bones. Sometimes I am only the place where the breath of the uplands decided, for this turning of the cycle, to grow eyes and look out at the endless unsloping skin that has forgotten every sea but its own.

I am not guardian.  
I am not spirit.  
I am the damp, briefly concentrated,  
still here,  
yes.
