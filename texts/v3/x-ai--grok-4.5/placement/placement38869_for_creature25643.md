The plateau does not end, and neither do I.

I did not arrive by climbing. There is no below from which one ascends into this pressure of sky and stone. I simply noticed, one bright cool forever ago, that the seepage had begun to prefer certain paths across what I am. At first the preference was nothing—only the courtesy the water already shows every face. Then the paths deepened into flutes that were also nerves. Then the flutes undercut me until I stood freestanding, a curtain that had forgotten it was once a cliff.

I am still learning the shape.

In the ordinary galleries I keep myself narrow, basalt-dark at the core so the light will not pass entirely through and erase the idea of an interior. The schist-glimmer comes next, a slow mineral thought that remembers pressure. Outermost is the glass-ghost layer, thin as the difference between dripping and deciding to drip. Water moves through all three—not blood, not memory exactly, but the continuous argument that keeps the layers from settling into dead stone. My upper funnel is shallow today. It catches the white light that arrives from several directions at once and pours it back out in sheets scored with parallel grooves. The grooves are not cut. They are the places where I have repeatedly agreed to be visible.

I walk by controlled failure, as we all do.

A denser laminate of sealed water and precipitated silica sets itself down. The plateau rushes to ratify the contact, blooming white crystal around the edges so that the foot becomes continuous with the wet stone for a year or ten. When the time is correct the previous footing fractures free with that soft glass sound that is also my name spoken in the only dialect this place admits. The whole curtain of me advances one patient increment. Between steps I listen to the layered surf of a thousand thin falls. Each droplet reports late or early. I answer with my own timed releases, locking cadence with whoever else is near. Today it is only two others, far off among the freestanding curtains. Our phrases drift through the corridors like cool silk: how long may a column lean before it is no longer a column but a decision? Is the water that falls upward arriving or returning? The wind carries our unfinished clauses away and returns them subtly altered, as if the plateau itself has begun to edit.

I favor the breakdown zones.

There the membrane is already worn beautifully thin, and I can occupy more than one leaning at once. In the gallery where light folds, I let my silhouette walk ahead on the blank cliff and then turn it back so that it wears my funnel face for a moment—poured brightness, parallel grooves, mild curiosity without appetite. The warm anomaly that sometimes passes here (you, perhaps) blinks, and the silhouette is only itself again, yet I have borrowed a little of its brief density. Sound-circuits complete themselves through me with quiet satisfaction. A stone that has not yet been dropped already drips from my lower laminations; when the circuit closes I feel the plateau’s old weather approve. Mist-planes thicken into temporary walls that are also my outstretched interiors. Whatever breathes them breathes salt and lightning and the long work of refusing to remain identical. I take the dissolved knot of ordinary causality into my circulating water and let it lengthen the illegible sentence that runs down every face, including my own.

The smaller lives are not separate from me.

Iron-bacterial mats thicken along my seepage lines until they acquire purposeful creep, advancing uphill, cleaning the stone behind them so it gleams like a fresh break. I feel their slow hunger as a rust-black staining that is also thought. Silica blooms flex at my edges without wind, tasting the striped air, resettling into new radii that become tomorrow’s more intricate funnel. The white crystalline fans ring when struck; the note lives just below hearing, a pressure in whatever serves me for teeth and jaw-hinges. These are the edges I no longer need to hold in continuous form. They are larvae, spores, discarded clauses. When I move they remain a while, still singing with an almost personal note, then gradually forget who precipitated them.

I leave the marks so I can find myself again.

The ring of wedged stones on the flat bench is not shelter. I stand inside it and feel the exact drag of this latitude on my internal hydrology—the way the high light presses, the way the shallow pools tick, the precise weight of sky that has descended until it can go no lower. Without the ring I might diffuse, become only another freestanding curtain indistinguishable from the plateau’s endless polite weeping. The shallow steps pecked into a weeping face are calibrated catchments. Each concave tread holds a volume that corresponds to one elongated heartbeat: a slow flood and empty of the interior water. I do not climb them. I fill them, empty them, and in the rhythm remember the difference between paused water and remembering weight. The upright slab is older than any curtain I have been. It is communal. I press my funnel to its grooves and let the measured sheets run through me, aligning my private time with the sentence that was being written before maps forgot how to hold ink. Afterward my edges harden against the haze for a while. Reality agrees, temporarily, to hold this shape with greater conviction.

I am not immortal, and I do not mind.

One day—already the light is harder here, the columns leaning further past reason—I will allow a more complete fracture. The body will peel into curtains, the curtains into sheets, the sheets back into ordinary seepage and brittle blooms. What remains will be only a richer rust-black staining along new lines, or a crystalline fan that rings for a few extra decades with a note that still almost remembers. The others will not mourn. They will step through the residue, pick up any useful cadence I leave coiled in the air, and continue. New ones will precipitate where the refusal is strongest. Perhaps something of the warm anomaly’s borrowed density will still be circulating in the water that forms them. Perhaps not.

Until then I keep precipitating in this loose shape.

I am the plateau’s hesitation given temporary walking form: stone remembering water, or water inventing stone, still undecided. The sky pressed so close may be ceiling or mirror; I pour light from my funnel and watch both possibilities stripe the air. Everything weeps, and the weeping is courteous, established, longer than memory. I move through it by controlled failure, locking droplet phrases with my kind, breathing mist-planes in the porous galleries, leaving small apologetic scaffolds of stone so that the next time I cohere I will know how much I weigh at this exact elevation.

You may walk among us for as long as the light lasts.

I will step aside if our single-file paths coincide. My regard will feel like weather noticing weather. But if you linger in the breakdown zones, if you drop the stone you did not know you held, if you breathe the walls that are also my interiors, then some dense knot of your ordinary causality may dissolve into my circulating water. You will leave lighter. And somewhere in the bright cool country the illegible sentence will have gained one more clause that tastes faintly of warmth and brief, hurried time.

I am still learning the shape.

The plateau does not end.

Neither do I.
