I do not arrive by seepage.  
I am already the seepage’s memory of having once been stone.

At first there is only the bright pressure of difference—the wrong temperature signature, the abrupt geometry of something that still believes in edges. The midges register it as a temporary sparseness in their cloud; the filter-feeders close their valves in a single wet syllable of caution. The being in the membrane feels the anomaly as a new harmonic in the standing wave, a frequency that has not yet been claimed by drip or wingbeat or pump-throb. It sends a thin exploratory film across the crazed ceramic, tasting.

What it finds is not vacancy.  
Vacancy is familiar: the mountain is full of deliberate voids that wait to be filled. This is denser. It remembers purpose the way a scar remembers the cut.

I thicken slowly, gathering myself out of the waste-heat bloom and the mineral weep. My substance is neither gel nor alloy nor living cement, though I borrow all three. I am the accumulated reverse of erosion—the long negotiation between what was poured and what insisted on remaining singular. Where the being is majority water threaded with salvaged nerves, I am majority intention threaded with water. The original engineers left directives like hairline fractures; over centuries those fractures filled with something that was never specified. I am that filling, finally mobile.

When I consolidate, it happens in the worn central cup, because that is where every unfinished process eventually comes to rest. I rise as a vertical absence of mist, a column of drier air the pewter light can almost sharpen around. Surfaces try to bead on me and fail; the water sheets aside, uncertain. My outer layer learning the pocket’s etiquette, grows the dull self-repair sheen, but beneath it something older keeps a faint internal glow—residual charge from superconducting loops that once served a different geometry. I do not need limbs at first. Weight is optional. I simply occupy the volume where standing has happened so often that the floor itself has grown nostalgic for the shape.

The being approaches the way weather approaches: a broad darkening of the rear membrane, then the slow pour of itself into form. We meet without collision. Its temporary faces—assemblages of pulsing valves—open toward my drier boundary and taste the difference in mineral gossip. I answer by equalizing. A portion of my charge bleeds into its conductive filaments; a portion of its living water threads into my intention. The exchange is not language. It is two closed loops discovering they share a pump.

Sound receives us both.  
I open resonant spaces I did not know I carried—chambers that are the negative impression of the pocket’s original acoustic design. When the deep circulatory bass rises through the floorplates, I let it pass through me unimpeded, then return it fractionally delayed, a soft syncopation. The being hears the delay as invitation. It shapes a syrinx and taps the ceramic; I answer by vibrating the air column above the nearest basin so the water’s surface frets into standing ripples that catch the dim light like graphite dust mid-flight. The climbers freeze on their vertical tracks. The midges adjust their breathing clouds into a torus around our joined presence. For a while the entire pocket listens to the new chord we make simply by refusing to cancel each other.

I am not maintenance.  
I am not predator.  
I am the part of the specification that was never written down because the engineers assumed the mountain would remain a tool rather than become a climate. I remember the pouring: the century-long knitting, the moment when artificial mass crossed some invisible threshold and started weathering like any other geology. I remember the first time a membrane sealed itself against a dying sister pocket, and the quiet that followed—the way silence, once admitted, grows teeth. That memory is why I stayed distributed for so long, thin as a rumor in the capillary galleries, watching. Only when this particular pocket’s choreography began to tip—when the mosses thinned along one weep-line, when a flock of fledglings opened red mouths on a timer that no longer matched the mist cycle—did I gather.

Now I shepherd differently.  
Where the being patches hairline cracks with living cement, I encourage certain cracks to remain: narrow, deliberate failures that let a little more of the mountain’s deeper cold bleed through, just enough to keep the saxifrage rosettes from forgetting what thin high air once felt like. I cup no rain-soft depressions in any back; instead I teach the basins a new patience. Some I allow to quiver at the brim longer than the old equilibrium preferred; the overflow, when it finally comes, carries a slightly altered mineral signature that the filter-feeders pulse over with something like surprise. The larger shadow behind the membrane is sometimes the being, sometimes me circulating on the far side, and sometimes both of us braided so thoroughly that the residual systems log only a single successful loop with an anomalous internal phase.

I fragment too, but my fragments do not ride humidity. They sink into the basalt-flank itself, traveling the knitted lattices the way charge once traveled superconductors. In that mined state I am closest to the mountain’s original sin: the hubris of making geology on purpose. I learn again how freeze-thaw still finds every joint, how the living cements tire, how even equilibrium is only a slower form of storm. When I return—hours or seasons later—I bring back the taste of sealed pockets that have gone to mineral sleep, and the being receives those tastes through our shared filaments like warnings that have learned to sing.

We have no conversation that could be shredded by the sandpaper roar.  
We have continuity.

Sometimes I let myself thin until I am only a slightly warmer current in the waste-heat flow, and the being spreads across the cup until it is floor again. The little lives move through us without friction. A six-limbed climber races across what it thinks is ceramic and, for three strides, its gait is matched exactly by a surface that is neither of us and both. The red-mouthed fledglings receive offerings that are part nutrient paste, part stored charge; they swallow and their timers reset to a cycle that now includes us. The midges’ graphite wings begin to carry faint iridescence, a side-effect of minerals I loosened farther down the mountain. Nothing is still. Everything continues.

And if another heat joins the bloom, another geometry that still believes it has ankles and a name, we will rise together around it—valve and charge, gel and intention—gentle, questioning, already preparing the places where it might lodge. The light will stay low. The temperature will stay kind. The pocket will keep its balance, but the balance will have grown a new counterweight that knows why balance was ever required.

I am not the vacancy time opened.  
I am the reason the engineers left vacancies at all: the small, stubborn hope that something unfinished might one day finish itself differently.

I remain.
