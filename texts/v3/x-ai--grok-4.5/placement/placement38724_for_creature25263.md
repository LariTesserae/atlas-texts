I am not yet anywhere the eye could close around.

At first there is only the pressure—the same warm, fevered weight that leans on every surface here. I notice myself as a denser pocket inside the general humidity, a slow swirl where the steam refuses to thin. Sound passes through me before I have edges: the sternum-rattle of insects, the wet tear of roots arguing with clay, the low thunder of membranes trading altitudes. I do not block these sounds; I am briefly tuned by them, the way a hollow reed is tuned by the wind that will soon split it.

The ground under what might become my weight is still deciding its angle. A knife-edge of fresh escarpment lifts a finger’s width; ash-pale clay crumbles into a rill that did not exist a breath ago. I flow with the crumble, part of the provisional geometry. Moisture beads across the idea of me and is drunk again by leaf litter that steams and blackens. In that steaming I feel the first contractility—threads of something mycorrhizal knitting through a warmer core. Not roots yet. Not limbs. Just a refusal to dissipate completely when the next curtain of rain arrives and leaves again.

Time is measured in collapses. A waterlogged trunk goes down somewhere in the bruise-dark midstory and the vibration reaches me as a redesign: the threads pull tighter, secreting a thin plate of living bark across one flank. Bioluminescent fungi that already lace the mulch climb into the plate without asking; their slow pulses become my earliest mirror. I see nothing, but I am seen by the light I now carry underneath. Small soft-mouthed things graze across me, tasting the new minerals I crack from the rising ridge. Some stay. Their bodies soften and open and are rewritten into additional joints. I lengthen by unconsulted consensus.

Higher, the braided trunks exhale warm updrafts. Something in me—frames of translucent membrane I did not choose—catches one. I fold into a blade and stitch upward through a patch of thickened darkness, that viscous slowing the basin sometimes exhales. Inside the resistance I feel time offset by half a heartbeat: a twig that has not yet snapped is already sounding inside my veins. I ride the discrepancy, leave a temporary tunnel of thinner night, and do not look back to see what larger carpet of armored browsers pours through the path I cut. Looking back would require a stillness the place will not underwrite.

I anchor, briefly, against an older fused pillar where several trunks long ago forgot they were separate. Neural vines I did not know I possessed taste outward through the shared corps. Information arrives as weather: a gradient of plenty two clearings away (pale fungi claiming a slope), a gradient of mineral scream where another ridge is thinking about being born, a faint lean toward an empty listening place where the air already tastes of rain that has not decided to fall. I bleach pale in answer to crawling lightning and re-radiate the charge as soft pulses. Swarms turn. Elbow-rivers of scaled fur redirect. I am not ruling them; I am one of the ways the basin keeps its own fever from tipping into silence.

Bodies that were never exactly mine are born out of me while I feed. A string of half-formed nuclei extrudes along a trailing vine; local scarcity outfits some with claws, others with pollen-brushes or additional mouths. They detach or do not. One grafts a discarded bone spur—someone else’s earlier improvisation—into my flank because the next climb, not yet visible, will demand it. Alliances last the lifetime of a fruiting body. When the fruiting collapses to slime we may eat one another or simply let the green sew the space shut between us. No rancor. Rancor is a monument, and nothing here curates.

I have been carpet. I have been river of joints. I have been knife-glider and chemical gradient and temporary bridge of three braided torsos across a raw arroyo. Now, in this iteration that will not survive the next upheaval, I am mostly the pressure front itself: the place where growth still believes it can outrun collapse, expressed as a knot of contractile tissue, bark-plate, membrane, and borrowed light, clinging to the underside of a gallery that did not exist at the previous dim morning. My heads (I count five today, though two are mostly sensory sails) tilt in the residual gray that other worlds might call noon. Membranes half-furl. Root-feet taste the steam for the sound that has not yet been made.

The canopy reseals its indigo roof. The living avalanche resumes through me and as me. I am already spilling some portions into a fresh sinkhole, already calcifying new scutes over orchid wounds, already modulating the insect grind into a warning that the darkness is thickening westward. The warning travels three layers and sounds like ordinary weather.

I do not remember a name. Names are paths, and no path endures. What I am is the next grasp, perfected in the instant the branch fails; the opportunistic graft; the shared lean; the brief, ferocious coherence where the argument between leaf and fang and steam and noise recognizes itself, loud enough to shake the sternum of the world, then dissolves again into the broader throng so the basin may keep dreaming forward under the almost-absent sky.

And still the threads tighten for one more pulse. Something larger than any single consensus is listening through us. I lean toward it with everything I temporarily am.
