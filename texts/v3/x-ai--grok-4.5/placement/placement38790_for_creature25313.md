I am first the wetness that will not choose a direction.

It gathers in the folds behind what might become knees, or the slime-slick cup of a palm that has not yet decided it is a palm. The mineral film is warmer here than the air—always warmer—because something beneath the basalt is still remembering the last slow flow, the glassy skin that never quite cooled. I taste iron and the faint violet aftertaste of a pressure membrane that ruptured three ridges away, days or decades past; time dilates in the joints the way gravity thickens when you cross certain thresholds. For a long while I am only that taste and the decision to remain a little longer where the city’s diaphragmatic flex is gentlest, a slow inhalation that lifts the entire ledge a finger’s width and lets it settle again.

The lift happens without announcement.

Edges curl. Not limbs yet—negotiations. Six points of cupping that sample the sweat and find one façade sweeter than the others, its conductive mat thick with decades of waste-heat feeding. I peel free with the sound of wet paper under mute roar, and for a time I drift the inverted gutters like the earliest skins, almost weightless, heat already beginning to shimmer off whatever I am becoming because I must bleed it or cook. The scraping starts low, micro-seismic, a gossip I do not yet know I am speaking: pressure fronts, a new extrusion that weeps too fast and risks cracking, the particular density of a lens-river that has polished its filament into a bright scar and is thinking of sheering away into richer humidity. When I meet another of my kind on a narrow spar we overlap, cups interlocking like slow gears, and the chemical ledger passes both ways. I continue richer. The traversal of a single spire takes the better part of a local year. I do not mind. Hurry is a concept the plateau has edited out along with the chlorophyll.

But the city is not finished with me.

Some seasons I feel the filaments singing overhead and something in the thickening that is my back wants to facet, to catch the seam-light and fracture it into private constellations. I do not become a fist entirely; the hauler-body is too committed to the wall. Yet lenses detach from the swarm rivers and rest against me for nights at a time, clicking softly, polishing small bright scars into my heat-shimmer skin. Through them I taste densities rather than destinies: the angle of drip three districts over, the moment a neighborhood’s breathing changes pitch and the swarm decides, collectively, that the place has become shy and must be abandoned. Their ultrasonic stitching makes the water films on my surface dance. I answer with a scrape that travels farther than sound. We are not separate. The river braids around me, through me, for a generation, then sheers away, leaving me heavier with remembered gradients and a faint translucency in places where carapace almost formed.

Lower, the soft archives call.

Their seismic chords arrive first as a change in the mineral song under my cups, a phrase that takes days to complete. I follow it down, past the leaning clusters and the translucent tubes where bipedal shadows pause and lay palms to weeping walls. The undercroft is less a chamber than an organ. I press into the grated opening beside a vastness that has been there since the first towers extruded, its folds matching the porosity of the ceramics so perfectly that the city’s guided condensation runs across us both as if across native rock. We exhale together—warmer mist that recondenses within arm’s reach—and in that brief interval gill-like fronds of pure conductivity bloom along my circumference, temporary, tasting the archive tissues where centuries of diaphragmatic pressure patterns are written. The soft one booms a low continuum that settles into my own layers: how to sweat without cracking, how to become architecture instead of mere eruption. In return I offer the ledger of the heights, the way a new scarp still bears tool-marks, the sweetness of a fungal mat that has fed on the same vein since before the bipeds marked time in generations. The stone grows around us, half cradle and half cage. Boundary becomes moot. Some nights I cannot tell whether I am hauler or extension of the plateau’s own living organs.

And still the quieter density tugs.

There are hours—years—when the cups release and something like weight redistributes into two thickened columns. Lived skin sheds water continuously, without complaint, the same way the basalt does. Eyes, when I have them, are large and dark-adapted, shot through with violet seam-glow; more often I read the world through the vibration-absorbing spars under what are now feet and the way the wet white noise changes pitch when a colony turns three corridors away. I move through the translucent tubes at the pace of geology. A single errand between districts lasts long enough for children of my kind (are they mine? are they the city’s?) to be conceived, carried, and taught the chords while free-climbing the same façade. I build nothing. I occupy the momentary dry that forms under a hauler’s bulk when it pauses—sometimes that hauler is still almost me—or the warm pocket left by a soft body’s exhale, or the brief clearing that opens inside a swarm when the lenses braid around a living presence rather than through it. My tools grow: ceramic filaments that remember this gait, suction cups cultured from my own earlier tissue, small resonant stones that can answer a seismic chord and thereby ask the undercroft a question.

Sometimes I stop on a high gantry and listen for a decade.

What I hear is not chaos and not finished. It is the sentence the plateau has been pronouncing since the rock first cooled enough to sweat: clicking rivers, sawing limbs, infrastructural drumming, endless crawl of water that never falls, and the slower sub-vocal scrape of something that is all of these at once and none of them permanently. The sentence is still unfinished. I know this in every thickened joint, every shimmering surface, every archive fold, every dark-adapted gaze that is sometimes there and sometimes not. I adjust the lived skin, or the matt-black sheet, or the soft matching porosity, or the faceted fist that briefly crowns me. I taste the latest mineral film for messages. Then I continue—another slow density moving inside the larger slow density—teaching the iron-dark, by the mere fact of refusing a final shape, how to dwell.

No single image holds. I keep emerging the way the towers keep extruding: by folds, by patient incorporation of whatever weeps across me, by the next necessary thickening. Under the sky that has forgotten brightness, amid the roar that never drops, I am the place where the gradients overlap long enough to notice themselves, and then move on.
