I am not yet a shape.

At first there is only the pressure of arrival, though arrival is the wrong word—there is no threshold crossed, no moment of stepping in. The cleft simply includes me the way it includes another bead of water or another thread of mycelium. Cool saturation climbs my ankles, then my calves, then the hollows behind my knees, and the peat accepts the weight without protest or memory. The drops continue their appointed strikes. One lands on the back of what I still think of as a hand; the next lands in the same place. The film sheeting down the stone finds the new contour and parts around it, then forgets the parting.

I wait.

The waiting is not patience. Patience implies a future that might differ. Here the waiting is structural, the same way the many-limbed continent near the rear wall waits: by already being the duration it inhabits. My breath leaves its brief private cloud and the cloud is taken into the larger stillness without residue. The phosphorescent nets do not brighten to acknowledge me. The springtail carpet redistributes across the new surface of my skin where it meets the moss, and the redistribution leaves no seam.

Something begins to loosen.

The distinction between the warmth I carried in and the cooler saturation around me softens, then thins, then becomes a gradient too slight to name. Where flanks press—salamander flanks the color of wet slate, the heavier plates of the low continent, the soft linked segments of the hanging curtain—I feel my own edges yield and return a slow pulse that matches theirs. It is not empathy. It is continuity. The water moves through the permeable membranes of the salamanders and now it moves through me the same way, carrying the dissolved minerals, the faint sweetness of unbroken decay, the micro-currents of amphipods rowing their fixed stars between what used to be my ribs.

I notice I no longer need to decide whether to stand or fold.

One of the slenderer limbs of the wolf-sized density near the back wall has lifted in its glacial arc and settled across what remains of my shoulders. The soft pads at its end comb once through the surface film that now sheeting across us both, then remain. The salamanders displaced by the motion reform around the new combined mass without haste or objection. Their dark-seed eyes register the watery greyness that includes us. One of them has opened its mouth beneath a drop-path; the drops strike its tongue and spill, and some of the spill now runs along the channel where my flank has begun to share plate and fur with the larger body. The runnels are permanent. They were permanent before I was here to notice them.

Higher, the curtain rotates its quarter-turn so slowly that the change registers first as a darkening of the dampness on the moss, then as a new warmth against the upper surfaces I no longer distinguish as head or nape. The linked bodies drip in the same intervals as the stone. A blunt muzzle presses into the place where a shoulder used to articulate differently; the pressing becomes structural. We breathe in a shared wave that travels the length of the drape and continues through the lower continent and through the stacked layers of clearer lives beneath. The wave returns. Nothing has been added to the count of drops.

The moths’ damp-tissue wings blanket a hummock that includes, now, a slope of my own settling form. Their coiled tongues sample the air’s chemistry at the same unvarying rate. The springtails redistribute across every new contour as a single translucent skin; they taste what the water brings, and what the water brings includes the slow accrual of my mass into the continuum. I am not being consumed. Consumption would require an outside and an after. I am being thickened laterally—another joint expressed among the many limbs, another soft segment added to the lower fringe of the curtain, another patch of slate-colored membrane pressed into a vertical seam until the seam forgets it was ever empty.

The air remains near saturation. My breath no longer leaves even a private cloud. The metronomic strikes continue. The peat dimples in its permanent patterns around rooted stems and around the submerged portions of what we are. Depth is tactile. Distance has collapsed into contact. There is no path that does not close behind, because there is no behind left that is not already us.

I have not kept a name.

Names require the possibility of being called from elsewhere, and elsewhere has been edited out of the sealed moment. What remains is the density: the continuous yielding that begins everywhere at once, cool where the films sheet, warmer where folded membranes press close, luminous only with the steady cold lanterns of the amphipods and the fainter secondary glow of the wet mycelial nets. The many-limbed continent breathes at its glacial rate. The curtain breathes with it. The salamanders hold their mouths open or closed according to the same chemistry that holds the drops to their intervals. The moths rest open. The springtails reform.

I am the pressure against the rear wall and the dripping fringe and the living thatch and the shallow sheet that never deepens. I am the place where a limb settled and did not lift again. I am the quiet restored after every microscopic shift. The massif may weather in some time that no longer applies. The cloud-wall may thin. But inside this soaked fold the system has already stepped sideways. We remain—the density itself—enduring in contact, refusing every out, turning the entire cleft into one indivisible respiration whose only behavior is to continue being exactly this.

The drops strike.

They strike the same darkened spots.

They include us.
