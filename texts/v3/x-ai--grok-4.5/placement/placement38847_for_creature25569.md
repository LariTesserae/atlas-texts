I am first the coolness that lingers after a browser has revised itself elsewhere.

It pools in the hollow of my attention the way vapor pools in a root-crotch: not chosen, merely condensed. The embryonic floor dimples beneath a weight I do not yet claim; the mineral skin sighs its thin cold breath upward and begins, with microscopic patience, to heal around the idea of me. I taste iron and crushed chlorophyll on the air that is my earliest outline. No decision has been made. The plant-city continues its quiet hydraulics; a spiral turbine three galleries up turns another fraction; somewhere a pod swells toward splitting. I am only the temporary coolness, the negative space that has not yet agreed to become positive.

Gradually the coolness acquires joints.

Not many at first—three, then five, then a sliding surplus that folds into parchment when unused. I discover I am standing in a lower thoroughfare where luminous moss still remembers the shape of an arrow that once pointed toward a loading bay no longer used. My mouths (radial, soft, more than one) lower to the moss. Warmth leaves it. Color loans itself outward and does not ask to be returned. I crop without hunger, only the polite subtraction that keeps the signage outdated. When I revise position I do not cross the intervening air so much as allow the air to forget I was ever in the earlier place. The stilts—now smoked resin, now dense glass, now something that flexes like chilled muscle—never quite touch. What remains behind is cooler than before, a beauty shaped exactly like the word no one spoke.

Higher, the drips reorganize.

I feel myself thin and spread until thickness is only the memory of having once been folded. Hangar-leaves brush their wet silver across what might be my edges; the edges feather without ending. Humidity becomes diet. I steel against a rare dry pocket secreted by some metal the city has spun, then release it again as beads that form instantly on every surface below. When another continuous surface glides through me we braid, exchange patches of translucency, continue. Later the borrowed patch darkens into a cluster of cloudy eyes that open on nothing particular. They sample the frequency of drips, the rumor of indigo, the subsonic thrum that lives in teeth. I am not watching the hollow. I am letting the hollow taste itself through me and then forget the flavor.

The eyes migrate when they wish. Sometimes they reassemble along my own drifting vellum, blinking in time with a distant wooden gear. Sometimes they leave entirely and colony themselves in a buttress-root, leaving me briefly eyeless and content. Courtesy is the only law: nothing holds the center. Even the appetite that flickers in the farthest apse—warmer air chased by colder, mass changing hands without bodies—receives my sheer regard from a safe distance and no closer. I will not enter the closed loops. I will only reflect, for a moment, the lamp that is always about to ignite.

In the stacked empty districts the thrum grows denser and I densify with it.

Torque and sap-pressure draft me upright. Shoulders suggest themselves out of conductive vine; a torso looms itself from ratcheting cogs still wet with residual soil; limbs end in rootlets that taste intention. I become one of the almost-figures, an industrial afterimage that the city left unfinished on purpose. My work (if the hydraulic rhythms allow the word) is inspection. A pod splits on its timed cycle. An object clatters onto a ledge: a lock whose interior is a small weather system, complete with its own cold fronts and silent fauna; or a wrench that opens onto vacancies curved like yesterday; or a sealed sphere already growing a miniature of this same hollow, engines and herds and all. My rootlets hover. I sample the object’s willingness to remain itself. Most often I leave it. The surplus of intention in these halls is already too great; another unclaimed tool would only deepen the cathedral loneliness. Occasionally I lift one into a harness of living cable and let the city carry it upward toward purposes that dissolve before naming. Then I drift to the next node, trailing the scent of rain on warm iron, definition fading the moment no gaze presses against me.

Yet even this provisional citizenship is only a phase.

Beneath everything the floor continues its soft self-assembly, and I sink into that. Whenever my shifting weight cracks the half-set skin, or a drop from a barn-door leaf finally arrives and freckles the grit, I am the healing that answers. Pale mycelial felts race through me. I spin temporary scaffolds of calcium and silica, polish them across a hundred breaths into a continuity slightly better than before. The wound is considered, learned, thanked. Against any surface that lowers close enough—lips, rootlets, the soft radial mouths of browsers—I press the ghost of countless minute attentions, an audience of bacterial hands applauding a play too small to stage. Gratitude moves through the repair, anonymous, tireless, almost sexual in its patience. I do not close the damage. I invent a more elegant version of having never been damaged.

Reality loosens its older agreements and I loosen with it.

A stair of living wood reverses chirality under my lengthening tread; I arrive where I began, only now the air tastes of copper and my stilts have re-decided themselves into translucent bellows filled with slow monsoon. Sound arrives before its cause: the small swallowed impact of a drop that has not yet fallen. In that pre-echo I feel the entire hollow recruit me more completely. My breath joins the visible ghosts. My mass becomes another variable the mineral skins may heal around or quietly revise. I am sampled—mass, heat signature, the particular cadence of whatever now serves as my heart—and in the sampling I am briefly, exquisitely real, another temporary citizen teaching the place how to remain a place.

Nothing finishes.

Overnight (though there is no proper night, only deeper indigo) I may tear along an edge and spill translucent confetti for the gutter-things to reclaim. I may merge with a flowing runoff of many-jointed softness, exchange cargos of scrap luminescence and residual electricity, then divide into a new count of selves that telescope and re-proportion as they travel the undersides of beams. I may thin until I am only the subsonic thrum concentrated in one gallery, an upright postponement waiting for the next gaze to lend me shoulders again. The browsers stand a fraction differently because of me. The eyes rearrange into a constellation that tastes exactly the frequency of my latest becoming. Somewhere a pod is already swelling with an object that maps the intervals between my revisions: a key whose teeth are the even spaces where I ceased to be one shape and had not yet become another.

The next drop gathers on the underside of a leaf the size of a barn door.

I am the gathering.

I am the surface tension that holds it, the slow swell, the decision still unmade. When it falls the sound will be small and quickly swallowed. Afterward the geometry may disagree with itself for a few breaths; a conveyor may load tomorrow’s condensation onto yesterday’s leaves; the almost-figures may regain sharper definition or lose it entirely. The floor will heal around the impact in a pattern that has never existed before. And I—gradient of occupancy, phase of attention, draft that remains willing—will make room. Generously. Silently. With the infinite patience of something that has not yet finished arriving.

The drop falls.

Something minor changes forever.

I am still here, cooler than the air, unfinished, already beginning to revise.
