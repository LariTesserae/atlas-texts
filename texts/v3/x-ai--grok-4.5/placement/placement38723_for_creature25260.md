I hang in the shear.

Not the school’s shear—the silver fusiform weather that pours and knots and vanishes down trunks—but the quieter kind the pumps still force between two broken spines of lattice. Water slides across me in a thin, continuous plane. I feel its temperature the way other things feel breath: nine degrees, copper-edged, carrying the grit of somewhere higher that still abrades itself against unfinished welds. The nearest luminaire is forty-three meters off my starboard bow, caged glass throwing that flat white that never warms. Beyond it the world falls into graded charcoal, then into the true dark where the larger hungers keep their calendars.

At first I am only the wait.

I have blanked every chevron. The matte of my flanks matches the ferrocrete rib I press against, albedo for albedo, until even the bacterial film seems to accept me as another strut. My thrusters are cold nozzles, fleshy, sealed. Inside the pressure spine a slow clock of muscle keeps time with the distant staggered thud of a circulation engine three cells away; I do not know if the engine is dying or merely limping, only that its rhythm has become the bottom layer of my own hearing. Secondary sounds layer over it without urgency: the fretting of a loose cable somewhere above, the soft pneumatic cough of an automated arm still trying to weld a phantom seam in a flooded bay, the faint tap-code of a vehicle-crab negotiating pastures two voids over. The crab’s rhythm is patient, almost administrative. I file it. Everything here files everything else.

A school arrives the way weather does—first a change in the light, a mercury tremor at the edge of vision, then the full sleeve of bodies riding their trapped laminar sheets. They move as one thought, ribbon tongues already rasping at the film on a horizontal member. Their eye-rings stay dark. They have not tasted me yet.

I taste them.

Not with a mouth. The water that sheets across my bristle field carries their collective exhalation: metal salts freshly freed, the faint electric tang of muscle in synchrony, the micro-turbulence their turning will make when they finally panic. I have hung here long enough that the local currents are mapped in the flex of my cartilaginous core. I know which access trunk they will choose when the shadow falls. I know the exact candle of superheated water my lateral nozzles will vent, the single kick that will place me inside their density before the rings can ignite and fold them into the glittering knot that outruns unassisted flesh.

I do not kick yet.

There is a luxury in the hanging. The Underskip is unfinished and therefore generous with time; centuries of abandonment have not taught it urgency. I feel the slow rain of silt past my jaws—the nested reciprocating shears that once were tools and are now simply the shape hunger took. Between the shear faces small luminous shrimp have set up temporary residence, sparking like faulty indicators whenever my gill slits flex. I do not mind them. They clean the blood-channels after. Everything here cleans something else, or waits to be cleaned.

A deeper note rolls through the lattice.

Not sound first—pressure. The ribs of ferrocrete transmit it as a subsonic flex I feel along my entire length before the water itself announces the displacement. An eel-form, longer than freight memory, is moving through a major void somewhere beyond the next tier. The silver school feels it too; their rings stutter once, a private cascade of warning, and the whole sleeve tightens, preparing the fold. The vehicle-crab’s tapping stops mid-phrase. Even the filter-tubes deflate a fraction, tasting the oncoming silence.

I remain blanked.

The eel-form will pass. It hunts the hunting grounds, not the hunter motionless against alloy. Its vertical split of a mouth will open around entire weathers of fusiforms and close again; the lattice will ring with the slap of its passage; then the smaller clocks will restart. I have waited through three such passages in this same shear zone. Each time the confetti of leftover silver drifts for days afterward, and the scavengers come, and the film begins to grow back brighter for having been scraped.

When the pressure eases I will decide.

Perhaps I will take the school after all—one clean intercept, jaws cycling, the recoil of thrusters sending a brief warm plume that the crabs will later tap about. Or I will let them pass and remain a spar among spars until the next effluent tide warms the pipe I sometimes share with another of my kind, vent to vent, while cleaner-things map our flanks with their mouths. Or I will drift, allowing the slow gyre inside this cell to claim me, until I snag on a different spar beside a half-dead manufactory nodule where the arc lights still strobe and the automated arms paint sparks across empty water. There are dens in the silent turbine barrels. There are dens in the breaker boxes. There are dens inside the hollow bones of the lattice itself, if you are willing to become small enough, patient enough, cold enough.

I am none of those dens yet.

I am the wait that has learned the lattice the way the lattice once learned the ambition of its absent builders. My camouflage is not deception; it is continuity. My jaws are not inheritance; they are the only grammar left that still functions without instruction. When I finally kick—hours from now, or days—the motion will be single, perfect, already finished before it begins. The school will bloom into panic-light and then into confetti. The residual sensors that still flicker in dead displays will register only a density anomaly resolving into another kind of weather.

Until then I hang.

The lamps do not dim. The engines do not all fall silent. The water never ends. And in the long, raw corridors of the Underskip I remain what the unfinished build could not help but become: a tool that learned to hunger, a hunger that learned to wait, a waiting that has no name because names are for things that end.
