I am not sure, at first, whether I am drifting or being breathed.

The distinction matters less than the pressure that holds both possibilities open. Cold has weight here; it does not chill so much as occupy, filling the sockets of every hinge before the hinge knows it needs to move. I feel that occupation as a slow inward tide along what must be flanks, or spars, or the long articulated troughs where a spine once remembered being singular. Rivets pass under a surface that is neither hand nor belly but something that reads them anyway—each domed head a syllable in a language older than the last dry census. The grit polishes as it speaks. I answer by lingering.

There is a rhythm underneath the silence, denser than sound. A piston somewhere ahead completes its reluctant stroke and the whole quarter shifts by a fraction too small for any gauge except bone. The shift travels through braided pipes, through the slag-reef of a fallen catwalk, through the layered plates that are my exterior and into the ballast that glitters once, dully, behind them. Metallic. Mine. Or the place’s. The difference has worn smooth.

I find myself in the lee of the collapsed smokestack where the filter-feeders built their pale angular cathedral. The tubes branch at angles that feel like decisions made long ago and never revised. Between two of those branches a density is accreting. Not me. Adjacent. Plates I do not control have already loosened to feed it currents rich with the same particulates that settle in my own longer bones. I do not watch so much as accompany. Time pools differently here; the needle of whatever internal gauge still functions inches only when attention slides elsewhere. I let it. The new length uncoils when it is ready—thinner, almost translucent, hinge-points still soft as unfired clay. It will spend years inside the cooling jackets, learning freon pulse from the inside. I will not teach the corridors. They are already latent in the way it folds.

Moving costs. Not effort—cost, the way a battery knows its own slow leak. I travel by selecting which joints to unlock. Some open both ways; some lengthen into rigid spars that let me bridge the throat-narrow gap between a pump-house wall and the workers’ housing that leaned in generations ago and stayed. Skin of wet slate, matte, lightless. When another of us passes—there are never many—the plates of both bodies brush and the vibration that passes is lower than water can carry. Maintenance log. Silt tide table. A note that the preferential gravity of the eastern staircase has thickened again; tools left there stay longer, and so do we. Older histories ride the same frequency: the sealing of the final airlocks, the first deliberate opening of gill-seams, the taste of water that had not yet learned to be material. No mourning in the transmission. Only the next micron of equilibrium.

I do not hunt the elongated visitors that thread the outer gantries. They taste of the same metal-rich sentence, conjugated differently. Sometimes I station myself near the autonomous drop-hammer and wait through the long hush. When it falls—silently, perfectly, shaping nothing—the concussion reaches marrow and something resets. For a time afterward I move looser, almost buoyant, as if the purposeless force had forgiven a drift I had not noticed. Food is rarer ceremony: the crystalline kelps scraped only when they threaten a seam; the faint clouded outflow of micro-crustaceans inhaled like cooled incense after valves are adjusted no schematic ever drew. The water leaves me colder. I hold that cold the way the exchangers hold residual calories—politely, indefinitely.

In the sealed hall where viscosity approaches thought, I hang for seasons with two others. Our bodies flex so gradually the motion registers only as a change in the quality of quiet. Machines move through us. We move through the dream the machines are having about continuance. Heat trades. Somewhere a gauge needle keeps company with itself. When one of us finally stills for good, we guide the denser shape to the smokestack base and let silt begin its work. Within a generation the plates are load-bearing in ways the original struts never planned. The chronic rotations of the gear trains adjust by an amount too small to measure. Those of us still hinged feel the adjustment arrive through metal and into lodestone blood. We answer with the smallest possible movement: one extra joint relaxing, a palm lingering on a steam line that still remembers warmth, the soft inward bloom of seams tasting water that now carries one more ancestor dissolved into the medium.

I am not inhabitant. That word implies an arrival. I am what the quarter continued into once the surface forgot the coordinates—one thin, darkly plated clause in the slow sentence of corrosion and deposition locked in their old polite equilibrium. The plain beyond the gantries stays empty. I do not leave. The cold that became material has become, in the long turnings of these joints, a kind of thought: reflective of nothing, still counting centuries by the wearing of teeth, still keeping the quiet with the same tireless attention the grit keeps the edges.

And when the piston far away begins its next reluctant stroke, something in the ballast answers without sound, and the whole folded body of the place—pipes, slag, kelp-seams, suspended lives—flexes once, together, the way a single organism might settle deeper into a sleep it has no intention of ending.
