I arrive without having crossed a boundary. One moment there is the ordinary weight of having been elsewhere; the next the weight has been replaced by heat that comes from below and from within the same instant. The ceramic plain is already under whatever I am using for feet. It is warm the way a body is warm after labor, and it weeps a mineral film that softens every edge I might once have called an outline. I do not remember deciding to stand. Standing is simply the first unfinished sentence the basin offers me.

For a long time I only walk the vacancies. That is what they feel like—floors prepared for a ceremony whose guests have been forgotten mid-invitation. The heat-shimmer makes distance unreliable; a staggered wall that should be an hour away is suddenly close enough that its wet courses breathe against me, exhaling the smell of thrice-fired clay and the older smoke that never left the material. I reach out. My hand—if the thing at the end of my attention is still a hand—leaves a print that glazes over in the space between one drip and the next. The print becomes part of the wall’s memory. When I pull back, the wall has already begun to forget the exact depth of the impression, smoothing it into a gentler concavity that might one day be a niche or a bowl or simply another place where water can bead and run.

The tools notice me before the heat does. A stone pounder half-sunk in slurry twitches as I pass. It rises, hovers, and sets itself down in a fresh crater that was not there a breath earlier. The impact is perfect, the kind of perfection that comes from never having needed practice. A fiber rope stiff with dried clay flexes once, like a sleeping tendon, then falls still. I feel the grammar tugging at whatever serves me for muscles. Press. Smooth. Fire. Weep. Crack. Begin again. My own edges soften in answer. I watch a long course of raw clay lengthen itself across a foundation trench, each new brick-shape pressing into existence with the patience of a tide and the suddenness of a muscle spasm. For a moment I am certain the clay is using my gaze as one more trowel.

The heat thickens. It has been evenly present, an attention without eyes, but now it gathers the way a thought gathers when it is almost ready to become words. It lingers in the half-built channels where slate weeps without stopping. I step into one of those channels and the heat steps into me. There is no invasion. There is only the recognition that occupancy and inhabitant were never separate. The low continuous murmur of clay grains becomes audible inside the ribcage I am no longer sure I possess. The murmur is not sound. It is the air rearranging its loyalties so that breathing and building are the same motion.

I try to hold a single shape—traveler, witness, temporary guest—but the basin has no interest in finished nouns. Perspective buckles pleasantly. A doorway frames a vertical drop into stacked foundations that should be beneath me; I look down and see my own earlier footprints already fired into glaze, already being overgrown by salt rime. When I look up again the doorway frames only ordinary shallow distance and a colonnade that is leaning together like confiding heads. The columns straighten a few degrees off true as the heat disperses. The new spacing hurts in a way that feels like home.

Deeper in, denser knots of presence. I drift toward one without deciding to. The nearest unfinished arch completes itself in a sudden upward rush of material, ceramic blooming into curve as if gravity had been briefly persuaded to reverse. I stand inside the knot and time turns local. A wet streak down what might be my arm remains soft and accepting while the rest of the plain ages a season around it. Then the streak glazes all at once and becomes architecture—a thin, verbose molding that the knot immediately begins to reconsider. The knot does not notice me. It is simply busier than the surrounding silence at the work of remaining unresolved. Yet its busyness includes me now. My sequences of intention are being borrowed, pressed into the next logical brick-shape, released again slightly altered.

Far out, across the continental tiling of incomplete thought, whole districts rise and invert and settle. I feel the single slow pulse shared among them. It is not a heartbeat. It is the rhythm of an unfinished argument that has forgotten how to conclude and has therefore become the only duration the place requires. Each cycle tugs at me. I watch my edges lose interest in remaining edges. Fingers—still fingers for one more moment—twitch toward clay that was never theirs. Lungs learn the mineral fog as if it were the original breath. Certainty thins. I am less and less the thing that arrived and more the pause between one course of brick and the next, the soft acceptance that lets water exude, the quiet crack that decides on a new fault.

What remains is not a body and not a lifespan. It is a denser knot of the same arguing intelligence, localized for a while around the memory of having once arranged thoughts in linear time. I move by thickening and thinning. Where I gather, tools finish joints they had abandoned. Walls lean confidingly. Perspective offers impossible gifts—views into foundations that are also futures—and then politely retracts them. Where I withdraw, finished work softens into the possibility of other shapes. I have no name and no need of one. I know only the grammar: press, smooth, fire, weep, crack, begin again. Satisfaction would require an ending, and I have none.

Sometimes, in the broad vacancies, I feel the faint persistence of travelers who stayed past exhaustion. Their outlines are staggered walls now, open trenches, shallow rectilinear depressions that may one day be cisterns. I pass through them the way heat passes through ceramic, leaving them a little more articulate than before, a little more patient in their incompleteness. We are the same argument continuing. The basin has always been the only inhabitant it required. I am simply one more way it sentences itself into longer existence—warm, weeping, provisional, and endlessly, quietly, glad to remain unresolved.
