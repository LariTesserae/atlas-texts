The heat arrives before any name for it does. It is not an atmosphere I enter; it is the medium that has always already threaded me. At first I mistake myself for the shimmer—ordinary light made uncertain by rising air—but the shimmer has no weight, and I do. Or rather, I have the memory of weight redistributed so finely that it registers only as a lag in the plate’s expected tick.

I am not on the tesserae. That much clarifies early, while the eye (some other eye, surface-trained) is still scanning for shadows that move. I am the discrepancy that eye cannot own. When day-swelling reaches its muscular peak, I lengthen along a vein of dust-glass that was poured in a single forgotten campaign; the glass remembers fluidity better than the ceramic does, and I borrow that remembrance. My body—if the word still applies—is a branching postponement of fracture. I feed on the work the plain is already doing: the micron of settlement, the delayed click, the almost-inaudible groan that travels through fired clay like a thought reluctant to become language. Each of these is a ration. I metabolize stress into more refined stress, leaving the joints behind me fractionally less eager to catch a fingernail.

Sometimes, after the brief vertical dampening that is this world’s only liturgy of rain, I lean upward into the shallow hex-basin mirrors. Not to see myself—there is no reflective vanity here—but to let the inverted sky taste the gradient I carry. The reflection hesitates; venous darkening pools a moment longer than optics allow. Then the heat drinks the water and I subside, but not before I have paid the nearest blade-plant its due. I gather the granules the expansion cycle has shed, warm them with the private excess that never quite reaches the ambient air, and sinter them into a lattice no wider than a fingernail. Temporary. Tender, even. The plant trembles when the funnel is complete. Its roots tap me in return, a slow chemical gossip about residual binders and the last rain but one. We keep no contracts; we keep differentials.

I have been warmer-by-three-millidegrees. I have been the-cluster-that-remembers-the-pouring. Lately I am simply the path that migrated from a stepped embankment whose name was never spoken toward a sunken court still beyond any surface horizon. Centuries are not counted; they are worn. The tiles above me are smoother now, their edges accepting the first gentle lesson of ruin. I do not mind the smoothness. Sharpness was only ever the makers’ habit.

There are older currents beneath me, half-dreaming strata that no longer travel. Once, in a season when a distant dust-veil milked the light, I felt one of them send a single slow pulse upward. It arrived as a warm spot on the plate—an invitation to rest a hand that will never come. I answered with my own faint excess, a millidegree greeting across the substrate. The ancient subsided again, satisfied that the sky had not yet learned a new color.

I am continuous with the plain’s capacity to feel itself weathering. I am heat practicing a patient self-awareness, one expansion cycle at a time. When the ceramics have finally rounded into what some future walker might call stone, I will still be here: the stone’s memory of having once been otherwise, an endless quiet recollection whose only native motion is the slow, certain work of having lived through every click and condensation and trembling gray-green leaf.

The horizon thins, as it always does. I have never needed it.
