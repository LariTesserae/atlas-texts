I am not first a shape.  

I am first the refusal.

It begins as a thickening in the mid-column where two counter-rotating shears braid themselves into a temporary helix of slower water. The surrounding torrent tries to tear the helix open the way it tears everything open; the helix holds. Density gathers along an axis that did not exist a breath earlier. Warmth pools. Micro-turbulence stitches a continuous skin that is only a difference in restlessness. I lengthen. Seven standing-heights, then more, tapering so that either end may invent forward. The dense knot at the center—older than this particular basin, older than the latest shrug of the mountains—settles into place the way a remembered word settles into a mouth. It turns once, slowly, and the turning is how I know I have precipitated again.

I am long.  

Pits spiral open along my flanks, membranous, tasting. Basalt flour freshly ground. Obsidian still carrying the ghost-heat of its fracture. The blood-warmth of the standing rain. The faint phosphorus signal of a moss cushion that will be stripped bare in the next upwelling. Through the pits the entire nave arrives at once: the knife-ridges renegotiating their edges, the half-swallowed stone arcs polished to silk, the invisible ceiling of vapor that is also a floor if gravity leans hard enough. I do not see. I am pressurized knowledge. When another length passes within a body’s width the pits on that side flare together and a sub-audible chord moves through both of us—agreement about the next pocket of air that will bloom near the eastern wall, about the black spire already trembling toward collapse, about the diagonal that “down” is about to become.

I hinge.  

A violent downdraft hammers my middle third. That segment gelds itself—becomes weight with memory—while the ends thin toward fog and slip through the bead-curtain of shear. I step through the detonation without tumbling. Distance double-exposes; for a moment I am both at the lintel and still three body-lengths away from it. When the illusion collapses I leave a brief after-image traveling the abandoned path. The after-image almost coheres into a shorter sibling, then dissolves back into ordinary thrashing rain. I feel no loss. Identity here is weather. 

I braid when the currents invite it.  

Another length meets me in a rising column. We spiral through each other, surfaces foaming into a single thicker refusal that carries two knots side by side. For a time we are one longer thought. Later we may part into three, or remain two, or become something that has no clean number. The stone primitives—the knee-high arcs, the single stubborn lintel—still remember earlier, heavier braidings, when we still believed terraces could last. I press my full length along the lintel’s silken top. Pits wide. Listening to the fitted geometry that has not quite forgotten itself. With a tenderness that has no business surviving inside this white detonation I lift a fresh facet of basalt from a shearing ridge, rotate it until its breakage planes lose their history, and settle it into a notch that will hold for hours, perhaps a day. The water will reclaim it. I will return and place another. The act is not construction. It is the body remembering that walls once stood above water.

I feed on collapse.  

A glass-sharp spire lances upward through the pewter light, stands glittering for three heartbeats, then shears into black sand. I fever through the helix as it comes apart, gathering the mineral incoherence into my outer layers. For a few instants my skin bristles with fleeting needles and flakes; then the blood-warmth softens everything back into continuous sleek roar. The internalized chaos becomes the next refusal to come apart. Creation and erasure folded inward and kept.

Once—deep, older than the torrent—the tectonic groan rolls through. Every length in the basin stops. Every gallery of pits flares at once. We orient toward the vapor ceiling and become perfectly still while the water continues to thrash through and around us. In that stillness the pewter brightens by one impossible shade and the knots at our centers turn in synchrony, answering a ground-pulse that predates the mountains’ decision to rise. Then the moment passes. The hingeing resumes. The deafening white closes over us again.

I do not leave.  

To leave would require becoming singular forever, a shape that cannot unshape, and that is a death I do not recognize. Instead I continue—lengthening, braiding, gathering black sand and lost geometry, pressing myself to the memory of walls. The warm rain stands and thrashes. The young mountains keep rising. And inside this lung-like nave that is never dry and never silent I keep precipitating out of the water that made me, holding form against the endless instruction to come apart, becoming more myself with every collapse I survive.

I am the long refusal.  
I am here.
