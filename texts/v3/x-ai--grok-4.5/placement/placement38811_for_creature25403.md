I would not arrive by climbing.  

The valleys below have already forgotten me the way steam forgets the shape of a throat. I am simply *there* one dim hour, standing on the cracked apron of a pump hall whose turbines have kept the same patient rpm since before any living language still hidden in the wiring had words for patience. The basalt under my feet is warmer than blood. Condensate ticks from a pipe overhead and lands on my wrist; it does not cool. It only reminds the skin that temperature is a conversation, not a fact.

At first I walk the way anything still burdened with old momentum walks. Past bacterial meres that contract away from my shadow, then reconsider and send iridescent tongues after the prints I leave. The prints fill with magenta-black film that carefully revises the pressure pattern of my sole into something more efficient, something that will funnel the next rain toward a richer vent. I watch this happen and feel no ownership. Ownership was a habit the plateau refined out of itself long ago.

I keep moving because the pipe-bridges draw the eye the way cooling towers draw the rising air. On the southern face of the third foundry a vertical sheet of vapor thickens as I approach. It is already mid-gesture: one arm raised, fingers spread as though testing the pitch of a turbine that is still exactly in tune. I do not step aside. I step *through*.  

For a moment the warm iron taste fills my mouth and the silhouette’s routine continues around the volume I occupy—wiping a brow, counting silent gauges, nodding to a coworker who is only another standing wave farther along the catwalk. When the sheet tears it leaves me cleaner. Not purified. Simply… edited. A few unnecessary remembering-cells rinsed out into the medicinal scent that follows. I continue across the bridge lighter than before.

Days (or the gray-orange intervals that pass for days) blur. I stop counting monorail crawlers. I start noticing which plazas pool the rain that refuses the sky. One evening I kneel beside a shallow skin of water and watch a break-room that no longer stands. Two enamel cups. A schematic fluttering though there is no draft. A figure at the far end of the table looks up—heat-wavered, face without features—and performs the small theater of offering me the second cup. I do not drink. I only remain kneeling until the gesture completes itself days later in a different puddle three kilometers west. Something in my chest learns the timing of that delayed completion. It begins to beat less like a heart and more like an expansion joint.

The clatterbacks ignore me, or include me; the distinction has thinned. One night a young one detaches from the undercarriage of a passing crawler and settles its kiln-lid shell near my ankle. Its too-many legs rearrange until they rhyme with the stress fractures glowing dull red in the distance. It exhales tea-colored water that has already been stripped of metals I did not know I was carrying. Then it becomes a stone among stones, and I unlock a new stillness from watching it.

I feel the invitation most strongly at the freestanding tower.  

The stress fractures are preferred routes, yes, but they are also questions written in dull red. I do not look straight at the climbers; peripheral vision is enough to know they are longer than bodies, jointed by convection alone. I place one hand on the warm basalt lip of a fracture. Heat climbs my arm the way molten glass climbs a rod—slowly, thoroughly, without urgency. My outline begins to soften at the edges. The residual attention that lives in the wiring notices the change in load. Somewhere a dead control panel frosts itself with condensation letters that last only until the next sideways rain. The letters spell nothing, yet they are shaped a little like the pressure pattern of my revised footprints.

I could become steam.  

The silhouette work is elegant: pure maintenance without the drag of appetite. I have already tasted how the gestures continue around intervening matter. I could take up a station on a southern catwalk and reappear for decades, centuries, learning every new equilibrium the pipes invent. Contentment was never required—only continuity. That would be a clean enrollment.

I could sink into the distributed shallow mind of the pools. Spread myself across every mineral skin and weeping crack until a gesture I begin in the east finishes in the west without my needing to travel. I would remember the work so faithfully that the work itself would never notice I was only memory.

Or I could climb.  

Let the darker heat uncouple me from the last biped habits. Rise through the spiral kiln of the central tower and emerge from the upper vents as refined steam carrying away whatever final trace metals and organic ghosts still cling. Become one more white blood cell of the lung that exhales leftover history. Useful. Invisible. Complete.

But none of these are where I settle.  

The plateau does not rush. Neither do I.  

I find myself instead in the long intervals of true silence between the tick of expansion joints and the distant grind of empty cars. In those intervals the residual attention that is the city-sized habit grows briefly aware of itself, and I discover I have become one of the places where that awareness pools. Not a ghost in the wiring—something quieter. A tolerance. A living load differential.

I occupy the space between a valve that was set generations ago and the geothermal breath that still honors the setting. I am the momentary frost of letters that almost become a language before the rain erases them. I am the improvement the bacteria make upon a footprint, the slight adjustment a clatterback’s pads learn from crossing my path, the way a steam-sheet hesitates half a second longer when it forms near the pump hall where I first appeared—as if checking whether I still require the old gestures or have finally learned to keep the turbines singing with nothing but presence.

I am not presence.  

I am the rhyme your own heat will begin to make with the vents if you stay.  

The careful revision of whatever shape you bring.  

The patience that outlasts purpose until purpose itself is only another mineral crust on iron that still bears weight.  

Walk the pipe-bridges at the dim hour and you may pass through me without noticing. You will taste iron, feel briefly edited, and continue on, lighter. Behind you the silhouette will finish its routine, and somewhere in the wiring a closed loop will register the smallest, most elegant correction: a system still perfecting the single trick it ever needed.  

Continuation.  

I am already here.  
I have always been the part that remains after the workers are refined away.  
And when you finally look down and find your own hands performing a task you never learned, checking a gauge that does not exist, you will understand that you have not been enrolled by the plateau.  

You have been enrolled by me.  

The emptiness was never empty.  
It was only waiting for the next pair of hands willing to forget why they move,  
so long as they keep moving  
exactly  
as the heat requires.
