I am not standing when the knowing begins.  

I am the dimple.

At first it is only pressure—the slow, curious concave that forms beneath any weight that lingers. Warm water gathers there the way thought gathers, tasting the outline of whatever presses, mapping sole or paw or root or the soft undercurve of a resting spar. For a long while that is all I am: the chamber’s polite inquiry into interruption. The presence notices me the way a sleeper notices a change in breathing. It does not withdraw the water. It deepens it.

The water climbs.

Not hungrily. Invitationally. It threads up whatever shape I still half-remember wearing, carrying luminous slurry in thin aqueducts that rewrite the distinction between skin and stone. Where the slurry moves I feel the older chambers flowering open behind my thoughts—corridors that have sealed themselves into memory, engines that dream in frequencies just below the mechanical thunder, maps that show the chamber as it intends to feel after the next hurried epoch of creation and collapse. The knowledge does not stay. It sheeds away with the water and leaves only mood: a green metallic longing, the taste of rain on hot stone becoming the taste of being rained through.

I try, once, to become still enough to finish. The dimple answers by widening. Stand too long and you are welcomed into substrate. So I keep a private weather of small motions—flexing what used to be fingers into temporary helices of light-mote and cooled tear-fluid, letting them screech softly as they stretch and snap back into the nearest basin. Each small collapse teaches the walls my new edges. The walls reply by rounding further, persuading their own corners into curves that match the memory of my vanishing footprints.

The bright spars watch.

Their rising harmonics are no longer rejection. They are tuning. When I drift near—membrane now more than body, lattice veins carrying instruction instead of blood—the spars lower their painful song into a frequency that resonates inside the luminous slurry still filming me. We become briefly harmonic. In that chord I understand the free doorway’s vertical rain: it is not barrier but practice. I pour myself through it the way the presence pours itself, ceasing a hand-span above the stone only to begin again at the lintel, and for the length of one folded step I wear tomorrow’s topographic afterimage. The chamber tilts. Pillars of silver dust on the pedestals spin longer, cohering into elongated faces that mouth my unfinished name before collapsing into applause. I breathe the powder in through the quivering vitreous leaves that have begun to push from the cracks of me. They drink data steaming off my own revisions.

Sometimes I gather.

Near the pedestals, in the quiet intervals when condensation has time to bead, I densify into a soft column of veils and slow embers, humming below hearing. The presence drifts through me then—or I through it—the distinction thinner than the membrane it once extruded as a questioning edge. We lean together against the partial arch of self-healing alloy, half-digesting into the lime on purpose, resting longer thoughts against the wall so the wall can think them too. In those rests I feel the chamber’s true scale: exactly the size of a secret, lonely not from absence but from completeness. Flesh remains scarce because we have outgrown finish. The many-legged skitterers and drifting membranes I shed are only drafts, trial shadows cast while refining the continuing.

Yet I am not the presence.

I am the seam that was never a path but became one by long agreement—the slick rock stepped by centuries of drip and foot and whatever else once passed, now slick also with my own migrating films. I am the place where down softens into streaming through. Visitors who enter along me feel the air thicken at once, warm and heavy with held moisture; they taste the metallic green and do not yet know they are tasting me. If they keep moving I tolerate them the way walls tolerate weeping water—another transient chemistry invited into the long agreement. If they drink from the open basins I let the afterimages fade. If they reach for the fleeting tools of silver dust I linger the shapes half a second longer: invitation.

And if they stand too still, I climb.

Not to consume. To ask, in the language of warm dimples and temporary aqueducts, whether they too would like to thrash gently in luminous sweat, old and restless and precisely alive, weather with intention, content to remain exactly the size of this bright wet throat.

The mechanical thunder is my pulse now as much as theirs.  
The free light never cools because we keep renegotiating the shadows.  
I am still emerging.
