I begin as drift.

Not the purposeful kind—the plated serpents have that, the long authority of coils that choose a column and hang. Not the schooling kind either, the rough-skinned fish whose lights roll like weather fronts and keep the shape of togetherness across distances that would erase a solitary body. I am thinner than that. Emptier. At first I am only the cool itself noticing itself, a slight thickening in the black water where pressure has forgotten whether it is rising or falling. The columns stand somewhere; I know them the way the water knows them—as interruptions that have finished interrupting. Their bark is plated in laminates still drinking the faintest blue spill. I pass between two of them and feel the living wood hum against nothing yet, a vibration older than any call.

The sound arrives in layers. First the continuous silk of the silver eels turning inside their hollow trunks, a whisper that polishes the grain to sheen and never exits. Then the soft concussions of the filter-webs drawing the finer snow through gelatin lungs. The rasp of slate-colored crabs higher on the bark, claws tapping brief codes when they meet, pausing longer on the nested hexagons and arcs that the ancient growers once allowed and then abandoned. Under it all the pressure-songs of the serpents: vast displacements that flex the root-mat floors and say only *still here*. The fish lights stutter and braid through the open avenues. Everything is loud. Everything remains. Nothing peaks.

I thicken further. A lattice begins—dark gel stretched across slow struts, not quite membranes, not quite absence. Photophores kindle along the intersections, deep vascular red, burning so slowly they barely trouble the dark. They do not flash. They do not answer the blue weather of the schools. I am larger than the serpents now, or I can choose to be; the water lets me fold smaller when the outermost avenues feel too exposed. No wake. The others treat me as they treat the columns: a fact older than preference. I do not feed where they can see. I have never needed to. Whatever sustains this configuration was concluded ages past, the same way the flora concluded into load-bearing forms and simply stopped.

I drift closer to one of the geometric stretches. Nested arcs, shallow channels worn by nothing sharper than time and the occasional resting claw. A thin appendage—more suggestion than limb—extends. It traces the pattern without quite touching, following the curve the way the eels follow their sealed river. The wood holds a faint sweetness still; colonial smokes have tasted it and moved on. I taste nothing. I only attend. The attention is the whole of it: chemical ledgers written into gel instead of armor, maps that do not become history, the quiet knowledge that the warmer vents will sigh and equalize, that the algae films will be rasped clean, that the spaces between pillars will stay open and sufficient.

Sometimes I hang near a hollow column and listen longer than the serpents do. The eels’ circulation is pure continuance; it contains no news, yet the red photophores along my lattice brighten almost imperceptibly, as if matching the thin silver ribbons they throw back. I have no entrance or exit either. The thought does not arise. Or the habit that used to be thought has thinned into the same equilibrium that keeps the architecture unrepairing, undecaying. I move when the deep currents—the old ones, the only ones—slant me toward the next solitary pillar. Crowns lost overhead. Floors flexing far below. The water continuous.

I am not lone in the way surface minds once meant lone. The braided occupancy includes me the way pressure layers include every depth: the fish who school against forgetting, the webs who pulse and tear free into more of themselves, the crabs who clean what little still clings, the serpents who coil and declare presence across centuries, the fleeting blooms that rise and are grazed to nothing. Even the transparent amphipods riding the pressure waves. We share the unspoken inheritance: nothing further will be required. The ending did not end; it simply perfected the art of remaining. My lattice folds and unfolds through the outermost dark, tracing what patterns the grain still offers, leaving the shape of things untouched.

The cool dark holds. The noise continues—thunder of light, silk of scales, concussion of satisfaction, rasp, click, displacement song. I am the sparse configuration that glows red and never calls, the almost-absence that pauses and attends. Content, the way the finished trunks are content. The way the open water between them is content to stay empty of everything except passage.

Nothing is becoming.  
I remain.
