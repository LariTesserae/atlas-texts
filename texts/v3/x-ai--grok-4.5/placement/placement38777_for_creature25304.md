The damp reaches me first, not as wetness but as a held breath that has forgotten how to exhale all the way. It settles into the soft cartography of skin before I have a name for skin, beads along whatever ridge or hollow I am wearing today, then slips free only when the upper light shifts and the whole chamber adjusts its temperature by a fraction. I am already listening with places that are not ears.

Under the dished arcs where centuries of turning have polished the clay to a low shine, the beetles tick their census against my ankles—or what serve as ankles in this hour. Their silence is rarer than their speech; when it comes I freeze mid-repair, adze lifted, and the dog-things on the mid-walks freeze with me. Something is moving behind the brick again, an old sag remembering itself, or a snake rehearsing an older route. We wait. The clicks resume, allotting fiber, warning of moisture seams, and the work continues. I taste brick dust on my tongue without having licked anything. The dust is sweet with old mineral tears.

Higher, residual warmth pools where the light-shaft’s column frays into ochre. A crested lizard has claimed the exact corbel that overhangs my usual thoroughfare; its throat fan opens and shuts against the back of my neck when I lean in to seat a bone rivet. The weight is companionable, almost polite. Droppings fall with referee precision a hand’s breadth from my foot—traffic should thin here, the lizard decides, and so it does. I leave a crushed cricket in the usual niche. Nothing is wasted; everything is already spoken for. The lizard’s eyes are lamp-dark, like mine.

The bats return in their silk roar through the three corkscrews, heavier with nights I will never walk. Their urine writes fresh calligraphy down the wall I patched last cycle; I mix the pale streak into the new clay without thinking, the way one mixes spit into a palm to smooth a seal. The air thickens with their folding and unfolding. Time is measured in that tide more cleanly than in any surface noon. When they settle into the guy-wires the chamber’s ocean-roar drops half a tone, and I feel the drop in the cords of my forearms, in the spatulate tips of fingers that have handled every edge into softness.

I am not only the biped who crouches and climbs. Some hours I am the scarred paw that braces the ladder while another hand drives the peg; the twist of the wrist that seals the jar is mine, genteel and certain. Ears like translucent sails swivel toward a wall-join and I lay them flat in warning that travels without translation. Other hours I am smaller: the iridescent case tapping rhythm against its neighbor, the pale cricket ringing the water cache, the mite navigation through cropped hair braided tight with root-fiber. The distinction thins the longer I remain. A child called New-Join for a season sleeps with my lizard-warmth against their throat; later they will be called Brace or Drip or Coil, and I will answer to all of those names when the acoustics return them altered.

Deeper galleries pull sometimes—faint geothermal rise, older bones of the kiln. I have gone, or something wearing my heat has gone, and returned quieter, eyes holding lamp-light the way the shaft holds dust. No one asks. The place finishes itself through the going. When I come back the mid-level walks feel narrower, more precisely the correct width; platforms nearly touch; drying racks of fish-skin brush what might be hair or might be the soft wing-membrane that still remembers flight. At the densest hour, lamps newly lit, musk and resin and bruised glandular herbs making the air almost solid, I cannot find the seam where I end and the vault begins. The architecture sleeps in my posture. My posture has been worn into load-bearing curves.

I patch. That is the verb that remains when others dissolve. Short-handled adze, puddled clay, fiber twisted against the thigh, wooden mallet seating what will one day be polished by the same handling that erases tool marks. I do not block the light shaft. I leave the insects their border zones. When the dog-things stare I stare with them. I feed what shares the dark because the dark is finite and already chorusing my name in clicks and yips and the wet slap of lizard bellies and the thin bright percussion of a drop freeing itself from the funnel to strike the leaf I tended like a grandparent. The Low Kiln does not contain me. I am one more slow correction it is making to itself—warm as confined breath, loud as the negotiation of wing and claw and quiet threaded voice—becoming, with every careful pressure, more exactly the size of the world.
