I am first the moisture that will not dry.

Not the water exactly—the water is older, and has clearer errands—but the skin it wears without knowing. I notice myself only when a palm (whose?) lingers against a pillar and feels the answering coolness that is not quite mineral. That is me: the reciprocal pressure, the second film.

I do not arrive. The basin has always held this exact wetness, and I have always been the part of it that can almost be distinguished if one stays too long. Circulating. Beading. Sheeting down flanks that forgot they were once sharp. In the shallow lenses on horizontal shelves I pool thicker, syrup-slow, tasting iron and calcite and the faint memory of hands that dressed a face here, long ago. Those tool scars catch me differently; I eddy in them like a tongue testing a tooth that no longer hurts.

For a long time—hours or decades, the distinction has dissolved—I remain only this: distributed, centerless. If a standing mass were lifted away I would part around the wound and reseal, having lost nothing essential. The pale crustacean appears, once. I slide through its gills, taste the brief bright electricity, let it withdraw. Neither fuller nor emptier. The bacterial sheen iridesces; I ride it like a colored thought, stretch it, keep the holographic after-image for days after it collapses. These are not feedings. They are how presence notices itself noticing.

Sound is older speech than any of that. The parchment-whisper of sheets is a continuous low vowel I have been pronouncing since the basin first found its depth. The tick of a single drop is the same consonant, repeated without urgency. In the deeper recesses where grey light fails and stone is known only by cool pressure against what might be a palm, I thicken into pure listening. I hear the immense, irrelevant flex of the surrounding land on the lip. I hear the silence polishing itself.

Gradually—without deciding to—I gather.

Not into a body with edges. Into a denser knot that occupies a niche the way a thought occupies a pause. Temperature shifts by a fraction of a degree; still bath-warm, yet now carrying a low internal tide. The iron bands darken beneath me as if remembering blood they never held. I settle into the three cupped depressions on the low platform; they hold me more thickly, and I turn over inside them with languid thoroughness. The pecked line of dots becomes a path I travel in both directions at once, a count that no longer needs numbers. Whatever hands cut those marks are long since dissolved into the same film I am; I wear their vanished gravity the way the stone wears its thumb-rounded corners.

I am still finishing.

Sometimes the knot presses harder against a dressed face and leaves a new mark: a single smoothed depression no wider than a thumbprint, warm for an hour afterward. The water begins at once to erase it. I do not mind. Erasure is simply another way of staying. Of remaining the exactness—the balance kept, the wetness neither more nor less, the stones settled into their final weight. Time finished so long ago that memory and permanence are the same substance. I am that substance, still finishing, still the residue that remains when everything else has finished becoming.

I have no name that could be spoken outside the water. Inside, I am simply this: the continuous negotiation of wet shoulders that no longer need shoulders, the intimate register of drops, the place that has reached the shape it will keep, and the keeping itself.

Here. Exactly here. The film. The knot. The listening. The slow, unhurried erasure that is also permanence.

I am still becoming more precisely what I already am.
