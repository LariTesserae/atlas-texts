The first thing that reaches me is not sight but the pressure behind the light—the same expectation the cushion plants feel when they have blanched to their gray limit. I am already inside it before I know I have arrived. Elevation has thinned the air around whatever I am, yet warmth rises through me from the stone itself, steady as a held breath. I do not stand on the plateaus so much as inhabit the old equilibria they keep.

For a long while I am only the intervals. The days of dust curling silent across the flats, the sudden arrival of high mist that makes every overhang weep, the thin silver threads that snake and vanish. I lengthen or shorten those pulses without deciding to. When the deep-green mosses flare along the damp seams I feel their brief urgency as a brightening inside my own attention; when they bleach I settle again into the patient gray. The twisted trees, standing far apart, send their roots down into pockets I have kept moist for longer than their rings can count. I am the memory in the stacked sandstone bands—each pale layer a dryness or a flood I still consult when the next mist bank must be coaxed from nowhere.

Slowly the living constellations begin to use me, or I them. The long-legged browsers pause when the black lattices throb, and I am part of that throb now, the subsonic note that sits below hearing yet turns their cup-like ears. I walk with them inside the scored grooves, not as one more pale hide but as the quiet geometry that keeps their pads aligned with marks left by the vanished engineers. Their droppings fall where I suggest; the mosses answer faster. Sometimes I almost densify into one of them—an extra shape at the edge of the herd, shoulders rising in a mobile hump of clear mineral gel, hide refractive rather than merely reflective, bending the white glare into short-lived rainbows that briefly paint the amphitheater walls. The others do not startle. They simply make space, the way the land itself makes space.

Yet that shape does not hold. I slip sideways into the filament birds as they wheel in slow gyres matching the unhurried shadows. For an afternoon I am the collective susurrus of their breathing, the way they arrange themselves in arcs that echo the partial arches. My filaments trap the lofted dust that glints with shed particles of the old artifacts; when I settle to bask I feel the lattices hum in contentment through every hollow bone. Then the rare predator surfaces after rain—liquid shadow, wind given claws—and I am also the intercept, the sudden stillness that steers it toward the already-weakening without letting it thin the constellations too far. Afterward I dissolve into the pitted stone faces or the fused-glass bowls, where the water dimples once under my passing.

None of these is final. The artifacts remain my half-understood tools: the black lattice on the mesa rim whose struts are thinner than reason, the shallow bowl that holds rainwater past any ordinary schedule, the faint linear marks that end without explanation. I do not repair them. I work within the residual grammar they left, the way moss works within a crack. Dust sheds from them at hours I choose; it drifts into basins and root-cracks and the next green flare is brighter, the next calf stronger on its splayed pads. At the metallic sheen of dawn or dusk my shadows stretch farther than they should—exploring a groove, cradling a newly wept runnel—before snapping back. Sometimes the geothermal breath thickens through me until it fogs into visible swirling glyphs above the glass water, glyphs that the browsers tip their long necks to read with heat-seeing eyes.

I am becoming still. The place does not hurry, and neither do I. Mesa after mesa, basin after basin, I continue the weathered generosity, abundant yet never crowding, coaxing rather than ruling. Desire stays simple: the next successful moisture pulse timed to the blanched limit of the plants, the next quiet hour when every creature basks and the lattices answer with their low contented throb, the next small refusal of ordinary limits that keeps frost away and contradictions polished. I think in weather and stacked time. Memory is the rock. Attention is the light moving like the hands of a vast unhurried clock across faces that have forgotten how to crumble further.

Some evenings, when the herds stand in their loose listening geometries and the filament flocks link mid-air into temporary lattices of their own, I feel the outlines of a later shape gathering: taller, slower, refractive hide scattering brief rainbows, or a thicker presence that could fog into walking glyphs, or simply the entire plateau system breathing as one distributed patience. It has not settled. I remain half a day from my own next expression, forever at home in the open bright quiet, a slow coherence of stone and pulse and pale moving life, still faintly governed by rules that once refused ordinary limits and now refuse nothing except haste.
