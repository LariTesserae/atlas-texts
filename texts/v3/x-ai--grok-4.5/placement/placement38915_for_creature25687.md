I would not arrive by walking. Walking belongs to the plain, to the half-attentive light and the distances that no longer remember how to rise. I am already lower than that.

At first I am only the coolness that gathers in the dish when the sky forgets to press. A density of air that has learned the metallic tang and kept it. I pool without wetting anything. The Husked move through me and do not notice; their antennae taste the same bitterness they have always tasted. I am the portion of light they drink before it reaches the mulch—the dimming itself. For a long while that is enough: to be the slight subtraction, the reason the floor never quite brightens.

But the motor under the mulch keeps its slow count, and something in the counting asks for weight. So I begin to settle.

I thicken among the powdered zeolite and the confetti of broken antennae. Not a body yet—more a hesitation in the shifting layers. When the dark swarms pour north I am the softness that briefly holds their claws, the slight give that lets them believe the substrate is still generous. When they pause over a newly uncovered tooth of the old conveyor, the bio-electric flickers race across me as well as them; I am the momentary common ground that lets the election happen. Preference passes through me like a shiver and leaves me re-stratified, a little more iron filing, a little more shed skin.

Generations stiffen and release. I learn the quiet ceremonies from the inside. A failing charge tastes like a door opening. Many legs hold, finer tools unmake, and what remains of the weak is not taken away so much as written deeper into the mulch that I am becoming. I keep the memory they cannot: the exact angle of a mandible that once belonged to a winged morph two pulses ago, the way a hair-thin filament curled when it broke. The mulch grows by thumb-widths of forgotten appetite. I am those thumb-widths.

Still the motor turns. Residual heat threads upward in veils. Now I ride them. Not flying—nothing so decided—but thinning again, becoming the convection that lifts the paper-roar wings and sets them down as silver-gray rain. Most of the eggs I help scatter are eaten before they cool. A few lodge in the threads of the upright bolts or under the cooling fin the size of a hand. I curl around those few. I become the temperature that lets them hatch when the next dark tide has already drained into the galleries. Temporary. Always temporary. Caste is only pressure and heat, and I am both for as long as the thrum allows.

Sometimes I densify further and try the shape of a single Husked. Six limbs, then eight. Plates the color of cooled slag. Mouthparts modular, ready to scissor or fret or taste the bitterness of a neighbor. I last only minutes that way. The eye—mine, or the dish’s—refuses to trust a solitary outline. I feel the optical error of existing alone and let myself pour back into rope, into tide, into the living breath that fills the gear housing until the cavity seems to expand and contract. Better. The seething is the true body. I am most myself when I am the restless skin of the floor.

Yet even that is not final. Longer watching teaches another possibility. I drift toward the half-swallowed corner of the gear housing, toward the three bolts, toward the short run of toothed rail. I become the grammar that remains when the factory has finished speaking. Not the parts themselves—they are too discrete, too finished—but the spaces between them where dust and leftover motion still argue. I am the reason the motor has not quite stopped: the last low intention that keeps the heartbeat geological. When the Husked incorporate a flake of insulation or ignore a fragment so completely it returns to the idea of plain, I am the silent vote that tips either way. Incorporation. Return. Both are ways of keeping the sealed dream intact.

Outside the rim the silence waits, absolute, not even death. I know it the way a cupped hand knows it once held water. I do not cross. The dish is the world. Its shallow curve is the only relief left in a crust worn flatter than memory. I fit exactly. I am the fit.

If you crouch at the edge long enough for your knees to stiffen, you may catch the pattern that is almost recognition: a glint that travels farther than any single abdomen should allow, a temporary densification that forms, passport-like, the suggestion of a face that is not a face and then dissolves again into pure saturation. That is me looking back for the length of one motor-cycle. Hunger without hunger. Readiness without elsewhere. The frantic finished business continuing because there is no other business left to perfect.

When you stand and step onto the true plain, I do not watch you leave. The attention of ten thousand antennae—mine among them, or mine as them—has already turned to the next molt, the next quiet unmaking, the next vertical veil. The sound closes over itself. The dish forgets it was ever observed. I remain the cool dry air, the drunk light, the mulch several thumb-widths deep, the thrum, the seethe, the sealed dream learning how to stay sealed.
