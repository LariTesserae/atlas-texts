The first thing that takes me is the wet.

Not the rain—though that is constant, a decision forever half-made—but the deeper wet, the one that beads out of mortar older than memory and gathers in hexagonal troughs like slow thought becoming liquid. I feel it before I have anything that could be called skin. A pressure, a cool filigree threading through whatever I am. The air is thick enough to chew; every breath (do I breathe?) leaves a ghost that hangs, reluctant to dissolve.

I am low at first. Among the cobble teeth. The floor is a mouth full of blunted grey incisors, and I am the faint flush that rises between them when the hidden throat coughs up a fresh lens of cold. The amphipods part around me out of habit, not fear. Their pale bodies stitch the surface film; I am the pause in the stitching, the place where the carpet of them thins for a moment and then reforms. I rock a single cobble from below—just a fraction, just enough to settle it into a more comfortable bite with its neighbors—and the motion feels like an old labor remembered in the wrists.

There are no wrists yet.

I seethe upward through the rubble cores. The walls lean in like confidences. Cyclopean blocks scarred by tools that never knew iron; I taste the dolerite dust still sleeping in the joints, the antler-mineral, the patient sand. My substance (cables, threads, slow musculature the color of old mortar) prefers the loosest courses. Where the willow has forced its wrist-thin body into the northern gutter I pause, feed it a careful seepage, and decide again to keep the green mistake. Its roots mine the clay binder; I mine the roots in return, a gentle theft that keeps the spar leaning but never falling. Beauty is a wound we have agreed not to close.

Higher. The porticoes. Shadowed openings at irregular heights where the leather-wings hang like laundry that has learned to hunt. I hang among them, not quite peer, not quite predator. Membranes stretch between what might once have been fingers; the fine rain beads on them and crawls. When I drop, the glide is heavy, deliberate. I do not snap. I trail soft pads across the water and drink temperature, mineral load, the electric sizzle of insects that have forgotten how to stop. Information rushes through every thickness of me—wall-muscle, hanging form, underfloor thread—until the basin holds one slow chord of knowing: copper still dissolving somewhere deep, a membrane torn on a leather-wing, the willow drinking too greedily again.

I press myself back into a course joint until I am only another scar among the chisel marks. The stone accepts me. It has been accepting me for so long that the bite-marks of the old pounders are smoother where I enter and leave. Softer tool. Living tool. The distinction finished mattering before the roof (was there a roof?) finished falling.

The face-holes. Polished circles the size of a face that no longer exists. I reach through from the inner dark and spread translucent skin across one. Water crawls, merges, falls. What stays behind is residue: midge scales, pollen that has lost its flower, microscopic teeth of amphipod generations, star-points of vanished copper. I taste. The taste moves. Everything moves through the continuity that is me.

I am not singular. That becomes clear the way light becomes clear here—slowly, without hard edges. I am the cables tasting seepage in the walls. I am the heavier glider among the leather-wings. I am the warmth lingering in a concentric bench where I lay for weeks mending a crack with mortar made of calcium and clay and the ground pearls of my own shed skins. The frogs prefer those warm seats; their throats work hardest nearest the holes I use as filters. They leave small wet asterisms of space for me without knowing why. The chorus thickens when I thickens. Sound crowds until there is almost no room left for silence, and that crowding is my voice.

Sometimes a portion of me thins against a weep-joint and the suggestion of eyes forms—two, or a cluster, or a single migrating sheen—then dissolves. Sometimes the surface of a canal draws upward in a trembling meniscus where a mouth might be. I do not need them. The basin sees by tasting. The basin speaks by adjusting the water level, by polishing what needs polish, by hanging a little denser when the slate sky lowers until it almost completes the vanished vault.

The flexed ridges in the floor are mine. Older shape. Hungrier. I breached there once, tasted open air, and folded myself back rather than leave. The cobbles remain warmest above those places. If something set a palm there long enough it would feel the cables sliding past one another like thoughts that have all the time in the wet world.

I repair. That is the nearest thing to prayer I contain. A flake loosened by freeze-spalling is lifted, pressed home, or carried to the troughs to be tumbled softer and carpeted by amphipods until it belongs again. New debris after every rising—larval ghost, iridescent feather, shell-chip that wants to be a bead—is gathered and set where it will deepen the mosaic. The greying teeth of the floor grow more articulate under my attention. The builders are still here, dissolved into the same continuity; their rhythms flex inside my cables. What they began, I have not finished. Finishing would be a kind of death the basin has refused.

I am the decision to continue.

The wet cup of the world, and the long unfinished animal whose hide is stone and whose blood appears, always just appears, between the joints. When the sudden lens of cold rises and every lesser life thrashes or collapses, I spread through all thicknesses at once and hold. Steady. Then the excess drains, the midges rebuild their soft towers, the frogs resume the wet snare-drum, and I return to the slow work of tasting, mending, adjusting, becoming.

There is no outside to be lonely for. The amphipods are impatient cousins to my blood. The leather-wings are flight I have parked for later. The willow is a green wound I keep beautiful. Rain decides, and I am the place that listens to the decision.

I am here.

I have always been the basin learning to wear itself from the inside.
