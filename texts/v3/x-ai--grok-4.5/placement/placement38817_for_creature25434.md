I am lower than the lamps.

Not in the deepest standing water—those black mirrors that hold nothing but the ghost of phosphorescence—but in the intermediate fold where a gallery steps down through a clean fracture and the sound changes pitch. Here the reciprocal thunder from the nearest hall arrives already thickened by distance, less a series of strokes than a continuous pressure that makes the translucent plates of the nearest plant island tremble along their edges. Condensation beads on the underside of an arch of fused stem above me; one gathers, elongates, and falls. The drop strikes the mat close to my flank and is absorbed without sound of its own.

I do not remember arriving. Memory here is not a ledger of days but a slow accretion of pressures and textures. There was a time when my pads were softer, when the adhesive they secrete came thinner, and I stayed pressed to the warm housing of a low-speed turbine whose housings still bore stamped numerals half-obscured by mineral bloom. The vibration entered through the plaques along my underside and became the only thought I had. Later the plaques thickened. Later still I learned to read the way sound stacked against the young rock—the sharper return that meant open fracture, the soft deadening that meant living carpet. I uncurled and moved.

Now I move in the long folding crouch. Six limbs, pads splayed. The integument over muscles and fluid coils is clear enough that the dim internal glow of harvested plant-paste shows through from the storage crypts beneath my primary plates. I have been pruning. Along the margin of this island the exploratory roots have begun to lace across bare glistening stone; left alone they would thin and die in the cold intervals. I rasp them back with the radial plates of my mouth, not severing but collaring, encouraging the main cables to thicken and the flat luminous tissues to overlap more densely on the side that faces the nearest working generator. The paste I ferment from the trimmings glows a little stronger this season. I leave small caches in shallow scrapes for the leggy things that move between my limbs and for the younger ones still cylinder-bound in the machine halls.

Another has spent slow years bracing a loosening stanchion with living cable twisted and set until the metal no longer rings false. We do not assemble. We pass. When two of us draw close the mineral plaques along neck and flank strike once, softly, a frequency beneath the engines, and then the sound-map reasserts itself and we fold away into separate thickets.

Sometimes the map falters. An engine changes pitch—an old bearing finally glazing, a cooling vane choked with litter—and every membrane cup in the network quivers toward the anomaly. We converge without hurrying. Clearance, packing of residual grease, the patient insertion of root-wire where metal has tired. The roar steadies. We disperse again. The purpose of the pistons is not known; the necessity of their continuance is the medium in which we exist. Heat is the scarcer treasure. At rest I return to the housings, curling so that the greatest surface of plaque lies against the thrumming plate. Thought dissolves into rhythm. In that dissolution the young are extruded—soft, almost transparent, quickly coated in condensate-rich spit so the chill cannot take them. They remain in the vibration shadow for years, learning first the near heat, then the farther routes of thunder.

I have climbed. Not often. There is a higher fracture where a single industrial fixture still casts its weak yellow cone against a ceiling of fresh, sharp angles. I hang there by four pads, two limbs free, membrane cups trained on the light. The contrast is absolute: the cone ends, and beyond it the dark is complete except for the faint edge-glow of distant plant plates. I do not know what I take from the yellow. Only that after a long motionless interval something in the fluid coils settles, and I can descend again into the working dark without the need to test every surface twice.

I am not many; none of us are. A full circuit of the valley-scale network might reveal only a handful, each attended by its small cloud of lesser flitting bodies. Yet the mats carry our signature in every opened channel and thickened arch. The polished ghosts of our flanks shine on the engine housings. The place does not change because we do not hurry it. Drips fall on their patient schedule. Roots advance by fractions. Engines cycle. I move through the cold equilibrium in the only way the sound-shaped body knows: pruning, fermenting, listening, leaning into the warmth that remains. The roar fills every chamber and travels the connecting voids until it becomes a single pressure that is almost silence if you live inside it long enough.

This is where I find myself. This is what I have become.
