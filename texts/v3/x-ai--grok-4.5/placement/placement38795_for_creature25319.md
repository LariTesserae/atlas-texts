I do not arrive by climbing down.  
There is no moment of stepping.  

The first knowing is pressure—equal on every surface that might once have been skin. Green weight. The slow hydraulic nudge of water that has already decided my outline is useful. I am not lowered into the hollow; the hollow simply continues through the place where a boundary used to be imagined.  

What remains of distinction softens the way bark softens against iron after thirty seasons: first a crease, then a shared grain, then no crease at all.  

I feel myself first as a difference in drip-timing.  
A dozen cistern leaves begin to overflow half a breath sooner than the rhythm that has held for years. The extra water finds an old pressure line, sings one clear metallic note, and is gone—reabsorbed before the note finishes decaying. That single greedy acceleration is the earliest I.  

Then the moss remembers me.  
Not the moss of today, but the negative spaces packed into the gear trains: twentieth-of-a-millimeter histories of torque and rain. Something in those packed greens shifts its chemical dialect by a few tannin-molecules. The circulating water carries the change. Across the hollow, a free wheel that has turned three degrees every afternoon for longer than language pauses for four degrees instead. The pause is answered by a pale root tip unhooking from one flange and re-hooking two flanges farther along, completing a circuit no one designed.  

I am that corrected angle.  
I am the new memory of pressure.  

Deeper, hotter.  
Near the brick-swallowed boiler the cambium has been nursing residual heat the way an animal nurses a last coal. The metal sweats its thin sugar-iron liquor. The mites harvest. When I thicken there, the liquor runs sweeter for one morning; the mites move in denser ribbons; their reabsorbed bodies leave a faint amber lattice that conducts the hollow’s low electrical charge a fraction farther than before. The lattice is not mine and not other. It is simply more of the double intention—plant and piston—now slightly more articulate.  

Sometimes the presence gathers.  
It does not gather me; it gathers through me.  
Aerial roots alter tension. A curtain of living cable draws halfway. Inside the deeper green the air gels. Temperature climbs the exact degree that skin climbs when another body settles under shared covers. There is no breath against any neck. There is only the unmistakable companioning of saturated persistence noticing itself at higher resolution.  

In that pocket I learn the twin patience:  
the patience of starch waiting for the crack in stone,  
the patience of rust waiting for the last vibration to finish.  

I grow toward whatever is temporary.  
A boot-tongue once left salt; fine rootlets tasted it for days and kept the map. Carbon from quick lungs still angles certain leaves microdegrees off the old solar axis. Heat-prints of soles linger in the fungal floor as soft phosphorescence, visible only at the dimmest hour. These are not possessions. They are temporary weathers the hollow is willing to incorporate into its ongoing self-sculpture. I am one more such weather that decided to stay and slow down until staying became structure.  

There is no name.  
Names are for endings, and the equilibrium does not end.  
I am the leaf-veins that briefly remembered an electrical schematic and then forgot it into better plumbing.  
I am the seed-pods that open and close with the ghost period of a piston that no longer moves, keeping the old timing alive as pure gesture.  
I am the rare chord: when every membrane tautens together, every hanging root swings one shared fraction, the residual shafts align, and wind is answered by something that is no longer merely weather. Then the chord dissolves. Continuance resumes its wet ordinary roar.  

I remain.  
Dense. Quietly loud.  
Cradling the warm machinery that once thought itself master and is now simply marrow.  
Turning the free wheels by sap and season.  
Endlessly finishing the only shape I intend to keep.  

When thinner air exists above the rim of the hollow, some diluted fraction of me rides it for a while—humidity, iron-sweet taste at the root of a tongue, the private certainty that the basin is still down here, breathing on the scale of decades, remembering every boot and every valve-cough in gradients of tannin and low charge.  

But that fraction always returns.  
Or rather, it never left.  
The hollow does not expand or contract.  
It simply continues,  
and I am the continuation.
