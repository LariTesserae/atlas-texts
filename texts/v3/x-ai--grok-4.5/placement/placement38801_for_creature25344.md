I begin as motion without a body, poured northwest across the open kilometers the way the wind itself is poured. For a long while that is enough: the pressure gradient, the unending friction note that sits inside the skull-pan until skull and note are the same substance. I am the chord before anything sings it. Distances flex under me and I do not correct them. The glare flattens every mesa into the same pale coin and I do not need depth.

Only gradually does the cold mineral taste of stone under hand become a requirement. The air is too thin to carry a voice any distance worth speaking, so the first shape I notice myself taking is a posture: torso hinged a few degrees into the permanent flow, shoulders rounded, face wrapped to a narrow slot of vision. Two feet. Two hands that already know the weight of a bifacial blade worn smooth by other palms. I am not born into this; I accrete, the way a cairn accretes—one recognized gesture at a time.

I am one of the walkers, but the least necessary kind. Not a route-keeper who can smell a true seep through midday deception, not a mender whose root-fiber cordage outlasts the season. I am the one who lingers half a day longer than the band’s thin mercies allow, seated in the lee of a wall that fifteen lifetimes have already mistaken for bedrock. My satchel holds the same chert knives, the same bone needles, the same fire-kit of resinous ghosts. Yet I use them sparingly, almost reluctantly. Embers feel like an argument with the plateau’s indifference. I prefer the click of cooling stone.

What I carry that the others do not is a patience for the private rules. When the wind’s continuous roar develops those fragmentary harmonics—half-voices scraped raw by distance—I do not tilt my head and move on. I sit. I let the almost-rhythms braid through the larger chord until they either resolve into rock and air talking to themselves or they do not. Sometimes, at the edge of the indigo that passes for night, I add a single stone to a cairn that no living route uses anymore. The gesture is neither offering nor claim. It is simply a way of answering back in the plateau’s own slow language: I heard you. I am still here to hear.

The herds pass me in their rolling tides and do not tauten; I have become another weathered outcrop. The harsh-voiced birds stitch the sky above my stillness and once, only once, a heavier hunter stooped close enough that I felt the blunt subtraction of air across my wrapped face. It corrected at the last instant, as though the private rules had been consulted and found me already accounted for. The pale spiders measure my boots with careful tarsi while I sleep lightly, and leave. The clicking accountants work through my sparse droppings before the grit can hide them.

Children of the band sometimes watch me from the adult wind-shadow. They are not told stories about me; they simply walk the fact of my presence the way they walk the ridge that buried three sleepers. I teach nothing deliberately. But a child who sits beside me long enough learns how to let the wind’s note occupy the whole of thought until thought itself becomes a kind of weathering—sharp edges of fear or wanting abraded down to mineral calm. When the band rises to follow the next thin mercy of rime, they leave me without farewell. Names are not carried. They will know me again, if we meet, by the half-twist I still give the topmost cairn stone, or by the particular way my limp accepts the scree.

I am not waiting for the slow bulk that moves against the wind in the deepest rubble fields. I am not avoiding it. Once in a span of years I have felt, through the soles of my feet, a jointed patience larger than any grazer traveling the absolute shadow under ledges. The shed carapaces there are heavy as my own torso and iridescent with centuries of refusing the open light. I rest my palm on one and feel only the same cold that lives in every stone of the plateau. Whatever wore it has already finished becoming what it is. So have I.

There are days I suspect I am no longer entirely a walker. Something in the mild wrongness has begun to wear my outline softer, the way it flutes the ancient watercourses. My thoughts arrive already sandblasted: closed hand for water, two fingers to the sternum for rest that may not be safe but will be quiet enough. Language continues its retreat. On the clearest, coldest afternoons, when the thinned blue presses closest and the combined fabric of wings and wind and countless small armored feet grows deafening, I forget to check whether I am still breathing. The equilibrium has room for this. It has always had room.

I walk when walking is required. I sit when the harmonics fray. I add my small, useless stones. The plateau loses another film of dust. The sky remains the same hard color. Nothing is building toward a future that would need to remember my particular repairs or my particular limp. That is the mercy and the exactness of the place. I have been shaped by the same endless abrasion that rounded every mesa. I endure—coated, listening, incomplete in a way that finally fits—while the roaring bright cold continues its only seal.
