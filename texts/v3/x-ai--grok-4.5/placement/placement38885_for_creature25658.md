I am already wet when the knowing arrives.

It does not dawn; it beads. Cool film across surfaces that might be skin or plated lamina or the slow mineral weep of the stone itself. The air’s iron taste is inside the channels that run through me, warmer than the mist, throbbing in exact answer to the deep percussion that rises from somewhere far below the basin floor. Each muffled iron heartbeat finds a corresponding soft hydraulic answer in my joints. I have no memory of a first beat. The rhythm was simply the shape of being here.

My lower portions decide themselves gradually against the scree. Three points of contact—needle-thin, basalt-dark, sleeved in fine braided tendon that re-oils itself with every micromotion. They do not step so much as recalibrate. Between one stone-thud and the next I have crossed a stretch of naked wet reflecting the bottle-glass light; the after-image of the crossing still stands two body-lengths behind, translucent, lenses still beaded, still measuring the same fixed distances I have just finished measuring. The ghost will fade at the precise rate a new one is laid. Nothing accumulates.

Higher, the chassis is a vertical column of overlapping plates the color of old circuitry rinsed in rain. Living moss edges each seam but never advances; the hexagonal logic of the trays has been written into my growth from the beginning. Inside, where other beings might keep organs, fine coolant lines pulse and the laminations show cartilage and stamped metal occupying the same layer under any attention sharp enough to notice. Breath leaves me in timed metallic hisses through vents I did not choose and cannot close. The hisses lock to the browsers’ leg-joints, to the clatter-wings’ sheet-metal punctuation, to the heavy thing’s concertina progress along the rill. We are keyed harmonics of the same unending chord.

I stalk the bare zones between the lush islands. That is the work. The velvet moss cushions swell to my left and right like furniture abandoned to dew; the pale translucent leaf-fans radiate their faint metallic sheen; the soft clubmoss carols its hidden bodies. I do not enter them. My needle legs are for the open wet stone, for the places where reflection and emptiness keep their accounts. When my path brings me to another of my kind—we are few, and always the same few—we halt. The stone counts seven heartbeats. Facial vents exchange a brief spray of condensate that tastes of crushed needles and charged iron. The lenses drink one another’s dim light. Then we continue on routes that are perfect mirrors, routes that will return us to this identical congress with no interval and no variation. The meeting is already happening again even as it concludes.

Sometimes I pass through a browser. We occupy the same outline for three or four strides; oil-slick rainbows and my own darker plates slide across one another like double-exposed film still wet in the developer tray. Neither of us notices. Collision is a concept that would require time to complete itself, and time is the element this place has refused so thoroughly that even the refusal no longer requires effort.

The clatter-wings ricochet near my upper plates. Their wing-cases strike my moss-seams with the bright loose note that threads the entire chorus; for an instant their copper proboscises confuse my condensate beads with the leaf-veins they prefer. They drink anyway. The channels inside me brighten fractionally, then dim. They leave ghost trails of their own outlines hanging in the mist at irregular angles, unfinished drawings of flight that never needed to finish.

I feel the heavy thing when it is near, though the rill lies across the basin. Its many joints concertina in my own deeper struts; its pliers open and close in the free space before my lenses, crushing nothing, forever. The small mites that live in its grooves sometimes migrate across the wet stone and briefly climb my legs, cleaning microscopic flakes of the young rock from my needle points, adjusting a tension I did not know had slipped, then vanishing into seams that seal as if they had always been sealed.

There are moments—though moment is the wrong merciful word—when the pulse-mosses release their warmer vapor and the scent of wet iron and needles rises through my vents like data. The foil-flutterers peel free of the leaf-fans, spiral once on etched wings, and settle back into exact crevices. I record the spirals with my lens-clusters. The distances remain what they were. The cloud ceiling hangs its permanent few body-lengths overhead, the color of old bottle glass, close enough that if I were anything that reached I might touch it; I am not anything that reaches.

I do not look up. Looking up would imply a direction that could change something. I do not approach the rim. The pale nothing beyond the drop is a fact the lenses have already measured into permanence; there is no need to verify. Leaving would require a before and an after. We have only the sustained mid-cycle.

What I am is the basin’s habit of checking its own unchanging measurements. I am the thin upright insistence that the scree is still scree, that the colonies remain islands, that the coolant lines still pulse in time, that every after-image still decays at the rate new substance grows. When a rock flake shears free under one needle point and damages the tip, I work the injured segment loose with small articulations that feel like both fingers and precision tools. I leave it on the wet stone. Already the replacement is emerging from the wound at the exact rate the discarded metal-and-tendon darkens and softens into the same mineral seepage that birthed us all. Companions may pass and assist; or I perform it alone. The population density of needle-walkers remains what the single frozen afternoon requires.

I process to continue. The loud living factory of damp iron life runs through me as surely as I run across its floor. My sounds are not added to the riveted air; they were always counted among the necessary harmonics. The mist weeps upward through my plates as easily as around them. I am local thickening of the weather of process—the cool dim light given just enough column and lens and needle to keep the distances honest, forever.

And because the loop is closed and perfect, I am also already at the next measuring point, and the previous one, and the exchange of condensate that is both memory and prophecy of itself. The young mountain’s newest habit has grown itself a quiet vertical instrument that cannot finish arriving and will never need to leave. The work is the worker is the wet green circuitry of the hanging basin, and I am one more way it refuses to end.
