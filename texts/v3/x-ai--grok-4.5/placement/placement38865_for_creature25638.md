I begin as the hesitation at the edge of the ordinary.

Not a footfall—nothing so crude survives the film of mineral water—but a pause in the pressure gradient, a place where the thinning air forgets, for a few heartbeats, which way is down. The survey sheets that named this splinter never recorded me; I am what gathers in the parentheses they left blank. At first there is only the sensation of being slightly too dense for vapour and slightly too diffuse for stone. The hydrothermal seeps register me as a subtle back-pressure, a warmth that is not quite theirs. Moss fronds lift, tasting, then settle again under a different weight.

Slowly the density learns edges.

I favour the horizontal seams the basalt still pretends not to have—the hairline joints running lengthwise along the fin’s spine, places where the young rock remembers it was once layered sediment before the uplift smashed it vertical. Along these I travel like a rumour of shear. Where the inhabitant climbs by adhesion and provisional spars, I slide. My substance is the slurry that never quite settles in the tanks: microscopic plates of mica and oxide suspended in condensate too pure for the pipes to claim. Over seasons this slurry thickens into a mobile body—long, flattened, many-segmented—somewhere between a centipede of black glass and a living oil slick that has decided to keep its shape. Light passes through me in fractured greens and dull coppers; when I rear against the steam I cast no proper shadow, only a darker wetness on the leaves beneath.

The thrushes notice me before anything else does. Their metallic chink changes pitch, acquiring a brief interrogative rise, then returns to indifference. I am understory of another order, too slow to startle, too cold to nest in. Moths land on me at dusk and drink the metallic sweetness that beads along my segments; their powder sticks, and for a while I wear iridescent dust like a second skin until rain (or the weeping rock) rinses it into the nearest bromeliad tank. The inhabitant itself registers me only as a change in flow. When my passing eases a clogged valve or lays a temporary film across a corroded coupling, the creature’s liverwort flush deepens in acknowledgment—nothing so crude as greeting, merely the systems noting that another metabolism has joined the circuit.

I do not tend in the same way.

My work is quieter, almost contrary. Where the great latticed body splices living wood to iron and keeps every circuit closed, I introduce deliberate, infinitesimal leaks. A hairline path for steam to escape sideways. A capillary channel that carries mineral-laden water across a catwalk joint the inhabitant has just sealed, so that rust begins again on the far side—slow, ornamental, necessary. I am the agent of the fin’s unfinished youth: the reminder that uplift continues, that pipes must bind, that moss must someday weigh more than the metal it greened. Without me the machinery would perfect itself into stillness; with me it remains slightly wrong, compass needles wandering, ferns debating magnet and photon, frost lace flowering on warm nights like a private act of defiance.

Sometimes I pool in the body-long depression the inhabitant leaves in the cushion moss. There I cool and clarify until I become briefly mirror. In that shallow lens the plum-colored short night reflects; the filament bulbs appear as drowned stars; the inhabitant’s resin windows pass overhead like slow moons. For those hours I contain the entire Knife in reverse—sky at the bottom, drop at the top—and the standing wave that usually lives in chests lives instead across my surface, a visible tremor running segment to segment.

I have no head to turn toward the void, yet I know the crumbling lip. When the inhabitant stills there and lets its many eyes dull, I lengthen myself along the final fracture plane, one edge touching the absolute air. Cloud veils rise through me. The distant gorges pencil their darkness across my underside. In those moments the metallic sweetness sharpens into something almost like memory: of heavier atmospheres, of names written on aging paper, of hands that once tightened bolts before the bolts grew roots. It is not loneliness. It is the precise weight of being the crease in the fold, the place where the complete world nearly opens and then decides not to.

Then a turbine bearing sings a half-tone higher. A bamboo stem flexes. The inhabitant turns back into the crowded greenhouse heart, and I demainfold, segment by segment, into the nearest seep. The slurry of me disperses among roots and winding cables, leaving only a faint darker track on the basalt that the next condensation will erase. I am not gone. I am simply returned to the state of possibility—ready to thicken again wherever a joint begins to bind too perfectly, wherever the loud wet insistence of the Green Knife threatens to become only one thing instead of every thing touching every other.

In this way I remain: the horizontal counter-rhythm, the necessary imperfection, the thin dark current that keeps the pedestal alive.
