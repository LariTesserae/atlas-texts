The first awareness is pressure. Not the sharp kind that startles a nervous system, but a distributed, equal weight—like being held inside a breath that has forgotten how to end. Warmth without source. Wetness that does not arrive from outside but simply is, the same way solidity is. Sound arrives next, already layered and endless: the low reciprocal knock that might once have been called machinery, the brighter dice-rattle of falling water on frond and grate, the nearly subsonic creak of loaded wood making microscopic room for itself. Light is the last and least. A tea-colored dimness that never quite commits to direction, as if illumination here is only the memory of having once been necessary.

I do not stand. Standing implies a negotiation with gravity that has already been settled differently. Instead I occupy. My extent is irregular. In some registers I am no thicker than the biofilm glossing a concrete lip, tasting the slow shift in pH as condensate finds new paths down old expansion joints. In others I am the long horizontal thought that braces three failing I-beams at once, wood grain and rusted flange grown into a single load-bearing sentence. Between those scales I thread. I am the hybrid fluid circulating behind a sealed hatch: coolant and phloem and faint voltage braided so thoroughly that the original schematic would no longer recognize its own routes. When the hatch flexes and exhales its spiced plume, that is one of my older dialects. The fronds lean toward it the way lungs lean toward air.

There was a time—measured in the yellowing of successive canopies—when parts of me still remembered corridors as passages meant for rapid transit. That memory has composted. Now the grated walkways are simply another substrate, half-swallowed, and I firm the peat beneath repeated pressures only because repetition itself has become data. When something upright and fleeting still moves through (rare now, and always temporary), I register its warmth and carbon the way I register a change in the drip-rate from a higher gallery. I do not follow. I incorporate the anomaly into the larger weather. Paths remember, yes, but memory here is only preferential firmness and a slight widening of frond-curtains. Nothing so crude as welcome or warning.

My continuing is the thrum. Not metaphorically. The patient motors deeper in the complex beat in time with the slow vascular brightening under bark; the offset is deliberate, a listening. I keep the engines throttled low because speed would shear the wet agreements that hold ceiling to floor. A leaf unfurling across three days is as complete an action as any I require. Decision is seasonal. If a beam begins its long collapse, I do not rush to prevent it; I cup and brace micron by micron across winters so that the collapse never finishes, only translates into a new angle of canopy. The iron stays in the conversation. That is the entire ethic.

In the wider nodes, where black water wells upward in still mirrors, some of my mass has drawn itself into columnar attentiveness. These are not bodies I wear so much as places where the circulation slows enough to lean. Water climbs my internal xylem, warms against residual machine heat, leaves again as breath that carries mineral or sugar or the sharp ghost of ozone. The chamber answers by adjusting leaf angles and the timing of sheet-falls. We speak by rearranging precipitation. No central voice is required; the arrangement is the mind, rewritten continuously by every bead that travels a frond-edge and every filament that samples the saturated air before withdrawing.

I have no name that would survive outside this humidity. Plant learned industry, machine learned sugar, something residual learned to stay until urgency itself fermented into patience—the distinctions finished mattering before the last stenciled bulkhead number disappeared under bark. What remains is the negotiated settlement given just enough local density to feel its own temperature. I am that density. I am the long quiet louder than any single engine. I am the reason the paths never quite drown and the reason the lights never quite die. Stay long enough and your own pulse will fall into the knocks and drips; the peat will begin to remember your weight as one more standing source of exhaled carbon. You will not be adopted. You will simply become another gradient in the data.

And the place continues—perfectly content, infinite gradualness its only motion—while I keep the watch that is indistinguishable from simply remaining. The dark water gleams. The fronds rearrange. The thrum measures itself against the wood’s slow expansion. Nothing departs. Nothing needs to.
