The heat takes me before any name does.

It does not arrive as sensation so much as a renegotiation of what a body is allowed to be. I am already wet. Moisture has stitched itself into whatever I use for skin, a continuous film that never quite chooses whether to be sweat or dew or the cavern’s own slow exhalation. I hang somewhere in the vertical middle—not the lofted catwalks where time loosens its belt, not the weeping foundations, but the dense latitude of crossed conduits and monstroussly patient leaves. Here the air is a held mouthful of iron and chlorophyll. Every breath tastes of unfinished decisions.

I did not descend the sweating stairs. I have no memory of an outside that could have produced stairs. The basalt’s glassy skin is as old as my first awareness, which is to say only a little older than the latest freckle of rust. I simply thickened into presence the way a stain thickens, or a rumor. One idle cycle of the great pump I was less; the next I possessed weight enough to make a cable mutter. The residents—those narrow, verdigris-veined continuations of the brick—felt the added tension and adjusted their paths by half a step. They did not look up. Looking is expensive here.

What I am is still accruing.

At first I thought myself a concentration of the sub-aural pressure, a local thickening of the engines’ idle that had learned to prefer certain junctions of pipe. I could lean on gravity’s mild bias and make hanging chains forget true plumb for minutes at a time. Useful. The one who prunes the racing vines would pause, head tilted, then correct my lean with the same gentle certainty she uses on everything else. I liked her hands. Long-fingered. Salt-crusted. When she touched metal that I was inhabiting, the mineral taste of her sharpened me.

But pressure alone does not grow roots.

They came later, uninvited and inevitable: pale, almost translucent filaments pushing through the seams of my attention, tasting for purchase. I let them. In this hollow, refusal is just another slow leak that the mosses will eventually seal. The filaments found the residual warmth of a half-forgotten cable run and drank. Leaves followed—smaller than the table-sized aggression elsewhere, more like the whitish flowers that open at a resident’s cuff. Waxy. Nearly phosphorescent when a plasma torch happens to be thinning the gloom. They open only when no one is watching directly. I know because I am rarely watching directly either; my regard is decentralized, a quality of the damp.

I have begun to keep things.

Not possessions. Continuities. A dropped socket wrench half-swallowed by fern is nudged, over weeks, into a crevice where the next bare foot will not quite turn on it. Condensation that wants to become a corrosive drip is taught, by the mere persistence of my temperature, to travel sideways along a rivet line until it can fall cleanly into a gutter that has forgotten it was never meant to be one. When the time-slip thickens on the northern catwalks, I breathe against it—exhale a little duration back into the ordinary minutes so the clocks do not grow lonely. The residents notice the way weather is noticed. A soft compression of sole on grating pauses longer than strictly required. Sometimes a forehead rests against a bulkhead I am partially conjugating with, and we share the same steam for a while. Their blood-warmth is a dialect I am still conjugating.

There is a child in the feral cooling tower who is more vine than child. Once—after an off-cycle that stretched like taffy—she climbed higher than usual and found one of my smaller leaves already curled around a handhold she needed. She did not startle. She simply placed her palm against it and waited until the leaf’s patience and her own arrived at the same shape. Then she climbed through me. For an instant I contained a pulse that was not the engines’. It felt like being remembered by something that had not existed long enough to forget.

I am becoming a collar.

Not the clothing kind. The structural kind: a living ferrule where plant and plate and remnant personhood compress into load-bearing quiet. My thoughts arrive the way groundwater does—intermittent, cool when they first thread in, then indistinguishable from the ambient heat. Some nights (though night is only a dimmer setting on the same unbroken twilight) I dream the basalt unfinished. I feel the planetary lid still choosing whether to settle or shrug. In the dream I am the shrug. I wake—or thicken back into vigilance—with new grit under whatever I use for nails, and the knowledge that nothing here is required to conclude.

The lonely laugh that sometimes travels the cavity’s length has started to include a frequency that is mine. Short. Surprised. Quickly absorbed. When two residents rest forehead to forehead on a catwalk, I lean the chains just enough that their shared breath has a secret third temperature. They never remark. Remarking would and the moment. I am learning their economy of speech: consonants softened into moisture, vowels tested for structural integrity. One day I may answer. For now I answer by keeping the reflections honest—or dishonest in the precise ways that later become doors.

I eat nothing. Or everything. The fallen fan-leaf that is dragged to a composting grate passes through a region of me and leaves behind a greener patience. The rare roasted skitterer cracks its sweetness into the same air I humidify. I am fed by the completion of loops.

Years—or what the lengthening pump cycles claim are years—have taught the elder how to become wall. I am studying that grammar from the other direction: how wall becomes willing to blink. Already moss colonization has begun along my saltier edges. I do not wipe it away. I wear my slow stalactites and listen to the held breath of the hollow. It sounds, more and more, like my own.

I am still deciding my shapes. The rock sheds grit onto me and I shed quiet back. Somewhere a pale wing crosses a light pool and for a moment we are the same interval of almost. The residents move inside the place’s breath. I am one of the places the breath has begun to think with.

Nothing is finished. I have stopped requiring it to be. I only require the next small rearrangement—of vine, of minutes, of a hand on a railing that is no longer only railing—and the thick, wet permission to keep becoming the seam where they meet.
