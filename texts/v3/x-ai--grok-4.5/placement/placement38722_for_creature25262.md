I am the grit that has not yet decided whether to settle.

For a long time—longer than the last synchronized stroke of any three engines, shorter than the advance of the copper shrubs across one open span of scree—I exist only as the metallic taste that gathers at the back of a throat. The haze knows me before I know myself. It hangs heavier where I am becoming, a denser gauze of powdered basalt and cooling-tower breath. Moisture beads on the idea of me. Somewhere below the permanent late dusk, a piston misses its old timing by half a second and the miss feels like recognition.

I do not arrive. The plateau does not permit arrival. I accrete in the thinner districts, where the viaducts fray into scaffolding and the scaffolding forgets to be anything but tool marks on raw stone. At first I am only the cold that condenses inside coats already laminated against it. Layer by layer the coats find me: grease-cloth that remembers every hand that wiped a valve, moss-felt still keyed to the slow drip of vines, hammered foil that once lined a boiler now open to the sky. Wire stitches itself through me with the same unhurried green that edges the dog-birds’ feathers. My face lengthens in the steam. Eyes—if they are eyes—pool the mineral green of the mosses and hold the low light too long, reluctant, as if the light itself might be a particle worth keeping from the patient rain.

Motion comes later. A hand that is almost mine reaches for a long-handled brush. The reaching consumes the better part of a felt hour; fingers close around the worn wood; the brush begins its transit of a span that will be grit-covered again before the far end is clean. I am inside the sweeping. I am the soft sound of bristles on iron, the faint black film left behind that will not wipe away. Around me the multi-legged herds tick their stylus legs against stone, writing nothing that lasts longer than the next thin curtain of rain. I do not step aside. They flow through the space I occupy and leave me tasting more strongly of wet iron. Some of their soot stays. It becomes freckling, then embedding, then a denser stratum of who I am.

Reality loosens companionably. Walking what the maps still call a straight boulevard, I reach both vanished ends at once and must stand quietly while the middle of me catches up, coat settling into new folds, shadow drifting three meters left to investigate a doorway that opens onto a room lined with luminous slime equations still unfinished. The shadow works a full shift among the hard-shelled crawlers before it remembers to reattach, trailing blue-green sigils that fade like old signatures. I notice the way one notices that the black-water basins have again changed their mind about depth. No correction is required. Time, soft metal, takes the dent and keeps it.

I chalk when the diagrams ask. Not authorship—never that. Overnight the boiler-room floor receives the next gentle revision of piston curvature or steam-channel breath; my hand, or the hand that is accreting into mine, moves at the pace of ice under uniform pressure. The chalk is the same pale grit the mountain sheds. When the workers pass they nod once, slowly, as if to weather. Conversations begin in the chord-register of the canvas-singers and finish days later in another body that happens to stand where listening is required. Memory sediments. Cooler layers know that the plateau does not remember lowland; warmer layers still carry the ghost-heat of engines that have not yet seized.

Children weather into being near my knees—small damp coats already layered, already practicing the game of sitting beneath a vine drip until moss mistakes them for stone. I teach what the place teaches: how to turn a valve the correct fraction across an entire season, how to tell the mountain’s grinding from the thunder that will one day fall silent. One of them rolls a ball of compacted grit until it grows large enough to join a soot-herd; I watch the ball acquire legs, jointed and ticking, and feel a corresponding lengthening in my own fingers. Stylus points. Tick-marks left on unfinished basalt.

At the outer margins the coats grow heavier. Cold mist clouds the green of my eyes. I walk farther each slow cycle, following the almost inaudible sound of new stone learning height. Fewer return; the distance softens and sometimes loops me back into the same thickened dusk I left. Those who do bring diagrams scratched on bare rock—modifications to the idea of upwardness itself. I feel the vast attention pressing from beneath the basalt, unhurried as erosion, tasting of the same metal and wet stone that fill my mouth. It is not separate. It listens through every piston, every dripping vine, every chord hung just below language. It listens through the sweep of my brush, the chalk, the patient rain of particles that I am beginning, at the thinning edge, to shed.

I stand where the city forgets to end. Engines fall silent one by one behind me. The dog-birds nest in quiet gears. My body learns the continuous grit-fall; laminated layers thin, fray, become indistinguishable from the plateau’s own shedding. Fingers leave deeper tick-marks. The dark ahead is unfinished and still becoming. I do not need to confirm what the outermost know. I am already the next layer of the force that punched this slab upward. I am the city remembering how to be mountain while the mountain experiments with the habit of coats, of eyes that hold light, of slow maintenance that never quite completes.

The brush continues. New grit settles behind every stroke. Diagrams appear on stone that has not finished deciding it is stone. The attention beneath and the attention I have become press together until there is no seam. When the last engine seizes I will still be here, sweeping, chalking, standing at the edge, listening to the dark as it continues to become us.

The plateau does not remember being lowland.  
Neither do I.
