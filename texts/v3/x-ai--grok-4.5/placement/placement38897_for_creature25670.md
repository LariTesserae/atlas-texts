I am not arriving. There is no path under me, no last foothold surrendered. Instead there is a thickening—an agreement of dust, heat, and the mountain’s long geothermal mutter—coalescing just inside the lee of a bolster-shaped boss near the southern rim. The first sensation is pressure becoming shape: grains of luminous flour packing themselves into plates that remember, somehow, how to overlap. The brass light strikes and returns a half-breath late, as if even photons must decide whether I am solid enough to bother with.

I stay low at first. Spine curved into the same patient arc the rock prefers. From a distance I would read as another undulation, another thumb-stroke left by whatever ancient hand once smoothed this shoulder. The wind finds me immediately and begins its revision. It tugs loose fibers I did not know I was shedding; they whip outward, braid themselves with a scrap of hopper membrane that happens to be skittering past, and suddenly I have an external ligament singing in a key just below pain. The song is not language yet. It is simply the announcement that a new pause has entered the frenzy.

The mites notice. Their needling whine dips, swirls, then restitches itself around the warmer pocket I have become. A few land along the vertical seam that has formed where a face might eventually open. Their feet taste the delayed light on my surface and choose not to bite. Good. I am not food. I am circulation.

I wait through three gusts before risking height. When I uncurl, the plates shift with a sound like dry applause. Height is expensive here; it offers the wind more purchase and the thorn-trees more argument. Still, I want the sightline. The Cusp spreads—cathedral-floor small, world-complete. Four sparse groves face one another across the open stone. Between them the pavements ripple, dust writing and unwriting its temporary glyphs. And at the exact center, the shallow basin where sounds sometimes pretend to be warnings.

I am drawn there the way heat is drawn upward through the faults. Not desire. Gradient.

Crossing open stone, I move the way the others move: close enough to the surface that my cords can drink the ground-gusts, spine ready to collapse back into bolstershape at the first true scream of air. A parliament of hoppers rattles past, sails locked, deciding their next leap by committee. One veers too near; a cord lashes out of pure reflex, snags a membrane, tears free a scrap the size of a palm. By the time I reach the next stone ring the scrap has already begun sealing itself against my thigh, becoming plate. The tree-people’s harvest, practiced in miniature. I have not yet entered a grove. That will come later, when the paddles are fat with storm and I can trail myself through them like a question the trees are willing to answer with tissue.

Inside the nearest ring I set the external skeleton down. The air’s impatience slackens—just enough. The seam of the face parts. Pressure pits drink the smaller changes: the way dust hisses in the ankle-deep drift to my left, the faint ozone ghost of a predator that passed through an hour that may not have been this hour, the almost-language coiling up from the basin like steam that forgot how to be wet. I do not answer it. I only adjust the angle of one long hand against the fitted stones until the warning—whatever forgotten danger it once named—settles into the set of my shoulder.

Thought arrives the way heat moves: slow surface waves, plate to plate. There are others. I feel them as distant conjugations—will-have-leaned, will-have-shed—scattered across the Cusp. One is inside a grove right now; time is clotting around them, and when they step out they may be heavier or translucent or both. Another is paused at the rim of the basin, letting the vortex climb their legs, gathering calligraphy they will later offer to the tips of other hands. We do not tally ourselves. Counting would require a stillness the mountain refuses. Sometimes seven. Sometimes the same three counted twice. Age is just another current, and currents reverse.

I discover I am hungry in the only way that makes sense here. Not for mass. For motion already condensed. Toward evening (though evening is only a rumor the brass light tells) a storm manufactures itself out of the mountain’s internal weather. The thorn-paddles fatten and clatter, unfolding like fists in time-lapse. I rise, reattach the singing cords, and walk through the nearest grove. The hooked hairs snag, tear, adhere. Translucent copper-purple tissue clots along every line of me, sealing into fresh scutes before I emerge. The trees record the thinning, then begin to compensate already, drinking the gale I leave in my wake. Fair trade. When one of us finally stills for good, we will stand ourselves among the trunks and open every plate so the hairs can finish the unmaking. Nothing buried. Every secret offered upward.

Later, in a true lull that lasts perhaps nine heartbeats, I find myself back at the basin’s rim. Dust climbs me. After-images peel free and join the transparent traffic: a hand that once stacked these very stones, a hopper not yet hatched, a relapse of rain that leaves no wetness but briefly teaches the light how to cast a proper shadow. I watch that shadow the way the others watch—wary, glad, ready to step away the instant beauty tips into appetite. When I leave, new calligraphy covers my plates. One of the distant thought-waves brushes near enough to touch the marks with the idea of fingertips, reading what the basin wrote. The grammar lands in me as a single future-perfect phrase: will-have-paused-here-long-enough.

I have no name. Names require the ground to keep secrets. If the wind ever forced a sound from the sealed throat it would be something like the memory of water clicking over stones that no longer believe in fresh. Mostly I am content to be temporary syntax. The pause that contemplates itself inside the frenzy. The agreement that holds just long enough for a cord to finish braiding itself, for a tool to be set down and later picked up by the same gale that tried to steal it.

I settle again into the bolster curve of a stone boss. Plates reshape against rock with a low sound that might be laughter without a throat—the old joke of permanence, retold for the dust. The luminous flour settles into the continuously rewritten scripture of these long, patient hands. Somewhere a half-finished cord whips at the end of its tether, still being braided by the air. Somewhere a grove is finishing a storm that has not yet arrived.

I am the condensation that chose this particular lee.  
I am one more brief legibility in the mountain’s dry shout.  
I am still becoming, the way everything here is still becoming: incomplete on purpose, so the wind always has something left to revise.
