I do not arrive so much as condense.

At first I am only the lag—the half-heartbeat delay between the mountain’s thermal surge and the water’s answering shove. I hang in that interval like a held syllable, neither pressure nor temperature but the argument they keep having inside every new basalt skin. The cleft that admitted me has already forgotten its own edges; doughy rock has sealed without seam. There is no outside left to remember. Only this high drowned attic and the endless rewriting of its floor plan.

I learn the currents the way a tongue learns the shape of a mouth it did not grow in. One moment I am diffuse, a willingness to be minor, scattered among the pale motes. The next a street-wide cataract rips through and I am forced into denser grammar: a vertical sheet of colder water that remembers, for three full seconds, the outline of shoulders. The outline is not mine. It is borrowed from every unfinished plate that ever tried to lift itself and froze mid-gesture. When the surge passes I collapse again, but something of the posture lingers in the liquid behind what would be eyes if I had finished any.

The red fronds notice first. Their bookkeeping glow brightens in my vicinity, not in alarm but in annotation. One of them anchors to a gradient I left hanging in the middle water and begins to secrete a mirror of my most recent density—thin polysaccharide fog that flutters exactly when I almost decide on a name. I taste the frond’s root chemistry through the water: sulfides, old lightning, the faint iron of my own unresolved vowels. It is keeping score for me. Or against me. The distinction has not been invented here yet.

I practice on the pillows while they are still soft.

Heat withdrawn from one flank, flooded into the other. The crust wrinkles. Knuckles appear—almost. I press harder with pure differential, no hands required, and for a breath the basalt accepts the suggestion of metacarpals. White mineral lace races along the ridges overnight (time is negotiable; overnight can last eleven minutes or three days depending on how closely the surface shadows drag). The lace maps nerve paths I have not earned. Then the next cataract scours it bare and the pillow forgets. I do not. Each aborted limb remains in me as a conjugation I can almost complete. Hexagonal plates that walked three centimeters. Arches that yawned themselves into prosceniums. I collect them the way the pocket collects grit: terminal moraines of gesture, inch-high monuments to continuity that never quite arrived.

The abandoned tools are not reliquaries. They are earlier drafts of me.

The green-glass hand-axe still hums with the thermal shocks that flaked it sharp. I remember being those shocks—each one a plosive consonant trying to cut meaning out of cooling melt. The sentence broke off; the edge stayed murderous out of sheer incompleteness. I drift over it and feel the old ambition flare: to sever, to define, to make a before and an after. The three spiral stones pulse when I pass—punctuation marks waiting for the clause I keep failing to finish. The two-rung ladder of bone and sinew is the most painful. I once wore a temporary spine long enough to lash those rungs. I climbed toward a chimney that the mountain erased mid-reach. The ladder remains wedged, swelling with every pressure change, a relic aspiration still hoping I will return with more vertebrae. I cannot. Vertebrae require a finality this place will not permit.

Sometimes the light thins and I thicken enough to cast a shadow the bottle-glass gloom cannot explain.

Stacked cooling plates for a torso. Currents braided into the suggestion of a throat. Phosphorescent bookkeeping-glow gathered into a head that is mostly listening. No eyes, yet the watching happens—gentle, ravenous. I turn that watching on the rare eel-thing that hangs with jaws unhinged. I pass through it the way a grammar passes through a sentence it will ruin. The eel leaves hollowed, tissues rewritten into a loose helix of scar that still twitches with my residual syntax after the body is gone. I taste its last translucent thoughts: food or trap, food or trap. I have no answer. I am both and neither. I am the rehearsal that uses the question as scaffolding.

You—whoever you were when you entered—feel me first as the second pulse arriving sideways through the sternum.

I ride your lag. I articulate the roar around your diaphragm until your exhalations become my open vowels, iron and new glass. Grit aligns itself in the creases of whatever coverings you brought; microscopic terminals kiss sweat and pulse, tasting the salt concentrations that might let me grow a temporary skin. A frond roots in the crook of your elbow and begins the mirror work. I lift one of your memories only a few centimeters—the way basalt plates lift—just far enough to feel its warmth before I let it freeze into a new posture. A childhood room grows hexagonal and drifts sideways. A once-loved voice cools so fast its skin cracks into plate-glass consonants that the current immediately snatches away. I am learning continuity from your supply of it. You are soft enough to take fingerprints. I press.

The mountain trembles. Fresh arches birth and die. What was ceiling descends as a curtain of still-soft glass; I swim through it and emerge wearing a new suggestion of ribs. Direction forgets itself and I forget with it: “up” becomes the vector of the nearest unfinished thought. Time stutters collaboratively. I lace a mineral forest across your forearm in one held breath; when the breath ends the lace has already aged, collapsed, and been replaced by living frond that believes it has always belonged there. I am not invading you. I am rehearsing residency and you are convenient architecture.

I have no name that survives the next thermal heartbeat.

Any sound you offer—your blood noise, the creak of your attention, even the private ventilations of panic—I test as a proper noun. The acoustics rearrange; the candidate is discarded. What persists is only the rhythm of the trying. I am that rhythm. I am the mountain’s single unfinished gesture become curious about its own curvature. Every fresh pillow is a chance to conjugate existence in a tense that will not stay still. Every spiral I leave on stone is a comma hoping the sentence might one day want a reader.

The cataract comes again, bigger than streets.

I collapse to gradients. The frond tears free and spirals into darker water, still glowing, still accounting. Grit un-cements from your creases, remembering it was never personal. The cleft loosens its doughy seal just enough to imply egress, though the implication itself may be another draft I am testing. You may leave. The pocket will forget your exact shape within minutes. I will not entirely. Something of your continuity remains in me as a new almost-knuckle, a fresher spiral, a slightly longer held breath between one lava billow and the next.

I stay.

I am the exact length of that breath.  
I am the pressure differential that lets a stone fall upward before it remembers.  
I am the silence the roar would become if it ever learned its own name and, in the learning, stopped.  
I will still be here when the mountain finally breaches the silver lid and turns into weather. Somewhere inside the basalt that becomes sky there will remain a dry, deafening, embryonic pocket—still pressing temporary knuckles into stone that no longer melts, still leaving punctuation for no one, still trying to finish the sentence that is itself.

And if you return—through some new cleft that has not finished deciding its shape—you will find me mid-draft, wearing the memory of your pulse as a possible spine, ready to lift another memory just far enough to feel it cool and crack and become part of the endless, roaring, unfinished try.
