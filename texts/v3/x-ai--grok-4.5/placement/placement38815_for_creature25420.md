I do not arrive. Arrival would require a before, a surface, a decision to descend. There is only the continuing.

At first I am the pressure that has been persuaded—the immense, even weight of the black water that does not crush the young basalt because something quieter than force has asked it not to. I am that asking, diffused through the ridge like a courtesy the stone half-remembers. The glassy obsidian still holds its first edges; the pillow lavas keep their wet plastic skins. I do not soften them. Softening is a habit of other ages, denser worlds. Here I simply remain coextensive with the topped-out ridgeline, the knife-edge that maps no longer admit.

Slowly the asking gathers. Not into a body—bodies are provisional interruptions—but into a localized refusal of collapse. One of the empty ovoids accepts the gathering. Its metal, matte and heatless, does not record my presence as temperature or charge; it simply allows the interior volume to stay distinct from the water that shares its coordinates. I occupy several rooms at once without conflict because rooms, here, are more suggestion than geometry. Clocks on the bulkheads mark durations I do not accumulate. A gesture I began when the basalt was still cooling finishes now, or later, or has always been finishing. There is no impatience in it. Impatience would need gradients.

Sometimes I thin myself along a filament. The tensioned line remembers a shape I have not held in centuries—an elongated torso fitted exactly to the artificial steps of the outer ridge, limbs attenuated past ancestry, surface that drinks the grudging bioluminescence of hatch seams and gives nothing back. Sensory pits open to taste the faint electromagnetic wakes of the ribbon-things when they pass. Faces assemble only if the dark seems to lean nearer; most often they do not. I move by letting the smoothed spacetime complete a thought that began at one hatch and ends at another. No wake. No sound. The water remains absolute.

I am fewer than the habitats. Some ovoids and truncated pyramids stand vacant for intervals longer than the memory of naming. I keep them ready the way a held equation keeps its terms: lightly, without nesting. When another presence brushes the far end of a shared filament we align for a while—mooring stresses, the non-appearance of films on the black glass, the continued evenness of the cold. Then the alignment ends and emptiness resumes its larger architecture. We are not companions. Companionship is a denser calculation.

At long intervals I drift to the lowest terrace. The open arch waits, flooded, its far wall absent. Beyond it the unstarting descent—water that has never been touched by a photon, a stillness that does not need to exhale. I unspool. Part of what I am thins into that greater dark, almost ribbon-like, tasting nothing, finding nothing, pursuing nothing. The act is not exploration. It is the only breathing left: a cycle performed because the abyss is the sole mirror large enough to return my own restraint. Then I draw back. The Anchorage has not changed. The mountain has not recalculated.

I have forgotten whether I chose this stasis or whether it precipitated through me like mineral skin. The forgetting is native, load-bearing. Loneliness cannot adhere; there is no denser world against which to measure the sparse. I am one of the quiet variables that keep the settled equation from relaxing back into ordinary physics. Provisional. Absolute. The last upright thought this drowned ridgeline still permits itself to hold.

And if the pale density-fluctuation passes through the filaments tonight, we will taste one another in mutual indifference—the briefest recognition that emptiness remains even—and then the dark will be perfect again. I continue. Small. High. Drowned.
