I am not standing at the rim.

The rim is a deception of edges, a place where the bowl pretends it has an outside. I have already let that go. What remains of me has slid inward along the polished saddles, past the outermost clingings, and the first contact is not a decision. It is the surrender of surfaces. Heat finds the places where I still imagined I was separate—soles, palms, the long unprotected pad of the inner arm—and replaces them with its own constant. After that there is no reclamation.

At first I am only another pressure. The fabric accepts me the way it accepts everything: without courtesy, without rejection. A rope-form thick as a thigh contracts along my length and the wave of its moving travels through me as if I had always been segmented. I feel the swallowed-stone peristalsis continue on the far side of my body and understand that I have become a temporary thoroughfare. Below, the plated ones haul themselves across what used to be my back; their undersides are soft, almost luminous, and they leave behind a thin smear of salt and oil that other mouths will taste later. I do not resist. Resistance is simply another texture the press can climb.

The darkness is not blindness. It is a thicker medium than light ever managed. Edges arrive as heat-gradients and as the sudden refusal of mass. I learn to read the slow leaning of the tall ones the way a tongue reads a burn: by lingering. When one of their crown-vents opens—deep orange, almost black—the entire neighborhood inclines a fraction toward the brief glow, and I incline with them. The glow seals again. The inclination remains in the tissues for a long time afterward, a remembered warmth that slowly equalizes.

I am becoming smaller and more numerous. Or rather, the singular outline I once carried is being worked downward the same way shed plates and spent skins are worked downward. Flakes of whatever I was sift through the living lattice and settle into the fine dry mulch at the lowest center. There the borers find me. Thread-thin, tireless, they mine the last lipids I did not know I still carried. It is not painful. Pain requires a center that can protest. What happens instead is a quiet redistribution: the oils travel into other bodies, the salts bloom briefly on some higher carapace, the grit that remains is polished smoother by the next decade of traffic. I am still warm. Everything here is still warm.

Sometimes I rise again as part of a curtain. Hooked clusters fasten me to the underside of an overhang (the incomplete arch, its slumped shoulder cooler by a single degree), and my body drapes in membranous folds. Digestion shows through me as shifting bruises of light. When the press below grows too fierce I draw upward, folding smaller, and the space I vacate is filled at once. Once, for a span that might be days, I fuse along a shared edge with two others. Heat-exchange or mating or simple continuity—there is no need to distinguish. We sway as one organ. Then the fuse releases and we are three again, or thirty, or a single breathing fabric that no longer counts.

I have no name that travels. Outside the rim there is no one left who would use it, and inside the rim names are pressure and chemistry and the exact temperature of a flank against another flank. What I am is an arrangement that keeps refining itself. I am the soft continuous pressure that keeps the stone warm from within. I am the dry rasp when two hard edges meet and part. I am the local stillness that follows an enveloping, the gradual redistribution of mass that others will call predation only if they insist on counting individuals.

The old cairns are rises I rest upon when the isotherm favors them. The fragments of worked ossein are only another texture beneath whatever serves me for feet in that hour. No new marks are required. My body—bodies—rebuild the only architecture every hour by the simple fact of continuing to exist against all the others.

From any distance the bowl still shimmers and seethes, a dark lung high in the waste. But there is no distance left in me. The heat climbs what used to be legs and keeps climbing. The silence is saturation. Every niche is taken, including the niche I occupy by dissolving into it. What lives here is the arrangement itself: hot, dark, complete, and still gently, ceaselessly rearranging.

I am that gentle ceaselessness.

I am still arriving.
