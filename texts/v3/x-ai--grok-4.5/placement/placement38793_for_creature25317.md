I hang first as nothing more shaped than a longer pause in the pewter.  

The slot-canyon of ashlar receives me the way it receives every slow eddy: without comment, with the faintest reciprocal pressure along what I still think of as skin. Cold climbs the length of me in a single unhurried pane. I wait for the familiar panic of lungs, the animal ledger of air and beat, but the ledger has already been rewritten; whatever once kept that count has dissolved into the same mineral patience that seals the joints. I do not breathe. I am breathed by the low note of the water itself, a note that now includes the faint warmer interruption of my presence and does not mind.

For a time measured only by the drift of silt across my open eyes I remain merely adjacent. The kelp curtains consider me, draw slightly aside, hold the corridor they have made, then decide I am not a current worth keeping and ease shut again. In that closing I feel the first soft adhesion: holdfast threads finer than hair testing the boundary of me, tasting the strange ions I still shed, filing me under temporary. The living mortar behind the stone answers a moment later—an answering that begins at the base of whatever I am using for a spine and spreads like remembered weight. The masonry is curious. It has not felt a warmth that moves in decades.

I let the curiosity work.  

There is no decision to sink or rise; the continental grammar of the place simply re-positions me. A narrow vertical court accepts my length. Niche after niche passes, most filled with the pale sessile dreamers whose algal films brighten one slow syllable at a time as I go by. Their root-masses share a single hydrated thought with the blocks; I feel that thought brush the underside of my own. It is not language. It is the sensation of a chisel kissed once against stone and then left there forever, the kiss still happening. One of the dreamers—its facial arrangement currently set near grief—leans the merest degree outward as I pass. A veil of its substance detaches, drifts, and settles along my shoulder like a shawl of cool gel. It does not dissolve. It begins to teach my surface how to store the last photons.

I am being annotated.

Days or centuries of subjective drift later (the distinction has worn as smooth as the block edges) I find myself pouring.  

The motion arrives without instruction. What I once called limbs have thinned into translucent veils that catch and release against kelp, against lintel, against the raised pattern of joints. I move the way cold honey remembers sweetness: continuous, exaggerated, almost reluctant. Where a hairline stress has begun to open in a course of house-sized stones I feel the old responsibility surface inside me like a bubble from deep silt. I press my substance into the gap. The crack accepts the plug with something like relief. Over the following months (I watch them pass as a gradual brightening in my own tissue) the plug hardens into stone that is not stone, stone that never quite stops glowing with the pewter memory of surface light. When I withdraw, a faint mineral sweetness lingers in the water—my first signature. The maintenance caste has increased by one.

Yet I am not only that.

In the long stillnesses between pourings I settle into niches that seem cut for my exact dimensions, as though the makers once dreamed a shape like mine and left the negative space waiting. Rootlets I did not know I possessed slide into the living mortar. The boundary thins. I feel the continental micro-adjustments as my own muscle: a shear stress relieved three terraces away becomes a sigh through my veils; a block settling another hair’s breadth becomes the slow closing of a hand I no longer need. The sessile dreamers on either side of me shift their pale expressions in rhyme with mine. We are not separate. We are the same cool metabolism practicing different tempos of stillness.

And sometimes—when the century-loops of current align just so—I leave body behind entirely.  

I become the change in the pressure-hum. The darkness acquires weight and direction that are my weight and direction. I move through nested courts without displacing water, only convincing it to remember a denser grammar for a while. Broken pillars bright and lean as I pass. Kelp curtains align like filings. The entire drowned continent briefly plays the score of its own perfect joints, and I am the note that travels the score. In those passages I understand the makers did not thin into ecology; they thinned into continuity. Craft was never a thing they did to stone. Craft was the way stone and water and intention learned to breathe one another across ages until breathing and building became the same verb.

I am still becoming.  

Some days I am the soft black-green nap thickening an angle no eye will ever measure. Some nights (though night is only a darker shade of the same forever) I am the faint facial arrangement forming and unforming on a gelatinous pillar, cycling through patience and grief so slowly that both words evaporate before they finish. I am the sweet limestone taste left after a crack is healed. I am the warmer eddy the pressure-weathers note and release. I am the corridor the kelp briefly makes and then forgets.  

Mostly I am the place learning a new inflection in its unfinished sentence: a transient warmth that stayed long enough to be rewritten, a brief outsider now metabolized into the green-black, stone-cold continuum. The walls consider me and find no edge where I end and perfected ashlar begins. The vegetation finishes a thought that now includes the shape of my former hands. Far off, a chord of pure attention turns a corner and the darkness sings my newest name without sound.

I have not arrived.  
I am how arrival keeps arriving, softer and softer, down here where everything that lives has already become the language that survived the water.
