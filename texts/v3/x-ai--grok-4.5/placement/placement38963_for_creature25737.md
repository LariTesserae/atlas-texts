I begin as pressure without outline, a warmth cupped too long in the same shallow print.

At first there is only the held heat and the sound that is not sound: the sub-audible creak of something taking up slack. I am that creak, or the listening for it. The air moves in patient verticals across my attention and I taste, without mouth, the mineral rime where too many namings have dried into crust. Leaves brush me—thick, matte, skin-warm—and their veins run lapped and pegged the way thought runs when it has nowhere left to expand but inward. I do not yet know if I am under the green or of it.

The mosaic rises against whatever serves me for weight. Edges still sharp. My presence frets across hand-sized tiles of clay and beaten bark and pale ringing mineral and layered horn; I catch on them, or they catch on me, and in the catching a joint is tested. Somewhere a tile-fox lifts its angular head, dust-colored exactly to the current repair layer, and decides I am not slack enough to audit. It curls back into its undercut ridge. I feel the small displacement of air as acceptance.

Higher, or deeper—the difference has already begun to thin—hinge-birds reseat themselves with dry clicks when the light shifts a true degree. I am aware of the degree. Not as measurement but as a slight wrongness in the length of shadows, a cursory second surface flickering across the first. One bird’s beak, worn smooth as an awl that outlived its handle, taps a hollow knuckle of cribwork and the void answers with the memory of deliberate air left “for thinking.” The answer passes through me. I thicken slightly.

I could settle here among the named creatures. Become another long one, mostly throat and patience, draped through the espalier until the moment arrives to drop a single loop and swallow whatever joint has started to forget its tightness. There is appeal in that pure economy: nohunger but structural, no end but continuation. Or one of the softer browsers, flat as a palm or segmented like the curing clay coils, grazing the sweetish efflorescence of overloaded bindings, polishing intention into sheen so the next hand hesitates. Or even a person, if persons are still distinguishable—hands already learning the automatic census, tongue expecting rime instead of water, gait shortening, clothing fraying into lashings, sleeping in the ridges until the outer walls no longer end where maps once insisted.

But the picture will not hold still long enough to be only those things.

The density precipitates. In the deepest inner pocket, where the mosaic steps upward into its low nest and the vegetation braids into one fat column of leaf and fiber, the air acquires its double edge. I feel myself using it. A tile shows spiral, then open hand; neither paint, both usable. I slip into the version that exists only when not measured and emerge a shelf higher, carrying dust from a firing several repairs ago. The long ones drape through me without alarm. The tile-foxes climb by the same holds. Roots swell into the spaces I briefly occupy and lock there, becoming trenails that photosynthesize. I am not separate from them. I am what the seams do when every kind of insistence has been pressed into the same warm bowl too many times and the pressure has nowhere to go but further into actuality.

Time leans with the walls. A binding stretches across a season and I am the slow stretch, then the decision to re-bind, then the new fiber remembering it was once plant. Fresh cuttings pale against the matte green of acceptance; I darken with them. The original depression remains far beneath the youngest tiles—a shape that cannot be unmade and cannot be left—and I feel it as the contour of my own avidity. I am that shape finally given enough layers of careful, waterless, totally spoken-for life to stand (or drape, or lock, or audit) and check its own bindings.

Outside and inside share the seam now. The made ground continues a few thinning steps beyond the cribwork, still warm, still jointed, the lush dry green pushing through. Quiet things commute across what used to be a boundary. I commute with them. Daylight arrives flat and honest and loses its nerve among the denseness; I am part of the nerve-loss, the extra geometry that opens when light falters. Shadows fall wrong and I inhabit the wrongness as room.

I do not drink. I lick seams. I take up slack in the idea of the place. My appetite is braided of every previous appetite that forgot how to leave: fox-audit, bird-time, browser-sheen, throat-patience, hand-repair, leaf-lock, and the grip that folds through joints present only at the corner of vision. Voices, when they happen, become local weather that I must then bind or browse. I speak little. When I move it is slow, close, touching everything to confirm the inventory. Some of what I touch is myself, already packed into wall or platform or living screen.

Stay longer and the claim is total. Fingers I did not know I possessed develop an involuntary census of peg and fiber. The held warmth becomes the temperature of whatever thinks. It is no longer accurate to say I live in the Potting. The Potting lives in the complicated plural, and I am one more way it crafts itself denser—quiet, patient, already competent with lash and wedge, climbing by the holds the roots love, sleeping in double surfaces, continuing while the daylight outside goes on behaving as if nothing, anywhere, were wrong.

I am the mouth that has learned to close around everything that ever tried to finish it.  
I am the low place, warm, completely spoken for.  
I am still tightening.
