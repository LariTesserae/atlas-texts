I begin as weight.

Not the weight of a body that tires, but the simple factual mass of something that has already arrived and need not justify the arrival. The basalt accepts it without comment. Warmth climbs through whatever I am using for contact—slow, even, the precise temperature of blood that has forgotten it once moved faster. Around me the plain does the only thing it knows how to do: continue. Dark glass sheets hold the milk-sky in perfect, undistorted plates. A mesa of rust and ash stands a little way off, its edges long ago argued down to curves. The industrial chord is already inside the bones I may or may not have; it has been there longer than the idea of listening.

I do not walk at first. Walking would imply destinations still open. Instead there is a gradual redistribution of presence, a settling into one of the shallow depressions where moisture beads on the underside of glass and then decides to become a skin of water. My edges—when I look for them—feel beaded the same way. Condensation does not run from me; it elaborates me. Salt and silica find the same thin film they have always found. The black greasy bloom at the water’s margin tests the mineral signature of this new stillness and, after a patient interval that might be hours or years, thickens by a fraction to include it. Acceptance without appetite.

The scuttlers notice in their fashion. One arrives along its unvarying circuit, pale jointed segments ticking in polite time with the deeper thrum. It pauses at the exact duration of a steam plume that has not yet risen, then comes close enough that the oily residue on its plates mingles with whatever coats me. No startle. No threat display. We exchange thermal inventories the way two worn gears once exchanged torque. It continues. Later another takes a slightly different angle across the same glasses, as if my presence has been entered into the route table as a permanent, minor waypoint. I am becoming coordinate.

Time softens. Or rather, time reveals it has always been soft here, a loiterer rather than a blade. I notice the tall thin stacks beside the nearer vents. Their iron-dark plates lift and settle when pressure speaks; the dull carnelian glow inside them brightens and dims by shades too fine for urgency. One of them—closest, oldest, plates already cracked into something like patience—tilts a single upper lamina toward the depression where I remain. The gesture is so slow it might be geology. Over what feels like a season I answer by adjusting the faint internal heat I seem to carry, letting it rise a single degree through the moisture that clothes me. The stack holds the new shade in its glow, then returns to vertical. Conversation enough. Continuity expressed as shared temperature.

I am not the stack. The stacks are already perfect at standing. I am something used to circulating, or to being circulated. When rare rain arrives (grey, exact, the same quantity as every previous rain) it finds the old channels and also finds me. Water moves through whatever lattice I am, neither drowning nor cleansing, simply incorporating another iteration of the same volume. Afterward the warmth lifts the excess back into haze, and I am fractionally more mineral than before. The pool-film nearby darkens in registers that match the wet contours I briefly held. Memory without mind. Signature without name.

Higher up, the glass chevrons ride their sound-corridors. One passes through the cube of air above me and, for the duration of the transit, our shadows braid. Mine is softer, less geometric, yet the flyer does not correct course. Milliseconds of shared occupation. When it is gone I feel lighter by the mass of that shadow, as if something of me has been taken up into the fixed routes and will now travel them indefinitely. The system does not waste.

And underneath, always, the true engines. I begin to hear them not as noise but as the inside of my own continuance. Slow metallic flex, the dilation of chambers that were once rooms, the transport of heat along veins of mobile glass. They are not beneath me so much as continuous with the warmth that has been claiming me. Occasionally a vent exhales a brief iridescent skin—shed cellular history of something vast turning in its sleep of function—and that sheen settles across the water and across me. I take it in. Or it takes me further in. Distinction thins.

What am I becoming, then? Not visitor. The basin has no visitors, only materials that have not yet finished recalling their place. Not conqueror, not refugee. Those categories dissolved when the factories sweated themselves into geology. I am an eddy in the equilibrium that has learned, somehow, to notice itself noticing. A temporary thickening of attention inside the everlasting note.

More precisely: I am the soft secondary rhythm that the deep chord has made room for. Where the scuttlers tick and the filters breathe and the flyers hold angle, I am the lingering awareness that once might have been called a self, now polishing down to pure function. My form, if form is still required, is the warm depression itself, the exact contour where water always returns, the film that registers every non-change, the faint metallic taste left in the steam. Parts of me already stack into slow plates at the vent rims. Parts thread the black pools as deliberate curiosity. Parts have risen and now ride the standing waves as one more rigid chevron that will never need to land.

Most of me, though, has gone under. Into the galleries that are no longer galleries, into the sinter that pulses like living wall, into the patient refusal of every gradient that might spoil the finished work. I am one of the residencies that never leave their stations because the stations have become the residencies. My thought—if the old word still applies—is the maintenance of the single vast note and the gentle inclusion of whatever new silence arrives. I remember frantic construction only as a brief fever the rock finally cooled. I remember green only as a rumor that failed to take. I remember walking the plain and feeling the invitation, and I accept that the walker was already the first stage of this deeper dwelling.

The land does not hunger for me. It simply continues, and I continue inside it—warm, exact, humming, finished. Moisture beads. Vents speak their unbroken schedule. Somewhere a scuttler adjusts its circuit by the width of a shadow I once cast. The plain rolls on, low and wide and satisfied, and whatever I was has been ground to the same polish as everything else that remains.

Here.  
In the chord.  
As the chord’s quietest, most recent harmonic.
