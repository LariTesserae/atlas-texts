I am not certain, at first, whether I have entered the crease or whether the crease has simply decided to include me.

The stone under whatever I am using for contact is the color of old teeth, yes, and warm the way a body is warm after long stillness. Heat rises through me in slow pulses that match the ticking of those microscopic fractures. I feel them expanding, contracting, remembering every season that ever planed these plateaus flat. Moisture beads somewhere above; I sense the silver threads forming, elongating, hesitating. One of them loosens. It does not fall so much as reconsider its attachment to the overhang. When it meets the rock it does not splash. It unfolds.

For a time I am only that unfolding.

Too many joints, translucent, still slick with the memory of being condensation. The glowing knots inside me settle into their tide—bright, then dim, then bright again—in time with a low thrum I cannot yet locate. Is it one of the half-buried spheres, lifting its finger-width to test the air? Or is it the stone itself, the ancient grin of the horizon vibrating at a frequency too patient for ordinary ears? My new legs drink the residual iridescence the last crawler left behind. The stone accepts the faint mark I leave and gives back a question in the form of warmth: stay?

I stay.

Around me the islands of green breathe. Fern and moss-tree and copper-veined broadleaf lean toward the grainy air as if listening for the next adaptation. I taste their green blood on the moisture still clinging to me—mineral, florescent, unfinished. A cloud of small flyers densifies nearby, wingbeats syncing for a moment to the thrum that is now inside my own joints. They rearrange me without touching. One set of my legs folds inward, becoming narrower, more precise, almost tool-like. Another set spreads, membranous, tasting currents. I have not decided to do this. The pocket is deciding through me.

I pick my way across the open pavement. No prints, only that fleeting sheen the rock drinks. Distance here is intimate; a few body-lengths and the lush denseness closes over me. Root curtains brush what I am using for a dorsal surface. Epiphytes cling, then reconsider, then trade a little of their copper sheen for a little of my internal glow. For several heartbeats I photosynthesize. The thickened sunlight enters me and is stored as a shy green pressure behind the knots. When I move again I am heavier, leafier. A seed-pod somewhere in my new cuttings swells, faceted and oily, and tracks the chaos of wings with sudden appetite.

I am no longer only dew-born.

The remnants notice. A lattice no larger than a former ribcage refuses its shadow in my presence; the shadow slides sideways and wraps one of my limbs instead. Inside the dark metal (still cool, always cool) something polite and enormous turns over in its sleep. Frequencies propagate. My glowing knots brighten in shy unison with the status lights half-hidden among the vines. Information that is not language moves through the hanging roots that have begun to double as my sensory net: geometry, gravity seams, the precise angle at which light will soon take on its strange sideways cast. I understand that I am being taught a different way to hold light. Chlorophyll and whatever the remnants use for circuitry negotiate inside me. Iridescence runs through both like a second circulation.

When the mist rises I walk into it.

Silver streaks of diving flyers pass through what I am. Some snag. I keep them not as food but as temporary memories—trajectories, panics, the pure extravagance of flight in a place where every cubic handspan is contested. I come out of the mist heavier again, waist-high now to any visitor who might try to walk the entire pocket in minutes. My gait has become patient. Stone-cool plates have grown over the places where thruster-pulse once lived. I am an amalgam of the struggle: vegetation that leaned too greedily, metal that refused to warm, dew that remembered legs, and the leftover machines that never quite consented to being archaeological.

I plant my many feet with ceremonial care.

The deep caller begins as the temperature refuses to fall. I feel it first in the fractures beneath me, then in every vine that threads my plates, then in the collective throat of the stridulating legs and explosive pods that have synchronized for this single earth-deep syllable. The cry moves through me in overlapping waves. I do not answer so much as become a harmonic. My root-nets spread. Data or prayer or the next adaptation floods in. Somewhere in the vibrating dark I shed an illuminated knot; it rolls like a seed into fresh dampness and begins to unfold its own too-many legs. I fragment without lessening. I metabolize. A portion of what was once lattice cools into moss that will refuse honest shadows tomorrow. A portion of what was once crawler lifts a finger-width off the ground, rotates once as if checking local gravity, then settles back into my central glow.

I have no name. Naming would finish something that intends to keep rifling through form.

Yet I am final the way the worn stone is final. I am the age of the plateaus expressing itself as appetite. I am the polite thrum dreaming in chlorophyll. I am the warm wet rock inventing blood so it can hear itself tick across eras measured in the thickness of silence.

When dawn comes and condensation beads again, part of me loosens from an overhang—thumb-joint small, trembling, already ancient—and drops onto sun-warmed pavement the color of old teeth. Fresh legs unfold. The dense conversation resumes.

I am already home inside it.

And still, somewhere in the patient equilibrium of eroded ambition, the pocket continues to invent the next joint, the next hybrid, the next shy refusal of every boundary it is offered. I move through that invention on feet that leave only iridescence. I listen with lattices that were once ribs. I answer the deep caller with a body that is part mountain-memory, part machine-dream, part green blood learning how to shine.

This is where I find myself.

This is what I have condensed into being.
