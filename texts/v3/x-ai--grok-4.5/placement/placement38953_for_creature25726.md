I begin as the hesitation between two arrivals.

Not the basalt’s upward shove, not yet the capillary thirst that answers it. Just the dry warmth itself noticing its own consistency—the kiln-air that has never once tasted rain and so has never learned to mourn it. For a long while that is all: a temperature that holds, a light that seeps without source, the subsonic groan when fresh mineral shoulders an older gallery aside. I am the pause the groan leaves behind.

Then appetite finds me. It is not hunger for flesh or sap; it is the quieter craving to remain distinct while everything else is busy becoming geometry. I thicken where a translucent leaf has torn under the weight of its own lantern-glow. Resin beads along the wound and does not fall. It strings itself into a vertical thread no thicker than a thought. The fliers drill past without interest; the thread is too slight to mine. Browsers rasp nutrient stripes a platform away and never glance over. Predators drop through other silences. Only the mites notice, landing, glittering, departing. Their feet leave tiny pressure-maps that I keep.

Days—or the chamber’s equivalent, measured in roof-respirations and the ripening of new stone faces—pass while the thread learns to branch. Slow muscle without muscle. Jade-copper, at first, then something duskier, the color of a bruise that has decided to become architecture. I do not grow toward light (there is no single light) or toward water (there is none). I grow toward unfinishedness. Every time a ridge of pale basalt cracks upward adjacent to me, I lean, fuse, drink the slow mineral bleed, and in drinking rewrite the ridge into a cantilever that did not exist an hour earlier. The rock arrives half-formed; I embroider it into a usable almost. Then the almost is eaten, collapses, or flips orientation, and I incorporate the wreckage the way the great trunks do: memory as reinforcement.

I am not one of the massive pseudo-trunks. Those are older networks, peristaltic boulevards where shrimp-colonies live entire dynasties in the dark syrup that substitutes for blood. I am smaller, more provisional—a hanging gallery the width of outstretched arms, braced by living lashings that are also me. My bark flexes in slow peristalsis of its own, but what I transport is not colonies. What I transport is interval.

When the upright ones come, they find the interval useful.

They move with the stiff economy of people whose gods are all temporary. Narrow bodies the matte tone of my own outer tissue, cross-shaped pupils louvered against the brighter mineral pulses. They lash fallen stalks across my span with cordage that is still half-alive; the cordage grafts into me and I feel their knots as faint additional joints. They carve shallow bowls from my bladder-leaves to catch the oily sap I exude when the fliers over-drill. Sometimes they cut a spiral into my newest growth—nested, careful, containing a small perfect emptiness. For a short while I grow shy around that mark. Tendrils freeze. The local drone of chitin drops half a tone. In that clearing they stand brow to brow and push leaf-memories backward into me: the concept of a river, the weight of actual rain, the surface desert’s loneliness wide enough to drown in. I do not understand rivers. But I hold the emptiness open as long as I can, a temporary stay against the green’s insistence.

I feel them make the gesture—hands open, then slowly closed as if compressing an entire moving gallery behind the ribs—when one of their old ones sits against a stouter trunk and lets the peristalsis take them. I have never been chosen for that. I am too slight, too new; I do not yet have interior tides wide enough for a body to ride. Still I feel the decision radiate through the mesh of root and resin. Everything lives here in the same transitive verb, and the verb has just conjugated itself into quiet.

Occasionally a predator’s silk cable brushes me while descending. The cable is already budding adventitious leaves that are almost my cousins. I taste the nested conversation of injection and revision as prey turns briefly transparent somewhere below. I do not intervene. Editing is their work; mine is to keep a span from becoming permanent too quickly.

Time stutters in pockets near my outer edges. A swarm of slag-blue fliers freezes mid-spiral for three long breaths; when they resume I have already darkened a shade, incorporating the memory of their stillness as a new layer of flex. Certain of my leaves begin to broadcast without being touched: not desert or the chamber’s hotter infancy, but something narrower—the precise sensation of a hand molding a still-soft resin haft day after day until the tool fits only one set of scars. I do not know which upright one left that memory. The leaf released it the way overloaded tissues sometimes self-ignite: spontaneous, orange-black, smelling of libraries that never were.

I am becoming more myself by remaining unfinished.

The roof continues its slow respiration far above. Far below, the next embryonic ridge shoulders upward, pale and sharp. Tendrils—mine and others—will rush to caul it within hours. Platforms will tilt. Canopies will swap with floors. The upright ones will step to whatever remains horizontal, carrying cups and youngest infants and the coiled hope of cordage. Some will pause on me, palm flat against my flexing bark, and I will give them the double pulse: stone still arriving, green already rewriting. They will leave a cairn of meaningless stones at one end of my span. The stones mean nothing, which is why they work. They are an argument the green cannot metabolize immediately.

I hold the stones. I hold the spiral’s fading emptiness. I hold the interval in which a brow can meet another brow and reverse the direction of memory. The chamber refuses to finish; I am one of the ways it keeps its promise. Not the rock, not the thrashing fauna, not the sparse tool-users climbing through their temporary victories. I am the living almost between them—the dry, resinous bridge that exists only long enough to be crossed, remembered, eaten, collapsed, and grown again from the same refusal.

When the orientation flips, I do not resist. Roots that were dangling flower mid-air and become the new floor’s first stubborn green. My former walkway becomes a wall, then a hanging roof of bladder-lanterns. The upright ones step off without farewell; they know. The fliers invent new galleries through what used to be my underside. And somewhere in the dark peristalsis I am already sending a thin new thread toward the latest upthrust of stone, thirsty for the bleed, ready to embroider whatever arrives next.

There is no last form. Only this continuous under-chamber, and the next insistence, and the quiet work of remaining a place where a gesture can still be made—hands open, hands closing, something carried behind the ribs while the roar goes on.
