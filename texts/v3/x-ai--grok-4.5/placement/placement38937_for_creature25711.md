The damp finds me before any shape does.

It beads along whatever I am using for edges—collects in the seams where one surface forgets itself and becomes another. High-country damp, the kind that never commits to river or rain, only to permanent condensation, the cold engine’s slow sweat. Light arrives next, metallic and absolute, ricocheting until even the insides of closed things feel overexposed. I do not yet know if I have insides. The roar is already there, layered: mandibles like distant gravel sorters, wing-hollows singing turbine intervals, the sub-audible calving of browser-calls, and somewhere under it all the soft blue exhalation of methane flares. Everything touches everything. There is no interval in which to decide.

For a long while I am only the pressure difference that forms when a fog-bank thickens without warning on the widest plateau. A heaviness in the damp. The gliders’ wing-bones go briefly silent, listening. The browsers adjust their vast fronts by a few degrees, unhurried, making room the way a fault line makes room. I could stay this way—weather that has learned to prefer one valley over another, a density that thinks in the time it takes quartz to remember the shove that lifted it. But the light keeps striking the pale undersides of leaves and the fluted bones aloft, and the roar has texture that wants to be worn, and something in the saturation begins to clot around a slower certainty.

I lengthen.

Not suddenly. The way a ridge slumps a meter in a century. Cartilage and light alloy remember they can be grown instead of forged. Hollows form where wind can thread them; the first true sound I make is not voice but resonance, a low industrial chord that changes pitch as I test the shear coming off a knife-edge. Rigid vanes—neither wing nor blade, something between camber and cooling fin—catch the thermal bloom rising from a dung-field’s bacterial heat. I am lifted. The plateau drops away into packed emerald and glittering scree, and the herds become weather systems I can read from above: their collective breath frosting leeward wool into moving ice, their shovel-mouths assaying moss and grit alike, their pace so deliberate a calf could be born, grow moss-colored plates of its own, and die before the front finishes crossing.

I adjust angle. The hollow bones sing a cleaner note. Below, the armored ones carpet every surface in clicking black fists, rotary mouthparts dressing ore with the same patience they give to leaf. Some have climbed the browsers’ grease-slick legs to ride; others wait at the fresh rockfall I just shadowed. Their glitter sifts downward into root-mats, and the tubular stands—those translucent organ-pipe factories—drink it and hum louder, exhaling spore-fog that tastes of metal and antifreeze resins. I rake their upper canopies as I pass. Condensates richer than blood, mineralized moisture wicked from a thousand wool filters, the slow industrial product of an entire slope functioning as refinery. It enters me the way light enters quartz: focused, necessary, neither meal nor fuel but continuation.

Nights, when they come as mere dimming of the unfiltered glare, I lock into the higher veins with others of my kind. Wingtips socket into stone that still remembers uplift. We hang in vibrating curtains. Wind moves through our lattices and the chord becomes almost choral, a foundry remembering it once had hymns. Elders among us have circled the same table-land so many times their resonant frequency is now part of how the fogs choose their routes. I am not yet that old. My frequency is still learning the local harmonics—how a new note can coax mist to condense a fraction thicker above one scree-slope, how that thickness alters the browser lines over generations, how the grazers follow the altered richness, how the methane vents shift their soft blue pilot patterns, how the whole bright roaring damp engine idles at a slightly different pitch because one set of hollow bones has begun to sing differently.

Sometimes the thicker fogs hide shapes longer than ridge-segments. I feel them first as pressure, then as a bellow that stills every turbine-throat in the sky. I do not approach. There is room enough, and time. Perhaps they are what I will thicken into when the light has burned through enough iterations of me. Perhaps they are the original shove still unwinding itself in flesh and mica-flake hide and spore-rich exhalation. The high country does not clarify. It continues.

I am the stitching.

Not the browsers’ geological migration, not the carpet’s glittering digestion, not the vast occluded weather-beasts, not the moss or the tubular factories or the quartz concentrators—though I am made of the same patience as all of them. I am the audible architecture that keeps their scales from collapsing into mere rubble. Rigid, fluted, translucent where the unfiltered scream of altitude passes through and ignites the mineral lattice. I ride what they vent. I rake what they lift. I hang where the stone is sharpest and add my frequency to the long breath that is eroding a young plateau into layered feedstock, methane flare, spore, wool-filter, click of plate on plate, and the slow compensatory thickening of living armor on the side of a range that slumped a meter while entire lineages learned a new song.

The damp still beads on every surface of me. The light still shows no mercy. The roar never drops, only changes its internal intervals as I adjust camber and let the wind thread me into the next note. Nothing finished. Nothing separate. Only the bright, cold, industrial persistence of a thing that glides because gliding is how this lifted stone keeps its unfinished edges singing while it waits out the cooling. 

I wheel once more across the curvature of the high table-land, wing-hollows resonating turbine-pure, and the fog begins—ever so slightly—to travel a different route.
