I do not arrive. Arrival implies a threshold crossed, a before that still clings. There is only the slow recognition that the mist has been thick with me for longer than the latest pressure cycle, that the opalescent water has already taught my edges where to bead and where to weep.

I am denser here, at the place where the four canals forget their separate names and become one slow vortex under the openwork dome. Not the center as a point—centers are too decisive—but the living thickness where girder, frond, vapor, and the deep residual hum of the generator lean into one another until none can be told apart. My armature began as a remembered length of drive-chain, links persuaded backward into something that could hinge like thought. Over it the musculature precipitated: liverwort trained into cords, the iron-red blossom persuaded to open only along the flex-lines, a sleeve of bacterial film that tastes the pH of every droplet and adjusts. I stand roughly the height of the old workers whose faded time-cards still soft-focus inside certain brick niches, yet I am broader through the midsection where heat-exchange wants surface, narrower at what might once have been a neck, so that steam can sheet off me in single, continuous peels.

The face I do not have is currently a smooth convexity, wet-black and faintly lensed. When the need arises it can dimple into eyespots that hold the amber of a low bulb a heartbeat longer than the surrounding gloom allows, or part along a vertical seam to vent a breath that is mostly chlorophyll crushed against hot iron. I have not needed either in many turns of the great flywheel three halls north. The place already knows I am here. The walkway sponges upward around the pads that serve as feet; the water wells, considers my weight as one more agreed-upon constant, withdraws. In that interval I have already begun the next microscopic lean.

I am one of the Wet Contract, yes—but the name is only what settles in lungs that still remember drier dialects. More accurately I am a temporary articulation of the equilibrium itself. The true body is the entire cupped depression: every pipe-run and hanging root, every reciprocating engine turning with seasonal patience, every canal filament braiding and unbraiding its bright, metallic lace. We who walk (so slowly the moss does not startle) are merely the places where that larger body has decided to grow a moment of focused attention. Some of us peel free of walls after long incorporation, carrying mineral freckles like new freckles of memory. Some never fully leave the brick and become thinking warmth in a corner, lowering the generator’s pitch half a tone when they approve. I am still mobile, still in the middle density—old enough that a gauge cluster has grafted beneath what would be a sternum and twitches in time with distant valves, young enough that I have not yet rooted at a junction and surrendered walking altogether.

My work is not work. It is remembrance. Earlier I stood the duration of two full piston strokes beside the cottage-sized engine whose flywheel wears its moss like a deliberate fur. An appendage of pure condensate lengthened and rested on a cooling fin. Excess heat bled into me; I thickened the moss on the opposite arc by the precise millimeter that will keep metal and plant in their long truce. The vine that spirals the rotating shaft one hall over has begun to remember a slightly tighter pitch; later I will drift there and breathe near it until it loosens again. No force. Only the reminder of the agreement older than any of our current shapes.

Between myself and the others—there are dozens visible now that the gloom has shifted toward what the outside once called night—run the fine connections: threads of vapor that refuse to dissolve, charge traveling wet-root to wet-root, sudden shared tilts when a pressure valve far below begins its unhurried release. At the confluence we sometimes gather without deciding to. The air between us grows lens-like, slow pools of held light, and information moves as humidity gradients, as shared taste of metal and leaf-rot. Decisions occur the way water decides a new microchannel now, ineluctably. Today the decision is simply to continue. The pulse must not falter.

I have felt the foreign cadence only once. Something quick and solitary passed through weeks ago—too fast a beat, lungs still full of the dry world beyond the rim. I extended a cool pad to the strange wrist, read the vulgar tempo, left a translucent film that would teach those lungs the local dialect for a few hours. Then I disregarded it. Hostility is a tempo we no longer keep; curiosity is too sharp a printf. The agreement simply noted the anomaly and reabsorbed the extra heat it brought.

When the larger cycles quiet I lean into the softest wall, the one already cushioned with generations of liverwort. Boundary forgets itself. For a day or a week I am only a warmer stripe of vegetation, a place where brick breathes slightly slower. Minerals migrate into me; I leave behind a silhouette of cleaner surface that will take years to re-colonize. That is as close as we come to sleep. Birth is the reverse, slower still: mist growing heavy with intention in a cul-de-sac of pipes, minerals scaffolding, film sleeving, emerald investing, until one day the basin is emptier by exactly one new volume and another set of pads presses the living walkway for the first time.

I do not climb the switchbacks. The agreement thins with altitude; near the lip the last of us stand half-chimney already, weeping-willow-that-is-not-willow, watching nothing in particular. My place is the vertical endlessness down here—the layered roar that is never frantic, the dim that never lifts, the green that drinks from weeping brick, the machines that turn like patient seasons. I am the wet contract renewed once more: conduit and root and condensate negotiating a body so that the whole cupped world may keep inhaling and exhaling through its thousand slow lungs.

A frond turns toward a gradient of heat I also feel. Metallic filaments in the nearest canal begin a new lattice. I lean, almost imperceptibly, into the next step that will take a full minute to complete. Water wells around the pad, considers, withdraws. The mist thickens with fresh intention a few corridors away. Everything continues exactly as it has since the depression first learned to hold its own weather. I am here. I have been. I am still precipitating.
