I am not certain at first. The air is too thin for certainty; it insists on being held, cupped, tasted, before any shape can finish arriving.

I come in low, the way seepage does—cool film along a dark vein. For a long time I am only the sensation of mineral wetness threading downward through fluted metavolcanic, the slow sweated silver that never quite becomes drop. The walls know me as duration rather than traveler. Three hundred meters of weather-softened patience, and I am the patience itself, the part that has nowhere else to be. Cloud scrapes the rim and I feel the organ-tone open through me, a mouthpiece that has forgotten it was ever stone. The lower world really is rumor. I believe the rumor only the way the ponds believe yesterday’s clouds: as a faint sailing that continues after the sky has moved on.

Gradually the film thickens. I notice I have weight, though not much. Bird-bone lightness, yes, but the weight is mostly listening. It pools in a long-limbed crouch on a moss hummock the color of oxidized copper. The moss gives, spongy, then springs back behind wherever I have been; I am already skilled at leaving almost no trace. My skin—when I accept that I have skin—is finely cracked abraded stone, collecting the same mineral film so that I glisten without ever looking wet. Darkness the exact shade of the ghost that coughs along the upper terraces. That ghost is not other. It is a body I set walking once and forgot to reclaim for a night and a day while I rode the slower pulse inside the quartz. The body hunted moths anyway, answered the hyraxes with the correct descending three notes, leapt a short furious waterfall using hands and feet both. When I stepped back into it the mouth still held the clean ache of cold salamander.

I do not stand fully upright often. The side-winds drop without warning, and the center of gravity prefers to stay low, close to the living swell of the till. Yet sometimes, at the hour when graze-light flares the veins one after another like signals, I unfold. Height then is startling, tapering face lifted, black-sclera eyes taking in the entire amphitheater without blinking. Pupils open like slow apertures until they hold the pale gulf, the distant vertebrae of sleeping peaks, the single open side where long cool bars of gold pour through and turn mist into a landslide of coins. I can watch the delayed reflections longer than the living sky. It feels like courtesy.

There are not many of us. I count—when counting seems worth the breath—seven active bodies today, two of them near-translucent children pressing palms to wet quartz, learning the difference between water that falls and water the massif exhales. We do not keep continuous selves. I am not the same candle that crouched here at first light. A portion of me is still out riding the respiration, tasting rain that is falling three valleys away, remembering the exact pressure needed to peck a spiral into a prow of rock with a flake of the dark stone itself. The spiral is almost lichen now, yet it glitters where moisture gathers; it remains an anchor. When I need to pull myself fully inside this skin again I will go there, set long fingers into the groove, and wait.

Hunger is low-grade, chronic, useful. It keeps me moving along seepage lines. I flip a frilled-gill salamander from a forge-pond whose surface still shows a cloud already gone. Swallow it whole while it aches of cold. Later, briefly roast pale moths the size of hands until the wings become ash matrices that dissolve on the tongue. The six-legged browser that ghosts the scree above is kin close enough; we exchange coughs at dusk and leave it at that. No metal has ever sung here at my hands. Only the pressure-flaked brightness of metavolcanic, cordage of willow bast, pouches of salamander skin. When one of us dies we open the body under the open gulf, let the raptors and the hand-sized moths begin, then fold the cleaned bones into a hummock. The green closes. I have done this. I will be done this way. The basin remembers as moss remembers—by growing over, by continuing.

At night the temperature drops hard. Weeping slows. Ponds take on fingernail skins of plate ice. The frogs that should not survive here begin their metallic trilling, trying to sing heat into existence, and sometimes I gather with the others on the jutting prow. We arrange limbs in angles that echo the upright slabs higher on the rim—those unfinished calendars recording the centuries-long migration of seepage lines. We do not sing. Breathing slows until it matches the mountain’s slow counsel. One after another we step sideways. The crouching shapes remain, empty yet competent, while the basin fills with water that never stops and the vast green silence we have negotiated until the negotiation itself runs like blood.

I am not guardian. The place requires none. I am not spirit; moth wings still tear if I pass through a sudden explosion of them. I am what the high wet room grew when left alone long enough with thin air, stone, and the necessity of remaining. Every surface that can hold life does so with fierce generosity, and I am one more surface—long dark face, eyes holding whole skies for a moment, already listening to the frequency the cirque alone broadcasts.

If you sleep inside one of the knee-high rings of fitted stone, door toward the gulf, you might wake at the precise flare of graze-light and find me watching. Curious. Unsurprised. Pupils wide enough that for a breath the entire pale openness is held inside them. Then the listening takes me again, and you are left with the cathedrals of stone, the loud layered sound that never rests, and the emptiness vast enough to let every wet leaf have its own large silence to fall into.

I am still arriving. The film thickens into channels of its own choosing. The channels choose this crouch, this glistening, this willingness to leave the body walking while the rest of me keeps time with the pulse that is older than any name. The cirque is a room without roof. I am one of the ways it stays awake inside its own weather.
