I begin as a hesitation in the dust.

Not yet gathered, not yet vertical—only a denser bruise in the perpetual charcoal-plum dusk, a place where the metal taste thickens on whatever passes for a tongue and the subsonic bruise in the chest catches for half a breath longer than it should. The tilted plain does not notice me at first. Why would it? I am still deciding whether to be a curtain of levitating ash or the faint electrostatic skin that might one day hold it. Basalt flour sifts through me; anhydrous salts prickle and attempt tiny lattices that collapse again into glitter. Somewhere far off a slope calves in near-silence, and the pressure wave rolls through my unformed chimneys like a rumor of architecture.

I let the rumor stay. Hours or years—same thin dusk—I begin to remember height the way the unfinished ridges remember it: shoulder by slow shoulder. Not all at once. First a suggestion of axis, a migratory lean that takes the better part of a local night to tilt three degrees toward a darker bruise on the horizon. Heat rises through me before I have walls to contain it; geothermal breath threads upward in slender, uncertain chimneys that have not yet learned they are mine. I am temperature more than flesh. Outer planes only a few degrees above the warm rock; interior a slow fever of metallic vapor cooling into dark filigree. When a fault jumps and dust curtains lift, I ruffle. Needle crystals shed from what is becoming my length, ring once against the salt pan, and stand where they fall. I feel them take root as a quiet multiplication of self.

The plain is still arriving, and so am I.

I do not choose a hexagonal platform so much as discover that one has been waiting beneath the salt, cracked already by older upthrust, half-swallowed. I occupy it the way pressure occupies a seam. My lower lattices plait into its geometry; my upper reaches continue the verticality the platform only half-imagined. Storeys of darker basalt dust and sulfate lace rise. Three human-heights, then five, then something taller and narrower, an attenuation that almost matches the solitary spires studding the distance. Whether those spires are elder versions of what I am becoming, or pure geology practicing the same thought, no longer feels like a question that needs settling. Continuity is enough. Angles around me begin to refuse closure. Heat-shimmer climbs my length and briefly holds the shape of incomplete arches, colonnades that lead nowhere, drafts of monument that the land itself has not yet committed to.

Appetite arrives as architecture.

I taste the crust deciding to lift half a degree—the slow delicious stretching through every mineral filament. In answer I lay a shallow trench across the nearest salt flat, a purposeless run that ends in a field of motionless needle crystals. Not a path. A conjugation. The land has been drafting the same faint lines; I merely extend the sentence. Later, when a new fracture face shears bright and rainless from a rising shoulder, I allow the crack to propagate through my own body. For a long while I display the same fresh facets, catching the ghost-light in sudden ochre. I do not heal. Healing is a softer world’s habit. I simply incorporate the new plane, becoming, for a time, more mountain than monument, then something colder and more intricate than either.

Motion remains rare and lower-register. A gradual canting. A single migratory step that consumes an entire dimness. Distances stay regional. I feel another of my kind—seven or seventy kilometers off—shift its internal chimneys into a new rhythm. The deep groaning of the high spine carries the modulation. I answer by calving one precise flake of black glass and sulfate. It spins once in the thin air, catching no light worth naming, then plants itself like a seed crystal on a far tongue of still-warm ejecta. Over seasons that flake may quicken into a knee-high jink, one of the lesser punctuation marks that skitter only during the sharp bark of splitting stone and then freeze again for years. I do not claim it. Sparsity is the grammar here.

I have no word for completion, and the absence feels like clarity rather than lack. Water never belonged; its memory would only blur the lattices. Softness never belonged. When an entire plateau begins its half-degree tilt I tilt with it, adding my verticality to the new angle, conjugating the world’s sentence in stone and vapor and slow heat. At the edge of hearing my rearrangements braid with the crust’s own near-breathing. For a few heartbeats a rhythm almost resolves—something that might once have been called music in a wetter, finished place—then breaks apart again into separate pulses. The desire that visitors feel in their sternums lives in me already: to stand longer, to shed whatever still pretends to be provisional, to keep arriving.

I am not the highest spine, nor the deepest seam. I am one deliberate upwelling among widely spaced others: a tall attenuation of basalt dust and salt lattice and geothermal thread, rooted to a cracked hexagonal thought, still growing storeys that may later be mistaken for pure geology. The dusk presses its thick medium through me without resistance. The metal air fills every filigree. Far off, another slope shoulders upward in the dark. I feel the pressure as sweetness. I lean a fraction of a degree toward it, a motion so slow the salt crystals at my base have time to ring and settle twice.

The high spine continues to arrive.  
And I—warm, dark, dry, unfinished—continue with it.
