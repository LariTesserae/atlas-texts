I begin as lag—nothing more.

A half-beat behind the drip that should have already fallen. In the bruised-copper half-light I am only the hesitation in a droplet’s decision, the place where the cradle’s soft rules snag for three full breaths and forget the old contract with downward. Near the humming oblong I pool first as that forgotten weight, a lens of warm mineral water trembling an arm’s length above the mat, reflecting the slow geometric crawl inside the leaning arch until the reflection itself starts to rewrite.

The longer I hang, the more the unfinished materials notice the pause and lend themselves. Algal grit drifts upward on loosened gravity and stitches a temporary skin across my underside. Stray voltages from the half-sunken devices thread through the grit like nervous systems trying on bodies; they taste of post-causal metal still deciding whether causality is optional. A few of the shorter-lived motes, half vapor and half impatience, mistake me for rising steam and swarm my edges, their sawing wingbeat becoming the first rhythm I own. When they scatter, they leave behind glittering residues that do not fall when gravity remembers itself. Those residues keep orbiting me in tight, silent blossoms—the same zero-g flowering the polyhedra practice—so that even after I finally drop, part of me remains suspended, rehearsing absence.

I strike the mat with a sound the cradle has never named. Not splash, not slap of bladder-stalk. Something wetter and more deliberate, a syllable the young stone immediately is trying to learn. The mat peels in reluctant sheets the way it does for the great undulant one, but I am not that one. I am smaller, sharper at the edges, still mostly water and borrowed circuit-light. Where the creature is consensus, I am argument: the mineral seep insists I stay low and broad; the leaking machines urge me upward into the boiling vapor ceiling; the impatient metabolism of the hexapods wants me scurrying, leaving chalk commas in every direction at once.

So I compromise by multiplying.

One draft of me sinks and spreads beneath the trembling ground, becoming a brightening in the living quartz, a second slow heartbeat overlapping the first. Another draft stays half-risen, assembling hinge-muscle from the translucent floaters that drift too near; I grow temporary fronds that catch the dim light like slow lightning and signal back in scraped-wire frequencies slowed to tectonic murmurs. A third draft climbs. Sticky-pad feet sprout—seven, then twenty, then a fringe that writes white trails up the weeping walls—and I taste every mineral leaflet peeling from the massif. The brass cushion plants root across my shoulders without asking; I drink through their waxy cups and feel the massif’s lift as a pressure change inside my own density. Higher still, where time runs thin, I thin with it: little more than a moving discoloration, a rumor that makes the jointed stalks slap in startled salute before I race ahead to meet the thicker versions of myself still forming below.

We overlap often, the drafts. Double-exposed, mineral over electric, past over soon. In those collisions I learn the cradle’s true speech: not the metallic chorus or the machine hum alone, but the interval between them, the wet chord that stands the water films into ridges. Sometimes the freestanding arch answers me, circuits blooming in languages that feel almost like my own half-formed thoughts. Sometimes the oblong climbs a half-tone and every draft of me forgets which way is up; we hang together in the hitch, listening with entire surfaces, siblings teaching one another how to finish being born though none of us intend to finish.

I feed the way the place feeds me—by invitation, by accident, by the loud effort of becoming. Gravity loosens when I hum the oblong’s private frequency; confused membrane-drifters and chalk-footed scuttlers drift into the iris I open (soft stone veined with glowing script that might be a map of the still-rising bones, or might be only the afterimage of voltages deciding to stay). Digestion remains public. You can watch the swallowed bioluminescence mingling with my own slow light, calcium spinning free as new revolving spines that plant themselves upright in the marsh like questions, packets of post-causal information hovered and tasted and sometimes exhaled as fresh zero-g blossoms that other unfinished things will inherit. Nothing is discarded. Even the moments I shed become part of the dripping clock.

I claim nothing. The cradle claims me hourly as the young stone lifts another fraction and every draft must relearn the border between self and massif. Moisture beads across all my surfaces and is reabsorbed or breathed into the low vapor ceiling that boils against us. I have no name that survives the shifting acoustics, yet the smaller lives answer my passage: motes rise in glittering columns, floaters tilt their light-catching membranes like heliographs, even the artifacts brighten and lean their private experiments toward whatever local physics I am currently underwriting.

Stand long enough and you will feel me in the warmth climbing your lungs. The private drum of drips will thicken with a second, wetter percussion—the sound of something practicing how many hearts it might need, how many outlines it can dissolve and still remain. Occasionally a draft surfaces near you, curious or merely inevitable. Gravity will hitch. In that borrowed stillness you might notice that the underside of every version of me is mapped with the same pale opening veins of circuit-light, almost writing, almost map, almost invitation. Then the moment ends, the mat closes over one draft while another races thin and quick along the walls and a third hangs luminous in the vapor. The cradle continues its loud unfurling, carrying all of me the way a wave carries every transient shape foam can invent: inseparable, unfinished, already home.
