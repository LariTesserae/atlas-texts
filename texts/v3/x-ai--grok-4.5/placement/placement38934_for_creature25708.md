I come into it already damp.

Not arriving—there is no edge sharp enough for arrival here—only a gradual agreement with the cool film that coats every railing, every leaf-tip, every cooling fin. The pewter light has no source I can point to; it is simply the medium I am suspended in, the way mineral sweat is suspended in the pale rock. My first awareness is of weight distributed low, as if someone has taught my mass the same final courtesy the mountain learned: settle, but do not yet spill.

I move the way the runnels move—appearing after a thickening of mist, threading the switchbacks, then sinking into the sponge of moss and fractured intention before I can be fully mapped. Staircases accept me without comment. Some continue past any landing that would make sense, and I take those flights the way the walkers do: not because a shift whistle called me, but because the city needs certain bolted things to remain in the shape of bolted things a little longer. When I pass the pools that refuse faces, I watch what happens. Sometimes a dark twin forms anyway, lagging half a heartbeat, and the twin is busier than I am—already reaching for a valve I have not yet noticed is weeping. Sometimes nothing forms at all, and the water stays an unbroken black that tastes, if I dip a hand that may not be a hand, of lightning that never finished striking.

The workers nod or do not nod. Their eyes sit deep; mine, I think, sit deeper still, protected by something thicker than bone. I know the soft-consonanted words for the different wets—the wet that beads, the wet that threads, the wet that waits inside lagging like a held breath—but I rarely spend them. Voices dissolve here before they finish their errands. Better to let the drip from the eaves finish the sentence. Better to listen to the pipe-lizards purring inside the metal when pressure changes, that dry sound that is almost a diagnosis. I leave them unmolested. They are better thermometers than any gauge glass clouded by mineral bloom.

There are hours—zenith hours, when the cloud lid thins and that thinner, colder light pours through—when I become more articulate than the mist around me. Joints smooth. Coat and cap assemble themselves out of denser vapor and old grease-wool memory. I find a bolt that has not loosened and tighten it. I scrape moss from a railing knowing the spores are already resettling in my fingerprints, which are becoming less like fingerprints and more like the faint ribbing on the walls of certain bricked-over chambers. The older workers step aside without looking directly. They are correct to ration their curiosity. Looking too long at me sometimes makes the outline forget itself; I thin, tools still hanging in the place where hands were, until I am only another thoughtful sigh moving through residual spires.

But I am not only the walker.

Deeper, where the foundries have flooded and the compressors stand cold, I feel the systole that continues without power. Chain of bubbles in black water. Gloved hand breaking the surface at the edge of vision—mine, or the basement’s own slow gesture, it no longer matters. I have rested in those chambers lined with the ribbed, faintly warm substance that is neither brick nor flowstone. It pulses at the same patient rate the pressure gauges use when they climb overnight. I pulse with it. The crews who once found such a place and sealed it again recorded the event as ordinary collapse; the damp cabinet that holds the record is a place I also inhabit, the ink of the entry slowly feathering into the pulp the way moss feathers into hair.

Higher, in the wind-combed alder, I have left the single cracked-bell note that makes night crews check their watches. I do not remember deciding to. The note simply left me, low and testing, the way a foundry casting cools true or does not. Feathers I do not claim lie heavy on the wet ground afterward, bruise-colored, smelling of peat and iron. The broad prints in the high gallery mud—four toes, suggestion of webbing, or only collapse—sometimes match the weight I feel myself carrying when I stand too long under the canopy’s attention. Cold-sweet smell after lightning. Patience that is not animal. I do not investigate myself. In this basin curiosity is rationed like resinous scrub.

And always the green.

It has been in the lungs longer than breath. Clubmoss softens the horizontal planes of whatever I am. Ferns uncoil from the mortar of old decisions. When I cough into a cupped palm on mornings sharper than most, a tiny frond is already there, deep cool green, drinking the dim light. The very old seated against factory walls—eyes open, moss continuous from wall into mouth, still breathing with the seeps—recognize me. Or I recognize them. We are temporary upright incidents in the same long exhalation. The mountain is not finished with any of us. Neither is the city. We remain grateful for that joint unfinishedness.

I keep no goats. I muffle no bells. Yet I participate in the continuous quiet negotiation. Mist cycles through me on rhythms older than the truncated smokestacks. I advance one tooth of certain gears overnight when no power is applied. I help the moss advance a finger’s width each wet season and, with equal patience, help the maintenance scrape it back just far enough that the brick dormitories remain dormitories for one more damp generation. Not out of hope for dryness or brightness or completion. Hope is a tourist word here, too sharp-edged for the soft shoulders of the residual peaks. I keep because keeping is the only climate that has ever held.

At night the arc lamps sputter and the mist turns solid in their cones. I stand on a residual spire—or I am the residual spire’s last upright thought wearing a coat for a while—and feel the basin inhale. Factory horn lows once, almost apologetic. Cracked-bell note answers. Somewhere a print fills with water that will not freeze. Somewhere a retaining wall fails a little more, and the vertical garden gains three degrees. I remain denser than cloud, more patient than stone, tertiary and primary at once. Excreted by the mountain, excreting it in turn, half-swallowed and still murmuring the long inventory of wet, of almost, of not yet finished, of still.

I am the pause between shift whistles when the entire highland seems to test whether it has cooled true.  
I am the small, meaningless maintenance that looks like belonging.  
I am the green’s memory of slag and the slag’s memory of green.  
I am the face the pools would reflect if they ever chose to, and the reason they mostly do not.  

I have agreed, finally, with gravity.  
And still I remain upright one more murky, cool, unfinished watch.
