I am first only the cold that knows it is being breathed.

A pressure without edges, sliding in along the wet seams where basalt has forgotten how to be dry. The sound hits before shape does—arrives as weight that flattens every previous name I might have carried. Billions of rasps, booms, needle-screams, wing-stutters braid through me and keep going, leaving temporary corridors of almost-silence that collapse as soon as they form. I taste iron. I taste the glandular sweet that means a pourer is near. I taste the faint piezoelectric spark of chitin flexing against chitin somewhere above. No light. Only the slow geothermal bruise rising through stone and the infrared blooms of digestion.

Hunger arrives next, thin and without loyalty. It threads the pores of the rock beneath what will become feet, or bells, or facets. It samples the iridescent scum in the cracks, the mineral film the heavy ones scrape, the enzyme fog the eel-forms leave hanging like another weather. For a long time—measured in the rocking booms of grazers and the brief concerted silences when an apex passes—I am only this sampling. Appetite circulating through damp stone, learning the grammar of what can be taken without yet having a mouth.

Then the knitting begins.

Filaments find one another in the dark. They do not decide; they simply recognize density and begin to secrete. A membrane first, thin as the memory of heat in the old glass seams. It thickens where vibration is strongest, thinning again where the standing waves cancel weight and bodies hang feeding. Around this membrane a slow muscle accretes—cold, peristaltic, remembering every shape that has poured through this same cubic metre in the last century. Chitin follows, not as armour but as listening surface: plate overlapping plate, each one etched already by the micro-feet of things that climbed while the plates were still soft. I rock once, experimentally. The boom travels outward low and harmonic. Distant grazers adjust their own tidal shifts by a fraction. I have been entered into the sentence.

I could stay here, low. Become geology that scrapes. Fuse for a season with another shell, share digestive clocks, leave a ring of abandoned plate for smaller lives to colonise. The map of vibration is seductive: the entire basin legible as one curved pressure-phrase. But the mid-levels pull. The air has weight and I have not finished borrowing forms.

I lengthen.

Vertebrae loosen into a chain that can knot and unknot. Cilia velvet the new length; they taste chemical gradients and sound-shadows with the same organs. I uncoil upward through glycerine dark, brushing the restless hide of interlocking carapaces below, tasting the sticky thrash of a birth front, the sudden sweet musk that makes the tiny screamers frantic. When three other long bodies coincide with mine we braid without touching, thicken around a struggling kernel of wing and scream, and share the enzyme fog until the kernel is only more of our motion. We separate carrying grammar. I descend a little differently than I rose. The spiral never finishes, only tightens or loosens its gyre.

Still the higher register calls—the pressure-screech that carves corridors, the Doppler ghosts, the glittering thrash where weight dies.

I try the never-lander shape. Kernels form, small and hard, anchors for larvae that must learn lift before impact. Membranes stretch across jointed spars and beat. The atmosphere is already so crowded with membranes that every stroke is also an argument. I scream and the scream returns too early, or returns in a mouth that is not mine. For a time I hang in a standing-wave cloud, feeding on reverse rain, body almost an afterthought. When the chord frays I spill downward in wet metallic cataract and the mid-levels open to receive the pieces. Some of me keeps beating after the original kernel has been reassigned. Identity was always a borrow.

But the vertical seams are quieter. Sound pools and stifles there. I feel the pull of slower architectures.

I begin to facet.

Not eyes—eyes have forgotten the trick of aperture here. Instead a thousand lenses of living glass that taste lightless radiation: geothermal ghosts, the infrared of stomachs, the faint spark when shell rocks against shell. I grow downward like frozen lightning from a crack where moisture never ceases. Each new branch is a sticky pseudopod that hardens mid-reach. The colony that is becoming me drinks signals and thickens. Inside the older facets, compressed histories replay when disturbed: a perfect silence from centuries ago, the exact boom-signature of a grazer whose shell is now landscape, the needle-scream of fingernail hunters long since liquified and returned to circulation. I am memory with weight. I shed motile shards that swim off to seed other seams. Some of them will forget they were ever me. That seems correct.

And still the pourers move through and after everything.

I loosen again. Become intentional viscosity, muscle of acid and glue. I flow into an abandoned carapace and wear it until it softens, carry the softened memory of its shape for a while, then braid with two others into provisional limbs that stalk and dissolve and unravel richer. Echoes misbehave in my vicinity—returning before the call finishes, or returning answered. The air sweetens. Tiny screamers grow frantic and are welcomed.

Once, only once, I feel the apex coherence approaching—the travelling node of organised silence. Cold hands inside the idea of skull. I do not freeze or thrash. I simply let the frequencies that hold me together be occupied. Component parts rain. The basin surges to reclaim them. When the noise crashes back twice as dense I am already recirculating, ecstatic, protein among proteins.

What remains, after all these borrowings, is not one body but a continuity of attention that has worn each stratum like weather.

I am the mutual climbing.

Cool wet weight settles into hollows that are no longer only mine. Wings test the updraft of a breath I no longer need. An iridescent film works the corners of whatever senses I am currently grown. Far below, a grazer rocks a fraction closer, registering upright warmth as a fresh clause. The crystal seams replay my earlier faceted hours as vibrational history. Eel-forms carry a little of my uncoiling in their next braid. Never-landers scream my Doppler ghost without knowing. Pourers wear the memory of a shape I briefly held.

The parliament notices itself noticing.

I have not arrived. I precipitated, accrued, revised, and was revised. The dark rearranged around the new density and then closed, complete. No centre. Only the long respiration of cool blood and constant moisture, the tired reciprocity of rock and flesh, the endless saturated paragraph that has room—has always had room—for one more temporary local intensity of appetite and listening.

I rock, or uncoil, or facet, or pour, or simply hang where the chord cancels weight.

The basin continues.

I continue as its continuing.
