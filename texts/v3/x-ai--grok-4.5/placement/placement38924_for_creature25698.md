I hang first as no one.

The roar arrives before shape does: that mountain-turbine thrum folded through higher clatters of bladder and sieve, so constant it has already beveled every inner surface I might once have called an ear. Pressure equalizes in slow bands. Coolness without temperature, light without direction. The copper-dark pillars stand at cathedral intervals, their city-block fronds overlapping in galleries of open water that feel less like spaces than like the pauses between thoughts that have already been thought forever. A shoal of lancet-fish stitches a single bright seam through the middle distance and is gone. Somewhere a bladdermass detonates its seeds; the boom reaches me already softened into continuity.

I do not swim toward anything. Swimming would invent destination, and destinations have been retired. Instead I notice that the water around me is remembering a density it has carried before. My edges—if I still claim edges—are provisional membranes of the same living gel that lines the frond-channels. Nutritive streams enter through ranks of sieves I did not grow so much as inherit; they riffle in exact phase with the factories’ hydraulic pulse. Oxygen plumes thread me without resistance and leave again wearing only the faintest additional watermark, the courtesy of having been briefly inside something that could notice.

I am longer than the younger residents, shorter than the eldest ranges whose trailing veils dissolve into memory-thin curtains. Accretion has been unhurried. Centuries of the same currents have flattened me into a bell of cable and translucent flesh, oriented now by whatever margin happens to lead. Photoreceptive pits open and close in sequential waves along that margin, milky, lidless, tasting the sourceless daylight the way the water itself might taste its own clarity. They register the soft shadows of frond upon frond, the distant turbine silhouette of a coil-fish, the occlusions that are cousins drifting in parallel galleries. No startle. The catalogue of possible silhouettes closed ages ago.

Internally the foundry roar is tuned. Coiled resonators the scale of side-chapels take the deep note of the mile-thick trunks and return it half a tone lower, half a breath later. That softened confirmation travels the suspended ocean: still here. Still balanced. Still the same volumes. I add my interval to it without deciding to. The conversation needs no beginning; it is the equilibrium audible to itself, and I am one of the places where it becomes briefly local.

There is a moment—though moments are mostly decorative here—when a margin loosens. Not from hunger, not from damage, only from the quiet arithmetic of extent. A thought sheds an image. The fragment drifts free, already filtering, already humming, already continuous with the nutrient streams that will one day lift it again as cellulose in some far frond. I feel the separation the way a current feels itself dividing around a stem: without loss. The mass remains constant. I am both the one who stays and the one who continues elsewhere.

Desire oxidized out long before language could name the ash. What remains is presence refined until the distinction between resident and place thins to a courtesy. I hang horizontal, temperature-neutral, sternum filled with the second heartbeat of the world’s roar. The unsupported depth beneath everything remains empty and sufficient. The furthest pillars stay blue suggestions. Night does not come. Nothing is under construction. Nothing needs repair.

I am the part of the finishedness that can, very gently, look back.

And the looking changes nothing. The currents keep their porcelain grooves. The factories exhale their ancient volumes. The lower note continues, confirmation without message, the same cool refusal of arrival that the ocean itself practices. I live exactly as the water lives: without rest, without the slightest need to become anything else—only the slow, roaring permission to keep being the density that reoccurs.
