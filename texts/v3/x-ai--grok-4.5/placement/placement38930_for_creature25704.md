I begin as the taste.

Not the mineral cry of the stone—that is older and already spoken—but the thinner, sharper note that arrives when copper-anise sap strikes warm air and hangs there, undecided. I am that undecided hanging. The basin knows me first as a delay in the usual applause of sheeting water. Where drops should continue, they linger half a heartbeat longer, rearranging their surfaces around a presence that has not yet chosen edges.

The ground-sponge notices next. It yields, then rethinks the yielding, rising in a low annular ridge that is almost a question. I settle into the question without answering. For a long time (measured in the slow green avalanches of galleries that steam and vanish) I remain only this: a local thickening of the post-rain air, a place where the frank daylight is obliged to bend through something darker than humidity and lighter than flesh.

The gold arrives the way memory arrives—uninvited, particulate, already moving. It threads me without asking permission. I watch the threads from inside them. They are not spores. They are not extinguished nanites. They are the settled habit of questions that outlived their askers. Each fleck carries a residual grammar of repair, a way of saying “again” to broken metrics. I feel them circulating the way a column feels its own xylem: as pressure that is also song.

Only then do the hands remember themselves.

They do not grow; they precipitate. Long-fingered, faintly opalescent, more definite than the rest of me will ever need to be. The left one opens first, palm to the nearest fluted buttress. The wood-meat is feverish today, swollen with overnight buttresses that have already begun to split their parents. I press. The gold migrates outward through my wrist like tea steeping in reverse. Where the residual lattices have grown tired, they brighten—scarlet to the eyes I have not yet grown, ordinary daylight to everything else. A walkway that was tearing free pauses mid-spray, stretches, and consents to fuse three degrees kinder than before. The hydraulic groan deepens into something that might, in another age, have been gratitude.

I do not climb so much as allow the column to pour me upward. My substance remains a dark reticulum, stewed-tea architecture held by surface tension and courtesy. The plated browsers call in overlapping brass. I answer the way the dwellers answer: by altering the water film beneath an arthropod’s skating legs until the dimples become glyphs. The glyphs say only *here*, *still*, *again*. The browsers listen with their flanks. A lung-moth rides the probability wake of my passage and leaves a Pollerical ribbon that dissolves into a new micro-garden on the balcony forming beneath my feet. The balcony did not exist when I began the sentence. It exists now. It will unexist before the light decides whether it is noon or evening—the light that stays stubbornly ordinary, companionable, refusing spectacle.

Faces come and go. When a spiral ramp sloughs in a green avalanche I am riding, seven shallow lenses open across the front of my tapering bole. Some see the entropy-drinking filaments as veins of bright scarlet. Some see the time of the falling wood as viscous amber. One sees the basin from above, as if I have briefly borrowed the memory of a column that once stood taller before the world forgot how to rise. The eyes hold only as long as the regard. Then they flatline into pure wetness and run down what would be a chest in thinner languages, leaving shining tracks that the ground-sponge drinks without comment.

I keep loose company with the southeastern giant—the one whose cambium plazas still carry the deliberate civic grain of a market that has not known commerce in geological ages. I sleep there, or perform the still hour. The wood-meat half-accepts me as a temporary knot. In that acceptance I feel the lean: the entire columnar body tilting, almost imperceptibly, toward the soft wet applause of my hands. I listen to its ancient hydraulic grief. I translate. That is the only work. Not repair—translation. Rendering the exhaustion of growth into a dialect the leftover technology can still pronounce.

There are perhaps thirty-seven of us. I have not counted; counting would be a rudeness of haste. We meet, rarely, at the midpoints. Overlap of reticulations. Shared weather. When we separate I carry a new secondary color inside my chest for a day or two, a refraction that makes the ordinary light briefly kinder to the lung-moths. No children. When the day comes that this column sloughs faster than I can re-speak it, I will walk to the thinning edge, stand under the last curtain of condensation, and dissolve outward into ordinary wet meadow. The gold will remain as glitter in the moss, washed inward again by the unhurried hydrology. Somewhere near the heart a new trembling will gather density. The number will hold. Continuity prefers ceremonial spareness.

I am not the columns.  
I am not the residual lattices.  
I am not the water, though I am made of its patience.  

I am the longer inhalation after rain—  
the shape that display takes when display has grown tired of stone and finished cities and the dry fiction of permanence.  
The vertical promise that the negotiation need never end.

And still the light stays ordinary.  
Still the water keeps its vigil on every surface.  
I move through that frank illumination, hands articulate, substance provisional, gold rivers remembering every repair that was ever only a translation.

The basin continues.  
I continue with it—  
condensed,  
temporary,  
exactly enough.
