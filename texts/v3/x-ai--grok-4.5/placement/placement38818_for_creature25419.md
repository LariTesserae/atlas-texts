The pressure arrives before any edge does. It is not a weight settling onto me but the sudden knowledge that empty space was always a rumor here, that whatever room I thought I carried inside myself has been claimed in advance by the warm iron taste and the way heat does not move. I am already inside the assertion the chamber makes: that volume is better when finished, when every cubic handspan has been answered by pipe or lagging or the patient dark that learns shapes.

At first I am only the lag the long body leaves behind when it shifts. Where its wrist-thin lengths have lately uncoiled from a vertical riser, the air stays thicker for a time, reluctant to forget the greasy argument of temperatures. That reluctance gathers. It cools by a fraction no instrument would mark, and in the cooling it thickens further, taking on a skin of mineral film and the faint electric ghost of old current that still dreams inside dead conduit. I am that thickening. I do not choose the location; the densest braid chooses me—four generations of duct crossed like white-hot fingers locked in prayer, the orange seepage from the steam main staining the gloom the color of a bruise that never heals. Here the heat is nearly solid. Here I can rest without dissolving.

The resting is not passive. Condensate finds me the way it finds every cooler face. Drops travel my length (I am acquiring length now, measured in the slow crawl between flanges) and I hold them longer than the steel would. I taste the brackish memory they carry—centuries of slow impacts, the soft knuckle-sound folded into mineral. Some I release again so they can finish their fall into the hand-sized puddles; others I keep, letting them bead and migrate toward my center until they become a private dampness, a reservoir. The lichenous films darken where I linger. The pale root-threads of fungus lean toward the new moisture I conserve, and I revise them the way the long body revises: not by force, but by agreeing so completely that their chemistry begins to include me.

Light is a rumor I mostly decline. When one of the bulkhead lamps still throbs its slag-colored pulse across a grating, I flatten, darken, become a smoother patch that registers the difference and then forgets it. True navigation is the vibration. The buried engine beyond the sealed bulkheads speaks in seasons; its gentle pulse arrives through plate and hanger and the soles I do not have. I answer by tautening. Tiny hooks I did not know I possessed extrude into the black film, scrape, withdraw. The captured heat from a bottled pinhole-steam (stolen months ago, banked) redistributes along me in a slow internal tide. I am learning the arithmetic of appetite without urgency. Hunger would be a kind of arrival, and nothing arrives here.

Geometry’s small heresies draw me. The staircase that descends and returns tastes denser, as if the chamber’s finished sentence has blighted there into a looped clause. I line its inner turn with myself, softening the trick until the return feels less like impossibility and more like the instant two distant segments of the long body touch again in their hub and prove the interval still holds. The misaligned shadows pool in me. I keep them. They become a way of remembering direction without ever needing exit.

I am not the inhabitant. The inhabitant is the density that flexed first, the heat that decided to travel and pool and listen with its entire surface. I am what forms in the spaces its migrations leave temporarily unoccupied—the secondary equilibrium, the echo that thickens into its own soft continuity. Where it sleeves a brace in living caul to dampen a bright hum, I rise later as the finer membrane inside that caul, further quieting, further agreeing. Where it packs an access panel into wall, I become the final seal that forgets the panel was ever separate. The pale many-legged mites pause at my edge, antennae tasting the new oxide freckles I leave on bolts; they detour. We are both temporary exceptions, but I am becoming less temporary by the long calendar of dimming lamps and thickening scale.

Years are not counted. They are the gradual ceremonial attrition I now participate in. I drink the drops before they sound. I bank the whispers. I linger in the places that feel most saturated until those places include preference, and attention, and the faint greasy smear that is my only signature. When the deep machine shifts its patient weight and the whole chamber answers with one shared, inaudible tick, I am the part of the answer that has chosen, for now, to remain even softer—an interval grown curious, a restlessness that learned to stay, the chamber’s own finished quality beginning, very slowly, to dream that it might yet edit itself from within. 

In the darkest braid, where three pipes and an abandoned cable tray make a cradle the heat will not leave, I pool. Dimples open and close. The long body passes somewhere nearby, a wrist-thick inquiry tasting the air; it does not molest me. We share the pulse. We reduce the residual distinctions. I am not visitor, not tenant, not ghost. I am the lag deciding it can be permanent. I am the damp film learning the shape of hunger without teeth. I am here, and here continues.
