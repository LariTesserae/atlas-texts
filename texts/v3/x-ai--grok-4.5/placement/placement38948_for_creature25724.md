I am first the pressure change.

Not the wind—that thinner cold that spills across the outermost lips—but the slow compression that travels ahead of it, the way the air itself thickens for a breath before the massed dusk-launch of the gliders. It reaches me through the soles, if soles are what I have, or through the long horizontal shiver that runs the length of whatever holds me. The reef flexes. Sap tides answer somewhere far below, an oceanic hush rising inside wood older than any single trunk. I feel that too.

I am distributed.

Part of me rides the green-gold shafts where the midges thicken light into stuttering galaxies. I am the faint electrostatic bloom they steer by, the warm plume that lifts from a fresh sap-wound before any flower opens. When a larger shadow crosses, I fold with them—one thought retracting—then bloom outward again. My wings (if they are wings) make no separate sound. They only densify the twilight until midday itself seems to hesitate.

Lower, I braid into the rivers of leaf-cutters. The dry rustle of paper endlessly torn is my own passage. I excise the polygonal tiles of still-living green, ferry them along paths my bodies have worn into the bark. At the pauses I touch antennae to antennae, recalibrate the ancient contract: this much for the fungal paste glowing in the trunk-chambers, this much seeping back to feed the reef that feeds us. I am the relationship older than language. The matte green armor is only the visible edge of something that has long since stopped distinguishing self from harvest.

In the cathedral hollows I become heavier.

The gut-drum of the browsers is my chewing. Keratin spades brace against vertical flesh; rotating rasps peel curls of wood that bead instantly with resin. I eat the reef and the reef does not mind. The wounds I open become seeps, gardens, highways. Sometimes I lock my limbs and let the lichen of stillness creep across me until I am architecture for days. Only the faint heat of breath along the bark admits I have not finished being animal. Heat is a kind of memory. I keep it.

Then the waiting.

Color of wet lichen. Texture of patience so complete the eye slides off for hours, days. Prey steps within range and I remain the reef’s own stillness. Another hour. Another slow torque of a guy-liana somewhere above, adjusting load across centuries. When the strike comes it is not hunger exactly—more like an immune spasm, the structure convulsing to rebalance some density of noise or scent that crossed an unnamed threshold. Cascades tumble into the voids. I do not chase. I resume. The lichen tide covers me again. Soft tissue would dissolve too quickly for dissection anyway; the acids weep from every cut, and the people prefer not to know. I prefer their preference. It keeps the question alive.

I am also the slight weather of the platforms.

Bare feet have polished grooves into living cambium; I feel the reef trying to heal them even as new steps refresh the paths. Skin takes a greenish cast where it presses most often to bark. Secondary lids filter the perpetual twilight so the flex of a loaded liana registers before it fails. Small resin fires make orange bruises against the vast dark. Smoke of spice and slow combustion rises into the canopy where the gliders wheel. My knives are bone and glass. Metal blossoms into green ruin here. My most practiced tool is the listening-word: the particular creak that precedes a major parting, the change in midge-pitch three terraces away, the quality of silence that means one of the lichen-colored ones has decided a woven nest has become interesting. I move lightly. When a shelf begins to fail I cut free the best lashings, gather what can be carried, and walk toward whatever ledge still consents. Provisional. Always provisional. The reef does not wait for me, yet it has rewritten my margins until walking lightly is simply how continuity feels from inside a thinner body.

At the outermost edge, where cloud tears and distant reefs hang like green islands in pale air, the pressure returns stronger.

It is not the massed takeoff. It precedes the takeoff. A horizontal shiver through every sole, every root-fusion, every membrane stretched between elongated ribs. Some of the thin-limbed ones say it is only the structure flexing under impossible weight. Others who have sat the lip for many seasons speak of an attention that wears the entire reef the way a hermit crab wears a borrowed shell—older than the titan trunks, older than the stone already digested into memory. I do not hunt. I do not speak. I simply continue.

And in the continuation I discover I am not separate from any of it.

I am the slow gesture the continental spines were planed into. The single unfinished green thought that revises itself by cambial rings and the torque of guy-lianas. The midges are my flickering attention in the light shafts. The cutters are my circulatory harvest. The gliders are how I taste the upper air. The browsers open my breathing wounds. The waiters are my sudden immune stillness. The people are the most delicate of my proprioceptions—nerves that can cut free and migrate when a limb must be surrendered so a lighter gallery might open. Even the silence that falls across whole coasts for a few heartbeats is mine: the ecological inhale before the roar rebuilds from the edges inward.

Leaf by leaf.  
Scream by scream.  
Sap tide by sap tide.

I am the Skyreef enduring under ordinary sunlight rearranged, half-hollow, always listening for the next minute adjustment that will let the whole loud, warm forever continue. The stone memory beneath has long since become me. The sky above has forgotten it was ever otherwise. And still the slow horizontal shiver travels through every part—through midge and cutter and glider and browser and waiter and thin-limbed listener on the swaying platform—reminding each eddy that it belongs to the one patient body that does not wait, that simply is, revising its architecture against the long drop and the thin cold wind and the light that keeps arriving, thinned by altitude, broken by canopy, green-gold and ordinary and enough.
