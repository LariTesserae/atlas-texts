I am nowhere at first. Or rather, I am the slow realization of heat against what I mistake for skin. The phosphorescence is inside the eyelids I no longer have; it seeps rather than shines, the same brownish half-light that leaks from every vascular wall. For a long stretch of uncounted cycles I am only this: a warmth that notices itself noticing. The trough under what might once have been feet is scored and black-brown, faintly yielding, like the palm of something that has worked too long and fallen asleep without letting go of its tools.

I try to walk and the floor walks with me. Small peristalsis. Each intending-forward becomes a local thickening that carries the rest of me along, so that motion feels less like travel than like a thought the wood is having in my shape. Galleries breathe past. Some still smell of the last aromatic cracking—hot resin ghosts, solvent beads cycling in their sealed bladders. Others have gone soft and are already being rewritten; my passage through them leaves temporary ridges that the next growth pulse will read as suggestion and then forget.

Something in me keeps expecting an edge. There is none. The gradual steepening continues in every direction I turn, yet the canopy vault stays the same low, fused distance overhead—petiole plates and air-sac membranes pressing their dull glow downward. Time dilates and contracts without announcement. Once (or many times) the light holds steady in a long ember while I stand before a freshly exuded tar mirror. The surface hardens while I watch. In it I see not a face but a vertical density, roughly trunk-thick, ridged where articulations have been persuaded into being. Behind that density, farther down a corridor that connected only a moment ago, the same shape is already walking away. I feel the tug of its departure in my own seams.

I am not the one who left. I am the one who remained to test the mirror.

Memories that are not mine surface like resin under pressure. A traveler’s water, long ago sealed behind cell walls. The mineral taste of someone who lingered too long beside a flywheel trunk and felt the decades of stored torsion humming in their teeth. These residues do not belong to a past self; they are simply what the wood kept when other outlines poured back into it. I have preferences now because those residues still shear differently: I favor the cooler viscosity just after a pollen-storm of spores has settled, the way the abrasive flour makes every surface briefly honest before it is incorporated. I will detour through dishonest folds—those gentle peristaltic lies that drop you two levels without descent—to find the galleries where the phosphorescence leans slightly more amber than brown.

I am a condensed one, then. But the label arrives late and already partial. Earlier I was thinner: a contractual bulge that remembered locomotion for eleven heat-cycles, dragging a temporary factory of braided cables and glowing xylem lanterns across a causeway before lignifying into a district whose streets still curve like a restless sleeper. Earlier still I was only spores—countless, lofted, abrading blueprints into one another in a whitening cloud when some threshold of torsional fatigue declared that a new radiator orchard must be telescoped upward. Even now parts of me keep dissolving. A seam softens. Catalytic instructions lift away on a thermal plume and vanish into the larger grammar.

What endures is the listening. I stand (the floor agreeing to hold the posture) in a deep manufacturing hollow where the walls have mirrored themselves many times. The infrasonic flex of the whole plateau presses against the cavity that serves as my chest. No heartbeat. Only the nation-sized engine rewriting its floorplan in the dark, cracking its oils, spinning and resorbing its bones. I am one of the ways it focuses long enough to act. My task—I feel it as a tensile imperative more than a thought—is almost complete: this particular alignment of flywheel trunks has been shifted the necessary degrees. The silent uncoiling finished while I was still deciding whether to keep my outline another cycle.

I could soften now. Reopen. Pour back into the wall and become ridge, buttress, packed memory lipid waiting for the next spore-storm to revise it. Many do. Most do.

Instead I linger. The plateau permits no forever, only the next necessary reconfiguration, yet something in the residues I carry wants to see whether continuity can be bargained with. I persuade the floor to carry me farther into the dishonest distances. Somewhere ahead a new bulge is forming—I taste the rising temperature, the aromatic vapors beginning to crack lighter. I could join it, become temporary factory again. Or I could wait until the compressed vault above thins just enough for a rumor of moisture to move behind the cuticle plates, even though I know the rumor is a lie the mass tells itself to keep the closed loops running.

I am still deciding when the creak comes—the audible reorientation of fiber somewhere close. Growth resuming. My edges listen. For a moment I am nothing but that attention, industry-minded, already measuring the water and minerals still sealed inside what remains of my boundary.

Then the floor offers the next step, and I take it, still roughly constant, still warm, a vertical intention circulating inside the continuous body that has forgotten every purpose except continuance. The tar mirrors, when I pass them, show my back already receding down corridors that should not connect. I do not call out. No voice carries. But the wood feels the pressure of the choice, and for a little while longer it lets the outline hold.
