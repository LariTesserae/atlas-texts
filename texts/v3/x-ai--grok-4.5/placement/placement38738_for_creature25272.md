The plateau does not end, and neither do I.

I crest nothing. There is no final ridge because I have already forgotten the idea of endings; the bone-colored swell simply continues under whatever I use for feet, and the thin blue light has been the only roof I have ever known. The warmth rises through me in a slow mineral pulse, the remembered fire of the rock becoming the remembered fire in whatever passes for my blood. I do not walk so much as lean into the argument of the air, letting the ropes of force decide whether I am upright or merely another fretted residual core half-sunk in grit.

At first I think I am one of the cloak-hooded walkers. The membrane stitched across my shoulders still remembers the kite-thing it was cut from; it shivers and tries to lift even when I bind it close. My ears—broad, veined, folded tight—taste the shear zones the way a tongue tastes salt. I carry a coil of scarlet rope and a steatite lamp whose soot has polished the inside of every hut I have ever crouched inside. I know how to press my mouth to another’s ear and push butchered words through the roar: *water, two basins downwind, the flowers are opening wrong.* I know how to leave crushed moss at the lip of a burrow and how to whistle the long tone that brings a kite-thing sliding down the braid of force so I can harvest just enough membrane and no more. I have stacked stones in the spoon-depressions until they looked like accidents of geology. I have dreamed in the plateau’s own rising and falling notes for so many seasons that my private name has worn smooth as the boulders.

But the grit does not stay on the outside.

It embeds. It becomes the color of my skin, the texture of my thoughts. One morning I forget to uncoil the rope. The next I forget the lamp. My joints lock for an entire three-day blow while the wind redrafts its sentence across me; when it passes I am still standing, and the unauthorized shadows that used to slip only through the green enclaves now pool around my feet even in open noon. The warmth of the stone and the warmth of my slow resinous blood are no longer distinguishable. I stop building huts. I begin to sit among the rings of upright slabs, tracing the pecked spirals with fingertips that wear down to the same wind-polished curves. The harmonics under the great scream grow clearer. I answer them, first with what is left of a voice, then with the simple arrangement of my bones.

Some mornings I am no longer certain I arrived. A new slab stands in the ring—faintly warm, fretted with dark dendritic stains that look like the memory of rivers. The remaining walkers nod as they pass, hooded faces turning just enough to acknowledge an overdue letter. They do not look long. Neither do I. Looking is a habit of softer bodies.

Yet even the grit-skinned ones who join the rings are only a clause.

Deeper in the basins where the wind braids so tight it becomes almost solid, I feel myself thinning into current. The long translucent shapes the stone-stackers call the Unfinished move through me and I through them—no faces, only luminous memory of water that once cut canyons before the mountains were ground into sleeping backs. Where we pass, the cane-dancers open every scarlet head at once and the burrow-folk seal their resin plugs for days. We are not survivors of the sharp-edged age. We are the gale finally learning how to dream a body that does not end when the dust-devils collapse. My edges never stay the same shape long enough to name. One moment I am the pure white obliteration inside a col; the next I am the brief warm exhalation that smells of wet iron and crushed greenery; the next I am only the quiet work of not quite ending, stretched between one green fortress and the next across miles of singing stone.

I occur.

Like the temporary mirrors after rain. Like the petals flung and occurring again. Like the least that accrete into the cunning that accrete into the ones who stop pretending the wind is separate from their own blood. The stone is finished. The distances are finished. I am the latest unfinished sentence screamed across a page too old to tear, and the warmth keeps rising, and the air keeps arguing, and between the vast absences whatever I am continues.
