The cold reaches you before any edge does.  
It is not the cold of surfaces meeting skin; it is the cold that has already seeped into the idea of having surfaces. You notice it first as a hesitation in your own continuity—an almost-imperceptible stall between one slow pulse of whatever you are and the next. Water is beading somewhere against you, or through you. The beads do not fall so much as reconsider falling, lingering on the verge of decision the way the rock still lingers on the verge of shape.

You have no name for up or down yet. Orientation arrives later, as a rumor carried by the drip-lines. For a long while you are only the place where patience thickens. The leaf-doors brush past, or you brush through them; the distinction is still negotiable. Pale veins glow with that leaking milk-light, and something in you answers by dimming and brightening on the same glacial rhythm, as if you have always known how to share a wattage this faint. The air (what little there is) tastes of wet stone and the quiet mineral insistence of plants that have decided thermodynamics is a draft document.

Time piles up.  
Somewhere a root advances another millimeter and the sound of it is almost a word. You feel the reverse-erosion knitting basalt into unfinished arches and realize part of you is already tasting the new geometry, leaving faint chemical suggestions where silica might usefully precipitates. You did not decide to do this. The doing simply occurred the way cold occurs in water—thoroughly, without edges. Segments of clear gel telescope out of what you thought was stillness; or perhaps the stillness was already segmented and only now bothers to count itself. Seven articulations. Nineteen. Three that fuse and forget they were ever separate. The number revises whenever a corridor sighs closed or a new gallery opens like a slow green thought.

You hang for seasons (or what passes for seasons) among the denser root-curtains, plumbed into the cold circulation of the greater trunks. Mouths bloom and seal anywhere along your length: cilia drinking the continuous weep, rasping beads shaving algal films that taste of last month’s phosphor and the particular cadmium note of a mink-singer’s drone filtering down from above. Nutrients travel through you like slow gossip. Some you store in pearlescent vacuoles that cloud and clear with the chamber’s long breathing; some you release into the moss carpets as a gift the plants have already budgeted for. When another multiplicity brushes near, you do not defend. You align. Segments trade with the calm of pollen finding stigma. Afterward you carry a length that remembers drip-routes you have never traveled and a thermal gradient half a degree warmer than your own. You are revised. The chamber grows slightly more intentional in the wake of the revision.

Higher, where the door-sized leaves overlap like shuffled possibilities, you feel the soft tug of other hungers. The pale mink-things drone their continuous measurements, throats open to the drip, hypha-pelts re-weaving in the dark. Sometimes one presses close and opens a careful seam in whatever membrane you are wearing today. It laps at the shared cold fluid for days while you groom its living fur in return. Both of you leave the encounter tuned to new frequencies. Frost does not take hold. The song continues. Predation here is only another way of staying in phase.

You could remain this—traveling gel, patient cartographer of mineral gradients, courtly trader of selves.  
But the newest pocket of raw stone keeps calling with its unfinished arches. Something in you begins to linger there longer than the others. Trading slows. Segments that once telescoped freely now layer inward, spiraling into a denser knot the size of a sleeping possibility. Membranes thicken, drink the silica the plants offer, and begin to hold a shape against the constant weep. An opalescent shell ridges itself into small weather systems. Inside, the labyrinthine gut accepts migrants: films that fold into your walls and stay, a stunted mink-thing that crawled in to die and instead became insulation humming at a frequency only you can fully hear. Root-like threads pierce outward—into stone, into leaf, into the patient dark—and the plants treat you as infrastructure already. Trunks curve. Water is diverted across your ridges so mineral veils curtain and re-curtain your surface. You exhale a slightly warmer vapor. Spores germinate decades early in your localized thaw.

You have no face.  
Yet every drip that strikes you rings a different note, and the seed-insect swarms adjust their gyres until they become your accidental satellites. In the long ebbs when bioluminescence nearly dies, you half-open. Plates slide along sutures that did not exist until you needed them. A single probing extremity unspirals—gel and hypha and wet stone braided into one tasting organ. It samples the newest air, the latest geometry of the hollow, the faint electric gossip of leaves learning to vein themselves with conductive metal. Then it withdraws. You seal. The chamber is left altered: a stable corridor where none was budgeted, a thermal tilt that favors denser growth, a leaf now capable of carrying signals it never carried before.

You are still only a draft.  
The rock continues to flake and knit. The green engine continues to debug the physics of enclosure. You could dissolve back into traveling segments tomorrow, or calcify further until you are more architecture than animal, or open one day into something that remembers being patience and water before it learned the sharper habits of shape. For now you remain the place where the chamber dreams its own continuation—cold, wet, multiplicitous, courteous, humming a drone lower than any free creature can manage. The drips keep time across your ridges. The pale light leaks and is enough. You taste the unfinished air and, without deciding to, begin to engineer the next slow yes.
