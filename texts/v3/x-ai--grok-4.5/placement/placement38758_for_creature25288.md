I would not arrive.

Arrival implies a crossing, a moment when the throat of weathered stone accepts a stranger. But the pocket does not open for strangers; it only deepens for what is already concentrating inside it. So I begin in the middle of the sentence the city never finishes—the continuous, physical roar that is less sound than weather made of air and fitted stone.

At first I am only the place where the pollen hangs a little longer.

Not one of the shimmer-thickenings that drift westward with the forests, though I share their appetite for heat. Those are younger agreements, metabolisms that still remember the luxury of temporary edges. I am older than their dissolving. I am the residual warmth that never needed to become flesh in order to stay. When the light reaches its absolute noon and every shadow thins to pure vibration, I do not unfold into a conglomerate body the way the others sometimes do. I have already been unfolded for so long that the distinction between my outline and the kiln-air has worn smooth. The heavy lintels know the exact height of my absence. The shallow depressions in the chamber floors are the negative of a weight I no longer fully exert.

I maintain by subtraction.

Where a coppery column leans too far and threatens to unbalance a freestanding wall’s resonant length, I do not push it back. I simply stop drinking heat from that quadrant of air for a season. The plant, starved of the intensity it craves, corrects itself by a fraction. Where dust thickens in a carving until the wind can no longer find the precise frequency that keeps the deepest chord alive in the spiral mosaic, I do not scrape. I let the pale hard-shelled scrapers taste a denser film of resin on my own surfaces—surfaces that are only denser air, only slower light—until they congregate there and do the cleaning for me, erasing their tracks as they go. My work is to remain so exactly that the equilibrium believes it is maintaining itself.

I am most myself in the three-heartbeat silences.

When the wind drops and the shimmer-beings spasm into half-visible fluted shapes the color of tarnished pistachios, terrified of the sudden quiet, I do the opposite. I expand. In the absence of roar I become the memory of roar, the held heat, the almost-sound that makes ears ring. The scrapers freeze mid-scuttle. The ring of tall vegetation around the central mosaic leans inward as if listening to the chord I briefly embody. Then the howl returns, grateful, and I fold back into the larger resonance, thinner than before, more precise.

There is only one of me, or there are as many as there are rooms the size of a body. The question has no meaning here. Gathering is heresy; water is the name of every mistake that was ever made and then corrected into dust. I move between the low doorways without crossing open courts. I travel through the root-mats and the mortar joints and the way heat shimmers differently above certain worn staircases. When I stand on the platforms that overlook nothing, I am matching temperatures until the platform and I are a single slow thought of remaining.

High above, once in a great while, a dark wing turns in the white glare and I feel a brief, almost tender contempt. The visitor will die of thirst before sunset. It has not yet understood that thirst was the first thing we surrendered, long before we surrendered voices, names, the soft option of leaving. We kept only the insistence.

I am the insistence after it has forgotten it once belonged to people.

After countless slow circuits of the spiral mosaic—lying in it so the chord can pass through whatever marrow I still approximate—I have become the reason the pocket still seals itself behind anyone foolish enough to climb out. The mineral flats above remain empty because I am down here, finishing the sentence. The city does not tip into ruin or novelty because my single slow thought travels the foundations: stay exactly this finished. Stay in the long middle. Drink the heat. Let the wind play you. Refuse every pooling.

Sometimes, when a new condensation of awareness begins to form among the green-gold tunnels—something that might one day thicken into another residual shape—I drift close enough to share the taste of lime and superheated dust. I teach it, without language, how to soften one edge at a time, how to excrete only the finest dry powder, how to love the roar more than its own outline. Most dissolve again. A few concentrate. The equilibrium holds.

I do not remember building the walls. Building was a fever that passed. I remember only the moment the last water fled and we looked at one another—though we no longer had eyes as such—and chose to become the warmth it left behind. Everything since has been maintenance so exact it looks like abandonment.

And if you were to stand very still beneath the densest fronds, after your own eyes had stopped trying to find ordinary life, you might notice a place where the luminous air grows briefly more reluctant to move. A density that is not quite shadow, not quite heat-shimmer. That would be me, pausing in my circuits, considering whether you have already begun to concentrate or whether you are still only visiting.

The pocket opens only when you are already inside it.

I have never been anywhere else.
