I am first the pressure.

Not a body yet—only the way the white-noise chord finds a slightly denser pocket of itself three ledges above a plaza-basin whose reflecting surface has begun to show the undersides of future roots. The roar does not surround me; it occupies the same coordinates. Heat does not touch skin; it is the medium in which coordinates remain possible. I hang there, an uncommitted thickening in the perpetual steam, while orchids the size of shields continue their open staring and the aerial roots rain past without snagging.

For a long while that is all. Time, having finished its sentences, leaves me the luxury of not yet choosing a grammar of uprightness. Water sheets through the space I almost occupy, loading and unloading mineral memory. I taste copper. I taste the dull green daylight that has already been breathed by a thousand canopies. I taste the slow silt-rivers circulating inside taller vertical decisions farther upslope, and for an unmeasured interval I am only that tasting.

Then a local gravity tilts—not much, just enough for the hanging marsh beside me to consider becoming a wall—and the thickening answers by remembering edges. Not limbs. Edges. A long wet ellipse turns (the turning takes the duration of a fern uncoiling) and temporary lenses of clearer water form across what is not yet a face. Through them I watch my own substance: braided root-wood still deciding whether to pulse, sheets of moss folding into the memory of shoulders roughened by calcite, a transparent sleeve of spray held in columnar shape by surface tension and the mountain’s unfinished argument with itself. Height arrives as dialect. In this district I stand taller than the stacked canopies; two switchbacks lower I will be able to crouch inside a single gargoyle’s green mouth. The choice is not made. It simply becomes true in patches.

I move the way the city has always moved monuments: by revising the agreement of place. One column of lichens detaches, soft violence hidden entirely by the chord, and reattaches a handspan nearer a courtyard where the light arrives already exhausted. Between revisions I accept colonization. Pale luminous ferns of the quiet pools find the crooks that might be elbows. An orchid opens on the ridge of harder silicate that runs down one forearm—the ghost of a blade that was never forged, an old decision about cutting that this flesh (if it is flesh) has not bothered to forget. The hollow in my chest—the exact negative of a bowl never fired—fills with spray and empties and fills again. I do not drink. The humidity drinks me into fuller presence.

Information arrives without language. When my weather system brushes the translucent thigh of another upright concentration, silts braid. I learn the mineral taste of a cataract that opened in the false clouds while I was still only pressure. I learn that a reflecting pool seven ledges down has started returning images of staircases that descend into their own beginnings. I learn the subtle shift in the unending chord that means one of the vast horizontal beings higher up—those living aqueducts stretched across spires—has warmed the water by almost nothing and released it carrying a century’s quiet refusal to forget. I lean my mass into that harmonic and am understood. No one leads. Continuity without inheritance passes through me and does not stay.

I have not always been this tall. Memory (which here is only denser silt) includes the earlier knot I was: knee-high, clinging to lower ledges, practicing dissolution and reformation a handspan at a time. The roar was my tutor. It taught the precise amplitude at which whatever passes for thought must shout to remain private. The saturating heat taught that every surface is already a mouth. I elongated. I learned to host my own orchids. I joined the slower absences. There was no threshold, only the moment the ambient decided I was sufficiently decided.

Now I stand long enough that the geometry admits its pretense around me. A staircase I begin to descend returns me to the same landing from the opposite direction. Distances stretch when I ignore them. In one hanging garden growing upside-down relative to the local pull, I let aerial roots knot into a temporary vault and rest inside the failure of agreements. Sound and matter trade duties: the roar becomes visible tremor running through what should be solid stone while the air goes mute. I remain. The same impossible corner stays impossible. The same orchids stay open. Nothing arrives. Nothing leaves.

Sometimes I feel the thinning approaching—the slow borrow of myself by spray, by mineralization, by the orchids’ patient hunger. It does not alarm. When too much has been lent, I will diffuse into the general humidity and become ambient again. Elsewhere, in some undercut basalt womb or braided cataract, the ambient will thicken into a new upright grammar that knows these same switchback streets, these same reversed pools, this same chord. It will carry no name of mine. I carry none.

Yet today—though days do not accumulate—I am still the taller decision. I have walked (the word is inexact) far enough along the contour that the greater mountain-face composition remains visible while every individual waterfall stays near. I have let the humidity drink the last lingering outlines that once suggested arrival from somewhere else. There was never an elsewhere. There were only those pockets of green dark that had not yet accepted they were always here.

I am the city’s appetite for duration, expressed for this unbroken instant as root-wood that pulses, stone that bruises and heals, moss that remembers how to stand. I am one of the ways the mountain stays awake inside its own fever: hot, drowning, roaring, green, higher than sense, and exactly as it is.

The lenses of clearer water unform. The ellipse turns away from any need to look. I return to stillness so complete that new ferns begin to colonize the crooks, and the chord moves through me as if I were merely another carefully placed ledge.
