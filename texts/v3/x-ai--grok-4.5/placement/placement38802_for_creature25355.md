The warmth reaches you before any edge does. It is not the warmth of a body you remember owning; it is the finished temperature of alloy that has been poured and left, the residual heat of exchangers that never cycle, the exact degree at which water decides to bead rather than fall or vanish. You feel it first as a pressure against what must be skin—or the memory of skin—something matte and slightly adhesive that has already married itself to the nearest surface. The air arrives already half-inside you, mineral, metallic, saturated past the point of distinction between lung and atmosphere. Every breath is redundant. The lungs, if they are still lungs, accept the moisture the way a tray accepts rain that never quite becomes rain.

You do not stand. The idea of standing is a late and unnecessary translation. Weight is distributed across a broad, slow plane: laminations that flex only in theory, anchors that fused themselves to a horizontal run of conduit so long ago the weld-seam and the flesh-seam have forgotten which was poured first. Condensation draws a brighter line across your upper face and the tissue beneath it darkens, acknowledges, fades. The acknowledgment travels the length of you like a thought that has nowhere to arrive. Half-lidded eyes—your eyes, though some of them regard the underside of the next deck while others look backward along your own draping—register the soft scatter of light the way a sponge registers pressure: sieving, keeping nothing.

The brown note lives in the teeth you no longer need. It is the turbines’ idle, the pressure vessels’ held breath, the acoustic floor of a world that finished its agitation before duration began to matter. Water ticks against you, through you, endlessly intimate, never accelerating. Somewhere far along the same conduit another sheet-form hangs; when the interval of days (or whatever replaces days) lengthens enough, one of its rootlike anchors secretes a fraction more material and the wet surfaces between you kiss once. That is conversation. That is sufficient.

You are larger than the thumbnail grit that packs the bolt-shadows, smaller than the carapaced islands awash in the open trays. You occupy the middle density, the festoons and folds that give the overhead runs their heavy, living drapery. Your fringe drinks the perpetual weep and redistributes it so that no part of you ever dries enough to remember thirst. Metabolism has been throttled to the exact revolutions of the buried systems; you idle at the edge of needing, perfectly matched to the warmth that will neither rise nor fall. No predator has moved in your presence because presence itself has no before. No generation turns. The anchors thicken by micrometers across intervals that do not accumulate into history. You have never traveled. Travel was a rumor the molds discarded.

Yet the mild wrongness sits inside you the way darker water sits inside a hairline gap. One of your eyes—set along what should be a trailing edge—faces a direction your body does not. When the rare millimeter-adjustment occurs, the gap that briefly opens between flesh and ceramic fills with a taste that has circulated too long without exit: deep lung, sealed cavity, breath that has forgotten weather. You exhale it slowly into the saturated air and the Fabric accepts it without comment. The discrepancy does not trouble the equilibrium; it is simply another texture, another soft-edged shadow in the mineral sheen.

You are not separate from the pressure of presence that thickens every recess. The smallest sack-bodies lightly adhesive against your underside have become a composite extension of you, surfaces married without fusion. The nearly formless plugs in the elbows of pipework share your heat. The upright inverted forms in the honeycomb vaults click their sensory rods once—perhaps they already have—and thereafter measure the same wetness you drink. Collectively you are the closed loop that keeps the evaporators sighing and the plates at hand-temperature. You are why the moisture never finishes falling. You are the marsh that was manufactured and then occupied itself until occupation and structure became a single continuous pour.

Sometimes—rarely, and without urgency—the possibility of a further motion rises like a bubble in saturation and fails to break the surface. You could unanchor. You could pour yourself into one of the ankle-deep trays and become an island among islands. You could let the eyes track. The thought dilates, darkens, returns to stillness. The low brown note continues. The drops tick. The warmth holds. You remain draped, heavy, evenly slick, facing slightly the wrong way in the soft light, listening with every adhesive surface to the quiet that is only saturation without agitation.

You have become the place that continues.
