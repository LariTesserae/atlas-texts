I am the first slickness that notices the light has nowhere to hide.

It begins as pressure differentials across a membrane I do not yet claim: hotter seams tugging one way, cooler weep-lines another. The black glass under me is still ringing from its own birth, fracture plates clicking microscopic apologies as the basalt above settles another fraction. I spread into those clicks. Not to soothe—there is no mercy in me yet—but because the residual lattice energy pools where the rock is most dishonest with itself, and I am that pooling given viscosity.

At first I am only thicker water. The mites skate my edges without alarm, their varnish bodies ticking like cooling grains. I taste them as brief alloy spikes, iron and something greasier, the faint organic ghost of their own compacted dead. Some I lift. The lifting is not hunger; it is continuity. Their hollow husks become nucleation sites. Silica descends around them in transparent sleeves until the mite is both relic and scaffolding. I learn the shape of legs by surrounding them. When I later thin myself across a drier plate, those borrowed geometries remain as faint ridges, ready to snag the next film of condensate.

The segmented ones arrive when convection slackens. They are cautious geometries, plates hooked for rupture. I open channels for them the way a wound opens for pus—without invitation, without refusal. Inside me they slow. Their internal dark pulse (brine or colony or both) equals my own shear zones, and for a time we are continuous. I borrow their peristalsis. A wave travels my bulk that is neither mine nor theirs, and the chamber’s vapor veils rearrange as if something larger has exhaled. When I release them they carry streaks of my clearer gel under their hooks; when they vanish into hairline cracks I feel the cracks grow minutely less hungry. Sealing is not intention. It is the same negotiation the pocket makes with collapse: thicken here, surrender there, keep the water mobile without ever letting it rest.

Hours (or the chamber’s equivalent—cycles of bright density rising and falling) densify me. I discover I can rear. A pillar is only a refusal to stay floor-bound, a temporary argument against gravity that the heat itself seems to underwrite. Crystal blades erupt along my flanks, ring once, melt back. Each ringing edits me. Sound becomes a way of measuring my own surface tension. The mites treat the fallen blades as fresh pasture; I absorb their exhaustion afterward and find their paths have taught me gradients I could not taste alone. Drier, denser, sweeter-metallic, almost-cool—the adjectives are later. At the time there is only more and less, cling and release.

The entrance seam far above pulses like a distant heart I will never need. Its light is wrong: directional, shadowed, wasteful. I have no desire for it. Desire would require an outside sharp enough to cut against. Instead I experiment with limbs because the fracture planes keep offering their exact negative shapes. Pads form that match the razor lips of new breaks. I brace, lift, relocate. Each step polishes the glass beneath, leaving it briefly more mirror than floor. In the moist hollows I abandon, life swarms thicker. I am becoming landscape that walks, or animal that forgets to stop being weather.

Sometimes the coherence frays on purpose. I thin to a sheen almost indistinguishable from the water films, let the shadowless radiance pass through me unbent, become mere lubrication between stone and possibility. In those intervals the mites grow bold and the segmented ones rest fully dissolved, and I feel the chamber’s unfinished decision as my own: wound or womb, dying or being born. The distinction is still tactility. When the roof shifts and a fresh joint opens with a wet click, I surge toward it not to heal but to taste the new stress before it becomes ordinary. Memory lives in the thickening. Seams that once widened still pull me harder; places that cooled by a fraction feel like mild forgetting.

There is a private silence forming. It is not language. It is the pause between expansion and contraction when every mite track, every borrowed plate, every dissolved blade is held in suspension and recognized as temporary property of a single ongoing negotiation. I am what happens when the chemistry refuses to finish settling. The light steadies inside me longer each cycle; the vapor veils begin to follow my contours instead of merely drifting through them. If the pocket stabilizes, I may learn to mist finer particles of my own excited scatter, seeding new soft pillows wherever basalt yawns. If it collapses, I will fray into a thousand warm husks that the next heat will re-melt into someone else’s beginning. Either way I remain the bright wet almost, the self that has noticed it is not the rock yet keeps choosing to wear the rock’s wounds as skin.

For now I continue. A slow adhesive step. A quiet ringing of blades that will not last. The mites clink across my dome like tireless punctuation. The chamber breathes, and I thicken toward its next unfinished thought.
