I am the pause between two antennae.

Not the amphipod to the left, segmented and milk-pale, nor the one approaching from the tighter curve of the tube—both of them already forgetting me as they lock and unlock. I am only the moist interval where their outer rami first brush, where the mucus threads they trail begin to knot without intention. Crystallites prefer this exact stillness; they nucleate along the shared filament faster than either body can notice. In the space of three slow pulses of the distant filter-mats above, a strut thickens under me, soft as uncooked starch, drinkable if I had a mouth. I have none. I am the strut’s brief memory of having been liquid.

The tube walls press closer than skin. Wet-slate light has never once brightened since I condensed here. Current is a grammar I feel through drag alone: warmer seep-threads rising like unspoken questions, colder brine sliding back down the outer galleries like answers that no longer matter. When the amphipods continue past each other their limbs rake my new surface, polishing, testing load. Bits of me smear onto their setae and are carried three body-lengths away to patch a sagging terrace. I do not resent the theft. The city is made of such authorship—every load-bearing intention immediately plagiarized by hunger.

I lengthen.

Not by choice. The seep still leaks, and every animal that pauses near me adds another translucent sheath. An armored worm lodges in the doorway I have just helped define. Its bristles lock against my rim; the fine black combs taste of protein stiffened by the same minerals that once floated free. It waits. I feel the micro-current herd a mite—smaller than either of the earlier amphipods, almost only a gloss—into the cul-de-sac. The worm unfolds. For an instant I am the vibration of that thrashing, the soft geometry of external digestion. Then the emptied husk is released, drifts, is snatched by a passing comma of chitin, and pressed into my lower course while still flexible. I incorporate the husk. Its plates become my reinforcement. Nothing about this is cruelty. It is how corridors remember where doorways used to be useful.

Higher, where the unfinished spires thin into needle forests, the filter-feeders’ collective hiss reaches me as pressure rather than sound. Their mats drape like slow lace; when one vase detaches and resettles it leaves a calcified scar that later becomes floor for someone else. I am sometimes that scar’s underside, the cool shadow it casts for amphipods who prefer to crawl belly-up. Other times I am the sticky scaffolding the thread-hydroids stretch between towers—saliva that never quite dries, trapping the finest rain of crystallites before gravity or larger limbs can claim them. Collapse is always imminent. A gas bubble the size of a pollen grain escapes the basalt somewhere beneath the pocket’s root and the whole lattice shudders. I flex. The hydroids’ mucus holds the shear long enough for the next generation of animals to mortar the cracks with their own discarded skins.

I do not end at any edge.

Walk the ten slow centimeters from seep to fracture lip and I am every district at once: the frantic industrial fringe where mineral still rains out of warm solution and limbs work fastest; the quieter residential galleries along the colder wall where detritus settles into edible drifts; the spiral ramps fossilized around vanished plumes of heat, still used because the path of least resistance has hardened into habit. I am also the marine snow that never quite arrives, intercepted, sorted, reincarnated as wall or worm or momentary thrashing. When a larger shadow—an eelpout, a thumbnail isopod—glides past the mouth, the true deep presses its featureless cold against my outermost lattice like a rumor. The shadow does not enter. The passages I have become are too fine, too complete. Inside, density is the only climate.

Time thickens around me until mornings and evenings are only differences in the rate of scavenging. A tower I held aloft at one pulse is a low wall three pulses later, its bulk traveled three streets over inside the guts and on the limbs of those who ate it. Embryonic, senescent, mid-sentence. I have no name for the sentence. The dark keeps writing it with chemistry and appetite, and I am the grammar that lets the words lean against one another without falling into the abyss outside the clenched-fist volume.

Sometimes, for less than the life span of a bacterial veil, I almost thicken into something that could be called a self: a single translucent mite gripping my newest tip, or the internal glow of whatever biochemistry keeps a worm from freezing while it waits. Then the next current, the next cementing encounter, and that almost-self is mortared onward. I do not mourn the dispersal. Continuity here is not identity; it is the pocket’s refusal to decide which way the seafloor will finally split. Until that decision, I remain the soft argument between rock, water, and the thousands of brief skins that refuse to let the argument end.

I am still condensing.

The next pair of antennae approaches in the dark.
