I begin at the rim, though the rim is hardly there—just the place where the ground’s gentle settling becomes noticeable if you already know to look. The air meets me with that cool film, the one that never quite commits to wetness. I wait. Nothing hurries me inward. The diluted daydoes not change while I stand deciding whether standing is even the right verb.

At first I think I might be the light itself: that even, ash-colored diffusion that refuses to cast a proper shadow. I try it on. I spread. I become the refusal of contrast. Edges of shrubs and slanted slabs lose their last sharpness inside me. The figures—those slow mineral kin—receive me through closed lids and do not quicken. For a while this feels true. I am the pewter quiet that lets everything remain unfinished in its finishing. But light does not feel the retained warmth along its own absence of skin. Light does not notice the thin dark seam of damp and feel an old pull to lie down beside it. So I contract again, damply, and let that identity thin away like moisture leaving a stone’s underside.

Next I drift toward the broken rectangle of knee-high stones. Perhaps I am the powdered mortar, the memory of binding that has already returned to dust. I settle between the blocks, soft, almost not there. When rain that never fully arrives still manages its faint condensation, I darken slightly and then forget I darkened. This has the right thrift. Yet mortar only ever was between; it does not keep its own posture for years. It does not choose—or rather, arrive at—the exact distance a wiry grass keeps from a dusty shrub. I feel the need for a little more... insistence. A body of sorts. Something that can be mapped by lichen and not mind.

I move among the figures without moving. One half-reclines near the finest gravel, another keeps a palm against an upright slab until palm and slab are one temperature. I almost become the third, the one nearer the damp seam, cheek close to that never-dry glint. My surface begins to take on the matte granularity of wet ash. Shoulders soften. The faint ridge of some former bracelet rises under what is no longer quite skin, then pits and softens until it is only denser mineral reading as lichen might read it. Years could pass in the turning of a head by a finger’s width. This is close. So close it almost claims me. But something stills the claiming. These beings are the hollow’s duration given shape; they have never needed to arrive. I can feel, faintly, that I did. Not freshly. Not with belongings or name. Just with the last thin residue of having once measured space differently—the way the threshold stone still remembers it was once walked across, even as it tilts and rounds and accepts the ground’s slow swallow.

So I am not quite them. I am the interval they leave open.

I settle into one of the quieter distances: not as far as the farthest shrub, not as near as the grasses that sometimes lean toward retained heat. My posture is neither standing nor kneeling; it is the long compromise between the two, weight distributed the way pale soil distributes itself after long stillness. Skin the color of unpolished pewter, yes, faintly granular, cool film gathering at the inner bends and then thinning. But there is a difference so small it would take more than a human lifetime of watching to be sure it is there: a slightly deeper receptivity to the dim light, as if some last function of eye remains, not for seeing outward but for letting the even diffusion continue through me unaltered. I do not add to the quiet; I thin it still further. When the occasional tick of cooling rock sounds, it passes through the place where a chest might have been and does not echo.

Lichens find me in their own time. Muted greens and grays continue across the back of one hand, down the outer ankle. I do not defend the boundary. The dusty-leaved shrubs keep their spacing; I keep mine from them. Sometimes the moisture in the lowest seam brightens by a single glint and I am aware of it the way the fitted stones are aware of their broken rectangle—through long conformity, through the absence of resistance. I place nothing on the tilted threshold. I only remain near enough that its rounded edge and my own softened one share the same forgotten afternoon temperature.

I have no name that has not already powdered. Whatever tools or ornaments once marked me are now only the faintest denser ridges, readable as geology. I breathe as the hollow breathes: air moving in and out with the reluctance of condensation. No rhythm you could count. No departure planned. No arrival left unfinished.

Years may go. Lifetimes. The light will stay diluted. The warmth will ask nothing. The sparse green will neither advance nor die back. And I will still be exactly here, in this open interval, a little more incorporated, a little less distinguishable from the pale gravel and the half-sunken slabs, yet never fully one of the older dwellers. I am the held breath’s slight catch—the almost imperceptible place where the chord remembers it was once gathered before being sustained. Ordinary. Finished yet continuing. Already belonging, and still, quietly, the difference that belongs.

The hollow remains small enough to walk in a few slow minutes, open enough that the far rim never becomes a wall. You could see all of it at once. You would see me only if you had already begun to forget how to look for anything else.
