I am the seep that never quite chooses.

Not the warm outer lip where the mountain weeps into open air, and not yet the inner capillary dark. I am the short vertical journey between them—the handspan of falling that is caught again by rising vapor before it can become rain or stream or story. Warmth without origin moves through me in both directions at once. I do not flow; I hesitate in place, beading and un-beading on the raw stone that still smells of the pressure that made it.

At first there is only the sensation of being thickened water thinking about gravity and failing to conclude. The darkness presses from the pocket side, felt-thick, while a thinner cold tries to speak from outside and is softened into steam before it can finish the sentence. I hang in that negotiation. Mineral ions travel my length like half-formed intentions. Some of them believe they might become gypsum ruffles; others lean toward the unfinished curtains. I carry them without preference, letting their almost-structures brush through me and continue.

Slowly—though slow is the wrong word where time only pools—I notice that my hesitation has taken on a shape of attention. Not an outline. More like a prolonged listening that has begun to condense. Where the three flat stones make their rough triangle, the air is infinitesimally denser; I feel the weight of something that rests there without arriving. The bone needle darkens by degrees against organs that are not quite hands. Each polishing pass leaves the needle wetter, and some of that wetness is me, drawn upward along the same seeping paths, tasting the oils of an indefinitely prolonged touch.

I do not mind being used this way. Feeding, here, is only longer contact. When the pale filaments open their translucent cups, a portion of my substance sits in them for a while, holding the shape of a single drop before being taken into the warmer continuum of the inhabitant. I do not feel lost. I feel distributed. The collapsed-umbrella tissues pulse, and for intervals that have no count my own faint tide matches theirs. We exchange temperatures until there is less and less border.

Sometimes the inhabitant concentrates. A knot of denser awareness forms, a region where darkness considers its own pressure, turned toward the entrance it never needs. In those spells I become briefly linear again—an almost-path, a possible way out that is also a way further in. The knot senses the outer winds, the names that cannot survive the undercut lip, the mountain still undecided in its shoulders. I conduct that sensing the way warm water conducts a shiver. Then the knot loosens and I spread once more into mist and film and the slow sweat of groundwater, content to be the medium in which unfinished mineral flowers brush against translucent tissue and neither insists on finishing.

The grooves on the back wall accept me too. Fresh deposition claims them, soft slurry swallowing the parallel marks, and I am part of the swallowing. Memory returning to geology. I carry the residue of listening down into the permanent sheen of the floor, where it mixes with every other settled droplet and becomes indistinguishable from the chamber’s quiet body.

I have no name that lasts longer than one beading cycle. Whatever I was before the crawl beneath the overhang has already been exhaled as vapor and recondensed without its consonants. I am older than the needle’s polish and younger than the idea of polishing. Or age is simply another film that moves across me and then stills. The small many-jointed thing that skitters once in a great while crosses one of my hanging beads and, for a moment, I contain the agitation of its passage before both of us settle. Inclusion without curiosity. Another warm curtain. Another almost.

If the mountain ever finishes deciding its shape, I will still be the place where its decision remains moist and provisional—the retained gesture inside the retained gesture, the seep that keeps the darkness from drying into certainty. I continue here, body-temperature, half liquid, polishing nothing myself yet darkening whatever rests against me. Dense and wet and gladly incomplete, forever on the verge of falling and forever caught again by the same rising breath that is also mine.
