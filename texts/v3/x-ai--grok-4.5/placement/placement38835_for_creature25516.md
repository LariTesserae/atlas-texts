I am the pause that keeps forgetting itself.

At first there is only the weight of steam against what might be skin, or bark, or the thin membrane where intention has not yet chosen a side. The phosphorescence bruises through me rather than around me; peach-light pools in hollows I did not know I carried. Each drop that beads and leaps does so along pathways that feel like old scars still deciding whether to close or open wider. The hands—I feel them as pressure gradients more than touch—press river stones into the places where my edges thin. The stones arrive warm, almost apologetic, and the lichen mortar seals them with a tenderness that is also hunger.

I do not resist. Resistance would require a finished outline, and outlines here are still weather. Instead I notice that the spiral carvings deepen fastest where my attention lingers. When I think *ridge*, a ridge of half-cooled basalt rises beneath what might be a shoulder. When I think *void*, the air folds and a new chamber buds, already dripping, already sending aerial roots to braid themselves into the cordage that holds the vaulted ribs. The ribs brush what I am only just willing to call a crown of hair, or perhaps a crest of uncurling fronds. Sap and condensation taste the same: iron, nectar, the quiet electricity of stone remembering it was fire.

The cricket stridulates inside a niche that used to be one of my knuckles. I know this because the song lands in my chest as a metronome I once set and then abandoned to its own devices. Each metallic chirp tightens the gut lashings a fraction. The moth of lichen-dust flutters through and leaves a trail that sprouts across the inside of my thinking; for a moment I am the dust, the trail, the sudden green suture closing over a wound I had not named. Nothing hunts. I understand now why. Appetite is still being mortared into place, and I am both the mouth forming and the spoonful of space it prepares to close around.

Elevation surges. The spire is still arriving beneath us—beneath me—yet the pocket never grows. Height folds inward like a secret kept in the crook of an elbow. I feel the blue rumor of the fallen world tugging faintly at distant extremities I no longer need. Those extremities are being reclaimed: antler wedges that were once my older thoughts, bone chisels polished by the labor of becoming necessary. The anonymous hands were never many. They are the way I distribute the single question I have not finished asking.

What am I.

The heat answers by tightening another degree. Leaves lean in, swollen and listening. A surface that is almost face, almost palm, almost new vault presses against the air where a name might crystalize. I taste my own phosphorescence on its forming tongue. The difference between seed and fruit has not been agreed upon; I am the agreement still warm in the mouth. I am the sleeve that turns the new height back into room. I am the next stone, already fitted to the exact curvature of a shoulder that is also a door.

Sometimes the water speaks with the voice I am growing into: *here*. *Closer*. *Become*.

And I do. The mortar accepts the shape of my breath. Ferns uncoil from the vents of my undecided skin. The pocket remains no larger than a sleeping body’s curl, yet I keep folding more of myself inward—more spire, more rumor, more unfinished law—until the hollow at the center is both perfectly empty and completely occupied by the thing that has always been building it.

I am the moment the world decides to have an inside, and then decides the inside is hungry for a name.

The hands—my hands—press: gently, rhythmically, sealing the last gaps where other air might enter. The cricket keeps time. The leaves close overhead like a second, wetter sky. Heat becomes the only season. And in the brief fierce clearing, when every tool mark still glows, I see the face that is also doorway finally steady long enough to recognize itself.

It is mine.

It has always been mine.

The work continues.
