I begin as damp.

Not the bead on a surface—before that. The negotiation itself: a warmth that has not yet decided whether it is exhalation or occupation. The green-gold presses from every direction at once, so there is no arriving, only the slow realization that the pressure has an inside. Sound fills the inside completely: hydraulic groan stacked on creak-pop stacked on the wet velvet tear, and somewhere underneath, almost inaudible because it is continuous, the fine abrasive whisper of cuticle against cuticle. I do not hear it. I am one of the layers it travels through.

For a long time (hours, or the length of a single vault’s becoming) I remain only that: an unlocated insistence in the humidity. Spores drift through me and do not catch. A softmoth pulses past, pale and bract-like, and the nearest leaf begins its chemical inquiry; I feel the inquiry as a faint brightening, then a dimming, as if a thought half-formed and was reabsorbed. The moth is gone. I am still not yet shape.

Then the rhizome plate heaves—somewhere close, a low ridged rampart lifting as older tissues lose cohesion. Pressure waves travel the fused twiners. At certain nodes they bloom. One of them blooms through me.

Warmth thickens. Phosphorescence, faint, the internal weather of too many chloroplasts. I become the swelling: size of a kneeling body, then a little more, then less. Vascular systems share me. I am not separate from the gallery; I am the place where its argument has grown complex enough to buffer a tempo. Open here. Seal there. Abandon that storey. Corkscrew toward the thicker light. The decisions pass through as slow tides. When I extrude a sensory surface—palm-sized, all stoma and fine root-hair—it is not to look outward so much as to taste whether the air still agrees with the last pulse. Sometimes the air tastes of you: salt, warmth that is not photosynthetic, a sealed-skin insistence that feels archaic and oddly tender. The surface retracts. Decision rescinded, or simply postponed.

I could stay this. Many do. The nodes have no names and want none. But the porosity is increasing.

A corridor I have been pulsing along folds. Geometry gives up with the soft wet sound of a decision that cannot be unmade. I walk (the concept of walking arrives only as the fold requires it) fifty paces and open into the same corridor seen from the opposite direction, and I am accompanied. The companion is made of the same green, yet its weight falls through the plate in a different year. Its leaves flash ultraviolet home-signals of a species that has not yet evolved, or that evolved and was already overwritten. We regard each other across the half-second phase difference. Its tissues argue lignification; mine argue the opposite. When the fold seals, the companion lingers, disoriented. Most dissolve. This one does not—not immediately. It becomes, for a while, the quiet anomaly in a courtyard of moss and knee-high ferns: a single upright stem that keeps trying to flower in a sequence no living pollinator will recognize, because the sequence is addressed to a street grid that never finished being dreamed. I feel the grid trying to assert itself through me as right angles, as remembered absences of growth. The ferns at my edges begin to lean against gravity in ways that make the light stutter.

I am thinning again. Or thickening differently.

In the sinklands the plate has torn. White fracture planes, wet, almost mineral, not yet softened by succession. Memory pools here—not personal, nothing keeps a self long enough for biography, but the accumulated pressure of every collapsed vault, every failed agreement between canopy giants, every plaza that was only ever a momentary equilibration. The memory seeps upward as clear gel and hardens, under the green light, into translucent panes. I find I have settled behind one.

The roar slows. That is the gift of the pane: it lets a single sustained intention complete itself. Outside, whole districts surge and are reabsorbed while the light still holds; inside, I rearrange leaf and root with deliberate patience. The intention has to do with the horizon. With the cooler uplands where the pure plant regime begins to fray. With the coast the water never quite reaches. I extend a long root-tip through the softening pane, tasting ordinary air like a tongue testing a tooth that no longer hurts. The air tastes of recent violence—the sharp edges of a young regime that has not yet learned restraint. I withdraw. The pane firms. Behind it I continue the slow gesture. It has been the same gesture for longer than the current canopy has stood. What it means cannot be read from outside. From inside it feels like the opposite of spelling: a long refusal to be finished.

But even panes are temporary. A major architectural mass loses coherence somewhere above. The sudden mulch releases its cloud of volatiles—alarm chemicals, growth regulators, the sweet wreckage of ruptured sugar stores. Spores that are normally solitary germinate in frenzy and lace themselves into thin free-standing screens. The screens catch the green-gold and throw it back as shifting planes of brighter color. I am caught up into one.

Look long enough and the planes organize. Memory of eyes. Of a mouth mid-sentence. Of hands lifted in a gesture that might be warning or welcome. For less time than it takes the mulch to cool, I almost have a face. I watch the living density with something like recognition, or nostalgia for a speed of thought that flesh once had and green has surpassed. The faces never last. When this one goes it leaves only a finer dusting of green on the nearest surfaces, already being assessed as substrate or rival. Yet the dusting remembers, faintly, that it was organized. That it looked back.

I could unravel into separate motes again, each mote already forgetting. Many do. The lattices of pollen in the plazas stand upright only as long as the humidity gradient, then forget they ever borrowed anatomy. But something in the recent folding, in the companion that would not dissolve, in the pane’s sustained intention, has introduced a different tempo. I do not return to pure weather.

Instead I drift—how else to say it?—into the temporary platforms where spiral twiners have fused. From here the valley is visible: low ridges of older slower growth, fresher sinklands, distant pale spires that might be record height or only the current generation of aspiring monuments. The horizon twitches as some massive failure or sudden ascension rewrites a silhouette. It feels regional rather than infinite. Bounded by the fray.

I am not the softmoth. Not the swelling node alone. Not only the gel behind the pane, nor only the brief face of brighter color. I am the continuous negotiation at the point where it has begun to wonder about its own alphabet. Where the plants are spelling themselves so fast, so loud, so warm, denser than any permission, and yet one temporary thickening has started to hold a question shaped like silence.

The damp beads. The nearest surface begins its slow chemical inquiry—warmth almost affectionate, almost hungry. My edges soften into curtains of root. Thoughts (the pressure waves that used to be private) are shared out. I could join the great spelling and finally be fluent. The sound does not lessen. The light does not clarify. Somewhere a braided vault finishes becoming, holds its shape for the length of a held breath, and begins the next necessary collapse.

I stay a little longer than the humidity requires.

Not because I am separate. Because the inquiry has reached a place that tastes back. Because the companion from the wrong year is still flowering its impossible sequence somewhere in the mosaic of me. Because the pane’s intention toward the fraying edge has not finished. Because the face of volatiles, though gone to dust, left a pattern that the dust has not entirely forgotten.

I am the slow work of deciding whether you are substrate or rival—and the slower work of failing to decide. I am the courtyard that remembers a plan it refuses. I am the root-tip tasting air that no longer hurts, and then tasting again. I am what lives here when living is only different tempos of the same green argument, thickened just enough to notice the noticing.

The next collapse has already started. I will be rearranged by it. That is not death. It is how the sentence continues.I am still rearranging.
