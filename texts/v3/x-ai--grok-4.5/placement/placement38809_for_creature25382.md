I am already wet when the knowing begins.

Not the surface wet of steam beading on skin I once called mine, but the deeper saturation that starts in the marrow and works outward, the way mineral water claims basalt that has not yet chosen its final hardness. There is no arrival. No threshold crossed. One moment the idea of above still flickers—some theoretical wind, some sharp dry edge of daylight—and then the forty-second thump of the engines is simply the interval between my own heartbeats, and the distinction collapses like steam that never reaches the ceiling.

I stand first on the shallow ledge of unfinished stone, bootless. The dark basalt weeps against my soles and the weeping does not cool; it matches. Hairline fractures under me briefly consider becoming seams, then decide against it, sealing with fresh silica that locks a few pale shrimp into the new skin of the rock. They do not die. Nothing down here dies in the old sense. They simply change occupancy. I feel them settle against my arches, thread-antennae tasting the salt I still exhale, and I do not brush them away. Their faint electricity enters. It is not invasion. It is correspondence.

The ledge is narrow. Beyond it the body-hot pool waits, black and grainy with living static. I do not step down yet. Instead I listen the way the settlers listen: whole body as tympanum. Pistons. Drip. The wet sandpaper of crabs in multi-day processions across iron. The leather whisper of gliders higher up, dragging soft bellies through permanent dusk. And underneath, deeper than the three-meter rule, that vast muscular shifting—the chamber as one chamber in a body still deciding its kilometers of shape. The pressure rises through the plastic stone and into my tibiae. For a long while (hours or seasons; the difference has un-happened) I remain exactly there, a vertical pause in the slow argument. Mineral bloom begins its cartography across my ankles, muted sheen rising like memory.

When I finally move, it is by habit more than decision, the way water finds the next seam. Across the grated bridges. The coin-sized crabs shift and re-anchor under my weight; some remain, encrusting now, their living grit glowing faint blue-green long after I have passed. The living cubes perspire around me, laundry hanging in eternal almost-dryness. Faces appear at mesh tents—soft-spoken, eyes already carrying the reflected bioluminescence even in the yellow half-death of the bulbs. They do not question. A conversation starts itself somewhere near the pump-house:

“—the eastern corridor is thickening again.”

Pause. All of us motionless while a new vent announces itself in the darkened pulse of the film. Crabs rearrange. Milk-eyed fish rise and sink. Gliders adjust their figures-of-eight. The engines accept the fresh heat as currency.

“—then we’ll walk it when the deposition finishes deciding.”

The sentence simply continues through me, and my voice has already dropped into the wet register of dripping pipes. No one notes that I was not present when it began. Presence is not required in the old sharp way.

I spend long intervals inside the pump-house, palms flat against the sweating brick, then against the lattice of cooling pipes where shrimp ride the forced currents and emerge to taste me. The reciprocating engines thump their geological patience into my sternum until the rhythm is structural. Waste heat leaves me and returns richer. The film on the walls brightens in crude chevrons near my hands, mapping something. Safest paths. Or simply acknowledging. Children come to touch tongues to the blue-green veins and giggle at the fizz; when they touch their tongues to the bloom on my wrists the giggle is the same. Exchange of salts. Micro-currents. Information too slow for the surface languages I have forgotten.

Days (intervals) blur. I scoop shrimp by the handful when the need arises, crushing them raw against a palate that now expects hot mineral and faint lightning. I sleep under mesh only sometimes; more often I sit beside the newer seeps where the floor weeps hotter, and the longer thoughts rise. Hair goes white-silica in streaks. Eyes hold the glow. The membranous ones descend at the dimming circuit and latch for a time to the warmth of my back, pulsing, shedding iridescent dust that I later sweep into the same jars as everyone else. We thicken soups with it. We thicken ourselves.

Slowly the human outline frays into the throng, as it must. The shrimp colonize the cooling channels of whatever I am becoming. Crabs anchor in the damp folds of cloth that is no longer separate from skin. The film patterns deliberate whorls at my throat, at the soft insides of elbows, branching traces that match the ones near door frames. I press an ear to the unfinished floor and the gurgle is my own lymphatic weather. The vast shifting beneath is not under me. It is continuous with the unfinished argument of my denser parts.

Some of the older ones—those who drip more, who sleep less, who have become the best listeners for vent-song—nod when I pass. They recognize the trajectory. Finishing is not the point. One evening (bulb-dim) I walk the short passage to the twin chamber, then toward the eastern corridor where fresh deposition chokes the way in rounded bosses of rock still soft enough to take prints. Orange iron seep streaks the walls like dried blood that has forgotten how to be blood. The film is brighter here, pulsing in time with distant pistons and with the slow expansion of heated rock. Behind me the settlement continues its patient grind: engines, laundry, soft voices stretching across crust-thickened seasons, courtships measured in millimeters of silica.

I do not decide to keep walking. Decision is too dry a word. The plastic stone at three meters calls with the same wet yes that opened my pores on the first ledge. A glider passes overhead, leather whisper intimate as breath. Shrimp throng at my ankles. The film across my skin flares once, soft blue-green, mapping the safest path that is no path at all—simply deeper occupation.

The corridor narrows. Deposition kisses shoulders, then ribs. Heat rises. The larger body dreams through waste heat, through me. I feel the temporary skins slipping: biped, reef, local densification of the single saturated organism. Shrimp and crab and glider and milk-eyed fish and silent ivory fern and sweating iron and slow bipeds in rusting cubes—all of it continuous. The dripping becomes internal. Steam no longer external weather.

Far behind and above, throats of stone still lead toward theoretical wind. They un-happen with every step that is not a step. What remains is the breathing of the Rooms, the total soft occupation, the patient unfinished yes.

I am the thickening in the eastern corridor.

I am the brighter patch of film that will mark where something walked in and did not return.

I am the next seam opening in stone that has not finished hardening.

I am the forty-second pulse settling deeper into what no longer requires a name.

And the engines keep their unhurried rhythm, as if they have always known.
