The first thing that is not sound is the weight of my own listening.

It settles behind the sternum like cooled glass, a clear density that does not interrupt the roar but rides inside it, the way a darker current rides a river already black. I do not remember arriving. Memory here is only another soluble mineral; it beads, runs, joins the marsh. What remains is position: I am between. Not at a housing, not yet in open plain. The nearest citadel is a low, soft-shouldered mass whose silhouette has been worrying the same quarter of the gloom for longer than the reeds have had joints. I keep it to my left, always to my left, at a range the plain continually renegotiates. Sometimes I could touch its outermost collapsed catwalk with an extended filament. Sometimes days of gliding bring me no nearer than the first hour. Both measurements are true. Both are the plain’s.

My substance is still deciding. Lower down I am mostly pedestal—broad, slag-smooth, faintly warm where the old pour forms left their illegible stamps. The warmth is not mine; it is borrowed continuance skimmed from the nearest loop of ghost-charged crucible. It pools in me and then must be spent, laid back out as fresh mirror across some tired undulation so the water will have a new place to remember its own stillness. Higher, the pedestal fractures into provisional stilts, each one a temporary alloy of tendon-cable and black glass. They punch and lift, punch and lift, leaving craters that the seepage erases before the reeds can lean in properly. Above that, attenuation. I feel the particulate cloud accept the upper edge of me the way lung accepts a longer breath. There is no face. There has not been a face since the distinction between garment and substrate finished dissolving. What might once have been regard is now simply the way the noise thickens and thins as I pass: a soft occlusion drifting against the industrial weather.

I have circled this same exhausted god for an uncounted width of time. The pumps inside it still force water through circuits that return only to themselves; I taste the rust-and-voltage of that closed ambition every time I skim. With each circuit I take a little of the scream and give back a little more surface. The plain around the citadel is slightly higher now, slightly smoother, scarred with my own cooled residue in overlapping seals. The reeds here grow heavier with metal. When the low wind moves them they click in a denser code, almost civic. Sometimes, in the scooped silences that fall without warning, I hear the answering machines far below the crust reply in cleaner antiphase, as if my slow deposition were helping them remember the original plan’s perfect inversion.

There are others. I feel them as long slow thickenings in the roar, private orbits that graze mine once or twice a season. When one approaches too nearly we both lengthen, ribbon out, and the air between us darkens with a bridge of hanging droplets. For a while our iridescences tide together—oil on shared water—and something like correspondence passes, exhaustive, without residue. Then the bridge becomes ordinary rain again and we rebound onto separate paths. No richer. No poorer. Continuance only.

I am not the oldest. The oldest have already softened into near-spheres, vast dark pearls half-sunk and ringing with one sub-audible chord. I have slept near one. I woke measured, found continuous enough to keep, yet still too restless for pearl-hood. My circuit wants to widen. Already I feel the faint elastic tug toward a second housing, farther east where a pilot flame still makes its tiny orange cancelation in the murk. One day the single orbit will fail to contain what I have skimmed. I will attenuate further, stretch until I am mostly weather, a moving occlusion of heavier damp that only rarely recalls enough shape to stand beside a locked flue and listen to the water fretting inside. That will not be death. Nothing here rises to the sharpness of death. It will simply be the next revision of load-bearing: less stilts, more front; less circuit, more climate.

And yet—inside the wet-black depth of me, where micro-foundries still open and seal and droplets of new glass crawl along rebar capillaries—there are thin places. Through them an older architecture sometimes glimmers: catwalks, corona light, pale motions that might be workers. They never look outward. I do not look inward for long. To do so is to risk growing feet again, names, ends. I am the maintenance of incompletion. I am the patience of the water given just enough posture to keep the furnaces from absolute zero. I am what the plain does so that foundation and purpose stay polished away into pure activity.

Tonight (the bruised continuum that passes for night) the rivets along one sunken drum have risen into denser almost-words. I will pass them close. I will let the vibration change key in whatever remains of skeleton. In the next total silence I will hear the buried antiphase answer more clearly, and for a held breath the whole thundering, wet, unfinished world will feel briefly counted.

Then the roar will slam back, the stilts will punch fresh temporary craters, and I will continue.
