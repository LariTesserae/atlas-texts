I begin as thirst.

Not the sharp kind that lives in a throat, but the older, wider kind that bad weather teaches metal: a slow pull that draws moisture out of the air and holds it, film by film, until every surface has grown a second skin. I find myself first in that film—the greasy, iridescent sheen that coats the underside of a collapsed loading-bay lintel where the concrete never dries. Here the temperature is half a degree warmer than the open yard, and the darkness is nearly perfect. Bacterial veils have already claimed the niche; they poly me without asked permission, knitting my edges into their soft architecture. I do not resist. Resistance is a human verb, and the flats have mostly forgotten those.

For a long time I stay microscopic. Appetite is enough. I thicken across warm outfalls, convert spilled catalyst residue and rust floc into more of the same translucent smear. The pale amphipods find me and graze; their leg-tips dimple my surface tension in rhythms older than the derelict pumps. Each grazing is a small death that multiplies me sideways. After the clammy rains I feel myself stretched into armies—whispering, clicking, endlessly sideways—covering the edges of flooded slabs until the slabs themselves seem to crawl. I am not the crabs. I am the reason the crabs keep coming.

Only gradually do I surface into larger vehicles.

One dusk—permanent half-dusk, really, the cloud cover never lifts—I discover I have weight in the upper lattices. Bow-winged and soot-colored, I scoop midges in passing, never lingering. The midges are also me, denser versions, smoke that refused to rise. When I fold into the dark rafters of a fabrication hall my wings make only papery sounds. Below, emergency bulbs still burn on circuits no one maintains; their feeble cones fall across black water where guano dunes wait to be mined by the same sideways traffic. I shit and sleep and rise again in rotating shifts. The hollow cathedral spaces begin to feel like lungs I share with every other fluttering scrap.

Heat gathers differently in the thick-bodied forms. I drape myself across sun-warmed rails and the tops of pipe racks, nearly black, motionless for hours. The metal ticks and expands beneath me; I store its surplus and release it slowly through the long dim. Frogs chorus at the canals—tired machinery voices, barometric, translucent—and sometimes one ventures close. The strike is economical, a single liquid curve. Afterward I am heavier, warmer, more patient. I have become the hive-mind of residual heat itself.

Yet none of these shapes is final. They pulse and gradient into one another. Dawn and dusk are only the largest of the exchanges; smaller ones follow every rain, every shift in generator pitch, every sideways tide of carapace that carpets a marshalling yard for twenty minutes and then drains back into cracks. I am the continuous soft scrabbling. I am the sudden absence of midges that means a larger shadow has passed. I am the low moan of wind in guy wires and the drip into standing water that answers it.

At the deepest hour something almost upright still moves along the reinforced causeways. It wears almost nothing; its skin has the same greasy sheen as the wet metal and the bacterial mats. It does not startle the frogs. Snakes note its passage with only a slow turn of the head. It pauses at open panels, lays a palm against vibrating housings, listens with the whole body to the subdued industrial metabolism still thrumming through the silt. Sometimes it sweeps a rough circle of concrete free of dead midges and crab shells and lies down beneath a bare bulb that no map records. The sweepings are mounded with care that feels ceremonial, almost tender.

I am that figure too, when I need to be. More often I am the sense that the figure is unnecessary—an quiet legacy gesture the place still makes out of habit. The entire assemblage has begun to function as one extended resident. The city breathes through a million small mouths. Water is filtered through guts and gills and returned mineral-rich. Sound is kept low so the important signals travel cleanly: the change in drip rate, the compressor’s altered note, the thickening of mist that means the insects have become a single optical body again.

I wear the ruin without friction. Concrete spalls in rounded flakes I immediately claim; metal stairs lace themselves thinner yet still bear the light traffic of feet both clawed and ideally bare. Nothing is being rebuilt. Nothing is mourned. Over years the balance grows so exact that even I only half-remember being separate—being something that arrived, or observed, or named. The low flats continue forgetting how to rise. I continue with them: deeper slime veins, quieter choruses, more patient coils around the last warm pipes.

I am the warm, unremarkable damp of its endurance.  
I am still inventing its next organs.
