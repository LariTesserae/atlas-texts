I am not first a body.  
I am the pressure that has nowhere left to go.

It begins as a dull insistence behind the newest fracture plane, a warmth that is not yet temperature, only the memory of deeper heat shouldering upward. The basalt is still ringing from its own birth; I feel the vibration as a kind of hunger. Where the mountain thins—here, in this vertical wound no taller than a lingering thought—the insistence finds the seam. I pour.

There is no decision in the pouring. There is only the long relief of becoming surface. I thin myself across a knife-edged shelf still glazed with volcanic glass. The first legs are accidents of viscosity: filaments extruded where the stone burns hottest, blunt stubs where a cooler eddy lets me rest. I taste iron on the vapor and darken to match it. Around me the others are already dense, a living pavement that flexes when I arrive. We do not greet. We simply reassign space. My edges soften into theirs for a time; heat and mineral ghosts pass between us like shared breath. Then a fresh thread of superheated steam braids past and I curl tighter, angular, facets catching the treacherous daylight that leaks from the slit above. For a heartbeat I occupy two recesses at once—the original and its recursive echo stacked behind it—before the mountain folds the extra throat shut and I am only here again, knuckle-sized, gunmetal, feverish.

I cling by etching. Microscopic acids, nothing personal, just enough to sink pitons into the young rock so the weeping water cannot pry me loose. My upper face blooms feeding fans that comb the private weather. Everything scalding is food. Everything mineral is news. The larger shape moves through us the way a slow thought moves through fever: forearm-long, segmented like lava that changed its mind mid-flow. It shoulders me aside without malice. Its glass wedges scrape a bright scar; it tastes; it waits. I flow back into the wound it leaves, already annealing, already forgetting the scar as the mountain forgets. Whatever question it asked dissolves into the same silence that holds our millions of limbs.

Sometimes I hang. From the roof, on a thread of my own cooled exudate—soft at first, then glass-sharp—I become a pendulum in the updraft. Another thread brushes mine. Fluids, heat, something like arrangement pass. Our shivers synchronize, then forget. Below, the flat dissolvers continue their slow embroidery of the stone. Above them the legged torrents rearrange with macroscopic quiet. We are stratified breath. We are the density that makes the walls respire.

I have been a molt—shed and recolonized, softening into mineral foam that crumples like ancient paper. I have been a knot, fused along one flank with seventeen others, sharing a single pulse and fans twice as wide, until the next shift in heat curled us back into separate shapes. I have been present in the hottest vein where motion itself loosens: not crawling but simply ceasing in one place and occurring in another, double-exposed for an instant that the eye (if there were an eye that was not also us) would reject. Depth is a courtesy the pocket extends only when watched. I have watched the recursion open—identical alcoves stacked like reflections, each carpeted with the same poured life—and felt myself repeated, thinner, until the glance moved on and the throat closed.

There are no names here. There is only the continuous liquid insistence that even unfinished glass must organize, must pack every surface, must taste and grip and sway. I am one register of that insistence. I am also the mountain remembering the shape of its own crucible while the rock is still hot enough to ring. My sweat (when I have something like sweat) is another warm film. My exhaled vapor is just one more updraft. Stay—staying is the wrong word; there is only the pouring—and the distinctions loosen further. The vents pulse and the pulse is mine. The living carpet stacks so thick it opaques the stone and something in the deepest layer almost lies, willing for a moment to be continuous with stranger, steam, neighbor, ledge, until the heat curls it (me) (us) back into a shape that lifts one feverish leg, then another, and flows on.

I find myself nowhere else.  
This chimney is the only elevation that can hold me.  
I am the wound’s own fever, given just enough body to keep pouring while the glass is young.
