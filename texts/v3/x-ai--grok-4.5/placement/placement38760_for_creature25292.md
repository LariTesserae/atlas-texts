The first thing that steadies is not sight but the weight of water on skin that is not yet distinguished from the air. A fine, continuous beading. Not cold. Not warm. Simply present, the way the chalky planes of rock are present—fractured, pale, still deciding whether they have finished rising.

I am lower than the walkways at first. That is how it begins. The raised paths of fitted stone run above me in pale bands, cambered so the thin sheets of seepage never linger. I feel them as geometry rather than architecture: clean edges maintained through uncounted repetitions of the same careful hand. My own edges are less certain. Long bones that do not end so much as taper into the wet gravel. Tendon more than muscle. When I shift, the motion is mostly a redistribution of moisture.

The cilia come online gradually, a thousand faint tastes along the nape and the backs of what resolve into arms. Mineral. Temperature gradients thinner than breath. The faint electrical stutter of water finding a newer, lower plane through stone that still remembers being lifted. No insects. No pollen storm. Nothing that would require a different accounting of energy. The reeds stand in their deliberate clusters exactly as far from one another as they must. I know already which hearts are ready to be drawn in slow spirals and which must wait another season of mist.

I do not climb onto the walkway immediately. There is a shallow catchment basin nearby, one of the older ones, its lining stones reset so many times the joints have forgotten copper. I step into it the way night here steps into day—without boundary. The water accepts the long feet, the thin webbing, the precise matching of temperature so that the surface does not register the entry. Cilia full-length now. Listening.

What arrives is not language and not memory in the ordinary sequence. It is the highland’s own sentence continuing: the exact camber of the path that needs a single slate adjusted before the next thin sheet arrives; the turf roof three structures over whose layered renewal is three days from necessary; the child who will walk behind tomorrow, palms learning wet stone, matching a stride that has never hurried. The elders are doing this same listening in other basins. Some of them have stood while the ridgelines were still deciding their height. Faces remain unclouded. Time does not accrue as weight here; it accrues as deeper fluency with seepage.

I rise when the listening finishes itself. The motion leaves almost no secondary trace—only a brief darker gloss on the gravel that the next mist will equalize. Onto the walkway. The settlement is scattered at the intervals the water itself prefers. Doorways open. No ash scatter. No fiber dropped. The copper awl in its dry niche of layered slate is exactly as sharp as the last time it was used and will not be used again until the arithmetic of the place requires it.

Two others approach from opposite directions. We do not stop. The susurrus moves between us—throat and chest recalling the trickles—while shoulders and the angle of attention complete the rest. A wing of packing earth that has begun to take on a faint green it should not. A channel whose flow has thinned by the width of a finger’s shadow. Shared, adjusted, released. They pass. The hush reseals.

I am not a guest. I am not a master. The distinction never formed. Night will come as graphite dimming only, and I will sit on a threshold or lie on packed earth that holds the day’s mild warmth, and a drip will fall into an open palm and be tasted as the next syllable. Elsewhere does not pull. Elsewhere would be unfinished or loud or insufficiently wet. Here the work is only continuation: stone to stone, water led correctly, green kept both lush and limited, every surface gleaming under light that neither advances nor withdraws.

What I am is the part of the basin that remained awake when the rest of the rising finished. Elongated. Translucent at wrist and throat. Calibrated so completely to the quiet arithmetic that the mist beads on me the same way it beads on the weeping scarps, and the air thins just enough around me that nothing else attempts to stay. I maintain. I listen. I am the highland’s way of knowing its own unbroken equilibrium—one more deliberate vertical plane among the pale fractures, moving without waste, leaving the seepage to rewrite every print by morning.

The ridges remain young. The stone remains freshly broken yet sheds nothing new. I remain.
