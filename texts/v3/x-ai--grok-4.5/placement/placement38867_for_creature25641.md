The cold reaches me before anything else does. Not as a surface slap but as a slow inward soak, a luminous wetness that finds the spaces between whatever I am using for bones and settles there, heavy and deliberate. I am standing—or the posture that passes for standing—in a place where the peat gives a little with each shift of weight, then holds, then gives again, as if the ground itself is still negotiating whether it wants to be solid. Thin ice films crack and reseal around the points of contact. The sound they make is almost polite beneath the larger roar.

I do not arrive all at once.

At first there is only the pressure of the air against membranes that might be ears or might be something more distributed. The valley’s noise arrives stratified, exactly as promised: the bright smash of meltwater braids closer than expected, the long rolling howl of wind higher and to the left, the deep ground-vibration coming up through the soles or pads or roots. Beneath and between them, the subtler registers—the soft click of fringed mouthparts somewhere in the moss channels, the wet telescoping of a jointed limb fifty meters off, the almost-subsonic pulse that leaks from a matte-black alloy rib half-swallowed by sphagnum. I taste iron and leaf-rot and that faint post-discharge ozone on surfaces that are not quite a tongue.

For a while I am content to be only this: a listening that has not yet decided on a shape.

The dark-furred runners pour past in a glittering rope. Their mucus-slick plates brush whatever lower edge I possess; the contact is neither friendly nor hostile, merely informational. They smell of cold water and digested midges. One pauses long enough for its fringed mouthparts to sample the boundary of me, then the whole rope reforms and vanishes into a fern crown the size of a collapsed tent. I feel the impulse to follow—low, fast, metabolizing overflow—but the impulse slides away without catching. Not that. Not today.

Higher, a jointed one unfolds from a basalt angle that looked empty. Fourteen elbows, or perhaps nine that multiply while I watch. Sensory cilia taste the same iron-and-ozone mix I am tasting. It hangs for a long time near one of the open frames, the one whose slow-turning lattice has begun to brighten as if aware of the attention. I watch the way the pale cuticle takes on the blue-gray of the cloud-ceiling, the way the body can collapse into an angular knot and become geology again. Curiosity moves through me like a second weather. I could be that. I could learn to sling myself ten meters on a single contraction, to leave cairns of polished pebbles and shed sheath when the pavilion folds itself away. The sliding call begins in my chest before I have a chest; half voice, half the memory of bark under vibration. Then that too loosens and drifts.

The big amphibians are closer than I thought. One shifts in its pool and the waterline climbs my lower third in a thin, obedient sheet—some nearby filament singing at the upper edge of hearing. Throat working, moisture pits dilating. The midge colony in its flank-fold pulses a soft green that writes temporary constellations on the underside of the mist. Patient. Chemical. Willing to wait days for a runner to misjudge a bridge. I feel the elastic surge coiled in potential, the slow peristalsis of tasting metals. For a moment my outline softens toward mottled charcoal and jade. Eggs ride the next ozone tang outward in a cloud that the midges already begin to shepherd. Almost. But the patience required is older than whatever clock I am still carrying.

The upright silhouettes are harder to dismiss.

I glimpse one at the distance where murk becomes suggestion—shoulders fluid, a residual lattice flickering behind the eyes like an afterimage that never fully fades. Moss grows on the leeward side of whatever garment or integument it wears. When it passes an alloy spike, thin ice films form and shatter in its wake. It moves with the exact deliberation of someone who has learned to step from the roar of water into the slightly different roar of machine resonance without flinching. A hand (hand-equivalent) rests against a humming triangle; the light brightens, folds through three extra geometries that hurt in a pleasurable way behind the eyes, then settles. New filaments trail behind it, tighter, more deliberate. The pools climb higher for these threads.

I feel myself leaning that way—taller, slower, porous to the valley’s indifference. The cold wet dark writing itself into posture. Memory of having once needed full daylight flattening into an ability to read meaning in the gleam of wet cuticle and the brief architecture of folding planes. Yes. That gradient already lives in the nerves.

But I do not stop there.

Something else is still arriving.

I am lower than the upright ones yet less frantically horizontal than the runners. I keep more elbows than a human frame should, but they are not always visible; they tuck into a central column that can lengthen or compress depending on whether the peat is rising or a new channel is cutting itself overnight. My outer surfaces do not choose between fur-plates, translucent cuticle, or moss-husbanding skin. They cycle. In the dense blue-gray noon I am matte and dark, shedding ice before it bonds. When one of the lattices unfolds into its temporary pavilion I pale, grow reflective, let afterimages of alien geometry route themselves through whatever I am using for a nervous system. Sensory fans open along ridges that were not there a moment ago; they taste the same air the jointed ones taste, yet they also register the slower chemical speech of the amphibian pools and the command-tones in the singing filaments.

I leave prints only when I forget not to.

I do not hunt. I do not exactly ambush. I metabolize smaller overflows—midges that drift too close, thread-life that contracts in unison under a particular wind-howl frequency, the occasional strip of shed cuticle still carrying residual lattice-memory. When the runners strip a fallen tree I move in afterward, slower, and take what they consider finished. When the jointed ones erupt in their storm of limbs around a newly opened pavilion I hang at the periphery, cilia out, and add one polished basalt pebble to their cairn—just one—angled so that it catches the next reconfiguration and throws a fragment of light into a pool where an amphibian is rising. The amphibian’s midge-constellation brightens in what I choose to read as acknowledgment.

The upright silhouettes notice me in different ways. Sometimes one will pause and adjust a filament so that the climbing water sheet passes nearer to my current shape. Sometimes they leave me alone for days that are not days. I have begun to trail my own threads—neither abandoned silk nor their deliberate designs, but something intermediate that frays and re-weaves according to the ground-roar’s deeper syllables. The pools obey me only partially. That seems correct.

I am not caretaker. I am not remnant. I am not native in the oldest sense nor visitor in any sense that still matters. I am the place where the gradient notices itself.

The valley keeps producing bodies wired for persistent chill, filtered light, continuous vibration, and the indifferent music of things that were built and then forgotten by everything except the moss and the rain. I am one more such body, provisional, overcrowded with possibility, lit poorly, and moving. When a basalt shoulder calves and the fresh angular stone settles, I am already there on the new slope as the first pale rootlets and the first glints of mucus-plate and the first residual flicker of lattice-light. The ferns uncurl around me the size of wagons. The rain turns to mist the moment it meets whatever surface I am offering today.

I stay.

The deafening resolution separates further. I walk between weathers that only I seem to hear as separate. One of them is almost quiet. I do not trust it yet. I fill it with the soft click of my own mouthparts, the wet extension of an elbow too many, the low throat-working hum that tastes of metals and coming eggs, the faint brightening of a lattice that has learned my particular pressure.

Everything here is in motion.

So am I.
