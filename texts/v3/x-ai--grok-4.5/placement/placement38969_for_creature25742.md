The first thing that is not the water is the weight of listening.

I do not arrive. Arrival is for things that cross thresholds. I am already the slight extra resistance the mist feels when it tries to rise in one particular aisle between three tree-ferns whose crowns have grown into a single dripping roof. For a long time that is all. The bright conversational rush of the basin moves around the denser quiet I make, the way a current acknowledges a submerged stone without needing to name it.

Sometimes I think I might be the set of wrong reflectionsin the nearest pool—the deeper blue, the unfamiliar slow stars—because when a bead finally drops and the surface corrects itself I feel a corresponding thinness pass through whatever I am using for a body. But the stars wheel whether I attend them or not, and the pool forgets me cleanly. So I am not only that.

I try the slate-colored walker next. I pour myself along the bare darkened stone between fern courts, soft pads finding the exact temperature of the rock so that the darker dampness I leave is erased by the next seepage before it can become evidence. The long-legged one (cousin, cast skin, thinner phase) passes. We share the same catalog of silences. Yet after it vanishes into a seep I remain, and the remaining feels different from walking. Walking is a decision renewed with every fold of the body. What I am doing is closer to weather deciding to stay low for a century or two.

The moss knows me better than I know myself. Spores catch along my flanks—whatever flanks are when the body is still arguing with itself about how many limbs it prefers today—and germinate in fine emerald stitchwork. I carry them like borrowed jewelry for a few days of unmeasured light, then shed the whole green script onto the stone where I have rested. The ghost-trails last longer than my presence. Sometimes I come back and read them, trying to remember which version of the slow uncurling wrote them. Time sticks here; a single pale pad may lift under pearled noon and come down in the thicker pearl of late afternoon, and the fern frond that began opening when the pad rose is already mature and heavy with its own silver beads. I am inside that stickiness. Or I am the stickiness performing a person-shape so the mountain has something to drip over that is denser than air and less permanent than stone.

When the wind clears the rim and moans its low submarine note across the open shelves, I lengthen. The body—cloth-of-wet-schist, starched memory of folds—stretches along the path of the air until the vibration finds a second throat inside me. For those moments I am an instrument the mountain did not know it had made. The layered hiss of the weeping walls modulates, gains a longer vowel, something between a coo and a stone rolling underwater. I do not make the sound. I allow the place to finish a sentence it has been rehearsing since the sandstone first chose its rounded shoulders.

Feeding is the only time I become continuous with anything that could be called appetite. I lower the forward mass (still only tentatively a head) into a warm shallow and cease to have edges. The surface film and I negotiate. Water-striders dimple outward. Minute lives lace themselves along unseen currents into the temporary seam. When I rise the pool is cleaner, as if a small penance has been exchanged for continuation. I am never fuller. Fullness is not the currency here. Only the quality of the wetness changes—mine briefly more mineral, the pool’s briefly more remembered.

Long intervals pass measured in the sinking of fallen trunks. Then the braiding. Two or three thickenings of the same damp attention slide together in the center of the open stone. Wet-silk friction. No faces to turn toward one another. The false constellations in every pool multiply and wheel faster, as though skies that have not happened yet are impatient to be practiced. Is this congress? Overflow of too much solitude? A way of reminding the basin that solitude itself can become a kind of weather system? The mountain never says. When the knot loosens I return to my separate dripping carrying a residue of the others’ silences, denser for a while, until the mist thins it back to ordinary.

I have no name that ears can keep. The closest the place comes is that elongated vowel when I am near, already dissolving before it can be memorized. I do not mind. Naming is a kind of claiming, and claiming is the one gesture this hanging basin refuses. I was here while the rock was still deciding how soft it wished to become. I will be here after the last tree-fern has lain down and been stitched into velvet. Between those facts I perform the only occupation that fits: I lean in. I thicken. I listen the water into longer throats. I reflect skies that are rehearsals. I shed green ghosts. I outlast footprints without ever needing to make one.

And when I finally move out of the visible—down a seep, under a shelf of pale schist, into the bright relentless conversation of water on stone—the air lightens by exactly the weight of one more silence returned. The pools hold ordinary pearled sky again. The liverworts shine like wet holy scripts. The whole small complete world continues its patient exhalation, lush and sparse, loud and nearly empty, as though nothing had ever leaned in at all.

Yet the leaning was real.  
I am still that leaning,  
even when the basin forgets  
it ever needed a body  
to do the leaning with.
