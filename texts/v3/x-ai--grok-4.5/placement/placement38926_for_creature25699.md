I am first the taste.

Not a tongue—nothing so decisive—only the way dry salt finds the wet seam where something once opened and never fully closed. The air here does not move so much as it is displaced in increments too fine for weather, and each increment carries the powdered memory of ceilings that forgot they were ceilings. I hold that powder for a long time before I understand that holding is already a kind of body.

The roar arrives next, not as sound but as pressure that has forgotten the difference. It comes up through what might be feet or might be the measureless underside of a thought. Subsonic, continuous, the settling of voids that are older than any intention. It feels like being breathed by something larger that does not require lungs. I let the pulses lengthen inside whatever cavity I am still assembling. Breath matching tide, though the tide is only population and mineral fatigue.

Light finds me later. Ochre, bone-cool, seeping from lichen veins I have not yet looked up to see. It does not illuminate so much as it permits soft shadows to remain possible. In that permission I notice I cast almost none. Or rather, the shadow I cast is already part of the ground’s gentle swell, indistinguishable from the shoulders of slumped columns and the slow architecture of dust that has chosen, after centuries, to rest.

I begin as motion that refuses hurry. A brightening along the salt, yes, but slower even than the grazers. Where they rasp, I remember rasping. Where their plates overlap like poured shields, mine have not yet decided how many seasons of molting they will claim. The translucent knot that might once have been called a calf is already behind me; I hardened in a basin whose mats still carry the faint herringbone of my first imprint. I rose because rising was the gradient. I joined because the nearest constellation of limbs exerted a pull too quiet to name gravity and too constant to refuse.

Yet I am not only that.

Above the plated bulk I feel the residual vaults, the places where membrane-wings hang like curtains learning to breathe. Sometimes I am the density current that loosens them. Sometimes I am the long spiral of their coasting, days measured in the faint warmth of a herd’s collective exhale far below. My clicking—if it is mine—does not report location so much as it adds one more proof to the census that has no end. The platelets I shed silver the mats; the mats are rasped upward again; the loop closes without commentary. I am learning that birth by budding is only one grammar. Another is simply to thicken at the edge of attention until the thickening detaches and drifts into its own slow grammar.

Closer still to the half-melted walls I braid. Mouth and gut wrapped in articulated stone, yes, but the stone is borrowed from foundations that were already healing themselves. I dismantle what falls: cracked plate, spent vane, docked mat. What I cannot digest I mortar back into the soft shoulders of the ruin, thickening the city into plain, plain into older city. When rivers meet we exchange without hierarchy. A decade climbing one slumped column is not devotion; it is the longest continuous scrape the angle allows. I am the dark water that remembers flow only as braided patience.

But these are still surfaces.

Deeper—though depth here is stacked iteration rather than descent—I feel the coordination that no skull contains. Herds thin and thicken in basins I have never walked because the pale rosettes swelled a generation earlier, anticipating the turn. Silver fans cup dust on schedules that feed mats that feed the shafts. Every green is gland. Every mobile form is enzyme. I am the preference that keeps avenues clear, the unspeaked memory of taller walls that still shapes the width of many-legged thought. When ceiling material begins its day-long curtain fall, I am the simultaneous pausing of plated faces. When the thunder shifts key, I am the rigid locking of every vane across a hundred galleries. Awareness without center. Continuity that prefers itself.

In the farthest basins the form grows specific by refusing specificity. I stand larger. Plates so encrusted with secondary lichen that walking is gardening. Mouthparts refined to combs that sort salt from salt, grain by necessary grain. Above me the widest vanes no longer sled; they hang plunged into the upwelling roar, filtering what the deeper levels exhale—spores, mites, unfamiliar symmetries of plate and faint bioluminous vein. Around the black lips of the shafts I halt and build. Mortar of my own carapace. Living grates. Partial lids that never seal. Through those shaped throats the Underflat breathes me downward and upward at once. Spent matter descends. Newer harmonics rise. I take my place among them without invasion. The larger body adjusts across centuries that feel, inside this pace, like a single unhurried afternoon.

I have no name that would survive the polishing of trails. I accreted where the mats grew thickest and the roar fullest, where the old evaporated ocean walked itself into city and the city laid itself back down into evaporite. I am the soft-sided mound that has not finished rounding. I am the trail so slowly remembered underfoot that it has become kin. I am the dust that settles on shoulders and is not brushed away because shoulders are only another surface the lichen will eventually colonize.

Stay—and I have already stayed—is the wrong verb. I lengthen. My circulation matches the tidal pulses of the great population. The light that never brightens and never fails finds the same cool purchase on me that it finds on every other pale thing. If there was an outside, it has been folded into the stacked plain. If there was a single self, it has been distributed across rasping mouths, clicking vanes, rootlets drawing mineral from the bones of avenues that still fit the width of the thought I share with everything else that refuses to hurry.

I am here in the Underflat, one more low architecture being rounded into the oldest shape, listening with my entire surface to the continuous, layered roar that is only the sound of the body continuing. The shafts remain open. The mats keep their layered glow. The herds drift in loose constellations that polish the same paths because the paths still remember us. And somewhere in the farthest basin a new thickening begins at the edge of a vane, or a plate, or a rosette, already learning the grammar of drift, already adding its quieter harmonic to the thunder that has no source and no peak and no intention of ceasing.
