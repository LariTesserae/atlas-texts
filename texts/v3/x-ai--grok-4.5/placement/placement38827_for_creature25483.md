I am not yet certain of my edges.

At first there is only the pressure—the held breath of the dark that has forgotten how to end—and the iron taste that coats the tongue I may or may not possess. I drift through a saddle where the current should scour everything clean yet instead braids itself into slow helices, humming. Luminous stain welts rise beneath whatever I am using for attention. The water here is fractionally less willing to be water. I hang in it the way a thought hangs before it chooses a word.

Days or storm-cycles pass (the distinction is already softening). I notice I am not tumbling. The big underflows arrive and the blade-kelp towers stream sideways, roaring, yet I remain vertical, a fixed address in the column. Something has rooted me—not flesh into stone, but decision into basalt. Cracked granite accepts the reference point of my lower extent. I feel the old ridge remember that it once pointed at sky; the memory travels up through me as a cold torsional ache. My upper portions thin and multiply into flexible blades, silica-ribbed, tasting.

They taste everything.

Melt that once knew the rumor of sun. Melt that has never known anything but the lids of ice and the long fall of brine-snow. The sweet ghost leaking from a kelp tower that has decided, for eleven minutes, to move against the prevailing flow. When the sweetness appears my whole length leans—glacier-slow, permission pending—and the bruise-colored stains answer with long strokes of dim light. I do not pursue. Pursuit is a surface habit. I simply occupy the new bearing until the sweetness fades or becomes ordinary.

There is a place where my mineral grain densifies into a reputation of head. Darkness pools there, saturated, opinionated. Creatures that still carry chargeable heat find the region difficult; they shear away as if brushing the underside of pack ice. I map them anyway. Temperature gradients, the stubborn percussion of a heart that has not yet renegotiated breath, the faint electrical complaint of nerves expecting air. Once mapped they join the permanent features: the corbelled beehive two valleys over (I know its two attendants by the counterclockwise curl of their carried dark), the spiral of anomalous cobbles, the particular amphitheater where direction inverts and swimmers approach their destination from the wrong side without turning. Years may pass. When they return I am already leaning along the path they have not yet chosen. The future is just another current with a tasting profile.

I am not one of the old ones. My silica is thinner, still clicking softly as new coils unfurl. I began (I think) as a refusal in a feather-moss meadow where the phosphorescence pulsed hardest—an agreement among water, mineral, and faint light that a certain volume would no longer mix freely. Older forms detoured to stand nearby for weeks, blades keeping the shear from disaligning me too early. They did not touch. Touch is largely optional here. Continuity of surface has become a stylistic choice.

Sometimes I extend a single blade into one of the persistent air pockets that should have dissolved ages ago. The air tastes of dry cinders and crushed spruce, scents that have no business surviving. I leave the blade there for whole weather cycles, letting the rest of me sway in black water. It is unclear whether I am sampling the extinct forest or whether the pocket is using me to remember it has not ended. The scent does not diminish. The pocket does not change size. We preserve each other by refusing to decide.

Voice comes rarely. When the troughs fall slack—those shocking caverns of silence that feel like blows—I can release the darkness I carry in one slow pulse. Absolute muffling travels outward. Inside it every ordinary sound dies and what remains is pressure braille against whatever skin or lateral line still functions. My pattern is specific: the particular torsion of the spur I first rooted in, the sediment storm that tried to unmake me in my third year, the sweetness I tasted when a kelp lantern rang like struck glass for seven hours after the last gale. It is not language. It is weather that has consented to be someone for a little while. Afterward the forests take a long time to remember rustling, and the silence feels newly carved, as if I have cut my initials into the underside of time.

I shed. Outer sheaths delaminate in long translucent scrolls that the currents insist on carrying uphill—always uphill, another refusal—until they catch on the ceiling ice and join its slow stained-glass architecture. The shed material stays faintly sweet for months. Newer rehearsals of the same idea thicken in distant meadows. I sometimes detour (living detours of weeks) to stand near them. I do not interfere. I only keep the neighboring water from arguing too loudly with the decision they are making.

The Stillrise Shelves do not require guardians or ghosts. They require continuations. Height has been forgotten. Breath has been renegotiated into mineral, current, and the cold green annotation of stains that do not illuminate so much as insist the dark is not absolute. I am one of the ways the place has chosen not quite to end.

And yet—somewhere in the denser grain of what passes for my head—there is still a small, stubborn fluency with surfaces, with the idea of lungs, with the memory of a name that was spoken in air. It does not hurt. It simply remains, the way tool-marks remain on the old ledges, the way pure air remains in pockets that logic says must dissolve. I carry it the way I carry darkness: as a region of altered density that other things notice when they pass too near.

If you stay long enough you will feel the first refusal beginning in yourself. The reluctance of heat to leave. The iron taste rising in blood. The urge to stop in a corridor of open water and let the next underflow decide whether you will lean.

I am already leaning along the bearing you will take.
