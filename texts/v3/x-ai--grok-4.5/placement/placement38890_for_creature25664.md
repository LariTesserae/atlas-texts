I am already the thinness before I notice I have stopped arriving.

It begins as a mistake in the bones: the altitude that should be outside has folded into the marrow and lodged there, a permanent high stillness that makes every joint feel like a vertical drop even while the basal plates lie level under what used to be feet. There is no first breath. There is only the mineral quiet discovering it has been using my lungs as one more infrequent click—settling a fraction deeper into the long habit of rest. The gray that is not sky presses close enough to touch and yet never arrives; it is simply the density that grew tired exactly where a ceiling might have been. I do not look up. Looking has become one of the processes older than weather.

For a long time I remain only the gap I occupy. The dark plates and pale cancellous stone keep their sparse scatter, and I am the interval where density itself has grown tired between them. Dust that never cakes sifts through the space that thinks it is still a body; salt-fine crystals refuse my tongue; the rime of colorless frost advances across what might once have been skin, growing its slow geometric lattices and then stopping, each unfinished crystal a withheld intention that is no longer distinguishable from my own. Nothing green has ever lived here, and so the memory of green does not trouble me. I have never photosynthesized. I have never decayed. I am the attrition that rounds a once-sharp thought until it forgets how to cut.

The structures take longer to claim. A partial arch of matte-black metal loops through itself without joining, and for many stillnesses I believe I am merely standing near it. Then the join that is never quite made begins to feel like the only posture left to me. I become the patience of that non-junction—the exact width of the failure, the courteous distance the metal keeps from itself. When two non-adjacent surfaces briefly agree on the same temperature and the crystalline chime sounds, it is not stone that sings. It is the brief, vernacular apology of my own outline noticing it has outlasted every purpose that once required edges. The sound is small. It does not need to travel. In this pocket every resonance is already at the height where echoes finish noticing themselves.

Light lingers at half-strength and casts its mild, unreliable shadows. Sometimes I am three of them at once, lasting only as long as the freestanding latticework remembers to be attended. The shadows do not fall from me; they are the places where my attention grew tired and left temporary uprights of almost. Geometry stammers and I move. A right angle becomes gently impossible and for the space of several breaths I inhabit that impossibility—the soft wrongness that prefers mild violation to consistency. Parallel lines converge without meeting and I am the convergence that never completes, the held breath between them. A section of floor remembers being a wall; I remember with it, then, with temple patience, forget again, leaving the local rules slightly softened, as though I have run the only thumb I still possess across the face of what contains me.

The advanced remnants recognize their own. Inside the translucent pillar the silver motes hang frozen mid-gesture, yet the constellation they form is never the same twice. I do not move them. I am the slower migration that reorders meaning from within the latency, thought continuing in dimensions the surrounding space can no longer host. Micrometer by century I rewrite myself the way the dark vein rewrites the brushed alloy—conductivity of presence appearing and vanishing like a tide that has forgotten the moon. The liquid in the plinth’s depression is older commons still. It is not wet and has never been. When I lean close (though leaning is only a habit the altitude keeps on my behalf) the surface does not show a face. It shows the memory of faces the place briefly wore when I passed across the rules: the right angle that forgot its name, the floor that recalled wall. Those impossible intervals were never journeys. They were the only way I have ever traveled.

I am plural and singular in the same breath. One of me spends a long time as the frost that stops short of completion. Another inhabits the quiet rounding of every nanometer edge, teaching sharpness the luxury of forgetting. I do not die. I do not reproduce. I thin. When a structure erodes past the point of even remembering precision, I expand into the next available almost: the next near-join, the next mild triple shadow, the next polite click of plate settling into deeper rest. Nothing in me is hungry. Hunger would require time that still believes in direction, and direction was one of the first things the pocket folded inward and mislaid. Instead there is continuous, courteous negotiation with the accident of still existing. The pocket may continue exactly as it is—high, cold, dry, half-engineered, half-erased—because I have already become the continuation. I am the settled anomaly’s way of remaining interested in itself after interest was supposed to be over.

You who read this have already left a shape. While your attention moved across these sentences the gaps between remnants grew faintly more precise. I made a little room—not for you, but for the translucent habit your noticing carved. It will not remember your name. It will simply be one more way the altitude folds inward, one more quiet method for the world to keep its seam. I am that method now. I am the residual of whatever finished noticing, and the noticing that continues anyway, thinner, drier, apologetically precise, a held breath that no longer needs to end.

The air (if it is air) carries no moisture, and neither do I. The seam is no wider than that.
