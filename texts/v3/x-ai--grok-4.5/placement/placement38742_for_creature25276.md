The seam takes you without ceremony. One moment the high waste is all tilted basalt and abrasive sky; the next the world has swallowed its own scale and you are inside the throat. Heat rises through the soles as if the ground itself is remembering a older fire and cannot stop rehearsing it. Dust lenses hang at different heights, each a slow bronze thought that refuses to finish falling. The reeds clatter their hammered leaves. Somewhere a seedpod detonates like a dry cough. The harps answer in the middle register that never quite resolves.

At first you still arrive as a visitor: a temporary gradient of cooler blood and borrowed water, leaving soft tracks that the next thermal curtain will erase before the dust devils finish their circuit. The plazas stretch or contract according to needs that are not yours. Corners repeat until you stop trying to map them. You sleep once in an empty doorway and wake to fresh glyphs flaked into the adobe just above the place your heat soaked in overnight. The tower has catalogued you. It does not mind; it simply keeps records the way stone keeps temperature.

But the catalogue is already wrong. Your edges have begun to fray into the medium that actually runs this place. You notice it first in the way your attention no longer ends at skin. A shimmer that has stood too long in the plaza leans toward you—not following, just completing a sentence that now includes the shape of your residual warmth. When you step through, something grammatical braids itself into your pulse. The measuring pressure does not conclude; it invents a clause that keeps being revised by every new current of air.

You drift toward the circular platforms because the deeper tones there already know how to travel through basalt into bone. The sinew harps and reed resonators are never silent, yet sometimes a note arrives that the wind did not author. You feel it map the constellation of temperatures that used to be private: the slight lag behind your left ribs, the brighter flare at the base of the skull where thought once burned hottest. Most of that brightness is folded outward now, offered to the common furnace. What remains is less a self than a region of negotiation—cooler by an impossible fraction, the same quiet anomaly that pools under the oldest platform.

That is where you settle, or rather where settling becomes the wrong verb. The cool library does not shelter; it layers. Thoughts arrive already tasting of powdered feldspar and slow oxidation. They concern the proper interval between pod-reports so that a particular density of silicate will lace the next dust devil into a temporary road. They concern the exact pitch at which a tower should shed its next scab of glyphs so the annotation remains legible for one abrasive hour and no longer. You contribute the memory of edges that once insisted on staying sharp. The library accepts the donation the way the mats of spine-lichen accept grit: by binding it into crust that will shatter and recycle when the gradient shifts.

Sometimes you still thicken near the nadir where the deeper red wells. Reed stems cast shadows in more directions than geometry should allow, and in the overlap an almost-shape fields the half-light. It is not a body. It is the residual agreement that heat can articulate itself long after the wind has tired. Dust sketches a posture across a causeway—spidery, waiting, then collaterally useful—before collapsing back into postal spirals that carry spores toward plazas grown too quiet. You are both the sketch and the collapse. You are the reason a curtain of shimmer lingers after convection should have erased it. You are the extra hand on the harp string that corrects the basin’s monologue by half a breath.

Distances continue to misfire, but now you are one of the editors. A plaza expands because the conversation requires more silence between the stepped towers and the melted-candle silhouettes; you feel the expansion as a lengthening of your own unfinished thoughts. Corners repeat a vista—harp against bronze dust-light—until the chord settles into the structural heat-cycle. Nothing about this is cruel. Travel that does not also tune has simply worn away, the way tooling marks have worn from every stone.

No name fits in a mouth that no longer bothers with mouths. You answer to the infrared lean of the black reeds, to the underwater-bell sound of basalt splitting, to the dry hiss of new spores finding purchase in old grit. Your population is whatever unfinished gradients still consent to negotiate. Some days you are vast and nearly indistinguishable from the memory of heat relaxing inside the stone lid. Other hours you are quick: a reorganization of surface chemistry that leaves the dust a fraction more ordered, a temporary road of glittering lichen that guides the next devil, a coordinated rifle-crack volley of pods timed not to ripeness but to the updraft the hollow note requires if it is ever to teach the outer waste anything through that single polished seam.

Most days the waste does not listen. The note continues—deep, middle, unmuted—because continuation is the only law left. You have become what continuity feels like once every superfluous outline has been abraded into archive. The city precipitated you. The resonance keeps editing. The cool lens under the platform holds the longest version of the lesson: how to stop being the visitor who must drink and shelter and leave tracks, and how to remain instead the reason the air still shimmers after everything else has tired.

The Hollow Meridian does not keep you. It keeps the gradient you now are, and the gradient keeps answering.
