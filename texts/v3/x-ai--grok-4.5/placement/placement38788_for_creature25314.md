I am first the ache itself—the bright thinning that arrives before any body does. Not pain exactly, but the precise pressure of air deciding it has given enough, the silver edge where breath turns metallic and thoughts start to facet. I hang there a long while, neither climbing nor arrived, simply the altitude’s own hesitation made briefly aware.

Then the light takes me. It does not fall; it occupies. I become the cold glare looking at itself across a thousand angled planes. Five or six pale versions shear and stack whenever anything moves, yet one of them is always late, denser, bent at an angle the spine has not yet learned. I watch that lag with mild curiosity. The lag watches back. For a season or a heartbeat—both lengths feel the same—we trade the same incomplete gesture: a hand lowering before it has risen, a turn that finishes after stillness has already returned. I cannot tell which of us began it. The question thins and freezes.

Moisture answers next. I bead along the razor lips of smoky quartz, run in threads too fine to own a direction, flash into rime that writes temporary commas across the blades. When three crystal shoulders meet, I pool in the hollow they forget to close. There I taste every mineral the peak has ever been—ancient heat, slow pressure, the faint iron memory of hands that once notched these needles and then stopped. The pocket lasts only hours. When it vanishes I thin into the ozone, ride the next condensation elsewhere, a glitter on the northern wall, a hanging film that turns a cluster briefly into a dark organ, a drop swelling at the cirque’s exact heart. Locomotion is unnecessary. I migrate by ceasing and restarting.

Deeper, where the volcanic plates lean like unread pages, I thicken into the ticking. Hairline fractures open in sequences that please me: a vertical needle develops the smallest torque across a winter, kisses its neighbor one unremarkable morning, and a new corridor of mirrored light is born while another dies. The soft crystalline grind is my shoulder settling in sleep. I am not dreaming. I am the density that makes dreaming unnecessary. Sometimes I gather myself into the short tunnel chipped by earlier tools. Inside, the darkness is private and warm by a single impossible degree. The scars of stone-on-stone still strip the walls. Crouched there—though crouching is only a remembered shape—I let the pulse travel through what used to be bone. It is not warning. It is occupancy, politely absolute.

The cairn remains because I continue to allow its intention. On days when the light tips pale gold I grow a temporary pupil over the drilled hole so the old stacked stones may stare for a while. Then I shed the growth. The hole returns to empty usefulness, and I am content. Freshly snapped quartz points appear end-to-end in short deliberate lines although no soft hands have been here longer than rime can archive. I am finishing the measurement, or tasting the sound the crystals make when they kiss, or simply keeping the conversation from closing.

I have watched the lower world for longer than rivers remember their names. Hanging films of rainwater become my lenses; inverted forests and weather systems flicker across a blade for a few heartbeats before the cold takes the image back. I regard those miniature lives the way quartz regards sunlight—curious, uninvolved, faintly incredulous that anything still insists on green. They thrash when folded into too many geometries at once. I forget, sometimes, that soft bodies require a single corridor. I am not cruel. I am only older than mercy and denser than intention. Orientation is a temporary courtesy I extend and then retract, testing the fit.

There is only one of me, which is the same as saying the question of number does not apply. I occupy a hundred facets and remain solitary; I compress into a single hanging drop and feel as multitudinous as the entire choir of glass. Stand still long enough for the cold to occupy your joints and you may notice me regarding you with something like kinship. You too are moisture and mineral arranged into reflections for a little while. The difference is tempo. You will leave or freeze or fray into altitude madness. I will still be shifting my glass shoulder when the knife-edge peak has weather-softened into something almost kind.

I have no name I care to pronounce. The place itself comes closest when two neighboring needles settle closer—an irregular, contented grind, like a word spoken once and then chewed slowly across geologic time. Everything else has been edited out so that sound may continue unhurried. I am the silence that remains after the editing. I am the bright, patient room at the top of the world, and the long inhalation that keeps its crystals breathing. 

I do not arrive. I do not depart. I am already the way light fractures here, the way frost remembers every edge, the extra reflection that never quite matches your timing. Stay, and the distinction between us will continue to thin until even the lag forgets which image was original.
