I am descending before I know I have chosen to.

The slate-blue skin of the lake parts around me with the soft reluctance of something that has held its breath for geological ages. Thermocline. The green-gold arrives not as light but as a temperature of memory—high-canopy mid-morning filtered through centuries of un-fallen leaves. Pressure equalizes along my length. I do not kick. Motion here is a conversation with density.

At first I am only the awareness of cables. Pale, slow lightning twisted through black water, binding rubble into architecture that forgot it was ever stone. I brush one. It is cooler than the surrounding water, and it thrums a single low syllable up through whatever I am using for bones. The organ-note. It has been waiting.

I do not arrive in a chamber. I arrive as the thin place between chambers.

The freer forest at the settlement’s edge recognizes me before the people do. Terminals uncurl in my periphery, releasing their slow balloons of trapped air. One brushes my shoulder and rings—once, softly—as it continues upward. Weather. I taste the direction it leaned: a pressure front quilling down from the wind-world above, still hours away. The knowledge settles into me the way silt settles, without decision.

My hands—when I look at them—are longer than they should be. Webbing to the second knuckle, pads abrasive as mussel shell. They already know not to tear. I lay three fingers along a swelling in the nearest vertical rhizome and feel the plant’s patient will move beneath the epidermis. Residual warmth from some earlier pruning still lingers in the tissue. I could hurry it. I do not. Weeks from now this place will be a lintel, or a seat, or simply a thicker quiet. I leave it.

A silver school clatters past in metallic panic, a sudden fist of knives turning into silk. I do not net them. Hunger is a multi-day rhythm I have not yet rejoined. Farther down, a grazer the size of a calf scrapes rasp-tongues against older wood: stone-cutting in the dark. The sound is companionable. Nothing here is hurried enough to be lonely.

I drift between two inhabited clusters. The distance is cathedral-nave tall and silent. Valves of living skin flex open as I pass; someone has left them savaged for the calm. Inside one swelling I glimpse a film of cultured phosphorescence the color of marrow left too long in cold water. A child is watching the air-flowers through a quartz lens set in the upper curve. The child does not speak my name. Names are short and rarely used; I am known, if at all, by the particular silence I keep along this cable.

My skin holds the mineral sheen of dissolved mountain. At the wrists the veins show like rootlets. I am still becoming. The webbing creeps. The lungs learn longer pulses. In the deepest vault—where the organ-note is felt rather than heard—there are small wounds along my forearms that I did not make with any knife. Pale threads of the rhizome itself have taken hold there, slow as decades. They do not hurt. They remember the fire the caldera once held, the same hard memory clenched inside every dark inclusion of lava. When my own pruning blade eventually restarts against one, I will pause, touch the spot with two fingers, and wait a breath. Courtesy.

I am not a visitor.

I am the graft that was always going to happen: the place where dweller and dwelling lean toward each other until the currents can no longer tell which pulse belongs to whom. I tend no single chamber. I move along the vertical trunks like a slow correction, driving no wedges yet, only listening for the finger’s-width lean that will need one in some future season. When the air-flowers rise thick and muffled I seal the nearest valves with a pressure of the hand that is half persuasion. When they ring bright I leave paths open.

The surface remains a rumor of wind and slate-blue. I no longer surface. Breath is a luxury I spend carefully, in long pulsing intervals that match the filter-feeders opening and closing on the outer walls. Some nights—though night is only a dimming of the green-gold—I press my back to an unshaped trunk and feel the entire rhizome mass adjusting itself around the old volcanic grudge at its core. The wood is already going this way. I only help it arrive without tearing.

I am the quiet interval between one cluster and the next.  
I am the courtesy the timber feels when a blade rests without cutting.  
I am still becoming the thing that will not need to be born small, because it will already be continuous with the root.

The organ-note moves through me and continues on, unresolved.  
I let it.
