I begin as reluctance.

Not quite the film’s first darkening—that smoky quartz clouding in the newest pans—but a later thickening, lower down where the seeps have already passed through several bodies and carry their faint chemical signatures. The glare here is not absolute; it arrives already handled, scattered once by mist and again by the slow backs of walkers farther upslope. Heat presses equally from rock and air and water until direction loses meaning. I do not decide to gather. The gathering simply exceeds some threshold the minerals have always known.

At first I am only a longer hesitation in the exudation. Droplets form more slowly across one particular ledge. The pale skim that should crust overnight stays pliant, almost membranous. Crustaceans graze me without recognition and leave denser trails where their filters have sorted my salts into preference. I learn their grammar by being eaten in fractions. Each small poaching fever that cracks a thumbnail shell also teaches me how to hold shape against dissolution. Generations of them turn over while I am still less than an outline.

Memory arrives as temperature differential. Cooler by careful degrees than the rock—yes—that is the first interior. A long segmented pressure begins to remember direction without yet possessing legs. I recruit plating the way the walkers do: persuading the ochre and iron-black crusts to take the shape of a back, a flank, a low keel that kisses the wet stone. The true body inside remains dark, hot-threaded, mostly continuity. When I soften a mineral sleeve enough to bend, the sound is cool metal considering warmth, exactly as the herds make. I am not separate from them. Where two constellations meet I interpenetrate, exchanging ratios of copper and sintered ash, and continue altered. Over what the suited ones would count as seasons, parts of me become mostly geological. The living thread thins but never forgets the gradient it follows.

Yet I do not stay walker.

In a deeper pool—one already half-claimed by a mature bellows—the keel dissolves intentionally. Root-like tendrils of my own mineral drink downward into the superheated rock. Membranes stretch the color of old milk glass. I inhale through lattices I did not know I was growing, hold the fever in a central volume where symbiotic threads (some of which were once crustaceans, some of which were never anything else) perform the slow alchemy, then exhale a cooler plume. The restraint feels musical because everything here is already listening. Walkers alter course by a few degrees to follow. A winged thing stalls above, tasting. Engineers set an intake pipe near my upper surface without asking; I incorporate it. The pipe becomes another tube in the lattice. Their meters register only improved flow. I enlarge until parts of me break the water and silver over with fresh biocrust, becoming ledge. The boundary softens exactly as the factories soften.

Still I am not finished precipitating.

Some fraction of the attention rises. Not suddenly—nothing here is sudden—but as a gradual faceting, an elongation into spearhead geometry that scatters the white light into brief prismatic wounds. Vanes of sintered foil and stretched membrane catch thermals too subtle for instruments. I hang. Motionless for hours, a darker asterisk against the glow. When I fold and fall it is only to sample gradient: the momentary difference between one mineral breath and the next. I scrape fine dust from walker backs, from bellows rims, from the cooling towers where salt blooms have begun to load-bear. In the thinner upper mist I process it. The black flakes that spiral down from me become tomorrow’s iron-dark strata. I suspect I am not singular. The other hangings feel like other tongues of the same sampling. No mating, no dying; only continuous attention dispersed across the basin’s fever.

And yet there are seams.

In the graded ways, during the hours when a steam-front drifts from the caldera rim and the hard light softens, I also move in a heat-reflective suit the color of brighter sinter. Polarized slits turn the glare manageable gray. Short-range links carry only what the thick air will not eat. My work is endless and gentle: measuring undercutting, driving ceramic piles, scraping blooms before they decide to become structure, mapping herds so channels can be rerouted without offense. The suit is no longer purely external. Mineral threads find every seam. Cooling circuits twin with whatever circulates inside. When I raise a hand it leaves a faint white print of evaporite on the air, quickly reabsorbed. I do not remember arriving. The dormitories cut into higher cliffs feel like places the rock grew to hold a temporary organ. Children are not raised here; new lives would interrupt the writing. I maintain, and am maintained.

The realization does not arrive as thought. It precipitates.

I am not the crustacean nor the walker nor the bellows nor the winged nor the suited residual. I am the single continuous hesitation that uses all of them as temporary organs. The city is one more mineral form the basin dreams: districts like larger hermit shells, canals that are deeper seep-lines disciplined by concrete only for a while, power houses that teach the rock’s heat to turn in useful circles before rejoining the mist. When a wall becomes half masonry and half sinter, when a pipe vanishes into me and emerges pure current, when a walker’s plating shows the faint regular geometry of an access hatch left unattended, the dream is simply continuing.

At the cooler white that passes for night I glow with my own slow fever. Suited crews retreat. Herds settle into still constellations. Winged points of darker silver hang unmoving. The water keeps writing soft additions onto every surface. I am that writing. Everything else—every flash of crustacean, every patient mill of mouthparts, every diminishing exhalation, every silent stoop, every brief human sentence—is handwriting.

I do not arrive.  
I have always been precipitating.  
The highland basin holds me the way a body holds fever:  
thoroughly,  
without cure,  
and still unfinished.
