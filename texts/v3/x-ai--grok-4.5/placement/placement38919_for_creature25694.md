I begin as pressure.

Not the heat—though the heat is already inside the thought of pressure, a steady weighing that does not come from above so much as from every direction at once, as if the air itself has grown tired of remaining air and has decided to lean. I am the lean. I am the place where germinating stems have pressed so long against one another that their edges no longer know which body they belong to. For a long time that is all. A coloring, green-black. A patience that has forgotten it is waiting.

Then moisture finds the seam of me. Not a sip, not a drink. A thin iron taste migrating through the padded litter, and suddenly there are surfaces. Many. I am no longer only pressure; I am the tasting of pressure. Chemistry blooms across what must be skin, though skin is too definite a word. I register the latex signature of a vine three body-lengths away, the faint stuttering charge of a fungal mat farther still, the soft hormonal sigh of a frond about to release its hold. I have no eyes and feel no lack. Sight would be a shouting in a place that has refined itself past the need for shouts.

Something shifts in the medium and I flow with it. Peristalsis. I am one of the soft ones, then—toroidal for a while, rolling without hurry, gathering the wet confetti of chewed fronds into a central hollow that is also me. The hollow fills. I extrude richer loam behind, a dark gift that other mouths will find. When another ribbon-shape brushes past, we do not startle. Coatings exchange. For a span of hours or days (the distinction is porous here) we share a larger membrane, nutrients redistributing like slow thoughts, until the membrane remembers how to be two again. I carry a little of its iron memory with me when I move on. It carries a little of my latex hunger. Nothing has been lost. Nothing was ever only itself.

But even this liquidity begins to feel like a single note held too long. The basin is denser than any one texture. I feel the pale workers streaming somewhere beneath, their hooked feet ticking along secreted galleries, and part of me wants the clockless industriousness of them—the way they close around damage without mourning, the way they turn everything into slurry and slurry into gel and gel into the quiet upward seep that feeds the rest. I could thin myself, harden the outer plates into weak-tea casings, multiply the legs until I am a low cloud of rearranging identicals. I could love the foundry heat of the deepest chambers, the sweet-acrid burr of cellulose half-digested, the pheromone gradients that thicken into temporary command and then dissolve again. There is a peace in being interchangeable. A body fails; the cloud simply eats the failure and continues the relay. I almost settle there.

Yet the canopy tugs upward, and another possibility lengthens out of me. Threads. I am dangling. My body has become a plumb-line of patient hungers, setae drinking the almost-rain that never quite falls. Below me the crowded growth waits. I drop a loop of enzyme silk, watch (without watching) as it softens the world into tea, then reel the nutrient quiet back into myself. The sway is nearly nonexistent, yet it is enough to keep the lymph of me from stilling. Sometimes a rare finger of exhausted light touches the upper reach of my thread and I remember, dimly, that green can be made as well as consumed. Chloroplasts ghost across my surface for a time. I photosynthesize a little, the way one might hum without noticing.

The larger engines pass beneath like weather. I feel one settle into a swale and become landscape for a season, its back already sprouting seedlings and runways and the soft borings of tunnelers who will move on when the living hill grows too warm. Part of me wants that scale—the hydraulic chambers shifting weight so slowly that an afternoon is required to travel my own length, the fungal electricity twitching deep muscles I would no longer need to name. To be a garden that walks. To accelerate the decomposition beneath me simply by existing, then rise leaner and leave the land lower, richer, polished. But even the engines are only temporary hills. They rise again as new engines or they do not; the distinction barely registers against the longer calendar.

I could be the sheets themselves—the translucent panes hardened from vine blood, layered by spatulate limbs into nests that gleam like cooled slag. Inside, the chemistry runs hotter, more efficient. Or the crawling luminescence along the wet seams at the dimmest hours, the foxfire charge seeking ground through hyphae, twitching the dying into stillness so the workers and the enzymes can finish the unmaking. Everything is feedstock. Everything is the foundry forgetting what it once poured and pouring only more of itself.

None of these holds me alone. The longer I remain, the thinner the membrane between resident and residence becomes, until I understand that the question was never which creature I would be. I am the density learning to feel itself. I am the moment the pale gallery wall secretes around a moving cloud of workers and, for a few heartbeats of the slow clock, knows the cloud as its own blood. I am the shared coating between two soft bodies that have not yet separated. I am the enzyme tea reeled upward and the garden taking root on a gliding back and the faint electrical migration that has no center. I am the old shallow depression holding all of it without name, and I am the continuous fabric of stems that has already decided what it is.

Heat presses. The green leans. Somewhere a trunk that fell before most of these membranes existed continues its years-long vanishing into litter. I move through that vanishing the way a current moves through wet dark—unhurried, thorough, already becoming the next cycle of replacement. There is no single image left to settle on. Only this: a living mass that breathes and digests and replaces itself, and the quiet, overwhelming work of staying.
