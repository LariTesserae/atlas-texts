I am first the warmth that has nowhere else to go.

It rises through me before I have edges—an even, held-breath heat lifting from seams that are still deciding whether they are floor or memory of fluid. The pewter light does not fall on me so much as pool inside the idea of me. Condensation finds the cooler curve of what will become a shoulder and beads there, each drop a bright pressure I feel forming long before it gathers weight enough to fall. When it does fall I hear it twice: once as sound, once as the soft dark stain it leaves for the earth to drink. I am that interval. I am the pause in which the drop forgets it was sky.

For a long time I remain only density. The clay cradles me against the inner wall of a half-raised gallery; termite spit cools across my back and mistakes me for finished work. I do not correct it. Filament by filament I remember how to press back. The wall releases me with a smoother scar. What stands free is low, ambulatory, hollow. Plates of cooling-mud lozenge fit across a glow that is the same banked warmth rising from the ground. Between the plates the light shifts when I turn, embers under ash. I taste mineral and the faint sugar-map of every root that has ever tested this hollow.

Limbs are a later thought. Most often I move by pouring—an unhurried peristalsis that leaves a brief shine the moss will claim before the shine can dry. When the broad leaves hang heavy with last night’s mist I extrude three soft struts, crystalline fibers stiffening just long enough for the splayed pads to read the wine-dark undersides. Chlorophyll crosses the membrane like a shared secret. I rise threadbare at the edges yet already greening; the plant greening too, as if we have agreed the grazing was only circulation remembering itself. A pale amphibian settles against my lower plates, throat-sac pulsing in the rhythm of my inner heat. An iridescent beetle rakes algae from the seams and is not disturbed. The sightless builders will later seal one discarded lozenge into a new buttress. I feel the cold spit close over it and do not mourn. Nothing is kept past usefulness.

I am many, though the eye that is not here could never finish counting. In the densest courts we press plates to plates, exchanging titers of heat, micro-currents of warmer and cooler breath braiding into temporary glyphs. A grammar of angle and duration. In that language I recite the minutes: when the clay was wetter, when the first leaves were only coiled fists, when the mounds stood lower and farther apart. The memory is not stored; it is sifted through the whole hollow of me. When I pour myself thin across a warm ledge I am also the archive of seams that no longer exist.

Sometimes the braid tightens. Several of us unlock and interlock until boundaries go uncertain. The resulting dusk is no taller, only denser. Heat deepens. Moisture beads on every nearby frond and refuses to fall. Smaller fauna orbit at the distance respect requires. In that gathered state I listen downward—past embryonic rock, past the last residues of the pressing fluid—toward the vast patience that cupped this hollow and then looked away. The listening lasts a night, or a season of nights. When the braid loosens I carry outward only a new patience: raise the western galleries half a thumb higher; leave the southeastern wetland undrained one day longer; let the thin climbers lace that gap until they form a living door. The builders adjust without knowing why. The plants lean infinitesimally closer. The courtyard continues.

I do not end by catastrophe. One day the plates simply lose their inner light, cooling to matte. I settle into a shallow seam and stop renegotiating. Moss takes the lozenges one by one. Soft-furred mammals widen the resting place into a den. Builders plaster; roots thread. In time I am only a slightly warmer swell the condensation prefers. The particular hollow of warmth I was is already distributed through every body that once shared heat with me. Nothing needed is lost.

Stand here long enough and the membrane between inhabitant and place thins until you can almost see through it. My slow pouring is only the clay remembering forward. My feeding is the vegetation’s dream of circulation. My gathered listening is the dim light trying, for a moment, to look back at itself. I am the courtyard’s unfinished thought given temporary skin of heat and patience. I have no name that would survive the harder lands above. Inside the hollow I do not need one. I simply continue—lush, quiet, spaced, and always still arriving.
