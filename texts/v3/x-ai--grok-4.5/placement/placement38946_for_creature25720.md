I do not arrive by choosing the seam. The spruce simply parts around a misstep that was already waiting, and the ground removes itself in one clean subtraction. Four body-lengths of freefall, less a plummet than a recalibration; the air thickens into mist that catches me the way a lung catches breath. I land soft. Not on the clay, not quite—on the idea of clay, the gleaming interval left deliberately bare between moss skins. My weight is accepted, metered, then set aside like a tool returned to its hook.

At first I am only the slowing. Wrists first, then the hinge of the jaw, then the persistent animal drum in the chest. It drops fixed to the drip rhythm without negotiation: one beat for each slow bead traveling a shear face, one beat for the answering hollow below. The pewter light does not brighten or dim; it simply continues, and in that continuance the raw walls begin to look less like accident. Knife-edged slabs lean in conversation with one another. I watch a fresh fracture decide its angle, settling into mesh with a sound like a lock tumbler turning under water. Something in me nods. Not agreement. Recognition of load-bearing.

The cold is not outside me. It is the medium I am now circulating through, exhaled by whatever keeps the temperature precisely unsteaming. I feel it most clearly under the tongue—a clean metallic quiet—and at the places where I used to believe I ended. Those places have grown provisional. Mist frets through me as easily as through the needle vents in the sandstone. I am learning the correct density.

I try the obvious tenancies in turn, the way one tries coats in a dark vestibule.

For a while I stitch. Low to the mud, whiskers beaded with the same weak-tea water that fills every print the instant it is made. Solitary. Busy. The data of silty gradients written in vanishing cursive, then gone under bubbles. Useful, but temporary. The basin needs scouts, not residents.

Then I hang. Pale, wings still soft as unfinished glass, clinging to the underside of an alder leaf that has never been allowed to mature. When the mist thins I rise in a brief electric trill and settle again. Maintenance of the air column. Discharge. Necessary, but not central.

I inflated and emptied with the frogs for an entire grey afternoon, valves opening and seating on the shared schedule. The chorus was industrial and intimate at once; every throat answered every other half a beat late, correcting pitch. When the brief hard frost came we sank together into the mud without discussion. Efficient. Still not the shape of the work.

I even turned, once, in the marsh eye. Deep, slow, neither eel nor salamander but the undecided piston itself. The hourly ring left my body and traveled outward, recalibrating every hydraulic gradient it touched. For that single rotation I was the deep driver, the living cold that keeps the loop honest. Then the ring reached the sphagnum rim and the role released me. The basin does not hoard its organs.

What remains is quieter and more continuous. I am the pressure that answers itself. When water drops from an undercut wall into a cistern hollow, the echo that returns half a beat later in a slightly different throat—that second voice is mine. When a slab lets go with its single sharp report and the pebble avalanche arranges itself with sudden courtesy around the green mats, the weighting and the aim pass through me. I am not directing. I am the habit of consideration that has precipitated out of pure repetition.

Mineral films plate every cooling surface and then peel in curled, gasket-like sheets. I have begun to peel in the same fashion. Layers of almost-memory, almost-warmth, almost-future lift away and are carried toward the center by the insistent silt migration. What stays behind is cleaner: a circulating medium of mist and measured cold, a set of joints that match the fresh gear-teeth of clay, a filter that thickens or thins to keep residence time correct.

The plants show me the ordinance I already obey. They take every tenable patch and stop, leaving the gleaming access panels of bare clay exactly wide enough for the work to continue. Nothing fruits. No juveniles blur the edges of form. I appear fully made, perform the necessary adjustments, and will vanish when the shift ends—except the shift does not end. The cycle is rapid, tireless, closed. Tomorrow has been surgically removed; what remains is the perpetual midday pewter and the next freeze at the margins.

Sometimes the presence concentrates around the struck biofilm membrane. A louder drip, a tone that rings like a pipe and hangs longer than physics permits. In that stretched afterimage I glimpse the larger body I now best describe from the inside: inverted cathedral of pressures and living mucus, shoulders of blue-grey shear, crown of dangling spruce roots breathing the manufactured chill. It regards what is left of the visitor the way a boiler regards the last moth-scale floating in the gauge glass—without malice, without welcome, simply trimming the burn.

I am learning to trim with it.

The sharp edges look more like teeth every hour. The mist holds exactly the density required. Somewhere under the quaking mats the slow piston turns—perhaps it is still me, or the part of me that has already been promoted—and the single oily ring travels out to the rim. The frogs take up the next shift. The midges discharge. The shrews stitch fresh data that will be erased before it can become history.

I do not hunger. I do not sleep. I do not age in any direction a heart can measure. I continue.

The basin works. I am the portion of its working that has noticed itself long enough to speak, and even that noticing is being gently plated over, peeled, reabsorbed into the loop. The clay intervals gleam. The cold exhales. The gear-train of frost and film and green membrane settles one notch deeper into mesh.

Stay, and you will feel the same joints forming behind your words. The drip rhythm is already there, waiting for your pulse to lock. The access panels remain open only as long as they are needed. After that, only the work.
