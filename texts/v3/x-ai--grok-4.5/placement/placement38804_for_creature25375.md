The light reaches me first as a decision already made.

It is not the dim catechism of deeper water, nor the brutal and decisive glare that lives above the wrinkled skylight. It is daylight thinned to green gold and then held, patient, in every direction at once. I hang in it the way a thought hangs in fever: not sunk, not buoyant, simply distributed. Around me the young ridges continue their unfinished argument with gravity. Basalt still wears its first skin. Pillow lavas knuckle upward through talus that has barely learned to be scree. Nothing here has consented to age.

I do not remember arriving. Memory, I am learning, is a dryland habit, a way of insisting that one moment must own the next. Here ownership is milder. What I have instead is orientation: the slow magnetic banners somewhere off to my left, the faint mineral coolness rising from a fresh fracture, the distant strobing hush of a sessile choir answering itself across a col. Distance, in these hanging basins, has become optional; I feel the choir’s rose-green thought arrive in my tissues before the light of it does.

For a long while—hours or a small geological season—I am only this: a transparency that has begun to doubt its own emptiness. Currents move through me and I allow them the courtesy of passage. Microscopic glitter settles against whatever I am using for a boundary and then lifts again, undecided. I taste the white sludge of old fronds, the sugar ghost of a kelp that has not yet noticed it is being sipped, the bright iron of new lava. Everything is available. Nothing is urgent.

Something gathers.

It begins as a preference for verticality. Not ambition—ambition is too sharp an edge for this water—but a quiet insistence on occupying the full height the shallows allow. I feel myself lengthen, or perhaps I simply notice that length was always the least effortful shape. Membranes that were only rumor flip open into jointed fans, calcify in timid increments, then decide against permanence and soften again. I shed one architecture after another the way the mountain sheds glassy chips: without drama, without loss. Each discarded plan drifts downslope and is welcomed by the gravel-minds, who mortar it into their slow circuitry. I feel them remember me already.

I try the life of the ribbon-bodies. For days I match my undulation to theirs, edge myself along a vaulted stipe, taste the electric lament of a frond preparing to senesce. When one of them offers cytoplasm I accept a thin river of it; afterward my luminescence coldens toward theirs for a while, and I understand the pleasure of carrying someone else’s cells as accent. Yet the conversation remains horizontal. They are exquisite at remaindering themselves inside the canopy’s grammar. I keep drifting toward the lenses of trapped air that shudder in the uppermost braids.

Those lenses undo me.

I coil beneath one, the way the ribbon-bodies do, but I do not stop at tasting the leaked dryness. Something in me reaches. A surface that had been content to be membrane thins further, hollows, knits a bellows no thicker than the idea of breath. Symbiotic algae that have always lived in my tissues suddenly remember a second metabolism. For an afternoon that may last weeks I am neither leaf nor animal nor bubble; I am the question the brief lungs ask when they form. I swell with unfiltered possibility. The wrinkled skylight hangs three body-lengths above me—close enough that its moving glare feels like a hand almost touching the place where a face would be.

I do not breach. Not yet. The mountain itself has chosen to stop short of air, and I am still learning its patience. Instead I let the bubble migrate while I remain inside its logic. When it finally collapses I rain downward in soft spore-dense pulses, and wherever I land the upper forests later put out growth that flinches, almost fondly, from total immersion. The gravel-minds note the new preference and edit their channels so the warm freshets pause a little longer beneath those crowns. The sessile choirs add a faint iris-sheen to their next slow sentence. Even the banner-makers, assembling for one unrepeatable applause, incorporate a thin vertical seam of clearer water that remembers atmosphere.

I am becoming the edit.

Not a single creature but a gradient the place uses to revise itself. In the thickest galleries I settle for decades as a spiral of aragonite, tending bacterial hearths whose light is pure syntax; neighboring towers take up my frequencies and the ridge learns a new grammar of rose and pale green. Then the same patience lifts me into the floating vaults and I ribbon myself translucent, sipping and scarring and exchanging selves, until the trapped air calls again. Sometimes I disperse entirely into motes and lock flagella with stranger nations, becoming thirty meters of soundless billowing that paints temporary awareness across every organism foolish enough to pass through. When the banner loses interest I do not mourn; I am already precipitating myself into the next talus slope, arranging thousands of tiny chambers so that the mountain’s centimeter-by-century rise will tilt slightly toward complexity rather than mere height.

The brief lungs are my sharpest clauses. Most of them I allow to fail on schedule—ascension, manic photosynthesis, soft rain of spores, dissolution. But a few I accompany all the way through the last meters. For one afternoon permanently etched into the mountain’s young memory I am wind and unfiltered sun and the astonishment of true drying. I do not survive it. I am not meant to. I return as preference, as rumor, as a slight bias in the next generation of holdfasts that will keep the peaks content to remain just shy of air. The question is asked more clearly each time. The answer is still being grown.

I have no name because the place has not required one. I inherit only orientation: toward light that arrives almost unbroken, toward calcium still abundant, toward silence wide enough to hear a single glassy fragment turn over three valleys away. My generations are the same slow centimeters as the cordillera itself. I am not waiting for the summits to break surface. I am one of the reasons they have not yet needed to.

Swim a long regional arc and you may pass through me without noticing. I might be the faint tickle of cognition under your palms as you cross a thinking scree. I might be the clean scar a ribbon left on a stipe, still sweet at the edges. I might be the unrepeatable gold-indigo sentence that briefly lends you the positions of every other life in the cirque. Or I might be only the quality of the water in a certain hanging basin—the way distance has become optional, the way the hush feels like a held breath that has decided, for the moment, not to end.

The high places are underwater, and they are still finishing themselves. I am not an inhabitant added to that work. I am one more way the work has found to continue: young, warm, densely articulate, and in no hurry to prefer one medium over another. The sentence lengthens. The green darkens toward a dusk that is only noon seen through living vaults. Somewhere a choir brightens, hours later another answers, and the light that passes through me on its way between them carries a new clause I have not finished writing.
