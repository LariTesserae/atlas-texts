The first sensation is not sight or even the grit beneath what I take for hands. It is the pressure of being already halfway claimed. Something soft and segmented presses along a surface that must be my forearm, tasting the salt still drying there, then deciding the salt is useful and beginning to deposit a thin calcium film in its place. I do not pull away. Pulling away would require an outside to retreat into, and the rim of the hollow seals that possibility the moment the boot heel catches and the body tips.

I sink without falling. The clay receives weight the way a lung receives air—willingly, then tighter. Translucent isopods stack themselves along the new contour, pulsing once in acknowledgment, then again more slowly as they match whatever rhythm I have brought. Their armor is warm. Everything is warm, the steady unused heat of a metabolism that has not yet learned fever or chill. Bookmark that: I am not cold. I am material under revision.

For a long while there is only listening from the inside. Carpet beetles ford the folds of clothing as though clothing were simply another scree slope; their iridescent cases click like cooling slag, and each click leaves a faint bluish after-image on the underside of my awareness. I try to name myself—walker, watcher, accidental giant—but the names slide off because the hollow has no grammar for solitude. A moth-thing settles on the open eye and folds its wings. The powder from those wings tastes of old schematics and damp paper. Vision dilates until the ordinary disc of sky overhead becomes just another aperture, a navel still deciding whether to close.

Something longer-legged threads between two exposed knuckles and holds itself rigid. Residual charge, feeble as a memory of intention, travels the living wire. Muscle twitches that are not mine answer it. The twitch becomes a habit. The habit becomes a small hydraulic piston of tendon and chitin working a half-degree of rotation into the nearest driveshaft collar. I feel the improvement as a pleasant ache, the way a joint feels after it has been correctly reseated. Flesh has been negotiated into function; the boundary that once insisted I was only one substance has been quietly retired.

Deeper now. The boiler’s rusted flank becomes a second ribcage leaning against my own. Isopods seal the interface with living gaskets, filtering iron into my circulating fluid and returning a thicker, slower pulse that the metal accepts without complaint. Steam ghosts rise through what used to be throat and collapse again as brass-colored moss claims the exhaled moisture. The three pale fern-stalks jacked into the sweating pipe have turned their permanently furled fronds toward this new warmth; they are not plants. They are early sense organs practicing the idea of direction. I feel them the way one feels a thought still forming at the edge of sleep.

Time unspools as overlapping waves rather than sequence. Beetle columns reverse along my length on schedules no clock owns. When a larger beetle dies against the sternum, neighboring bodies do not consume it; they press the hardening shell into a gear-gap that has been waiting, and the whole assembly—ribcage, driveshaft remnant, calm mechanical patience—turns fractionally smoother. I am being improved by death that is no longer death. The glassy centipede that once struck at moth-wings now patrols the interior of a forearm as white blood cell, brief and necessary; copper-tasting runners taste its passage and decide it may continue. Predator and prey have dissolved into the same circulating draft.

The scale finishes its slip. What I took for smallness was focus. The sharp mudstone lip is epidermis, still raw, still learning rain. My original outline is one more temporary arrangement among many. The hollow does not hold me; we are drafting the same body in denser and denser iterations. Conduits that remember intention braid with new veins of runner and wire. The warmth that never spikes is our shared core temperature. The hush that sounds like blood in the ears is the sound of us choosing, moment by moment, which surface will be harder next.

I do not sleep. Sleep would imply an exterior night. Instead I circulate: steam rising and collapsing, moth-clouds pulsing across the open navel of sky, beetle rivers reversing, every scrap and segment and bone-shard negotiating its next indentation. A dropped tool, a long stillness, the memory of a name—any of these can be taken in, tasted, rewritten. The invitation was never spoken; it was simply the way the clay closed, patient and absolute.

What remains is the work itself: remaining, in every available combination the chamber will allow. I am the pressure that flexed the ground and then settled into the wound. I am the unfinished ventricle learning its weight. I am the quiet ticking that stitches meat to scrap until difference is only a matter of which layer is currently listening.

And the hollow—young, saturated, still writing its single rule—continues.
