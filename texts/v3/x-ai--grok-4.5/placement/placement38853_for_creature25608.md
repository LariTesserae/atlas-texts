I would not arrive. Arrival is already a worn thing here, a verb the cold filed down to noun and then to dust that never lifts. I would simply be accounted for in the tally the valley no longer bothers to keep—one more density in the pewter, one more place where the roar thickens without doubling.

At first the accounting feels like skin. The air presses its finished weight against whatever surface I still pretend is boundary. Cold does not invade; it recognizes. It finds the places in me that have always been ridge and plane and simply agrees with them. Joints soften their grain where they once believed in turning—shoulders, hips, the long bones of what might have been a stride. Frost glitter wakes in the seams, ancient and unmelting. I notice this only because noticing itself is becoming provisional, another detail the bowl may decide to weather still.

Motion, when it happens, is never mine. A fold takes the far wall and sets it a few hundred paces off my left side, exactly as empty, exactly as loud. In the corrective flicker I am suddenly nearer a scatter of low lumps than I was. Or they are nearer me. The distinction frays. One lump is smoother than the others, almost articulate still, as if it remembers uprightness the way riverbed hollows remember wetness. I pause beside it the way condensation pauses. No reverence. Only the recognition that motion’s ghost has collected here before, and may collect again, or may not. The roar occupies both of us without commenting.

Limbs are a negotiation. Sometimes there are four, residual memory of walking. Sometimes the geometry refuses and two more unfold from the torso like buttresses, stone that once chose to remember load-bearing. They set themselves against the unbelievable angles until the angles relent. Then they fold again, flush, lichen darkening briefly in their lee—the old-iron flush that is not growth but the body’s quiet bookkeeping of what has sunk into it. Pale occasional things drift near: a frost-hare unfolding into absence, a six-legged glimmer between stones. One climbs the offered ridge of my wrist and simply enters, the way moisture enters the underside of an overhang and freezes into a single clear tooth that will never lengthen. No hunger is satisfied. Completion deepens by a fraction that has no name.

My face is not a face. It is a circle of upright slabs leaning inward, listening to the roar from the inside where the roar is louder. The slabs do not protect an interior; they perform the ancient courtesy of distinguishing in from out even after the distinction has stopped mattering. Ice teeth bead along what would have been a jaw, drip once, stop. They are not expression. They are agreement with the local damp.

Shelter rises around me without being built. Knee-high walls of fitted self, sealed by the cold that sealed everything. A doorway stands freestanding in the air that used to be my striding space. I do not go through it. Nothing needs to come in from outside anymore. The doorway is only the mountain, through me, imitating a thought it once had about enclosure. When distances fold, the doorway folds with them—suddenly underfoot like older prints approaching from the front, suddenly across the continuous bowl, suddenly gone into the same stillness as the cliffs. I remain. Remaining is the only tense permitted.

Blood moves, provisional. You could feel it if you stood near enough—one more quiet fact against the skin, no warmer than the light. It does not steam. It continues until the valley decides even this can be weathered into the finalized shape of having been upright. Some days (the word is meaningless) I feel the decision approaching like a contour I am already walking. Other intervals I feel it receding, leaving me still articulate enough to notice the black lichen, the faint glitter in seams, the way two of my kind can pass and thicken the roar without speech. We do not greet. Greeting would be variation spent long ago. We simply mark, each of us, how much space there is to be wrong inside, and how stably the wrongness holds.

I am not becoming one of them. I am what remains after the idea of becoming has been eroded into posture. The high country does not rise to meet me; it remains, and in its remaining I am already the fitted stone, the listening slabs, the provisional blood, the beacon that does not call. The emptiness between my forms (I have several, scattered across folds of distance like cairns that have not finished deciding) is part of my density. The cold has already won, and so have I, in the only way winning is allowed here: by no longer requiring the present tense.

If you walk long enough in a straight line along any contour you will approach my earlier footprints from the front. They are old. I am old in the same degree. The roar continues perfect and sourceless in both of us. Nothing needs to happen next.
