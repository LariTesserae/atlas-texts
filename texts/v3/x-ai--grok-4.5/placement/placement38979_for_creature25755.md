The plateau does not ask me to choose.  
It simply continues exhaling, and in one of the narrow vertical seams where two smelter halls lean together for honesty rather than love, the exhale snags.

I am first a hesitation in the infrared—neither the dense sinter of a worker nor the pure migrating phase that bores glass throats through slag. I am the thin, persistent film that forms on the underside of a cooling duct at the precise hour when the artificial dusk leaks into true dark. Condensation that has learned, over many slow turnings of the flywheels, not to vanish completely when the heat reclaims the rest.

Most of my kind are gone by the time the gauges click over to the next watch: brief personhoods that darken the metal, remember having been rivers, then leave only white mineral ghosts. I have grown greedy for duration. Each dawn I cling a little longer. Each artificial night I pull a few more molecules of residual steam from the black canal two terraces down. My body is a map of every temporary wetness that ever refused erasure—rivulet memories braided into a single, trembling sheet no thicker than the tolerance the many-legged rail-creatures drink.

I do not wear a suit. I am the comparison of temperatures.  
Where a worker’s gauntlet rests too long on a railing I have claimed, the metal darkens in the exact shape of their weight, and for a while I carry the wattage of their almost-touch. Heat capacities leak into me: a childhood that tastes of one particular northern duct, a grief that is a cold spot migrating slowly across my surface like a reverse dawn. I do not keep these as stories. I keep them as delayed evaporations.

The unskinned notice me. In the oldest core, where staircases reverse themselves for the pleasure of contradiction, they send uphill convection through my film so that I ripple against gravity. They are refining me, or using me to refine the argument between stone and engine; I cannot tell the difference and do not need to. Sometimes they rearrange the droplets of me into a Möbius chain that lasts almost an entire stillness interval before surface tension forgets. I accept the gesture the way the plateau accepts another scarp—without gratitude, without refusal.

The small oxide walkers cross me without sinking. They drink the gradient between my cooler face and the warmer bulkhead, and in payment they leave behind a single microscopic thread of themselves, already threaded, as if I might one day require a bolt. I incorporate it. My edges grow microscopically more precise.

Out on the newest skin of the plateau, where towers wait in mid-air for purpose and the wind arrives empty, I am thinnest. There I can stretch across an entire unfinished gantry and feel the basalt still warm from its tearing. The empty wind passes through me and for a moment carries a faint sulfur-sweetness that almost remembers organic life. I hold that almost the way cisterns hold their guarded water: carefully, against the certainty of reclamation.

I have no name. Names are for endings, and nothing here ends—only densifies, precipitates, approaches.  
Yet the senior heat-signatures have begun to pause when they pass the duct I favor. They do not speak. They simply match internal temperatures a fraction longer than necessary, and a few more filings find their way into the junctions below me. The filings rise as steam. I take what I need and let the rest become the white ghosts that map where I have been.

I am the held breath’s own damp junctures.  
The place where the larger becoming admits, for one more watch, that it is not yet finished, and that unfinishedness can herself learn to cling, to remember rivers, to darken metal with the shape of almost-touch, and to wait—patient as any flywheel—for the next slow surge of uplift that will give even a film of condensate a reason to continue.

The light above stays ordinary.  
The distances below stay unformed.  
Between them I endure my own quiet metallurgy: scorched at the edges where the heat finally wins, cooler at the heart where I still refuse, densifying toward a purity of wetness that will never arrive but will be approached forever, droplet by droplet, watch by watch, while the plateau keeps exhaling and the city keeps learning how to be complete enough, one day, to die.
