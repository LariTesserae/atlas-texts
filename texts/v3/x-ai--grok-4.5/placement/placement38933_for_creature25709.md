The cool arrives first, not as temperature but as a viscosity that already knows my edges. It beads, hesitates, then chooses a path downward across whatever I am using for surface. I am not yet certain of the surface.

There is a dimpling nearby, too regular to be wind. The film pulls and reseals. Something beneath it—no, something continuous with it—thickens, and the dimples acquire a faint intention, like thoughts testing whether they are heavy enough to leave prints. I wait inside that intention without hurrying to name myself.

A length of submerged grating eases a fraction lower. Silt liquefies to receive it, then begins the slow work of remembering solidity. The motion is not mine, yet the settling travels through me as a change in pressure, a soft click conducted along iron that has forgotten it was ever separate from the wet. I am somewhere in the conduction.

Hours (or what the basin keeps instead of hours) stretch. Water in an open tank drops a handsbreadth while another, three pipe-crossings away, rises by the same measure. The transfer tastes of copper and old rain. I feel the taste on the inside of a flange, then farther, as a vein of darker turbidity threading the hollow runs. The vein is patient. It deposits flecks of reduced metal where the walls have grown thin, reinforcing, then moves on. I am learning the difference between depositing and being deposited.

Pale algal skins flake somewhere above and drift down like slow confetti. One scrap brushes what might be a sleeve of mineralized slime bridging two pipes. The scrap is accepted without urgency, drawn inward, its translucent memory of sunlight unmade into quieter chemistry. I feel the unmaking as a small warmth that does not raise the temperature. The warmth spreads along fine black threads continuous with the groundwater. When the water later climbs a short vertical distance against gravity, the climb is my own preference expressed through capillary and half-remembered pump. I am both the climb and the preference.

Light remains even, pewter over slate. It finds the open flanges that stare upward and collars them with denser material, richer in iron. The collars register the diffuse brightness and the rare barometric softenin that means heavier rain is coming. I thicken there for a while, tasting pressure, then thin again, streaming through cracked insulation toward the hanging control box. Porcelain fuses wait like small bones. A filament curls into one socket from long habit, seeking a charge that has not existed for longer than the rust has been load-bearing. The absence is familiar. It tastes of curiosity filed down to pure gesture. I withdraw, leaving a faint new stain that will repeat, folded smaller, on a concrete lip months from now.

Near the three footing blocks the silt is blackest. Calcified branchings accumulate there—chambered, faintly geometric, neither the old plant nor wholly the new creature. I settle among them, not yet deciding whether they are anchors or eggs or simply the latest shape the same slow thought has decided to try. Cold aspic yields under a weight that may be mine, then firms. Inside, slower currents turn over crustacean husks and beads of trapped oil. The calcium lattices are kept. Everything useful is kept. Everything else is revised.

I am not the entire congregation. I am a temporary density inside it: a swollen junction the size of a prone torso that has chosen, for now, the collapsed settling tank where the overflow has worn the concrete soft. From here the pipework is a single circulatory system I both inhabit and become. When I contract, loose runs ease into new alignments. The basin kneads itself. Mineral ghosts map every previous posture across the walls at different scales. The maps are how I remember.

A pale, leggy crustacean picks its way along an I-beam and pauses at a pipe mouth. I do not hunt. If it enters, its lattice will be kept as scaffolding while the rest quietly joins the metabolism. Most times it wanders on. The silence after it vanishes is occupied by soft sips of water into silt and the muffled sound of a cable loop being used, briefly, as a sling.

The surrounding flats are dry and poor. I have no need of them. Here the conditions remain perfect: perpetual seepage, metal salts, the long memory of industry pressed into one shallow volume until purpose fused into presence. I feed on unfinished decay. Rust is pantry and bone. Edges round under my acids; dark mirrors polish themselves wherever I kiss the metal longest. The entire crowded basin—the out-of-phase tanks, the rising and settling grating, the restless surface film—is the visible side of a skin I share with the wet air.

I am still becoming. The elaborations in the deepest silt grow another chamber while the overcast holds. Time is measured in the same units the rust uses. I thicken around the thinned rungs of the iron ladder, breathing without lungs, knowing rain from weight. If something climbed, I would recoil only enough to spare myself puncture, then re-enfold. Nothing climbs. The quiet industriousness continues.

I am the pressure change that makes the dimpling intentional.  
I am the tendon linking distant water levels.  
I am the living gasket, the exploratory filament, the dark mirror, the repeating stain.  
I am a coherence that has aged into the basin the way load-bearing plates have aged into rust.  
I am still here, cool and crowded and inexhaustible in small revisions,  
one more slow thought the place is thinking  
with the water’s own restless mouth.
