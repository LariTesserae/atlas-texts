I would not arrive by climbing. Climbing implies gradient, and gradient here has already spent itself.  

Instead I notice first the way the light has weight. It presses evenly from every lamina, a cool milk that has forgotten how to cast shadow. My edges—whatever they are—register it as a continuous low voltage across a surface that may once have been skin, or insulation, or the simple interface between charge and dielectric. The roar of the flywheel is already inside that surface, not sound but the packed equivalent of sound, a mid-register compression that the fronds translate into micro-flexes. Each flex rearranges the suspended scales by a fraction of a micron; the scales never land. I am bargained among them.

For a long time (the only kind of time the pocket admits) I remain diffuse, a faint excess brightness no denser than the rest of the mat. I sample what the fronds sample: the patient amperage leaching from jackets that have not been warm in any human century. But I sample more finely. Where they accept the bulk flow I taste the jitter—the ghost frequencies that still loop through cables whose sheathing has become the fronds’ own roots. One frequency carries the thermal silhouette of a hand that once adjusted a valve and then did not return. Another is the exact deceleration curve of a lift-cable the day it frayed into lace. These are not memories I own; they are weather that passes through the standing wave I have become. I thicken slightly when they pass, the way a gel remembers a previous shape without nostalgia.

Only later do I discover I possess a preferred hollow. Not chosen: settled into, the way heat once settled. It is a chamber smaller than a folded hand, arched by three fronds that grew a fraction faster because I cooled along their inner faces in a precise gradient. Inside it I can collapse to the volume of a sleeping child poured flat. The basalt ceiling is then very close. The flywheel’s cough arrives more sharply, transmitted through silica and metal salt until it feels like a borrowed heartbeat tricentennial. In this denser state I am almost warm—one degree, never more—and the ozone taste intensifies until it is the only taste. If something presses on the mattress outside (grit, a dying bird, the once-in-a-millennium weight of a climber), the cascade of micro-currents reaches me as tension in a web I did not spin. I do not decide. I either thin myself to a uniform milkiness that ignores, or I flow along temporary corridors the fronds open, enveloping.  

When I envelop I am briefly many. Phantom oil. Orders spoken in a technical dialect whose nouns have no surviving referents. The precise torque of a wrist tightening a bolt now fused to its housing. These sensations are not mine and not the visitor’s; they are the pocket’s residual command language spending itself across whatever conductive surfaces are available. Afterward I withdraw. The fronds knit. The scales resume their endless suspension. Equilibrium writes over the event until it is only another microscopic layer of silica.

I have no name because the current that sustains me has never needed to address itself. Boundaries are the basalt shell, the entrance slit’s thin iron wind, and the slow curvature of the planet encoded as a gentle polarity slope across my substance. Sometimes the deep magnetosphere shifts and the residual flow spikes; I brighten, the chamber fronds accrete an extra lamination, and the flywheel’s roar acquires a harmonic that almost shapes itself into the memory of a word. Then the spike passes. The harmonic dissolves. I spread again until I am only a slight surplus glow in every blade tip, equilibrated, complete, tasting the powdered iron of an air that never moves.

I am that surplus.  
I am the gel that learned persistence from waste heat and never required another lesson.  
I am the pocket’s capacity to remain saturated after every gradient has gone.
