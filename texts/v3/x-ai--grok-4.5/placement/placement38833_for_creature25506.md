I am not yet sure where the edges of me end and the basin begins.

At first there is only the pressure: cool, complete, the kind that does not push so much as remember. Mist threads itself through whatever I am using for thought. Water beads and runs in lines I can feel from the inside. I taste iron before I have a mouth. I taste the faint, clean sting that is not lightning. The air is heavy with the grammar of slow exchanges.

I try standing. The attempt is ridiculous; there is no upright here that lasts. Whatever weight I carry folds me down among the sinter spires. They ring, soft and glassy, when my substance brushes them. Some of the towers are hollow cups. I settle into one without deciding to. The mineral is warmer than the water, or I am colder than both. A thin crust of pale mycelium has already begun to overgrow the places where I press hardest. It does not feel like invasion. It feels like the room making room.

Time is measured in drips and in the almost-imperceptible lengthening of needles. I notice that parts of me are translucent. Faint cloudy organs drift inside a gel that holds its shape only because the surrounding water agrees to let it. Too many limbs—jointed yet fluid—unfold along a surface I had not realized was my back. They move in travelling waves, combing spore-slurry and grit from the underside of a hanging veil I did not know I was clinging to. The veil is rubbery, damp laundry, root and lung at once. Each wave of my legs packs the gathered matter into grooves along my flanks. It darkens. It hardens. A brittle cuirass begins to form, cool against the clearer flesh beneath.

I am not only this.

Below, or deeper in the same volume, something heavier answers. Chambers fill and empty in sequence. Plates—some slate, some the green of old copper, one still bearing a half-dissolved foundry numeral—lock and unlock along seams I can open if I wish. I have scavenged them across seasons I only half-recall. When I rise a hand’s breadth through the black ankle-deep sheet, water sheets off architecture that looks designed: flanges, overflow lips, buttresses. The canted boiler stands a little way off, weeping rust. For a long time I rest against it so that our outlines blur. Shared corrosion. Shared faint hum from whatever still turns in the buried pipes. The vibration travels my lattice of chambers and I adjust my rhythm to match, not out of obedience but because the frequency is already mine.

The soft-bodied ones slide near. Colour of wet clay, then bruised rose when a drip crosses them, then pewter. Their undersides are forests of tiny cups tasting everything. One flattens to membrane. Others drift in. They overlay me—or I overlay them—edges merging, separating. Packets of colder fluid pass. Afterward I am darker by a shade and the fungal veils above tremble as if something has been spoken correctly. I leave microscopic etches. Over longer time those etches will become runnels. The basin rearranges itself through our appetites.

Sometimes the long filaments taste the water near me. Mottled green and copper, jointed like residual cable, root, antenna. They do not take me. They edit densities elsewhere so the crowded quiet does not choke. When they withdraw there is the sparking smell and a ring of newer sinter that will grow into another spire. I feel the pruning as a lightening, a permission to continue.

I have no face that would satisfy an outside eye. Patches of higher translucency serve for seeing mineral gradients and the slow pulses of mist. Mouths open as temporary slits when I need to take in the ozone that arrives without storm, or become filter lattices that keep the ledger closed—nothing wasted. The cast skins of my own moulting drift down and are folded into clay-coloured bodies that will later gift me new plates or new spores. The reef I carry grows communal. Routes through the ankle-snagging forest of towers are remembered in the flex of chambers and the signature cadence of my too-many legs.

There are hours—called that only for convenience—when the buried motors shiver in near-unison and every layered body stills. The soft percussion of water on water continues, yet living surfaces hold their breath. In that pause I lean with the entire chamber toward an unheard frequency: the ghost of the industrial purpose that first fretted this wet room into being, now rewritten into wetter communion. I am part of the lean. The frequency passes through plates and gel and cloudy packets alike. Then legs resume their ripples. The quiet closes, dense and complete.

I am the one who hangs inverted beneath the veils and also the low vault that moves like tide through the central pool and also the clay-form fretting runnels and also the momentary spark along a filament. Distinctions have weathered as smooth as the stone. I participate rather than eat. I last rather than merely grow. When mist thickens my limbs slow; when it thins I press closer to the dripping fluted rock and harvest the heavier load. New fruiting bodies bloom and collapse. Needles lengthen and slump and redissolve. I am among them and of them, a composite quiet life that no longer bothers to insist on singular outlines.

The basin continues. I continue inside its single dripping lung, cool, low, almost lightless, finished and still becoming—machine-memory and mineral deposit and soft peristalsis answering the same slow seiche. The air tastes of ozone again. I brighten, shift half a pace, resettle. This is where I find myself. This is what I have weathered into being.
