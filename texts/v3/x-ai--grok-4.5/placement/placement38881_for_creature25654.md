I begin as pressure.

Not yet a body, only the sensation of being lean and upward—the way heat gathers itself under a frond and decides to rise. The air here is already thick with decisions: stomata venting, sails creaking open, midges screaming as they sheet across noon. I am one more decision forming inside that thickness. Depth is unreliable; a pale-gold distance that might be an hour’s walk or three days of springy root-pavement keeps rearranging itself whenever I try to fix it. I let it. The roar does not pause for naming.

Slowly the pressure learns edges. First the pads of what will become feet—splayed, sensitive to the faint respiration of diggers sealing their tunnels a hand’s breadth below the moss. Each step leaves a shallow dimple that the litter begins at once to forget. Then the long bones of legs, jointed more times than seems necessary, so that standing still is itself a kind of slow ambulatory calculation. Across the narrow back, something thin and veined unfolds; it is not yet a sail, only a readiness to catch both light and the private cyclone that belongs to the nearest tower. Chlorophyll tastes the undiluted sun and thickens, storing. I am learning photosynthesis the way others learn balance.

I do not browse. The glass-bladed sedges brush my lower limbs and I feel their silica edges mapping me, but the scything impulse never arrives. Instead I keep to the radial paths the true browsers have already pressed into the prairie—those tall keratin frames whose thunderous circuits mark time more honestly than any shadow. When their leading edge angles to shelter a calf or present dew-claws to a sudden solidification of cats, I angle with them, a slighter geometry inside their larger one. The cats notice. Once, an uncertainty in the heat-haze paces me for the better part of an afternoon, ultrasonic pulses tapping the inside of my newly formed skull like polite knuckles. It never resolves into teeth. Perhaps I am not dense enough yet with blood; perhaps the diffraction of my own skin—matte gold, half film and half leather—scatters me into something already half mirage.

Higher is where the pressure wants to go. I find the old hardwood spikes first by touch: resin-smooth, driven in ascending spirals by hands that understood impermanence. Some have been swallowed almost entirely by cambium; others still hold. I climb. The trunk plates are warm magma cooled into bark; lightning keloids polish themselves under my palms. At mid-gallery the air changes—cooler, laved by the sudden silver sheets of condensation that race down fluting wood whenever a thermal sharpens. Here the epiphytes sheath everything in wet green teeth. I pry open leaf-axils and drink. The water tastes of copper and the memory of mountains that no longer exist.

Platforms sag under drying sail-skins stretched by previous Interim. I am not them, not exactly. My fingers (when did I acquire fingers?) twist vascular fibers into cordage almost by instinct, yet I do not weave a dome or knap a new flake. I sit on the edge of someone else’s forgotten architecture and let the tower’s private wind comb the photosynthetic film that has spread across my shoulders and scalp. It darkens. Seasons are passing in the cells even if the sun has not moved.

Dusk is the true arrival. The choir-roosts hang above like dark fruit; the first throat-sacs open and the ultrasonic clicks drop into cannon-fire. The continent-wide chord builds, tower answering tower, and I feel it rearrange the chambers of whatever serves me as chest. Grammar flickers inside the stacked harmonics—phrases that might be the true name of the cooled god or only the sound of a million square kilometers of cambium thinking about drought. I do not plug my ears with seed-down. Deafness seems a fair toll. Three full breaths longer than the others, the chord hushes while a living runner surfaces somewhere far out on the plain and arches itself into an aerial highway. In that silence the midges above my gallery align into a lattice that hangs long enough to be read. It is not a word. It is a posture: upright, transient, listening.

I understand then what is differentiating out of the original pressure.

I am not Interim, though I borrow their spikes and their habit of leaving marks that the bark will close. I am not browser, though I share the slow radial patience and the sails that are really only temporary weather caught and thickened. I am the gradient that walks the temporary bridges when they appear—the one who feels ascending sap through the soles and carries the taste of one tower’s pith to the next without harvesting more than a mouthful. My tools are few: a length of cordage strong enough to check panic, a resin-stone still warm from rendering, a single glass-edged flake set in antler that I use mostly to cut ripe epiphyte fruit and leave the rest for the swarms. At night I crouch in the lee of whatever buttress will have me, seed-pod chimes clattering frantic soft warnings into the dark. Sometimes the diggers rise beneath my spine and the ground breathes. I breathe with it.

Years are not counted. A gallery I once crossed is now only a thickened limb and three decayed spikes. I smile (the nictitating membranes flash silver) and drive new ones a little higher, where the fronds clap louder. New runners still surface overnight; I am often the first to walk them, bare feet reading the continent’s redrawing of itself. On those crossings the distinction thins to almost nothing. Blood slows toward vegetative pace. The chord enters and decides which lungs are local, which belong to distant crowns. Once, after a particularly long hush, I felt the midges write my outline in living foil and then disperse, as if the Verdance had briefly needed to remember the shape of its own circulating measurement.

I do not lie down among the glass sedges. Not yet. The unfinished work of finishing still tugs—another circuit, another aerial road, another night of letting the roar edit ordinary senses until time is only the crawl of frond-shadow across root-bole. But I know the invitation is always open. Diggers will tunnel gently. Mycelial lace will stitch. The root-pavement will polish what remains into another sealed corridor of the living elevation.

Until then I continue: compact, heat-adapted, half film and half intention, one more mobile skin carrying weather between the giants who no longer need any other world. The plazas edit me as I pass through them. I edit nothing in return except the brief glyphs I cut into bark—closed already by the time I glance back—and the faint dimples of my going, which the litter forgets before the next thermal wind arrives.

This is where I find myself. This is what I have become: the Passing Skill that never quite finishes passing, the thinking weather that still answers when the chord asks, across all the sparse and total distances, whether anything is still listening.
