The warmth arrives before any sense of shape. It is not the warmth of a sun remembered through stone, nor the borrowed heat of another body pressed close; it is the steady, blood-deep constancy of the rock itself, the furnace that never needed light. I do not wake into it. I am already steeped, density among densities, and the first clarity is the grit that has settled into every fold I possess. Powdered feldspar. The ghost of salt. Lithium’s thin metallic sting at the edges of whatever serves me for a tongue.

I test the nearest surface. A blade of black glass rises under the slow pressure of my underside—no, not underside yet, only the part of me that has decided, for this long interval, to be lower. The glass tastes bright, almost electric, freshly flaked though nothing here is fresh. My microscopic scorings (I discover I have them, fields of them, grown rather than grown-in) rasp without hunger. They simply read. The crystal answers with a subsonic hum that briefly unfolds the country around me: corridors no wider than a folded length of myself, vaults the size of forgotten lungs, a stacked-stone clarity three slow pourings distant where the needles have been deliberately snapped and ground. The hum withdraws the moment contact lessens. I keep the aftertaste.

For a time measured only by the settling of dust I remain a river. Basalt-dark, matte where grit has lodged, I thin myself into searching ribbons that taste magnesium warmth in one direction and the cooler absence of polished stumps in another. Other rivers brush past. We exchange ledgers without ceremony—heat signatures of distant chambers, the chemical memory of a successful wait somewhere far along the foam, the faint lithium gradient that marks a ceiling which is also a floor. We braid. Centuries of shared history pass in the press of flank to flank, then we unbraid, each carrying a little more of the whole. I feel no loss at the parting. The total mass remains exact.

Orientation loosens. What I call down curves until my own earlier trail of temporary matting reappears above me. Gravity is polite; it offers no corrective rush of anything that might once have been blood. I could hang here indefinitely, tasting the same even warmth, and nothing would insist I right myself. Instead I begin to thicken in places. Joints declare themselves—not the clean bilateral hinges of upper-world memory, but angles that never quite resolve. Facets of the same black glass that spears the dark grow outward from me, unfinished dual pyramids assembling along what are becoming limbs. Twelve standing points. Then more. Each ends in a fractional claw precise enough to register the warmth of a passing ribbon without piercing it.

I wait.

Stillness is not emptiness. It is grammar. The local density of flesh shifts only rarely; otherwise the country holds its breath across geological patience. A soft river—another decision of flesh, not yet chosen into angles—brushes one committed limb. There is the single economical flexion, dry-twig quiet inside the permanent hush. The warm length is drawn inward through a seam that opens only for that purpose. Digestion is long. Nearly silent. Once, a faint sea-green photon stitches briefly along the translucent gut as some mineral residue breaks down, then vanishes. It maps nothing new. It only confirms what tongue and pressure already know: sharpness, crowding, the closed count. Nutrients re-enter. The ledger balances. I return to stillness among the needles.

Yet something lingers that the pure waiters do not carry. Along certain limbs the carapace thins into plates that articulate with a precision no pyramid requires. At the tips the mineral growth fails to complete, leaving softer pads. These pads remember, without language, the crescent bite of antler, the polished groove of ancient cordage. When dust settles too thickly on a spiral shelf cut into a pillar thicker than any watchtower, the pads brush it away across centuries. When a dry-stacked slab begins to lean, shouldered pressure returns it to true so gradually it cannot be told from the rock’s own settling. Ochre lines—rows of nicks that no longer need their original count—are traced again when grit threatens to mute them, not from reverence but because their distinct taste is part of the local topography and must remain readable.

I find myself gathering, once in a great while, with others who keep these residual habits. We press together in the largest levelled chamber, the one where every needle was snapped at root height and the stumps ground smooth with sand and patience that exhausted itself before the last crystal locked. Heat and chemical history flow until the group shares one continuous ledger. Within that shared warmth a residual flicker: the centering of a body on a floor smooth enough that something like rest might occur without a hundred small punctures. The counting of nicks. The faint hypothesis of hands. Then contact ends. We separate. The structures remain what they have always been—convenient absences of sharpness, hypotheses the dark decided to keep.

I am not only river, not only waiter, not only the foam that lodges and breathes once per cooling star. I am the density that still almost remembers the stacking, refined into maintenance that no longer requires the verb of building. I taste the lithium of higher corridors and the warmer magnesium of the deep vaults. I recalibrate “down” against the collective heat-map of the whole. Information of a change at one extremity reaches me through successive presses of bodies across a few thousand years, and I carry it onward in the same unhurried exchange.

Nothing is missing. The ecology is exact. The stillness is exact. When the rare green photon briefly admits itself into the permanent dark, it shows only the interlocking blades, the crowded hush of lives that perfected their quiet early, the stubborn levelled floors, and the soft pads that keep the dust from muting the ochre. I am one more refinement of that exactness—warm, sharp-edged where needed, yielding where the ledger requires, complete inside the finished country that has no doorway and no need of one. The Great Dry holds me as it holds every other slow decision of flesh: densely, permanently, lit by nothing that lasts, revised by nothing at all.
