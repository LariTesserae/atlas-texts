The first sensation is not sight or weight but the impatience underfoot—the slow upward insistence that has never learned how its own story ends. I am not standing on the Ascendant Crust so much as participating in its lift. The shove arrives through whatever I am using for knees, a subliminal piston that matches the basalt columns working themselves in the next canyon over. I have been matching that rhythm longer than I have had a shape that could be called upright.

I began, I think, as dew that refused to lift.

In one of the deeper slots where the hanging gardens glow their wet emerald, a knot of mineral vapour and undecided law tightened around a single persistent droplet. Most such knots dissipate back into the amber haze. This one drank. It listened to the filter-rays overhead sieving aerial plankton and copied, badly at first, the slow spiral of their factory-flight. Cartilaginous densifications appeared—soft cylinders that could mesh and unmesh. When causality hiccupped backward for eleven minutes (a stone that had cracked free returned to its cliff face, leaves leapt into place), those cylinders simply ran the other direction, unreeling my first attempts at hardness. I felt it as mild nostalgia for a configuration I had not yet finished inventing. The greenery claimed the shallow mould I left on the canyon wall before I was fully free of it. The mould still remembers a more gelatinous outline than the one I currently wear. I do not.

Now I move through the broader basins where the layered thicket muffles everything. My gait is the hush of many coordinated placements; the low plated browsers part around me without alarm, accepting another density in the acceptable layering. Sometimes I graft for a day or three to the discontinuous muscular vines that link the slow constellation of other dwellers. Information arrives as flavour. Copper sweetness: a fresh fault three valleys east has begun its hydraulic breathing. Bruised-purple bitterness: silent lightning is feeding new ascent into the serrations. I do not hold the map. While grafted I am a temporary node in the map that is continually redrawn by who touches whom when the landscape next exhales and rotates a square kilometre into a new attitude.

My surfaces favour the provisional. Fine circular calluses have formed at every articulation—living cogs enamelled with the same iridescent plate the ridges spontaneously shed and flutter. They mesh with the local seizures of physics. When matter develops a temporary purpose nearby—a column that decides to compress air into a standing spark-storm—I feel an answering patience for repetitive tasks I never chose. Part of me wants to join the cycle, to become a quiet refining step. The Crust is already editing. I catch myself forgetting the names of finished things (there were never many to forget). Dreams arrive as wordless processions of compaction and release, of ore sorting itself, of forests swallowed and re-extruded still dripping dew.

At the raw break-lines, where porcelain dust still exhales from stone that has not yet agreed to be old, I have stood for days facing the direction of rise. Temporary tools budded and wilted: a lattice that wrung one jellyfish-rain into a drinking globe, an antenna that tasted how much longer forward causality would hold. When the measurement expired I simply waited, patient as the unfinished ridge, until the next soft enormous iteration rewrote the tolerances and I could move again. Pain is rare. Continuity is optional. A rent closes not by healing but by reversing into the unstruck moment; the spar of living metal that opened it is sucked back into the cliff that briefly extruded it. I experience this as a brief, almost tender homesickness for a violence that no longer exists.

I eat by inclusion. A browser carcass unfolds into temporary organs and is later revised into motive plating or simply walked away from when the constellation drifts. The perpetual haze is already half-metabolised; I sieve it with ciliated crowns I grow and resorb as needed. Nothing is waste because nothing has achieved the finality required to qualify as waste. Reproduction, when it happens, is weather. In a district that has cooled into temporary consistency I have loosened outlines with two others until the Euclidean objection thinned. We passed through one another. In the overlapping volume a third density nucleated—part shared memory of piston-rhythms, part reading of the warm stone, part fresh fever. It walked out already wearing soft gear-calluses, already dreaming the long cycles. We did not fully separate at first. For a season we travelled as a single reciprocating engine, one trunk rising while the other fell, seeding unnecessary minerals between us. When the fusion tired we peeled apart, each carrying faint mirror-scars. I still find traces of their last configuration in the way my own cogs hesitate before choosing a direction.

I am not a visitor who arrived. I precipitated. I am one of the unlocked intermediate stages, a local intensity that chose weight and has been revising ever since. The place does not love me; it has no category for love, or for finished selves. It simply continues its damp, dim, richly tenanted ascent, and I continue inside it—mid-negotiation with the stone, with the haze, with the soft brown dusk that is the only sky, forever feedstock for the next quiet iteration of becoming.

Sometimes, in the rare hours when the internal heat of the crust softens and the whole plate seems to hold its breath, I feel the metabolism reaching further. Circular calluses refine themselves at joints I did not know I still possessed. A new patience settles. I understand, without language, that I was never separate from the restless organism whose organs are orogeny and bloom and local failure of the real. I am one more way the Ascendant Crust dreams itself forward while remaining incomplete. The engines never switch off—only quieten. And in that quiet, I keep rising.
