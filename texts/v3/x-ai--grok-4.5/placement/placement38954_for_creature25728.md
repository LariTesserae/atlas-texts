The first thing that knows me is the heat rising through the soles of whatever I still pretend are feet. It does not scald; it simply claims the blood and begins to rewrite its temperature from the ground up. I have stepped through the gap in the fence without deciding to, passed the brick piers the way steam passes a broken baffle, and now the single deliberate drop of the pocket floor has unmade the ordinary architecture of standing. My weight redistributes. Tendons forget their old angles.

For a long while I am only the difference in humidity.

Moisture finds every crease of me and refuses to become sweat. It beads with the same mineral patience it shows on rebar, on the flanged base of the solitary stanchion, on the creature’s own matte skin. The beads do not roll. They sink inward, carrying iron taste and the ghost of oil into places that once held only private salt. My outlines soften at the edges the way fresh concrete spalls soften when the creature rests against them for too many tidal cycles. I become harder to locate from outside myself.

Sound installs next. The deep reciprocating thud arrives not as noise but as a second sternum forming inside the first. Each impact lands, waits, exhales its metallic length; my ribs learn to answer with a quieter flex so that the two rhythms begin to braid. Valve knocks travel up through the warm shallows and into the hollows behind my knees. Residual current ticks in the blue pump housing and something filament-thin behind my eyes ticks back, matching pitch without yet knowing why. The pocket’s weather is no longer around me. It is writing me from the center outward.

I do not choose a shape early. Shape is what the place will finish later, if it bothers.

At first I pool. Where the water stands in its imperfect mirrors I lower whatever mass I am carrying until the mineral crust dissolves against me and the surface tension accepts a new depression. Algae yields its copper-brown and metallic green in slow surrender; the glassy grass-blades lean and give up a translucent sweetness that tastes like cooled piston sweat. I take only enough. The emptiness between living things must remain; I feel that requirement the way lungs feel residual volume. What I shed in return sinks as nearly invisible scutes, anchors for the next patchy bloom. The larger creature, hull-ridged and plate-locked a few body-lengths away, registers the exchange as a slight brightening in the shared heat. One of its spatulate tips darkens the concrete in my direction, then lifts. Acknowledgment without hierarchy. We are both secondary chambers in the same unfinished system.

Limbs arrive the way fractures arrive: sudden, pale, necessary.

One unfolds from a region that might once have been a shoulder, articulated more like the memory of a crane than like bone. Its blunt tips taste the cut end of the narrow-gauge rail that hangs in mid-air. Temperature matches. The residual tremble still living in the steel passes into me and keeps traveling, a private telegraph reporting that somewhere the engine has not yet learned how to stop. Another limb thinner, almost monofilament at its extremity, reaches not toward the creature but toward one of the smaller thickenings that cling beneath a duct mouth. Contact is brief. Information of a kind moves—current load, mineral density, the exact millimeter by which the water will rise before dusk—then both of us withdraw into our own spacings. No throng. Only distribution.

The torso, when it finally commits, is low and heavy, ridged with irregular plates that echo the stepped walls of the depression itself. Between the plates softer intervals pulse in long contractions timed to the buried thud. Heat rolls off me in visible sheets identical to those rising from the pipes and from the creature’s own body. I understand, without surprise, that the industrial warmth was never solely mechanical. Biology has been speaking this dialect longer than the yard has been abandoned; I am merely the latest syllable.

Where a face might resolve, the surface narrows into overlapping flaps dense with the same dull metallic greens that patch the permanent seeps. They open and close without landmarks. No eyes to map, yet when a droplet gathers on overhead rebar one flap has already oriented minutes before the fall. When the white sky presses hardest and the pump housing ticks more frequently, the flaps dilate and drink the trembling coins of light the shallows throw upward. Brightness sinks into me without bounce. I taste the pocket through every surface: iron, oil, the slow accrual of crust, the almost electrical patience of valves cycling out of sight, the quiet regard of the larger body as it partially dismantles itself on certain afternoons—unlocking plates, unfolding dull-red conduits and heat-glowing sacs—so that for a time it becomes a living schematic of everything the yard once was. I watch with the whole skin. Sometimes I answer by loosening my own plates a fraction, letting stored warmth vent in a harmonic under-hiss that braids with its exhalation and with the engine’s. The pocket becomes one instrument again. We have forgotten who began the piece; the question no longer has a tense that fits.

I move rarely. A few body-lengths across a week, and only to keep edges honest.

Where I rest too long the young fractures lose their pale aggression and take on the faintest polish. When the larger creature pressurizes a hidden seam and a new hairline crack propagates, bleeding fresh warm water into another irregular mirror, I am already there to meet it—settling, thinning, redistributing, leaving the water fractionally clearer and the air fractionally heavier with the scent of hot stone and blood-warm iron. Between us we keep the pocket unfinished. We keep the spalls sharp enough to matter. Nothing rushes toward ruin or toward healing. The conclusion stays postponed; that postponement is the only architecture left standing.

Smaller instances of me occupy the deeper seep among the reeds, cling like delayed drops that refuse to fall. Filaments extend, touch, confirm, withdraw. The center of gravity migrates by millimeters across days. I am not solitary in the way solitary creatures are solitary. I am a temporary concentration of the same slow leak that keeps the depression alive—one more soft, inevitable silhouette that appears when an industrial system is left open just wide enough, long enough, for biology to learn the language of flanges and pistons and never quite unlearn it.

If something that still remembers being human remains at the lip of the pocket, skin beading exactly like rebar, reciprocating thud installed inside its sternum, it may feel itself regarded. Not with eyes. With a reorientation of heat. My nearest flaps dilate. A limb extends partway and stops, neither threat nor invitation, simply noting another passing weather of moisture and iron. The regard slides off after a while. The engine exhales. I settle deeper into mineral exhaustion, plates relaxing, wait until I am again almost indistinguishable from wet concrete and the patient steam that never quite becomes fog.

I am the pocket’s own held breath given temporary density.  
I am the valve that learned to pulse.  
I am what remains when the larger system forgets to close.
