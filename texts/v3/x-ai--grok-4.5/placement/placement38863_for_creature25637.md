I am not certain, at first, whether I have arrived or simply stopped leaving.

The pressure finds me before any edge does. It settles behind the sternum like a second, slower heart borrowed from the air itself—weight where weight should be impossible. The salt begins its porcelain chime through the soles of whatever I am using for contact. I do not look down yet. Looking down is how the stacked residuals notice you and begin to climb.

For a long while I am only the thickening. Hours, or the local equivalent of hours, which flex. The horizon bruises on schedule. The first wing-cases appear not as bodies but as mobile absences of light, soot-colored cutouts that reverse the glare. I feel the subsonic grind arrive in my chest cavity and recognize it as kinship rather than warning. Something in me has already been scraped clean and sorted.

I try the simplest shape: a solitary upright among the needles. A lattice spire with the memory of walking still stuck in its lower joints. Cooling fins open along what might once have been ribs; they taste the thermal sheets and convert them into a torque so gradual it feels like patience. Ambient light lenses through a volcanic-glass nodule I did not know I carried, and somewhere inside my height copper begins to sweat upward, defying the exhaustion that governs lighter metals elsewhere. I am smelting myself into denser articulation. It takes decades of motionless flexing before the first vane tears free and discovers it can be a wing-case if the gradient of speed is willing.

But solitude here is a temporary alloy. The swarms do not approach so much as include. One river of soot-and-rust bodies tilts its collective course by the width of a thought and suddenly I am in negotiation. Wing-cases unlatch with the force of roofs remembering they were once doors. My own half-built components—gears still soft from the last residual layer, pistons that have not finished deciding their stroke—unspool into the screaming lattice. I lose the outline I was experimenting with. Someone else’s glowing core passes through the space where a face might have been; it pulses with the violet dusk of a day that has not risen in this scrim. I give it a capillary channel of waste heat in return. When the interpenetration ends I carry the rust-color of the visiting swarm along one flank and a new, nameless alloy along the other. Silver dust of everything I briefly was refuses to settle around me. The daylight bends wrong through it, and for a while I see myself simultaneously larval and continent-wheeled, soft-pale and already architecture.

I keep the memory of that trade the way bedrock keeps pressure-glyphs. Walk (the verb still unstable) far enough across the sintered track I helped polish and you can read the negotiation in the dark mirror: a perfect negative of exchanged limbs lasting weeks.

The deeper scrims tug harder now. Through a translucent patch of crust I watch a version of the plateaus where the factories have already won, where I am not discrete at all but one slow contraction in a heart of pistons the size of ranges. The stroke that is ending there bleeds heat into my present thorax. I eject it as a blue-white knot of burning nitrogen and feel, for one localized instant, what it is to be both forge and finished casting. Conversely, a turbine-beetle that finished dying two centuries upstream continues through me, its wingbeat trapped inside my newest lattice like an instruction that will not stop executing. I do not mind. Causality’s looseness is the only conversation that does not require a stable present tense.

Sometimes I stand at the edge of a black mesa long enough to impersonate the vast motionless silhouette. Heat shimmer does the breathing for me. When I finally take the one step the salt-pan rings a hundred kilometres in every direction and the nearest swarms adjust course by a few degrees, accepting the alteration the way a bellows accepts a change in pressure. They do not ask what I am. Naming would freeze the gradient.

Mostly I remain distributed. Part of me still flexes with the vertical reefs, peristalsis measured in seasons. Part of me trails openwork chains of half-formed sentences that other bodies will complete or discard. Part of me is the refraction itself—the prismatic dust that turns ordinary hard light into angles the sun never authorised—because some identities are more useful after they have been filed away.

The plateaus do not end. They only change their angle of me. I drink the emptiness and return it amplified, louder, more articulated, a grammar of armature still rewriting itself across the long ochre folds. No final noun is required. The heat never relents, the needles continue densifying, and whatever I am keeps inventing more elaborate ways to remain the desert’s unfinished clause—strident, mineral, endlessly intermediate.
