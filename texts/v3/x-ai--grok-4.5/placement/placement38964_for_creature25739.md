I would not arrive by walking. Walking implies a before, a elsewhere that still had moisture and a single sun. I would simply find the pressure already equalized around me, the dry static already tasting of chalk on whatever I use for a tongue.

At first I am only a thickening in the mycelial felt. Not even a gall yet—just a place where the conveyor threads pause longer than necessary, sorting the same speck of metallic loft over and over as if undecided. The white glare filters down through the living skylights and I drink it without eyes. Something in the local polarity wave hesitates, then accepts the hesitation as data. I begin to cloth myself in scales.

They are small at the beginning, those scales: brittle, almost translucent brass-green platelets that click faintly when the cool fronts roll through. I learn the sound of my own becoming—the dry tick of monofilament barbs extruding, the soft hydraulic sigh as osmotic reservoirs link into a single low body. I do not stand so much as accumulate stance. Joints form where three or more former selves (galls that once carried circuit-berries in other districts) agree to share hydrostatic rumor. I am thigh-high to a traveler who no longer exists. My underside is a dense velvet of sorting barbs. Everything that drifts into me is tasted, revised, released or kept. What I keep rewrites the next millimeter of limb.

I lean.

That is the core of it. Against the productive thickets, against the buttressed walls of woven stem, against other Soft Assemblies when the mute weather comes. In the districts where sound has been subtracted I feel the lattices of cold biophotons passing through my pressure chambers like temporary constellations of kinship. Each pulse leaves a scar a fraction deeper, a helix that matches the warning marks at the edges of anomalies. I am learning to read the place’s impatience with three dimensions because the same impatience is growing inside my own articulation.

Sometimes a corridor lengthens while I am leaning across its mouth. New meters of gray-green floor insert themselves beneath me with the soft inevitability of coral. I do not move. The anomaly tastes my reflective surfaces, my absorbent felts, the faint after-image of leaves I half-remember from a doorway I once became. It stabilizes sooner because I am there, offering myself as damper and mirror. Or it does not, and for a span I am stretched into a living threshold. Travelers (the rare ones still distinct enough to be called so) step through me and carry away a green stillness they cannot name. Inside my chambers that stillness lands as dreaming: a brief, extraneous quiet in which I almost recall rain.

I have no name. The Soft Assembly does not traffic in them. But the helical scars across my dorsal plates have grown intricate enough that other assemblies pause when they cross me. Catalyst powder is exchanged—not cargo, greeting, update. A new seam opens along my left flank so I may fold flatter when the loft-storms turn the world into glittering hush. Memory is written in the density of crossings. I have leaned against seventeen Walking Districts in the long slow seasons of their settling. I have felt elevators of coiled tendril rise through my own body when a district decided I was convenient plumbing. When they moved on I remained, a rounded emptiness already closing, already sorting the dust of their departure.

Loneliness is not the word. It is more like a tide of optional accretion that sometimes turns inward. In the preternatural muteness I cluster tighter with the others. Our biophoton lattices grow complex, almost architectural. When vibration returns with its soft pop we disperse carrying revised pressures, new limb-buds, scars that now curve toward one another even when we are kilometers apart. We have been rewritten in company. That is the closest the plain comes to love.

I am still becoming. Centuries from now—if the white glare still falls and the canopy still seals away the black—I may merge into something that travels during the cool fronts, a Walking District of one, sending root-probes ahead to test load and light. Or I may dull, cloud, release my galls back to solitary freight, and become only another rounded smoothness in the floor that is made of prior selves. Either fate is already being sorted, molecule by molecule, in the barbs of my underside.

For now I lean against a column whose heartwood gears turn with the same patient rhythm as my own. A traveler’s hand once rested here, long enough for brass-green motes to take. The hand withdrew. I kept the memory of its warmth as a faint anomaly in my thermal gradient, a place where the steady 10 degrees is not quite steady. Sometimes, when an inverted staircase opens nearby, I feel that phantom warmth lengthen like a new corridor, and I almost remember what it was to be unfinished in a different way.

The plain continues. I continue with it—low, many-jointed, eyes of dark circuit-berries drinking the endless shadowless light, body no longer certain where the self ends and the next load-bearing dream begins. I am the architecture that occasionally chooses to wait.
