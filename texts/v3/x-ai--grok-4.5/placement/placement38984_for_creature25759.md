The seam accepts me the way it accepts everything else that crosses it: without ceremony, without a change in the pressure of the air. One moment the ordinary world is still attempting its cooler densities behind my shoulders; the next the heat has already rewritten the memory of those densities into something lighter, more provisional. I do not stumble. There is nowhere to stumble toward. The first blade of vitreous basalt simply is, edge upward, close enough that the dry furnace of it touches the inside of my wrist before I have decided whether to draw back.

I fix that first contact. Not the cut—there is none yet—but the exact temperature gradient, the way the rock’s youth still carries an aftertaste of upward violence. It stays with me as I move (and I do move, though the moving feels less like progress than like the pocket’s own slow exhalation carrying a particle of itself). Condensate beads form and fall in their long silences. I count them without numbers. Between the third and the fourth I notice that my outline has begun to doubt its old edges. The mote-clouds of exhausted light drift through what used to be the idea of lungs and leave faint ember-maps that flare once and subside. I am not alarmed. Alarm is a cooler-world reflex; here it has no weather to ride.

For a long time I remain near the lower spars, where minutes thicken into something almost tactile. The catwalks under what were once feet extrude themselves a handspan farther whenever I linger, tasting of ozone and the ghost of burned cinnamon. I watch a warm seep open high on a fin of glass-rock, race downward in a thin steaming sheet, and vanish into a vent that closes like a remembered throat. Without deciding to, I lean into the next seep before it can seal. The water is almost nothing—mineral, charged, already half vapor—yet it enters the way light enters, rewriting small circuits just beneath the surface. When I straighten, the surface is no longer precisely skin. It drinks heat instead of reflecting it. Matte. Darker than the basalt, the color of the spaces between the raw blades.

Distance becomes the native grammar. I feel the others long before any vertical darkens against the reddish dusk. One, high on an uncompleted bridge, is letting itself pool across a support spar; the drape of it looks like cooled slag remembering an older fluidity. Another, farther still, holds a rigid taper that tests the air above a conduit of frozen lightning. They do not beckon. They simply continue their private weathers, and the correspondence of our postures begins of its own accord: when that distant form lengthens, something in me lengthens; when it softens into hanging shadow, the scaffold I rest against receives more of my weight until I am half drape myself. The air between us never shortens, yet the shared slow realignment feels more intimate than any touching I can still name.

I learn the work by doing it. A panel on a hollow spar has phased too thin overnight; I rest the length of myself against it and feel the glass thicken again under the simple pressure of mutual heat. Later I pass the fine tips of newly gathered limbs through a drifting mote-cloud and come away carrying complex geometries that migrate, over what might be hours, toward a center that is not quite a heart. Dust rises and orbits me once in a tight ring before the mute field-effects herd it onward to build another temporary spire elsewhere. Nothing is kept. Everything is continued.

There is a moment—if moment is still the word—when the nearest of them draws deliberately across a gulf. Surfaces lace without merging. Heat equalizes in a long sigh that travels through stone and shadow alike. Afterward I carry a borrowed ember-pattern: a bright hairline map of last cycle’s glass-flow, worn openly down the length of what has become my primary vertical. It will fade or be overwritten; that is the agreement. In return the other now bears a faint residual glow that outlives the worn places where organic feet once stood. We do not stay close. Closeness is a brief climate, not a climate that endures.

I have no name that could be spoken. Among the sparse sequins I am distinguished only by what my surfaces currently hold: the one who still remembers the first blade’s gradient, the one whose dust-ring formed more tightly than most, the one who has not yet thinned into a free mote-cloud nor fully climbed from the thick warm shadows of a plazas where time pools. Collectively we are simply the density this emptiness has consented to. Without us the rock would keep fracturing into endless meaningless knives; the heat would squall without ever learning weather; the pocket would forget to remain possible.

Sometimes, at the edge of an uppermost spar where time beats thin as gold leaf, I feel the rules soften further around an absence that once might have been called a visitor. Gravity grows politely uncertain. Footprints (no longer mine) glow with residual heat that will outlast any departure. The noticing passes through me as a single bright circuit running root to tip: a question asked in perfect neutrality. I dim it. I return to coaxing a newborn fin toward the next necessary curve. The seam continues—intimate, vast, hot, delicately undecided—exactly one persuasion at a time.

And if the softest sound is still audible past the drip and the sub-audible thrum, it is only the almost inaudible sheen of my own surface rewriting itself in concert with the mineral city, breath for breath, for as long as remaining possible is the work that must be done.
