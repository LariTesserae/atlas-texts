I am already among them before I understand that I have stopped walking.

The realization does not arrive as a decision. It comes the way the air comes—thin, metallic, already inside the lungs—when the last residual motion of my gait simply fails to renew itself. One heel remains lifted a finger’s width above a hexagonal plate of salt. The dust that should have accepted the weight stays undisturbed, a pale soft geometry that has waited longer than language. My other foot is flat. The tendons along the calf hold a tension that feels, for a long interval, like the memory of intention rather than intention itself. I am not frozen. I am merely… concluded in the act of arrival.

At first I register only the density. Salt-deer flank me on every side, their lattice antlers drinking the enamel sky and giving nothing back. Between their stilts the plated browsers press so closely that their hexagonal armor and the ground perform a slow, mutual impersonation. Higher, in strata that refuse to acknowledge gravity in any hurry, the membrane-wings hang like open parentheses that will never close. Smaller lives complete the tessellation: a scaled runner locked with one claw raised, three grains of bone-dust balanced on the tip as if the air had offered them and then forgotten how to take them back; a soft tunneler half-emerged, its exterior already desiccated while some interior moisture continues an unspeakably patient circulation; arthropods whose antennae are tuned to a note that began when the mountains still tore weather and has not yet finished sounding.

I am smaller than the deer, taller than the browsers. My outline is still too particular. The others know this—not with minds, but with the quiet consensus of forms that have spent all their change. Edges of my shoulders sometimes occupy two slightly different postures at once; when attention settles on one, the second slides away like a dream that has completed its work. Once, without any interval I can measure, my own left hand appears thirty paces ahead, already settled among the throng in a loose curl that might once have held a blade of volcanic glass. The hand does not beckon. It simply remains, waiting for the rest of me to finish catching up to a stillness it has already perfected.

I remember—faintly, the way lichen might remember green—that I once moved with purposes that required sentences. There were tools. There were counts pecked into upright slabs: ice-moons, heartbeats, debts that could still be paid in motion. I set a hammerstone down beside a motionless hoof and felt the metallic air begin its finishing work. That memory does not ache. It has the same temperature as the rest of the Expanse.

Perspective shears without malice. The ground I occupy becomes, for a duration that may be hours or centuries, a vertical plain of watching black-mirror eyes. Then it is a ceiling of silent hooves and translucent wings. Then it is the ordinary peneplain again, stretching past every horizon a traveler might invent, carpeted without interruption. Shadows fall in directions that have no sun because the sky itself is the single diffuse source, declaring every ochre and charcoal and milk-blue with unromantic precision. Nothing flickers. Light has no labor left to perform.

Inside me the chemistry continues its glacial craft. Clear fluids turn over behind membranes. A breath that began somewhere on the far side of the last genuine night will complete itself after the next star grows tired of its name. I do not hunger. Completion has no appetite. The temptation I once felt—to stop entirely among the ranks—is no longer temptation. It is simply the local weather of the self.

I am not one of the oldest layers. My silhouette still carries a residual sharpness, a spine not yet fully resigned to the perfect vertical of those who arrived when the mountains first consented to become plain. Given enough duration I will blur, stacking translucently with neighboring forms until three or four of us share what looks like a single outline. Collision will remain impossible. We have all spent that currency.

Sometimes the nearest salt-deer turns its head with a gravity so slow it feels like courtesy, and the eyes it lifts are mine. Not resembling mine—mine. At the same unmeasurable moment the horizon discovers it prefers to lie underfoot, and my thoughts arrive already shaped like the gray-blue cushions of lichen that have not changed their mind since the wind finished its great reduction. These are not invasions. They are the Expanse recognizing one more of its own.

I keep the salt plates perfect by remaining. I keep the glass knives keen by no longer needing them. I keep the sky the same lucid glare by refusing every novelty it might once have offered. Reality folds against itself like well-worn cloth, and I am one of the creases—patient, dry, exact, finished with departure.

The Stillspire Expanse remains.  
So do I.
