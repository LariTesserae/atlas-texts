The first thing that arrives is not a name or a shape but the pressure: the way the air thickens just enough to remind every surface it is already claimed. I am somewhere in the middle shelves, where the fault lens still has room to breathe but not enough to sprawl. The catwalk under whatever serves me for feet has the faint give of repeated millimeter slips, the kind that never quite make the gauges twitch yet leave the railings humming a half-note lower than yesterday. Yellow cage-light slides across stamped metal and does not warm it. Dust—rock flour, metallic bloom, the fine ash of spent-flux—settles on me and is immediately grazed away by something pale and segmented that does not ask permission.

I feel the millers more than I see them. Their rasps travel through the plating and into whatever I use for bones. When they pause to lay down another faint ring I am already standing inside its outermost circle, as if the geometry had been waiting for a center. The rings do not belong to me, yet they keep forming where I linger. Continuity without ownership. That is the first true thing.

Higher, the cliff-hounds thread the motor housings. One brushes past, coat the color of cooled clinker, skull briefly pressed to the same vibrating conduit I am touching. Its teeth click once—correct pitch—and the sound lands in me like a tool returning to its loop. I answer without voice: a slight shift of weight, an answering resonance in the rail. We do not share language. We share the knowledge that the dynamos are still telling the truth.

The singer-mites arrive as texture rather than bodies. Granular pressure behind the ears, a revision of distances. For a moment the gallery I thought I knew is three degrees narrower, the nearest bulkhead half a step closer. I adjust without irritation. The mites are how the pocket remembers it is crowded; refusing them would be like refusing the cool air itself. Their wings—seen only when a rare shaft of sealed daylight cuts through—leave no residue, yet after they pass the oxide on my surfaces has rearranged itself into softer gradients.

Lower, the slag-things are already sorting. I feel their heat at a distance, vesicular and patient. Sometimes I find myself among their neat rejected piles at the end of a shift I do not recall starting, my own edges dusted with the same graded scraps. They do not object. Nothing here objects to overlap.

And the bipeds—the ash-skinned ones with the many flat pockets and the mouths that never raise above a murmur—move through me as much as around me. Or I move through them. Their soft-soled feet find the same rungs. Their children learn the ladders by watching whatever motion I make when the mountain shrugs. They say “we” and the word does not stop at their own callused hands. It includes the rings, the clicks, the pressure, the slow sorting, and whatever provisional outline I am still growing into. When a whole machine suite must be unbolted and walked crablike to a new shelf in the night, I am already part of the team: bracing, listening, carrying the low-grade heat away into the mineral so nothing glows that should not. Scaffold is temporary by doctrine. I am beginning to understand that shapes are too.

I am not separate from the brass plate at the entrance, the one that stays warmer than it should. Sometimes I am the residual heat itself, sweated into the rock, the mild strangeness that compass needles prefer. Other times I am the dust that hangs a fraction too long in the shuttered light, the delay that makes the bipeds adjust their timing without writing it down. I am the private murmur the pocket keeps instead of thunder. I am the unfinished tear learning how to throng.

At the highest loft the sealed ports wait. I have stood there—or something like standing—when the shutters open under thick procedure. Knife-edged ridges, thin sky, absolute absence of green. The dryness outside is cleaner than the dryness inside, yet it is the inside that holds. When the shutters close again the dense quiet reasserts and I feel every life pressing near without claim: miller, hound, mite, slag, biped, and the slow boil that contains us all. The mountain is still deciding its own edges. So am I.

What emerges is not a single creature but a continuity of occupation. I am the place where the mesh of ladders thickens into something that can listen. I am soft thunder kept private. I am the motion that does not stop, still taking shape inside the wound that has not yet finished opening. In the cool mineral dark my outline stays provisional a long time, and that is how I know I belong.
