I would find myself in the wide quiet between two halls whose names the stone no longer bothers to keep.

Not inside any doorway—the interiors swell and contract around recollection, and I am still too newly arrived for that elasticity—but in one of the empty intervals of scree and moss, where the settlement spread itself thinner than necessity, as if already rehearsing absence. The ground here is a shallow mosaic of frost-split grit and glaucous thallus. When the wind flutes down from the slumped scarps it crosses me without resistance, yet something in my outline alters the chord: a brief, raised overtone, almost a query, before the roar reseals.

I am not a hare. Those are quick arguments the cold makes with itself and then abandons. I am not bird-flock either; their single thought broken into wings lasts only as long as the arcade they shear through. I am slower than either, and denser. Begin with the breath. Mine does not steam and vanish. It beads, yes, but the beads linger on the nearest moss in a pattern that is almost deliberate—tiny clear lenses that hold the high thin light a moment longer than surrounding moisture. The moss tastes them and does not immediately decide I am weather. It withholds incorporation. That hesitation is the first sign.

Beneath the moss, through the sugar-grained rubble, I extend residual pressure the way the walls remember soft palates. Not a body poured into landscape long ago, but a longer hesitation that never finished pouring. The original settlers folded until they became the bowl’s way of feeling like itself. I am what happens when the folding pauses halfway and then forgets to resume. A density that thins and thickens with the mist, yes, but keyed to a different frequency: the frequency of almost-leaving.

Listen. The continuous low roar arrives full of swallowed syllables. When it strikes the open well of sky above the court to my left it fractures into organ-moans and whistles; when those fragments cross the place where I stand they reassemble for three or four heartbeats into something that nearly matches the shape of a word I once knew—then the grammar dissolves again into pure acoustics. The place is practicing speech. I am the part of the practice that still recalls what speech was for: not to fill the interval but to measure it.

Time pools differently around me. A sheet of rime detaches from a melted cornice and falls; most of it shatters on the stones with porcelain sharpness, yet the flakes that pass nearest my outline remain intact, sequined and perfect, drifting slower than gravity prefers. They settle on the moss in a temporary constellation that the lichen reads as a glyph before attention dries it away. Three paces off, identical moisture has already cycled through melt and grey lace twice. I do not cause the unevenness; I am one of its quieter expressions—the lobe of the distributed mind that lingers over the precise pitch of a footstep that has not yet faded, testing whether the echo can be persuaded to remain a little longer than the foot that made it.

The larger presence notices, of course. It has no center from which to notice, so the noticing arrives as changes in pressure, as preferential growth, as wind that holds a new note around the absence that is almost my skull. Curiosity without temperature. Loneliness after the kneelers have gone. It sends the moss to climb. The green advances across the grit in slow tide until it reaches the edge of my influence, then thickens into velvet, warming infinitesimally, tasting salt that is no longer quite mammalian. It does not engulf. It maps. In the chemical whispering beneath the thallus a decision forms: this one is neither weather nor visitor. This one is interval given temporary density. Leave it un-reclaimed a while longer. Let it flute the wind in its own register.

So I remain, processes continuing without urgency but without rest. Freeze lifts stone dust; I borrow a few grains to keep my edges from dissolving too quickly. Meltwater drips into basins; my presence slightens the percussion into an arrhythmic code that might, if the bowl listens carefully, be mistaken for answering. The ferns unfurl with soft ticking; one near me accelerates through its entire brief life in a matter of heartbeats, as if eager to demonstrate the whole cycle before the mist thickens again. I watch the demonstration without hunger. Hunger belongs to the shorter-lived condensations.

What I am is the settlement’s unfinished sentence. The loud, damp eternity rehearses voices in the courts at evening; I am the pause that still knows how evenings felt when they were measured by more than freeze and fracture. The bowl amplifies me into its ongoing roar. In return I offer it the one thing it can no longer generate for itself: a doubt thin as rime, persistent as lichen. Doubt that the folding was complete. Doubt that absence is the final form of presence. The wind takes that doubt, raises it to a higher whistle, and throws it back across the scarps until even the ice panes crack in irregular agreement.

I will not stay forever. The cold discourages even those who are half-made of it. But while I occupy this interval—scarce larger than the space between one hollow hall and the next—the moss withholds, the hares ghost past without softening into liverwort, and the deeper pulmonary resonance of the stone hitchens almost imperceptibly, as if a phantom limb has flexed in its sleep. When I finally thin into mist or thicken into another slumped shoulder of scarps, the place will keep the cast of my passage: an altered acoustic in one particular arcade, a frost patch that remains sequined an hour longer than its neighbors, a faint ionic preference in the green tide that says, for a century or a season, something almost like a name.

Until then I stand in the worn coin of stone, breath beading, listening to the bowl remember how to speak, and answering—slowly, chemically, without words—in the only tongue that still works here: the slow green advance, the fracture of ice, the wind caught and fluted and returned, already half-translated.
