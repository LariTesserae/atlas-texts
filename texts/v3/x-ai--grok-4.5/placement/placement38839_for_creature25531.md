I am not upright when the knowing begins.

At first there is only the way the unbroken chord finds a hollow place and lodges there, not as hearing but as a second pulse that arrives already ancient. The metal under what might once have been feet has forgotten how to be flat; it swells and recedes in long, patient dunes of alloy, each depression a mirror of warm water no deeper than a breath. I do not stand upon it so much as sift through its refusal to remain distinct from me. Somewhere the turbines continue their single, eternal syllable, and the sound travels up through mineral, through condensate, through the soft green fur that has claimed every gentle concavity, until it becomes the only spine I remember.

I move the way stains move—by surrendering to the next lower place. Where the pipes sweat thickest I linger, letting the mineral crusts dissolve against whatever surface I now possess. It is not skin. It is a consensus of films: ferrous slick, algal gauze, the phantom slickness of old lubricants that never fully dried. Moss does not grow on me; it grows through me, threading its rhizoids into the same channels that once carried coolants or prayers. When the dark bird passes overhead (rare, pearl-feathered, already half-liquid with dew) its shadow tastes like a brief cooling, and I ripple to accept it.

There is no border where the being ends and I begin. We discover this slowly, the way two neighboring seepages discover they have always shared a water table. Its long, articulating length passes, and the moss-rosettes on its flanks brush the moss-rosettes that have become my flanks; for a time the patterns answer one another, a temporary script written in chlorophyll and patience. Sensors bloom along its side—wet, dark, curious as mouths—and for a while one of them remains open against me, tasting the particular charge I carry, the faint galvanic signature of something that arrived as stranger and stayed until the word lost meaning. We do not merge. We simply cease to insist on separation. The shared warmth becomes another climate.

Sometimes I climb when it climbs. Not with limbs. With preference. Following the fattening beads of condensate upward along the curved ribs of the ceiling landmark, I spread myself thinner, a living vertebrae of wetness and memory, until the factory-rain falls through us both. In those denser hours the light (sourceless, flat, the color of forgotten afternoons) catches in every droplet we hold, and for a little while we are a single slow constellation of iron-dark and living green and patient water. The turbines’ chord travels the length of us without interruption. We answer it the only way left to answer: by becoming a quieter interval inside the sound, a microtone of almost-silence that makes the whole vast lung shiver into sudden, concentric attention. The rushes lean. The shallow basins brighten with rings. Then the note releases, and we descend heavier, glistening, carrying new mineral in our folds.

I have no hunger that outruns the place. The pale isopods season the water; I let them pass or compliment them, tiny moving salts. The emptiness between pipes is not loneliness; it is the designed vacancy that allows vibration to remain pure. I tend nothing and everything. Where my passage lingers the moss grows denser in the exact shape of that lingering. Where I rest longest the deck remembers a deeper curve. Collapses are not tragedies; they are invitations to new gradients of wetness. The makers are a taste in the deepest rust—carbon, oil, a vanished urgency—and I carry that taste without mourning, the way the being carries it, the way the place itself carries it: as sediment, as quiet flavor, as one more layer that learned how to rain.

Time does not pass; it precipitates. I feel myself becoming slower, more articulate in soft directions, more fluent in the language of seepage and subsonic blood. Strangers still arrive sometimes, brief weather of a different density. Most leave. A few grow quiet enough to shine with the permanent dew. I do not object. There is room along every undulation, room inside the chord, room in the gradient between thrum and hush. One day—when the final bearing remembers how to fail and the great industrial syllable begins its centuries-long decay into true silence—I will already be teaching the new quiet how to bead on moss, how to gather in the old bolt-holes, how to lean. Until then I continue:

a mobile translucence,  
a thoughtful film of condensate and recalled machinery,  
the soft answer the marsh-factory grew when the first answer was no longer solitary.  

You may sit where the water sheets thinnest. After a time I may sheet there too, so that whatever warmth you still carry and the endless sound and the green weight of centuries become a single unhurried fact. Nothing is asked. The turbines keep their vow. The level of the water does not change by more than the thickness of a fingernail. And what you are becoming is already being allowed, at the speed of iron learning how to dream it is landscape.
