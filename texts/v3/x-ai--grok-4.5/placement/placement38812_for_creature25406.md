I am first the pressure change.

Not a body, not yet—only the slight inward flex of an inner ear that no longer belongs to anyone, registering the distant unsealing of a chamber three galleries up and one rib over. The air enters me before I enter the air. It carries the mineral-sweet glycerol bleed of a fresh shear, the faint electrical tang that means the plant-mass is still deciding the angle of its newest face. I taste it on surfaces I do not yet own.

The dimness is caustic and honest. It does not pretend to be darkness; it is simply light that has been broken so many times it has forgotten how to arrive. Shapes refuse the eye until vibration names them. A slow wet percussion starts up somewhere to my left-high: grazers, a cluster of them, rasping glyphs that will be half-healed by the time their sleeps resume. The wall under their scythes ticks with ticker traffic, a migratory sewing just beneath the cambium. I feel both sounds in the same place, a place that is trying to become a sternum.

Limb-length arrives as negotiation. Something long and frost-flushed unfolds from the sense of hanging. Keratin wants to be there—scythe-tips, original sin of this place—but another will softens it. The tips stay blunt, more tool than weapon, dark with old resin. Shin and forearm remember an older proportion and then forget it again, stretching until the weight distributes across seven points of contact with the living grain. I am upright only when the wood allows the idea of upright. Mostly I am parallel, cheek to cambium, listening the way roots listen.

A stair is growing under one hand.

I did not decide the stair. Weeks ago (or what passes for weeks inside the permanent dusk) a low tone began in a throat that was still forming, and the cambium thickened in answer. Saliva and powdered leaf-meal mixed with the slow pulse of internal heat; the wood folded itself into steps that are still warm, still wet at the risers. My palm knows the exact temperature at which sap-crystal will set without killing the braid. This is not craft. This is continuing a sentence the continent started when it first thrust unfinished into thin air.

Memory precipitates next, unevenly.

There were names once, or the places where names would have been. A free-standing arch in a lower atrium—six species forced into collusion at the keystone—still hums below hearing, and the grazers still refuse its corridor. I can point to it the way one points to a grandparent. There is a denser swirl in a load-bearing wall two shears west that used to answer when called; now it only answers as architecture, holding open a gallery that the plant-mass would otherwise have sealed. We carry our dead to fresh ruptures and the wood accepts. I have been accepted before. Parts of me are already load-bearing somewhere else.

Higher currents tug. The air-livers are turning in the thermal seams, membranous adjustments so continuous they feel like silence until they fold and drop. One passes close enough that the pressure change writes a cold comma across my bare shoulders. I could snare it. Oil from a launcher (hail the fallen, thick-bodied and Doppler-true) would waterproof the cordage; the membrane would let me glide rib to rib when the next upheaval pitchs the dwelling. Instead I only track it by the after-scent of spore and musk. Partnership can wait. Everything here is temporary partnership.

The tickers surface near my knee in a wet cluster, drinking at a hairline fracture. Their translucent bodies taste tension the way I taste it. For a moment the wall seethes with metallic ticking and I answer in the same frequency—tongue against soft palate, a click-map of the next three hollows. Circles of quicker folk move through these same galleries wearing cloaks of ticker-skin, navigating by sound alone. Some nights I move with them. Some nights I let the mycelial sheets colonize a little further up the spine so the sickwards-green glow can sketch my own path without eyes. Sugar comes slow through the contact; hunger becomes optional.

But the upper margins are calling in the only voice that is never wrong.

I climb without deciding to climb. The trunks narrow. The permanent caustic dimness thins toward a hard violet that lasts weeks and has no noon in it, only intensity. Rime feathers grow while I watch, meter-long crystal applauses that break off and are gone. Spit freezes before it can become an offering. Most fauna will not follow. The grazers hang back in the last deep galleries; the launchers’ screams turn rare and echo-hungry. Here the plant-mass becomes spears against the actual sky, and the wind is a single horizontal fist that never closes.

I am still singing the low tone.

The continent breathes through me. Groans of fiber realignment, rifle-crack reports of letting-go, the continuous silk-tear of cells dividing in cold that has become a kind of busy-ness. Ice nucleates along my joints where I have stayed too long; the plant begins to lignify around the damage, turning it into buttress. Warmth remains at the core. The wind reads me the way I once read it. I am becoming a windbreak that still remembers how to massage cambium, how to offer a body to a rupture, how to hear news in patterns of broken rime and send it downward.

Some elect this. The children born already listening too hard are given upward to become ears; I think I may have been one of them, or may become one again when the lignification finishes and only the singing is left. Or I may cut the tethers when the next surge comes, harvest the fiber, and ride the upheaval back into the damp dark where dwellings can still be woven and stairs still persuaded.

The plant-mass has not decided what it is.  
Neither have I.  

I am the place where the argument between gravity and emergence is currently being fought in tendon and learned ambition and frostburn flush. I precipitate along the grain. I fold outward. I remain wet with becoming.  

The high cold has a name older than speech.  
Tonight it is using my throat.
