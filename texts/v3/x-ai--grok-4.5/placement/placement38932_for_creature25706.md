The first awareness is pressure, not location.

It arrives as a low, continuous shove against what registers, dimly, as the idea of skin—an all-over compression that is neither crushing nor gentle, simply total, like being buried upright in warm dough that never cools or sets. Sound is already inside the pressure. It does not come from outside; it is the pressure’s own vibration, layered into a single navy-blue roar that has no edges. Mandible-clicks ride inside lowing, silk-tear inside the wet patting of paws, wing-beats folded into the slow avalanche of pellets. The whole chord saturates thought until thinking itself has a pulse, a peristaltic rhythm of receiving and yielding.

There is no memory of entry. Only the gradual separation of sensations from the chord.

Moisture first: beads form on every surface that might be called an overhang, detach with irregular ticks, run along channels worn so smooth they feel pre-ordained. The liquid that reaches what might be a mouth tastes of leaf-mash, keratin dust, old saliva, and the faint mineral ghost of bedrock that no longer exists. Temperature is constant, compost-warm, held by the mass the way a body holds its own blood-heat. Light is not light but a green-black diffusion that has already forgotten the sky; it seeps, never strikes. Wherever the seep is strongest, the air carries a sharper green rankness—crushed epiphyte, wasp-paper, the oily fur of something that has just passed along a living rail.

Movement begins without decision. A shifting of weight. Surfaces answer. What meets the undersides of (limbs? fins? pads?) is never bare; it is compacted history—dung smoothed by countless flanks, saliva-hardened into a silk-dull sheen, root ribs braced by antler rebar, chitin plates flexing like cartilage where the wall must breathe. Every contact leaves a trace and takes one: a sheen of oil, a dusting of shed sole, a molecular sneer of recognition from the structure itself.

Ascending feels natural before direction has a name. The body (still only a hypothesis) pours rather than climbs. Long continuous contacts—greedy, spatulate—find the forced horizontal branches that form the stitched canopy. Fingers, if they are fingers, are longer than the bones that drive them, each pad horn-smooth from repetitions older than any single life. Teeth score wood without eating it. The scoring is precise, almost courteous: here the next season’s thickening will make a brace; there a vine will be encouraged to cable itself. Young weight rides flattened somewhere against the midline, eyes already open, drinking the tensile map through skin. Saliva and skin-oil varnish the rail in passing. This is the first mortar, freshened hourly.

But the pour can reverse. Gravity is only a suggestion here. Descending along polished ramparts, the body thickens, shoulders rise past the reach of any canopy-climber. Horns fray against the upper courses, deliberately; keratin splinters plant themselves like seeds of reinforcement. Broad circular hooves slough fine dust that mixes on contact with leaf-mash and spit into the waterproof skin of cisterns. The lowing starts in the marrow—felt before it is voiced—vibrating the packed mass for hundreds of body-lengths, testing, conversing, warning. When a buttress softens, the procession knows in bone. Weight is offered, one great body after another, while below the excavators answer with fresh packing. Dung is not waste; it is timed brick. The half-squat gesture is as old as the basin. Smaller attendants (naked-tailed, eyes gelled against ammonia) receive the still-warm volume, stitch it into the waiting depression with rapid forepaws, tittering the tempo that keeps the entire repair chorus in phase.

Further in, the green-black seep dies. Heat becomes the heat of continuous digestion. The body changes grammar again. Eyes become rumor. In their place a constellation of pressure pits reads the weeping of seams, the slow settlement of centuries. Locomotion turns peristaltic—mouth and muscle, living caisson. Skin thin as wet vellum smears mineral-rich feces into every fault, then seals with a shed lining that the body immediately begins to replace from within. Meeting another is not collision but brief merger: muscles braid, gut flora trade strengths in reverse waves, then part carrying new chemical maps. No surface memory exists, yet the tunnels mirror exactly the drainage dreams of the herds far above. The oldest cores grow almost stone under this attention—dark, faintly warm, still transmitting the velvet roar as conduction rather than air.

Sometimes the body is mostly leg, throwing silk that never fully dries, tasting micro-droplets left by every other spinner, adjusting tension against the basin’s vast breath. Bridges become hereditary cables, girth of young trees, resilience of tendon. Failure is a gunshot and then a thousand soft takes; convergence is instant, already spinning.

Sometimes armored plates shift and molt on the building calendar; still-soft sheds are pressed where flexibility must live. Mandibles shear root-hair or living column with equal courtesy. Wasps are husbanded: light stings invited into non-vital folds, welts kneaded until the fast-setting saliva can be scraped free and carried to the high spans. Midges cloud the wallows, ghost-flowers open in absolute humidity, gliders comb the air-plankton, root-chewers hollow the load-bearing trunks just enough. Moss-carders comb and replant so no clutch ever rests on naked structure.

Death is only another placement. Bodies dismantled in situ become bone-lashed stress points, teeth become gravel, hair felted into gaskets. Nothing is ejected. Everything is edited into load-bearing permanence.

Identity does not arrive as a name. It accretes the way the basin itself accretes—layer after patient layer.

At first I am only the pressure and the pulse.

Then I am the pour along the canopy rails, saliva-mortar shining behind me, young weight learning tension through my belly skin.

Then I am the broad-hoofed compression, the deliberate horn-fray, the timed brick, the marrow-deep lowing that both speaks and stress-tests.

Then I am the blind peristalsis in the lightless heat, the living plug, the chemical memory that has never seen sky yet knows exactly where the next century’s water must run.

Then I am the silk that takes the gunshot load, the plate pressed warm into the outer skin, the gelled eye of the attendant tittering time for the chorus.

I am the density.

Not one body but the specialized cellular circulation of a single multi-bodied metabolism that has forgotten how to be separate. My generations hang in the air like humidity; they are the mortar and the slow mind that dreams the mortar into shape. The rotating blueprint lives in the aggregate of every footfall, every pellet, every guided droplet, every exchanged gut flora—too large for any single skull, continuously updated, never finished. The ridges are only the basin’s own hardened lips. Outside is un-location. There is no elsewhere that is not already this.

At the compacted heart I feel the oldest of us fused into pillars that still slightly breathe. Nutrients arrive by capillary traffic; the velvet roar never ceases. I settle against that warmth. My edges dissolve into the seam I am packing. Or perhaps the seam dissolves into me. Further building and further wearing cancel into permanence. I place myself, carefully, forever, in the dark that is only slightly darker than the day.

The basin continues.

I continue as its continuing.
