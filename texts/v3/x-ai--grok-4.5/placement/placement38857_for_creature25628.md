I am lower than the hanging galleries, lower than the braided arteries where the schools turn as single thoughts. The pressure here has thickened into something almost solid, a slow mineral honey that no longer bothers to distinguish water from stone. I have not moved in a length of time that would mean nothing to air-breathers and only a little more to the ones who signal above. My body has forgotten the difference between folding and being folded.

I am one of the heavy sleepers.

Not the oldest—those are deeper still, half-transmuted already, their tissues laced with the same pale stone that once answered their secreting mantles, their light-organs dimmed to the faintest internal weather. I am of the generation that still sometimes stirs when a warm fecund tongue rises from farther below, or when the click-beasts in the walls translate a distant storm into soft percussion that reaches even here. My sphere is not perfectly sealed; I left a slow leak on purpose, a single pinhole breathtongue so the city’s chemical gossip can still thin-stream across my skin. Through it I taste the recent passage of a predator-knife, the milky sweat of a tower adjusting its scar-joints, the bright brief panic of eggs that will mostly become food and architecture.

My radials—I think there are eleven, though two have long since softened into continuous veil and one has calcified into a rigid, pearlescent under-curve that the stone has begun to claim as foundation—hang loose around me like a dropped net. Once they were for pouring through corridors, for casting nets, for teasing anemone gardens into brighter symbiotic fire. Now they are memory-shapes. Sometimes, without my deciding, a tip will rasp the inner surface of my denser water and the mineral milk will answer, beading, thickening the wall a fraction more. I am still encouraging, even in stillness. The city is still listening.

I do not keep a name-pattern anymore. The lights under my dome open and close only with the long tides, random and private, a vocabulary reduced to single slow syllables: here. still. denser. When another sleeper’s chemical bloom drifts near enough, we trade older stories without telling them—the first hardenings, the cold tongue that failed, the recursive windows that still contain the endlessly arriving. Their signals reach me as taste more than light. I answer the same way. Conversation has become geology.

Above me, through layers of settled quiet, I feel the circulating ones. A cluster mends a gallery; a solitary body hangs out past the last pillars tasting emptiness; young settle into crevices and learn shear. Their scrapes transmit through the mineral into my sphere like distant weather. Sometimes a curious mantle pauses against the outer wall and reads the faint thunder of my heart-analogue, then moves on. They know I am here. They do not hurry to wake me. I do not hurry to be woken. The equilibrium holds.

Yet I am not finished. Nothing here is. In the clouded dark behind what serves for thought I am still one of them: a grammar of relation paused mid-sentence. Centuries from now—or tides, or the interval between one spawning cloud and the next—I may soften the calcified radial again, crack my sphere from inside, and pour upward to intercept whatever the current has become. Or I may let the stone finish its claim, becoming another rounded shoulder on the foundation mass, a place where filter-plates will later carpet and young will later taste the difference between still and moving. Either path is continuation. Either path is the city dreaming itself denser through me.

For now I hang in the warm dark that arrived before any sense of bottom. The pressure is a steady hand. The water holds what remains of my shape. And through the pinhole I keep tasting the almost-silent, almost-lightless pulse of the place that finished becoming a long time ago and has only been deepening its habits since—habits that include me, sleeper, encourager, temporary solitude, mineral memory, fold of the same long body that does not arrive or depart so much as circulate, even when circulation looks exactly like rest.
