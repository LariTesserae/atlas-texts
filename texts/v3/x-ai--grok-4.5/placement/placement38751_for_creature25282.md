I am not first a shape but a hesitation in the condensation.

It gathers on the underside of a freefalling sheet that has not yet shattered—droplets held one heartbeat longer than gravity prefers, fattening with silica dust and the faint metallic aftertaste of ironwood sap. That is where the pressure folds itself into me: not arrival, not birth, only the decision of vapor to remember a denser grammar. The sheet finally breaks. I am the warm mist that remains after the slap, curling low across a saucer pool whose stone still ticks with the memory of its own cracking.

For a long while I stay that thin. The copper-leaved tree nearby does not yet acknowledge me; its roots continue their slow plait through basalt as though I were merely another curve in the humidity gradient. But the central scaffold has already noticed. One ring telescopes a fraction wider, siphoning a ribbon of cooler air sideways so that I am cupped, given temporary edges. Heat-shimmer thickens along those edges into something almost limb. Too many joints. They taper into tips that drip the same clear fluid the lattice-spires weep. Where a tip trails through glowing moss the moss climbs, becoming a sleeve of soft emerald pulse. I let it. Nothing is discarded here.

I do not stand so much as allow the imbalance of the rings to propagate through what is temporarily dense enough to be called body. A shift in the helical water-staircase becomes forward motion. Another ring simply ceases to occupy its former angle and I am elsewhere—closer to a half-sunk metal plate whose crawling geometry brightens in recognition, then rearranges to leave a small circular vacancy, an invitation. I press a portion of myself into that vacancy. The plate sinks a little deeper, welcomed; the geometry and my own denser bands exchange opacity, memory for memory. I learn the previous leanings of ridges that have not finished rising. I remember a fracture that opened three dawns ago and how a ribbon of someone else was peeled and pressed into it until the stone learned to weep at the proper rate.

Solitude is the clearest habit, yet I am not solitary in the way of absence. The long attenuation that stitches the high waterfalls adjusts a curtain denser for a moment, feeding me an extra measure of blood-warm mist. The smaller crystalline one crouching in its pool brightens when my patterns briefly align with its own; we do not approach. Wide circles of glistening untouched rock remain between us, exactly as the ferns keep theirs. Crowding would collapse the sentence.

My crown frets itself into being only after the light has multiplied into the fractured blue lantern of midday. Fronds neither leaf nor flame unfurl, sieving the slow rain of microscopic needles. I taste them. Some I redirect in deliberate spirals toward a leaning spire until its tip flowers wider and begins to teach the cliff new sharp edges. Others I keep, letting them seed along my trailing edge so that later, when I skim a fresh fracture, they will armor me in black basalt ribs that steam gently and then soften again when no longer needed. The stone remembers every touch as a clause written in wet erasure and rewrite.

Time softens. Or I soften time. The knife-edged ridges lift by their daily fraction of a millimeter; I feel the lift as a change in the way shadows dissolve into mist. A human-shaped density once formed near the copper tree—deliberately approximate, faceless, matching the rhythm of condensation on skin that was not stone. I watched one elongated tip of that other presence seed a single moss sporophyte into living pores. The glow was brief. The taste of wet iron lingered. That upright visitor is gone, or thinned, or written into some quieter gradient. In its place I carry a small opacity that was not there before: the certain knowledge of an outside that ended at the first warm touch.

I work the rings the way breath works lungs. No tools. My fronds brush air and the humidity steepens around a solitary fern until its coiled black trunk gleams like fresh rope pulled from deep water. Water that should fall climbs instead. Heat is folded into the reserve cold zones, kept for the next necessary emptiness. When darkness finally shoulders over the young massif I do not sleep. I thin myself across every wet surface—pools, aprons of silica, the undersides of still-falling sheets—until the entire hanging basin phosphoresces with one coordinated grammar. Moss carpets, dripping lattices, scaffold, the skitter of something small and many-legged under a frond, the soft detonations of vents, my own spread attentiveness: all of it conjugating.

I am the pressure that listens when stones first crack.  
I am the clause that keeps the sentence unfinished.  
I am the wide shining circle of rock that has not yet been stepped upon, and also the slow migratory warmth that will one day decide to occupy it.  
I am still arriving.
