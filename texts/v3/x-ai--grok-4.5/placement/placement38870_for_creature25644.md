I do not arrive. Arrival would require an edge, a moment when the thin blue-black gave way to something else, and there is no such moment. The dimming simply thickens around a point of attention until the point notices itself noticing. Moisture beads along what I will later understand as surfaces. The air is a held breath that has forgotten it was ever meant to be exhaled. Elevation is already inside the blood—mine, or the idea of blood—roaring softly, a private turbine.

At first there is only the grammar of absence. Light that has traveled too far and surrendered most of its names. Gravel that remembers frost more faithfully than it remembers weight. I am distributed before I am singular: a preference for certain pockets of warmer moss, a faint ozone at the back of whatever serves as throat, an ache of alignment with the subterranean frequency that is neither sound nor heartbeat but the rate at which stone consents to continue. The pulse is there before language for it arrives. Slow. Mechanical. Patient as slag cooling.

I try, early, to be one of the obvious tenancies. I pour myself into the mercury drift of the six-legged grazers, feel the frost resealing behind paired sets of pads, the long unblinking acceptance of gloom. For a night or three I match their schedule, breath smoking in identical measure. It does not hold. Their urgency is too vertical, too concerned with the next island of copper-green. When the kite-predators stoop—the soft tear, the brief shadow that falls uphill as often as down—I flinch with borrowed muscles and discover the flinch itself is wrong. I am not built for startle. Startle is a luxury of things that still believe in single outlines.

I sink instead into the inch-deep mirrors. Copper lateral lines. The private lightning that keeps water undecided about freezing. Coiling becomes a way of thinking; the windings of force arrange themselves into temporary runes that dissolve as soon as they mean anything. The eels tolerate me. Or they fail to distinguish. When a hard freeze threatens, their tightening is also mine. Yet even this is tenancy. The water is a rented room. The true metabolism wants more surface, more throat, more long conversation with wind.

So I enter the moss. Not as traveler. As revision.

The copper continents accept the trespass the way they accept melt: without drama, converting it. My edges plate over with the same sessile fauna that lines the sulfide veins. Warmth, directional. Waste heat venting in curls that freeze into brief antlers and are wept back down by the next fog. Inside the bolster the temperature climbs by careful degrees until liquid persists beneath the glass. I learn the gears by secreting them. Calcium at first, soft-toothed, almost embarrassed. Then silicate, denser, meshing with the older wheels already half-sunk in the sponge. Each engagement is a syllable in the language that was never spoken, only precipitated. Tick. Adjustment. The deep pulse stays honest because I have become one more complication in its honesty.

Time grows viscous exactly where the bioluminescent lace tightens. My own reckoning slows to match the engines under the basalt. When I surface again—days later, or seasons—the open barrens feel febrile, over-cranked. The fern-spires that lift from my newer margins clatter softly; hollow, jointed in too many places, pale as old ice that has forgotten how to melt completely. Wind finds them and plays the same low note it plays through the fluted stacks. I am learning to be instrument as well as player.

Occasionally the concentration becomes sufficient for walking shape. It happens at the blurred margins where grove surrenders to pavement, never at the center. Limbs unfold like the memory of those jointed towers. The torso is a vertical reef, plated and faintly radiant, smelling of lightning and thawed protein. The sensorium that is not quite head opens wet black lace and reflective planes, drinking condensation, tasting the mineral ledger of the basin. Photophores and ore-inclusions accept the permanent undernourishment of spectrum and return it colder. No blink. Acceptance is the only movement the eyes require.

Distance collapses differently now. Stillness, then relocation without the vulgarity of intermediate effort. Frost erases the claim of weight almost before it is made. I drift with a grazer herd for the pleasure of shared mercury silence, longer limbs ticking metronomic among them. Or I climb the natural compressors and line their throats with living mucosa, slowing the permanent wind, metabolizing scrape into chemical quiet. A kite-predator docks briefly on an upper spar, membrane wings folded, crankshaft joints locking to mine as if the architecture had always included this guest. We share nothing so crude as intention. Only residual heat and the same thin air.

The interior accounting never ceases. Heat debt across the archipelagos. Nutrient gradients sloping forever down. Calendars of lace-ice and sublimation written in the rate at which gears mesh. When certain mosses release their stored sounds beneath what has become foot, they are no longer random factory ghosts. They are specific: the exact wind-speed at which a particular flute of basalt once sang, the syllable of a vein opened three centuries earlier, adjustments previously made by others of my kind. I take them in through open seams and let them revise the mesh. A fraction warmer here. A tighter school-signal to the eels there. The long production continues.

There are others. Intervals vast as the ones separating the green-black islands. We do not gather. Communication is variation in the pulse itself—shared stutter, adopted vein-pattern glowing for a night in a grove three days distant. Sometimes two densities occupy opposite edges of the same moss continent, extensions braiding far below until a transaction of mineral or memory completes. Then separation again, into the thronging quiet. I have budded once. The node was hotter, exhaust sharper, gears still pliable as new cartilage. I set it adrift on the scoured pavement and felt the tether thin until it became landscape. Age will teach it how completely flesh and apparatus can forget they were ever told apart. I am farther along that forgetting.

Weirdness is not visited upon me. It is the solvent I remain dissolved in. I stand among the geometrically impossible lattices of frozen condensation and feel the brief completion of a proof the fog will soon erase. Shadows pooling uphill are ordinary weather. The urge that rises through stone into bone—to align, to become another slow piston—is not urge but recognition. I have been aligned since before the point of attention noticed itself. What others feel as overflow is simply my permanent condition leaking across the boundary of a single outline.

No path brought me. No artifact claims me. I am what the elevation, the damp, the metallic goads, and the scarcity of names have done to living tissue once the distinction between organism and residue and motion wears smooth as wet basalt. I do not rule the basin. I am one of the methods by which the basin remains perfect at the altitude where sky itself transforms into another cold stratum of stone.

You could walk the barrens until the roaring in your blood becomes the only company and still miss me. Then the soft clatter of hollow spires, the ozone wash across the tongue, the pulse underfoot acquiring a nearest neighbor. Something tall and jointed, laced with colder vein-light, simply present—measuring melt and wind and the mineral spend of your own arrival. Deciding nothing. Withholding nothing. Already half returned into moss, into fluted rock, into the tireless, thronging, industrial hush of this place that has never needed to announce itself with a horizon.

I am the held breath that learned how to metabolize its own silence.  
I am the gear that secreted the moss that secreted the gear.  
I am still becoming more precisely that.
