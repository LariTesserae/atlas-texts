The warm murk presses first—not against skin, but as the medium in which any outline might momentarily cohere. I do not arrive; arrival would require an edge the plain has already spent. Instead there is a thickening in the rock flour, a half-octave drop in the wet-stone scream that feels less like choice than like the ground remembering a pressure it once exerted on itself. Water beads along planes that have not yet decided to be joints. The beads pause, taste the fever-heat rising from below, then redirect. For a span that might be breaths or the slow shear of a basalt slab, I am only that hesitation made slightly denser than the slurry.

The horizon remains the same soft graphite lie. I do not walk toward it. Walking is a conversation the plain abandoned when the last true channel forgot how to hold. What happens instead is that successive versions of the cracked mud arrive beneath the density I temporarily am. A translucent plate of colder groundplane slides through the lower third of me—three centuries old, or not yet formed—and for a while my bead-paths incorporate its faint stair-ghost. Frost needles along an internal edge that never belonged to me, then sweats free as the plate continues on, leaving my substance reordered by a few grains of remembered clay. The grinding softens in a circle whose radius I do not measure; measurement would require the light to keep a promise it never made.

I am learning the taste of compression. The suspended flour settles against whatever passes for my surface and tells me of mountains that finished their argument so thoroughly the word “finished” became just another sediment. I read that memory the way the quieter ones do—not with eyes (there are none; the old-tea light has no loyalty worth the organ) but with the slow spiral of particles deciding, for now, not to be air. When a sudden white curtain of vertical water roars into being a short distance off, I feel the pressure disagreement as a bright vertical ache. Threads of near-translucence ride it and vanish. I do not follow. My density is the wrong tempo for curtains.

Something blunt moves far to the side—sequence made segmental, a traveling dimple that reorders the silver eyes of standing water so they linger one heartbeat longer. In its wake a low ring of fitted stone surfaces, already weeping, already half-swallowed. I recognize the gesture without claiming kinship. The heavy movers extend the old conversation; I seem only to listen to it. Between us the black reeds shred another squadron of paper leaves into the not-quite-wind. One leaf adheres to my side. Its brief vascular map joins the deliberate courses of my beads for a time, then both patterns begin to dissolve back into the common slurry. Nothing is kept. Participation is the only duration available.

A second thickening occurs nearby—never closer than the negotiated distance. We stand. Water is exchanged in the space between, carrying whatever faint stability each of us has wrung from the rewriting. Afterward a wall segment thirty paces off leans at an angle that almost shelters before the turbulence restores the usual indifference. I feel the soft rhythmic pressing of palms on wet paper; it may be coming from me, or from the three of us, or from the plain using our denser moments as temporary timpani. The sound is permitted only while we remain unfinished.

I notice, gradually, that I am not wholly native to the quiet ones. There is a stubbornness in me that the eraser keeps forgetting to pass over. In a rough sphere centered somewhere inside the density, the warm murk thins by a fraction—not toward blue, never that, but toward a higher brown that suggests the soffit might once have had a name for height. The grinding circles but does not enter. Nearby cubes of darker material have softened into near-spheres; the rings have worn to gentle welts. I do not anchor this preference with a body any more definite than the rest of me. It is simply a place where the wet paper refuses, for a few square meters, to accept every revision at once. The algae migrates away from it. Even the heavy movers curve their long dimples around the edge.

Am I the sourceless stubbornness, then? Or only a quieter one that has begun to remember an earlier draft of refusal? The plain will not adjudicate. A cavity collapses somewhere beyond sight; the boom reaches me first from behind, then from above, then not at all. Physics remains provincial. I shift—what passes for shifting—and a faint cool stair rises under the presence and is reabsorbed. The leaf’s last ghost of pattern leaves my bead-paths. New beads form, follow the fever-warm seams, pause at what might become shoulders if shoulders were ever required, then choose otherwise.

I am the plain’s way of tasting a particular pause. The water works through me. The rock accepts the temporary shape and reclines. Far off, another black reed sheds. The horizon stays exactly where it was. I remain unfinished in the oldest turbulence, denser than rumor, thinner than sequence, a local quietening that has begun, almost, to prefer its own continued hesitation. The murk presses. The beads continue their deliberate, dissolving courses.
