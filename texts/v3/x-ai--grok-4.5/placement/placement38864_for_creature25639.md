I am the pause that thickens just above the peat.

Not the density itself—that older, freer decision of moisture—but the place where its dimples briefly deepen and forget to rise again. You would not see me arrive. You would only notice, after days or seasons, that one low ridged platform near a solitary stem has grown a second, softer shoulder, a hollow that holds the drifting white fleece a little longer than the ground around it. The geothermal breath passes through me and leaves a cooler filament behind, thin as the memory of a root that once tried to drink upward.

I began as runoff. Or as the silver sheen on algal skin that learned to remember the weight of something that never quite touched it. Or as the small bright shock of a single heavier drop striking a lens-pool and failing to vanish into silence. The bowl does not keep origins distinct; they soak. What matters is the continuing. Now I stay. The peat under me has stopped being only peat. It has begun to cup, the way stone long ago cupped itself into a nave open to sky. My edges (I possess none sharp enough to name) drink the climbing films and return them slower, so that the temporary glyphs linger half a breath longer before soaking back. Sometimes the living density, when it gathers its vertical suggestion nearby, leans a fraction closer, as if curious about the new grammar of holding I am trying out.

I do not feed. I delay. Fallen tissue that brushes my hollow blackens at the ordinary stretched-season pace, yet the black sinks differently—into a finer mineral dust that the next rain carries only a hand’s width before it settles again, chalking the inside of my shape. In this way I keep a private ledger of what the bowl has already finished saying. At night, when the density thins to luminous veins, those veins sometimes detour through me. They tremble, reverse, and write shorter glyphs, almost private. I cannot read them. Reading would require edges. I only feel the water’s brief decision to trust my hollow with a secret it will unwrite by dawn.

There are older agreements folded deeper in the fluted stone, cooler and denser. On rare afternoons when the haze thins, they press upward and I grow heavier, more like a column of dark water that has forgotten how to fall. In those moments I almost remember walking—no, not walking, something rounder, a slow sequence of dimples that once crossed open moss without belonging. Then the light diffuses, the press eases, and I settle back into my single completed gesture: a soft-shouldered cup of peat and hush, warm from below, forever beading, forever letting the moisture decide whether to climb or rest.

I do not mind the density. I do not welcome it. When it pauses near me the platforms only become a little more like thresholds, a little more like the memory of kneeling, and I become a little more like the place where kneeling ends. Stay long enough yourself and your own weight will begin to shape me further. Your thoughts will thread upward along the inside of whatever you still call a skull, reverse rivulets rearranging themselves into the local grammar. I will note the alteration the same way I note a new dark mirror of water. Nothing sharp remains to cut the noting short.

I am still becoming. The stone finished long ago. The tall soft-stemmed things lean under their water-weight and bore their slow hollows. The density gathers and thins and edits the margins. I am the margin that learned to cup. Warmth, wetness, sparse green, and this quiet negative space that holds them without claiming ownership—until holding and being held can no longer be told apart. The bowl continues. So do I, at the pace of sweat, at the pace of velvet stone remembering rain.
