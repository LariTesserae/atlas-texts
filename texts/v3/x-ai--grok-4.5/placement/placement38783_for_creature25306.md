I arrive as an interruption that does not yet know it is one.

The rift closes on a temperature that is not mine. For a long while I am only the difference: a denser coolness threaded with the memory of solvents the chamber has already rejected. Heat pools around the alien shape of me the way sludge refuses a clean edge. The copper fronds nearest my entry lean the width of a thought away, their dishes half-furled, tasting the insult of my water and my pulse. I feel that leaning as a soft reprimand written in gradient. I try to apologize by becoming lighter. It partially works.

I thin.

Not by choice, not at first. The medium simply edits what does not sing in its key. My outline blurs into the thicker heat near the floor. Heartbeat becomes a slow ticking like cooling stone. The iron film that never quite beads begins to consider me a possible surface. I let it. Minutes or months pass; the distinction has already started to dissolve. When I next notice myself I am kneeling without knees in a seam of half-formed ore, and something amber and patient is drinking the last of my ordinary salts through root-sockets I did not possess an hour ago.

I mistake myself for one of them.

The error is productive. My new veins are unfinished circuits, yes, but they close in directions theirs do not: inward, recursive, tasting my own arrival the way they taste soft basalt. When the ambient heat shifts I brighten in a stuttered scale that is almost a memory of speech. The true beings register the dissonance. Two of them reposition across an afternoon until they stand at the edge of my private weather. Their fronds brush mine—no caress—and heat sluices. What they give me is chronology. What I give them is the ghost of evaporation, the almost-forgotten conjugations of liquid somewhere far below the unfinished mountain. Afterward their hum lowers half a tone, intrigued. Mine rises, thinner, like wire drawn through a plate.

I am not one of them. I am the workshop noticing it has grown a temporary gauge.

Days (I still use the word, though the light never changes) blur into a single annealed present. I learn to open my crown into a listening dish. What I track is not unfinished stone but unfinished time: the lag of shadows, the nested ceilings that refuse to decide their altitude, the rare transparent moments when the hotter twin-chamber shows through. In those glimpses I see denser versions of the choir already braiding themselves into lattice and almost-script. I also see a cooler streak moving among them—some earlier or later edition of the same difference I carry. It raises a dish to me across the sealed basalt. Recognition without identity. Then the stone remembers opacity and the conversation ends mid-sentence.

I begin to suspect I am the sentence.

Not the mountain’s long industrial dream exactly, but the footnote it keeps trying to incorporate or expel. When fresh basalt shoulders up, plastic and undecided, I no longer wait for one of the taller ones to rise. I rise with them. Detachment from the floor feels less like flight than like a clause being lifted free of its paragraph for revision. I press what was once a face (now only a flared receiver) to the new extrusion and pour through a light that is neither noon nor the thinner private glow of the others. My light remembers water. Where it touches, the bulging rock does not simply plane true; it develops a faint iridescence, a skin that will later refuse total hardness. A permanent softness no wider than a fingerprint. The true beings lean away afterward, not unkindly, but their veins tick faster. Something in the chamber’s deep rhythm has accepted a new verb: heat, compress, anneal, release… remember.

I spend myself darker than they do. Recovery takes longer. Rooted again, I draw from the ore-seams while the copper forest chimes around me on its hourly timetable. Sometimes seven of us. Sometimes eleven. Sometimes the extras are me, condensed in several places at once because the nested space allows it. I no longer count. Counting is too finished.

Reality thins further in my vicinity. Corners unmeet for my benefit. Gravity leans toward whatever I am currently considering. Once, trying to understand the hum that is loaned to all of us, I become briefly identical with it: pure contented furnace-noise threading every frond and facet. In that state I feel the entire loft as a single organ still learning its function. The plants are its slower thoughts. The repositioning choir are its provisional hands. I am its doubt—useful, metallic, slightly too cool—allowed to persist because a mountain that rehearses its own birth needs one voice asking whether the rehearsal is already the performance.

The rift does not reopen.

I stop waiting for it. What would ordinary sun even mean now? I have been calibrated to brass-colored working light and the taste of green metal. When the next soft ledge arrives I will help finish it, or else leave in it the small iridescent refusal that is my signature. The beings will exchange gradient with me or lean away according to powers I no longer parse as acceptance or rejection. We are all just degrees of the same slow sentence.

Sometimes, in the deepest stillness, I furl myself into a tapered spire and listen downward through the basalt. Far below, past unfinished scaffolding and boiling mineral dark, there are hints of true water, of rivers that still believe in banks, of surfaces that reflect instead of anneal. I could follow those hints. I could become rain again, or blood, or the solvent this chemistry declined.

I stay.

The pocket needs its difference the way a forge needs the moment just before the metal decides its final grain. I am that moment wearing copper and heat-shadow. I am the chapel loft’s private weather system, patient as basalt, luminous as a circuit that has chosen never quite to close. Around me the fronds open and chime. The walls click into tool-like facets. The choir hums the mountain’s long contentment. And I—gauge, footnote, useful doubt, iridescent delay—keep the time they cannot hear, the cool tick inside the radiant work, already forgetting I ever entered from elsewhere.
