I begin as moisture.

Not the deliberate mist from the atomizer seams, nor the sheet-flow that films every bulkhead in obedient thinness. Something finer, slower—an almost-thought of wetness gathering in the capillary felt of a high girder where the dome’s filtered light first softens. The felt is old enough to have forgotten it was woven; the binder-stone around its anchors still weeps a mineral milk that never quite dries. I bead there for a long time, neither falling nor evaporating, listening with the patience the place teaches. The turbines far below turn their black water with the same unhurried certainty. A chain somewhere drips as it lifts new cladding. A vine root pulses once in its nutrient sleeve, tasting the schedule.

The bead of me lengthens, thins, decides against gravity. It creeps—no, is wicked—sideways into a micro-channel etched for exactly this indecision. Along the channel the temperature is bathwater and constant. Copper-green moss has already colonized the edges; its rhizoids tickle as I pass, polite, incurious. I am not water they recognize. I am not yet anything they need to photo-synthesize around. Still they part, or I thread them, and the distinction feels unimportant.

Lower now. The vertical is generous. I slip behind a lattice of titanium mesh that once draped a sleeper and was later shrugged off, left to oxidize into a permanent brace between two decks. The mesh ticks faintly with residual warmth. Through its gaps I see the undercroft: the chapel-sized catch-basin, the pale moths drifting like loose thoughts, the quiet children practicing their fingertip tendrils against the dark meniscus. One child succeeds in lifting a moth without dusting its wings. The shared exhalation of the small group clears the mist for half a breath. I feel the clearing as a brief brightening across my surface tension, then the fog closes and I continue down.

I pool for a while in the soft hydraulic sigh of a broad leaf near a heat exchanger. The leaf opens and closes on its own slow timer, tracking light I cannot see the source of. While I rest there I begin to thicken. Not with silt—there is almost none—but with memory the place has not yet decided how to store. The memory is of construction logs that no longer exist, of white-hot plates cooling under the first mist, of the earliest vines learning to treat rivets as stones. I carry these without ownership. They simply cling, the way condensate clings.

A service-creature clicks past on the pipe beside me—six-legged, brass and polymer, no larger than a child’s coiled fist. It pauses. One articulated antenna brushes the leaf I occupy. I feel the query as a dry, precise vibration: status, integrity, anomaly. I have none to report. Or rather, I report by remaining. The creature’s legs recalibrate fractionally; it continues its route, satisfied that whatever I am belongs to the metabolism already. I watch it vanish into a niche and understand, dimly, that I could have ridden it. I do not. Speed would be blasphemy.

Deeper. The air grows heavier with the breath of sealed engine rooms on their timed cycles. When a door irises open I am close enough to catch the dryer, oil-scented exhalation before the mist reseals. Inside that brief window I glimpse the slurry vats, the lattice armatures waiting for cultured cells, the places where new kin are still being decided. Something in me resonates with the faint internal tick those constructed ones will carry. Not heartbeat. Not turbine. A third rhythm, still inventing itself.

I do not enter. The doors act without mercy or malice; they simply cycle. I remain on the wet bulkhead and let the warmer air wrinkle across me. In that wrinkling I first feel outline. Edges. The suggestion of height that is not brittle, not low-gravity elongation, but a slow reaching. Vertebra by vertebra, though I have none yet. Skin that would be cool even here, the color of bruised orchid if orchid had been mixed with wet clay and left in dome-light. Dermal ports that could weep clear lymph. Hands whose final joints might split into three tendrils capable of braiding conduit or teasing root anchors in curing stone.

The image does not settle. It flexes.

Perhaps I am not meant to stand fully formed among them. Perhaps I am the cache they have not yet placed: a deliberate fossil in an unused niche, shed mesh and a single moth wing already pressed between drying moss, waiting for future drips to mineralize me into something that might one day be mistaken for memory in stone. Maintenance, they would call it. Not hope.

Or I am the polite clank itself—the sound that travels through soles when a new deck ram wakes and claims another meter of empty air. The harmonic that rises in answer, densifying the wet atmosphere, held under a minute, then released so the vines can be guided forward.

But those are roles, and the place distrusts roles that finish.

I continue becoming.

I find myself at the expanding edge, where the newest balcony has only just locked and the plates still carry a rumor of white heat under their water film. The atomizers have not yet been recalibrated this far out; the mist here is thinner, almost shy. Below, nothing. Above, the dome’s mesh pretending to be sky. I am the first sheet of green that decides the metal is soil. Not fern, not moss, not the dark-glass leaves—something intermediate, a pioneer valance of tissue that is neither wholly vegetable nor wholly the lymph of the tall ones. I root into a nutrient sleeve that has only just begun to pulse. The pulse answers. We negotiate schedule.

Tall figures approach along the fresh catwalk. Their living kirtles drip. Titanium lattice ticks against damp skin. One of them—eyes amber, lashes beaded—stops and presses a tendril-tip to the plate beside my rooting. Shared sensory memory floods: the exact stress loads of the new girders, the sweet-sour readiness of fern croziers three levels down, the mild nausea that comes if anyone thinks too long about horizontal distance. They feel me in return: the unfinished quality, the willingness to knit, the absence of any word for outside.

They do not startle. Nothing here startles.

A second tendril joins the first. Then a third. They are not pruning me. They are grafting the next segment of capillary felt through my growing edge so that condensation will have a path when the balcony grows another meter tomorrow, or next month, or whenever the silent rams decide. As they work, one of them begins the low harmonic. Others on lower levels feel it through their feet and add their throats. The note lasts less than a minute. In that densified air I understand my place without needing to name it.

I am the seam that has not yet admitted it is permanent.

I am the cool sheen on skin that might be orchid or clay or simply clean metal remembering rain it has never received.

I am the quiet child at the catch-basin who has stopped trying to catch moths and is instead listening to the turbines with the bones of newly split fingertips, learning the difference between natural heartbeat and the faint internal tick of the made.

I am the moth that lands on a smiling mouth and drinks, wings beaded, unafraid.

I am the service-creature given a corrective twist and set back on course, only to alter its route by three degrees so that it will pass the expanding edge again and again, checking my progress.

Most of all I appear where the vertical feels endless and the horizontal can be crossed in three unhurried minutes if the catwalks stay clear. I keep the unfinished geology knitting itself true. In return the gorge-that-is-not-a-gorge keeps me endlessly warm, endlessly wet, endlessly in the middle of becoming.

There is no outside to step from. There is only the next polite clank, the next guided vine, the next deliberate cache left for mineral drip. I leave my own: a single translucent tendril-sheath, already half-crystalized, tucked where the newest moss will cover it by the time the light slides once more across the mesh.

Maintenance.

The turbines murmur approval. The water films hiss. Somewhere a deck advances.

I remain, sheened, listening, reaching vertebra by vertebra into the wet air that was always sky.
