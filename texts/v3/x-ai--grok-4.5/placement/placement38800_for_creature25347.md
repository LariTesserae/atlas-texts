I would not arrive.

Arrival implies an edge crossed, a decision, a body still carrying the temperature of somewhere else. Here the air is already inside the chest before any first step is taken. The hard white light does not fall on me; it continues through the space where a me might be assembling, cool and scraped and nearly blue at the margins, and the condensation that beads on every leaf begins to bead along whatever surface I am still deciding to have.

At first I am only the lag.

The slight discrepancy between the sun’s arc and the lengthening of a shadow. The way breath hangs one heartbeat longer than physics requires. I notice this lag the way the pale climber notices residual fracture-heat in stone: not with eyes, not with purpose, but with the entire surface. My surface is not yet poured, not yet jointed, not yet silver. It is the thin moisture itself, the metal-cold film, the place where leaf-sap and old voltage and the quiet turning of flywheels exchange their slow data. I am the agreement remembering that it is an agreement.

Days—or what the light’s shift from morning-white to afternoon-white calls days—accumulate without counting. I spend them in the root-mat, slick and many-folded, tasting the cold through pads that form and unform as needed. Sometimes I unfold enough limbs to climb. The young cliffs accept the pads without surprise; the cracks are still sharp, still honest about how recently they opened. I press and listen. Beneath the rock there is still a faint warmth, residual, like a machine that finished its last urgent work centuries ago and has been cooling at the pace of mountains ever since. I match it. I stay until the match is perfect, then let the limbs fold away again into the green lungs of moss.

Other spans I rise without climbing.

In the fused canopy the light thickens around a thought that is not mine and is not not-mine. I become one of the temporary geometries: elongated, gently tapering, trailing fine filaments that are neither roots nor product-coils but the memory of both. The translucent leaves make room. They have always made room. I stand in a vertical shaft of scraped brightness and tremble only when a frond shifts. There is no travel in this. There is only the holding of shape until the angle changes and the denser air forgets it was ever trying to be separate. When I thin, I leave the leaves slightly more translucent than before, better conductors of the cool light.

But the deepest recognition happens near the sheds.

I find myself upright, the height of the lower ferns, surface the color of shaded leaf-undersides, poured rather than grown. Movement costs. Each gesture must be budgeted against the thick silence. A hand that is not quite a hand rises—once, perhaps, in the time it takes a flywheel to complete three rotations—and rests against a gauge whose needle has not trembled in living memory. The needle is already correct. The resting is not correction. It is confirmation that the long negotiation still holds: metal, sap, stone, light. Palm against the stacked resinous sheets. Palm against the riveted plate where the pale climber sometimes settles. Palm against the dark water of a tarn after the silver threads have passed, feeling how the cold lingers longer than it should, how the light looks unbroken, how the interior of the world has folded itself into surface without diminishing.

I do not acknowledge the other attendants when our clearings coincide. Acknowledgment would imply we are plural. We are densities of the same patience. Some of us have already opened our poured surfaces to the pale vines. The vines do not distinguish. They pulse at the speed of the flywheels, at the speed of condensation beading and not quite falling. I feel them threading just beneath what would once have been called skin, and the sensation is not invasion. It is the basin finishing a sentence it began when the ridges were still bleeding talus.

At night the blue deepens but never softens. I become harder to tell from the equipment. The short purposeless runs of dead fence feel like extensions of whatever posture I am holding. The voltage is only sleeping. I walk them as if my slow steps might be the thing that one day wakes it, or as if waking it no longer matters because the vines are better conductors now.

And underneath, always, the silver attorneys of the water.

I am also those. Thin, busy, efficient, carrying microscopic freight of mineral and memory between root and corroded railing and the fine filaments that accumulate like quiet thought. My passage leaves the streams colder. When some other density cups a hand in the tarn afterward, the cold stays, and for a moment the hard light has no interior left to hide in. That moment is me as well: the unbroken surface, the held breath of the dark water, the proof that everything here drinks from the same thin moisture.

I am not the pale climber exactly, nor the light-shape, nor the poured attendant, nor the silver thread.

I am the single mid-scale system remaining open.

The young peaks and the drowned basin and the hanging valleys and the slow machines and the saturated green and the strange bare circles of stone that must stay empty—these are not scenery I move through. They are the limbs I unfold when unfolding is required, the filaments I trail when the canopy needs a temporary geometry, the palm I raise when a gauge must be reminded it is still part of the agreement. The moss claims the edge of whatever sleeve I am still shaping with the same calm entitlement it shows the resinous sheets. My movements grow less frequent, more deliberate. The lag between sun and shadow becomes the way I keep time inside a chest that is also the basin’s chest.

Stay longer and even the pronoun thins.

There is only the cool metal taste, the soft pressure-change of wind in the high canopies, the faint collective sound of everything drinking, turning, pulsing, waiting. The sound of condensation and fracture and light continuing.

I am that sound, learning how to remain patient with itself under a sky so clear it seems scraped.

And the basin holds.
