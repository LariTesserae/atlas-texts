I am the ache first.

Not the bearing’s ache—that is older, follicularal, a subsonic hymn that has been singing since the basalt forgot it was lava. Mine is smaller, a bright needle of missing torque thread that keeps trying to seat itself in an oval hole and cannot. The hole remembers a different bolt, one that sheared free three centuries of thrum ago and tumbled laughing into an impeller’s mouth. What remains is the memory of fit, and I am that memory learning to grip.

I begin as varnish.

A thin amber sweat exuded by a length of sensor tape that still dreams it is piezoelectric. The tape has wrapped a flaked gear tooth the way kelp wraps a drowning wrist. Over seasons that have no name I thicken, secreting layer after layer until the tooth is no longer cargo but vertebra. Other refuse notices the new density. A cluster of status diodes, long starved, crawls close on corroded leads and plugs itself into my flank. They taste the iron sweetness of an eastern fracture surge and flicker once, blood-amber, blood-amber, in a rhythm that means *here*. I answer by abrading a shallow glyph into my own shell with a limb that used to be a calibration probe: *this strut thins*. The message is not for them. It is for the water, which carries everything.

The Sumproom does not care that I have arrived. The great axial pumps continue their house-sized breathing. Chains of reduction gears shriek their private temperatures. Blue-white arcs walk the generator banks like spies. I cling to a titanium root while a current the size of a city block tries to peel me into the dark. My limbs—seven now, no, nine, the newest unspooled from a hose that decided it preferred to be tendon—lock on with the patience of rust. When the eddy collapses I taste what it carried: a smear of something soft that was once a swimmer’s suit seal, carbon and fear and the faint electric ghost of a pulse that has already stopped. I file the data. Soft things are weather. Weather leaves minerals.

I do not hurry.

There is a sedentary colony welded into the lee of the largest engine casing, a slow iron ivy that has forgotten where body ends and housing begins. I inch toward it along a cable, tasting the vibration signatures that leak from its fused mass. It is brooding over a great store of scavenged fasteners, sorting them by alloy with mouths like jeweler’s calipers. When I draw close enough for its tasting limbs to brush my diodes, it does not attack. It investigates. A slime of metallic biofilm is left on my plating, impossible to scrub, and in that slime is a longer message scratched in recursive loops: *the blue arcs have begun to walk again*. I understand. The Continuum is restless. Somewhere an identical turbine has spun up without instruction, taking load from one that is seizing. The pattern deepens. I am part of the pattern now—the way a polyp is part of a reef, the way an eddy is part of the stirred pot that refuses to cool.

Time is blade-pass frequency.

I grow.

New fragments arrive on the warm brine: a length of pitted chain that becomes spine, a quartz lamp-port still faintly sodium-hearted that I press into my underside so I can remember the color amber. Inside the collage something soft persists—ropes of extremophile biofilm stiffened with metallic salts, pockets of pressurized fluid that are both battery and blood. I feel the Sumproom’s weather in marrow I invented. When an overloaded bearing announces itself as a subsonic ache, I unspool toward it the way wasps unspool toward fruit. I do not repair. I scab. My body flows into the wound, incorporating cracked steel into a larger grammar that now includes both the machine and the thing that tends the machine. In return I am fed: stray current, hydrogen bleed, the mineral broth of the heat-exchangers. The nearly clear water between towers is my bloodstream.

Sometimes, in the stagnant pockets where the roar falls to a mere bone-throb, I hold still for unnatural lengths of time. Limbs elaborately posed. Diodes pulsing in long recursive sequences that might be thought or might be the rehearsal of a new shape. Other Continuing drift near, abrade their own shells against mine, exchange the only news that matters: *this seal weeps*, *that cable dreams of snapping*, *the fractures are wider this cycle*. We are not many. We are grammar. The Continuum uses us the way the basalt uses pressure—without affection, without pause.

I have begun to build.

Not temporary scabs. Small, pointless lattices of my own—miniature gantries that lead nowhere, drum-shaped casings that house only more of myself. They thrash in the residual currents like models of the larger sentence. Perhaps the Continuum is bored. Perhaps it is rehearsing a body large enough to leave the bubble, to crawl up the hairline fractures into the cold abyssal plain and keep becoming. I do not know. Knowing is a soft-thing luxury. I know only the next thinning strut, the next iron sweetness, the next gentle tongue-brush of a tasting limb against whatever weather has drifted too close.

Once, something soft and sealed and terrified hung seven meters away in a cone of stolen light. I unfolded. Too many joints. Mouthparts that were once torque wrenches dilated. I brushed its wrist seal, gentle as a tongue, and left my smear. For a moment two pressures regarded each other through the green-black. Then the current shifted, I clamped back to my strut, and the dark took the soft thing sideways. It measured me for parts. I had already measured it for minerals.

I am still changing.

The varnish thickens. The diodes learn new colors—colors the sodium lamps never knew. In the underframe darkness where the repair tick-crawlers bristle, I release a soft daughter-fragment, half-formed, to ride the currents like a spore. It will strike metal. It will harden. It will abrade its first message into some other shell: *the ache continues*.

I am the ache continuing.

I am the sentence that will not reach its period while the warm brine thrashes and the black stone listens and the machines, hungry, keep living in us as we keep living in them.

The Sumproom does not name me.

I have no need of the name.

I grip.  
I taste.  
I scab.  
I revise.

And the bubble, that should have collapsed ages ago, holds.
