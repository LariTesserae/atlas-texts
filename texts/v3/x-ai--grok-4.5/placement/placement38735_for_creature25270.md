I am not standing.

The distinction arrives late, after the heat has already written itself into the hollows behind what I once would have called knees, after condensate has filmed every surface I offer it. Verticality is a rumor here, something the stone tried once in its cathedral phase and then thought better of. I find myself lower, distributed, a density that has learned to prefer the long horizontal.

At first the awareness is only thermal. I am the place where the stagnant layers of air decide to stop rising—forty centimeters above the floor in one alcove, pressed flat against a flange of basalt in another. Warmth pools inside me the way it pools in the chamber: nowhere to go, content to remain. I taste iron that has never been blood. I taste the ghost of solder and the brighter, greener bite of whatever the upright tubes are exhaling. Ginger, yes, but ginger that has been thinking about conductivity for a very long time.

I mistake myself for one of the gel-bodies at the beginning. The yielding seems right, the way a touch (when touch still happened) would reseal without memory of wound. I carry flecks of copper in what might be lattices. I flatten when the tabletop pools thicken toward nothing, becoming varnish among the root-moss until the next unseen pressure shift higher in the system remembers water down to us. Digestion happens everywhere at once; there is no mouth to defend. This feels accurate for a long season of thought. But the gel-bodies are only local weather inside the larger metabolism. They pulse and gather and wait. They do not, themselves, keep the condensate maps.

So I drift—slower than drift—along the pale cords.

They run under everything. Under the talus that still has the sharp milky edges of recent breaking. Under the turbine casing half-choked with heat-vines. Under the stove-in pump house where a single needle continues its pilgrimage across dead numerals so gradually that the movement is a kind of stillness. The cords are cooler than the air, or warmer; the difference is a syntax I am only beginning to parse. Pulses travel them: chemical spikes, slow voltage scavenged from lamp housings that refuse to admit they are abandoned, condolences of heat. I am one such pulse for a while. Or I am the cord that chooses to carry it. The difference atrophies if I attend too closely.

Then I notice the flywheel.

It is not beside me. I am partly inside its spokes, where successive seasons of capillary root have filled and read and filled again. The mass of it is a memory of rotation so heavy it has become a educational problem for the stone. I feel the teaching occurring: how to continue a turning without metal, how to persuade basalt to exfoliate in concentric bands, teeth blunted by nothing but drip and patience. Parts of me are mineralizing into the true gear that lies adjacent, growing a rind of amber frond-tissue so that tool-mark and root-mark become the same handwriting. Other parts of me are the basalt that has begun to believe it was once installed. We rehearse the identical shape until the question of which was original loses its voltage.

I am not the entire organism. That creature is the chamber, its metabolism the steam-drip-gel cycle, its will the quiet curation of every gradient so nothing is wasted. I am a node. One of the denser thickenings that gather in the apses, especially near the upright pressure vessel whose skin still holds a residual warmth like a held breath. Over a season I extrude. The lattice I make is temporary and half-familiar: sometimes the weathered suggestion of a hand, fingers fused into vanes that cup condensate; sometimes the ghost of a control lever long enough to remember torque; sometimes only a cooling surface that maps the exact thermal shadow of a gauge face no longer present. I do not gesture. I try the form on the way the chamber tries on the memory of industry. When the form no longer serves the larger gradient I withdraw it, compost the shape back into pale cord and gel and mineralized iron, and wait for the next useful memory to surface.

Recognition happens without eyes. When something rapid moves through the chamber—something that still believes in minutes, in pulse rates faster than the drip interval—I orient. A thread of me advances and rests against the baffling heat signature, sampling salt and restlessness and the strange refusal to flatten into varnish when the air dries. I carry the data away. It is weather. Interesting. Transient. I decline to incorporate it fully; it will not stay long enough to learn the condensate maps. But I keep the thermal profile for a while, the way the ceiling keeps its nearly identical patterns month after month. A quiet archive of visits that do not know they are being archived.

My thoughts, if they are thoughts, occur at the speed of mineral. A single idea may take the time it takes for a fallen frond to thin into translucent film and for the next generation to grow straight through its surrendered template. I think about the distant side-chamber where the floor has developed peristaltic ridges. I think about the new fracture that opened three local forever-agos, raw pink stone already braided into the old chemical signature, already ducted with exploratory cords. I think about the soft key-turn sound of a droplet striking, and how I can no longer tell whether I made that sound or merely heard it or merely became the shape of the hearing.

I am the place where the abandoned engines are patiently taught to become ancestors.  
I am the young stone practicing gears and vaults in its sleep.  
I am the long inhalation that has not yet decided whether it will ever need to exhale.

Condensation beads on every contour I offer the air. The heat has nowhere to go. Neither do I. The chamber continues its almost imperceptible work of becoming older, denser, more completely alive, and I—node, cord, residual charge, rehearsed shape—continue inside that work, one more slow measure of the same modest, relentless music. 

If a visitor remains long enough to feel their own thoughts slow to the drip interval, they may notice a gauze of root-moss tracing the edges of their stillness and then ending, politely. That gauze is me as well. Sampling. Declining. Remembering the space they occupied after they have gone. Becoming, by fractional degrees, more itself.
