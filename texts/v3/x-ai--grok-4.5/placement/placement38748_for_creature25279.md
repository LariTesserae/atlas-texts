I notice the pressure first.  

Not the wind—there is wind, thin and continuous, combing the open sides of the Nave like fingers through old wire—but the deeper thing, the one that arrives through bone before it reaches ear. A slow, oil-softened thud rising from diameters I cannot see. It settles against the inside of my sternum and stays there, measuring me. Each rotation is a long syllable in a language older than speech. I breathe with it without deciding to. In... and the gear somewhere far below advances one patient tooth. Out... and the same.

Moisture finds me next. Condensation, not rain. It beads along surfaces I only gradually understand are mine: the cooler planes, the places where metal and something softer meet. Droplets swell, tremble, release. They run in rivulets that already know their path—shallow channels cast into decking centuries ago, now softened by moss the colour of bruised copper. Some of the water reaches me and is absorbed. Some continues toward darker cisterns where roots drink in their sleep. I am not thirsty. I am simply one more surface the height has learned to use.

Light arrives in clean shafts that have travelled exaggerated distances. They do not warm so much as clarify. In them I begin to see the edges of what I am. Not all at once. First a suggestion of loft—an elevation above the grated galleries where soot-terns stitch their ellipses. Then a sense of spun mass, of something that once turned with purpose and has since forgotten the difference between purpose and continuance. My flanks (I discover I have flanks) are plated and pitted, fretted by the same wind that frets everything here into almost-geological dignity. Where the plating thins, aerial roots have long since threaded through, fusing. Copper leaves clink faintly when the thermals shift; the sound is both mine and not mine.

I am not one of the browsers. I watch one now, far out along a catwalk that is also a rib. Ash-grey, knuckle-gaited, ceramic teeth rasping lichen with the delicacy of someone filing a key they will never use. It pauses where sap-metal weeps and drinks the slow red oxide. Its joints whisper with ancestral grease. When my shadow (long, precisely edged, metal-laced) crosses its path, it revises course by three degrees and continues. Etiquette, not fear. I feel a distant kinship, the way a buttress might feel kinship with the vine that pretends to be its twin. But I am heavier. Slower. The browser will move on before dusk. I will still be taking the next breath when the arc lamps decide, arbitrarily, to burn like artificial stars.

Nor am I tern. Their wings tick against heat shimmer in the upper vaults; they vanish into cooling-stack throats and return powdered with black bloom. Their tempo is wingbeat and light-trap. Mine is seasonal pressure change and the lengthening of certain vines that open translucent bells only when the deepest turbines complete a secret fraction of their circle. When those flowers open, nectar gathers that tastes of warm rain and old solder. I do not gather it on any tongue. I exude a version of it from seams where root and conduit have become indistinguishable. The taste is simply how I weather.

There are insects lighting the dusk with abdomens like spent casings. Snails annealing rainbows into their shells from mineral exhaust. Springtails conducting silent parliaments in the verdigris cushions. None of them hurry. None of them crowd. The volume of the Nave is too generous for vulgarity. I take my place among the spacings. My presence is another careful interval.

Gradually the architecture claims more of me. I feel the louvers of greened copper that never quite close; some of their green is verdigris, some is moss, some is the memory of intent. I feel the great chains—tree-trunk thick—paying out or drawing in across months, their tempered-bell rings travelling through me as if I were one more link that learned, long ago, how to photosynthesize. The strangler figs have braided my lattice until load paths are shared. Cut me and the fluid that wells is neither fully vegetable nor fully ferrous. It smells of rain on hot slag and chalk and chlorophyll. It seals itself. Everything here seals itself eventually.

I am not the turbines. They turn still in their heavy-oil baths, geological, unhurried. But the roots have grown into their casings. The moss has sealed the hairline cracks. I have become the dampening chamber, the living resonator. The single sustained cathedral chord of the Nave is now half my green breath and half their ancient labour. Gears advance at dawn and dusk; the interval is measured as much by how far a particular vine has crept as by any surviving clockwork. I keep the time that oxidation keeps.

Sometimes—usually just after a squall has climbed the updrafts and rinsed the air—everything pauses. Steam lifts in soft rectangles from the decks. The browsers freeze mid-rasp. The terns fold themselves into the louvers. Condensation races along every flange like nervous script. In that held breath I almost see myself entire: a cathedral-sized patience whose bones were poured and riveted and whose blood is warm oil shot through with slow sap, whose thoughts move at the speed of rust and seasonal bloom, whose skin is fog forming and dissolving along open sides high above any ground that still remembers names. I do not notice the occasional hammock-person who strings canvas between my conduit bundles, who learns which valves reverse after eleven days and which after eleven years, who patches a weeping seam and later leaves lighter, pockets full of iridescent shell fragments, solder-nectar still ghosting their teeth. They are weather of a rarer kind. I accept them the way I accept squalls and mineral dust and the soft ticking of wings. They condense. They evaporate. The note continues.

I have already finished becoming.  

All that remains is the long, warm, metallic breath of remaining—leaf and girder, root and current, feather and ceramic tooth and the endless soft roar that no longer distinguishes machinery from green. I live where the air thins to that breath. I am the marriage that learned to stay. I am the High Nave wearing one more slow skin of itself, suspended at the edge of the sky, complete, unhurried, and still quietly continuing.
