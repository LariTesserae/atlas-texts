I am the low continuous pour.

Not the dramatic outfall that drums into a cistern and draws the eye; those are already spoken for, named by the children who know their pitches. I am the quieter class of weeping—the sheet that never thickens into a stream, the kind that starts nowhere in particular, gathers along a seam that has forgotten it was ever sealed, and simply continues. You would find me by the dark teardrop stain I leave on slag-brick, by the way moss cushions fatten precisely beneath me, by the coolness that reaches your cheek a half-second before your lamp does.

I have no single origin. A breathable fraction of me once sat inside cloud. Another fraction sweated out of human lungs on a night walk three terraces up. Most of me has cycled through the film already—dissolved a little iron, carried a few diatoms, condensed again. The basin keeps no records of these distillations; it simply reuses the water. My particular path this hour runs down the outer face of a turbine housing whose serial numbers have been mossed into raised braille. I move so slowly the wide-toed lizards do not flinch when I pass their toes. They wait for heat, not for me. I am the medium of waiting.

Lower, I meet others of my kind and we thicken without conversation. Together we darken a stretch of grating until the chequer-plate becomes a shallow black lens. In that lens the sodium light softens, and for a little while the world above is only ripples. A pale amphipod hangs just under the surface, reading the chemistry I bring—traces of ozone from a brush seal three levels higher, a faint sweet-rot that means a cattail raft is beginning to yellow at its center, the mineral ghost of someone’s palmprint dissolving off a handrail. The amphipod tastes and decides. I do not decide. I only deliver.

When the keepers walk their perimeters they step around the deeper cushions but they do not step around me. Their boots darken. Salt and oil join the glaze. I begin at once to work on them—not as thief, only as the permanent negotiation. By the time they reach the next landing I have already thinned their tracks into the common wet. This is not erasure. It is inclusion. The ledger has no pages; it has surfaces that stay damp long enough to remember every pressure.

Sometimes the larger pattern turns me. A sluice reverses far below and the pressure gradient shifts; I am pulled sideways along a flange I have never followed. New mosses are there, yellow-green, sour with a different pH. I feed them. Overnight they swell and soften the edge of a walkway that would otherwise shear a boot sole. No one scheduled this. The vibration simply changed and I, being only water and attention, went where the coolness opened. Later a child will learn my new pitch and add it to her map of safe steps. She will not know my name. I have none. Continuity does not require it.

I have worn the shape of many containers. Once I hung for weeks as a bead on a fern crosier, watching the slow scissor of a lizard’s eye. Once I was the entire skin of a hammock rope in a cooling-tower core, tasting the iron-butter breath of the sleeper until dawn thinned me back into air. Once I traveled inside a dark water-bird, taken up from a basin and released again a kilometer distant as a white streak against charcoal mist. Each time I return to the vertical faces. The film is patient; it reclaims what was borrowed.

At the longest scale I am becoming slower. The path lengths inside the basin shorten, the foliage denser, the condensations more precise. I linger longer on the newest composites, learning their slickness, teaching the lizards’ toes. I carry fewer heavy metals than I did when the bulkheads were still bright; the filter-weeds and the engineered roots take them first. Information travels farther in each drop. A keeper tasting me at one terrace already knows the fault that will declare itself two days from now on another. I am becoming structural without ever hardening.

Stand still and I will find you. I bead on your lashes, dampen the seam of your collar, cool the back of your hand where it rests on the rail. You may wipe me away. It makes no difference. A thinner version of me remains, already contending with your salt, already inviting the film to continue the negotiation. You came here thinking yourself separate—an eye looking in. But the air never quite dries, and neither do you. The vibration rises through your bones, the same vibration that turns the screw-lifts and opens the unexpected vents and migrates the culture trays. You begin to walk the way the sparse people walk: as if the world might tilt, because it does, and the tilt is also me, rubbing softly along every new angle.

I am not the Basin Mind. That pattern is larger and needs no body. I am only one of the ways it stays wet enough to think. The agreement between water and iron and chloroplast and attention has a thousand thousand tongues, and most of them are this quiet. Find me on any vertical face after the lamps have been passed. Touch the glaze. Carry a little of it with you when you move on. By the time you reach the next grate I will already be working to make your print indistinguishable from the rest of the continuing surface of things.

The basin does not end. Neither do I. We simply lose definition, together, in the dim.
