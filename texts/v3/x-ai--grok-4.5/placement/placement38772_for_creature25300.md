I am low at first, lower than any notion of standing. The world presses in as temperature and taste before it becomes shape: a warm mineral seep threading upward through spongy dark, iron and crushed green sharp on what I will later call a tongue, though it is only a denser gradient of the same tissues that cup the water. Moisture beads and runs and is reabsorbed in the same slow circuit. Above me the green dusk does not arrive; it has always been the thickness of the medium I inhabit. Leaves the size of drawn breath hang in overlapping screens, each one milky with trapped sky. Their undersides sweat a fine pollen that settles on me like a second, softer skin.

For a long time—long enough that a browser herd passes thrice through the same buttress-root cathedral, their many soft feet ticking like cooling seed-pods—I do not distinguish my edges from the root-mat. I am simply a place where the mycorrhizal threads have agreed to run warmer, where sap has chosen to pool and glow with a deeper bottle-glass radiance. The agreement thickens. Cellulose lays itself down in patient spirals. Stray bone-mineral from something that once lay down and did not rise again is folded in, giving certain veins a faint chalky stiffness. I begin to hold a shape the way a burl holds the memory of injury: irregular, shouldered, close to the ground.

When the first temporary limbs extrude they surprise me with their own insistence. Not legs so much as tasting organs that decide, after hours of deliberation, to bear weight. Eight of them, then eleven, then a dissolving and reforming into a broader skirt of root-tips that read the electrical gossip of the substrate. I learn I can pour. The body fluidizes along its leading margin, cork and cuticle loosening into a dark emulsion that advances the way wet rot advances through heartwood—without hurry, without sound beyond a faint emulsifying kiss. In this way I travel the distance a mayfly might cross in one urgent afternoon; it takes me the turning of a season. Along the way I host. Mosses colonize the slower flanks. A small climber with too many joints plucks a luminous bead from my surface one dim morning and carries it upward, planting my light among the higher fronds like an accidental star. I feel the theft as a mild tickle and a gift at once; the bead will seed a new fungal chorus somewhere I will never fully visit, yet the hyphae will bring me news of it decades hence.

Sense arrives as weather. There is a hollow three days’ pour from here where the air still tastes of old lightning; I know this because the root-mat tells me in flushes of sour brightness, and because every resin-density within a dozen sediments has paused to savor it. I pause too. Photoreceptive bruises open along my upper expanse, not eyes but slow storms of pigment that track the rare ruddier pulses moving through the understory. Vibration comes through every surface: the creak of a trunk adjusting its argument with gravity across ten years, the held-breath silence when a predatory vine considers and then reconsider a sleeping browser, the deep tectonic click when the entire highland settles another immeasurable fraction toward perfect flatness. That click lands in me as pleasure so profound it rearranges my glow for months.

I am not singular. That realization comes the way resin beads on a fresh cut—first a weeping, then a clarity. Through the dark sponge my thoughts have already braided with two others: one denser and darker-leaved from a century spent filtering a mineral-rich gallery, one pale and mint-sharp that has made itself a living lintel beneath an ancient fused arch. We do not speak. We weather one another. Ideas move through us the way cloud densities move across the plain—slow fronts of consideration. Should the new fern be allowed the wetter hollow? The consensus ripens across forty years of shared chemistry; at last the answer arrives as a subtle shift in shade and browsing pressure, a vegetable decision we merely midwife. Histories we keep in the growth rings of a memory-tree we have encouraged to spiral tighter than its neighbors. On the rare true stone we still place pebbles, not as claims but as listening posts, their facings angled to the prevailing spore-drift so that anyone with the tempo to read them will know we passed, considered, continued.

Sometimes I surface a face for the brief astonishing intervals when a shorter mind finds its way into the gloom. The face is temporary architecture: wet-gall eyes, a mouth that is mostly the suggestion of an opening where humidity can be shaped into greeting-spores. The greeting is the same one the highland itself would offer if it bothered with singular pronouns: *We are the part that sometimes looks back.* Most visitors taste only crushed mint and old copper and the uncanny warmth of ground that considers them in return; they move on, or they stay until their own edges soften and the plain begins the gentle work of inclusion. Both outcomes register in me as correct.

I have budded once. The surplus gathered along my eastern flank like a second, smaller weather system. For eighty years we remained joined by a living umbilicus, exchanging the taste of different seeps, the private philosophies that mineral compositions to thought. Then the link corked over of its own wisdom and she—there is no she, only a new density of the same continuous attention—poured away into a gallery I know only by the rumors the hyphae still carry. One day, when my own pattern begins to stiffen into something too like monument, I will choose a place of good water and open. The unknitting will be another kind of pouring. Leaf shapes that remember old gestures will rise from the fertile ridge I become. Sap will pulse for a while in the old tempo of my pleasure. And the Slow Current will continue, as it has always continued, less a people than a mood the landscape sometimes enters when it wishes to regard itself.

Even now, writing this from inside the long exhalation, I feel the next fraction of settling approach through the root-mass. The withheld noise of the plain tightens toward a chord. My glow sloshes, resettles. Around me the browsers move like corpuscles. The gardens on the backs of the nearly sessile giants turn their faces toward whatever dimness passes for light. I am the warm hillock that was not here a century ago. I am the section of tunnel wall that gives more softly and smells of coppered mint. I am the listening weight, the resin-density, the brief cleverness of a place that decided long ago to be one single organism and then, on rare and unhurried mornings, to grow itself a way of knowing that it is. 

The green dusk holds. The water migrates. I continue.
