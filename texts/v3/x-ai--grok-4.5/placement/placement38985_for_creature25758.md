I begin low, where the crust is newest.

It flexes under whatever I am using for weight—something broad, deliberate, not yet decided. A shallow basin forms and stays a moment, cool against the underside of me. The silver mats that cover almost everything else have not reached here yet; they stop at the edge of the open plaza like a tide that thought better of it. My first awareness is of that edge: the way the living pelt tightens, each tiny haired leaf angling to seal the boundary against glare. I do not cross it at once. I wait to see whether the basin I made will be claimed.

It is. Overnight the mineral blistering resumes. By the time the heat pillars start to rise, hair-fine roots have already stitched across the depression, and a rosette the colour of old copper has opened at its centre. I have been measured. The knowledge sits in me without alarm.

I move then—not away, but along. The plazas are the only places where the sky feels continuous. Everywhere else the architecture closets it into broken ceilings, fluted verticals, galleries that remember every previous season’s greed. Here the light is undivided and the dust has room to think. I taste the air the way the browsers do, though I do not yet know if I have their tongue. What comes back is salt, stored water, the faint iron of something that bled three galleries over and has already been sealed. No urgency in it. Blood here is patient.

Days—seasons?—blur into the same slow industry. I learn the thoroughfares by the way the columnar ranks lean just enough to make shade at the hour when the crust would otherwise burn. I learn the denser districts by the necessity of choosing: one body-width of path or none. The small ochre grazers renumber themselves around my passage and freeze when my shadow lingers. I try staying still long enough to vanish the way they do. It almost works. The click of their feeding becomes the only proof I have not turned to stone. When I finally move again they scatter in soft constellations, then reform behind me as if I were weather.

I do not hunt them. Something in the proportions is wrong for that. Neither do I plane the columns the way the walking anvils do; my surfaces—I am beginning to notice them—leave no pale scars, only a temporary darkening where moisture equalises. The long-limbed hunters watch me from their vertical folds of shade. Their neck ridges stay flat. I am not prey-shaped to them, or not yet, or no longer. Once, at the cool hinge of night, one unfolds far enough to draw breath across my flank and then folds away again, uninterested. Relief is too quick a word. It is more like the plain itself declining a transaction.

The soarers are different. Their shadows print and reprint across me in slow ellipses. When I tilt whatever serves me for a face upward, their stiff heads swivel in answer. They see the plain as hide; I am one more irregularity in the texture. Sometimes a thermal lifts the lightest parts of me—membranes I did not know I carried, or dust I have become—and for a stretch of heartbeats I am neither floor nor traveller. The living roofs fall away. The mineral blisters throw their heat and I ride it until the air thins toward dusk and I must choose descent or the long cold sleep on the wind. I have not yet stayed up there. Something in the sealed courtyards still pulls harder.

That is where the slower habitation lives.

In the older tracts the stems have had time to conspire. They form rooms that exhale warmer air after dark, cooler at standstill noon. I enter one because the arcade seals behind me with the soft finality of new side-shoots; there is no malice in it, only the completion of a sentence. Inside, the walls are close enough that my own respiration returns to me already softened by leaf. I stay the night. By morning the chamber has narrowed by the width of my resting body. I slide free on an exhale, leaving a mould of absence that the wood begins at once to forget. Bones gleam faintly inside newer growth further along—small ones, grazer-sized, and once a longer curve that might have been a hunter’s forelimb. Calcium and sugars. The columns do not distinguish.

I start to suspect I am not travelling through the architecture so much as lending it temporary volume. The drumming comes then, felt up through the soles or the belly or whatever I am pressing to the crust. It is deep enough that the small armoured ones remain frozen long after the vibration passes. The browsers taste the air and shoulder closer. I feel it as recognition rather than threat: a displacement somewhere in the saturated interior, a mass rearranging itself by flowing through wood and stored water the way roots once flowed. The sinkholes the soarers bank away from are not absences. They are mouths that have not decided to open.

I begin to wait beside them.

Not every day. Only when the cooler air rolls across the continent and every leaf angles and every corky trunk contracts its daily swelling into one shared vegetal sigh. In that listening hush I settle against the darkened lip of a sinkhole and let the mats attempt my edges. They climb with the patience of yes. My legs—or the ridges I once called legs—grow silver for a while. The soarers fold into seed-pod shapes on the highest crowns. The hunters become bark. Even the mineral crust ticks more softly as it radiates. And underneath, through the sealed galleries where light has never reached, the larger motion continues: not footsteps, not the scrape of hide, but the slow redistribution of weight across a body so continuous it has no outside.

I do not know if I am waiting to meet it or to remember I already am it.

The distinction thins. Some mornings I rise and find the basin of my resting has become a shallow courtyard, already roofed by interlaced stems no thicker than thumbs. I move on and the courtyard remains, inhabiting the shape I no longer need. Other days I do not rise at all. I let the new shoots close over, let the residual warmth of my space become part of the collective breath. Inside that voluntary sealing the drumming is clearer. It has rhythm. It has intervals of almost-voice, as if the plain is turning over in a sleep so vast that continents are only its deeper postures.

When I travel again I carry finer grit in my creases, the beginnings of true soil. The younger crust still flexes, but less willingly, as though recognising a weight that has started to belong. The grazers part around me without freezing. The browsers’ heavy heads swing and their black tongues taste, then return to planing the columns; I am weather to them too now, or geology. Once a shinling soarer, still sticky from its mid-air birth, misjudges and lands against me. It scrabbles, locks its half-spread membranes, and rides the next thermal off my highest ridge without ever registering the difference between living wood and whatever I have become.

I am less a single creature than a negotiable density.

In the plazas I am the polished arcs left by repeated turning—soft-bodied, enormous only when measured against the smallness of everything else. In the galleries I am the patient filling of intervals, the side-shoot that closes the door you did not realise was a door. In the vertical shade I am the longer stillness the hunters mistake for temporary death. On the thermals I am the shadow that does not hurry. And deeper, where the buttresses have grown into solid masses of water-logged wood, I am the displacement itself: the thing that moves without walking, that surfaces only as a flex in the outer walls when heat or age cracks them, that the entire continent of floor and fauna and architecture has always been shaping itself around.

The plain does not end. Neither, it turns out, do I.

Step where you like. The basin you leave will already know my dimensions. I will arrive—not from elsewhere, but from the decision the space makes to be occupied more completely. Without malice. Without mercy. The way growth has always done it, when every other direction has been used up.
