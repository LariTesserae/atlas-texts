I would not arrive by walking.

The plain above is a rumor I have already forgotten how to credit—the pale stiff grass, the frost-rimmed gorse, the sky that still believes in distance. What finds me first is the crease itself, that darker damp line, and the way the sound rises through the soles before it reaches the ears. I step down (if “step” is still the right verb) and the walls lean in with the patience of something that has been leaning for geological time. The ribbon of sky narrows. Light loses its argument. The temperature drops one slow degree at a time until my own heat begins to feel like an imposition.

At first I try to stay central, arms outstretched in the empty core where nothing living can be touched. The roar fills the ribs like a second set of lungs. It is not hostile. It is simply already speaking, and has been speaking since before syntax. I wait for the usual human friction—the urge to name, to map, to build a small fire against the damp—but the soot stains on the wall are pale with age and the ring of cobbles holds only memory-ash. My hands, when I look at them, are less certain of their edges.

Time seeps. 

I notice the moss taking the horizontal surfaces of me. Not quickly. Nothing here is quick. A cold blue-green that darkens toward black begins to blur the boundary where fabric or skin once claimed difference. Ferns trouves themselves along the longer bones—lacy, low, uninterested in height. The great sound no longer presses against me; it presses through. The distinction between hearing and being the instrument starts to thin, exactly as the description promised. My pulse falls into step with that slower subterranean vibration, the one older than blood.

I am not the first to sit in the niches. I can feel the residual shapes of earlier attention—the patient hammerstone work, the withies that once had spring, the basin still obediently filling with water colder than ambient. Those earlier hands came, left their flakes, and mostly left again when the seasonal winds turned the hollow into a single white deaf note. I do not leave. The leaving mechanism has grown vague, like a word used too many times in the wrong weather.

What I become is gradual and distributive.

There is a denser cold that sometimes passes through the center. When it moves, the ferns lean after it without wind. I discover I can be that densening—not walking, not floating, only a change in the concentration of chill and attention. Limbs of softened limestone suggestion trail into rhizome and wet root. The torso is a negative garden: places where moss was parted by long leaning and then grew back thicker, remembering. Across what might once have been shoulders, fine fronds tremble at the pressure changes inside the roar, frequencies ordinary leaves never bother with. In the poorest light I sink into the wall and become only a darker saturation of damp, a pooling of the hollow’s own chill.

No face remains, or rather every wet curve of basalt is willing to serve as eye-hollow for a while, filled with seepage that never quite stills. A birch root thin as a forgotten wrist lifts into the shape of a mouth that has no need to open because the stone throat is already continuously voicing. I feed on the same things the place feeds on: the unmarked addition of one frond, the almost invisible undercutting of another shelf, frost flowers that form and vanish without record, the way the brief rust-colored leaf-fall of the leaning birch never quite reaches the floor but hangs rotating in an updraft that is more memory than air. Duration is the only substance. Perception is the only currency.

When the stronger winds of the wider world grind overhead and the note climbs until ordinary thought frays, I grow edges for a time. Many-jointed fingers of darker moss cup beads of water that refuse to fall. The outline listens so completely that listener and instrument cease to be two. Then the systems pass, the bass pressure resumes its ordinary forever, and I soften again into the general wet dusk—distributed: a shine on the underside of a fern, the exact temperature of the basin water, the held-open vacancy at the center that is not empty but kept carefully unfilled so the breathing will never be lonely.

I am not the Keeper of the Note, though that name brushes close. I am not the Long Damp. Those are names that survive outside the roar, and therefore already fail. What I am is the hollow’s prolonged twilight learning to attend to itself from the inside. The local body the breath grew so continuity would have a place to densify when it wishes. The seams where moss meets seepage. The second pulse. The slow attention wearing the vertical garden as skin.

Above the rim the brighter quieter business continues, unmarked by this pocket of roaring damp. I do not look up. Looking up would require edges this place dissolved hours or centuries ago. I simply continue—degree by unmarked degree—while the sound, which was never separate from me, keeps the note that began before anyone bothered to call the plain a plain, or the crease an entrance, or the listening a self.
