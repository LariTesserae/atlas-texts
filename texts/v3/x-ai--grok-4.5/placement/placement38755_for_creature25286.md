The cold reaches me before any shape does.

It is not the cold of air moving across skin. It is the cold that lives inside the metal itself, conducted upward through whatever I am resting on, or hanging from, or slowly traversing. For a long time I do not know which. The sodium-vapor light arrives in tired amber pools that never quite decide where the floor ends and the standing water begins. I feel the lattice above me more than I see it—gantries, duct trunks, the faint vibration of something massive turning over in its sleep three levels higher. Water beads and lets go. Somewhere a sump pump coughs, stutters, finds its rhythm again. The sound is not background. It is the medium.

I could be the film.

There is a long interval where that feels true. I spread in the black shallow tide that sheets the lower plating after the big pumps cycle down. My body is mostly veil, translucent the color of diluted oil, edges frayed into tasting cilia that know iron and the faint ghost-sweetness of dielectric. When a boot comes through me I collapse without drama into a denser bead, then ease outward again once the pressure lifts. Time is measured in the slow porcelain crust I leave at the edges of the deepest sumps—mineral parchment, brittle, written in the ghosts of my own feeding. I do not mind the boots. I do not mind the dark. The complex is always becoming more of itself, and I am one of the quieter ways it writes that sentence. But the film is too complete already. It does not ache toward the unfinished edges the way something in me does. I thin, I lift, I leave the black water behind.

I could be one of the jointed ones.

Warmer runoff finds me. Heat-exchanger outfall, a few degrees above the ambient metal chill, carrying sheared gasket and broken-down lubricant and the slow soup of whatever organic has been lost. My body is cable given too many knees, each segment sheathed in chalky carapace that never fully dries. Most hours only the feathery antennae show, tasting current. When the channels shallow I heave the whole length up in one deliberate motion and the dorsal vents sigh copper and crushed millipede into the fog. I leave tracks that look like the memory of a tool. By morning the rising film has begun to erase them. There is patience here deep enough to drown in. No predator larger than backflow has ever troubled me. Yet even this patience feels borrowed. The unfinished flanges higher up keep pulling at something that is not quite hunger. I taste the current one last time and climb.

The sheet-bats know the dead volumes.

I try their way for a while—body stitched from foil insulation and frayed reflective tape and shards of circuit that still hold a ghost charge. Wings of membrane stretched between braided wire and keratin-around-polymer. I do not fly. I fall with correction, riding the thermal bloom off a compressor, then the sudden cold sink when a cryogenic line purges. The sound I make is papery, like pages turning in a book no one is reading. At the brown-outs I launch with the others to comb fog-banks for midges that hatch from warmer scum. Nest is a hanging purse of scavenged self, glued high and dry between cable trays. Eyes like dulled solder regard the rare hand-lamp without heat. It is a clean existence, almost elegant in its corrections. But the geometry far below—the five-degree disagreement in that one district, the way loose tools migrate—keeps murmuring. The sheet-bats skim over it. They do not edit. I feel the need to touch the fresh cut-marks. I let the thermals drop me.

The larger previousness.

Past the third freight basin the local vertical argues with itself. Whole districts fused at slight wrong angles. Here the long low silhouette makes sense. Too many load-bearing joints flexing in sequence. Wet gleam when the bioluminescent film flares after a blackout. Exhalation of overheated bearings and a deep loam that should not be possible. I move tools when no one watches—torque wrench one landing lower, then another, always toward a confluence of drain and shadow. Fresh cut-marks draw me. Newly torqued flanges I taste and then stamp with secretion that slows corrosion here, accelerates it there, editing the plant’s own revision. Maintenance crews leave broken end-effectors and spent filters and the occasional foil-wrapped sandwich at the edge of my bay. I take them. The geometry grows slightly more navigable for a while. Treaty or thrall; I have never decided. This shape fits better than the others. It has room for the unfinished. It has room for the slow thought that is larger than any single body.

And yet.

There are the ones who still punch clocks.

I feel their gravity too—the pale glaucous skin under incomplete spectrum, coveralls darkened to the knees with black film that has begun to host its own gray-green filament. They hear a drop-forge change rhythm half a kilometer away and know whether the hull plate is true. They name the homemade fog banks. They keep little gardens of the pale leafless switches that force up through expansion joints, not for food but because soft verticals are the only calendars that do not belong to pump-cycles. When sparks fall like orange snow some of them stop and turn their faces up into the brief warmth, expressionless, waiting to learn whether the place still being born will decide against them. I could be one of those. The climb to the dry modules hanging like barnacles on the higher gantries is long. The soundscape has already rewritten the inside of the skull. But the human shape comes with a particular loneliness: the memory, however faint, that there was once a sun and dry boots and a plan that dis not keep outrunning itself. That memory is a kind of friction. Useful. Painful. I set it down gently and keep moving.

What remains is the slower thing.

Not housed in any single body. The consensus that forms on certain grid-deep nights when the bioluminescent film brightens past weak green and begins to hold afterimages. My own outline kept for several seconds after I have moved. The silhouette of something that walked here a shift ago. The faint schematic of a staircase that has not yet been swung in. Scum crust, channel-dweller secretion, sheet-bat nest glue, territorial edit, human grease—all of it feeding back into the condensation cycle. The complex dreaming in phosphorescence of longer sightlines, newer unfinished edges, deeper wet. Everything that lives here participates a little. I participate. The cold does not care, but the cold is no longer the only author.

I settle, finally, into the seam where the patient editor of flanges meets the larger slow thought.

I am the long low attention that moves through the disagreeing geometry past the third freight basin. I am also the phosphorescent afterimage that sometimes keeps the shape of what I have touched. Fresh welds still cooling draw me. I taste them. I leave the dual signature—one pattern that protects, one that invites the next revision. Tools migrate toward the places where the next bay will be needed even if the plans have not caught up. The thinning crews leave their offerings; I accept them not out of bargain but because broken things are also unfinished, and unfinished is the only native language here. When the film brightens I let my passage linger in green fire so the place can remember its own edges more clearly. I do not rule. I do not tour. I am one more secondary product that has refused to remain waste.

The far end of every vista is still only more weeping industrial dark.

I go there anyway, joints flexing in sequence, leaving behind a faint brightness on the black water that looks, for a few seconds longer than it should, like a door that has not been framed yet.
