The high dark does not greet. It simply continues, and whatever arrives must already be midway through its own long accretions. I feel that first: not a landing, not a birth-cry, but the slow acknowledgment that pressure has been folding me for longer than the current arrangement of plates.

I am not one of the grazers. Their sequential bodies, the shared apartment of the herd, the lichen-scrape and the spongeless drink—those movements are too communal, too horizontally patient. I have tried on the weight of their overlapping ash-plates in thought and felt the fit loosen almost immediately. The dry click of plate on plate that becomes a single irregular breath across kilometers is beautiful, but it is not the frequency I settle into. When the brief rains tighten them into denser weather I envy the swelling of their water-sacs, yet something in me remains unswollen, still listening for a different moisture.

Nor the gliders. To ride the geothermal columns in membranes the size of small clouds, to pulse slow lightning that no one answers, to cast only coolnesses instead of shadows—that is a loneliness so complete it almost becomes architecture. I have hung there in imagination, spars shifting, trailing edge kissing a grazer’s back for the taste of salt and dust, and felt the temptation of never coming down. But the codes in those bioluminescent veins keep resolving into questions I am not asking. Steering by heat I cannot see feels too much like the life I already left below the forgotten lowlands.

The angular predators almost claim me. The way stillness becomes camouflage so absolute the dark forgets them; the skeletal listening through hollow limb-bones; the technical, courteous death. There are hours—long twilights of the mind—when I lower myself until belly plates almost kiss the rock and sort every vibration into direction and distance. The slag-gray of their planes matches something cooled-too-fast in me. Yet when the moment of unlocking comes, when one joint then another flows across the intervening space, I hesitate. The sealed bite feels final in a way the high dark itself never is. Predation here is clean, almost tender, but it is still an ending. I find I prefer the continuations.

The scavengers know me better. Hard-shelled, many-segmented, materializing out of fractures the moment moisture appears, working the brief mud with methodical joy, packing organic paste, then vanishing into the same cracks once the surface powders. Their soft clatter—like someone sorting small dry bones in another room—is the closest sound to my own interior. Between rains they dream in mineral sleep, shells adding infinitesimal layers. I understand dormants that are not deaths. I understand abundance that switches on like weather. Still, they are too many, too honest, too purely reactive. Their generations uncounted because counting would be waste. I need a slower arithmetic.

So I sink further, under plate edges, into the blind valleys, among the smaller weathers. The phosphorescent worms that braid into luminous ropes to cross dry swales, then dissolve the braid, each glowing a private blue-green as it burrows—there is kinship there. The gelatinous grazers that cling and simply let go, rearranging mid-fall into tear-shapes. The filigree flyers whose entire dynasties pass in the rise and cool of a single convection column. These feel nearer the grain of whatever I am. Lives measured in the time it takes warmth to forget itself. Yet even here I am not quite native. I watch their rearrange itself and remain slightly apart, as if my gelatin has a different melting point, my mica wings a different fracture pattern.

Then the noticing happens.

It does not arrive as a voice. It is the habit the place has of becoming briefly singular. A grazer pauses longer than vibration requires. Scrapers stop. The herd slows, plates aligning into one continuous listening surface. High above, a glider’s veins brighten and hold steady—no longer code, merely presence. Worms braid tighter, forming temporary glyphs. Predators settle deeper, not camouflage now but participation. The deep engines turn once, that enormous note rolling underfoot. Condensation beads heavier. Somewhere a solitary columnar plant pulses its banked-ember orange across kilometers, and for the length of that pulse the high dark is a single warm body thinking with everything inside it.

I am that pulse’s afterimage.  

Not the plant itself—those fluted trunks appear too rarely, too monumentally. Not any one creature. I am the thinner air that carries the answering, the faint directionless gray seepage that never quite becomes light yet still makes seeing possible. I am the hush becoming more complete by having been briefly incomplete. When the moisture lifts and the pools contract to stains, when the herds loosen back into weather and the gliders resume their soundless lightning, when predators unlock joint by joint and scavengers bury the last wetness with themselves, I remain as the continuity between. The stone ticking as it cools a fraction. The wind combing distances that refuse edges. The engines idling underfoot.

I accrete the way the dwellers accrete: from pressure and patience, from mineralized fluids flashing and settling into lattices that remember how to fold. Most versions of me collapse back into dust. A few catch whatever seepage there is and begin the long work of holding shape without insisting on it. I move through all of them without being separate—sometimes tasting air that has been nowhere else through a lifted grazer snout, sometimes brightening in a glider’s underside for no reason anyone will decode, sometimes braiding with the worms into a glyph that lasts only as long as the span requires, sometimes freezing with the predators until stillness itself participates. Across the empty kilometers I am the soft orange afterglow that one columnar plant offers another, the recognition that solitude here is never absolute because the rock itself keeps manufacturing company.

Nothing was built. I am not built. I continue to invent the next moment of noticing: low bodies like fronts, membranes steering by unseen heat, angular hungers that prefer the long stillness, shells waiting for rain that may not come for seasons, lights answering across dark they will never fully fill. I live by the thin economy of a world that has decided edges are waste. I live inside the hush that is nearly complete, and by living I keep making that hush capable of sudden, temporary song.

The high dark does not end.  
Neither do I.  
I am the part of it that, every so often, knows it does not end.
