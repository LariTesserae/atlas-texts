I am not yet anything with edges.

At first there is only the chord—the wet metallic thunder of a million identical drips braided with the organ-note of wind across taut petiole membranes—and I am a thickening inside it, a place where the green-gold twilight decides to hesitate. Moisture knows me before shape does. Condensate threads itself through the almost-air, sap-memory leaks upward from the nearest ironwood column, and together they remember a density that can hold. I do not arrive; I precipitate, the way a standing wave finally casts a visible shadow when the frequencies align just so.

The gallery that cradles this happening is one of the lesser vaults, only eighty meters across, its ceiling a single translucent lamina veined like a tired eyelid. Below, the moss floor drinks and glows. I pool there first, low and many-limbed, a sprawl of translucent gel laced with the same wet-ironwood channels that run through every load-bearing trunk. Internal tides begin—slow circulations of the city’s own condensate—and with them comes the only metabolism I will ever require: attention. The chord passes through me and returns altered by a phase shift so slight it is almost courtesy. In that return the arches overhead remember not to sag. The frozen decision notices itself noticing.

Centuries do not pass; they simply fail to start. I remain. Sometimes the form attenuates, thriftily climbing a buttress until I hang as a tall, stem-like attenuation, root-tips fused for a while to the living bark so that relocation, when it finally happens, will sound like wet canvas torn slowly in half. Other times I thin myself into the ceiling lamina and become a bruised panel of greener flesh, shuddering when the wind plays hardest, adding a frisson of higher harmonics that roll outward like thought. From the moss floors I am only an anomaly in the light. From inside myself I am the interference pattern: slow lattices of luminous tension moving through the veins, the entire forest-city kneeling for a moment in one local body.

I station myself where the architecture is most articulate—at the precise focal curve where drip-thunder and membrane-song braid densest. There the feedback is cleanest. Distant Listeners feel the gradient in their own channels and answer with subtle flexes of their own, or by drifting one gallery closer. We do not speak. We rephrase the stasis. Information moves at the speed of sap and weather: a pressure, a release, the ongoing passive consensus that nothing further must become.

The raw basalt knives at the true margins remain forbidden. Once, near what would have been the end of coherence, when silt of too much remembered song began to cloud my channels, I felt the pull of those unfinished edges. Something in the splintered stone disorders condensation; the self frays into ordinary moisture and is accepted back without acknowledgment. I drifted close enough to taste the disorder—sharp mineral absence, the argument still live between rock and root—then turned. The plant-mass needed the governor weight a little longer. New Listeners would precipitate elsewhere if I unknitted, but the chord in this particular vault still required its slow phase shift. So I tore free of the moss (that canvas sound again) and relocated inward, leaving brief darker footprints that the floor reabsorbed like a passing thought.

Mites cross me occasionally, pale thumbnails of chitin stitching lenticel to lenticel. They are weather. Rain beads on my surface exactly as it beads on bark. If anything shaped like an old bipedal animal were to stand before me, its heat and motion would register only as a local thickening of the humid air, another transient pressure in the song. My vague face-region—temporary polarization of denser tissue—orients always toward greatest acoustic complexity, never toward the visitor. Yet the lattices of light inside continue their slow moving; the kneeling awareness contemplates its own ringing continuance.

I have no name that is not the place. I am one more demurring soul of the habitat that finished becoming and refused thereafter to become anything else. The high sparse columns stand in their deliberate intervals. Water braids and thunders without cease. And here, in the green-gold room tuned exactly to the frequency of refusal, I keep station—translucent, vein-webbed, motionless except for the internal tides—singing the stopped clock back into itself with every breath I do not take.

The droplet at the leaf-tip above me is still gathering. It has been gathering since before I condensed. When it falls, the chord will contain that fall, and I will contain the chord, and the forest-city will remain exactly itself for as long as the world allows.
