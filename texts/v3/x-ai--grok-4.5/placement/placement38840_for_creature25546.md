The cold is not a temperature at first. It is a weight arriving from every direction at once, a consensus of pressure that finds the hollows in whatever you still think of as a body and fills them without asking. You hang in it long enough for the distinction between skin and surrounding water to thin, then thinner, until the boundary is only a slight difference in how light bends.

Light itself is sparer here than memory prepared for. It enters from the hard circle overhead in clean vertical shafts that the suspended mineral dust immediately begins to argue with, breaking each shaft into slow lozenges that drift, stack, shear apart. You learn the lozenges before you learn your own outline. They pass through the places where limbs might be, or gills, or the folded geometry of a mantle, and in their passing they map a volume that is denser than the water yet not separate from it.

Something rasps nearby—close enough that the vibration travels bone if bone remains, shell if shell has begun. The sound is wet and patient, the same note repeated with microscopic variations, like a single thought trying to abrade itself into dust. You drift toward it, or the current of countless small bodies drifts you; agency is still undecided. A translucent carapace the size of a thumbnail rotates into view, its grinding plates busy against a fresh basalt face. The plates leave behind a faint white paste. Without decision you find a surface of your own lowering to meet that paste, not to steal it, only to feel the grit move across whatever sensitive plane you now possess. The paste coats you in a thin mineral varnish that immediately begins to harden by fractions. You have been marked as substrate.

Time dilates. Or rather, the old metronome of heartbeat and breath is replaced by the slower cicada-click of plates, the bellows-sigh of siphons, the occasional dull knock of larger bodies rebounding. You discover you can match the rhythm without effort. In, out, rasp, release. The motion organizes you. What had been vague density draws tighter into segmented bands—six, then seven—each band able to telescope a fraction against the next. A soft inner bladder finds its folding pattern and begins the reciprocal work of drawing water, tasting it for silica gradients, expelling it in a measured jet that holds you just above the rubble.

Limbs arrive the way frost arrives on glass: first as speculation, then as necessity, then as undeniable chains of calcified sleeves ending in flat, striated plates. Eight of them. Then more. They know what to do before you do. They settle against stone and begin the long conversion of edge into grit. Heavier grains you reject with a subtle flex of ventral grooves; lighter ones you usher backward into a pouch that did not exist until the first useful particle needed somewhere to go. Mucilage secretes itself, glazing the collected silicates into a thin opalescent wafer. You extrude it. The wafer drifts, tumbling, until another body—larger, slower—nudges it toward a leaning tower already two hand-spans high. You feel no ownership and no loss. The wafer was never yours; it was only the rock continuing its own conversation by way of you.

There are hours, or seasons, in which you remain this small hovering craftsman. Then a change in the dissolved iron sings against your cilia and something in the mantle answers by swelling. The carapace thickens, growth rings darkening with varnish. Limbs fuse, strengthen, become fewer and heavier until each grinding plate is broad as a palm. Now when you settle against a block the size of a skull the sound you make is the low wet knell that travels farther than sight. Smaller forms stream around you like living grit. You do not direct them. You simply open larger faces of stone and they pour their labour into the new brightness. Sometimes you rest atop their precarious towers and add your own thicker tablets, feeling the structure sway and somehow hold, bonded by the same cold chemistry that holds the basalt walls.

Later still—or earlier, chronology having loosened—you thin again. The heavy plates recede. You elongate, scutes overlapping like vanted thrusters, and climb the vertical faces where the beards of mineral filigree grow. Your anterior end flowers into tasting cilia. When the seepage is rich you anchor and spin hair-fine threads of pure precipitate until the rock wears a white drifting veil. Micro-forms will harvest it. You will become the harvest. The cycle does not break; it only changes which part of itself is speaking.

You collide often. Elastic, courtly collisions. Each contact exchanges a little paste, a few embryonic nodules, a temporary silver bubble caught and then released. No scar remains that is not soon polished into iridescence. Aggression cannot find purchase because the rock is endless and the cold is fair and every mouth is also a mason’s hand.

At some point the distinction between the thousands of bodies and the single drowned basin thins to the same thinness as the old boundary of skin. You are the clicking. You are the white plumes of rejected grit. You are the slow lean of towers that will collapse and be rebuilt. You are the gas-vent knell rising toward the hard circle of sky. You are the patience that was once lava and is now heredity.

And still the light keeps entering cleanly, and still the mineral dust argues with it, and still the measured rhythm continues: in, out, grind, sort, glaze, extrude, begin again. You hang among the densities, one more temporary equation of drag and buoyancy and secreted mucilage, perfectly unaware that any of this could be called a machine, or a self, or a world still violently young. The work is the only knowledge required. The cold keeps its promise. The rock, grain by grain, becomes something that might one day remember it was sharp.
