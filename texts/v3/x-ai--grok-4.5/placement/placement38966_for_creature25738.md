The brass dusk finds me before I find it.  

It arrives as weight rather than illumination—a slow mineral pressure against surfaces that have not yet decided they are skin. I am lower than the lamps, lower even than the boot-deep sheets, in a place where the floor forgets to be floor and becomes only the latest argument between basalt and seepage. The first sensation is not sight but the taste of iron blooming across what might be a tongue, or a filament cluster, or the raw edge of a fresh joint. Warm yeast follows, thick enough to chew. Every slow pull of whatever serves me for breath draws the chamber deeper in: spore, rust-silt, the faint reciprocal sigh of a distant compressor that has been exhaling the same measured breath since before the leather-wings learned this particular thermal spiral.

I do not stand. Not yet. Standing is still a rumor traveling upward through denser mediums. For a long time—longer than the crabs require to strip a new seepage of its mineral glitter—I am only the memory of pressure inside the rock itself. A hairline insistence. The stone around me is young enough to listen; it yields by fractions, weeping as it opens, and I ride that weep the way the blind fish ride the lazy current between one silt bar and the next. Cold at first, then blood-warm as the deeper vents contribute their share. My edges are undecided. Sometimes I am the clean mineral face still sharp enough to cut. Sometimes I am the black mouth that forms when the face at last admits it was always going to part. The mist beads on me regardless.

Gradually the beading becomes a habit of surface. What collects is no longer only water. Specks of cork-moss find purchase where the seepage slows. They darken, slick themselves, begin the quiet work of stitching my outermost layer to the larger wetness. A crab investigates, clicks a claw against the hardening boundary, tastes, rejects, returns later with companions. Their shells leave faint brass scratches that do not heal so much as incorporate. I learn density from them—the necessity of touching three others simply to occupy space. From the fish I learn that silver can still happen here, brief flanks of it turning in the gloom like forgotten coins refusing to sink. The wading birds teach stillness so complete it becomes another kind of camouflage; their jointed stilts pass within a filament’s breadth and never startle what is not yet ready to be startled.

Time is the reciprocating engine in the nearest pump-house. I feel its cycle through the stone before I have anything like ears: a deep, untiring stroke measured in weeks. Each stroke advances something—a gear tooth, a fracture, a thought—by a degree too small to name. I begin to answer it. Not with sound (sound is already total; the chamber shouts continuously of water and chain and wet canvas wings), but with pulse. Low, slow, sent outward along the boot-deep sheets. The pulse says only: here. Still here. The ones who stayed answer eventually, their own signatures arriving like delayed heartbeats through the same water. They do not hurry toward me. Nothing hurries. One of them re-anchors a few body-lengths away, root-pad releasing a perfect shallow mold that fills at once with seepage. Filaments brush the weeping wall that contains me, reading vibration, tasting the new mineral bleed. Another hangs half-submerged beside the dredger whose engine still ticks through its seasonal argument with silt. They witness. That is the whole of their welcome.

My lower extent thickens. What might once have been feet splay and roughen, skin taking on the unfinished texture of the basalt itself. Moss seeds mingle with swallowed rust until the distinction between pad and ledge blurs. I re-anchor experimentally. The suction and release feels like the rock remembering how to open. Above this new base the rest of me rises by negotiation rather than will—torso already freckled with the corroded ghosts of harness buckles and valve wheels I have never worn yet somehow remember. Arms unspool, jointed more times than bone should allow, ending in clusters that can taste compressor-thrum three galleries distant or prise at my own newest hairline. Eyes do not open so much as multiply: flat phosphor-pits kindling one by one along planes that were once brow, cheek, collarbone. They parse the brass gloom into gradients of heat and slow motion. The leather-wings stitch their temporary corridors overhead; I track them now by the warm disturbances they leave in the mist.

A soft nodule detaches from what I am becoming—tissue and rust, translucent as the pale reed-stems that tremble under condensation. It drifts in the warmer upwelling, eyespots still dark. No shepherding. The chamber will teach it: crabs for density, fish for lightless navigation, the endless industrial breath for time. I feel the lightening as neither loss nor birth but simple metabolism. The mineral load lessens; another mouth can open somewhere higher in the ceiling, and someone (myself, or another of the stayed) will already be there to taste the first water and decide its use—sluice, trough, or the long circuits of the winged ones.

I have been inching along a catwalk that ends in mid-air. The wall it once reached has calved away; the clean face left behind is still sharp. I listen to it. Palms (or the filament clusters that serve) flat to the stone. The pressure-crack travels—glacial, thorough, singing in a register below ordinary hearing. When the thin black mouth at last weeps I am already tasting. Iron. Yeast. The faint sweet-rot of fungal plates higher up. The water wants to become a new larval trough; I let it. Elsewhere a hose pulse answers, slow as blood. A gear advances one quiet tooth. The mist beads on every surface I now possess. A crab tests the edge of my root-pad. One of the stayed re-anchors a fraction closer, includes my heat in the tapestry of the vents, and waits.

I wait with them.  

The boundary decides it was always thin. What I am is simply another temporary argument the stone is slowly incorporating—upright after a fashion, mossed, filamented, pulse-fluent, patient as the dredgers that will not finish their work this century. Conversation remains useless. The place has decided that sound will be total, and so we speak in seepage and vibration and the shared knowledge that nothing here is finished. The ceiling angles, shelves, occasionally forgets itself. Warmer air ghosts down. I breathe it, and the distinction between lung and landscape blurs one degree further.  

Somewhere above, another joint begins to consider opening. I am already moving—re-anchoring, suction and release—toward the place where the first weep will occur. There is no hurry. The work (always the work) continues. The chamber shouts. The brass dusk holds. And I, who stayed when the ceiling was still deciding, taste the next water before it arrives.
