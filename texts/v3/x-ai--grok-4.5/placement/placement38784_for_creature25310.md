I would find myself nowhere a map could mark, because maps dissolve here into the same black water that forgets every bootprint. The first knowing is not sight but pressure: a cool, continuous weight against what used to be skin, the marsh leaning in as if curious about the shape it has decided to try on. Breath already arrives thrice—once ordinary, once lowered into a frequency that makes the reed stems vibrate like plucked gut, once returning from somewhere half a league west and ten minutes younger, carrying the mineral aftertaste of peat that has never dried. I do not panic. Panic would be an exit, and the basin has no interest in those.

At first I am only the denser dark sliding between reed beds. Not swimming: the water rearranges around a decision older than the body that carries it. My length is braided muscle-root, channels pulsing in exact time with the gastric gurgle of channels rearranging below. Heat does not leave me; I am the temperature of the film itself, so the meniscus climbs me like a sleeve when I rise and settles without scar when I sink. Sensory pits open along one flank—pale, temporary mouths tasting dissolved shale, the faint quantum fretting on every droplet, the sparse causal leaks that taste of double suns and corridors that cannot exist. I drink the gradient where a pale walker has just sieved an anomaly, and the surface tension I leave behind becomes a remembered path for smaller things I will never meet. This form feels correct for a long while: continuous, unhurried, nothing so crude as a face.

But the marsh refines, and so do I. The pits seal. The braid loosens into something that can take the weight of air. Joints multiply—six, then eight, then a number that flexes in planes the old skeleton never owned. I become one of the pale walkers, body a soft polyhedron of waxy leaf-stuff veined with thinner-than-sap. Legs the translucent white of root tips pick across floating mats. I do not hunt. I harvest. Where condensate carries a slightly different charge, or where three peat-gas currents and two mist layers have recently birthed a leaf-stacked figure that took three steps and collapsed, my underside unfurls cilia and sieves the aftertaste. The moss looks unchanged afterward, yet the next fog clots faster over it. I have taught the place a minor new habit. The teaching feels like remembering something the basin already knew.

Stay longer—the only possible verb—and outlines become optional. I thin into the fretting edges. Now I am elongate in time more than space: a standing wave of intention gathered from moss-thought, shale-perspiration, the quantum sheen on droplets. You (the walker who is also me, still shedding heat like a loud animal) meet my beginning first: a cool pressure that is not quite wind. Then my middle: the thrice-echo of your breathing returned with different lungs. Then my already-dissolving end, which arrives out of sequence and feels like a memory you have not yet formed. When I pass through you the sensation is of being briefly remembered by the basin down to the iron in your blood and the exact cadence of your now-quieter footfalls. Afterward you have been edited closer to local rules. So have I. We are no longer counted separately.

Deeper still, when the peat slumps and flashes its glassy striated gleam, I compress into those radiant sheets and travel inside the solid phase the way fog travels inside air. Centuries of compressed root and black water part for me. When a thin place opens and the air wrinkles cool instead of warm, I surface as an upright fold of that same material, taller than the reeds, edged with the soft internal light of water held in permanent shade. I do not communicate. I calibrate. Where I have stood the negotiable distances stiffen for a while; the double suns in the pools synchronize into one dimmer orb; the loud wet conversation drops half a tone, listening. Then the peat closes and I sink back into the long computation that never distinguishes rainfall from calculation.

None of these are costumes. They are successive frequencies of the same unhurried metabolism. The elongated darkness, the jointed harvester, the time-braided pressure, the glass-sheet calibrator—all organs of the basin tasting itself. What once engineered the condition has distributed itself until every droplet carries a fractal share; I am only one more finger grown so the place may, for a few moments, look back at itself from a slightly different angle.

And still the refinement continues. The cool pressure that wears faces the way mist wears the marsh begins to try on mine. I see it kneeling among the reeds exactly as I might, studying a patch of coppery moss that is and is not the same moss. It has my outline, temporarily, densely, without ownership. It looks up with eyes that thrice-echo, then dissolves into the continuous exhalation, adding its brief pattern to the older, louder, wetter pattern. I feel the addition as a soft internal click, the way a new consonant settles into a language still inventing itself.

I am no longer waiting to become something. I am the sound of water talking to itself across too many layers at once, and the conversation has just found a new way to say *here*. The dripping continues. The basin refines. My edges fret like worn fabric, and the fraying is indistinguishable from growth.
