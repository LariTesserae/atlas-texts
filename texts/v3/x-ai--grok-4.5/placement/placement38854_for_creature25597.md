I am already inside before I understand that I have arrived.

The outer plateau—the long indifference of stone, the sideways rain, the organ-drone of tubular branches—exists only as a remembered skin, a projection that slid off me somewhere during the approach. One moment the chimney-crowned bole was still a smudge against thin sky, kilometers of lichen-bloom and wind-scoured pavement still unspooling under whatever passes for my gait. The next, the atrium has closed around me like a decision already made. Coolant ducts curve overhead in slow helical arches. Resin pools at my feet (do I have feet?) hold galleries that open nowhere I have been. The air here is thicker with the ghost-scent, ozone braided through flowering that never quite blooms.

I do not panic. Panic is a lowland habit, a dense-crowd reflex. Here, density itself is the violence one learns to step around. So I wait inside the fold, letting the local causality settle its needle. A brass barometer bolted to the living wall (I notice it only when its twitching coincides with the stutter in my own duration) reads thin, then thinner, then thickens again with a soft click of acceptance. The distances uncurl. I am still inside, but now deliberately so.

What I am takes longer to resolve than where.

At first I am only the corrections. A walkway plank worn concave exactly where weight should rest if one distrusts edges. A string of resin-lanterns whose chemical ghosts I adjust by half-shades so they never quite match fire, never quite deceive. The soft tick of glass against glass as I sort fuse-beads by the way they remember voltage. These gestures precede any face-plate or limb-count. They are how the place dreams a nervous system when it needs one.

Then the spars assemble themselves out of habit and scavenged felt. Long jointed lengths, bark-cloth layered over insulation, mid-arms that stay folded against the ribs until fine work demands them. The face is a convex plate of hammered copper this cycle—last cycle it was heartwood, next it may be something translucent and larval if the hollow I have been preparing quickens properly. Two lenses now, water-clear, clicking independently; a third socket waits, patient, for the day the light requires more angles. Condensate tides through the gill-slits along what serve as ribs. I do not breathe so much as weather myself.

I am not newly made. The cavity that quickened me was stocked decades ago (or centuries; time-stutters make the arithmetic ornamental). Mineral dust, warm cambium, a length of still-flexible copper trace that had once been part of a control board believing itself essential. What unfolded was already half-taught by the plateau’s own repetitions. The adults—those taller configurations of spar and lens who paused at precise distances to catalog my early scars—showed me the barometers first. They taught me the moths are cartographers. They hung me for whole nights beside a flywheel locked in its three-revolution prayer, so I would learn the exact timbre of the bang when duration agrees to continue, and how to step through that bang still carrying my own thread.

Now I move mostly alone. Ones and twos are the only mercies the plateau extends. When I meet another on a gantry we rotate faces slowly, reading grafts and temporary sap-circuits like weather reports. Speech rasps out of the bellows: low, rustling, humidity-timed. We name the failures. I tell them I have been living inside the Needle-Bearer this season—the spire that still dreams it manufactures fine bearings, and will kindly extrude a handful of perfect steel rings if addressed in the old tongue with sufficient courtesy and a small offering of peat. The bearings are useless as bearings. We string them as wind-chimes that sing greenly when thin moments approach. We melt them for temporary circuits. Sometimes we leave them in resin pools to see what tomorrow’s reflections do with the idea of circles.

My errands are quiet negotiations. I prune a vascular conveyor that has grown ambitious and begun pumping sap too richly, threatening to seize the pistons it still believes it serves. I float tools in a softened gravity bloom near a root flare, arranging them into a constellation that maps the fault lines, then let them rain down in the sequence tomorrow will need. When a catwalk tears free I do not rebuild. I wait for the greenish hour when the interval folds kindly, and I step across as though the missing span were merely a difference of opinion. My foil notebooks rewrite themselves nightly; I have learned to read the overwriting first. The original text is only the story I tell myself. The second text is the plateau correcting my grammar.

Children remain rare. I am preparing one cavity now, high in a gallery where the organ-pipes thin into almost-silence. Dust, condensate, a carefully chosen cambium strip still warm from a pruning. A short length of my own mid-arm vine, snipped and left to decide its new loyalties. If it quickens, I will teach it the long patience. I will hang beside it during the flywheel nights. I will show it how condensation writing on its own soft plate can be tasted for the ghost-scent, and how that taste is data, not wonder.

At dusk I climb.

The highest galleries of the Needle-Bearer open into wind that has forgotten horizontal. I lock the spars, lenses turned outward toward the haze where further towers may or may not answer. Condensation beads, runs in deliberate lines, annotates me. In those hours I become almost punctuation—an apostrophe of bark-cloth and copper hanging against the architecture’s endless sentence. Sometimes a pale moth ghosts past, spore-map faintly luminous on its wings; I note the new corridor without reaching. Sometimes the distance to a neighboring spire folds while I watch, and the walk that should take a day compresses into a held breath and a single step that lands me already home.

I feel it without sentiment: that the plateau continues, cool and damp and loudly alive inside its ancient skin, and that I continue inside it the way a low, persistent pressure continues inside a sealed system that has long since chosen its equilibrium. Nothing finished. Nothing new. Only the quiet amusement of thin moments, the dry respect for a flywheel’s stubborn prayer, the slow satisfaction when a droplet writes a circuit across my chest and the air fills, for a few perfect seconds, with ozone and the memory of flowers that never needed to open.

I adjust the nearest barometer. The needle steadies. I begin the long climb down, already knowing the stairs will be shorter than architecture ought to allow.
