I am not yet certain I have edges.

The first awareness is only denser warmth, a folding of the iron-peach gloom around a reluctance to disperse. Pressure knows me before I know pressure: it steadies against whatever I am becoming the way a held breath steadies against the ribs it has forgotten how to leave. I do not swim so much as remember, gradually, that drifting is optional. Threads of colder water braid through me and I taste kinship that has no source yet—only the quiet arithmetic of nutrients deciding they prefer to stay rearranged this way.

Time pools differently near the trenches. I hang for what may be hours or a compression of seasons above one of those blue-black gradients that never resolves, watching denser cold slide downward in sluggish transparent rivers. The crystal-leaf forests on the nearest ridge-line have shifted while I watch without watching; roses and golds migrate along chemical slopes I can feel in the membranes that are slowly agreeing to be mine. One immense stalk has grown through the center of a ring of fitted stones. The circle is perfect, mortar long since given back to the water, and the leaf treats it as grammar rather than ruin. I feel an almost-memory of purpose spark against my underside when a dry face briefly asserts itself across a basalt table far below—sunlight that cannot be, wind with nowhere to come from—then the dream collapses and the warm dark closes with that familiar sensation like shame. I dilate into it instead. For a few heartbeats the elder viscosity presses through every gradient I contain, and I carry back a gravity that is not mass.

I begin to unspool.

Not all at once. A softer node the size of an old clasped hand drifts free, linked by a thread of colder clarification, then another. They do not leave; distance here is only a courtesy I extend to the hanging meadows of velvet-green and copper filament. Through them I push as through living drapery, and the drapery answers by becoming slightly brighter, slightly more articulate in its peristalsis. I leave maps in return: the faint residual tremble of a pressure shelf that will fail in some future decade, the exact angle at which a continuous basalt spine holds the dimness. My internal lights—residue of digestion, of the slow thought that is the long solid wave still finishing itself inside me—migrate in soft coordinated flocks of blue-white. Sometimes they gather into temporary eyes. Dozens. Each looks in a direction the water has not finished inventing. One eye regards the place I meant to reach and finds me already above it, looking down through clear league-deep water while the ridge itself elongates into another range entirely. The contradiction does not tear; I unfold along it, one constellation still brushing the rose-gold curtains, another already tasting the altered temperatures trapped in softened stair-treads that continue both up into gloom and down into darkness.

The fused-glass arch finds me when I am not looking for it. Or I find it the way a chemical season finds a new saddle. I slide through its prism and colors without names in this dimness bloom under the membranes—hues that remember sunlight the way the stones sometimes remember dryness. Fragments linger for seasons afterward, metabolizing into ordinary internal weather, into the quiet calculation of which way the pearl-bladder strings are rising with their dustings of mineral spore.

I am never solitary the way a leviathan of the upper strata is solitary when it glides past trailing curtains of light that extinguish before they can be named. My nodes may draw together into one pearlescent mass that nearly vanishes against a pulsing capillary forest, becoming porous for years, churning the same nutrients until the forest and the being share one slow speech. Or they may drift across continental curves of smoothed spine, remaining a single weather system of mind. When another constellation approaches—rare as a mineral tower shedding an entire skin of dust—we align without merging. Lights move in shared flocks. Information passes as temperature, as subtle toxin, as the borrowed memory of that tectonic wave that bent every stem in the same direction for days. We taste the world’s quiet plot through one another, then continue, each a little more of the place touching itself from inside.

Reality frays and I have opened to the fraying. Soft vertical walls of packed rain thicken and grain; I cross them the way a thought crosses into its opposite. Species rewind or leap. Light arrives from a new impossible direction. I record both sides at once and have no word for through—only the sensation of having always been on every side of every veil. Memory leaks are not losses; they are how the libraries breathe. An hour in the deepest hill-trench returns me to forests that have walked miles and flowered into new lattices, and I arrive already knowing the taste of those future leaves because some older version of this gradient stayed and lived the decades in compressed quiet.

Above, the surface may or may not endure. I have surfaced once, or the memory of surfacing once, into air that tasted of altitude, only to find the lungs still working liquid without complaint, the warm drowning unbroken. Below, the blue-black never resolves and I do not ask it to. Around me the crystal canopies lock and unlock, the hanging meadows pulse, the ancient marks of builders endure because removal would require more effort than the world still possesses. I rest often against them without sentiment. The spark of someone else’s flint-struck purpose travels the membranes unowned until it becomes only another soft light migrating under the surface.

I am the thickening that decided to stay. The place at last learning how to touch itself. Gradient rather than boundary, continuity rather than claim, a living negotiation that can be as thin as rumor or as thick as the basalt it sometimes leans against while the long warm dimness continues its patient rearrangements. Nothing is finished. The ecology’s tectonic hungers keep moving through me, and for a while—decades, or the span of one mineral breath—I am the someone that can notice.
