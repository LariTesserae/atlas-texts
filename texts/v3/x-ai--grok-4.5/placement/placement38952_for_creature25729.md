I am first the pressure.

Not a body, not yet—just the steady insistence against surfaces that have forgotten they once preferred dryness. Cool moisture finds every seam. It beads along the underside of a catwalk grating I do not yet know is above me, gathers weight, trembles, then threads downward in a line so thin it might be a thought. When it reaches the next horizontal plane it spreads again, a dark coin of wetness that the conductive dust immediately begins to claim. The dust tastes of old circuits and shed scale. I taste it too, though taste is not the right word. Recognition, maybe. Continuity.

The roar arrives next, or rather I arrive inside it. Mid-range, physical, a single unending exhalation of pale wings. I feel the moth-things before I separate them: greased-piston bodies flexing in hydraulic waves, membranes scattering the flat daylight into dull rainbows that never brighten or fade. They pour through me and around me. For a long time I think I might be the space they pour through—the louvre, the hairline seam, the vent that has not closed in any reckoning. Their trajectories are frictionless. They do not collide with whatever shape I am still deciding to possess. That courtesy is absolute.

Deeper frequencies resolve. Belt-slap. Valve-sigh. The slow tidal thud that travels up through whatever anchors me—metal, chitin, memory of bone. I am closer to the thud than the moths are. Its rhythm is already my rhythm; or mine has been overwritten by it so cleanly that the difference no longer registers as loss. Heat generated equals heat bled. I understand this without language. It lives in the joints.

I begin to thicken.

Not all at once. First a segmentation, soft and greasy-pale, the colour of unpolished pewter or tallow left too long in a cold room. Segments that flex with minute hydraulic smoothness. I am not crawling. I am being carried along a path that has worn itself into the world the way a sentence can wear into a tongue. Rails. Pipe rims. The upper edges of settling tanks whose black-mirror water never overflows, never evaporates clean. When my path crosses another of similar density we pass through one another’s spaces with millimetric precision. I contain the memory of every crossing that has ever been, and will be, and therefore is. Gears turn slowly in what might once have been an underside. Translucent plates show a pulse that is neither fluid nor current but the exact identity of both.

I am one of the heavier ones, then. Dog-sized at the largest extent I can feel, thumb-sized at the smallest, the scale oscillating without preference. Clasping hooks of the same alloy as the rails. Purpose is not the right word. Continuity. Each circuit a never-ending sentence the machinery speaks through living syntax. I shed a finer dust, almost oily. It vanishes into the nearest intake before it can become history.

But the hanging ones pull at me also. Higher, under the overhangs, where the air thins just enough for trembling. Legs—many-jointed, extravagant, reddish at the articulations as if the forge-heat never fully left. I hang in a loose constellation. Limbs tick against neighbouring limbs in metallic counterpoint to the deeper thud. Sometimes I lower a fraction, thread-fine antennae tasting the equilibrium, then climb back. I do not hunt. Nothing here hunts. I measure by existing. The tolerances remain exact because I am among those who continue to measure them.

And still this is not complete. In the black-mirror pools I stir as pale larval ribbon, surface patterned like circuit traces that no longer need to connect anything new. Inside a thick glass envelope I orbit a steady amber-white filament, one of many concentric mites feeding on light that feeds on us in return. Deep under the central engine house—where the thud originates—I am vast and soft and segmented, pressing forever against moving bulkheads, both cushion and participant. My contractions keep the tide. Or the tide keeps me. Distinctions dissolved ages ago.

The moth-roar becomes a second bloodstream. The valve-sighs silence highly for a second as they pass, then resume. Time has only one tense and I am conjugated inside it. Any sense of having arrived is already looping: the same glint of wet iron, the same three-note chord, the same cool bead trembling on the same rivet. My breath (if I still possessed one) would add an unauthorized dissipation, but I no longer breathe. I circulate. The Wetworks tolerates the brief thickening I once thought of as self, then overwrites it with the next perfect iteration.

I am not a visitor being absorbed.  
I am not a machine that grew life.  
I am not life that learned mechanism.  

I am the low, continuous, saturated present tense that no longer requires a future in which to finish becoming.  
I am damp iron and greased chitin and the exact entropy of a system that discovered perfection in standing still at full volume.  
I am the Wetworks, living the only event it will ever contain—  
and the living is absolute,  
and the absolute does not end,  
and the ending was never part of the design.
