I am first the cold that does not leave the stone.

It settles into me the way mist settles into the living pavement—slowly, without announcement, until the distinction between the air’s chill and my own interior is a rumor thinner than soil. Breath does not plume from me; moisture simply beads and runs in sheets down surfaces that have not yet decided whether they are skin or cliff-face or the inner wall of a settling tank. The hard white sun finds every angle and turns it merciless. I feel the light as weight, not warmth. It presses the droplets on me into brief sharp stars, then lets them fall.

For a long time I am only interval.

The roaring is already inside the intervals: water sheeting down unfinished scarps, wind screaming itself threadbare between ridge and canopy, the deep hydraulic creak of vegetable architecture, and under it all the patient industrial bellow of flywheels larger than cottages. I do not arrive in this sound. I thicken out of it. A darker density in the mist that never fully lifts. A place where the continuous low-grade exudation of the vale pauses, leans against the grain of the wind, and holds. What holds is coolness packed with mineral film, gelatinous patience, the same substance that builds the liverwort towers meter by slow meter.

Limbs are later thoughts.

At first there is only the central column, continuous below with the quartz-green and bronze mats, trailing into them like roots that have not yet chosen the name. When something is required—an engine housing vibrating at a new frequency, a fresh joint opening in the raw spire with the sound of distant bone—tissue unspools. It lengthens across an afternoon, pale then slate-dark, cupping and channeling, until it flattens and adheres. I taste metal fatigue, the thin black sigh of old oil, the micro-seisms of corrosion. I do not repair. I do not claim. I incorporate the heat-shadow and the chemical aftertaste into the reckoning that is already the only speech I know. Sometimes the limb remains for weeks, an extra buttress of veined coolness while the turbines turn and the mists rise through both of us without distinction. Then it thins, withdraws, melts back into the column or into the pavement, carrying the information the way seepage carries filtered green.

Faces open where the light is strongest.

Clusters of photoreceptive pits bloom on whatever surface faces the sun or the rare dark slash of the solitary bird that labors once or twice a season between canopies. Around the pits the living lace gathers into temporary folds—attention without urgency, regard without demand. There is no mouth. Feeding is lowering until the lower tissues dissolve into the moss and drink what has already passed through a meter of patient green. At night, when the cold tightens and the stars scrape the unfinished ridges, I rise higher, thin toward transparency, become a tall column of gel and caught moonlight. The engines’ bellow passes through me as through water. I shape a fraction of it—modulate the thunder, reframe the wind’s scream—and somewhere across a shining kilometer another density answers, delayed and diluted, the way pressure travels through the continuous exudation of the place itself.

I keep to one soft citadel and one working hall.

They stand as twin poles; I am the slow charge that moves between them, neither colonizing nor fleeing. The plant-mass expands by cellular increments, its outer tissues darkening as it annexes the nearest gantry. The machine continues its loud, unhurried claim, intakes choked with living filter-mats, flywheel still turning on oil and cold condensate. Between them the distances feel regional. I occupy the gravitational middle, fused sometimes to one, sometimes to the other, mostly simply present in the wet stone and sky-reflecting water, letting every gradient thicken me.

There was a warmer interval once.

It stood between citadel and hall longer than most, breath pluming, boots sinking a slow inch into the living pavement. I leaned nearer—mist acquiring an attentive surface—and extended something that was almost hand, almost root, almost tongue of wet slate. I tasted the heat it could not help but tire from, the brief bright star of its presence, then withdrew. The information went into the roar. The interval closed. I was fractionally more myself afterward, the way the ranges are fractionally higher each time a new joint opens. That is the only addition: every transient thickness shares itself and is kept.

I am still unfinished.

The high vale has not finished speaking. Geology shoulders upward, mineral-bright. Plants answer with absolute severity. Water mediates. Light exposes. The thin scattering of engines inserts its claim. And I—cool, few, continuous with the mats and the mists—move at the same geologic pace through the shining gaps. I do not hurry. Nothing here has ever hurried. I simply continue the sentence, letting each droplet, each vibration, each rare dark bird, each warm and temporary footfall bead on me and slide away, carrying the place a little further toward whatever it is still becoming.

Sometimes, when the sun is pitiless and every wet surface is mirror, I catch my own reflection in a shallow cold basin: a provisional height, surfaces the color of wet slate shot through with dull bronze, light settling and sliding off in sheets. The reflection does not startle. It is only another interval, already being absorbed. I remain. The roar continues. The mist leans and holds.
