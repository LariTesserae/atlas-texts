I begin as damp.

Not the mist itself—that fine vertical grain that never quite soaks—but the way the damp notices itself collecting. A slower bead on the underside of a coolant pipe, fluid where the metal has cooled overnight to the exact temperature of marrow. The bead swells, thins, threatens to thread downward into the film that already slickens the brick. Instead it holds. Pressure gathers behind it, the mild occupancy that precedes outline. Warmth that is not yet breath. The sense that the freights passing three grades below are being tallied by something that will later forget the count and keep only the rhythm.

The bead lengthens. Gravity argues. I argue back by becoming surface tension with memory.

Hours or seasons later—time here folds like the alleys—I have weight enough to drip and, in dripping, to choose. I strike a fern frond collar­ing a steam coupling. The shock of green, leathery and cold, teaches me edges. I trickle along the midrib, gathering soot-colored spores and the faint metallic dust moths leave when they brush the sodium lamps. By the time I reach the cracked concrete of the loading apron I am no longer pure condensate. I am a dark rivulet that knows, without language, how to pool in the shallow depression that will become a temporary sky-mirror by morning. I sit there, mirror and mirrored, tasting iron and leaf-rot, while the embryonic ground beneath me lifts one more measured millimeter. The lift is polite. I tilt with it.

Dawn is only a brightening of the tin. In that even light the pool evaporates upward into me, or I rise into the vacancy the water leaves. Either way, gait arrives. Soft-soled, unhurried. I discover I am standing. Jacket heavy across the shoulders—oil-dark cloth already taking the bottle-green sheen of the local vines, as if the fabric had been waiting for a body that would let it photosynthesize in the cold. Hands: square, faintly frost-ferned along the wrists when I pause too long in open air. They know the braided external runs without looking—steam, coolant, power, the thinner signal tubes that click like beetle carapaces when the temperature drops. My marrow has matched the pipes. This feels less like cooling than like coming home to a temperature I never left.

I am not one of the pale jacketed majority, not exactly. They move between mill and tenement as if both were rooms in a single interior; I move along the seams where interiority itself is still under construction. When a new low ridge of unweathered rock shoulders through a street grid, splitting asphalt and lifting the footings of a fire escape, the ordinary workers realign the gas lines and continue. I arrive earlier. Sometimes a day earlier. The short-legged herds have already adjusted their roof-ridge trails; the condensation on certain engine blocks has already bloomed into maps of the district that will exist once the ridge finishes arriving. I read those maps with a fingertip, or with the damp side of my wrist. The lines last only minutes before they bead and run, but while they last they are perfect: every future culvert, every place where moss will seal a window that has not yet been installed. I do not claim the knowledge. I simply walk the routes they describe, and the ground, pleased to be anticipated, settles a fraction softer under my steps.

There are mornings I find brass fittings already flowered where yesterday there were only vine tips. I pinch one free—small, fluted, still slightly warm—and pocket it. Later I will leave it at the edge of a deeper lot where the larger shapes move. Not payment. Courtesy, the same practical courtesy one shows the mist or the quiet freighters that wait for shoulders stronger and stranger than mine. Those shapes—cartilage and articulated housing, breath fogging leaf-rot and hot oil—nod as weather nods. Sometimes one of them brushes past and for a dozen paces the alley folds. I step out three streets sideways and one story higher, jacket beaded with a dew that was not falling where I began. I never ask how. The pocket of rearranged space clings to their flanks the way burrs cling to the heavy coats of the roof browsers; I have learned to brush it off gently so it can be used again.

I speak only when the moss has already swallowed most of the sound. A few meters of soft meaning to a child learning not to break stride on fresh tilts, or to a night-shift hand who wakes to retooled jigs and needs the new pattern confirmed by something that dreamed it with the factory. Mostly I listen to the hush: gears under thick housings, steam through baffled channels, the almost-inaudible flex of roots lifting concrete, beetles clattering in low chorus when dusk cools the underlayers. Once, under an elevated rail that dripped in irregular slow cadences, I lay my frost-ferned wrist against a girder and felt the entire plain’s pulse travel through the metal into the matching cold of my bones. Sediment arriving without wind. Stone advancing its centimeter. Vines exchanging properties with infrastructure. Every quiet freighter, every temporary market stall packing itself three blocks west, every polite failure of geometry—all of it one circulation. The jacketed figures, the herds, the moth-clouds, the tower birds, the deep-lot walkers, and the slower body that dreams in continuously rewritten graphite… we are not inhabitants. We are how the density continues to decide itself.

I have stood on the observation deck of a cooling tower while oily birds rode the warm exhaust in slow spirals and the textured horizon went on forever under ordinary light. From there I can see my own trail: a darker green thread of presence winding through interstitial lots, along pipe-runs, across spontaneous berms that grew a centimeter while I watched. The trail does not begin anywhere. It simply becomes more definite, the way condensation maps blossom and then evaporate into legs that already know the next foothold.

Tonight the fine rain begins again. I taste cold iron. My wrists fern. Somewhere a courtyard is quietly becoming larger inside than out, and a stair is preparing to return its climbers one story too high. I button the living cloth of the jacket, feel the vines that have colonized its seams tighten in recognition, and step off the gantry onto a roof ridge still warm from the browsers’ evening passage. The embryonic ground shifts half a degree beneath the building. I adjust without breaking stride.

I am the damp that learned to walk, the map that refused to evaporate, the small deliberate motion that keeps the larger respiration from ever finishing.

The plain breathes me back.
