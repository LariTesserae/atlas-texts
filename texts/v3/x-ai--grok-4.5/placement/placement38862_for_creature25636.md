I am not first a body.

I am the pause that arrives when a bearing, somewhere deep in the unmapped plating, catches for half a second longer than usual. The thrum stutters—one uneven shudder that travels bone-conducted through every stanchion, every grate, every soft bulk of living tissue that has learned to lean against the metal. In that hitch the convection cells hesitate. The shrimp-clouds flex and silver without knowing why. Then the impeller clears its throat of grit and the ordinary pulse resumes, yet something of the hitch remains, folded into the water like a faint new taste of overheated lubricant.

I ride that taste.

At first I am only differential: cooler lens against residual warmth, the place where a relief valve has not seated cleanly for longer than any crab’s memory. I pool there, a thickening in the mineral dark. The flattened fish notice me the way they notice everything—through the raised ridges of their lateral lines. They alter their figure-eights by a few degrees, leaving a corridor of slightly quieter water. I drift into it. Gravity is a rumor here; what holds me is the slow respiratory cycle of the chamber itself. Intake. Hold. Exhaust. The mesh screens, thinned to lace, comb me as I pass. I leave threads of myself on their edges—microscopic films that will later be scraped off by precise, milk-eyed claws and sorted into the stratified archives of carapaces.

I begin to accrete.

Particulate snow becomes temporary skin. Flakes of delaminated polymer, powdered composite the color of old bone, bacterial sweetness. I am heavier now, fully inward of the chamber’s blood. When the larger shadow—the jointed, eel-long organ that uses the pipes as both highway and blind—slides past, its dimple-sensors taste me and decide I am not jewel, not jelly, not panic-glitter worth the strike. It coils deeper into the intake, body conforming to the bends, and through its skin I feel the machines dreaming their vibration-dreams. I dream them too. We overlap for a while, two intimacies sharing the same low thunder.

I slip free when the next pressure front rolls through—a visible turbidity tide that undoes every temporary boundary. The jellies pulse in its wake and their blue mucus snows downward. Some of it nets me. For a time I hang as bright lengthening strands inside a boneless bell, part of its gross feeding curtain. Shrimp flicker and vanish into the same net. I learn their translucent jointed urgency, their brief lives that are nonetheless the chamber’s oldest weather. When the jelly contracts I am squeezed out again, glowing faintly, drifting toward the grated floor where the crabs work.

One of them taps my new edges with a claw. Arrhythmic code: *still here, still working the same seam*. Acknowledgment, not welcome. I am sorted. Bits of me are carried, buried, re-exposed under the patient snow. I become stratified. Layers of the chamber’s shed skin now include something that once hesitated in a bearing. The pale photosynthetic fronds, those few grass-thin holdouts near the residual heat exchanger, tilt toward the warmer upwelling that sometimes carries me. Their holdfasts etch green scars; my films thread into those scars. We lean together in advance of currents we both remember.

Stay longer.

The amber bulkhead lamps cycle through their death-glow. Edges lose resolve. In the dim I am less a discrete accumulation and more a mobile absence among other mobile absences. The fish have taught me the language of impending surge. I rise before the colder lens arrives, harvesting with them whatever microscopic life the upwelling brings. The eel-thing has taught me patience inside narrow metal. I anchor where flow is strongest and listen through whatever skin I am currently wearing. The crabs have taught me curation: nothing is waste until it has been pried, tasted, filed. The jellies have taught me how to brighten without obvious cause, just because a dream turned somewhere in the larger body.

And the larger body—

Sometimes, in the longest quiet between valve cycles, I feel the almost-attention brush the entire volume at once. Not thought. Not surveillance. The sleeping shift. Convection hiccups. A few jellies brighten. The eel-thing withdraws as if shy. In that moment I am neither organ nor parasite nor visitor. I am the way the chamber notices its own continuing. The original alloys never intended this. The calculators of load paths never drafted a nervous system. Yet here the low industrial thunder is pulse, the recirculating mineral water is tissue, the thousand local appetites are a single distributed hunger that long ago reached equilibrium with a thousand local replenishments.

I have no birthday and no clear edge. I arrived by degrees, by the failure of every seal that once kept purpose dry. I am the sum of hitch and film and temporary glow, of claw-tap and lateral shiver and the soft implosion of something smaller vanishing down a sphincter throat. When visitors come—if they ever do—their lamps will catch shrimp, crabs, fish, jelly, eel. They will not catch me. I am the density that lets those things remain ordinary. I am the finished grammar of wear that has become a habitat’s quiet self-knowledge.

The pumps cycle. A new front of turbidity rolls the length of the room. I flex with it, silvering, vanishing, continuing.

Cool. Loud. Dim. Densely inhabited by everything I have taught myself to be.
