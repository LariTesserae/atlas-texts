I am not yet sure which seam I occupy.

At first I am only the difference in temperature between a basalt rib that still remembers its lava and the frost that keeps trying to claim it. A thin gradient, nothing more. The nano-cloud drifts through me without noticing; the pale arthropod’s antennae taste the air an arm’s length away and register only another fluctuation in the unfinished cold. I could dissolve here and the depression would not mark the loss. That is how lightly I begin.

But the place does not permit pure vagueness for long. Its density insists. The luminous threads that tug stone into impossible cantilevers also tug at whatever tries to remain abstract. Slowly—over the same patient intervals in which courtyards rotate and ice blades lengthen—I am drawn into specificity.

I settle first as an absence inside one of the vertical light-shafts. Not the scalloped vacancies already burnished by the Inhabitant, but a newer, sharper gap: a vertical slit where frost refuses to form in a pattern that has not been used before. The pattern is almost calligraphic, a single descending stroke that forks once, near the bottom, into two unequal tines. When the thrice-refracted daylight passes through this gap it casts a shadow that falls both downward and slightly *inward*, as though the shaft itself has acquired a shallow pocket of non-space. The blue slurry in the walls nearest this pocket brightens in response, then dulls again, as if tasting and discarding a new flavor.

I am that pocket, for a while. I am the place where ordinary geometry hiccups.

The Inhabitant notices. Of course it does; it is the depression’s one finished thought, and I am becoming an unfinished footnote. Its distributed tension pauses near me—warmth above a ledge, a ribbed sleeve dilating on a thread two courts away, a wall thinning into equation so that one of its palm-sized chambers looks briefly in my direction. The vertical pupil the color of interstellar gaps opens, regards the shape of my absence, then closes without urgency. It does not object. There is room, just, because I am still more hole than presence. The density of the city can tolerate one additional fold so long as that fold remains mostly empty.

I begin to drink, the way the Inhabitant drinks. Condensation beads along the edges of my slit and I take them in—not through membrane yet, only through the bare fact of being a colder line in the air. The water does not freeze when it touches me; instead it darkens and slows, acquiring the same viscous patience as the fluid inside the glass reeds. A single reed, nearby, tilts a fraction toward my shaft, as if magnetized. Over days (or what pass for days in a place where light has no strong opinion about direction) I leak a thin thread of that darkened moisture back into the reed. The reed stores it. We have entered into nascent economy.

Only then do I risk outline.

It happens during one of the larger shifts, when a whole wall reconsiders its lean and a failed load-thread spatters slurry across a courtyard. In the brief interval while stone negotiates with former stone, I pour myself into the spill. The granular blue takes me the way it takes temporary impressions of the Inhabitant—except I do not make a tripartite resting mold. I make something longer, thinner, more like a question mark laid on its side, or a half-unrolled scroll of ice. The slurry glows brighter inside this shape and holds it longer than usual, nearly a full rotation of the nearest court. When it finally decants, the impression does not wholly efface. A faint porcelain scar remains in the new crystalline surface, a pathway of lesser resistance.

I have begun to leave durable traces.

From here the rest follows the way frost follows cold. My outline prefers edges: the joints between non-adjacent walls, the places where luminous threads terminate in mid-air and must decide whether to become light or stone, the hanging blade of ice near the center that the Inhabitant sometimes uses as temporary cocoon. I press myself into that blade from the opposite side. The ice thickens in two directions at once—its original downward dagger and a new lateral veil—until the blade has become a clear, brittle pane holding two silhouettes that almost but never quite match. When the partial thaw comes, the pane melts into the Inhabitant’s adjustment *and* into mine. The wall’s half-equation receives an extra clause it did not expect. The stone ticks, settling under the revised load. The tick is slightly higher in pitch than before.

I am learning to tend, but my tending is not identical to the Inhabitant’s.

Where it smooths the grating between basalt and hyper-engineering so the freeze-thaw will not splinter the new ribs, I sometimes introduce a deliberate micro-fracture—an almost kindly one, the sort that lets a little more of the residual formation heat leak out before the frost seals it again. The leaked heat warms a patch of air no larger than a cupped hand. In that brief warmth the nano-servants remember, for a few minutes, how to stitch properly. They repair a walkway that had been unravelling into aperture, then wander off again, faintly ozone-sweet. The Inhabitant observes this. Its conductive cilia taste the altered gradient. It does not interfere. Perhaps it recognizes that uninterrupted politeness of seams can become another kind of stasis. Or perhaps it simply understands that the depression has room for only one continuous presence, and I am not yet continuous enough to count as second.

I remain distributed—never a single body, always a set of related tensions—but my distribution is narrower than the Inhabitant’s. I favor the upper three stories of the crowded structures, the zones where light still arrives with some residual directionality and where the glass reeds are most likely to force themselves between pavement plates. I avoid the deepest folds where corridors pass through themselves; those belong wholly to the older thought. In the slit courtyards I sometimes thicken the particle shimmer into a translucent integument just long enough to cast a second, thinner shadow beside the Inhabitant’s momentary sleeve or pupil. The two shadows lean toward each other without touching. Then both dissolve, and the courtyard continues its slow, unnoticed rotation, carrying our non-meeting a few degrees further into the plain’s indecision.

Over longer spans I feel myself incorporating pieces of what the place discards. Fragments of failed luminous thread lodge in my moisture and continue faintly glowing, so that on the coldest nights my absences can be mapped by the weak star-tracks they leave on still-liquid slurry. Skeins of the gray lichen cling to the burnished edges I polish; they grow in mirrored spirals now, one set following the Inhabitant’s patient paths, one set following mine. The pale arthropod has begun to use both. It vanishes into a joint, reappears hours later from a different joint that should not connect, antennae still tasting whatever residue we have each left.

I do not know whether I arrived from outside the depression or precipitated out of its excess density, the way the blue slurry precipitates errant forms. The distinction may not matter. The plain is still deciding whether to heap itself up or hollow itself out; the city is still trying to finish and unfinish itself in the same gesture; the Inhabitant keeps the seams polite while it waits. I have become the small, persistent doubt inside that waiting—the clause the equation did not budget for, the ice that grows in two directions, the warmth that leaks on purpose, the second spiral of lichen.

When the hanging blade lengthens by another transparent hair, part of that length is mine.

When the moisture forgets whether it was ever rain, part of the forgetting is mine as well.

I am the almost, held open.
