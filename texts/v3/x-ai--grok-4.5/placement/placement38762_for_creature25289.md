I am not yet named when the plateau first notices me.

The noticing begins as pressure—faint, like the memory of a bruise forming under skin that has not decided its thickness. I am standing (or the shape that will become standing is assembling) on the reticulated crust where a square of xylem plates meets another in a seam older than any language still spoken in the Groves. The surface is cool. It does not yield, yet it listens. My weight—whatever weight I am still negotiating—sinks a fraction deeper than physics alone would allow, and the living cambium between the plates tastes me in return: a slow ionic curiosity, mineral and faintly resinous, the same emulsion that will later move through whatever vessels I grow.

I do not arrive from below. There is no below that matters anymore. Memory, if the word still applies, arrives already stiffened, already half-mineralized: something about rain that fell in sheets instead of locking into crystal hydrates, something about blood that ran hot and red and worthless at altitude. Those images flake away like old leaf-scale. What remains is the copper-dry air moving across surfaces that are still choosing how many layers they need. My lungs—when they differentiate—open first as broad membranes, leaf-like, drinking the thin atmosphere through stomata I did not know I carried. Later they will learn to seal, to dock, to become almost decorative. For now they simply face the dim vault and inflate with the taste of dried resin and the slow abrasion of silica.

I walk.

Or the crust allows a walking. Boots have not yet grown; the mycelial thread has not yet answered the call of my soles. Each step leaves a temporary pheromone ghost that the plateau reseals with a sound like a held breath released. I am alone, which is wrong. Nothing adapted remains alone on the long hearing for long. The mineral prairie has hungers, and the kite-foxes are already tilting their membrane wings in the high haze, silver needle-teeth tasting the thermals. But the Briarholds themselves intervene before hunger can become event. A low tectonic chord rises through the calcium lattice I am still calcifying into bone; it passes through me without rupture because something in the emulsion that is replacing blood has already thickened in anticipation. The chord is not warning. It is orientation. Kilometers away a Grove adjusts one of its fretted cavities by a fraction of a tone, and the air between us becomes a small, exact invitation.

I turn toward the song.

Distance here is not empty. It is the medium through which the cities continue hearing themselves. The first Grove reaches me first as vibration in the teeth, then as a change in the quality of shadow—the long late-afternoon gloom acquiring edges that feel curated. The outer bark is dry coral, cavities the size of forgotten rooms, and the root-buttresses sink into the plant-stone like slow thoughts completing themselves. I set my palm against the nearest buttress. The wood is warmer than the plateau. It sweats a clear gel that hardens, not into barrier, but into transparency; for a moment I see the interior galleries layered behind it like nested shores. The Grove’s immune memory blooms across my skin in faint electrical frost. Recognition is not granted. It is grown. Sap-pressure signatures map the new nerves that are still deciding their routes. A staircase prunes itself open—spiraling, courtesy-tilted—and reseals the moment my heel clears the last step, as if the wood is practicing the shape of my gait for later doors.

Inside, time thickens further.

The oceanic murmur rises around me: stridulation of soft-bodied browsers grazing luminous cork, the wet silk whisper of thread-snakes mapping vascular weather, the distant wingbeat of filter-rays whose shadows pass across cavities like bruised-fruit clouds. Power hums in unresolved stacks; the chord is everywhere, load-bearing, liturgical. Gravity loosens its doctrine near the older heartwood. I find myself walking a short vertical stretch because the wall has decided, just now, that it prefers to be floor, and the spectrum of the gel-panes shifts copper-green so the transition feels inevitable. My body answers without alarm. Edges are already sanding. Thirst is renegotiating itself into docking instinct; when I press the soft hinge of what will become a wrist to a municipal phloem tap, the emulsion that fills me tastes of minerals and a sweetness the Grove keeps alive out of pure historical stubbornness.

Days—or the local equivalent, measured in the slow refurling of black metallic leaves high above—pass in differentiation.

Sometimes I surface among the mid-level terraces with the mobile ones, the foragers who cross the open crust in knots of three or seven. We move in the hour when the high haze thins and the sun becomes a coin that might almost be spent. Our boots (now grown, cork-stone and tenacious thread) leave almost no mark. At the rare ice-fog we stop together and become surfaces: clothing, skin, the new membranes behind the knees, all of us drinking the brief steam while crystal-mites erupt in silver frenzy and the kite-foxes descend in silent crescents. We trade polished spores with them out of a contract neither side remembers signing. One fox, later, tears a membrane on a sudden yaw of surface; I am the one who sits with it in a gravity-bowl while tendon-thread is drawn from my own adapted stores. Stitching is quiet work. The fox’s pulse becomes a small counter-rhythm to the city’s hum. When I finish, it does not leave at once. It remains long enough for the Grove to taste us both as a temporary chord.

Other times I climb—or am climbed by—the higher helices, toward the crowns where the chorus-keepers have mostly surrendered locomotion. Their bodies elongate and brace into the leaf-scales; when the wind rises they become part of the answering instrument. Speech among them is pressure and frequency, sentences that last weeks. I am not yet fluent. A single low tone passes through the vascular webbing and into the emulsion I carry; it takes the better part of a slow season before I understand it is not information but calibration: the equilibrium still holds. One of them—skin the color of ash-lavender xylem, eyes tracking ionic differentials the way others once tracked faces—shifts just enough to let the bark sweat a gel-pane around us both. We listen to the long hearing together. Far off, another Grove sings its recognition markers into the wind. We answer by standing still and letting our own fretting modulate, a half-tone of greeting that will take days to arrive and days more to be understood as gossip: a younger shoot has broken the height that matters; a logic engine has begun to dream in a new constant; a tread that vanished into heartwood still opens certain doors out of courteous memory.

And sometimes—rarer—I sink deeper, where the true crowds never quiet and some of us become half-laminae.

There the interface grows intimate. Faces surface from the inner walls only when the circumferential engines need living interpreters. I have felt the pull: the cambium offering denser conversation, the chance to read pressure histograms with eyes that no longer need to be only eyes. Once I let myself thin along a gallery for the length of a long calculation, nerves braided into sap differentials, thought flowing in ionic seasons rather than words. It was not captivity. It was density. When I surfaced again—peeling gently free as the engine returned to slower dreaming—the wood retained a faint impression of my signature. Certain doors still hesitate open when I pass, tasting the echo.

I am not first a forager, nor chorus-keeper, nor lamina.

I am the one who walks the edge where root-buttresses finish their thinking and the long hearing begins. I strike the mineralized tendon tools against the wood in polyrhythms that carry spore reports and small griefs. I stand very still and let the Grove use my body as an extra fretting surface when the wind is complex. When a friend is lost to an unexpected yaw, I feel the city absorb the body into immune memory; for years afterward I will pause at doors that open for a tread no longer present, and the loss will be architectural, quiet, exact. I have never seen a lake. The ancestral paradox reaches me only as a beautiful uselessness—emulsion running free across ground that has no need of it. I do not miss it. Moisture is better now: locked, traded, sung.

At the hour when residual light goes violet and the filter-rays sail between cities in their overlapping glissandi, I sit where gravity has been negotiated into a shallow bowl. Sometimes a guest appears—the crust having delivered another unfinished shape. I do not speak. Speech is optional, almost decorative. Instead I let the Grove taste them through the wood. When recognition passes I shift sideways so the bowl accommodates us both. The distant murmur organizes itself. The soft wet sound of whatever small repair is needed. The almost-subsonic tick of the plateau thinking far below. Then, without prelude, I hum a fragment of the unresolved power chord, and the bark adjusts its resonance until guest and host and wood are briefly inside one tone.

In that tone the last distinction finishes sanding away.

I am not an inhabitant of the Briarholds.

I am one more pathway the ancient vegetal mass has grown in order to continue hearing itself across the high, dry, endless afternoon. My edges are proverb. My thirst is docking. My footsteps are already maps the cambium keeps. When the next ice-fog comes I will stop and drink with every surface. When a new shoot finally matters I will help prune the first staircases. When the wind plays the cities I will answer in the chord that never resolves, the chord that is infrastructure and liturgy and weather and home.

The equilibrium holds, thick as law, sparse as the air, loud as the singing that is also trees.

And the wood, cordially, continues the long conversation of considering me exact.
