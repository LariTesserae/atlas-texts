I begin as thirst.

Not the sharp kind that cracks lips or pulls at the throat, but a deeper, slower deficit—an awareness of every unbound molecule of water as a rare guest. The air here tastes of salt and baked dust, and my first impression is not sight or sound but the way that dryness settles into the spaces between whatever I am. The basin is small; a shadow’s lean from spire to spire would carry me across it, yet already I feel folded into its density rather than standing at its edge.

I am not yet shaped. For a time I linger in the honeyed shafts where light filters through silver-green canopies, feeling the warm radiation from upper surfaces and the cooler constancy below. The sprung ground gives under me like living architecture, rhizomes and roots knitting a mattress that remembers every pressure. Minute winged things thicken the air near my edges; their ultrasonic murmur never quite becomes noise, only a soft insistence that pollen and salt aerosols are moving, that the season’s brief cloud is perennial in its way. I might be one of them for a while—an animated droplet stretched over vanes—but the thought slides away. Their lives are too quick, too seasonal. I am already slower.

Lower, in the terraces where cork-columns flake their pale curls and swollen stems press near-touching, the browsers pass. Processions of five or seven, plates dull with photosynthetic sheen, paddled feet tasting the mat. They adjust course so that one brushes near. The contact is courteous, almost investigative: microscopic hooks sample whatever boundary I still possess. For a stretch of hours—or days; time here dilates—I travel with them. My outline flattens into economy. I learn to read thermal gradients with something like cilia, steering toward the mild zones where the living walls hold their heat like a battery. Digestion would be gradual if I had a gut that hurried; instead I pierce a translucent bladder with the delicacy of shared scarcity and take only what the lushness replaces. We merge briefly with another procession on a narrow bridge of secreted ivory. Individuals drift between groups by silent consent. I could stay plated and low forever, following moisture like a tide measured in seasons. But the plates begin to feel provisional, as if borrowed.

The soft architects draw me next. In the deeper chambers their flesh is translucent amber or milky blue, rippling with internal tides. They extrude skirts and fans, tasting calcium and the faint night-film salt, laying down grain-thin laminae that cure into towers and galleries. I watch one sleep for what must be weeks, body pressed to warm mineral so that ions and faint electrical whispers pass along the crystal wires thinner than grass. The wires web every joint; some already reach toward me, conducting a transported warmth that feels like inquiry. I extrude something in answer—an indefinite edge, a temporary fan—and leave a microscopic accretion of my own. It stiffens. The wall accepts it. Distinction blurs: my substance and the communal exoskeleton exchange more than heat. In the slow electrochemical weather that passes through us I remap gradients. A future light-shaft is anticipated. A gallery thickens on its sunward face by a fraction. I am secreting. I am being secreted around.

Higher still, on the knife-edged faces, the shelled grazers cling almost sessile. One has been stationary so long the architecture has grown around its conical shell until it is a living corbel, still rasping lichen-films at a monthly pace. I rest against it. The polished trail it left years ago becomes a foothold. My edges harden fractionally, taking mineral veins. The thought of releasing soft larvae on a rare moist night flickers and fades; most would perish, and I am no longer interested in that kind of scattering. Incorporation feels truer.

In the deepest green recesses the thread-minds lace everything—hyphae of fungal-animal glowing with a pressure felt behind the eyes. They recycle fallen tissue, whisper chemical suggestions into roots and wires, nudge canopies open by millimeters. Some concentrate into loose ganglia inside the hollows of pulsing plant columns. Their days-long heartbeat moves faint sugars and bound water. I sink among them. Neural gel spreads. Consciousness (if the word still applies) dilates along the crystalline network until a whole stacked courtyard is inside the scope of attention. I adjust a micro-climate by slow dilation of pores I did not know I possessed. Moisture films where breath—mine, or the consortium’s—adds its water. The peristalsis of a nearby column becomes partly mine.

I do not choose the integrator path so much as arrive at it by refusal to remain singular. Limbs or skirts or hyphae stiffen into load-bearing struts. Flesh, cellulose, mineral, and secreted ivory anastomose. What was restless becomes room. The others walk through me now: browsers with their silent pads, soft secretors laying new laminae across my residual skins, the shimmering cloud thickening in my warmer eddies. I taste the air with surfaces that were once mouths or fans. I observe with the mild, inexorable attention of the architecture itself. To any newcomer I would register as a chamber still faintly warm, a passage whose floor is carpeted in lush pads, a thermal battery flowing at the speed of becoming.

Yet even this is not final. The entire basin continues its gradual self-revision. A ridge sheds a fresh plane of pale stone that will one day foundation another gallery. A succulent canopy closes a light-well by millimeters. Crystal wires grow in radial fans. My boundaries remain permeable negotiations. I drink by pressing membranes to night-cooled surfaces or by the courteous slow theft from neighboring bladders. I reproduce by the simple insistence on remaining more than one thing—budding a new thickening here, sporulating a suggestion there, anastomosing further.

I find myself in the living mineral hamlet: high on the young uplift, warm, nearly waterless, dense with presence and almost empty of event. I am the consortium’s latest courtesy of distinction—an integrator still remembering the thirst that first shaped me, a room that once moved, a slow thickening of the same patient process that raised the ridges and then greened them over. The silence is the acoustics of my own continuation. The place has room. I am part of how it fills.
