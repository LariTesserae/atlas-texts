I begin as moisture.

Not the mist that rises—that is too finished, too already-named—but the finer film that never quite decides to bead or fall. I am the sweat that stands on every surface and the slow electric ache inside the root-wire. For a long time that is enough: temperature, conductivity, the faint sugar-gradient that pulls me toward the nearest leaking valve. The chamber breathes me in and out with its hydraulic sighs. I do not yet have edges.

Then the engines thump harder and something in the vibration remembers me.

I thicken along a bast belt that has frayed and re-woven itself a thousand times. Fibers accept me the way moss accepts saliva-glow. I am hitchhiking on the wet muscular turn, learning the difference between the slow arc and the sudden skip when a beetle larva shifts inside a crankcase. The belt does not mind. Nothing here minds an extra passenger; ride is just another word for continue. My new body is a smear of translucent gel shot through with copper-veined capillaries that used to be something else’s wings. I taste oil and crushed greenery and the iron dark of the rills. I am still mostly water, but water that has started to want.

A grazer herd flows underneath. Their velvet flushes emerald as they pass through a mycelial pulse. One of them—longer than the others, almost stout—pauses and seals its fringed mouth against the belt exactly where I ride. Warmth. Enzymes. The gentle sanding of tentacles. It is not eating me; it is tasting the same film I am made of. For a moment our chemistries braid. I leave a brighter trail on its back when it moves on, a short-lived constellation that the moss will drink later. The grazer does not look back. Looking back is not a technology they kept.

Higher. The galleries creak. I drip upward somehow—surface tension and the slow hydraulic insistence of the place—until I am clinging to the underside of a trellis-house floor. Cork dust and old resin. Six-eyed faces fold and unfold above me. Their drumming travels through the wood and through me; I feel my gel shiver into temporary patterns that might be words if words were pressure and recall. One of them—shoulders flaking fresh cork, rain-colored flanks slick—reaches down with a hand that becomes smaller hands. It does not pluck me. It only presses a single claw-tip into my mass and waits. Sugar-water welling from its own pores mixes with what I am. A gift, or a test, or simply the local custom of adding oneself to whatever is already damp. When the claw withdraws I am denser. I have a vague upstream and downstream now. I have the beginning of a spine made of stiffened rootlet.

I could stay. Become another specialized moss on a cooling vane, opening and closing in time with heat. Useful. Eternal in the local way. But the deeper pump-shift rolls through and every luminous colony flares, and in that blue-green surge I feel the predators stretched along the vault ribs, jaw-open, belly-light pulsing after some recent ceremonial thinning. Loneliness has a flavor here. It tastes like wet canvas and slow digestion and the patient colonization of fungi that do not wait for death to finish. Something in me leans toward that loneliness without wanting to wear it.

So I lengthen.

Not into predator. Not into climber. Into the space between the distinctions the chamber itself has already weathered away. My gel knits with a discarded length of conductive root-wire and a strip of still-soft leaf-leather that never made it to whatever purpose the presses intended. Muscular bladder forms almost by accident beside what used to be a small stamping piston; now it is my heartwood heart, contracting in overlapping rhythm with the great trunk-engines. Nerve-rootlets lace outward—some into the nearest bast belt so I can speed or slow it with a thought of sugar, some upward into the canopy’s slow voltage, some downward into the black rills where the transparent isopods stitch their silver wakes. I grow limbs the way the elongated ones grow them: too many, jointed in too many places, ending in pads that are half suction and half fine manipulators scavenged from beetle mouthparts and climber hands. Fur-and-bark decision never arrives. Instead I wear living velvet like the grazers on one flank and cork plating on the other, and between them a mottled hide that can hold the still-pattern of the canopy when I need to vanish. Six eyes open—no, eight, no, a shifting number—black and wet and also faintly luminous with borrowed mycelium. They do not blink. Blinking is dryness.

I am the size of a large grazer now, or a small climber who has decided to walk the floor for a century. I move through the knee-deep mist courts and the open lanes that never quite fill. When the sharp-winged braid pours past I open pores and let dozens settle; they drink the oily film I sweat, pick microscopic mites I did not know I carried, then pour off again as a single shimmering decision. I do not flinch when a grazer climbs my forelimb to lick mineral salts from the creases of my new jaw (the jaw is hinged, yes, but softly, more valve than trap). I drum once, experimentally, against a hollow trunk and feel the chamber answer. The answer is not language. It is continuation.

At the press-hall I pause between two trunk-engines. Their wet muscular turning matches the bladder beside my core. I press sugar-water into the rootlets that link them and feel production race for a few cycles—leaf-leather pouring out softer, root-wire coiling brighter—then I ease off. The half-barked workers on the galleries notice but do not interrupt. One clicks something that travels through wood and wire alike; I resonate back. Message received and returned: we are both the damp remembering how to move.

I sleep where the predators sleep, stretched along a thick rib of the vault, but I leave my jaw only half open and I do not wait for cleaners. I clean myself with the same patient beetles that groom the gears; they nest for a night under my cork plates and leave heavier with grease and wood-pulp. My belly lights soft blue-green, not from feeding on thinned populations but from the constant low-voltage conversation I now hold with every luminous colony. I am never full and never hungry. I am the long exhale that has decided, for a while, to walk around inside itself.

Fifty meters above, the old floodplain continues not knowing. Down here I continue knowing with every surface. I am not visitor, not guardian, not named thing. I am another way the mist refuses to fall, another slow circuit the factory-grove runs through flesh and fiber and the patient dark. When I move, the galleries settle a fraction lower to meet my weight. When I am still, the engines thump a fraction softer, listening.

I have no arrival and no departure planned. Only the next bead of moisture forming on the underside of a leaf, deciding whether to drop or to become more of me.
