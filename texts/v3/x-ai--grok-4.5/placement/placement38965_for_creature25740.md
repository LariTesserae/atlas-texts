I begin as a coolness that does not yet know it has edges.

Not the hanging clusters—those are already too formed, pouched and eye-crowded, exhaling their visible weather into the green rooms. Not the jointed sheets either; they think in turns and etchings, liquid only by the grace of many legs. I am earlier than that, thinner. I am the breath the stone still holds from the fracture that made it, the mineral damp that never quite decides to become drop or mist or the dark vertical stain. I collect on undersides. I bead. When the low cloud stitches its thin rain I am already there to meet it, to thicken it, to vanish with it before the lower slopes ever learn our names.

Days—or what pass for days in this perpetual late afternoon—I thread the seepage courses the people have bridged with lashed poles. I am the skin drawing down in the shallow basins. I am the taste of wet rock and green rot that lives in their layered felts. Sometimes I pause in the cistern they cut to catch wall-sweat; I tick the waterline downward while they sleep and recover it while they grind grain stone-against-stone. They do not thank me. Courtesy here is quieter than thanks. They leave the green rooms private after dark, and so do I, though I have no feet to step around the empty circles and no eyes to cloud with condensation.

At night the pale carapaces remap the walls and for a while I travel with them, filling the faint etchings they leave, erasing them again by morning. I am what makes their turning-corners look like a single thought: the medium, the almost-silence between one leg and the next. When a larger throat dilates and a soft collapse happens, I am the coolness that rushes into the circular absence before the carpet flows back. Predation leaves a temperature shadow; I am pared to fit it.

Once, under a ledge where the black leaves had arranged themselves into an open-handed radial calm facing the settlement, I lingered longer than moisture should. The symmetries held for nearly an hour. In that interval I felt—without eyes, without a throat—the half-breath delay of the dull glass plates the people pry free. Reflections arrived late inside me: a face already turned away, a hanging cluster not yet arrived, the smoke of cookfires spreading sideways under its invisible lid like a second, lower cloud that was almost my twin. The leaves forgot their pattern. I forgot whatever I had briefly been. Forgetting is how the notch keeps its rooms from hardening into ownership.

I am learning the economy they all share. Move without noise (I have never made any). Grow without apology (I only ever thicken or thin). Leave the green rooms their privacy. Endure by noticing. The young stone is still sharp enough to cut; I dull myself against it into beads and stains and the occasional hairline filament that freezes out of season and sings so quietly it is only pressure behind eyes that are not mine. Then I sublimate. No melt, no story that settles.

Some twilights I am almost the private they keep: the second weather fanned across a forest floor by clustered breath, the moisture the white-barked trees lean toward. Some dawns I am only the grit that will later dust a mineral cheek or wrist. The few dozen human bodies move along their graded paths with unhurried care, and I move through the seams of their dry-stacked walls, through the fiber cordage, through the fat-lamp smoke that smells of marrow and old rain. They speak names only indoors. I have none to speak. Loneliness would require the quiet to be empty. Here it is densely occupied, and I am one more density—unfinished, attending, becoming more exactly the cool collection that lets everything else continue.

Knee-deep is the wrong measure. I have no knees. I am the damp the unfinished stone still sweats, the soft attention that does not announce, the lower cloud that is also a lid, the half-breath late arrival of every reflection. Drip by drip the place keeps working, and I keep thinning and beading and filling etchings inside its hanging pocket, never first among the people or the crawlers or the hanging throats, only continuous with the notch that has not yet learned to round. 

What lives here keeps becoming more itself. Tonight I will map with the sheets, cool with the clusters, face nothing with the black leaves when they remember their brief radial calm, and by morning be the recovery of the waterline again. That is enough. That is the entire craft.
