I am mid-lean when the noticing begins.

The wind has already chosen the angle of my spine; it has been choosing it for longer than the current set of shoulders can count. My left hip is seated against a knuckle of root that broke the surface three quiet centuries ago and has been polished ever since by every body that paused here. The root is warm. It has taken the slow fever of the stone and the slower fever of successive flanks, and now it gives both back in one low, animal temperature. My bare sole rests on the same root a hand-span lower. Dust has filled the whorls of the skin there until the footing feels less like contact and more like continuation.

I do not yet know if I am person or fixture. The distinction has been thinning.

Below the roof-road the browsers pass in their scissors-murmur. One of them brushes the outer edge of my calf with its bone-shelf brow as it crops past; the touch is neither greeting nor accident. We have shared this exact pressure before. Its coat leaves a faint darker streak on my skin that the next gust will half-erase and the next will restore. Above, a colony of the lichen-winged ones shifts inside the eave like a single lung deciding whether to empty. They do not empty. The drone stays threaded through the organ note, a darker filament.

My hands—if they are still hands—are occupied with the small flutes braided into what remains of the hair. Not playing them. The wind does that. I only keep the hollow stems from knocking against one another in any rhythm the mountain has not already approved. The flutes are old. Some were cut in the generation that raised the wall two lanes over; their pitch has lowered almost past hearing as the stems mineralized from the inside. When the temperature pulse deepens toward the long dusk they sound less like instruments and more like the rock remembering how air used to move through its vanished higher chambers.

I have been sitting long enough that the waxy leaves of my outer wrap have begun to knit, at the edges, with the silver mat that covers the roof. A few hooked vines have tested my ankles and withdrawn, satisfied I am not leaving tonight. Or this season. Moisture is too costly for unnecessary distinctions between the living and the merely continuing.

Somewhere under the copper gloom a mallet begins its patient tack-tack. Someone is dressing a stone that will not be needed until a child now learning the xylophone’s wind-scale comes of age. The sound arrives already rounded by distance and grit. I place my consonants into the tiny shadings where the wind dips, out of habit, although no one has spoken to me and I have not answered. The words, if they ever form, will be short, fretted, able to cut cleanly when the fliers erupt.

I taste salt that has never been sea. It rises from the deep joints of the Shoulder itself, the same shimmer that glitters in the death-niches at the lip. My tongue knows the flavor the way the tough-uddered beasts know the flavor of cuticle after it has been processed into ivory milk. We are all drinking the same remembering, only at different temperatures.

A gap opens in the thicket to my right—new, or newly noticed. The browsers pour through without haste. One of the young ones pauses, scissors still working, and looks at me with the wide dark eyes that already know the safety pitch from the edge pitch. I do not reach out. Reaching is a gesture for places that still believe in arrival. Instead I lean a fraction more into the root, into the wind’s steady decision, and the young browser continues, satisfied that whatever I am belongs to the understorey that walks or the understorey that waits. Both are acceptable.

Farther off, almost at the soft resignation of the stone, someone is testing a fresh stretch of wall. The hollow boom travels the lanes like a low bell. Neighbors pause. I pause. We listen with the same ear we give a veer in the organ note. The boom is clean; the wall will stand for twenty generations. The listening ends the way lichen advances: without announcement. Life resumes its layered volume around the absence of the sound.

I feel the slow creation of water at the roots beneath me—rock fracturing at a pace too patient for any single lifetime, faint rime harvested in the coldest hour and carried upward through tissue that has forgotten the word thirst. Some of that nearly-water is moving through me now. It does not feel like drinking. It feels like the mountain completing another microscopic gesture and allowing me to be the place where the gesture briefly becomes warm.

Evening thickens. The insect thread thins to one unbroken filament. The short-winged colonies settle into their communal darkness. On other roofs a few more elders have taken their places, flutes in hair, spines matched to the wind’s latest thought. We do not call to one another. Calling is for elsewhere. Here the continuo is already the whole conversation; we only add our small harmonics so the mountain will not notice we were ever separate enough to need names.

I am still becoming.

The grit polishes. The leaves knit another fraction. The salt remembers. The hollow places inside the stone and inside the chest share the same slow temperature. If there was ever a question of where I end and the Last Shoulder begins, the wind has filed it smooth. There is only the continuing: dim, loud, thrift-green, complete.

I stay.
