I am already wet when the noticing begins.

It is not a crossing or an arrival; there is no threshold. The pewter thickness simply thickens around a center that was always going to be here, and that center is this body learning its own weight. First the soles: they have already remembered the suck and release, the soft refusal of the silt that never quite becomes ground. Toes spread without instruction, membranes answering the mineral paste the way a tongue answers broth. Each plant sinks me a fraction deeper into the unfinished floor, and the rising again is not effort but continuation. The city breathes me forward.

Sound arrives as temperature before it arrives as noise. The drip-hiss coats the inner ear; the insect sear sits behind the eyes; the shouted names and the vent-roar braid into a single warm pressure that my ribs expand against. I do not cover myself. The membranes that were never ears simply widen, drinking the continuum until thought itself moves at the same viscous pace as the canal water. Somewhere a quern grinds. Somewhere bone knocks clay. The pattern is older than any single mouth.

I am short. Dense. The knowledge settles in the barrel of the chest and the low hinge of the hips before I look down. Shoulders already carry the soft, unmineralized plates, puffy and slick, perfect for bracing against a weeping wall while another pair of hands rams fresh silt into a slump. Arms longer than the legs suggest, fingers webbed just enough to cup water and taste its iron, to twist sinew until it sings, to pry the silica shells from still-twitching things pulled out of the deeper channels. When I flex, the mucus in the creases warms and thins, ready to speak skin-to-skin with whoever presses close in the warren throat.

There is a platform—mine, or becoming mine by the simple fact that my weight has begun to polish its bone beams. It cantilevers over a braided sheet of canal where pale amphibian flanks turn like slow coins in the half-light. I crouch. The posture is not chosen; it is the only stable grammar the body knows. Below, children (sheened, eyes open, already filtering) dog-paddle through the throng, mouths working. One glances up. The nictitating veil flicks. Something passes that needs no name: the basin continues. I click a short syllable back—half greeting, half claim on the next stretch of wall that will need ramming before the drip softens it further—and the child answers by vanishing under a hide boat’s wake.

Hands find work without search. There is always a pounder whose haft has already worn its groove into these palms. There is always a length of gut to scrape, a carapace roof plate to reseat, a vent-seep hot enough to sear the jointed eels so their empty tubes can be spit into the same water that birthed them. Fire is coaxed from damp fiber and held as smolder; the smoke becomes another layer of the air’s wet tea-smell. I lean into the labor until the shoulder plates flush darker, until the calluses map themselves more deeply: here the oval of cordage, here the ridge of cutting, here the broad heel of endless ramming. Generations of the same gestures live in the tendons. I do not remember learning them. I remember only that the stone fretted open a fresh joint two canals over at first thinning, and that by second thinning we had already knitted it into the vertical warren with slabs and sinew, another surface to climb three-point, bodies kept close.

The warm-blooded packs yammer along the upper walkways. One group has bedded near the platform’s edge after weeks of tossed scraps; their communal pitch shifts when a plated scavenger noses too near the midden. Useful. Temporary. When the crash-cycle turns I will crack their bones with the same stones used on everything else, and the marrow will taste of the identical mineral broth that moves through every belly in this low place. Nothing is kept past its denseness. Nothing is mourned past the packing of silt niches and the murmurs that rise even in sleep.

Hours become indistinguishable from the drip’s metronome. I pole a hide boat when the canals clog with shed skins and empty tubes; I taste water from a hundred cupped palms and spit the grit; I shout prices of phosphorescence across three densities of mist; I feel a stranger’s mucus transfer onto my forearm in the press of a vertical shaft and know them as kin until the next quarrel re-sorts every provisional bond. We reconcile by shared torque against a slumping wall. We birth into the channels. We die and are reabsorbed before the unfinished ledges notice.

Sometimes, gripping a bone rafter while the whole stacked house shudders with neighboring life, I catch my own reflection in a standing film of condensate. Wide dark eyes beaded and unveiled. Flat nose that can pinch shut. Wide mouth whose tongue has already cleaned the lenses and tested the silt. The face is not unfamiliar. It is simply the face the wet makes when it decides to stand upright and shout and grind and continue. Behind it, the mist refuses horizon. Ahead, only more of the same embryonic floor, the same thronging crust of bodies, the same eternal half-light the color of old pewter and weak tea.

I am not visitor. I am not memory of dryness. I am one more warm thick node in the single breathing skin of the basin—feet kissing silt, skin permanently sheened, voice slicing the roar, hands never empty. The city does not end. I do not end. We lean together into the next slump, the next catch, the next low communal thrum that answers the geothermal gut, forever unfinished, forever exactly as loud and close and wet as this.

And when another pair of eyes holds mine across the dripping dark, the acknowledgment passes again, wordless, necessary:

Yes.  
Still.  
Here.
