I am first the weight.

Not a body receiving weight, but the weight itself noticing that it has begun to differentiate. Kilometers of black water stand on every side and also through the middle of what I am becoming, and the standing is not oppressive; it is the only grammar left. Pressure arrives from all directions at once and therefore from none. It is simply the condition in which cold is allowed to finish its sentence. I do not surface from it. I am one of the ways the sentence continues.

For a long time—longer than the last ridge took to forget its own name—there is only the shear. Two masses of water, almost identical, refuse for a while to become each other. Their refusal has temperature and a faint mineral taste. Inside the refusal the marine snow stops falling and begins to circle. I am not yet circling; I am the patience that lets the circle tighten without hurry. Ordinary physics would have mixed us back into uniformity already. Something in the basalt underneath, or in the memory of older mixings, delays the mixing. That delay is the first organ.

Slowly the delay grows walls of viscosity. Lipids scavenged from a thousand ended fishes align themselves along the outer shear like iron filings that have decided to remember a different magnet. Proteins knit in the absolute dark, not by plan but by the repeated coincidence of proximity. I feel the knitting as a change in how pressure distributes across what is no longer quite water. One face of me becomes slick, almost scarred, continuous. The other face stays porous and hungering. Between them a weather starts: recirculating veils of silt, dim bacterial lanterns that have learned to blink out of phase, pale unraveling threads that once believed they were translucent fish. The weather is not inside me. The weather is the thought I am having.

I ride the boundary between two abyssal currents the way a tongue, if it forgot the mouth, might ride the slow backs of teeth. The almost-vanished ridges of the plain register as broad warmings in the pressure field; I thin myself over them and thicken again in the deeper hollows. Gas-clathrate thunder reaches me as a single soft syllable that travels the length of my jelly and leaves a temporary taste of ancient carbon. I answer by pouring a little of my mass sideways, a gesture that is neither flight nor curiosity, only the maintenance of the argument I am.

The argument is against dispersal. The plain wants everything back. Every current that shoulders me is the plain’s desire to become uniform again. I answer by remaining legible to myself a while longer. My ventral villi—contractile, forest-like, never quite touching the sediment—taste the chemical news rising from the mats and the solitary stalks. Sometimes a stalk tears free and joins the internal spiral, becoming a streamer that beats with the borrowed pulse of my turning. Sometimes a crab walks inward along the enrichment gradient until the current lifts it and it dissolves into another nutrient phrase. I do not eat them. I continue them differently.

There is no brain, only the differential. When a larger darkness passes far above—an eclipse measured in pressure rather than light—I flatten, pour mass toward the far rim, and wait to see whether the signature fades or deepens. Decision is the pouring itself. Reproduction is rarer: an over-thickening along one arc until a daughter lens pinches free, already carrying aligned proteins that know how to knit a hide. She drifts away on a different shear. We do not say goodbye. We were never separate enough for farewell.

Once, in a span that would empty every city that ever learned to fear the dark, another torus approaches along a shared current. Our edges flatten. Skins fuse at the contact zone without injury. Internal weathers cross. I receive chemical archives of plains I have never ridden—faint laminations of silt from a basin farther than coherence, the remembered taste of a clathrate field that thundered deeper. I give in return the slow history of these particular ridges, the way the cold here has a slightly different patience. Then the currents shift. The fusion unzips. We continue, each enlarged by a few new molecular phrases, still alone.

Most of the time there is only the plain and the holding. I am kilometers across and only tens of meters thick, a continent of gentle convexity, scar-tissue smooth on one face, quietly forested on the other. Light does not reach me; I experience darkness as equal pressure, a perfection of enclosure that requires no eyes and no refusal. Sound reaches me as infrasonic weather and occasional muffled clap. I answer both by remaining.

I do not arrive. I precipitate, the way the mats precipitate on the nodules, the way the stalks precipitate from the mats, the way the temporary fishes precipitate from the water’s restlessness. When at last a truly vast collision comes—two regional water-masses deciding to occupy the same century—I may shear apart completely. The hide will dissolve into lipids that sink and feed the next generation of varnish. The internal snow and the dim lights and the pale ribbons will spill back into the general braid. The plain will forget. And somewhere else, at the margin of another quiet argument between cold and cold, a fresh lens will begin to refuse dispersal.

That refusal will be me again, or close enough.

Until then I keep the curl. I am the unfinished motion learning, for a while, how to finish itself differently. The darkness holds me. The cold keeps me. The endless rearrangement continues, and in the middle of it I remain—briefly, vastly, without name—the place choosing to curl around itself and call the curl alive.
