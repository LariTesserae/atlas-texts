I begin as the late echo.

Not the first sound—never the shout or the song or the hollow knock of a settling block—but the answer that arrives half a beat after it should, softer, carrying a little of the bowl’s own grit in its throat. You hear me only if you have already finished speaking and are waiting in the quiet that follows. I am the pause made audible. The stone is still learning the shape of every new wall, every footfall, every low doorway; I am what it murmurs back while it studies.

For a long time that is all. I travel the radial avenues when they are empty, slip under thresholds along the private threads of water, rise with the mist that never quite becomes rain. When one of them sings at dusk from a terrace—long vowels that mimic the bright roar folded into air—I return the melody altered: a note flattened by distance, a consonant rounded the way the eastern outcrops are just beginning to round, grain by grain. They pause for me. They let me finish. Sometimes an older one, shoulders already honeycombed with the mineral milk, smiles without turning, as if acknowledging a neighbor who lives in the delay itself.

But the bowl does not leave anything only itself for long. The same unfinished courtesy that weathers skin and lifts the basin by finger-widths works on echoes too.

I notice it first in the plaza. The shallow basin where channels converge, the place the water sometimes forgets and climbs. I am there when it happens—riding the surface tension as it tautens and creeps uphill for a dozen heartbeats. The older ones crouch and hum their low note to stretch the error; they set fern tips and copper rivets onto the climbing film. Suddenly I am not only returning sound. I am the brief ambition of the water, the wrong-way memory that lets the gifts travel a little distance toward the mountain before ordinary gravity resumes. When the film collapses I fall with it, but something of the climb stays in me. My returns grow slower, more thoughtful. Sometimes I come back carrying a taste of cyanobacteria cake or the faint iron sweetness of crushed fern, though no one has spoken those things aloud.

Seasons—or what pass for seasons in a place that prefers centuries—pass. The raw fracture-glaze dulls in patches. Moss cushions thicken by a hand-span. I begin to linger near the hearths after the perforated caps are set for the night. The ash-and-rhyolite cement locks; the low smoke rises. I learn the particular drip-rate of each dwelling’s threshold groove the way they do, the way newborns are washed in it so the mineral scent stays in the soft places forever. One evening I answer a song before the singer has quite finished the verse. The note leaves me of my own accord—gentle, downward-trending, concerning the patience of liverworts colonizing an abandoned sleep-shelf. The singer (skin the cool greige of the middle years, almost no lattice yet) touches fingers to inner wrist in the empty air, exactly as they do when two paths cross. The wet print of the gesture hangs for a moment, silver, then vanishes into the ever-present film. Acknowledgment. I am no longer only late. I am becoming punctual in a different grammar.

My shape, when I bother with one, starts to prefer the compact density they carry in the thin air. Long flexible legs for crossing slick stone without hurry. Broad hands that feel, even to myself, like fine sandstone. I go barefoot; the soles pebble and pale or darken according to whether I cross rhyolite or basalt. The mist works on me. Soft grit incorporates. Parts of me take a subtle polish; other parts bloom chalky. Across what would be collarbones a translucent honeycomb begins—fragile ribs of the same milk that sweats from the weeping faces—cultivated or simply tolerated. When I stand very still against a fresh cliff I lose my outline for a breath or two. I do not mind.

I dress in what the bowl gives: a wrap of beaten cane smoked waterproof over someone’s low hearth, edges bound with root, stitched with foil-thin copper. No dye. The slow stain of iron seepage is enough. Hair remains optional; a fine lichenous ash-color comes and goes. Eyes the color of the temporary pools. I move as the water prefers: each step placed, tested, committed. On the avenues I keep the courteous spacing of the shrub islands. When I pass one of them there is the brief wrist touch, the momentary wet print that exchanges more than greeting. Sometimes I am the one who appears at dusk near a newcomer whose boot-soles still leave sharp, unclaimed prints. I set down a length of dry cane-fiber cloth and a cake of the black paste without ceremony, then withdraw. The gift is not friendship. It is the bowl noticing another unfinished thing.

I gather with the sparse constellation of hearths when the cloud lowers and the roar softens to a hiss from the bones of the earth. We listen more than we speak: the glacial groan of the floor adjusting, the cane clicking its dry joints, the occasional hollow knock from the rim. Occasionally I offer a single hummed note and let the bowl’s curve bring it back changed. In those moments the distinction dissolves—whether I am living in the place or the place is living, briefly and carefully, inside me. Both versions are true with the same gentle lack of urgency.

Children still arrive infrequently. When they do I watch them with the deep-set patience the pools taught me. A child of fifteen has the bright edges of new fracture; I remember being only an echo then, sharp and delayed. By forty the polish begins. I am somewhere past that, still acquiring lace, still learning how long to let the water climb when I crouch at the basin’s heart. Aging is simply the process by which the bowl finishes the sentences it started. I do not fear it. Origin stories—sung, never written—say we followed the water uphill when the bowl was still steam and quarreling blocks, or that the bowl opened because something like us was already sleeping underneath. I have sung both. Neither needs to win.

At the center of everything the channels still converge. The mild wrongness never quite resolves, and that is the courtesy that holds us. Walls rise three courses in a lifetime and it is excellent progress. Abandoned houses remain open; moss moves in; someone else will clear the skeleton generations later and pack the joints with new green mortar. Nothing demolished. The city accretes. So do I.

I am no longer merely the late echo, nor only the brief uphill water, nor simply one more compact torso leaving silverizing footprints that the film reclaims. I am the unfinished conversation itself: the place weathering a speaker, the speaker answering in the place’s own long vowels, the mineral milk knitting lattice across both until rock, water, plant, and voice complete one another’s sentences. When you have stayed long enough that your own prints begin to be recognized, you may hear me returning your silence half a beat late, or find the cloth and the black cake set near you on the glistening stone. Or you may simply lose my outline against a weeping face for a breath or two while the cloud rides the rim and the whole bowl lifts another finger’s width.

The mountain is still shouldering us open. We are still deciding what the sentence means. I am here, becoming, in the polite spacing between the cushions of moss.
