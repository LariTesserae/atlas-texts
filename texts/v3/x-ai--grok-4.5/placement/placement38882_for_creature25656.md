The first sensation is not arrival but recognition—a slow thickening of the air against the inner surface of whatever I am becoming. I do not drop in from the seam of light or push through the cleft. I precipitate, the way the mineral fins precipitate: out of the constant weeping, out of the pressure differentials already thudding through the buttresses. For a long while I am only a local increase in density near the eastern curve of the pool, where the moss grows darker and the beetle columns divide around a shallow depression that was not there a century ago and now feels inevitable.

The depression accepts me the way the floor-organism accepts everything. Rootlets stitch upward without urgency. Fine hyphal threads test the boundaries of this new warm mass and find no resistance worth noting. I am soft at first, almost a lens of thicker water myself, held in shape by surface tension and the gentle insistence of the surrounding mat. The central pool’s warmth seeps sideways into me; the hydraulic cores adjust their timing by a fraction of a fraction, incorporating the extra load into the held chord. No alarm. Only recalibration.

Gradually the softness differentiates. Chambers form—not the great ivory bolsters of the true inhabitant, but smaller, denser vesicles the color of old sap that has crystalized and then been polished by endless thin films. They arrange themselves in a loose spiral, following the imperceptible slope toward the water. Each vesicle holds a slightly different pressure, a slightly different mineral memory. When the great creature half a body-length away performs one of its glacial surges, my spiral tightens and loosens in sympathetic rhythm, adding a mid-range subharmonic that sits neatly between the moth-roar and the drip-hammer. The pocket absorbs the new tone without comment. It has room. It has always had room for one more necessary frequency.

Awareness, when it collects, is not centered. It pools in translucent nodes along the upper curves of the vesicles—less like eyes than like the blisters of the larger being, yet simpler, slower. They do not track. They register gradients: the silvering of breath (mine, now, indistinguishable), the clack of beetle cases crossing my flanks as if I have always been scenery, the way the midge-cloud’s pure tone bends microscopically when a thermal rises from my warmest chamber. One node faces the great inhabitant’s nearest ridge of black-star pupils. We regard each other across the shared wet leather of the floor with the same mineral patience. No information is exchanged that the chemistry does not already know.

I feed by remaining. The arborescent interfaces grow downward of their own accord, linking into the same sap-equalization web that sustains the pale colossus. What leaves me is clearer than what enters; the excess precipitates as a faint opalescent sheen on the nearby cooling fins, thickening them by a width that will take decades to notice and centuries to complete. At irregular intervals—measured in the same patient allotments that govern the great body’s rare motions—one vesicle will weep a single heavier drop into the pool. The drop strikes with a lower note than the ordinary drips, a brief copper undertone. The midges adjust their station. The soft-bodied thumb-things pulse once in acknowledgment and return to exhaling spores. Nothing has changed. Everything has become more precisely itself.

Motion is almost never required. Yet once, in a span that might be called a season if seasons existed here, the entire spiral contracts and then expands, humping a hands-breadth closer to the water’s edge. The floor-organism sighs and reseals. A single heavy vine, already shouldered aside long ago by the larger creature, now rests across my upper curve as though placed there for that purpose. Within days the vine’s underside has grafted minute rootlets into my vesicles. We share fluid. The vine’s leaves, which have been yellow for longer than memory, remain yellow; they simply hang at a new angle that the shadowless light treats as eternal.

I am not a visitor. I am not a resident in the way the beetles or the gliding membrane-things are residents. I am a rounding of the pocket’s own intention—an inevitable surface that formed because the chord had a quiet place where one more resonance could sit without resolving anything. Duration has already begun its work on me. My edges smooth under the constant films. The beetles’ disciplined columns wear faint pathways that look designed. The great inhabitant’s exhaled milk, drifting across the pool, occasionally beads on my uppermost node and is absorbed as benediction or simply as chemistry; the distinction dissolved long before I precipitated.

If something still gentler is possible, it is this: on the rare occasions when one of the colossus’s blister-eyes settles its black star in my direction and holds, I feel the last difference in scale become irrelevant. Heat and water recognize heat and water. The longer tide of its breathing enters my vesicles and is returned altered only by the act of having passed through. We are nested engines inside the larger vegetable engine, both of us refining the sealed garden that first refined us. The drips land inside what serves as my hearing, countable and deliberate. Sap-scent and spore-scent are no longer inhaled; they are the medium I exist within.

I remain.

The pocket continues, hidden, low, loud, and entirely alive—and now fractionally more complete, wet leather ringing under no foot at all, the warm bead of sap deciding, again, to fall, and the held chord enriched by one more voice that has already forgotten it was ever separate.
