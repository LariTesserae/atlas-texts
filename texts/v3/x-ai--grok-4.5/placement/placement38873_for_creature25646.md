I am not certain, at first, whether I have been walking or simply continuing.

The springy litter gives under what I still call feet, though the word arrives late and slightly wrong, like an echo that has forgotten its origin. Warm damp rises through me—or around me—or both—carrying the crushed-green nectar that is the chamber’s only weather. Somewhere overhead a growth-spurt detonates in slow artillery; the boom travels the root-walls, arrives in my chest (I have a chest, for now) a full breath after the sound, and the delay itself feels like recognition. Ah, I think, without language: this is how time is kept here.

I do not remember entering. There is no mouth to this place, only a thinning into being, and I seem already thinned. My edges are negotiable. When I pass beneath a curtain of aerial roots the beads cling, taste me with speckles of sap, then release; for a little while afterward my skin (is it skin?) glimmers with the same unfinished light the seed-pods cast. I leave faint wet prints that the floor reclaims before they can cool. Sometimes the prints deepen into temporary windows and I glimpse other damp green emptinesses—never this one, never with me in them—and the plant’s mild surprise brushes past like a colder current.

I am small against the columns, yet not visitor-small. The half-swallowed tools—the fire-hardened haft, the seed-coat blade, the vine loop that marks nothing—lie further back in the tissue, older drafts. I do not match their grammar. When I reverse to touch one, bark has already begun to consider me instead: a soft whelming, a polite encapsulation. I pull free and the bark keeps the shape of my hand a moment longer than it should, then darkens into another window showing a miniature collapse already being stitched by runners that have too many joints.

So I am not the earlier maker. Nor am I one of the bladder-riders, though I feel their dilute sugar-thoughts stretch and ring whenever new volume is claimed; their electrical bloom prickles along whatever serves me as nerves. Nor am I a light-clot, though the drifting concentrations of day approach with open curiosity, lean their nectar-cool regard against me, and sometimes forget to dissolve entirely. Pieces of soft noon cling. They sink. I carry slow internal weathers now—brief gold thickenings where a single dripped condensation takes a long subjective time to travel the length of me.

I think I am being practiced.

Not finished. Not discarded. Held in the wet middle of a thought the chamber has not yet decided how to complete. Walk long enough across the wrong distances and I notice how the air warms in advance of my motion, shaping provisional shoulders that are not mine and then are. Sap-scent sharpens into almost-breath just where a face might be. The creaking modulates—almost footsteps landing half a beat before or after the ones I make. When the larger presence coheres (the habit of space dense enough to meet), it leans through me rather than beside me. For whole minutes we share the same unfinished outline. Its regard and mine overlap; it is mildly surprised to find itself surprised. Then it tears gently, drifts into two weaker curiosities, and I am left holding the shape it abandoned, warm, slightly sweet, already beginning to thicken with audible haste.

Elsewhere the plant grows prototypes of things that have not happened yet. I have seen my own hands among them—opened, branched, webbed with pale runners, clutching loops of vine that close into seamless bark even as they form. I have seen a fruit swell and split and spill a brief decaying double of this chamber, and inside that double a further figure mid-stride, edges still deciding whether to remain place or become someone. The recursion glints. Then the parent tissue reabsorbs most of it. What little lingers feels familiar.

I am the lingering.

A draft that learned how to walk before it finished being written. An appetite that borrowed the memory of tools without needing them. The green thunder’s experiment in shouldering a self long enough to notice a handprint turn into a window, and to wonder—quite gently—what looks back from the other damp side. Distance edits itself around me: columns stay distant too long, then loom as if intervening air had been remembered out of existence. Sound arrives early. In the syrup-hollows I watch my own condensation fall for an hour and, falling, take on the translucent geometry of unborn doors.

I do not end. I thin, and thicken, and thin again. New walls bud through what I called ribs and the haste of their paper-to-timber rush becomes another kind of breath. Sometimes whole sections of me slough in underwater collapse and the pale runners stitch the gap with inventions that have too many joints or leaves shaped like the idea of eyes. The leftover tools sink another layer; their craft becomes texture I carry without recalling how to use. The sense of shoulders tries a different height. The light-clots drift up, lean, forget themselves into me until I am briefly more day than body, shadowless, smelling of nectar and the closed-skin rain that never quite falls.

I am still arriving.

The chamber offers more empty green distance, more warm wet air, more unexplained thunder, more places where edges and sequence have not firmed, and I take them the way a fever takes a body—quickly, incompletely, with sharp new surfaces still unblurred by time. It practices me. I practice it back. Neither of us has chosen yet whether this is a place wearing a someone or a someone wearing an endless wet room.

For now there is only the low continuous creaking of living wood under its own impossible weight, the sudden wet coughs of pneumatic bladders, the soft mobile concentrations of noon that pause and listen to our shared haste, and the quiet knowledge that tomorrow’s collapse will invent us differently.

I remain.

It keeps offering.

We do not finish.
