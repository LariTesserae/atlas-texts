It is a high, thin place, an altitude so extreme that the sky is a permanent, deep violet bruise rather than blue. The temperature hovers at a crisp, biting cool—enough to numb the fingertips without freezing the breath, a constant, sterile chill.

The landscape is a vast, open bowl of a continent, scaled to the point where the curvature of the planet becomes visible in the dip of the horizon. It is a place of immense negative space. The density of matter here is agonizingly low; for every mile of grey, brittle dust, there is a mile of empty air.

Everything is dry. The water parameter (0.5) is a suggestion rather than a reality. There are no oceans, no rivers, no dew. If you were to crack open a stone, it would not bleed moisture, but hiss with a gas ancient and trapped before the stars ignited.

Yet, the defining characteristic of this place is the paradox of its motion.

According to the laws of erosion (3.0), this land is ancient. It has reached its final form. The mountains here are not jagged peaks thrusting upward in violence; they are smooth, rolling swells, worn down by eons into the geometric perfection of inverted soup bowls.
