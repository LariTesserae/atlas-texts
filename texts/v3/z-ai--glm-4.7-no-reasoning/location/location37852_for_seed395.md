This is a world that should not exist—a vast, continental sprawl of dense urban construction built without tools, without water, and without any precedent of erosion. It is a metropolis of pure biology, an endless city that was never engineered but instead *grown*, and it is still growing.

**The Terrain and Materials**
The ground here is not ground. There is no soil, no stone, no dust. Beneath the “streets” is a continuous, buckling crust of chitin and dense cellulose, laid down over eons by the flora itself. It is a warm, low expanse; the elevation barely shifts across thousands of kilometers, creating a horizon that stretches impossibly far, curved only by the sheer scale of the planet itself. The surfaces underfoot are firm but give slightly, like thick cork or dried cartilage. Everything is constructed from biological polymers—lignin variants, hardened resins, compressed fungal hyphae, and silicate-infused keratin. It is an entirely sealed, dry environment. There is no rain, no rivers, no dew. The chemistry of this world is non-aqueous; the fluid of life here is a slow-viscosity lymph that moves through capillary action, locked entirely inside cellular walls. 

**The Vegetation: The Urban Jungle**
The vegetation is the city, and the city is the vegetation. With a density of 2.7 and a built score of 2.1, there is no empty space. The flora has evolved into architectural forms. Towering columnar trunks, hundreds of meters thick, act as load-bearing pillars, their surfaces fluted and terraced like the steps of a ziggurat. Between them, cascading shelf-fungi form balconies, walkways, and bridges, interlocking in a saturated, fully occupied 3D lattice. Creeping vines with fused, interlocking plates create sheer walls. The canopy high above is airtight, a layered vault of overlapping leaves the size of lake beds, locking in the heat and the atmosphere. It is lush beyond comprehension—a riot of growth that mimics the most densely packed metropolis, yet there is not a single tool, nail, or mortar joint in sight. It is pure nature, pushed to an urban extreme.

**The Atmosphere and Light**
The air is warm—stubbornly, uniformly warm, trapped by the massive biological superstructure. Light filters down from above, passing through the translucent, layered canopy. It is normal daylight at the highest tiers, but as it penetrates the dense, packed infrastructure of the flora, it is fractured into a billion shafts. The middle and lower levels exist in a perpetual, golden-green twilight. Because the growth is so dense, the air is sluggish, heavy with the exhalations of the flora—a thick, breathable gas exchanged through billions of microscopic stomata on every surface. 

**Erosion: The Embryonic Metropolis**
The erosion score is devastatingly low. This place is embryonic, raw, just coming into being. The edges of the shelves and bridges are not worn smooth by wind or time; they are soft, rubbery, unfurling at the margins. You can see the growth happening if you have the patience. At the ends of the walkways, the keratin is still wet and translucent, curling outward to harden in the trapped heat. There are no ruins here, no decay, no ancient rubble. The city has no past; it is entirely present, an organism in the throes of a continuous, continent-scale genesis. The geological processes are just beginning; the world is still under construction, its raw geology literally pushing upward as the root-pillars swell.

**Fauna and Sound: The Silent Procession**
The fauna is abundant, yet the world is nearly silent. The creatures here are builders, cleaners, and weavers—biological counterparts to urban infrastructure. There are immense, multi-legged isopods the size of buses, their feet padded with thick, silent bristles, moving slowly along the chitin highways to clear away the dead cellulose. Above them, swinging from bridge to bridge, are armored arboreal grazers, their mouths designed to clamp and shear the unfurling edges, pruning the city to keep it from collapsing under its own weight. 

Their movements are deliberate and eerily quiet. There is no wind to whistle through the corridors, and every surface is soft or fibrous, absorbing impact. The loudest sounds are biological mechanics: the deep, rhythmic subsonic thrum of sap-equivalent pumping through the massive pillar-trunks, the faint, wet snap of a new shelf-fungus unfurling, the whisper of a million padded feet finding purchase on the resin-strewn floors. It is a quiet that feels sacred, the silence of a sealed temple the size of a continent.

**The Rules of the World**
This is a world of biological determinism and absolute saturation. There is no weather, only the steady, internal climate of a living megastructure. There is no seeking water; hydration and nutrition are internalized, distributed through a mycelium-like network that connects the entire city-flora. Space is the ultimate constraint—growth is a zero-sum game of pushing against neighbors, which drives the city ever upward and outward. The ecosystem is in constant, active motion, a slow-motion avalanche of predator, prey, and builder, all functioning as the immune system and circulatory system of the city itself. 

Standing in the lower avenues, looking down the kilometer-long corridors of interlocking bio-architecture, you do not feel like you are in a forest. You feel like you are in the living tissue of an infinite, embryonic lung, just before it takes its first breath.
