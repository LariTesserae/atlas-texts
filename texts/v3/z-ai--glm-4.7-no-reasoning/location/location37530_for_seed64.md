The space is not a valley, nor a cavern, though it mimics the claustrophobia of both. It is a crack—a hairline fissure in the flank of a mountain range so high that the air outside has long since turned to thin, electrified ice. Inside, the scale is intimate, a pocket universe carved from the living stone, a vertical slot no wider than a outstretched arm’s span and descending into a darkness that feels physical.

Everything is structural, sharp, and unbending.

They feed on the piezoelectric charge running through the mountain’s granite bones and the faint, resonant hum of the earth. They do not transpire; they do not sweat.
