You stand upon a bridge of woven mangrove roots, thick as ancient pythons and bound together with a technology that relies on leverage and friction rather than iron or nail. This is the Walk of the drowned, a hamlet-sized archipelago hovering just above the surface. The water below is not the turbulent abyss of the open ocean, nor the murky stagnation of a swamp; it is a clear, voluminous liquid, so distilled it acts like a lens, magnifying the forest floor twenty feet below.

Because the elevation is high, the water here behaves strangely. It rests upon a shelf of the world, a high plateau where gravity pulls at the water with the same insistence as the air. The result is a sense of inverted sky. Looking down is vertigo-inducing; you see the tops of "trees" that grow downward, their branches reaching toward the unseen depths miles below.

The walls breathe, the floors pulse with slow capillary action, and the roof is a lattice of living branches that sheds the endless rain.

But beneath that, there is the sound of the water itself: the suck and gurgle of the roots drawing sustenance, the drip-trickle-plash of the runoff channeling through the city's gutters, and the constant, resonant thrum of the atmosphere.

Fish with leg-like fins climb the dangling aerial roots to graze on the moss. Larger creatures—amphibious arboreal mammals that move with a slow, liquid grace—watch from the hollows of the "buildings." They are used to the people here; the people are just another animal in the weave.

There are no straight lines, only the curves of wood. Ropes made of dried vine fiber hum in the wind. It is a city that has established itself (1.9/3), weathered and settled, finding a balance where the rot of the wood is perfectly matched by the speed of new growth.

Time here is slow (0.8/3). The erosion is balanced; the roots that rot away are replaced instantly by new growth. The water level rises and falls by inches over the course of years. The people move with a languid, deliberate pace, matching the rhythm of the dripping world. There is no rushing here, only the endless, gradual cycle of the flood.

It is a strange, unusual place (0.9/3), not because of magic, but because of biology. It is a world where the plant is the continent, where the city is a parasite upon the tree, and where the water is the floor, the walls, and the ceiling all at once. A quiet, loud, cool, submerged grove suspended in the clouds.
