The world feels less like a place and more like a fever dream of a mason god, left unfinished on the drafting table.

It is an architecture of infinite span, a low-slung ceiling of masonry that extends until the curvature of the horizon bends the lines of mortar into a closing circle. This is the Haze-Work. The air is thick, not with fog, but with heat and suspended moisture—a heavy, silken humidity (Water 1.7) that clings to the skin and beads on every surface. The sky is nonexistent, replaced by a vault of dark, soot-stained brick and distant, dripping catwalks, illuminated only by a weak, bruised luminescence that seems to seep from the mortar itself (Light 1.4).

There are no trees here (Vegetation 0.5). The concept of "root" is forbidden. Instead, the ground is a chaotic quilt of tiles—slate, terracotta, limestone—laid by hands that clearly did not possess a ruler. The tiles do not fit; they are jagged, mismatched, and violent in their arrangement (Built 2.9, Tech 1.2). It is a total construction, a world where even the dust is manufactured, crushed from the ruins of earlier, grander schemes.

It is immensely hot (Temperature 2.6). The heat radiates from the earth itself, from the friction of things moving. It is a dry, baking heat, but the moisture in the air creates a slick, tropical oppression. Sweat does not evaporate; it accumulates. Puddles form instantly in the depressions of the brickwork, only to hiss and vanish as the ground shifts.

And the ground does shift. This is the terror of the place: the landscape is embryonic (Erosion 0.2). It is not eroding; it is *arriving*. The solidity is an illusion. The tectonic plates of this world are not stone, but massive, sliding mechanisms of simple, brutal engineering—gears the size of mountain ranges, levers fashioned from the trunks of ironwood trees that do not exist, pulleys made of bone. The earth rolls underfoot like the deck of a ship in a hurricane (Dynamic 2.6).

You stand on a plain of ziggurats, but they are not finished. They are half-hewn, their raw flanks exposed, rising from a shallow, oily sea that fills the low basins (Elevation 1.3). The water is dark, tepid, and restless. It washes against the raw stone, but the stone does not weather. The water cannot carve because the stone is too new, too raw for time to touch it yet.

In the distance, the logic of the world fails (Weirdness 2.8). A massive span of bridge stretches across the empty air, connecting two floating platforms that are slowly rotating in opposite directions. As you watch, the mortar between the bricks begins to un-cure, turning from hard stone back into wet slurry, dripping upward into the dim light, defying gravity. A tower in the far distance folds in on itself, the bricks softening like wet clay, collapsing into a heap of geometric sludge, only to instantly reconstitute into a spiraling ramp. Causality is fluid here; effect precedes cause, and corners sometimes have three right angles.

The silence is profound (Sound 1.4), despite the turbulence. The massive grinding of the tectonic gears is felt in the teeth and bones rather than heard in the ears. The crashing of a collapsing spire happens without a sound, a silent movie of destruction playing out in the gloom. The hiss of steam is the only constant noise, a whispering snake that warns of the heat.

There are no animals (Fauna 0.4). The movement in the periphery is not biological. It is the *Work*. Simple, spider-like automata made of clay and rusted iron skitter across the tiles (Tech 1.2). They have no faces, only limbs and a singular purpose. They carry buckets of slurry from the puddles to the high walls, troweling wet stone over dry stone, building without a blueprint, repairing structures that were never meant to stand. They do not speak. They do not stop. If you stand still too long, they will begin to trowel mortar over your feet, attempting to integrate you into the vast, incomplete masonry.

The scale crushes the spirit (Scale 2.6). You are an ant on a floor that stretches into forever. The ceiling is so high it creates its own weather system of swirling micro-climates and localized rainstorms that fall from the upper darkness to evaporate before they hit the ground.

This is a place of becoming. It is a violent, chaotic nursery for geology, where the rules of physics are mere suggestions and the landscape is a frantic, unfinished sketch drawn by a trembling hand in the dark. To walk here is to walk on the skin of a world that is still trying to decide what it wants to be.
