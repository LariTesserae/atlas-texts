Welcome to the Green Engine.

### Terrain and Architecture
The ground beneath your feet is not stone. It is cortex, the dense, fibrous meat of a world-tree that has been hollowed out and hardened over eons. You are standing on the 'floorboards' of a biological entity the size of a continent. The terrain rises in great, rounded bluffs of woody material, scarred by ancient, regular grooves. These aren't natural formations; they are tread marks from mega-fauna that died a million years ago, or perhaps the tracks of the world’s own internal tectonics.

It is a "built" environment, but the builders were not human. Everything here is *cultivated*. The archways are formed by bending massive lignin pillars while they were soft. The plazas are flat-leaved mosses grown into a pavement so tight and interwoven that a boot cannot dent it. Pipes—massive, vascular tubes as thick as subway tunnels—burst from the walls, leaking oxygen hisses and dense clouds of pollen. It is a factory where the machines are geraniums the size of skyscrapers, and the fuel is pure solar desperation.

### Water and Chemistry
Water is the enemy here. The parameter reads 0.5—virtually none. The plants do not drink; they inhale. This is a xerophyte’s nightmare, a system evolved to harvest moisture from the air and recycle waste breath. The visible 'rivers' are not H2O. They are channels of mineral oil, hyper-concentrated ammonia, and sticky fructose syrups that flow through translucent canals. Slime molds the size of vehicles creep along the walls, digesting the dead and turning them back into nutrient paste. Do not touch the glowing puddles; they are acids strong enough to etch glass.

### The Soundscape
It is mostly quiet—a 1.3 on the scale—but it is a heavy, waiting silence. It sounds like the inside of a lung. A deep, rhythmic thrumming vibrates through the floor plates: the great heart of the Engine pumping fluids up from the deep core. Occasionally, there is a sharp *crack* as a seed pod the size of a house bursts open, releasing a cloud of spores that rains down like hot snow. The hiss of escaping gas from a vent is the only birdsong here.

### The Dynamic State
Despite the age suggested by the erosion, the atmosphere is turbulent—a 2.7. The place is unstable. Sections of the 'floor' heave and shift as the underlying muscles of the world contract. Tumors of rapid-growth fungi erupt spontaneously, sealing off corridors that were open moments ago. A 'storm' here isn't rain; it is a violent gust of heated methane and spore-clouds that howls through the vascular tunnels, stripping leaves and tearing down the weaker structures. It is a Solaris-like chaos; the landscape changes its mind daily.

### Ecology and Life
There are no animals—0.4 on the scale. No birds, no insects, no beasts of burden. The movement you see is the plants themselves. Vines lasso passageways with lightning speed. Flowerheads snap shut with the force of bear traps. The ecosystem has evolved to be self-sufficient; photosynthesis is too slow, so the flora here has become predatory or parasitic. Some massive trees act as parasites on the ceiling itself, draining the structural integrity of the world to fuel their own blooms.

### Atmosphere and Rules
The rule of this world is simple: *Grow or be Consumed.*

It feels ancient. The erosion parameter of 2.7 suggests this has been going on for a long time. The machinery of nature here has ground down into a smooth, terrifying equilibrium. The turbulence isn't birth; it is the violent thrashing of a dying thing, or perhaps a living thing that is simply too big to notice the ants crawling on its skin.
