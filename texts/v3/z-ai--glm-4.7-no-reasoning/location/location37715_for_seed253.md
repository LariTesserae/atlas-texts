This is the Sunken Spire.

The air has the viscosity of thin syrup, pressing against the body with the weight of a deep-sea diver’s suit. It is a wall of rain that never hits the ground, suspended in eternal static suspension (0.4/3).

They are architecture. But there is no mortar, no brick, no steel (0.5/3). This is a fully constructed environment made entirely of biological matter (2.9/3). The hills are ziggurats of compacted peat and wood, overgrown so completely that the distinction between "building" and "plant" has vanished.

This is the plant-as-substrate. The world is a single, living organism that has been shaped into a city, or perhaps a city that has grown wild until it became a planetary lung.

It is a closed system, a terrarium of the gods.

The "buildings" are hollowed-out husks of massive fungal growths, perfectly smooth and geometric, arranged in a precise, alien grid that hints at an intelligence that has long since departed, leaving only their garden behind.

It is a place where you walk underwater in the sky, surrounded by the ghosts of architects who grew their cathedrals from seed, where the rain never falls, and the darkness is alive.
