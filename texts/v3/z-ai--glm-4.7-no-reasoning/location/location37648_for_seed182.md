The environment sits low, tucked into a hollow scooped from the earth’s crust—a subterranean blister, a blister that has only just popped. The elevation is negative, a sunk place, not the deep crushing dark of an abyss but a sub-basement, aBelow the Root, pressed against the ceiling of a world that feels too heavy for it.

The heat here is oppressive, a wet blanket of thermal pressure. It radiates not from a sun, but from the walls themselves and the machinery lodged within them. The air is thick, nearly chewable, holding a temperature rating of 2.7—hot enough to sweat while sitting still, hot enough to make metal warm to the touch. It smells of steam, wet iron, and the sweet, cloying scent of rotting verdure.

Everything is damp (1.4), but not in the way of a swamp. There are no open lakes here, no deluges. Instead, the moisture is architectural. It weeps. The stone walls, jagged and fresh from a recent geological rupture (young erosion, 1.3), glisten with a perpetual film of condensation. Water droplets form with agonizing slowness (dynamic 0.7) at the tips of stalactites that are sharper than glass, swelling over hours before finally letting go to strike the metal floors with a singular, ringing *plink* that echoes for too long. The moisture gathers in the troughs of the gear-work and in the palm-sized hollows of the masonry, creating a world of micro-pools, each stagnant and filled with oily rainbows.

And it is lush (vegetation 1.8). The density of the space (2.7) means there is no distinction between the built and the grown. The urban construction (built 2.3) is packed so tight that the buildings—narrow, industrial towers of brick and blackened pipe—have effectively fused into a single mass. Nature occupies the negative space. Vines as thick as a man's thigh throttle the support beams, forcing their way into the mortar. Moss carpets the catwalks in deep, bruising greens. The flora is aggressive, exploiting the fresh cracks in the foundation, turning the sharp edges of the young rock into soft, suffocating mounds of wet leaves. It is a cathedral-scale (scale 1.0) space; you can stand in the center, beneath the low, dripping dome of the hollow, and see the limits of the world in every direction, a claustrophobic circle of steaming industry.

This is an industrial oubliette (tech 1.8). Huge, riveted pistons the size of cottages protrude from the earthen walls, moving with glacial lethargy. They exhale clouds of white vapor that drift lazily through the dim light (light 1.1). The illumination is weak, provided mostly by bioluminescent fungi growing in the hotter crevices and the dying ember-glow of exhaust ports. There is no hum of activity, no clanking of a busy factory. The industry here is of a different sort—terrestrial processing, perhaps, or the slow tectonic manufacturing of the planet itself. It feels abandoned, yet active.

Silence (sound 0.6) is the baseline. The dominant noise is the "breathing"—the slow expansion and contraction of the metal ribs of the place as the temperature shifts by a fraction of a degree. It is the sound of patience, of processes that span centuries rather than shifts.

Fauna (fauna 0.9) is scarce, a calculated mistake in this ecosystem. You might see the solitary, wet-feathered shape of a "Soot-Swift" clinging to a pipe, or the translucent eye of a blind salamander watching from a puddle, but for the most part, the occupants of this place are not biological. Or perhaps they are indistinguishable from the machinery. There is a weirdness here (weirdness 1.9) in the way the vines seem to plug themselves into the electrical conduits, sipping voltage as readily as water. The stone is too geometric, too fresh; it looks like it was cut yesterday, yet covered in a thousand years of moss. The laws of biology and metallurgy have blurred into a single, sluggish metabolism.

The atmosphere is heavy, static. You wait for something to happen, for the machine to speed up or the sun to rise, but it never does. The steam drifts, the water drips, the piston extends a foot, and the leaves unfurl a millimeter more. It is a slow, hot, green prison, a greenhouse built in the dark, sweating in the dark, growing slowly toward a light that does not exist.
