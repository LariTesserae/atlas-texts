The air here tastes of iron and old spit. It hangs heavy in the throat—a cool, damp weight (Temperature 0.6, Water 2.0) that settles into the lungs and refuses to leave. This is not a deep cold, but a pervasive, seeping chill, the kind that makes knuckles ache and joints stiffen.

It is a small place, a tight, defined world (Scale 0.9). It could be a hamlet, or perhaps just a single, sprawling district of a larger city that was swallowed long ago. The boundaries are marked by the sharp, sudden rise of the "Old Breaks"—jagged cliffs of grey stone that look less like geographical features and more like wounds in the skin of the earth (Erosion 1.2). These cliffs are young, fresh, terrifyingly sharp; they haven’t been rounded by wind or time. They weep constantly. Groundwater trickles down the raw faces in a thousand unbroken rivulets, turning the rock faces into slick, black mirrors that weep tears of clear, cold water.

Down in the basin, the water has nowhere to go. The ground is saturated, a soup of grey mud and black slime that sucks at the boots (Density 2.7). There is no "open space" here. Every inch of solid footing is occupied by the city.

The settlement is dense, a claustrophobic huddle of primitive architecture (Built 2.0, Tech 0.8). It is a town of leaning towers, built entirely of rough-hewn timber and stone dragged from the weeping cliffs. The structures are low, dark, and packed shoulder-to-shoulder, creating narrow canyons where the wind dies. The construction is crude—post-and-beam, lashed with soaked leather ropes and twine, joints pegged with wood rather than nailed. It feels temporary, yet permanent in its mass. The buildings are not square; they conform to the tight spaces, bulging at the seams, braced against one another as if the only thing keeping them upright is the crushing pressure of the neighbors.

There is no grass here (Vegetation 0.7). The ground is too wet, the light too blocked by the overhanging eaves. Instead, the ecology is slick and dark. Mosses that look like wet velvet coat the northern sides of the buildings, thick and pulsing. Fungi, tough and leathery, sprout from the mortar between stones—shelf fungi the color of bruised liver.

Light falls here, but it is a strangled light (Light 1.5). The sky is visible only as a narrow ribbon of grey above the canyon roofs. Sunlight rarely touches the mud; the city lives in a permanent, gloomy twilight. This is the realm of the weird (Weirdness 1.4). The water doesn't just flow; it behaves with intention. Puddles don't settle; they creep, climbing the wooden pilings of the huts as if defying gravity, leaving behind mineral deposits that look like rust.

It is mostly quiet (Sound 1.1), but the silence is deceptive. It is the held-breath quiet of a place that is actively working (Dynamic 1.7). The dominant sound is the *drip-drip-drip* of the weeping rocks, a rhythm that syncopates with the soft *squelch* of the mud shifting under the weight of the buildings. Occasionally, a wet, heavy *thump* echoes from the mines—somewhere deep in the saturated earth, a shaft has collapsed, and the city settles a fraction of an inch lower to compensate.

Fauna is rare, but what is here is shaped by the damp (Fauna 1.2). There are no songbirds. Instead, the silence is broken by the occasional, heavy flap of flightless, water-logged birds that roost in the eaves, their feathers oily and dark. They move with a awkward, hopping gait through the mud, watching with glassy, dead eyes. In the shadows of the alleyways, small, pale crustaceans—scavengers from the brackish pools—skitter over the wet stones, clicking faintly.

The city lives by a simple, brutal rhythm dictated by the water. When the rain comes, it doesn't fall in showers; the mist simply condenses out of the air, thickening until the world is white and wet. The people—if there are any—are ghosts in this machine, moving through the tight spaces, repairing the wood, clearing the slime, constantly bailing water from the cellars of their primitive, hulking towers. It is a place of preservation, of holding back the wet, of living in the tight, fresh scar of the earth where the water never stops trying to reclaim the stone.
