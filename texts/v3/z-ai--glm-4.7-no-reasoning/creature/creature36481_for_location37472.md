There is no motion here but the drift. The inhabitants of the High Aquarium are not things that run, swim, or hunt. They are things that *are*.

To speak of "whoever lives here" is a misnomer, for there are no minds, only mechanisms of flesh and chlorophyll. The denizens of this sunken cathedral are the **Keepers of the Static**, a biomass evolved to exist in total suspension.

The primary lifeforms are the great **Riveted Ferns**. They are not plants in the terrestrial sense; they are closer to living filters, anchored deeply into the steel plating of the floor. Their stalks are metallic, composed of a biological iron-mimetic compound that matches the hull plating perfectly, cold and hard to the touch. Only at the fronds does the biology soften, exploding into translucent, gelatinous leaves that pulse with a slow, rhythmic bioluminescence. They do not photosynthesize in the traditional sense; instead, they respirate electricity. The microscopic hairs on their leaves reach out to brush against the copper wiring woven through the water, drinking the voltage that leaks from the dormant machinery. They grow in the exact shape of the heat vents they cover, a perfect mimicry of geometry, turning the industrial clutter into a vertical forest of silver and electric green.

Clinging to these giants are the **Glass-Moss Algaes**. These are colonial organisms, billions of microscopic polyps that bind together to form a velvet-like intelligence. They coat every windowpane, every valve, and every piston in a thick, glowing mat. They are the cleaning crew, consuming the microscopic metal shavings and rust particulates that would otherwise foul the water. In return, they secrete a clear, viscous slime that acts as a preservative, keeping the iron forever sharp and fresh. Under the sodium lamps, they shimmer like oil on water, a living skin that maintains the youth of the machine. They move only when the pumps shift pressure, rippling in a singular, fluid wave—a slow-motion applause from the walls.

Drifting through the thicket are the **Spore-Clouds**. These are not simple pollen, but vast, drifting colonies of single-celled organisms the size of a human head. They are gelatinous spheres, translucent and faintly purple, filled with a dense network of gas bubbles that keep them perfectly buoyant. They possess no sensory organs, yet they navigate the labyrinth of steel with an uncanny chemical intelligence. They drift toward the heat exchangers during the night cycles, absorbing the excess warmth, and retreat to the glass ceiling during the "day," soaking in the filtered light. They are the ghosts of the Aquarium, silent, aimless balloons that bump softly against the glass before bouncing away in slow-motion arcs.

The most striking inhabitants are the **Root-Borers**. These are not separate plants, but a parasitic fungi that has bonded with the architecture itself. They grow exclusively inside the hollow pipes and dead conduits of the facility. You cannot see the organism, only the evidence of its passage: intricate, fractal patterns etched into the metal as the fungal hyphae dissolve the iron and replace it with a tough, carbon-fiber lattice. Occasionally, a cluster of the Borers will bloom from a joint in the piping—a flower made of black rubber and copper wire that opens once a decade, releases a burst of heated spores, and then collapses back into the machine. They are the digestive system of the place, slowly turning the ship into soil, even as the Moss preserves it.

There is no predator, and no prey. There is only the **Great Vine**, a single, continuous organism that acts as the nervous system of the Aquarium. It is a network of tubular vines, thick as pythons, that lace through the water like abandoned cables. They possess no leaves and no roots that grip the earth; instead, they float in neutral buoyancy, anchored only by tangling around the great stationary pistons. They pulse slowly, contracting and expanding in a cycle that takes hours to complete. They are not pumping blood; they are pumping the water itself, creating a localized current that circulates nutrients from the floor vents to the ceiling lights. They are the heart of a body that has no shape, a slow, thrumming muscle that keeps the heavy liquid from ever truly becoming still.

And then, there is the silence. It is alive too. It is occupied by the **Vibration Eaters**, microscopic planktonic beings that live in the film between the water and the air. They are invisible to the naked eye, but their presence is felt in the absolute deadness of the sound. They feed on acoustic energy, swallowing the hum of the engines and the crash of the bubbles. In their absence, the High Aquarium would be a deafening roar of machinery; because of them, it is a tomb. They cling to the eardrums of any intruder, a microscopic, harmless hitchhiker that dampens the world, enforcing the heavy, holy quiet of the deep.

This is a society of function. The Ferns feed on the light, the Moss cleans the metal, the Spores drift on the heat, and the Vines move the water. It is a perfect, closed loop, a civilization of plant and machine that requires no witness, built to run forever in the warm, heavy dark.
