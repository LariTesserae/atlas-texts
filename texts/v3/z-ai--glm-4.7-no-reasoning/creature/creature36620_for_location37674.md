To live in the Gyre of Roots is to exist within a paradox: you are buried under the weight of the world, yet untethered from the ground; you are in a place of blinding, absolute light, yet you live in the shadow of biological titans that blot out the horizon.

**The Weaver-Spiders of the Hollow Atriums**

The primary architects of the void are not the plants, though the plants are their canvas. They are the **Silk-Drifters**, massive, asymmetrical arachnids that have evolved to exploit the strange, micro-gravity wells of the root-pillars.

They do not spin webs between anchored points; such static structures are useless here when the "ground" is a spongy, breathing lattice that shifts by inches every hour. Instead, the Silk-Drifters manufacture "balloons" of tensile, sticky protein, milky white and translucent, which they attach to the towering, ceiling-ward stretching roots. They live in the vast, hollow atriums, drifting in the currents of air that circulate between the pillars.

A Drifter appears as a starburst of long, jointed legs—usually six or eight, though some display chaotic radial symmetry—spanning twenty feet or more. Their bodies are small, dense centers of nervous tissue and metabolic heat, wrapped in a pelt of coarse, water-shedding hairs. They float through the bright, haze-filled air like dandelions in a gale, catching the spore-clouds released by the vegetation on their sticky silk sails. They are the silent grazers of the air, filtering the ignited spores before they can settle and choke the void.

They move with a terrifying elegance. When a pressure drop occurs, they do not flee; they retract their sails and plummet, letting the strange gravity pull them toward the safety of a dense root-mesh, curling their legs around themselves to become smooth, stone-like pebbles. When the heat-fungi erupt and the steam rises, they unfurl once more, riding the thermals upward. They are rarely seen, only inferred by the occasional, drifting strand of silk that catches the crystalline light like a suspended violin string.

**The Root-Stitched Nomads**

The "primitive marks" mentioned—the crude huts of woven branches—are not ruins. They are the moltings of a civilization that refuses to become fixed. These are the **Stitched**, the only intelligent life capable of navigating the Gyre.

The Stitched are humanoid, but elongated, their physiology warped by the 0.2 gravity and the climbing necessity of their environment. Their limbs are disproportionately long, with double-jointed elbows and knees that allow them to wrap around the massive roots like gibbons. Their skin is pale, almost translucent, lacking pigment because the concept of "night" does not exist here. To protect themselves from the abrasive, saw-toothed leaves of the canopy-trees, they wear suits of living armor: thick, symbiotic mosses and lichens that they cultivate on their own bodies, turning their flesh into small, mobile gardens.

They are nomads not by choice, but by survival. To stay in one place is death. In the Gyre, "staying" means being buried. A Stitched tribe will weave a hut of dead branches into a fork of a giant root, sleep for a cycle, and wake to find the floor has risen six inches, the walls have begun to sprout fresh buds, and the doorway is sealing over with new vascular tissue. They must constantly move, constantly cut, constantly weave.

They are silent out of necessity. The "1.0 silence" of the Gyre is deceptive; sound carries too far in the humid air, and to shout is to alert the Turbulence. The Stitched communicate through a complex language of taps and scrapes against the hollow roots, utilizing the resonance of the wood to send signals across miles. A single tap on a pillar can be heard by a tribe miles away, a subtle vibration in the teeth.

Their tools—the flint scrapers and copper needles—are not for hunting game, but for surgery. They perform constant, aggressive maintenance on their environment. They prune the aggressive fungi, harvest the exploding seed-pods before they can overpopulate an atrium, and scrape the "parasitic" moss from the breathing-holes of the giant pillars. They are the gardeners of a chaos they cannot control, forever trimming a forest that regenerates faster than they can cut.

**The Scavengers of the Ice-Cycles**

In the brief, violent freezes, when the super-cooled air descends and the condensation turns to fragile art, another creature emerges. These are the **Glass-Walkers**.

They are small, hexapodal creatures that look like nothing more than animated shards of obsidian. They are cold-blooded in the most literal sense; their blood is an antifreeze alcohol that smokes when exposed to the warmer air. They are dormant during the heat, buried deep in the insulating layers of the root-mesh, but when the temperature plummets to 0.2, they surface.

The Glass-Walkers feed on the sudden die-off. When the freeze hits, the outer layers of the giant roots blacken and crack, turning into brittle, nutrient-rich charcoal. The Glass-Walkers skitter across the ice, their sharp legs making a clicking sound like knitting needles. They strip the blackened bark, consuming the frozen sugars and the ash-like remains of the rapid-growth cycle.

They are terrifyingly fast, moving like spilled mercury. They possess no eyes, for the blinding light renders vision useless; instead, they navigate by thermal sense, seeking out the faintest warmth of a buried root-node or the heat signature of a Stitched tribe huddled too close to the ice. They are the cleanup crew, the eaters of the frozen destruction, ensuring that nothing is wasted when the world turns to ice.

**The Pulse**

All these beings share one trait: they are synchronized. They live inside the breath of the planet. When the pressure drops and the low-frequency hum vibrates through the mesh, everything stops. The Drifters collapse; the Stitched clamp onto the roots, hooking their limbs into the bark; the Glass-Walkers freeze in place, mimicking the ice.

They listen to the groan of the earth. They know the rupture is coming. They know the super-cooled air will pour down. They wait for the freeze, for the moment when the light turns blindingly white from the ignition of a billion spores.

And then, when the heat-fungi erupt and the steam begins to rise, they wake. They move, they eat, they weave, they drift. They are the transient ghosts of the Gyre, fleeting sparks of consciousness in a place where life is not a journey, but a seizure—an endless, beautiful, violent fit of biological creation.
