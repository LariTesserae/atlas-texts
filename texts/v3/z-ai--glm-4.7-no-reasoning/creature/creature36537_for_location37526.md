They are not quick things. Quick things die here, startled into heart failure by the silence or dashed against the obsidian shards in a panic to escape the light. What lives here has learned the specific art of waiting. They have learned to be slower than the rust.

**The Stone-Breakers**
The primary architects of this vertical forest are not the plants, but the lithovores. They look less like animals and more like geological anomalies—large, low-slung creatures that move with the tectonic patience of the mountains themselves. They are essentially living tanks, their backs plated with thick, calcified armor that mimics the jagged grey rock. They wedge themselves into the fissures of the plateau, anchoring against the aggressive upthrust of stone.

Their purpose is mechanical. They possess immense, hydraulic jaws that exert a crushing pressure, not to eat the stone, but to grind it down. They process the "arrogance" of the mountains, turning razor-sharp obsidian shards into a fine, gravelly powder. This dust settles into the crevices, creating the soil that the impossible green requires. You rarely see them move; if you sit and watch a boulder for an hour, you might realize the boulder has shifted slightly, or that a new fissure has opened where the creature’s mouth has slowly churned through the granite. They are silent engineers, the unseen force turning raw wound into habitable ground.

**The Glaziers**
There is life in the sky, though it is not birdlife. The light is too bright for feathers; it burns and blinds. Instead, the air is ruled by the Glaziers. They are high-altitude gliders reminiscent of stingrays or manta rays, but with bodies composed of a reflective, chitinous material that mimics the polished quartz of the spires. Their skin is faceted, catching the sun and refracting it into blinding rainbows, acting as a mirror to the "firmament" that rules the place.

They do not flap. They ride the thermals that emanate from the heated rock faces, drifting in slow, hypnotic circles. They feed on the intense solar radiation, their wings acting as vast photovoltaic collectors, but they also skim the canopy to snap up the moss-moths—small, powdery insects that thrive in the damp pockets of the vertical forests. Because they are essentially mirrors, they are nearly invisible to the eye until the light hits them just right, at which point they flare into existence like a sudden sun-glare on a windshield.

**The Light-Weavers**
In the deep, saturated shade of the vertical forest, where the light is filtered through a hundred layers of neon-green fern, live the Weavers. They are the scavengers of the machine-age, arthropods with bodies of burnished copper and brass colors, perfectly camouflaged against the rusted iron of the monoliths.

They are communal creatures living inside the great riveted cylinders. They have spun vast, intricate webs not of silk, but of a metallic, conductive filament they extrude from their abdomens, harvesting the trace copper and iron from the water that drips through the factory's vents. Their webs hang in the dark interiors of the machines like golden harp strings, humming with a low-voltage current generated by the static in the air. They are blind—the dark interiors of the machines rendered eyes unnecessary—but they are exquisitely sensitive to vibration. They catch the small, pale rodents that scuttle through the pipes, ensnaring them in nets that shock the prey into stillness. They are the ghosts of the factory, tending to dead circuits with a hive-mind intelligence that treats the rusted hulks as their hive.

**The Pale-Crawlers**
Down in the damp, velvet darkness where the "sweat" of the day collects in the basins, live the Pale-Crawlers. These are amphibious, neotenic creatures that resemble salamanders but possess the pallor and translucence of cavefish. They have no skin pigment; they are clear as glass, their internal organs and the slow, rhythmic pumping of their three hearts visible beneath their skin.

They are the only creatures that make a sound, though it is rarely heard. They possess a bioluminescent organ in their throats that pulses with a soft, violet light—the same color as the amethyst insulators. They float in the cold, clear pools, looking like discarded lights from the machines. They feed on the algae that slicks the submerged gears and conveyor belts, their small, rasping teeth scraping the metal clean. When they gather in numbers, the pools glow with a faint, underwater constellations, the only softness in a world of sharp edges.

**The Overseer**
There is a rumor of something larger. A silhouette seen only in the periphery, a shape that mimics the industrial silhouettes—standing tall and rigid against the cliff face, motionless for days on end. It is said to be a mimic, a predator that has evolved to look exactly like one of the rusted exhaust pipes or a dormant crane. It waits for the Glaziers to drop low, or for the Stone-Breakers to venture into the open. It is part-machine, part-flesh, a biological paradox that suggests the plant life isn't the only thing adapting to the iron bones of the mountain. But it is just a rumor. In this silence, confirmation is hard to come by. The landscape holds its secrets as tightly as it holds its breath.
