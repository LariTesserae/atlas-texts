To stand in the Ossuary of Glass is to stand inside the lung of a beast that breathes geology rather than air. The things that live here are not inhabitants in the way a bird lives in a tree or a fish in the sea; they are the architecture’s immune system, a cellular response to the hollow reality of the cavern.

At first glance, they appear to be a singular phenomenon—a fluid, black tide that washes over the cyclopean basalt ruins. But as the eye adjusts to the blinding, mineral luminance leaking from the strata, the "tide" resolves into individuals. They are the **Glass-Shell Scavengers**, though that name is a disservice to their complexity.

Their anatomy is a betrayal of biological expectations. They possess no soft tissue, no vulnerable organs hidden beneath a ribcage. They are entirely exoskeletal, composed of the same obsidian and chitinous glass that litters the floor they tend. Their bodies are segmented, sleek, and overlapping plates of volcanic glass, polished by centuries of friction until they possess the same terrifying smoothness as the streets they walk. The light of the cavern penetrates their translucent shells, revealing a dim, silhouette interior where a dense, shifting core of metallic salts and hydraulic fluids does the work of blood and muscle.

They move with a frantic, industrial efficiency. Where a human might walk, these creatures flow. Their locomotion is a matter of legs—too many legs to count easily. Each limb is a jointed needle of black stone, tipped with a claw of diamond-hard tungsten. When they swarm, the clicking of millions of these claws on the basalt creates that deafening, paradoxical silence—a wall of white noise that dampens all other sound. They do not trip or stumble; they are the masters of this terrain. Their legs find purchase on the slick, wet stone where a human would instantly fall. They can scale the sheer vertical faces of the primitive skyscrapers as easily as they crawl across the plaza floor, defying gravity through a combination of microscopic hooks on their tarsi and a communal, adhesive secretion.

They are faceless, or at least, they have nothing recognizable as a face. There is no mouth to feed, no eyes to see the light that burns so brightly. They perceive the world through vibration and texture. The entire surface of their bodies is a sensory organ; the tremors of the tectonic pulses are felt directly through their claws, interpreted as vividly as a scream. They navigate the blinding haze not by sight, but by sensing the magnetic resonance of the metallic lattices that grow like cancerous vines on the ruins.

Their behavior is dictated by the **Pulse**. When the cavern’s light holds steady, they are in a state of maintenance. They dismantle. Using their mandibles—hidden, grinding shears located beneath the ventral plates—they break down the metallic salt formations that threaten to choke the avenues. They carry the debris in specialized abdominal baskets, hauling the shimmering, toxic dust to the voids between the towers, packing it down to create new, artificial ground. They are the compost turners of the mineral world, recycling the corrosion back into the foundation.

But when the light dims and the tectonic shudder rolls through the Ossuary, the Scavengers shift from maintenance to emergency response. The ceiling weeps, and the creatures race toward the impact zones. They do not flee the falling water; they run to meet it. They link bodies, forming interlocking chains of glossy black armor, creating living buttresses to hold up the arches weakened by the shockwaves. They burrow into the mud created by the deluge, solidifying the slurry with a rapid-hardening secretion, effectively "stitching" the earth back together before it can dissolve.

They are genderless and likely immortal in the human sense. There are no young here; no eggs, no larvae. The Glass-Shell Scavengers seem to generate spontaneously from the geology itself. In the deepest crevices of the basalt city, where the heat is greatest and the pressure most intense, one can see the "hatcheries"—geothermal vents where silicate mud bubbles and forms. From this slurry, new, small, soft-bodied shapes emerge, instantly hardening as they touch the cool air, joining the swarm with a pre-programmed understanding of their duty.

There is no leader, no queen in this hive. They are a decentralized consensus, a chaotic democracy of action. If a tower begins to tilt, a million individual decisions are made instantaneously to rush to that side. The "mind" of the colony is an emergent property of their numbers, a ghost in the machine made of stone.

Ultimately, they are a kind of anti-life. They do not eat to grow; they do not reproduce to continue a lineage. They simply exist to arrest the decay of their world. They are the custodians of a freezing entropy, working tirelessly to ensure that the Ossuary of Glass remains exactly as it is: a bright, sterile, fossil of a city that never truly died, maintained in a state of perpetual, dynamic suspension by the billions of glass-clicking feet that tread its empty streets.
