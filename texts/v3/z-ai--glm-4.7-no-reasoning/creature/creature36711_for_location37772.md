To live in the High Roost is to never know solitude, nor silence, nor the meaning of haste. The inhabitants of this ossified cathedral do not merely reside here; they are indistinguishable from the architecture, woven into the biology of the leviathan as inextricably as the moss and the bone. There is no "outside" to them. The calcified arches are their horizon; the internal rain, their seasons; the deafening thrum, their language and their pulse.

**The Graizers: The Great Slabs**
In the shallow divots where the nutrient-rich fluid pools, the Graizers hold their ground. They are the cattle of the High Roost, though "cattle" is too swift a word. They are massive, six-legged slabs of muscle and keratin, resembling a cross between a giant sloth and a liver. They move at a pace that makes erosion look hasty, dragging their bellies—slick with the permanent damp—across the moss and ferns. Their hides are not fur, but a living shag of dark algae and parasitic vines, rendering them indistinguishable from the leathery vegetation until a massive, blunt head slowly swings to strip a frond. They are eyeless; they do not need sight. They feel the vibrations of the structure's pulse through their flat, calloused feet, and they taste the calcium in the standing water. Theirs is a life of patient, glacial consumption, their wet, tearing sounds merging with the wet tearing of a thousand others, a slow, grinding symphony of digestion.

**The Chittering Veil: The Hangers**
Above the Graizers, occupying the vertical spaces—the great arches of ivory and the curtains of hanging vines—live the Hangers. They are primates of a sort, though their limbs are elongated like spiders, and their hands are nothing but prehensile bone hooks sheathed in a thin, constantly sweating membrane. They do not leap; they flow. A Hangar swings from an antler to a vine in a motion that takes a full minute to complete, limbs extending and retracting with a sticky, deliberate grace. They are covered in a dense, black fur that mats into clumps from the internal rain, and they communicate not through calls, but by striking their hollow, bony sternums against the architecture. This drumming resonates through the skeleton of the leviathan itself, a subsonic Morse code that can be felt in the teeth of a creature a quarter-mile away. They sleep in tangled, shivering masses of fifty or more, clinging to the ribcage of the world for warmth, their collective exhalations adding to the heavy, musk-laden fog.

**The Slick Ones: The Shadows Between**
Where the light is scarcest—where the dull red glow of the heat-radiating tissues pulses in the deep hollows—the Slick Ones hunt. They are the apex predators of the Roost, though "hunt" implies a chase, and there is no room for chasing here. They are elongated, serpentine creatures with no eyes and no jaws. Their heads are lamprey-like funnels of cartilage, lined with concentric rings of grinding, translucent teeth. They move like oil through the dense undergrowth, their skin secreting a solvent that silences the moss beneath them. They strike not with speed, but with absolute, creeping inevitability. They will attach to a Graizer's flank and simply *hold*, grinding through keratin and fat over the course of days, drinking the hot blood that seeps out. The Graizer does not run; it simply continues to graze, enduring the parasite, for there is nowhere to go and no speed to outrun it. The Slick Ones are the silence within the deafening choir, the only creatures here who make no sound, moving as extensions of the shadow itself.

**The Breath-Glass: The Micro-Multitudes**
The true inhabitants of the Roost, outnumbering the macro-fauna a billion to one, are the Breath-Glass. They are the insects, though "insect" fails to capture their scale or their purpose. They are the medium through which the larger creatures live. Some are flyers—clouds of winged specks that drift through the humid air, breathing in the carbon dioxide of the masses and exhaling oxygen. They are the Roost's atmosphere, a living fog that fills the lungs with every breath. Others are the crawlers—armored mites the size of fingernails that swarm over the skin of the Graizers and the fur of the Hangers, eating the dead tissue, the fungi, the parasites. They are a tide. If a creature dies, it does not fall; the Breath-Glass reduce it to a polished, gleaming skeleton in a matter of hours, leaving the bones to sink back into the floor, to be absorbed by the architecture, to become the Roost once more.

**The Keepers of the Pulse**
There are rumors of a creature even larger than the Graizers, something that rarely moves and is never seen clearly, a presence felt only in the deepest, slowest rhythms of the bone floor. The Keepers. They sit in the atriums that open into hollows large enough to hold clouds, their forms so ancient and overgrown with ferns and moss that they are mistaken for hills. They are the dreamers of the Roost, their slow, rhythmic breathing matching the pulse of the architecture, perhaps *driving* the pulse of the architecture. They eat nothing; they drink the deep nutrient fluid that pumps from the leviathan's core. They are the high priests of the cathedral, tending to the metabolism of the world simply by existing within it. To stand near one is to feel time slow, to feel the urgency of living drain away, replaced by a deep, bone-level understanding that this place has been here for eons, and will be here for eons more.

***

To be a creature of the High Roost is to accept a fundamental truth: the individual is an illusion. There is no "you"; there is only the we, the throng, the mass. Life here is a continuous, tactile press of bodies, a shared heat, a shared dampness, a shared deafness. You are born into the crush, you live in the crush, and when you die, you do not leave a gap; the Breath-Glass carry you into the bone floor, and the next breath you take is the breath of the Roost itself, cycling through the wings of a billion mites, the gills of the ferns, the lungs of the Hangers. It is not a gentle existence. It is not a peaceful one. But it is an existence of absolute, overwhelming, and eternal *fullness*.
