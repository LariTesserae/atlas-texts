Here, the architect is hunger, and the blueprints are written in genetics and geology. The residents of this crushing pocket are not inhabitants in the traditional sense; they are the tissue of the place itself, differentiated cells in a single, sprawling organism that mimics a city.

**The Masons**

Dominating the skyline are the **Coral-Constructs**. They are not merely animals; they are living concrete. Imagine a reef, but stripped of the chaos of branching shapes and forced into the rigid, utilitarian lines of brutalist housing projects. These are massive, benthic organisms—ancestors of tube worms and coral polyps that have evolved to build upward rather than outward. They secrete a calcium carbonate so dense it chips like stone.

Their bodies are soft, pale sacks buried deep within the calcified towers they have secreted, visible only as a moist, pulsing membrane deep within the "windows" of the structures. They possess no eyes, for they need none. They sense the pressure gradients of the room, the vibrations of the shifting mud, and the chemical signatures of their neighbors. They communicate through the water via slow-release chemical plumes, changing the acidity of the micro-currents to signal distress or the readiness to spawn. They stand like silent, tenement-dwelling titans, anchored to the floor and fused to the ceiling, straining against the geological crush that constantly seeks to flatten them back into the ooze.

**The Scavengers**

Coating the "streets"—the narrow, vertical alleys of water between the towers—are the **Glass-Grazer Swarms**. These are isopods, but evolved for a world of sedimentary density. Their exoskeletons are not the hard, crunchy armor of their surface cousins, but translucent, flexible plates of chitin that fit together like wet silk.

They are the sanitation department of this sunless city. They move in great, glacial rivers across the faces of the coral-constructs, their thousands of hooked legs scraping the "sidewalks" for the bacterial slime that coats everything. They are eyeless, navigating by taste and touch, their mandibles clicking open and shut in a motion that is frantic and microscopic, yet to the observer appears as a frozen stillness. They consume the detritus of the city—the dead skin of the masons, the fallen "bridges" of fungal mucus—processing the waste and converting it into the nutrient-rich paste that feeds the builders.

**The Weavers**

Spanning the gaps between the towers, hanging like wet, suspended cables or the rotted remnants of power lines, live the **Mucus-Weavers**. They are gelatinous creatures related to salps or larvaceans, but grown to monstrous, architectural proportions. They do not swim; they anchor.

A Weaver will extrude a long, incredibly tough strand of protein-based mucus from a specialized ventral gland, anchoring it to one coral tower and reeling it out until it catches on another. Once anchored, they build a net-like dwelling within this suspension line, a tangled, sticky web of bio-luminescent snot that drifts in the current.

They are the passive trappers, the city's electric grid reversed. Instead of conducting energy, they capture it. Any particle of organic matter larger than a grain of sand that drifts through the alleys is snared by the Weavers’ sticky lines. The creature then slowly reels in its line, consuming the catch, dissolving it in a bath of enzymatic fluids. They glow with a faint, sickly green light—the only sparks in the gloom—pulsing rhythmically as they strain against the weight of the sediment caught in their nets.

**The Shadows**

Lurking within the darker recesses, where the "apartment blocks" cluster too tight for the light to penetrate, are the **Ribbon-Stalkers**. These are the apex predators of this micro-metropolis, nemerteans shaped like thin ribbons of black oil. They possess no true form; they are fluid, able to pour themselves through cracks and crevices no wider than a coin.

They hunt by displacement. They slide silently through the heavy water, tasting the chemical trails of the Glass-Grazers. When they strike, they do not lunge; they unravel. Their bodies evert instantly, turning inside out to engulf their prey in a living sack of acidic slime. The digestion is external as much as it is internal; the Stalker simply wraps itself around a Grazer and dissolves it where it stands, absorbing the nutrients through its skin before moving on, leaving behind a clean, white patch of coral shell. They are the silent assassins of the deep, the dark shapes that flit through the peripheral vision of the city, maintaining the cull.

**The Dwellers**

And finally, there are the **Endo-Parasites**, the ones who live *inside* the city's infrastructure. Burrowing deep into the respiratory vents of the Coral-Constructs are colonies of pale, rootless worms. They are the vegetation of this world, the "plants" that grow in the concrete. They anchor themselves in the mucus membranes of the giant masons, siphoning off oxygen and nutrient-rich blood.

In return, they filter the water that the masons draw in to breathe, removing the finest particles of silt that would clog the larger creature’s pores. They are a mockery of symbiosis; they are barely tolerated guests, their bodies extending like thin, white hairs from the walls of the towers, swaying gently in the current. They are the moss growing on the sidewalk, the ivy in the cracks, living a life of passive theft and essential service.

All of them—the builders, the grazers, the weavers, the stalkers, and the parasites—are packed into a single, suffocating room. They have no concept of an outside world. To them, the crushing pressure is the atmosphere, the cold is the temperate zone, and the slowly closing walls are the horizon of their existence. They are a civilization of silence, going about the complex business of survival in a place that is actively trying to erase them, building a history that will eventually be crushed into a single layer of limestone.
