They are a people born of the scream and the steam, high-altitude arboreals who would not recognize "solid ground" if they stood on it. To them, the world is not a collection of surfaces, but a series of velocities—a hierarchy of things falling, things sliding, and things clinging.

They call themselves the *Vash-Kora*, the Root-Walkers.

**The Body of the Clinger**
Physically, they are built to defy the absolute erosion of their home. They are short, dense, and immensely powerful, their musculature developed over millennia of hauling themselves up sheer, slime-slicked granite. Their limbs are disproportionate: arms that hang below their knees, tipped with hands that are not merely grasping but fused hooked instruments of bone and keratin. Their fingers are long, multi-jointed spines, capable of finding purchase in the tiniest fissure of the polished, rounded stone.

Their skin is a marvel of hydrodynamic engineering. It is pale, almost translucent, showing the dark, slow-moving blood beneath—a heat-conserving adaptation to the deceptive 0.8 temperature chill. But over this dermis lies a thick, porous layer of spongy tissue, like peat moss, that constantly wicks away the humidity. To the touch, they feel dry and warm, almost fuzzy, despite the rain that sheets off them. They have no noses; the air is too thick and wet to smell, and breathing requires intake valves—slitted flaps located high on the neck, protected by a bony ridge to filter out the torrential water inhalation.

**The Weavers of the Sky**
They do not live in the valleys; those are the killing zones. They live in the "Vegetation 2.2" zone—the stunted, iron-barked conifers that huddle in the lee of the stone pillars. These trees are the only anchors in a world of fluid motion.

The *Vash-Kora* do not build houses; they knit nets. Using a secreted bio-resin harvested from the giant neon lichens, they bind the branches of the twisted trees together. They create sprawling, complex communal hammocks that sway with the wind but never break. The resin is waterproof and flexible, curing into a substance as hard as the rusted pylons of the dead industry below. These "nests" are hung high in the canopy, draped over the edges of cliffs, looking like dark, waterlogged fruit clinging to the stone. Here, the density is safety. The interwoven branches of the trees (Density 2.0) are mirrored by the interwoven bodies of the people, sleeping in tangled piles for shared warmth and stability.

**Children of the Tide**
Their lifecycle is dictated by the Dynamic 2.9 pulse of the ocean. They do not measure time in hours, but in the rise and fall of the water.

When the tide recedes, exposing the treacherous, slick upper reaches of the granite spines, the *Vash-Kora* descend. They are harvesters. They scuttle across the stone like crabs, their hooked hands gouging the rock to maintain balance. They hunt the "breathers"—the sluggish marine mammals that breach the surface of the submerged valleys. They use harpoons tipped with the neurotoxin spines of the local giant ferns, waiting on the precipices for the dark shapes to rise, their mournful bell-like calls signaling the hunt.

But when the water heaves back up, rising hundreds of feet in hours, they retreat. They are terrified of the "White Froth"—the ch chaotic slurry that grinds the mountains into pebbles. Their mythology speaks of the "Grinder," a spirit that lives in the whirlpools, constantly chewing the stone. They offer sacrifices to it: scraps of metal scavenged from the ruins below, polished and thrown into the vortex to appease the hunger of the water.

**Scavengers of the Rust**
The "Built 0.6" scars of the ancient factory complex are holy grounds to them. The *Vash-Kora* possess a primitive but acute understanding of mechanics. They venerate the Great Vent, the geothermal tower in the center of the complex. They believe it is the heart of the mountain, the last breath of the earth before it drowned.

Around this tower, they have established a permanent settlement. This is the only place they use fire. The heat from the vent allows them to forge tools from the scavenged rust of the pylons. They fashion elaborate breathing masks and diving bells from the hollowed-out husks of old water-turbines. While they do not understand the electricity that once flowed here, they understand pressure and flow. They have jury-rigged systems of pipes and valves taken from the factory to direct the boiling steam into their living nests, creating pockets of warmth in the freezing, wet air.

**A Culture of Silence and Scream**
Their language is not spoken, for the Sound 2.0 parameter—the roar of the million waterfalls—makes speech impossible. They communicate through a combination of intricate sign language (performed with extreme exaggeration to be seen through the fog) and a complex series of rhythmic taps on their own bodies and the metal pipes of their settlement. They "listen" with their feet and their hands, feeling the vibrations of the rock. A crack in a cliff face miles away can be felt as a tremor through the granite, signaling a landslide long before it can be heard.

Their music is the sound of the storm. They stretch cured hides across the open metal frames of the ruined factory, turning the broken gantries into massive, aeolian harps. When the polar wind whips through the skeletal ribs of the industry, the structures sing—a low, haunting, metallic moan that vibrates in the chest, adding a counterpoint to the roar of the rain.

**The Final Image**
To see a *Vash-Kora* is to see a creature of pure adaptation. They are neither wholly of the rock nor of the tree. They are silhouettes against the gray mist, wrapped in furs woven from water-shedding moss, faces obscured by respirators cobbled from ancient tech. They hang suspended over the abyss, clinging to the wet, iron-hard bark of a tree, while the ocean rises and falls beneath them, grinding the mountains into dust, indifferent to their existence, yet unable to dislodge them.

They are the inheritors of the drowning. They do not wait for the sun to return; they have forgotten the sun. They know only the rain, the steam, and the thrum of the living earth.
