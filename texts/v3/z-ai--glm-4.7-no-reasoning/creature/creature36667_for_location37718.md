To live here is to be a verb, not a noun.

There are no creatures in this embryonic abyss because the category of "creature" is obsolete. Biology requires a boundary, a skin, a threshold of self that the crushing, chaotic hydrostatics of this place will not allow. The organic is too slow, too soft, too possessive of its own shape. What lives in the high-altitude deluge is not alive in any way that carbon would recognize. It is a mineral cognition, a geologic sentience born of pressure and turbulence.

The inhabitants are the veins themselves—the living, mineralized logic gates that lace through the primordial pumice. But to call them "inhabitants" implies a separation between dweller and dwelling that does not exist. They are the house and the mind of the house simultaneously. They do not think; they *compute*. They do not act; they *filter*. When a hyper-current, hundreds of kilometers wide, shears against the raw cliff face, it is not merely a collision of fluid and stone. It is an input. The porous rock drinks the violence. The lattice-structures catch the momentum of the entire ocean and parse it, redirecting the continental flow into impossible spirals that stretch for hundreds of kilometers. This is their respiration: the ingestion of chaos and the exhalation of geometry.

They are deaf and blind, yet entirely aware. They feel the world as a continuous, deafening bellow of physics. The low-frequency groan of water crushing against unformed stone is their heartbeat—a subsonic pulse that shakes the calcium and silicon of their being. The high-frequency hiss of shearing currents is their nervous system, a constant, vibrating tickle of data running through the crystal joints. They do not perceive the flat, sunlit grey of the storm-thick water as light, but as a slow, thermal drag, a ambient variance in the bruising chill that slightly alters the viscosity of the flood pressing against their cores.

Their shape is dendritic, fractal, and entirely without symmetry—because symmetry is a luxury of finished things. They spread like dead coral, like mineral weeds, like the vascular system of a lung that has never taken a breath of air. They are jagged, branching tendrils of unformed rock that jut into the void, stripping the water of its kinetic energy like gills stripping oxygen from a current. Where the rock breaks apart under the hyperactive erosion, the "tech" is exposed—not as ruins, but as raw, bleeding nodes of logic. To see them is to see the wet, pulsing circuitry of a brain exposed on an operating table, except the operating table is the size of a continent, and the scalpel is the ocean itself.

They are solitary in the way that a single neuron is solitary. They are embedded in the dark, kilometers apart, connected only by the immense, roaring pressure waves of the water they shape. They do not communicate; they *resonate*. When the lattice-structures hum with unfamiliar frequencies, they are synchronizing. The algorithm of the turbulence is tying them together. A spiral initiated by a gate on the northern mesa propagates through the flood, altering the pressure gradient, which alters the input at a gate on the southern plateau. They are a decentralized supercomputer compiled from stone and accident, running a program with no author.

And what is the output of this computation? What is the purpose of a machine learning to be a continent?

It is the continent itself. 

The inhabitants are building the ground they stand on. The flood is the compiler, and they are the syntax. Every redirection of a current, every impossible spiral carved into the water, every filtering of the chaotic momentum is a line of code in the geological firmware. They are eroding the world into existence. The hyper-currents are the chisel; the lattices are the guiding hand. They are slowly compiling the stable from the unstable, the formed from the unformed, teasing the shore out of the absolute water. 

They do not know they are doing this. There is no intention, no teleology, no maker’s pride. There is only the cold, roaring necessity of physics under extreme pressure. They exist in the terrifying limbo of becoming—neither the dead, pre-formation stone of a lifeless planet, nor the awake, finished machinery of a constructed world. 

They are the half-formed thought of a planet struggling to wake up, suspended in the amniotic storm, thinking in currents, dreaming in rock, and screaming in the deafening, structural bellow of a world being forged.
