The thing that lives here is not a creature of comfort, nor is it a beast of burden. It is a manifestation of stubbornness, a biological anchor in a universe of fluid chaos.

It hangs inverted from the rotting underside of the wooden platform, gripping the rough-hewn timber with hooked extremities that are sharp enough to carve into the grain of the wood. It is roughly the size and mass of a medium-sized dog—a terrier or a beagle—but there the similarity ends. Its morphology is dictated by the crushing density of the water below and the biting cold of the air above.

The creature is an invertible glass crab. Its carapace is not a shell of calcium carbonate, but a bell-shaped dome of transparent, industrial-grade silica. Through this clear helmet, the observer can see the wet, pink machinery of its life: a heart that beats with a slow, deliberate thud, distinctive from the frantic rhythm of the water; lungs that resemble crumpled wet paper, expanding and contracting in the thin, chilly air; and a digestive tract that is currently empty, a long, pale tube waiting for sustenance.

Its color is the absence of color. It is perfectly clear, save for the faint, blue tint of oxygenated blood pulsing through its veins. When it moves, it does so with the fragmented hesitation of a broken projection, snapping its claws from one position to another without traversing the space in between. This is not teleportation; it is simply moving too fast for the "normal" friction of the air to register on the human eye, a trait that serves it well in the hyper-pressurized currents below.

It possesses ten limbs, though they are specialized. The back four are hooked anchors, driven deep into the wood of the platform. These are the primary grippers, locking the creature in place against the seismic vibrations that travel up the rock. The front four are long, articulated scythes, composed of a metallic chitin that rings like a tuning fork when struck against the stone. The front two are sensory pedipalps, waving gently in the damp air, tasting the spray for the chemical signature of crushed bone or minced meat.

It is blind. The space is too bright, too chaotic, and too full of refracting turbulence for eyes to be of use. Instead, it "sees" through the soles of its feet and the tips of its antennae. It feels the world as a symphony of pressure gradients. It knows the position of every bubble in the vent, the trajectory of every piece of ironweed, and the moment the standing shockwave at the ceiling begins to collapse.

Its temperament is one of absolute, stoic patience. It is a creature that has evolved to wait for disasters. It does not hunt; it collects the results of violence. When the boiling updraft smashes a school of smaller, unfortunate organisms against the jagged stone walls, or when a piece of debris breaks loose and tumbles down the vortex, this glass guardian simply extends a scythe and snags the wreckage from the froth.

It breathes the damp, freezing air that gathers in the microscopic pockets above the waterline, filtering out the salt spray with dense, sieve-like gills hidden under the carapace. It is cold to the touch, its body heat leached away by the hyper-efficient thermal exchange of the geode, giving it the tactile sensation of a piece of meat that has been forgotten in a freezer.

It is alone, the apex predator of a volume no larger than a cathedral nave. It does not call for a mate, for the roar of the water (2.8) would drown out any sound it could make. It exists in a state of solitary vigil, a transparent eye watching the birth of an ocean, waiting for the turbulence to feed it.
