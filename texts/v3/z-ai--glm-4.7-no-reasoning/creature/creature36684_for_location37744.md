Here, in the Sunken Ossuary of Verdure, you do not walk upon the earth; you tread upon the dermis of a sleeping titan. The inhabitants of this place are not merely residents; they are parasites, architects, and antibodies existing in a tenuous, symbiotic stasis.

**The Incisors: The Root-Weavers**
The primary architects of this hollow are the **Root-Weavers**, a caste of humans adapted to the high-voltage, low-humidity environment. They are gaunt, nearly translucent beings, their skin stretched tight over high-density bones to minimize moisture loss. Their eyes are covered by protective, metallic visors that interface directly with the city’s optical data streams, shielding them from the ceaseless pale-white glow of the ceiling-bulbs.

They move through the city on magnetic boots, clanking softly along the gantries that straddle the root-columns. They do not build; they excavate. Armed with thermal lances and spinning bone-saws, they perform the daily surgery required to keep the city alive. They are constantly cauterizing the advancing edges of the "rooms," burning back the healing flesh of the world to prevent the city from being swallowed whole. Their hands are stained black with carbon and green with the antibacterial paste they spread on every fresh cut. To them, the world is a hostile patient that must be kept sedated and open, a constant, bloody negotiation between survival and suffocation.

**The Grazers: Copper-Gut Sloths**
Moving with a geologic slowness along the catwalks and the massive, vertical avenues are the **Copper-Gut Sloths**. These are the dominant megafauna, massive six-sided beasts armored in plates of keratin that resemble rusted iron. They are peaceful giants, singular in their purpose, ignoring the humans unless cornered.

Their mouths are biological horrors—industrial grinders filled with teeth made of enamel-coated iron. They do not graze on the soft, pale root-hairs of the floor; instead, they feed on the city itself. You will see them clamped onto the heavy-duty pipes stapled into the walls, grinding through insulation and guzzling the conductive fluids within. Their diet is almost entirely metallic; they consume the copper wiring, the solar collectors, and the conduit casings to fuel their own internal electrolytic batteries. As they feed, their spines flicker with absorbed charge, casting a nervous, stuttering shadow over the Weavers below. The city attempts to shock them off, but the Sloths have evolved rubberized, calloused pads on their tongues, turning them into unstoppable, insulated thieves of energy.

**The Scavengers: Membrane Skimmers**
In the open air between the root-columns, where the spores drift like silent snow, glide the **Membrane Skimmers**. These are avian-like creatures, but they possess no feathers, scales, or skin. They are essentially raw, exposed muscle wrapped in a thin, clear film of mucus, allowing one to see the pulsing organs and the metallic nodules of their digestive tracts beneath.

They are silent flyers, riding the thermal currents rising from the planetary vents. Their beaks are long, hollow needles. They swarm the Copper-Gut Sloths during the beasts' long resting periods, harrying them not to kill, but to feed. The Skimmers land on the Sloths' armored hides, probing the joints for the rich, metallic ticks and lice that feed on the beasts' waste. In return, the Skimmers keep the Sloths clean of parasites that might otherwise interfere with their electrical conductivity. It is a gruesome, mutualistic dance—a razor-thin membrane landing on living steel.

**The Defenders: Static-Wasps**
The city’s automated defense system is biological rather than mechanical, comprised of **Static-Wasps**. These are fist-sized insects with wings made of vibrating, razor-sharp crystal. They are hive-mind creatures, bred and maintained by the Weavers to protect the precious siphons and regulators.

They do not sting; they discharge. A Static-Wasp will dive-bomb a grazer getting too close to a critical power line and detonate a bio-electric capacitor in its abdomen. The explosion is small—a sharp *crack* like a whip—and smells of ozone, but it is enough to startle even the thick-hided Sloths. Swarms of them hover around the periphery of the inhabited zones, a living fence of buzzing voltage, ready to sacrifice their bodies to maintain the integrity of the incision.

**The Invisible: Wall-Mites**
Deep within the fibrous walls live the **Wall-Mites**, tiny, crab-like organisms that burrow into the "skin" of the world. They are blind and deaf, sensing only vibration and electromagnetic flux. They eat the dead root-hairs and the desiccated bark that flakes off the ceiling. They are the decomposers of this dry realm, turning the dust of the titan into the fine, spongy soil that the Weavers walk upon.

Though they are small, they are dangerous in numbers. If a Weaver stands still for too long, the Mites will emerge, sensing the bio-electric rhythm of a human heart. They will attempt to burrow into the soles of the magnetic boots or the fabric of the suits, seeking the minerals and salts within. They are the reason the city never truly sleeps; the constant *thrum-hiss* of the regulators is as much to keep the Mites at bay as it is to breathe.

**The Denizens of the Deep**
Finally, there are rumors of things that live in the gloom below the catwalks, in the "Basin" where the light fails and the root-hairs are thick and matted. These are the **Deep-Dwellers**, pale, eyeless humanoids who have severed their connection to the city’s scaffolding. They have slipped between the ribs of the world, living in the negative space. They are said to no longer need the regulators; they have learned to breathe the raw, unfiltered voltage of the planet’s nervous system directly through their skin. They are rarely seen, but sometimes, when the generators flicker, you can hear their singing—a harmonic, electric resonance that vibrates in the teeth of everyone on the gantry, a reminder that the city is a guest here, and the host is slowly waking up.
