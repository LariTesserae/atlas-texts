Here, in the bowels of the Deep Canopy, the concept of "individual" is a lie. To survive is to be part of the circuit, to be a component in the vast, grinding architecture of the dark. The dwellers of this place are not distinct peoples so much as specialized subsystems, evolved to exploit the grotesque abundance of heat, metal, and pressure.

They are the wet things. They are the lit things.

**The Architect-Moths**
First, there are the harbingers of motion in the gloom. You do not see them with your eyes—you feel the disturbance in the air, the static charge that raises the hair on your arms. These are the Architect-Moths, creatures with wingspans that rival the gliders of the old world. Their wings are not paper; they are hardened sheets of chitin that mimic the rusted steel plating of the Engines. They do not flap so much as they vibrate, riding the thermal updrafts from the superheated vents.
Across their backs, rows of bioluminescent nodules pulse in complex, rhythmic patterns. They are not signaling for mates; they are broadcasting. They read the magnetic flux of the copper webs, tuning their bio-electricity to match the hum of the grid. They lay their eggs inside the hottest, most high-voltage junction boxes. Their larvae, known as *Copper-Grubs*, are armored, blind things that feed exclusively on the thick, insulating rubber of the cables, stripping the wires to expose the naked energy beneath, which they drink through conductive mandibles. They are the forest’s pruning shears, ensuring no wire grows too old, no circuit becomes too stagnant.

**The Blind Walkers**
On the floor, where the stone is slick and the air is thick enough to choke, live the maintenance crew. They are hexapods, towering and stoop-shouldered, their skin the color of wet basalt. They possess no eyes, for the Black Canopy renders sight useless. Instead, their heads are dominated by massive, complex sensory pits—shark-like ampullae of Lorenzini adapted for air. They "see" the world as a map of voltage gradients.
A Walker can detect the flicker of a single neuron firing in a swimming fish ten meters away. They can taste the fear of a prey animal as a spike in electrical potential. They communicate not by sound, but by clicking their claws together in a way that produces sharp, piezoelectric sparks, a language of flashing light in the magnetic spectrum. They are the scavengers and the sentinels, swarming over leaking pipes to patch the breaches with secreted resins that harden into ceramic, driven by an instinctual need to maintain the flow of the heat that warms their eggs.

**The Lake-Weavers**
In the vast, boiling basins of tepid black water, the true titans dwell. These are the Lake-Weavers, leviathans that resemble nothing so much as a collision between an eel and a power station. They are immense, sinuous muscles wrapped in thick, scarred hide that serves as insulation against the boiling water and the stray currents.
They do not swim; they drag themselves through the murk on limbs that are part tentacle, part hydraulic piston. Along their flanks, rows of dorsal spines act as grounding rods, constantly discharging the excess bio-energy they accumulate. They hunt the schools of blind, electric fish that dart through the superheated water, but they are also farmers. The Weavers cultivate vast, hanging gardens of sulfur-eating bacteria from the ceiling of the lakes, using their own bodies as the scaffold. When they die, their bodies collapse into massive, reef-like skeletons of cartilage and metal, becoming the foundation for the next generation of fungal shelves.

**The Shrouded Ones**
Then, there are the things that live *on* the others. Symbiosis is mandatory here. Clinging to the underbellies of the Walkers and the backs of the Leviathans are the Shrouded Ones—small, simian shapes with wet, velvet fur. They are the groomers of the deep. They have developed a resistance to the scalding heat, their blood running hot and fast. They move in frantic, nervous bursts, cleaning the parasites from the hides of the giants, harvesting the glowing blue moss that grows in the crevices of the larger beasts' armor.
In return, they are granted protection from the larger predators. But they have a purpose beyond grooming: they are the tinkerers. With opposable digits that are surprisingly delicate, they manipulate the small valves and gears of the mid-level machinery. They cannot comprehend the Engines, but they can feel the rhythm of the pressure. They tap on the pipes, listening to the flow, tightening a bolt here, loosening a vent there. They are the tiny, nervous consciousness of the machine, the white blood cells of the iron god.

**The Iron-Fungus**
Finally, there is the flora that bleeds. The "electric-synthesizing" plants are not passive; they are predatory and invasive. The most prevalent is the Wire-Vine, a black, vascular plant that pulses with a sickly violet light. It does not root in soil; it roots in iron. It sends microscopic drill-tendrils into the hull of the ancient machines, drinking the lubricants and siphoning the electrical current directly from the grid.
It grows rapidly, forming thick, suffocating nets that bridge the gaps between the pipes and the stone. Where the vine grows, the air crackles. It defends itself with invisible fields—get too close, and you feel the nausea of induction, the hairs on your hands standing on end as the invisible fence of the plant scrambles your own bioelectricity. It is the bridge between the organic and the mechanical, the flesh that eats the metal and becomes the light.

In the Deep Canopy, everything eats, and everything is eaten. The machines eat the earth; the plants eat the machines; the animals eat the plants and the machines; and the slow, relentless decay of it all feeds the roots of the Black Canopy above. It is a single, stomach-churning cycle, lit only by the cold fire of electricity and the dying heat of the pistons.
