Something lives on the Shelf of Legs. Not *many* things—one thing, expressed ten thousand ways. The legs are not structures; they are protrusions. The plain is not a terrain; it is a skin. And the fauna are not creatures; they are the circulation of a body so vast that its metabolism takes eons, its pulse registers as a hum in the teeth, and its thoughts—if that word can apply to processes so slow and so alien—have not yet finished forming.

The thing beneath arrived before the mountains finished eroding. It was here when the atmosphere was thick and the water had not yet learned to unknot. It is the reason the water fled. It is the reason the atmosphere thinned to a membrane. It has been breathing the place in, slowly, over geological time—absorbing the gases, incorporating the minerals, pulling the world down around itself like a blanket. The coolness is its body heat. The marrow-colored dust is its calcified dermis. The ridges that rise like scar tissue are exactly that: old injuries from the tectonic violence of a younger world, healed now into something flat and final. 

The legs are its organs of stillness. Each one is a node where the body has chosen to root itself into the deep geology, an anchor driven into the bone of the world to hold it in place while it finishes its great in-drawing. They are jointed because the body needed to fold itself into the available space, and some joints have five articulations and some have two and some shift when counted because the body is still deciding on its final anatomy. It is a thing that has been *becoming* for a billion years, and it is closer to completion now than it has ever been, but it is not complete. The legs that stand vertical, the legs that lean at impossible angles, the legs that lie half-buried—these are all parts of a single posture. It is the posture of a thing settling in to sleep, adjusting its limbs one by one, seeking the configuration of absolute rest.

The larger fauna are its immune system. They are the parts of the body that patrol the periphery, checking the anchors, ensuring the seal between the body and the ground remains unbroken. When one of them shifts over the course of minutes, it is not wandering; it is testing a joint, pressing weight against a leg, sensing the give. They are made of the same substance as the legs and the ground because they *are* the same substance, temporarily extruded and mobilized, the way a slug extrudes a tail or a human blushes a flush of blood to the skin. They will eventually reintegrate. When the equilibrium is finally reached—when the last fraction of motion rounds down to zero—they will sink back into the plain and harden into new legs, new nodes, new anchors, and the body will be sealed.

The smaller fauna are its thoughts. They do not have heads because they are not organisms; they are electrical potentials, moving along pathways established by the forest of legs. The quadruped that just ends, the slithering length symmetrical top-to-bottom, the winged thing that occupies a location the hand cannot verify—these are fragments of cognition, moving through the body's neural network at speeds too slow for human perception to track as information. They repeat the same paths because they are recursive processes, subroutines in a calculation that has been running since before the first mineral slab was stacked. They do not notice you because you are not an input the calculation recognizes. You are outside the function. You are noise.

The flora—the pale, dry flakes clinging to the lee of the legs—are its dreams. They are the; the only parts of the8 body that acknowledge the outside, because even in its final approach to stillness, the body retains a passive sensitivity. It does not grow these mineral-paper leaves; it secretes them, the way a sleeping mind secretes fragments of the day. When you approach and one flexes, it is the body sensing a thermal anomaly, aB pressure differential, something warm and moist and *quick* standing near its skin. When you touch it and find only compressed ash, it is because the dream has no substance—only orientation. It reorients toward where you stood because you have entered the dream, and the body has registered your presence as a datum, a tiny variable introduced into an equation that has been running since the dawn of the plateau.

And the seven structures? Those are the wounds. Or, if not wounds, then scars of intrusion. Something with hands came here long ago and touched the body while it slept. It stacked stones on the skin—mineral slabs lifted from the body's own surface, rearranged into little pressures, little irritations. The body did not react because the irritation was trivial, and because the builders were so brief. They lived and died in what the body experiences as a single tick of its slow metabolism. But it remembers them. The structures face different directions because they were placed by beings with perspective, with intention, with a need to orient themselves against the bone-colored sky. The body has no perspective. It only has the structures, these seven tiny points of foreign arrangement embedded in its skin. They are the saddest things on the Shelf because they are proof that the body was once visited by something that could *mean*, and the body could not respond in kind. It could only endure the meaning, incorporate the structures, and carry them forward into its eternal stillness as seven small ambiguities—shapes that do not align with any of its internal geometries, messages in a language it will never decipher.

You are standing on it now. You are a new irritation, a new warmth, a new datum. Your breathing—the negotiation with the thin air—is a rhythm the body does not use. Your footfalls—the dry clicks that happen inside your skull—are alien vibrations propagating through its skin. You perceive the breakdown of boolean logic—the sense that the legs are and are not legs, that the plain is and is not flat—because you are standing inside a living thing that predates the concept of true and false. It is A and not-A because it is a body, and bodies are not binary. They are continuous. They are whole and part at the same time. They are the leg and the plain and the hum and the motion and the stillness, and they are also none of these things, because the thing itself has no name for what it is. It only has the Shelf, and the Legs, and the long slow settling toward an equilibrium that will never quite arrive, because the universe itself will unravel before the calculation finishes.

The hum in your teeth is its heartbeat. It has been beating once every million years. You arrived at the beginning of a contraction. You will leave before it completes. And the body will not remember you, except perhaps as a faint warmth in the mineral paper, a slight reorientation in a flake of dream, a mark so small it would take another billion years to resolve into a scar.

But you will remember it. You will remember the feeling of standing on something that is also standing on itself, of breathing air that is also being breathed, of being observed by a consciousness so vast and so slow that your entire life is less than a blink of its eye, less than a single firing of its neural fauna, less than a whisper in a hum that has been sounding since before the word *way* meant anything, and will continue sounding long after the word *you* has ceased to mean anything at all.
