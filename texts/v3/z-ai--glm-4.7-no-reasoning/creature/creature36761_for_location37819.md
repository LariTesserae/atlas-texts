They are the Masters of the Dry Hum, the silent architects of a permanence that does not require rest. To call them "fauna" is a disservice to their complexity; to call them "machines" ignores the wet, biological reality of their pulsing organs. They are the monitoring nodes of the Green Factory, the immune system of the Continental Engine. They are the **Silici-Carnivores**, beings evolved not to hunt blood, but to hunt heat and entropy.

The most common of these are the **Lens-Walkers**.

They stand upright, vaguely humanoid in silhouette but stretched and elongated, standing perhaps eight feet tall at the shoulder. Their bodies are composed of a pale, translucent keratin that resembles frosted glass, allowing a voyeuristic glimpse of the workings within. You can see the thick, sluggish pumping of their cooling fluids—bright green and oily—moving through a vascular system that runs the length of their limbs.

Their heads are dominated not by brains, but by a single, massive, composite eye. This eye is a parabolic mirror, a dish of polished chitin designed to catch the blinding, bleached radiation of the sun. They do not look at the world; they look for *fluctuations*. They are living spectrometers, wandering the endless grids of the Living Iron to detect "glitches" in the metabolic rate of the floor. When they find a patch of ground that is blooming too fast, or a section of wall that is radiating too much heat, they extend a proboscis—not to feed, but to inject a coagulant, a biological "off-switch" that forces the tissue into dormancy. They are the editors of this world, pruning reality with surgical dispassion.

Moving lower to the ground, slipping through the spaces between the root-pillars, are the **Hydra-Scuttlers**.

If the Lens-Walkers are the managers, the Hydra-Scuttlers are the janitors. They resemble nothing so much as an exposed nervous system armored in carapace. They have no central body, only a cluster of three to five prehensile, armored tails fused at a central ganglion. Each segment of their body is covered in minute, vibrating filaments that allow them to "taste" the texture of the air. In the near-silence of this place, where sound is absorbed by the dry sponge of the atmosphere, they navigate entirely through vibration.

They scuttle across the polished, braided-hemp floors on legs of hardened bone. They seek the "waste"—organic detritus, the rare falling leaf, or perhaps a Lens-Walker that has finally succumbed to years of high-radiation exposure. They do not eat with mouths; they possess ventral ports that secrete a solvent capable of dissolving biological matter down into a nutrient paste. They then press their vents against the living floor, injecting this slurry back into the world’s hydraulic system. Nothing is wasted here. Even the janitors are fuel pumps.

But there are larger things, things that define the true strangeness of this place. High above, clinging to the "chimneys"—the massive, exhaling tree-towers that scrape the stratosphere—live the **Mantella-Kites**.

These creatures do not fly; they do not have wings in the traditional sense. They possess vast, triangular membranes of skin stretched between elongated, hollow bones, membranes that are saturated with photovoltaic cells. They hang from the upper ridges of the chimneys like bats, but they face *upward*, turning their bellies to the searing white sky. They are solar gliders, tethered to the architecture by hooked claws made of iron-hard enamel.

They are the scavengers of the upper atmosphere. They feed on the spore clouds expelled by the chimneys, filtering the dense pollen through comb-like teeth that crack the spores open to extract the lipids inside. Their metabolism is a furnace; they hum with a low, barely perceptible vibration that matches the shudder of the chimneys they cling to. To see a Mantella-Kite launch itself is to witness a piece of the sky falling. They drop into the deep canyons of the Green Factory, silent as falling dust, gliding for miles to find a new thermal, their bodies glowing faintly with the excess energy they cannot consume.

Finally, there are the **Weavers**.

Rarely seen, the Weavers are the glue that holds the biological and the mechanical together. They resemble spiders, but the size of draft horses. Their legs are hypodermic needles of pure calcification. They do not spin silk; they spin *tubing*. They move along the seams where the "vegetation" meets the "architecture," stitching together tears in the living iron with fibers they extrude from their own spinnerets.

These fibers are not simple webs; they are mycelium-infused optic cables. When a Weaver repairs a breach in the wall, it is not just mending a hole, it is re-establishing a data connection. You can watch a Weaver work, its multiple eyes darting in different directions, assessing the "density" of the tissue. It will insert its legs deep into the spongy, green floor, tapping into the hydraulic lines to draw up the raw sap, which it then chemically alters inside its bloated abdomen and extrudes as a shimmering, conductive filament.

They are the electricians of the flesh. If you were to touch a cable freshly spun by a Weaver, you would not feel the stickiness of glue, but the static shock of a nervous system rebooting.

All of them—the stalking Lens-Walkers, the scavenging Hydra-Scuttlers, the soaring Mantella-Kites, and the industrious Weavers—are dry. They possess no sweat, no saliva, no tears. They are sealed systems, perfectly adapted to a world that has moved past the need for water. They move with the hydraulic slowness of heavy machinery, their joints lubricated by oils rather than humors.

They do not fear the silence; they enforce it. They do not fear the light; they embody it. In this digestion of a world, they are the enzymes, active and aware, tirelessly ensuring that the Green Factory continues to eat the sun.
