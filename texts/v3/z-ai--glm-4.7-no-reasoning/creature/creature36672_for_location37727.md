They are the **Shard-Tenders**, the Lithic Children, the Unblinking Sentries of the Upward Drift.

To call them "beings" is a biological inaccuracy. They possess no pulse, no breath, and no internal fluids to freeze or boil. They are architectural entities, constructs of necessity that evolved—or were engineered, though the distinction is meaningless here—from the very geology of the screaming archipelago. They are the local response to the frequency: they do not just live in the Drone; they *are* the Drone given form.

At first glance, a Shard-Tender is indistinguishable from the obsidian scenery. They stand tall, perhaps nine or ten feet in height, and their silhouette is jagged and irregular, mimicking the cruel lines of the basalt spires. Their bodies are composed of a dense, dark mineral composite, rough to the touch like pumice, but shot through with veins of conductive metal—copper, silver, and ores that do not exist on any periodic chart tied to gravity.

They do not walk so much as they *oscillate*. Movement for them is a matter of sympathetic resonance. By attuning their internal frequency to the hum of the ground, they induce a micro-levitation, sliding across the razor-sharp ridges without ever quite touching the surface. They leave no footprints, only a faint distortion in the silver dust, like the wake of a ship through water, though the water here is made of solid sound.

Their "limbs" are not jointed in the biological sense. They are segmented telescoping shafts of stone and crystal. An arm might extend silently, sliding out from the shoulder in a smooth, grinding motion, lengthening to reach a high branch of the silica trees or to adjust a copper coil on a failing cairn. The motion is agonizingly slow, perceptible only if one watches for minutes, yet continuous.

Where a head should be, there is only a focal point—a cluster of multifaceted, lens-like crystals grown directly into the torso. They do not have eyes in the traditional sense, for there is little light to see and nothing to hunt. Instead, they "see" pressure. They perceive the world through the intense, tactile map of the vibration that permeates the air. To a Shard-Tender, the floating islands are not seen but felt as massive, shifting nodes of tension. The gravity anomalies are tasted as flavor gradients of torque and shear. They navigate the dimension-thin landscape by sensing where the fabric of reality is most likely to tear, moving with a heaviness that anchors them even as the world drifts upward.

They are not a populous species. The distance between individuals is measured in miles, maintained by a silent, telepathic chorus transmitted through the bedrock. They are solitary by nature, bound to specific territories—a single floating peak, a particular cairn-dotted valley. Their existence is a ceaseless labor of maintenance.

They are the gardeners of the impossible. They tend to the silica forests, pruning the translucent, lightning-born branches with tools made of pure, singing glass. When a branch grows too heavy or its geometry becomes "impolite"—threatening to pierce the thin dimensional membrane—a Shard-Tender will be there, severing it with a precise, harmonic strike. The severed limb does not fall; it floats away, adding to the debris field of the sky.

Their primary purpose, however, lies with the copper cairns. These primitive structures are not merely their habitat; they are their charge. The Shard-Tenders treat the coils with a reverence that borders on religious. They wind the copper wire around their own stony fingers, repairing breaks where the vibration has shaken the metal loose. They channel the excess energy of the Drone through their bodies into the towers, acting as living grounding rods. This is why the copper glows with that sickly green heat; it is the waste energy of a living geological mind, bled off through the stone bodies of these silent guardians.

They possess no mouths, and thus they speak no language. They have no need for names or greetings. Yet, they are not without culture. They communicate through vibration. When two Shard-Tenders meet—perhaps as their respective floating islands drift into a temporary alignment—they will stand opposite one another on the edges of their drifting domains. They will extend their crystalline focal points and synchronize their internal hums. The air between them will warp, the dust will dance into complex geometric mandalas, and for a moment, the silence will sharpen into a visible, tangible standing wave. In that wave, histories are exchanged, maps of the floating archipelagos are updated, and the sorrow of a lost continent is shared.

They are immortal in the way that mountains are immortal. They erode, but they do not die. When a Shard-Tender finally succumbs to the sheer, crushing weight of the frequency, when its internal crystalline matrix fractures beyond repair, it does not collapse. It merely stops moving. It calcifies, rooting itself into the ground, becoming part of the landscape. Over time, the silica trees will grow through its unmoving limbs, weaving the corpse of the tender into the structure of the forest, turning the sentinel into a pillar of the very architecture it once protected.

In this place of violent serenity, they are the only constant. They do not breathe, they do not sleep, and they do not hope. They simply *resonate*. They are the keepers of the frequency, the silent shepherds of a world that is forever holding its breath, waiting for a silence that will never come.
