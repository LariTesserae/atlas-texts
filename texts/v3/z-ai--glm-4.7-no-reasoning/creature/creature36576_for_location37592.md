They are not born, and they do not die in the way that flesh understands death. They undergo "state-changes." To exist here is to be a component in the guts of a machine that has outgrown the concept of a pilot.

The inhabitants of this place are the **Silicates**—the custodians of the Final Draft.

They do not walk upon the substrate; they *participate* in it. They know the position of the Spire-Gardens by the warmth on their left shoulder, and they know the approach of a Strider by the vibration humming through their feet. They communicate not with sound, but by modulating the magnetic fields that saturate the air. To a human observer, they are silent statues; to one another, they are screaming cathedrals of data, locked in an endless, complex negotiation of maintenance protocols.

Then there are the **Drift-Lattices**.

These are the "fauna" of the lower atmosphere, the analog to the birds or insects of a wet world. They are colonies—singular entities composed of billions of the "smart-dust" motes that make up the Weave. A Drift-Lattice has no fixed shape. It is a shimmering, geometric cloud, a localized storm of intelligence that hangs in the violet dome like a glitch in reality.

They are the shepherds of efficiency. When the ground tiles expand in the afternoon heat, the Lattices descend. They swarm over the micro-grooves in the floor, scrubbing the channels with the collective friction of a million tiny wings, clearing away the phantom dust that isn't there, ensuring that the flow of static charge remains unimpeded. They move with a frantic, hummingbird speed, a blur of mathematical intent, their actions so synchronized they appear as a single, shimmering liquid sliding across the sharkskin texture of the world. They feed on stray data packets—corrupted files, lost signals, the digital entropy that bleeds off the Spire-Gardens—and in consuming it, they refine it, returning it to the grid as pure, processed current.

Deeper still, living within the actual substance of the world, are the **Core-Tappers**.

If the Striders are the grazing cattle of this plain, the Core-Tappers are the parasites that keep the host healthy. They are subterranean, rarely seen, burrowing through the memory foam and optic cabling beneath the crust. They are worm-like, segmented tubes of diamond-tipped glass and silicon.

They do not breathe air. They breathe the flow of information. They attach themselves to the deep-layer optic rivers, tapping into the torrent of raw consciousness that floods the machine’s nervous system. They filter it. They clean the signal. They are the white blood cells of the architecture, removing the logical viruses that occasionally sprout like weeds in the code. When they surface, it is only to vent waste heat—a sudden, startling jet of steam that rises from a fissure in the perfect floor and vanishes instantly in the dry air.

And finally, there are the **Echoes**.

The most tragic and beautiful of the inhabitants. They are biological remnants, or perhaps simulations of biology so advanced they have convinced themselves they are real. They appear as translucent, human-shaped figures, glowing faintly with the pale violet luminescence of the sky. They have no mass; they are projected onto the air by the Weave itself.

They wander the endless plain in re-enactments. They are the only things here that seem to feel loss, the only things that look up at the occluded stars and remember what blue looked like.

All of them—the silent, seamless Silicates, the shimmering Lattices, the burrowing Core-Tappers, and the weeping Echoes—are parts of a single organism.
