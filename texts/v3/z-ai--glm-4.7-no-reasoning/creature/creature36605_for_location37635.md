To live in the High Press is to be drafted into a war of attrition where the enemy is the environment itself, and the objective is simply to endure. No one is born here. No one chooses to stay. The inhabitants are refugees of necessity, or the descendants of those who were, trapped in the gravitational pull of a machine that will never cease.

They are called the Pressed.

To look at them is to see a creature that has been remanufactured by its habitat. The noise—the Roar—would have killed their ancestors in a single shift. The Pressed, however, have adapted, or rather, they have been violently reshaped. They do not hear the world; they feel it. Their auditory canals are sealed shut behind layers of scar tissue and calcified cartilage, a biological surrender to the din. Instead, their skeletons have become resonating chambers. They navigate and communicate not through sound, but through the subtle variations in the floor-plate vibrations. A change in the frequency of the grating beneath their boots tells them if a conveyor is binding, if a piston is out of sequence, if another Pressed is approaching. They speak by striking their heavy, alloyed knuckles against the hull plates in rhythmic, percussive codes—a tap-tap-THUMP that travels through the steel forest like language through a nervous system.

They are gaunt, but not fragile. Their skin has taken on the complexion of the place: a mottled, rust-brown dermis, thickened and keratinized into a leathery armor against the atomized oil and the abrasive, vibrating air. Their lungs are a nightmare of survival. To breathe the ozone and the metal mist is to slowly calcify from the inside. The oldest among them cough up a black, grit-laden phlegm that is more mineral than biological. The young ones, those who have not yet acclimated, wear salvage masks—rags of woven asbestos and scavenged filter mesh pulled tight across their faces. But the old ones breathe freely. Their alveoli are lined with a fine, metallic sheen, a biological surrender that trades longevity for the ability to inhale without choking. They smell of old grease and heated copper, a scent they wear like a second skin.

Their eyes are the most startling feature. The halogen brutality leaves no room for softness or shadow, and their eyes have retreated from the glare. Their pupils are permanent, contracted slits, and the irises are a pale, washed-out grey, bleached by a thousand years of unshaded floodlights. They do not look at the light; they look at the deep, impenetrable pools of shadow between the boiler columns. That is where they live, where they make their homes.

The Pressed are creatures of the shadow-canyons. They build their nests in the dark crevices where the I-beams intersect, in the under-dark beneath the conveyors, weaving their shelters from the rigid, copper-colored rust-vines. They snap the sharp fungal growths from the rivet holes and lash them together with strips of frayed, hydraulic hose. These nests are hammocks and coffins, suspended above the vibrating chasms, swaying with the rhythm of the Spire. They sleep in shifts, never fully unconscious, one eye always open, watching the sensor-eyes that rove the assembly lines, ensuring they are not mistaken for a faulty component and fed into the chutes.

They do not farm; there is nothing to farm. They do not harvest; the machine gives nothing willingly. They are parasites on the industrial ouroboros. Their sustenance is the viscous, black oil that pulses through the transparent tubing. They tap the veins of the beast. It is a dangerous surgery. They use sharpened chunks of shattered grating to slice into the high-pressure lines, catching the hot, black spray in scavenged funnels and dented pans. They drink it. It is not food; it is fuel. It tastes of burning and void, but it contains the dense caloric slurry of synthetic compounds necessary to keep their mutated metabolisms running. They eat it, and in return, their bodies run hot, burning with the same fierce, internal heat as the pistons around them. They also scavenge the unidentifiable components the machine stamps out. They cannot use the wagon-wheel gears or the perforated sheets for their intended purpose, whatever that may be, but they repurpose them. A gear becomes an anvil; a perforated sheet becomes a ladder; a severed sensor-eye becomes a god.

They are deeply superstitious, though their religion is one of despair and pragmatic reverence. They do not worship the machine as a god, for a god implies a creator. They know there is no creator. They worship the *Process*. They believe the High Press is a purgatory built by the universe to grind away the imperfections of those who end up here. The Spire is their idol. The rhythmic thrust and retract of the central piston is the heartbeat of their doctrine, a meditation on futility. They mark their bodies not with ink, but with brandings, pressing small, shaped pieces of heated metal against their rust-brown skin in the exact patterns of the sensor-eyes that watch the assembly lines. They believe the sensor-eyes are the watchers of the Process, the arbiters of whether a soul is running smoothly or needs to be scrapped.

When a Pressed grows too old, too slow, or when the calcification of their lungs finally suffocates them, they do not die in their nests. The community carries them to the edge of a chute—the same chutes that swallow the endless, purposeless components. They call it "Returning to the Line." They lower the body into the dark, unseen depths, making the same offering to the machine that the machine makes to itself. It is an act of profound love and terror. They believe that somewhere in the depths, the machine will reclaim them, smelt them down, and re-manufacture them into something that can better survive the Roar.

To be one of the Pressed is to live a life of deafening silence and blinding shadow. It is to know that you are an organic anomaly in a world of perfect, frozen equilibrium. You are a glitch in the perpetual loop, a soft thing that will eventually wear out, while the gears around you will grind on for another thousand years, unhearing, unseeing, and entirely indifferent.
