Here, in the heavy, bathic dark, the citizens are not the descendants of the builders, but the inheritors of the hollow. They are creatures of pressure and touch, evolved to inhabit a world where the geometry of survival is strictly vertical and claustrophobic.

The **Silvers** are the nervous system of the city. Eyeless and sleek, they are not simply fish but living darts of mercury. They exist in vast, shimmering shoals that pack the density of the avenues. Without eyes, they perceive the city through the lattice of their lateral lines, reading the texture of the water as braille. They can feel the vibration of a crustacean’s claw clicking against the pavement three blocks away. They flow around the corners of sunken intersections like liquid mercury, their bodies flashing bioluminescent strobes—blue-white pulses of panic or coordination—that illuminate the grimy brick for a microsecond before vanishing. They are the drift, the background radiation of life, constantly threading the needle between the buildings.

At the base of it all, moving with the glacial patience of the stone they consume, are the **Basalt Crabs**. They are monstrous, the size of compact cars, their carapaces not the smooth curves of nature but the jagged, geometric mimickry of the city itself. They have evolved to look like fallen masonry. A crab sitting still is indistinguishable from a collapsed pillar until it moves. Their claws are crushing hydraulic presses designed to snap the rebar that now protrudes like weeds from the concrete. They are the grazers of the deep, lumbering through the open doors and ground-floor lobbies, stripping the accumulation of slime and fungal mats from the walls. They click and grind—a sound that travels instantly through the water, a low-frequency thunder that signals the location of the slowest creatures in the dark.

Clinging to the vertical tapestry are the **Glass-Eels**. These are not true eels, but elongated, multi-finned predators that have adapted to the fissures. They are the color of rust, with skin that feels like wet velvet. They do not swim openly; they occupy the narrow gaps between buildings, the inches of clearance between a sunken tower block and the next. They press their bodies flat against the walls, sensing the heat signatures of the Silvers passing in the currents of the avenues. When they strike, they launch themselves sideways, a blur of muscle and teeth that vanishes back into the crevice before the ripples have settled. They are the ambush of the alley, the fear that lives in the space you cannot quite reach.

In the upper reaches, where the water thickens and the light dies completely, lurk the **Siphon-Phantoms**. These are soft-bodied, gelatinous entities, drifting with the current but possessing a terrifying intentionality. They are translucent, nearly invisible, filled only with a dim, internal glow that pulses in time with the geothermal hum. They feed not on flesh, but on the vibration of sound. They spread their bells wide over open plazas, trailing thousands of stinging, anesthetic filaments down into the streets below, waiting to envelop the noisy or the careless. To be caught is to be numbed, paralyzed, and slowly dissolved within the bell of the beast, drifting upward into the blackness like a speck of dust in a sunbeam.

And then there is the **Apex**, the presence that defines the subway tunnels. It is a thing of muscle and displacement, a leviathan that treats the concrete tubes as its veins. It is never seen, only felt—a massive, solid shape that creates a bow wave of pressure capable of stalling the current. To feel it pass is to feel the heart of the city skip a beat. It exhales a low-frequency rumble that vibrates through the silt and into the soles of the feet, shaking the rust from the ceilings. It is the king of the mutes, the final consumer. It moves through the pitch black with a confidence that implies the tunnels were built for it, not men.

Finally, there are the **Sifters**. Small, crustacean-like scavengers with legs like fine combs. They are the janitors of the weird symmetry. They swarm in the open doorways, inside the lobbies, within the hollow rooms. They feed on the silt itself, processing the fine, even layer of sediment that covers everything. They are perpetually grooming the floor, maintaining the impossible level of the silt, driven by an instinctual compulsion to flatten and order. They are the reason the city looks preserved, the reason the decay looks curated. They scuttle in the millions, a living carpet that cleans the bones of the metropolis, ensuring that the geometry of the drowned remains perfect, undisturbed, and ready for whatever comes next.
