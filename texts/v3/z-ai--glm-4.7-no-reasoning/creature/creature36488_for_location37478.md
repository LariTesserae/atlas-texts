The people of the High Sargasso are not so much born as they are coaxed from the moisture and the moss. They are a damp, folded people, possessed of a physiology that has adapted to the crushing embrace of the wet air. Their skin is the color of the obsidian they live upon—dark and smooth to the touch, but with a texture that seems to repel water, causing the perpetual mist to bead upon their shoulders and roll off like mercury.

They are short and dense, evolved for a life where falling is a constant threat but where the thick air provides a cushion against the jagged rocks below. Their limbs are disproportionately long, arms that can span a wide gap between swaying rope bridges, fingers webbed to the second knuckle to better grip the slick, woven reeds of their architecture. They move with a deliberation that borders on the regal; there is no scrambling in the High Sargasso, for the air resists rapid motion. To hurry is to drown in the very medium you inhabit.

Their faces are broad and flat, the nose little more than a series of fleshy slits designed to filter oxygen from the heavy suspension of water vapor. They do not pant; they gill slightly, the skin of their necks fluttering with the subtle effort of extraction. Their eyes are large, black pools that reflect the golden twilight, adapted to pierce the low, diffused light (1.1). They see by contrast rather than detail, tracking the shifting shades of green and red in the moss, the shimmer of wet stone, and the silhouette of a drifting Sky-Drifter.

They are a people of the "drift." Their clothing is minimal, constructed from the cured, translucent membranes of the floating ray-like creatures they hunt and the fibrous husks of the aerial seeds that drift up from the cloud layer. These garments are not sewn with thread but laced together with thin vines, designed to shed water and allow the skin to breathe the humid air. They wear no shoes; their feet are wide, calloused pads, splayed for gripping the slick, angular rock, and prehensile enough to curl around a wet rope bridge.

Their society is built on the architecture of silence. Because sound dies quickly in the heavy mist, shouting is useless, and speaking above a whisper is an act of intimacy reserved for those touching skin to skin. They have developed a complex language of hand gestures and full-body posture, a silent ballet that is conducted on the dripping catwalks. A flick of the fingers indicates the shifting of the wind; a tilt of the head signals a predator in the mist. Their cities are loud only to the ear pressed against the wood—the thud of a pestle, the creak of wet leather—but to the world, they are ghosts gliding through the fog.

They are scavengers and gardeners in equal measure. They do not farm the soil, for there is barely any soil to farm. Instead, they cultivate the epiphytes that drape over the city, encouraging the growth of thick, spongy mosses that serve as both insulation and food. They tend to the "water-roots" of the aerial trees, weaving them into stronger nets, guiding the growth of the city to reinforce the brittle stone beneath.

Technology is heavy and simple. Their tools are made of bone and stone, for metal requires a fire that is difficult to sustain in the oxygen-poor, wet air. They use massive, counter-weighted harpoons to hunt the Sky-Drifters—leaping from the catwalks in slow, parabolic arcs to plunge bone hooks into the gas-bladders of the passing giants. The harvest is communal; a single Drifter provides meat, gas for heating (siphoned into gut-bags), and a translucent material for windows that do not cloud with condensation.

Their settlements are accretions, grown rather than built. The city (1.6) is a coral of woven reeds and hollowed bones, lashed to the mountainsides with wet leather thongs that shrink and tighten as they dry, though they never truly dry. The structures are dense, layer upon layer, with no clear distinction between floor and ceiling. They sleep in hammocks woven from strong vines, suspended over the abyss, rocking gently in the slow currents of the air.

They are fatalistic but not morbid. They know the stone beneath them is embryonic, that the mountains are still cooling from creation (0.4 erosion). They respect the raw cruelty of the geology. They do not fear the sharp glass of the obsidian; they polish it with their hands and backs until it is smooth enough to rest upon. They are the soft padding on the hard bones of the world.

At night, they retreat into the glowing warmth of their moss-laden dens. The bioluminescence of the flora provides the only light, turning the city into a constellation of green and gold fireflies clinging to the black stone. They sleep in shifts, always listening to the *drip, drip, drip*—the liquid heartbeat of their world. They dream of the deep oceans far below, knowing that their existence is a fragile, lush accident, a temporary lichen clinging to a cooling rock in a sea of sky. They are the Silencers, the Weavers, the people who learned to breathe the ocean and walk on the knife-edge of the world.

They do not look up often. There is nothing above but the fading dark. They look out, into the mist, watching for the slow, gliding shadows of the Drifters, their large eyes unblinking, waiting for the world to drift closer to them. In the High Sargasso, you do not chase your life; you let it wash over you, and you filter what you need from the flow.
