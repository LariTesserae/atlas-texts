In the absolute, blinding whiteness of the Below, where the air itself is thick enough to chew and the light strips the world of shadow, life exists not as a collection of individuals, but as a single, continuous convolution of hunger.

There are no creatures here in the traditional sense. The concept of an "animal"—a discrete entity that moves through an environment, separate from it—is incompatible with a bio-density of 2.6. Here, the fauna are the architecture; they are the furniture, the floor, and the walls, indistinguishable from the flora that suffocates the stone.

**The Great Dermal Layer**

The primary inhabitants are the ones you walk upon. They are the "Ground-Weavers," massive, rope-like organisms that serve as the planet's epidermis. In any other world, they would be classified as vines or roots, but here, they possess a rudimentary, distributed intelligence. They writhe constantly, a slow, muscular peristalsis that keeps the sharp, un-weathered geology of the 0.2 elevation hidden beneath a cushion of green and bruised-purple muscle.

If you were to stand still for too long, the Ground-Weavers would begin to negotiate the soles of your boots. They do not creep; they ooze. They are sensation incarnate, sensitive to the slightest vibration. Their purpose is to cover, to swaddle, to protect the jagged embryonic stone from the sky that does not exist. They breathe through pores that line their length, exhaling a sweet, cloying scent of fermentation that saturates the heavy, wet air. They are not aggressive; they are simply inevitable. They are the tide that never goes out.

**The Drip-Feeders**

Clinging to the upper reaches of the Root-Mass are the Filter-Breathers. They are not distinct animals so much as autonomous organ systems. They resemble slugs the size of river barges, their bodies translucent and gelatinous, allowing the piercing 2.9 light to shine directly through their organs, illuminating the slow, rhythmic pumping of their hearts.

They have anchored themselves permanently into the vascular systems of the massive vines. They do not hunt; they tap. They act as living valves, regulating the immense hydraulic pressure of the plant-as-substrate. You hear them before you see them—the low, infrasound groan (1.3) of their bodies expanding and contracting, a sound felt in the teeth rather than heard by the ears. They feed on the excess sap, the overflow of the forest’s metabolism, and in return, they filter the air, trapping the floating spores and dust, their undersides shimmering with the condensation they collect. They are silent, blind sentinels, glowing like lamps in the haze, completely integrated into the green cathedral.

**The Monitors**

In the claustrophobic tunnels where the density reaches 2.2, there are things that move. They are the Solitaries, solitary organisms that navigate the tight spaces between the woven roots. They are long and limbless, possessing a sleek, lubricated skin that mimics the wet gloss of the leaves. They have no eyes—for what use are eyes in a world of blinding white haze? Instead, they navigate through pressure and taste.

They are the custodians of the 0.1 dynamic. In a world where nothing rots and nothing dies, waste is the enemy of stasis. The Solitaries glide through the mazes, their mouths circular and specialized, consuming only that which has fallen out of suspension. A leaf that has detached from the ceiling but has not yet integrated into the floor is seized by a Solitary. It does not eat the leaf; it swallows it whole, wrapping the leaf in a mucosal cocoon and depositing it deep within the root-matrix to be reabsorbed. They are the white blood cells of this vast organism, ensuring that the biological amber remains unclouded by decay. They are ghosts, felt only as a sudden displacement of humid air, a cold draft in the stifling heat.

**The Architects**

And then there are the things that exist near the stone remnants, the geometric artifacts left by the vanished presence. These are the Shapers. They are small, beetle-like entities, but their shells are not made of chitin; they are made of calcified wood, harder than granite. They congregate around the single stone plinth, worshipping it not with prayer, but with secretion.

They possess a technology score of 0.2, not because they are primitive, but because their technology is biological. They do not carve the stone; they coat it. They excrete a resin that reacts with the constant 2.9 light, hardening into a transparent, diamond-like glaze. They are attempting to finish what the erosion (0.0) left unfinished. They are trying to polish the raw, embryonic world, smoothing the jagged edges of the reality, constructing a shell over the hollow. They move in synchronized, silent waves, a shimmering carpet of intent, forever sealing the world away from the space it has not yet occupied.

**The Conclusion of Life**

There are no predators here. There is no chase, no blood, no violence of survival. The heat (2.6) and the damp (1.3) have cooked the evolutionary process down to its purest form: maintenance. Everything that lives in the Below is a component of a single, planetary metabolism. The Ground-Weavers are the skin; the Filter-Breathers are the lungs; the Solitaries are the immune system; and the Shapers are the scabs forming over the wound of existence.

They do not fear the light. They do not fear the heat. They exist in a state of perpetual, glowing readiness. They are waiting for the signal that never comes, suspended in a checkmate of biology, perfectly adapted to a world that is not yet a world. To look upon them is to look upon the insides of a god that is holding its breath.
