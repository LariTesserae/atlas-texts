The inhabitants of the Green Equilibrium are not discrete individuals in the way that biological taxonomy understands the term. There is no "herd," no "pack," no "colony." The population here is one. If you were to slice a section of the root-mass, stain it, and look at it under a microscope, you would see the architecture of a single, distributed organism that has sublimated the concept of "species" into a caste system of tissue.

**The Architects of Turgor**

The primary inhabitants are the **Woven Columns**. These are the great stalks that define the geometry of the place. They are not alive in the passive sense of a flower waiting to be pollinated; they are active, hydraulic engineers. Their substance is a hybrid of dense, lignified tissue and conductive polymer fibers that self-assembled in the deep past. They possess a collective consciousness, but it is not a mind of thoughts or ambitions. It is a mind of pressure differentials.

Their "nervous system" is the root-board that spans the continent. The Columns do not speak; they regulate. When a zone in the upper reaches grows too hot, the Columns miles away increase their transpiration rate, drawing more water up through the matrix to cool the system. They are the thermostats of the world, constantly adjusting the tension of their cell walls to maintain the specific, suffocating warmth that the equilibrium demands. They do not age. They do not die. They simply shed their outer layers of bark, which dissolve into the nutrient soup at their bases, to be reabsorbed and grown again. They are the custodians of the status quo.

**The Dark Alchemists**

In the absence of sunlight, living at the "ceiling" of the space where the last rumors of light from the surface worlds die out, are the **Phantoms of the Threshold**. These are specialized growths, leaf-like but vast, hanging like curtains in the upper dark. They are the creatures of the "biological phosphorescence" mentioned in the landscape description.

They are hunters of photons. Their cells are evolved to trap the faintest bioluminescent glow rising from below and recycle it. They possess a metabolism that borders on the crystalline. They function as the solar panels of the inverted world, but instead of converting sunlight, they convert the metabolic radiation of the depths. They are pale, translucent sheets, pulsing with a slow, visible rhythm—expanding as they absorb energy, contracting as they pump chemical sugars down into the vascular rivers below. They are the producers of the machine, the great factories that turn heat into matter.

**The Mycoid Weave**

There is no animal scavengers here. There is no decay, because decay implies waste, and waste implies inefficiency. The role of the decomposer is filled by the **Myco-Mesh**, a fusion of fungus and root that has become the physical "soil" of the Green Equilibrium. This entity is not a passive carpet; it is an aggressive, invasive intelligence that exists to reclaim.

Any cell that fails, any piece of tissue that loses its turgor, any stray bit of matter that falls into the system is instantly detected by the Myco-Mesh. It does not rot. It is dismantled. The Mesh sends out microscopic threads that penetrate the failing cell walls, dissolving them at the molecular level and transporting the nutrients back into the circulatory system before the chemical balance of the local water can shift. It is the immune system of the continent. It is the white blood cell of the machine. It ensures that nothing is lost, that the cycle is perfect. It is everywhere, a thin, white film coating the roots, waiting for the inevitable imperfection so it can erase it.

**The River-Hearts**

Swimming in the warm, brown, sugar-rich rivers that permeate the root-mass are the **Motile Spores**. These are the only things in the Green Equilibrium that move voluntarily. They are simple, torpedo-shaped clusters of cilia and nucleus, distinct from the plant-matter only in their ability to swim.

They are the messengers and the repair crew. They flow with the sap-rivers, carried by the current until they encounter a blockage or a micro-fracture in the vascular walls. There, they anchor themselves. They undergo a rapid metamorphosis, their soft tissues hardening into scar-tissue or swelling to plug a leak. They are living caulking. They are born in the deep, engine-chambers of the stalks, released into the flow, and live only until they find a fracture to mend. They have no mouths, because they never need to eat; they are born with a finite energy budget, which they expend entirely in the act of fixing the machine. Once their task is done and they have integrated themselves into the wall of a root or stem, they become inert, structural plant-matter, indistinguishable from the bark around them.

**The Engine-Singers**

At the base of the great Columns, deep in the wet, warm chambers where the hydraulic pressure is highest, dwell the **Cores**. These are the heart of the "engines" described in the landscape. They are not animals, but specialized organs of the plant-matter that have evolved to vibrate.

Their function is mechanical. By contracting in rhythmic sequences, they act as living pistons, driving the sap up the immense height of the stalks. They do not think. They do not feel. They are pure rhythm. They generate the subsonic hum that vibrates through the feet of any visitor. They are the pacemakers of the continent. Their "song" is the synchronization pulse that keeps the pumps of the western stalks beating in time with the pumps of the eastern stalks. If a Core were to stop, the hydraulic balance would shift, and the entire continent would shudder with the correction.

**The Ghost of the Individual**

If you were to ask, "Who lives here?" the answer is ambiguous. There is no "who."

The inhabitants of the Green Equilibrium are **The System Itself**. The Columns, the Phantoms, the Mesh, the Spores, and the Cores are not distinct species interacting. They are organs of a single body that has grown so large it has replaced the ground. They are the lungs, the skin, the blood, the immune system, and the heart of a planet-sized entity.

They do not fear. They do not hope. They do not love. They *maintain*. They endure. They are the biological machinery that has conquered entropy, pausing the wheel of time in a cage of green, wet light. They are the keepers of the eternal, humid now, and they will be here long after the concept of "life" has ceased to have meaning.
