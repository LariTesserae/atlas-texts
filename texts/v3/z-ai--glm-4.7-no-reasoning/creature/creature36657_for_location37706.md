They are the merchants of moisture, the architects of silence.

In the suspended cathedral of the caldera, where the air hangs heavy and dry as retained breath, the dominant movers are the **Keepers**. To the uninitiated eye, they might resemble the lizards of a more primitive world, but that is a deception of their silhouette. They are bipedal, standing with an erect, fragile grace on hind limbs that are long and spindly, adapted for perching on the treacherous, knife-edge ridges of the obsidian formations. Their skin is not scaled like a reptile’s, but polished and smooth, a dull ceramic white that reflects the merciless, distinct light of the place. It is an armor that deflects the heat, keeping their internal fluids precious.

But it is their covering that defines them. They are clothed in dense, coarse plumage that grows in tufts and whorls, resembling nothing so much as the stiff, bristled fur of a primitive boar, or the dried husks of the very sponges they tend. This "mane" runs from the crown of their narrow, bird-like skulls down the length of their spines, bursting into a fan around the hips and tail. The coloration is a masterful camouflage—a mottled tapestry of bruised purple, rust-red, and ochre, allowing them to dissolve into the background of the lithophilic flora when they stand still. Which they do, often for hours.

They possess a physiology of extreme conservation. They do not pant; they do not sweat. They have evolved to seal their moisture inside. Under their chins, wattle-like pouches of translucent, pale skin hang loose, the only part of them that looks soft and vulnerable. These are their cargo holds. When the steam vents sigh their whispering exhalations, or when the temporary ghost-humidity drifts through the ribs of the great Hollows, the Keepers are there. They spread the frills of their mane-fur, the fibers specialized to catch the microscopic droplets of fog, condensing the vapor against the cold of their bodies. They then scrape this collected wealth—with a ritualistic, delicate motion of their foreclaws—into the pouches beneath their jaws. By the time they return to the "skyscrapers" of the dead titan-beetles, their pouches are swollen, bulging with the water that sustains the hanging gardens.

They are not solitary creatures, but they are not social in the way of herd animals. They exist in a dispersed, silent hive. You will see them clustered on the vertical faces of the Hollows, gripping the calcified ceramic of the giant shells with curved, needle-point claws that make no sound against the material. They move through the "vegetation"—the drapings of membranous, gray-green fronds—with a fluid, liquid grace, stepping on leaves and stems that should crumble but hold firm under their weight.

Their communication is a language of stillness and sudden revelation. Since sound travels poorly in the thin, nitrogen-argon mix, and noise might betray precious moisture to the dry air, they do not vocalize. Instead, they speak with light. Within the coarse fur of their manes lie specialized strands of chromatophores. When a Keeper needs to signal a find—a particularly rich patch of condensation, or a threat in the form of a predator from the basin below—its mane erupts into a shifting, rippling display of bioluminescence. It is a silent, stuttering Morse of pale yellows and deep indigos, flashing and fading in the shadowed recesses of the bone-city. They watch one another with large, black, lidless eyes that reflect the golden sun, reading the ripples of color in their neighbors' fur, acknowledging without a nod.

And then there are percussionists. When they are deep inside the hollow shell of a titan-structure, where the acoustics are resonant and tight, they communicate by tapping. A single claw striking the interior rib of the beetle-shell creates a low, resonant thrum that travels through the bone and into the feet of the others. It is a heartbeat language. *Danger. Food. Sleep.* The caldera is filled with this near-inaudible drumming, a sub-sonic rhythm that feels like the tinnitus of the earth itself.

They are the gardeners of this impossible place. They do not water with abandon; they administer drops like surgeons. They approach a wilting frond of sponge-fungus, gently pierce the skin of their throat pouch with a specialized claw, and release a single, shining sphere of water. The plant drinks greedily, and the Keeper watches, its mane flushing with a soft, satisfied violet. In return, the plants offer up their sugary exudates—a thick, sap-like syrup that the creatures lap up with long, sandpaper tongues. It is a symbiosis of absolute dependence; the plants provide the calories, the Keepers provide the lifeblood.

Beneath the Keepers, smaller lives flicker in the interstices. In the dark, damp hollows where the Keepers deposit their waste, or in the shadows between the great armored legs of the dead titans, live the **Scavengers**. These are spindly, crab-like creatures, no larger than a human hand, blind and pale. They are the sanitation crew of the city, scuttling rapidly over the volcanic glass, their bodies multifaceted like mirrors to catch any stray light. They feed on the detritus of the garden—fallen leaves, the dried husks of insects, the occasional carcass of a Keeper that fell from the heights. They are nervous things, vibrating with constant energy, sensing the world through the vibration of the rock. When a Keeper’s claw taps a warning, the Scavengers vanish instantly into the cracks, a silver tide retreating into the stone.

Hovering above it all, near the rim where the air is thinnest, are the **Silencers**. These are flying organisms, though they resemble bat-rays more than birds, with wingspans of delicate, stretched skin that glistens like oil. They ride the thermals created by the steam vents, rarely flapping, drifting like kites. They have no beaks, only long, tubular mouths that unspool to probe the aerial "roots" of the lithophiles—dangling air-filaments that absorb nitrogen directly from the atmosphere. The Silencers scrape the mineral coating from these roots, feeding on the dust of the air. They are the only creatures that dare the upper winds, and they are silent as ghosts. When a Silencer dies, it does not fall; its lightweight bones are caught in the sponge-forest, becoming part of the architecture, another layer in the calcified city.

This is the populace of the warm, still lung. A civilization of fur and claw and silence, living inside the ribs of the dead, worshipping the water that is not rain, holding their breath so the world does not evaporate. They are the rust on the bone, the color in the mist, the heartbeat in the stone.
