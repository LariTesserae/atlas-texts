The inhabitants of this place are not merely living *in* it; they are the mortar between its impossible stones, the oil in its crushing gears. Given the constraints—a vertical, pocket-scale micro-world of extreme height, crude construction, and maxed-out weirdness—the residents must be as paradoxical as the architecture.

Here is what lives there:

### The Gear-Shepherds (The Architects of Density)

The primary inhabitants are small, humanoid figures, standing no taller than a thumb. Their skin is the texture of wet slate—grey, cold, and grainy. They possess four arms: the upper pair are slender and nimble, designed for the intricate weaving of vines into gear-teeth; the lower pair are thick, callous, and immensely strong, used to lever the massive, primitive pulleys that define their sky-high existence.

They do not speak. The sound level here is too high (2.1), a constant roar of grinding wood and hissing steam. Instead, they communicate by drumming on the hollow bones of the walls, creating a rhythmic Morse code that travels through the structure’s vibration.

They are shepherds not of animals, but of *motion*. The ecosystem is dynamic (2.5); the tower is alive and shifting. The Gear-Shepherds spend their lives running along the sharp, fresh edges of the construction (Erosion 0.0), jamming wedges into expanding joints and hacking away the aggressive, fast-growing moss that threatens to lock the machinery in place. They are terrified of stillness. To them, if the gears stop grinding, the altitude will collapse, and the pocket-universe will fall out of the sky.

### The Aerial-Worms (The Passive Fauna)

Because fauna is rare (1.2), there are no swarms of insects here. There is only one other macroscopic creature: the Aerial-Worm.

These are long, sinuous creatures, resembling a cross between a glass eel and a floating orchid. They swim through the humid, thick air (Water 1.9) with a undulating grace, their transparent bodies revealing not organs, but gears and clockwork springs ticking inside them—a biological mimicry of the crude tech that surrounds them.

They are filter feeders. They drift with their mouths open, catching the spores released by the giant, heat-loving ferns. They are fragile and ephemeral; when they die, they do not rot. They simply shatter, like thin glass, leaving behind a pile of dry, silvery dust that the Gear-Shepherds use as a lubricant for the tallest reaches of the spire.

### The "Weeping" Moss (The Aggressive Flora)

The vegetation here (1.8) is less like a garden and more like an infection. The dominant plant life is a moss known as the "Weeping Red." It thrives in the intense heat (2.8) and dim light (1.1), glowing with a faint, sickly bioluminescence.

This moss is semi-sentient. It "knows" the geometry of the tower. It grows exclusively on the load-bearing beams, sensing the stress points. Its roots are sharp and hard, digging into the primitive wood like burrowing termites. The Gear-Shepherds are in a constant war with it, scraping it away, but the moss grows back within hours. It is the primary force of erosion in a place that theoretically has none—a biological counter-force to the mechanical stasis.

### The Ghost of the Unbuilt (The Weirdness Incarnate)

With a weirdness factor of 3.0, there is a third, non-corporeal inhabitant. It is not a ghost in the traditional sense, but a "memory of the future."

Because the construction is raw, new, and constantly emerging (Built 2.6 / Erosion 0.0), reality is unstable here. Sometimes, the air shimmers, and a translucent image overlays the physical world. It shows a room that *will be*—a fully finished, golden hall where the current damp, rotting gear-room stands. Or, it shows a gear *un*-breaking, snapping back together with a sound like a whip crack.

The inhabitants interact with this entity casually. A Gear-Shepherd might reach through a shimmering patch of "future air" to grab a tool that isn't there yet, pulling it back into the present to use on a broken strut. They treat time and causality like just another building material.
