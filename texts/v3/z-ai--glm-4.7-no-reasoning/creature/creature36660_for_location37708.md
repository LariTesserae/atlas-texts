They are the Mason’s Chorus, though they possess no mouths to sing. They are the custodians of the Unfinished, the fever dream’s immune system manifest in rust and clay.

To see them is to understand that "biology" is a restriction this place has outgrown. The creatures here are not born; they are *erected*. They emerge from the fissures between the mismatched tiles like termites from rotting wood, clicking and whirring with the frantic, industrial energy of the Haze-Work.

The primary denizens are the Trowel-Limbs. They are the spider-like automata observed in the periphery, but up close, their simplicity is a lie. Their bodies are asymmetrical lumps of terracotta and iron, held together by the friction of their own motion. They possess no true sensory organs—no eyes to see the weak, bruised light, no ears to hear the silence. Instead, they navigate by vibration and the taste of the humidity. They "feel" the geometry of the world through their legs. They walk in perfect, geometric patterns, spiraling out from the center of their birth-pits, seeking the imperfection.

And they find it everywhere.

A Trowel-Limb will approach a jagged scar in a ziggurat where the raw flanks are exposed. It does not pause to admire the view of the oily, tepid sea. It extends one of its many limbs—some ending in spatulas, others in needle-fine probes—and begins to mix the slurry. They scrape the "sweat" of the world—the beaded moisture that hangs in the heavy, silken air—mixing it with the crushed dust of earlier ruins to create a paste that glows with a faint, sickly heat. They trowel this paste over the raw stone, smoothing out the chaos, trying to force the violent, jagged arrangement of the world into a flat line. They are terrified of empty space. To them, a gap is a sin; a crack is a screaming wound. They will fill the hollow of a missing brick, and if your foot happens to occupy that hollow, they will fill it too. They do not hate you; they simply cannot conceive of a shape that is not a part of the wall.

Then there are the Geodesic Eels. These live in the shallow, oily basins where the dark water washes against the raw stone. They are long, serpentine constructs of jointed copper and slate, swimming through the liquid with a sinuous grace that belies their rigid materials. They have no heads, only a tapering series of interlocking segments that rotate in opposite directions, driving them through the water like a corkscrew. They are the recyclers. When a tower folds in on itself, collapsing into geometric sludge, the Eels slither up from the water to consume it. They do not eat; they *de-construct*.

They crawl up the dissolving brickwork, their rotating segments acting like grinders, breaking down the softening clay back into its component sands and silicates. They render the failure of the world back into raw materials, dragging the slurry back down to the oily depths to be used again. They are the sanitation engineers of the draft, cleaning up the erasers of the god.

Higher up, near the catwalks that drip into the void, live the Haze-Walkers. These are solitary giants, hulking assemblies of ironwood and bone—though the wood is petrified and the bones are cast iron. They move with agonizing slowness along the distant, dripping pipes, their footsteps heavy enough to shake the ground, yet the sound never reaches the ear. They are the architects of the repair, carrying massive slabs of limestone on their backs, slabs that do not fit the local geography.

A Haze-Walker will place a block of granite into a space meant for marble. It will hammer a square peg into a round hole until the hole gives up and becomes square. They enforce the will of the blueprint upon the reality that rebels against it. Where the world turns from wet slurry back into hard stone, where causality loops, a Haze-Walker is usually found, standing like a monolith, holding the fabric of the world together through sheer, blunt force.

And finally, flitting through the micro-climate rainstorms near the ceiling, are the Mortar-Flies. They are tiny, specks of drifting dust given temporary will. They are no larger than a human hand, formed from dried flakes of mud and chips of chisel. They swarm in vast, chaotic clouds, blackening the dim light. They are not builders; they are the counter-agents. They seek out the *finished* things. They land on a smooth wall and etch cracks into it. They carry tiny grains of sand in their legs and scour the polished surfaces, returning the completed work to a state of ruin. They ensure that nothing is ever truly done. They are the agents of entropy, fighting a losing war against the Trowel-Limbs.

To exist here is to be caught between these two forces: the compulsion to build, to solidify, to close the circle; and the inevitable, dripping decay that turns stone back into mud. The creatures here are not alive; they are processes given form. They are the antibodies of a world that is sick with its own incompleteness. If you stand still, the Trowel-Limbs will bury you in mortar. If you move, the Geodesic Eels will dismantle you. And if you try to climb, the Haze-Walkers will crush you flat.

But they do not do this out of malice. In the fever dream of the mason god, you are just another piece of debris that needs to be either cemented into place or ground down to dust.
