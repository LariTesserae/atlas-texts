They do not scurry. Nothing here scurries, for to scurry is to panic, and there is no panic in the Vastness, only the long, slow calcification of survival. The things that live here are shadows cast by giants, creatures molded by the crushing weight of the altitude and the suffocating embrace of the heat.

To exist here, you must either be vast enough to ignore the scale, or small enough to slip through its cracks.

**The Architects of the Air**

First, there are the leviathans. You hear them before you see them—not the screech of a hawk, but the thunderclap of wings displacing cubic tons of humid air. These are the sky-sharks, but "shark" implies a sleekness that these creatures lack. They are flying engines of death, bloated and heavy. Their wings are not feathery but leathery, stretched over bone structures as thick as shipmasts. They possess no grace; they possess only momentum.

Their skin is the color of a bruise, a mottled purple-grey that blends perfectly into the shadowed light filtering down from the canopy miles above. They ride the thermals generated by the rotting floor—columns of hot, rising gas—without needing to flap. They hang in the air like vultures the size of locomotives, their eyes evolved to track movement across the immense, empty grid of the stone plains.

When they strike, it is not a sudden pounce. It is an inevitable collapse. They drop, folding their wings to become a meteor of muscle and beak. They target the grazers on the sides of the World-Bones, and the impact is audible for miles as a carapace shatters and the wet, heavy sound of consumption begins. They are the cleanup crew of the upper air, the lords of the nothingness between the pillars.

**The Bark-Strippers**

Beneath the aerial hunters, clinging to the vertical flanks of the World-Bones, are the grazers. From a distance, they look like moss or a rash on the side of a mountain. As you draw closer, the "rash" resolves into thousands of individual creatures—the bison-beetles.

They are grotesque magnifications. They have the shaggy, resilient coat of a high-altitude bovine, but the body of a chitinous tank. Their legs are hooked and spiked, evolved to dig into the fibrous, steel-hard wood of the trees. They do not walk; they excavate. A herd of them moves across the trunk of a World-Bone like a slow-moving landslide, their massive, fused jaws grinding laterally.

They scrape the bark in long, curling ribbons, exposing the raw, green cambium beneath. They are silent eaters. The sound of a herd feeding is a constant, low-frequency crunch, like glaciers rubbing together, amplified by the hollow trunk of the tree. They have no eyes on the front of their heads; their vision is a panoramic strip on the sides, forever watching for the shadow of a falling sky-shark. They live in terror, but it is a slow, cold terror that accepts its eventual inevitability.

**The Scavengers of the Spongyloom**

On the ground, the "soil" is alive with the detritus eaters. The floor is a spongyloom of compressed leaves, rotting for ten thousand years, radiating a secondary heat that smells of sweet decay. Here, the humidity is absolute. The water does not drain; it lingers.

Living in this steaming mulch are the *Dredgers*. They are amphibious, resembling a horrific cross between a salamander and a pangolin. They are the size of draft horses, but low-slung and flat, covered in scales that weep a mucus to prevent them from drying out in the stagnant air. They have no legs, only powerful, dragging flippers that push them through the muck.

They eat the fallen giants—the leaves, the branches, and occasionally, a bison-beetle that has lost its grip and plummeted. They possess no teeth, only grinding plates in their throats that crush bone and wood alike. They are blind, their faces dominated by sensitive feelers that taste the chemical composition of the air. They move through the fog like ghosts, visible only by the ripple in the standing water and the occasional glimpse of a slimy back breaking the surface of a stagnant pool.

**The Weavers of the Mist**

Between these massive tiers of life—between the roots and the canopy, between the stone and the sky—exist the ephemeral ones. These are the gliders.

Since the World-Bones are spaced miles apart, walking is inefficient. The ecosystem here solved this with evolution. The weavers are creatures that might have once been rodents or primates, but they have been stretched by the wind.

They are not large—perhaps the size of a human child—but their "limbs" are elongated sails of skin and cartilage. They cannot fly like the sharks; they soar. They launch themselves from the highest branches of the World-Bones, catching the invisible rivers of air currents that flow between the pillars.

They live in the air, never touching the ground. They drink water by skimming their snouts through the bowls of leaves that collect condensation. They eat the insects that swarm in the heat—gnats the size of softballs, beetles that glow with phosphorescent heat. They are brightly colored, streaks of neon orange and electric blue cutting through the purple gloom. They scream as they glide, a sound that Doppler-shifts as they pass, a piercing warning that they are fast, elusive, and watching.

**The Echo in the Stone**

And then, there is the question of the "Built." The carvings are empty, the rope bridges rotted away, but the structures are not entirely abandoned by life.

The *Anthill-Makers* have moved in. They are insects, social creatures that build paper nests in the hollows carved by the lost civilization. They treat the ancient flint-tool marks and the smoothed-out sanctuaries as the foundations for their own sprawling hives. They are black and shiny, glistening with oil, and they swarm in thick, writhing rivers along the stone walkways.

They are the new custodians of the high places. They farm a mold that grows on the damp stone inside the carved rooms. They defend their territory with a ferocity that belies their size; to approach the ancient dwellings is to be coated in a biting, stinging armor of tiny bodies. They have inherited the legacy of the builders, turning a place of worship into a factory of slime and paper.

**The Silence Between Them**

But the true resident of this place is the tension itself. It is the slow-motion wrestling match of the trees. The flora is alive in a way that hurts the eyes to watch. The trunks groan—not creak, but groan deep in the wood—a sound felt through the soles of your boots. The World-Bones are pushing against each other, leaning in, crushing the space between them.

In the gaps where two titans press together, life is being extinguished. Caught in the vice of slow-moving cellulose, you can see the fossilized remains of other creatures—ancient grazers, perhaps, or gliders that missed the wind—flattened into the bark, becoming part of the wall. The trees are eating the history of the plain, compressing the bones of the dead into their expanding girth.

The life here is a festival of consumption happening in slow motion. It is a wet, hot, loud purgatory where everything is eating everything else, and the heat preserves the struggle forever.
