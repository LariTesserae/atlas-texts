They are the architects of the damp, the custodians of the Drip. To call them a species is to imply a separation from the stone that their very forms deny. They are the Dwellers, or simply the Hushed, and they are not so much inhabitants of the Under-Vault as they are organs of the cavern itself, animated coral growing in the dark.

Physically, they are forged by the specific, crushing geometry of the vault. They do not stand upright; the ceiling forbids it. Their spines have curved into a permanent, question-mark stoop, the vertebrae thickened and fused into plates of calcified gristle that act like natural armor against the low, jagged teeth of the stalactites. Their necks are short, almost nonexistent, disappearing into hunched shoulders that roll forward to protect the head from falling water. They are compact, dense creatures, possessing a gravitational weight that makes them seem heavier than they should be.

Their skin is the grey of wet slate, thick and hairless, textured like sharkskin but cold to the touch. It glistens perpetually, not with sweat, but with a hydrophobic sheen that allows the constant condensation to bead and roll off them, carrying away the silt of the city. They have evolved to be waterproof, but the water is still inside them—it fills their lungs, their blood, which runs sluggish and dark with high iron content. They taste of old metal.

The face is a study in minimalism, worn smooth by the erosion of time. The eyes are covered by a translucent nictitating membrane, a miliness that filters the bruised purple twilight. They do not need to see far; the horizon is a myth. They see the texture of the mud, the grain of the rock, the glint of the silver fish. Their noses are flat slits, adapted to ignore the smell of sulfur and wet limestone, saving their olfactory sense for the scent of decay or the subtle shift in airflow that warns of a collapsing arch. And their mouths… their mouths are sealed. The lower jaw is underdeveloped, a vestigial hinge. They do not eat in the way surface creatures do; they absorb. They press their palms against the nutrient-rich runoff gutters, or against the thermal vents where the heat churns the minerals into a broth, osmosing sustenance through the skin of their hands and the soles of their feet.

Clothing is not a choice; it is architecture. They wrap themselves in the "skin of the city"—heavy, waterlogged felt made from pulped fungal fibers and layers of woven silt-grass. These garments are heavy, dripping, sodden robes that drag behind them, acting as both insulation against the chill 0.6 temperature and as a means of trapping body heat. The robes are patched and layered, growing thicker with the decades, until the oldest Dwellers are wrapped in cocoons of grey rags that make them look like walking pillars of stone. They adorn themselves with the only metals available: pitted iron rings hammered from the rust of the walls, and necklaces of hollow bones—bones of the crabs, bones of their ancestors—rattling silently against their chests.

Their movement is defined by the 2.2 erosion score: they are glacial. They do not walk; they shuffle. Their feet are wide and flat, splayed toes acting like suctions cups on the polished black mud. When they move, it is with the friction of tectonic plates sliding. There is no wasted energy. Every gesture is a calculation of leverage. When they clear a drain with a bone hook, the motion is repetitive, hypnotic, a pendulum swing that has been happening for a thousand years. They do not scratch an itch; they do not fidget. They are statues that occasionally shift position.

Society is non-verbal. With a sound rating of 2.3, the roar of the water is a physical wall. To shout is to scream into a hurricane and be ignored. They communicate through vibration. They press their foreheads against the dry-stacked masonry, sensing the hum of the city, or they tap their iron rings against the stone pipes—a code of clicks and resonances that travels through the basalt faster than sound through air. A conversation is a series of tremors felt in the fingertips. They are solitary agents of a collective will, knowing exactly which stone needs to be moved, which leak needs to be plugged, without ever exchanging a glance.

They fear the open air above them, a place they call the "Great Dry." They believe that to stand up straight is to break the back of the world. They maintain the bridges and the domes not to improve their lot, but to ensure the pressure remains stable. They are the equilibrium.

And then there is the interaction with the rare, jarring fauna. They share the space with the blind, white crabs and the schools of silver air-fish, but they do not interact as predator and prey. The Dwellers treat the crabs as sanitation workers, stepping aside as a dog-sized crustacean scuttles over their heads, indifferent to the weight. The air-fish are omens. When the silver darts flash through the purple gloom, the Dwellers stop, their membranes narrowing, tracking the movement. It is the only time they freeze, watching the ghosts that swim where they cannot. They do not try to catch them; the fish are light, and the Dwellers are heavy. To touch one would be to dirty it.

Ultimately, they are the mineral echo of the city. Their hearts beat to the rhythm of the drip: *pause, pause, drop. Pause, pause, drop.* They have no dreams of the sun. They have no concept of green. They are the grey, the iron, the wet limestone given breath. They are the Drip, waiting in the dark, holding up the roof of the world until the heat dies and the water finally turns to ice.
