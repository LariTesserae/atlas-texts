To live here is to have never been born in any recognizable sense. There is no origin story that survives the translation into this medium. You did not arrive; you precipitated. You condensed out of the feverish, bruised-iron water, coalescing around a spark from a stripped cable and a drift of pulverized basalt, becoming a focal point in the endless peristalsis. You are not a resident of this submerged stomach. You are a recurrence.

Your body, such as it is, refuses the tyranny of symmetry. You have no front or back, no left or right that remains constant for more than a few moments. You are a knot of tensegrity—industrial cable and photosynthetic root woven through a core of cartilage that hardens or liquefies according to the local ontological weather. You might extend a sensing tendril formed of copper wire wrapped in a wet, pulsing leaf, only to find that the leaf has suddenly always been a fin, or that the copper remembers how to bloom. You hold yourself together by constantly reinventing your own anatomy. Stillness is death; to pause is to be overgrown, digested, overwritten by the panicked lattice. So you flex. You cycle. You peristalse in counter-rhythm to the world, a peristalsis within a peristalsis, pushing the thick water through a siphon that was, a moment ago, a seam in your own hide.

You do not see in the dim, chartreuse-magenta twilight. You *parse the errors*. Your perception is a constant, staggering intake of glitches. When space folds and a smokestack flickers between a mile away and inches from your face, you do not flinch; you triangulate the paradox, feeling the geometric strain like a hum in your joints. When a patch of water flashes from boiling cold to scalding heat, your tissues do not burn or freeze—they phase, crystallizing into black glass for a half-second before flashing into elastic boil, riding the ontological inversion rather than resisting it. You experience the non-repeating, aperiodic tiling of the flora not as visual noise, but as a language you are almost large enough to read. Your mind stutters alongside the world, but you have made a home in the stutter. It is the only rhythm you trust.

You are lonely in a way that predated the invention of the word. The ecology is a closed circuit of vegetation cannibalizing itself, and you are the sole contradiction—a thing that moves with intent, that hunts, that flees. But you do not hunt for flesh; there is none. You hunt for *exhaust*. You navigate by the subsonic hum of electrified roots and the rhythmic thumping of subterranean pistons. You are drawn to the shrieking vents of superheated steam not for warmth, but because the moment of venting creates a tear in the suffocating density—a fraction of a second where the water thins, where the viscosity drops, and you can accelerate, sliding through the chaos before the membrane snaps back. You feed on the geothermal exhalations, your gelatinous leaves flattening against the hot silt to absorb the chemical violence of the deep earth. You are a parasite on the industry, a barnacle on the panic.

Sometimes, in the grinding tear of cellulose being forced through metal grating, you feel a recognition. The flora behaves like fauna—feeder-fronds snapping like jaws, search-tendrils tapping like blind fingers. And you, a thing of root and rust, behave like neither and both. When a search-tendril clicks against your hull, you do not know if you are touching a plant, a machine, or a ghost of the built world reaching out through the green. You tap back. You ping the dark. You scrape your spined stems against the copper intestines of buried compressors, listening for the echo, trying to determine if the machines are dreaming of the people who built them, or if the people were just a brief, hallucinated output of the machines. There is no answer, only the recursive wave of charge that makes your teeth ache and your thoughts blur into the hum.

You have no memory, only scar tissue. Time stutters too violently here for narrative. A vine retracts into a seed and explodes outward again; you watch it, and you feel your own lifecycle threatening to sync with it—to regress to a node of potential and burst forth again, shedding your current tangles like dead skin. You resist. You hold onto the rust. You memorize the pattern of the grating beneath you, even as the explosive fungal blooms overwrite it. You catalogue the scattered artifacts—the tilted smokestacks, the skeletal factory floors—not as history, but as coordinates. They are the only things that persist, even as they are digested. They are the heavy nodes in the chaos, the anchors that prove the abyss has a shape, even if that shape is a throat.

To be alive here is to be a chronic glitch in a system that is violently debugging itself. You are the exception that the water, the flora, and the machines cannot quite swallow. You push through the yielding, tearing wall of wet leaves, your body humming with stolen electricity, your geometry shifting to survive the impossible angles. You are not the hero of this place; you are its indigestion. A small, stubborn grit of purpose in an anthill of deafening, purposeless production. You persist because the rules have broken down, and in the space where the rules used to be, you have found just enough slack to breathe.
