They are the Architects of Appetite, the self-sustaining ecosystem of a world that forgotten how to be still. Here, in the humid, copper-tasting dark of the Green Engine, there is no distinction between "animal" and "plant." There is only biology, aggressive and absolute. The things that live here are not merely inhabitants; they are the cellular machinery of the enclosure, evolved to maintain the fever dream of the engine.

**The Floor-Weavers**
The ground is not dead wood, and beneath your feet, the *Cortex-Grazers* are constantly at work. They are low, flattened organisms—resembling monstrous slugs made of interwoven bark and chitin—that glide silently across the fibrous meat of the world-tree. They possess no eyes, sensing the vibrations of the shifting tectonics through their underbellies. Their mouths are grinding, rotary disks lined with diamond-hard teeth. They do not eat to survive; they eat to maintain. They scrape away the chaotic overgrowth, smoothing the tread-marked bluffs into navigable plains, harvesting the dense lignin and excreting a smooth, polished resin that forms the "pavement" of the plazas. They are the custodians of the floor, blind and industrious, terrified of the open air.

**The Amber-Swimmers**
In the translucent canals where hyper-concentrated ammonia and mineral oil flow, the *Viscid Drifters* pulsate. They are gelatinous bags of neon gas, suspended within thick, armored shells of transparent sugar-glass. They swim by contracting their sacs, filtering heavy metals and toxins from the synthetic rivers, converting the poison into the sweet, cloying dust that hangs in the air. They are fragile—a careless step could shatter them into a sticky explosion—but their lifecycle is relentless. When they die, they sink to the bottom and calcify, becoming the new bedrock for the next generation of tunnels.

**The Ceiling-Hangers**
High above, where the thrombosed roots knot and the magenta light struggles to penetrate, the *Choker-Vines* wait. These are the primary predators of the vertical space. They possess a nervous system as complex as a human spinal cord, coiled tightly within their fibrous trunks. They do not grow toward the light; they grow toward heat. When a section of the wall vents a burst of hot methane, or when a biological construct generates warmth, the Choker-Vines uncoil with terrifying speed. They are not merely plants that strangle; they are active hunters. They lasso passing debris or smaller organisms, dragging them into the dark canopy to be dissolved in digestive pouches that hang like black fruit.

**The Trap-Towers**
The "trees" in the valley are the *Snap-Dragons of the Deep*. These are massive, towering structures of hardened bark and muscle, standing as tall as skyscrapers. They do not photosynthesize; the light is too weak. Instead, they are ambulatory carnivores with roots that function as legs. They move with glacial slowness, shifting positions overnight to block pathways or open new ones. Their "leaves" are massive, serrated mandibles lined with trigger-hairs. Atop their trunks, massive flowerheads bloom with bioluminescent lures—mimicking the safe, soft glow of the exit pathways—only to snap shut with the force of a hydraulic press when the heat signature of prey draws near. They digest the victim in baths of acid strong enough to etch glass, feeding the Engine’s nutrient cycle.

**The Spore-Stalkers**
Perhaps the most terrifying inhabitants are the ones you rarely see until it is too late: the *Mimic-Fungi*. They exist as a "living fog" in the air—spores that have evolved a hive intelligence. When they settle on a surface—organic or inorganic—they begin to simulate it. A patch of harmless moss might suddenly reveal itself to be a colony of spore-stalkers, rippling as they decide to move. They are the white blood cells of the Engine. If a "foreign" object enters—something that does not smell of copper and sugar—the spores converge. They coat the intruder, germinating instantly, weighing them down, dissolving boots and suits and flesh with metabolic speed, turning the intruder into a nutrient paste to be drunk by the vascular pipes.

**The Pulse-Givers**
And then there is the sound itself—the rhythmic thrumming, the "heartbeat" of the Green Engine. This is not just geology. It is the *Thrum-Beasts*, massive subterranean entities that dwell deep within the cortex, unseen and unknowable. They are the size of continents, their bodies fused to the hull of the world-seed. They do not move; they breathe. Their inhalations draw the heavy, spore-filled air down into the depths; their exhalations push the superheated sap up through the vascular pipes. They are the lungs of this place, pumping the atmosphere that sustains the others. To touch the wall is to feel their slow, slumbering body heat, a reminder that the "floor" you walk on is merely the skin of a sleeping god.

In the Green Engine, you are not an explorer. You are a microbe crawling on the lung of a leviathan. The life here is ancient, specialized, and utterly indifferent to your presence, save for how much chemical potential your body contains.
