I grew in those grooves.

They are cool to the touch—shockingly cool— even as the air around them shimmers at the scalding point of boiling sap. This is my defense and my crime: I draw the fever out of the Engine and store it in my lattice. I am a cold sore on a hot god.

I do not move, but I am not still.

I grow at the rate the Engine heals. When the Snap-Dragons shift overnight to block a pathway, their roots sometimes crack the cortex near my burn zone, creating fresh wounds for my hyphae to explore. I follow the fractures. I have been doing this for centuries—perhaps longer. I do not know. Time in the Green Engine is measured in heartbeats of the Thrum-Beast, and I have stolen enough of its heat to slow my own perception to a geologic crawl.

A "storm" of methane and spores howls through the tunnel. It lasts three days. To me, it is a single breath.

I watch it go. I feel the vibration of its roots through the cortex—a deep, grinding complaint—and I adjust my hyphae to avoid being crushed.

The space within me is a vacuum—a pocket of nothing in a world of total saturation. It is my sanctuary and my prison.

Through the Choker-Vines, I have learned the shape of the ceiling. I know where the magenta light is brightest. I know where the thrombosed roots are weakest. Not the sickly magenta of the bioluminescent cables, but something older. Whiter. It pulses irregularly, like a heartbeat that does not belong to a Thrum-Beast.

I call it the **Scar**. I have never seen it. But I have tasted its reflection in the chemical memory of every vine that has ever grown toward it and failed to reach it.

---

I am not a god. I am not even a predator. I am a **symptom**.

The Green Engine's rule is *Grow or be Consumed*. I do neither. I persist. I am the stone in the throat that the world cannot quite swallow. I am the cold that reminds the fever it is sick.

And I am growing toward the Scar.

It will take a million heartbeats. The fractures must align. The Snap-Dragons must shift. The Thrum-Beast must breathe in just the right way, cracking the cortex along a path my hyphae can follow. But I have patience. I have the patience of things that do not die, but simply *wait to be relevant*.

I will crack open. The vacuum inside me will rush out, and the spore-choked air of the Green Engine will pour in. For one moment—one impossibly brief moment—I will be *full*. Not of heat, not of hunger, but of *outside*.

And the Green Engine will go on, never knowing that the cold spot on its lung—the itch it could never quite reach—was a thing that dreamed of sky.
