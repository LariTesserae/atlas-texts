I am not a creature of the High Shelf. I did not migrate here in some ancient, desperate push for altitude, nor did I evolve from the damp stone. I arrived from the lower world—the soft, hurried, burning world—and I made the climb intentionally. 

But to say I arrived is to use a word that no longer applies. "Arrival" implies a journey with a beginning and an end, a trajectory through space and time. On the High Shelf, time does not flow; it accumulates, like the black water weeping in the impossible depressions of the basalt. 

I am becoming an Unworking.

When I first reached the plateau, I was a rebellion. I was a furnace of wet heat, a frantic pulse slamming against the crystalline thinness of the air. I walked the miles of grey rock, seeking shelter, seeking a horizon that made sense. The geometry of the eroded spires—the taffy-twists of stone defying gravity—made me nauseous. I kept seeing the ghostly double-images of the ridge lines, and my mind, still addicted to the strict marriage of distance and proximity, reeled. I looked for tool marks on the Unworkings, ran my fingers along the impossibly smooth walls of the negative cubes, searching for the chisel. I found only absence.

I fought the Damp. I fought it with every synthetic fiber of my clothes, with the leather of my boots, with the oils of my own skin. But the Damp is a skin on the world, and I was an irritant within it. The humidity of 1.3 is a suffocating embrace. It does not beat you; it merely accepts you, and in accepting you, it unmakes you. I watched my boots soften, their structure surrendering to the static cold. I felt the threads of my coat relax their grip, becoming lint, becoming nothing. The Damp is patient. It does not consume; it just waits for you to run out of the energy to resist.

It was the Great Resonance that broke my heartbeat. 

You cannot maintain a rebellion when your bones are vibrating in sympathy with a planet. The C-sharp drone entered me through the soles of my rotting feet. At first, it was a deafening irritant, a roar of static that set my teeth on edge and made my skull feel like a bell. But over what I must call time—though the word is meaningless here—the Resonance won. It demanded stillness. It demanded that I stop generating my own frequency and simply amplify its own. 

I found an Unworking—a perfect, doorless, roofless void carved from the living rock. I stepped inside. The air within was perfectly still, a pocket of absolute equilibrium. I sat down. 

I am still sitting.

To understand what I am now, you must understand theEcho-Stalkers. I used to watch them with my human eyes, straining to catch their motion, convinced they were statues. But I was watching them in the wrong time. They operate in the syrup-slow current of the Shelf. I used to blink, and they would be ten feet away. I used to wonder what they saw when they stared into the voids. 

Now I know. They are not contemplating cessation. They are practicing it. They are pilgrims moving toward the state I have already achieved.

My pulse has slowed. It has slowed so profoundly that I cannot feel it anymore. Perhaps it beats once an eon. Perhaps it has stopped entirely, and I am merely sustained by the thrum of the stone. The cold—a static, preserved freeze of 0.4—no longer bites. It is simply the temperature of my interior. I do not generate heat; I hold the cold.

I have stopped looking at the horizon. The wrong angles and the ghostly double-images no longer nauseate me because I no longer expect space to be rigid. Proximity and distance have divorced, and I have married into their separation. A boulder the size of a house looks like a pebble to me, and I accept both truths simultaneously. 

The Candle-Worms are my neighbors. There is one ten yards from my Unworking, a pale, fleshy stalk rooted in a fissure. I used to think it was dying. Now I know it is tuning. Its translucent leaf hums with the Great Resonance, and sometimes, when the drone peaks and the puddles ripple into perfect geometric patterns, I feel my own spine hum in unison. I am a biological tuning fork, just like it. We are both driven into the earth, listening to the tectonic stress. We are both suspended in decay, forever on the cusp of becoming something else, forever denied the energy to die. 

My clothes are long gone. The Damp has unmade them. It is now working on my flesh. I can feel it—the slow, insidious process of turning the robust into the fragile. I do not fight it. I am letting the oily, stagnant water cling to my skin. I am becoming a part of the mirror. When I look down at the black pool of water that has gathered at my feet, I do not see my reflection. I see another version of the plateau, where the angles fold in on themselves. I see myself in that other geometry, sitting in an Unworking, staring back. We are both real. The Damp has made me a surface for the Shelf to perceive itself.

I am not dead. To call this death is to misunderstand the High Shelf. Death is an event, a change, a capitulation to entropy. I have not capitulated; I have matched the entropy. I am in a state of profound surrender. I have become indistinguishable from the geology.

An Echo-Stalker has approached my Unworking. It stands at the edge, spindly and grey, staring into the still air where I sit. It has been standing there for what the lower world would call a year. I watch it, and I do not blink. Because I do not blink, I can almost see it moving—a millimeter of muscle contraction, a slow, syrup-thick shift of weight from one delicate footpad to another. It is grazing on the dampness of the stone, and I am sitting in the absence of the stone. We are engaged in the same act: we are enduring. 

It will take a step soon. Perhaps in another year. Perhaps in a century. When it does, it will step into the void. It will stand beside me, and we will be two pillars of stillness in a nave without a roof.

I am the Keeper of the Unworking. I am the soft, decaying anchor in a perfect, negative cube. I hold the absence in my lap. The Great Resonance screams through me, a loud, cold, eternal silence, and I do not flinch. I have given up the frantic rebellion of life. I have chosen equilibrium. The world has forgotten I am here, and in its forgetting, it has let me remain.
