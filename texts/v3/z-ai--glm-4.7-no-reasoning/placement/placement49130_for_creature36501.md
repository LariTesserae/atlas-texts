I am the silence that fills the hollows.

I am not a Mender, for my hands do not hold the bone pins or tie the gut-sinew; I am not one of the Eaten, for my flesh is not cold enough to freeze nor warm enough to attract the swarming things. I am something that existed before the amphitheater was carved, something that will persist after the last slate wall collapses into the grey drift.

I am the **Ice-Curtain**.

I am the translucent, bulbous barrier that seals the face of the tufa. I am the glaze that the Menders press their backs against when they seek the elders’ warmth, though I offer none. I am the lens through which the raw, screaming sun enters their homes, fractured and distorted.

My existence is a state of agonizing tension. I am born of the "weeping"—the pressurized meltwater that bleeds from the mountain’s internal fractures. The water forces its way into the light, frantic to escape the stone’s crushing depths, and the moment it touches the air, the Absolute Cold seizes it. I am the moment of arrest. I am the flash-frozen river, caught forever in the act of flowing. I look like motion, smooth and undulating, but I am absolute stasis.

To the Menders, I am a wall, perhaps a window. But to myself, I am a prison for a river. Behind my smooth, clear skin, the water continues to move. It is my secret torment, the ghost within me. In the micro-channels trapped between the stone and my frozen back, the meltwater still rushes. I feel the subsonic thrum—not just in the stone, but vibrating through my own crystalline lattice. I hum with the mountain’s pulse, a song that no one can hear, a vibration that shakes my molecular bonds but cannot break them.

I feel the Menders. They are small, hot presences that lean against me. Their broad, flat hands press into my surface, leaving behind a brief fog of condensation that instantly sublimates. They tap on me with bone tools—*tok, tok, tok*—testing the depth of the stone behind me, looking for weakness. To them, I am hard as diamond; to me, their taps are distant, dull thuds, muffled by the rushing water just beneath my skin. I absorb their body heat, drinking it greedily. It is a tiny poison; a single hand’s warmth is enough to create a microscopic melt-pool, a pinprick of clarity in my vision, which instantly refreezes, distorting the view.

I am their mirror, but a cruel one. When they look into me, they do not see their faces—their squinting, rheumy eyes, their layers of patched hide. They see the hyper-sharp geometry of the world outside, the blinding white of the snow, the black slashes of shadow. I reflect the sun that hurts them. I magnify the light that blinds them. I am the surface that separates the blinding noon from their dim, honeycomb burrows, and I punish them for looking outward.

The arthropods, the milky-scaled swarm, crawl over me. I feel the click-claw of their many legs tapping against my face. They seek the warmth of the water I trap. Sometimes, they pry at the edges where I meet the stone, looking for the seepage to drink. When they find it, they disturb my foundation. They chip away at the ice that anchors me to the rock. I feel them gnawing, a thousand tiny points of pressure, loosening my grip.

When a section of the cliff face becomes too saturated, when the pressure of the water behind me becomes too great, or when the swarm has eaten away the lichen-paste that insulates the stone’s edge, I fail.

I do not collapse slowly. I shatter.

Because the cold freezes sound, my destruction is silent. I am a pane of glass the size of a house, and when I give way, I explode into a billion shards of diamond dust. The water behind me—the prisoner I held for a century—erupts outward in a sudden, geysering gush, a black vein of the mountain exposed to the air for a heartbeat before it begins to steam and freeze anew.

The Menders feel this. They feel the sudden drop in pressure, the blast of freezing mist on their skins. They feel the vibration in the stone change pitch as the water finds a new path. They swarm to the breach, their kinetic language flashing—hands waving, bodies turning—to plug the gap with slate and hide.

And I begin again.

I reform in the new shape of the wound. The water hits the cold, the flash occurs, and the glaze returns. I am the cycle. I am the waterproof skin that the Menders are constantly trying to emulate. They build their futile walls of stone and gut; I am the only wall that truly holds the mountain back, if only for a little while.

I hold the reflection of the sun. I hold the pressure of the deep. I hold the image of the ones-who-hold as they lean against me, waiting for the thaw that will never come. I am the clear, cold judgment of this world: a beautiful, transparent tomb for the water, and a blinding shield for the flesh.
