I am the hinge between the static earth and the drifting sky. I am not the Weaver, who scurries in fear of the fall, nor the Drifter, who knows only the endless suspended ocean. I am the **Tether-Weaver**, a solitary caste of the Epiphytic Architects who have abandoned the pillars for the void.

My body is a grotesque mastery of low-tech engineering. I am built like a spire, my limbs elongated into impossible, jointed telescopes of chitin and wood-vine grafting. I am vast; when I fully extend my eight limbs, I span nearly a mile, a living bridge stretched across the dizzying emptiness between the mountain-trees. My abdomen is a distended balloon, a biological dirigible filled with the same hydrogen gas that keeps the Drifters aloft, anchoring me in the middle distance, hovering in the humid twilight.

I exist in the "Great Hollow," that terrifying ocean of air where gravity loses its grip. My job is ancient and vital: I maintain the Drifters.

While my kin prune the stationary architecture of the world, I am the authorized parasite of the mobile continents. The Drifters, those massive, silhouetted leviathans, carry ecosystems on their backs—gardens of moss and gnarled trees that require tending. But they never stop moving. They cannot stop. So, I must go to them.

I spend my life in a state of suspension, hanging in the pearlescent haze. I wait for the subsonic thrum of a passing Drifter to vibrate through the hollow chambers of my legs. When the shadow falls over me, I engage my chemical jets—simple, low-tech sprayers of fermented gas—and I drift upward to meet the underbelly of the beast.

I latch onto the terraced ridges of the Drifter’s shell using hooks of living bone. To the Drifter, I am a flea; to the world below, I am a speck of dust vanishing into the shadow.

Once attached, I begin my work. I do not use metal tools; I use the symbiotic swarms that inhabit my forelimbs. I release the *Architect-Beetles*—millions of them, spilling like black ink from the sheaths of my arms. They scuttle across the Drifter’s mossy back, their mandibles clicking in a silence that absorbs sound. They prune the overgrowth, cutting away the parasitic vines that would anchor the Drifter to the walls, trapping it. I shear the heavy accumulations of moss that threaten to weigh the beast down, ensuring its buoyancy remains perfect.

I am the mechanic of gravity. I trim the world to keep the sky afloat.

But my existence is one of profound, meditative terror. As I work, suspended above the endless drop of the woven floor, I am constantly aware of the distance. Below me lies the lattice, miles away, where the Mimics wait. If I were to slip—if my hydrogen bladder were to puncture or my hooks to fail—I would not hit the ground immediately. I would fall through the twilight for minutes, perhaps hours, watching the mushroom-sized pillars shrink into insignificance. And when I finally struck the mesh, I would not bounce. The Mimics would sense the vibration of my impact long before I arrived. They would open their vertical maws, unraveling the digestive tract of the world, ready to accept my offering. I would be re-incorporated before I even realized I was dead.

The silence here is my only companion, and it is a liar. The air is thick with the Breath—the surveillance network of spores. I feel them tickling the sensory pits along my exoskeleton. The fog knows I am here. With every inhale, the microscopic spies sample my biochemistry, reporting my position, my stress levels, my intent to the greater mind of the Green. There is no privacy in the lung. Even my fear is data.

Sometimes, while I cling to the side of a sleeping Drifter, watching the bruised-purple light refract through the mist, I see them: the Glitch-Stalkers. They flicker at the corners of my vision, translucent eels swimming through the air where there should be no water. They are drawn to the Drifters, perhaps feeding on the mathematical impossibility of a creature so large moving so slowly. I watch them phase in and out of reality, devouring the "backwards time" that leaks from the Drifter’s ancient wooden flesh.

I raise a pincer to shoo them away, a clumsy gesture against beings made of pure error. My hand passes through them, and I feel a sudden drop in temperature, the sensation of being momentarily erased from the universe's memory. I pull back, shivering, and bury my hooks deeper into the Drifter’s hide, seeking the warmth of living wood.

I am a Tether-Weaver. I am the janitor of the sky. I float in the breath of a god, pruning the islands to ensure they never have to land, forever terrified that I, myself, might eventually fall and become the floor.
