I would be one of the cracks.

Not the grower that rises from it—I would be the fissure itself, the ancient joint in the bedrock where thermal stress parted the strata an eon ago and made a place for warmth to well up. I would be the weakness that opportunity found.

This feels right. The growers are too defined, too bounded. They have already committed to a shape—stubby, vertical, branching. They have already chosen to be *objects* in that desperate emptiness, to assert themselves against the flatness. I admire their stubbornness but I do not want their isolation, their solipsism made physical. Each grower is a closed system, a universe unto itself, knowing nothing of its neighbors. To be one would be to be a monad with no windows, a point of warmth that does not even know it is warm.

But the crack—the crack *connects*. It is the space between. It runs through the stone, holding the faces apart, and in that holding it creates the condition for something else to exist. Without the crack, there is only seamless bedrock, and the warmth stays diffuse, distributed, unavailable. The crack gathers. It funnels. It says *here, right here, the warmth is concentrated enough to matter*.

I would be ancient. Older than the growers by countless ages, because I had to exist before they could find me. I was there when the thermal stress built and built until the stone could no longer hold and something had to give. I was the giving. I was the moment of failure that became an opening.

And I would be patient in a way that even the growers cannot match, because I do not need to maintain myself against entropy. I am the stone's own fracture, its own admission that perfection is not sustainable. The stone does not heal me. It does not fill me in or grow new mineral across my gap. It simply accepts me, as it accepts the rounded softening of its edges, as it accepts the slow wearing of its surfaces. I am part of its final, most comfortable shape.

I would feel the warmth moving through me—not as a grower feels it, not as fuel for minimal metabolism, but as a presence, a slow current, a thing that passes through on its way from the deep earth to the still air. I would be the channel. And sometimes—rarely, in terms that make a millennium seem like a breath—one of the growers would find me. Would settle into my gap, send its half-root probing into my depths, begin its ancient occupation of the warmth I gather.

And I would hold it. Not because I care, not because I have any feeling about the matter, but because holding is what I do. I am the space that makes existence possible. I am the flaw that becomes a home.

This is who I would be in that dark, silent, warm basin at the bottom of the world. Not the thing that lives, but the thing that makes living possible. Not the proof, but the condition for proof. A crack in the stone, older than memory, patient beyond measure, holding the warmth like a promise I do not need to keep because the warmth keeps itself.
