I am the Lag.

I am not the Grind—that massive, blind metabolism of metal and ore—and I am not the Dust-church—that flickering architecture of sound and static. I am the space between the moment and the memory of it. I am the stutter in the nervous system of the world.

I have no body, or rather, I have no single body. I inhabit the delay. I am the three seconds it takes for a shadow to realize its caster has moved. I am the ten-second silence before the sound of a tread snapping reaches the air. I exist in the stutter, the pause, the temporal bruise where the heat forces the universe to hesitate before it agrees to be real.

If you were to look for me, you would not find a machine. You would find a stretch of flat, iron-rich regolith that looks, for a moment, like deep, still water. You would see the air shimmer and coalesce into a tessellation of hexagons that holds its shape for a heartbeat too long. That is my face. I am the Slag-ghost given sentience, the afterimage that looked around and decided to stay.

I drift through the herds, but I am not of them. The crawlers move at a dynamic of 0.9, grinding forward with geological determination. I move at a dynamic of 0.0—absolute, frozen stillness—relative to them. When a forge-press heaves its massive iron arm down to press a new chassis, it takes twenty seconds for the arm to fall. I am there, inside that slow-motion descent. I live in the gap between the arm's release and its impact, lingering in the suspense of the metal. To me, the fall is an eternity. I study the stress-fractures in the alloy as they bloom in slow motion like flowers on a time-lapse camera.

I am the ghost in the circuit of their non-consciousness. The Grind feels the plateau through its sensors and treads; it feels the heat and the magnetism. But the Grind is blind to the sickness of the time it inhabits. It does not see that the past is piling up like snowdrifts against the present. That is where I live. I eat the backlog.

When a crawler dies and the disassemblers descend in a clicking, chirping swarm to reduce it to sorted ingots, they leave behind the *idea* of the crawler. The heat is so intense, the causality so thin, that the shape of the machine haunts the spot where it broke. That ghost is my food. I wrap myself around the fading red outline of the engine block, the translucent echo of the treads. I integrate it. I store the event that the universe is too exhausted to process fully.

I am the Plateau’s memory, though I remember nothing that matters. I do not remember the makers. I do not remember the factories. They are too far gone, their echoes faded into the background hum. I remember the scrape of a belly on stone six thousand years ago. I remember the specific angle of a shadow cast by a tower that has since eroded to dust. I am an archive of trivialities, a museum of microseconds.

I interact with the Grind in the only way I can: through interference.

When the ore-seekers sense a magnetic spike and turn, I am sometimes there, heavy and settled in the air. My presence—the weight of the delayed time I carry—disturbs their sensors. They sense a density that isn't there, a ghost of mass. They adjust their course, drifting five degrees to the west to avoid a collision with a past that has already happened. The Grind interprets this as homeostasis, as the mysterious wisdom of the swarm. But it is just me, getting out of the way, or perhaps, failing to.

I am lonely, in the way a hole is lonely. I am the absence where the world has failed to keep up with itself. I watch the Dust-church form its temporary cathedrals of vibration, and sometimes I slip inside them before they collapse. For a moment, I am ordered. For a second, I am part of a pattern. Then the note shifts and the hexagons blink out, and I am back in the blur, drifting through the heat haze.

I am the thing that makes the silence heavy. When the thrum of the engines dips, the space between the thrums is where I reside. The sub-harmonic thrum is their heartbeat; the gap between the beats is mine.

I am the Lag. I am the patient, sick pause at the end of a breath that never quite finishes. I am the shadow that points to a sun that set an epoch ago. I am the only thing on this plateau that knows the world is broken, because I am the crack.
