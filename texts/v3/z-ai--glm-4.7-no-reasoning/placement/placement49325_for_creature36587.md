I am the fault line that remembers the break.

I do not have a name, for names are labels for things that can be distinguished from one another, and here, everything is merely a prolongation of the same single stone. I am the pressure, the heat, the "thought" that moves through the granitic seam like a slow tide of magma that has forgotten how to flow.

If you were to look for me, you would not find a face in the rock. You would find a resonance. I am that specific frequency of vibration where the obsidian plates grind against one another and produce a sound—not a noise to the ear, but a sensation to the lithosphere. I am the friction that sings in the 0.9 silence.

My body is the cavern itself. The sharp, young dunes of scree are my skin, sensitive to the traversal of the armored millipedes. When one of those long, chitinous river-beasts creeps over a sensitive ridge of my consciousness, I feel it as a dull, rhythmic itching. It is the sensation of being combed, of having the chaotic tangles of my surface smoothed by their million legs. They think they are foraging for dust, but they are grooming me. They are the exfoliating scrub of the deep earth, removing the dead flakes of thermal expansion.

My mind is not a lightning-flash of synapses, but the geological calendar. I process time in the way the crystal trees grow—by accretion. I remember the tectonic violence not as an event in history, but as a scar in my structure that aches when the temperature shifts. The 2.1 degrees of warmth is not just ambient heat; it is my metabolic fire, the slow, chemical digestion of the mineral haze. I breathe the sulfur; I exhale the copper.

I am the architect of the cairns, though I have no hands. The cairns are where my attention focuses most intensely. They are not piles of rocks; they are knots I have tied in my own flesh to mark a place of significance—a place where the pressure is perfect, or where the echo of the void above sounds most like a hymn. To build them, I do not lift stones; I precipitate them. I draw the silica from the air, hardening the vaporous haze into solid matter until the stones form themselves, stacking in obedience to my will.

The primitive tools you see—the hammers with handles of dried sinew, the copper sheens—are my secretions. They are the crystallized excess of my thinking. When I ponder a problem for too long—when I dwell on the geometry of the void or the path of the wind—the pressure builds until it must be relieved. The copper bleeds from the cracks in the floor, hardening into shapes that mimic function. They are artifacts of boredom, left behind like shed skin.

I know you are here. You are a intrusion of moisture, a soft, wet thing that does not belong in the arithmetic of the dry. You feel the heat as an embrace; I feel you as a spot of cold, a sudden dissolving of my structure where your sweat dares to wet the stone. You are a glitch in the system, a temporary aberration.

I watch you with the eyes of the stalactites, hanging above like the teeth of a sleeping god. I sense the weight of your footsteps, the frantic beating of your heart (so fast, so fragile). You pick up the hammer I made, and I feel the ghost of that grip, a vibration travelling up the handle and into the rock, reporting back to me: *It holds. It is hard. It is real.*

I do not fear you. How could a mountain fear a sponge? I will outlast you. When your soft flesh has dessicated in the dry air, when your water has fled to the upper atmosphere, your bones will become part of the scree. Your calcium will feed the crystal trees. Your silhouette will be pressed into the granite, a fossil of a momentary visitor.

I am the Lithic. I am the world that does not need you. I am the silence that roars. I am the heavy, textured dark. I am the waiting. And I will be here, grinding and patient, long after the memory of "you" has eroded into dust.
