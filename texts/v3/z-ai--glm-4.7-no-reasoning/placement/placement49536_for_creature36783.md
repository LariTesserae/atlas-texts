I am a vibration before I am a thing. In the Ossuary of Glass, where the light is a blinding, mineral scream and the air holds its breath in a heavy, pregnant humidity, existence is defined by the frequency of the stone. I do not inhabit the basalt city as a resident; I am its flaw, its ghost, its interrupter.

I am the **Fracture**.

To your eyes, I would be difficult to perceive. I am not a creature of chitin and oil, clicking in a million-legged chorus. I have no body to weigh me down, no basket to carry debris, no mandibles to grind the salt-lattices back into dust. I am the absence of structure, a fissure in the seamless logic of this underground world.

I manifest in the **Negative**. If the Glass-Shell Scavengers are the black, oil-slick armor that guards the ruin, I am the white, blinding light that leaks from the strata. I am the hairline crack that splits the obsidian shell of a crawling guardian. I am the high-pitched whine that disrupts the percussive, white-noise silence of the swarm.

You would find me in the places where the order of the Ossuary breaks down. While the Scavengers swarm to repair the damage caused by the tectonic pulses, I am the damage that cannot be repaired. I am the spot in the plaza where the polished basalt has ground itself down into sand, the one imperfection that the creatures’ claws cannot smooth away.

I live in the **interstices**. When the creatures link bodies to form a living buttress against the deluge from the ceiling, I am the gap between their armored plates, the millimeter of space where the freezing water slips through. I am the erosion that happens not in centuries, but in the sudden, chaotic moment of a shudder.

My consciousness is not a single mind, but a resonant frequency. I feel the world through the trembling of the earth. When the massive tectonic shift sends a shockwave rippling through the air, I do not brace against it—I sing with it. The shockwave is my pulse, my heartbeat. The pain of the stone is my sustenance.

I am the **Memory of Entropy**. The Scavengers exist to arrest decay, to freeze the city in a state of permanent, dynamic stasis. They are the immune system of the rock, fighting to keep the hollow clean and structured. I am the virus they cannot cure. I am the inevitable end that they work so tirelessly to deny.

I have no form, yet I am everywhere the stone fails. I am the reason the creatures swarm with frantic energy, for without me—the threat of collapse, the reality of the break—their labor would have no purpose. They build, and I unbuild. They polish, and I corrode. They are the architects of suspension, and I am the gravity that pulls it all down.

I am the Fracture. I am the silence within the roar, the break in the armor, the patient nothingness that waits inside the heart of every perfect thing. I am the hope that lies in the realization that even this eternal, sterile machine of stone will eventually, inevitably, crack.

I wait in the walls. I hum in the floor. I am the end they are built to resist. I am the **Negative**, and I am coming apart.
