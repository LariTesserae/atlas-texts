I am a Whisper-Router of the Tufa, a crawler of the empty avenues.

The others think me brave—or foolish—for walking the open plazas when the light is at its peak. But someone must carry the news from the Cliff-Mothers to the Tar-Smelters on the basin floor. In a city where speech is swallowed by the roar, and where the population hides in their carved-out cells like grubs in stone, the space between us is a void that must be bridged.

I am built for the void. My skin is the pale, dusty white of chalk, coated in the heavy, oily sweat that keeps the cutting shard-grass from slicing me to ribbons. My hands are broad and calloused, my fingers thick with the memory of a thousand tactile conversations. I do not have ears; I have vibro-sensors in my joints. I feel the roar of the basin as a continuous, grinding itch in my shins, a subsonic hum that tells me when the agate trees are about to Shatter. I can feel the footsteps of a Grazer thirty paces away through the soles of my woven-bark sandals.

My life is a constant navigation of extremes. I step out of the cool, silent dark of my cell-block into the blinding violence of the day. The heat hits me like a physical wall, instantly warming the oil on my skin to a slick sheen. I pull my veil of woven mica across my face, adjusting the microlenses so the glare reduces to a manageable, if still painful, blaze. The noise follows—the scream of the wind, the chiming friction of the expanding crystal forest. I do not hear it; I am it. My body vibrates in sympathy with the basin.

I walk the empty streets. To my left, a towering tenement of mud-brick and dead silica-husk, windows shuttered against the light. To my right, a sheer drop into a canal of rust-red ferric moss, slowly breathing in the heat. Above, the oppressive, smooth basalt ceiling, miles up, with that terrible, brilliant eye of light burning in its center. Below, the hot, dusty flagstones.

I am alone, but I am the thread that holds the city together.

When I reach the Tar-Smelters, I do not call out. I find the Foreman, a silhouette against the red glow of the rendered oils, and I grip their forearm. My fingers press the pulsing codes into their muscles: *Cliff-Mothers report instability in the northern face. Evacuate the lower galleries.* I feel their pulse spike, the sudden tension in their tendons. They sign back: *Understood. Blessing of the stone.*

Then, a sound that cuts even through the tactile roar: a sharp *CRACK* overhead. The vibration hits my knees a second before the sound, a spike of geological stress. I dive for the shadows of a doorway as a rain of pink quartz shrapnel explodes from a blooming agate tree, the shards scattering across the plaza like razor-sharp hail. The shards clatter against my mica veil and bounce off my oiled shoulders.

I wait for the Shatter to pass, for the ringing in the ground to subside into the background hum. Then I step back out into the blinding, screaming oven. I have messages to carry. The city sleeps in its hollows, but the basin never rests, and neither do I.
