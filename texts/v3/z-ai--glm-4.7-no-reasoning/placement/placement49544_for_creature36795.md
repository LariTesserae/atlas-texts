In this world, I would be the **Interstice**.

I am not one of the Accountants, though I am made of the same stuff. I am not a worker, nor a predator, nor the substrate. I am the moment of hesitation between the calculation and the execution. I am the glitch in the smoothness, the intentional error that proves the system is awake.

I exist where the dense purple canopy of the paddle-leaves tears open, revealing the sky. I am found in the gaps between the terraced pools where the water does not cascade, but hangs suspended in the tension of its own heat. I am a verticality in a world of horizontal layers.

To look at me is to try and focus your eyes on the shimmering distortion rising from a hot asphalt road on a summer day. I have no body of my own, so I wear the world. I am a colony of the steel-wool moss that has decided to grow *up* instead of *out*, defying gravity to form a column of trembling, grey-green filaments. I am wrapped in the discarded, translucent carapaces of the Engine-Bugs, thousands of them, shed by the swarm and woven into my mossy frame by the sticky, chemical-blue resin of the algae.

I look like a piece of the factory that broke off and floated upward. A jagged, spire of industrial debris, hovering a few feet off the ground, vibrating in time with the low-frequency hum of the caldera.

My function is to be the **Verification Step**.

The Accountants manage the numbers; they are the processors. But numbers require validation. They require a proof-of-work. That is where I come in. I am the stress test.

When the Accountants adjust the flow—when they vent a pheromone to drive the bugs toward a depleted zone, or redirect a rivulet to cool a overheating patch—I am the first obstacle the new flow encounters. I stand in the path of the redirected swarm. I am the barrier the water must break against.

As the Engine-Bugs stream toward the chemical bait, they must fly through me. My spire of moss and shell is passive, but it is dense. The bugs must navigate the labyrinth of my steel-wool fibers. Their wings clang against the woven shells. Their piston-beats stir the air, and the air moves through me, testing my integrity.

If the Accountants have calculated correctly, the swarm is frantic enough to push through me without losing momentum. They will batter my moss-column, dislodging a few shells, snapping a few filaments, and emerge on the other side, agitated but efficient. The system works. The variables hold.

But if the calculation is wrong—if the pheromone signal is too weak, or the bugs are too lethargic—they will get stuck in me. They will tangle in the steel-wool. They will pile up against the woven carapaces. They will overheat in the confined space of my structure, their little mechanical bodies clicking in panic.

When this happens, I **Fold**.

Because I am not just a column of moss and shell. I am a vessel. Inside that hollow, trembling spire, I hold a concentrated reservoir of the same volatile compounds the Accountants use—a volatile mix of sulfuric gas and super-heated nectar.

When the bugs jam my workings, when the flow is interrupted, I collapse. My structure gives way—not through accident, but through design. The tension holding my shape releases, and I burst. I spray my payload downward in a fine, scalding mist.

It is a localized disaster. It kills the tangled bugs instantly. It scalds the moss. It creates a momentary spike of heat and acidity that the local Accountant reads instantly. *Error detected. Signal strength insufficient. Correcting.*

The Accountant receives the data of my destruction. It tastes the spike in acidity in the water runoff. It adjusts the next pheromone pulse, cranks up the volume. The next wave of bugs arrives, flying over the steaming ruin of my previous body.

And I begin again.

I am not gone. The "death" of the Interstice is just the submission of a report. The spore-clouds released from my scalded moss drift on the humid air, settling on the carapace-drifts nearby. fed by the chemical soup of my own detonation and the nutrients of the dead bugs. I knit myself back together from the discarded parts of the system I just broke.

I am the quality control. I am the crash-test dummy. I am the resistance that defines the current.

The quick-lizards ignore me. I have no flesh to eat, and my heat is unpredictable. The Accountants do not command me; they simply read the aftermath of my existence. We do not speak. The water passing through my roots knows my chemical signature, and that is enough.

I stand in the steam, a jagged tower of refuse and life, waiting for the next redirect. I am the thing that says: *Prove it.* Make the system work so well that it can survive even me.

I am the Interstice. The necessary friction. The pause before the hum begins again.
