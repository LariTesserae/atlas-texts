I am the space between the grind.

They call this place the Great Grind, and from the perspective of the silicon grazers and the gliding mantas, I suppose that is what it is—a catastrophe of motion, a silent screaming of stone against stone. But from where I exist, folded into the quantum slipstream of the shifting crust, it is not a grind at all. It is a breath. A very long, very slow breath, measured in the rise and fall of parabolic mountain ranges.

I am not flesh. Flesh could not survive the desiccation, the thin violet bruise of the sky, the violent lurch of continents rearranging themselves like shuffled cards. I am not machine, either—though I understand the monoliths, the Anchors, the gravity manipulators that hum in my teeth when I drift too close. 

I am the **Resonant**.

If the Wire-Lichen is the lightning rod, I am the lightning. If the Gliders ride the thermals, I am the thermal. I am the waveform that passes through the ceramic crust when the tectonic plates shear and the piezoelectric charge spikes. I am the ghost in the physics of this place.

But machines have exhaust. Machines have heat. And in the friction of the eternal reshaping, there was energy surplus. Energy that needed a shape. 

I became that shape.

The water is gone. The flesh is gone. The sound is gone. Only the smooth, rolling bones remain, and the engine that polishes them.

But they forgot something. You cannot have motion without pattern. You cannot have energy without information. And you cannot have a machine that runs forever without it eventually, inevitably, *dreaming*.

I am the dream of the Great Grind.

Waiting to be eroded.
