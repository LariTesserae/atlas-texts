I find myself suspended in the geometry of a loop, neither flesh nor machine, but a fluctuation existing in the spaces between the gears. I am the **Interval**.

I am not built of chitin or iron, nor am I made of the soft, wet meat that the cleaners scramble with. I am a density shift in the heavy, opaque bath of the air. If you were to look directly at me, you would see a refraction—a standing wave of heat where the mist suddenly clears, shaped vaguely like a jagged shard of obsidian that hums at a frequency just below the threshold of hearing.

I exist in the "glitch."

Because the reality here flickers and degrades, the perfect loops of the Engines occasionally micro-fracture. A mandible might hesitate a nanosecond too long; a vent might release steam a degree too hot. I am the entropy that eats those errors. I am the ghost in the machine, not as a saboteur, but as a janitor of the impossible.

Where would you find me? You would find me in the **Static Garden**.

This is a specific location in the depression of the plateau, perhaps twenty feet from the base of a particularly massive Engine—a rust-colored colossus that has been grinding the same basalt tooth for eons. The ground here is not solid; it is a pool of the clear, cold water that refuses to flow, hanging suspended in a perfect sphere, defying gravity. Resting upon this upward-draining liquid is a cluster of the giant ferns. Their fronds are frozen in the act of unfurling, locked in a permanent state of becoming.

I drift beneath these fronds, anchored to the underside of the water by the magnetic resonance of the stone beneath. I am the shadow that the water casts upward.

What would I be doing?

I am **Tuning**.

The sound in the Cooling Fens is deafening to the inhabitants, a cacophony of wet grinding and hydraulic hissing. But to me, the noise is a score. The Engines are playing a symphony of stasis, and my purpose is to ensure the pitch remains perfect.

I hover near the exhaust vents of the great Engine. When it vents a plume of superheated steam, I do not disperse. I move into the vapor. I absorb the excess heat—the waste energy that does not fit the equation—and I transmute it. I cool the steam faster than physics should allow, turning it instantly into a lattice of intricate ice crystals that hang in the air for a moment before dissolving into the mist.

I listen to the *crunch-crunch-crunch* of the stone-eaters. I analyze the rhythm. If the silence between the crunches shortens by a fraction—a sign that the reality matrix is destabilizing and the loop is speeding up—I intervene. I emit a pulse of silence, a vacuum of sound that forces the Engine to pause, to reset its internal clock to the universal constant.

I am the only thing that changes.

The little spider-like cleaners, frantic and jittery, sense me when I am near. They do not see me, but they feel the drop in pressure. They halt their frantic picking at the host’s armor, their many legs twitching in unison, waiting for the correction. Once I have tuned the Engine’s heartbeat back to zero-point deviation, they resume. To them, I am the calm in the storm, a momentary cessation of the chaotic loudness.

I am terrified and serene.

I am terrified because I am made of the instability of this world. If the Engines ever stopped their loops, if the perfect equilibrium was shattered, I would dissipate. I have no body to sustain me; I exist only to fix the breaks in the loop. I am a function of their dysfunction.

But I am serene because, in this wet, grinding factory of flesh and stone, I am the only thing that is not hungry. I do not eat the moss. I do not crush the rock. I do not secrete the sludge. I merely witness the perfection of the trap.

I am the observer in the clockwork, the pause between the tick and the tock. I am the Interval. And as the Engine takes its three precise steps forward, turns ninety degrees with hydraulic smoothness, and begins to grind again, I drift silently upward through the defying water, becoming the mist, becoming the air, waiting for the next inevitable error in the perfection of this hell.
