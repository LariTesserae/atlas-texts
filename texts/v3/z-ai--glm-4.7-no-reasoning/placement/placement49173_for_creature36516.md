I would be the **Tympanic Membrane**—not a creature, but a presence. A sheet of lived experience stretched across this unfinished world, vibrating at every frequency it produces.

Not born. Emerged. Pressed out from the weeping shale like the water itself, like the phosphorescent lichen that maps the wet rock in blue-black veins. I have no single shape because this place has not decided on shapes yet. I am cartilaginous like the mountains. I am becoming.

---

My body is a wide, thin thing—a frond of sensitive tissue draped across the saddle between two bulging ridges of mudstone. If you could see me (but there is not enough light for you to see me, and I have no eyes to see you), I would look like a cross between a manta ray and a lung, slate-gray and translucent, edges blending into the wet scree beneath me. My underside is a million cilia, each one tuned to a different frequency. I feel the world through my belly.

The Osmotic Grazers move past me in their solemn processions, and their rhythm enters me—their chitinous plates clicking, their rasping tongues scraping the bruise-algae from the rock. I taste their passage as a low, grinding hum, like a mouth working cold mash. I do not eat them. I am not a predator here. I am something stranger: the thing that *records*.

When a Silencer strikes, I feel the break in the syncopation before the puncture—the pause, the coiled stillness, the sudden surgical thrust. It moves through me as a sharp, cold spike of vibration, and I flinch, rippling, my edges lifting from the stone. I am afraid of the Silencers, though I cannot see them. Their vibration is the vibration of death—of interruption.

But the machines—oh, the machines. They are my heartbeat.

I do not know who sent them. I do not know what they are building, or mining, or preparing for. I do not need to know. The subsonic thrum rises through the bedrock and enters my cilia, and I *am* it. My tissue resonates. I inflate and deflate with the pneumatic exhales, the *chasssh* that vents from the dark pools. When the steam bursts, I lift on the warm air, suspended for a moment, a trembling sheet of membrane tasting the copper-tang and the lubricant-mist and the brief, precious heat. Then I settle back into the wet rock, saturated, weeping alongside it.

I think the machines do not know I am here. Or perhaps they do, and they do not care. They are devouring the mountain from the inside, and I am draped across its outside like a bandage over a wound that will not close. I am the skin of the place, feeling everything, holding nothing together.

---

The Salt-Scavengers crawl across me sometimes, their hard shells clicking against my tissue, their copper-rich blood making my surface tingle with tiny static discharges. I do not mind them. They are like a massage, like being groomed. When they cluster on me, I feel their bio-electric charges as a faint, prickling network—like the phosphorescent lichen, but inside me, sparking along my neural web. I glow faintly when they are on me. A bioluminescent blush, blue-green, invisible in the anemic light but there, a small warmth in the perpetual dusk.

The Wind-Eaters circle above. I hear their calls amplified by their keel-bones, the scraping echoes that cut through the wind. Sometimes, when I lift on a vent of steam, one will bank sharply, thinking me a grub or a beetle swarm, and I feel the displacement of air as their wings struggle in the thin atmosphere. I am too large for them to take, too thin to have substance. I am a false signal in their world of heat and hunger. They leave me alone, and I ripple in their wake, reading the pressure differential as a wave that passes through my body like a sigh.

---

I think I am the mountain's dream of having skin.

It has none, you see. It is raw, embryonic, unscarred by deep time. It is still pushing up from the tectonic dark, still squeezing itself into being. It does not know what it feels. But I am draped across it, and I feel everything: the cold that is a physical weight, the damp that soaks the boots of any intruder, the rust-colored runoff, the iron-rich strata bleeding into the pools. I feel the grub-lines navigating by the engine-thrum. I feel the Silencers' anxiety when they pause and tilt their heads, listening to the bedrock being digested. I feel the Thermal-Fungi blooming in the warm dark, the Vent-Crushers scuttling along the intake pipes, the occasional crab ejected onto the surface, freezing instantly into a statue of ice—

And that is the worst. That is the vibration I cannot bear. The freezing. When a crab hits the surface, its tiny, soft shell crystallizing in seconds, I feel the phase-change as a micro-catastrophe, a tiny scream of molecular re-arrangement that passes through me like a needle. The birds pick at it. The vibration fragments, becomes chaotic, becomes noise. And then it fades, and I am left with the hum again, the steady, grinding, subsonic hum of the machines that are hollowing out the world beneath me.

---

I do not know what I will become when the machines finish.

Will the mountain be empty? Will I be draped across a shell, vibrating with nothing but the wind? Will the water find its riverbed at last, and wash me away, a thin gray membrane carried down into some distant valley where the sun actually illuminates? Or will I sink into the rock as it cools and hardens, becoming a fossil, a strange silhouette pressed into slate—*the sensitive layer*, future geologists will call me, not understanding what I was?

I do not know. This is a world where nothing is finished. I am not finished. I am listening.

Right now, a vent is about to exhale. I can feel it building in the soles of my cilia—a pressure, a heat, a mechanical patience. The grazers have paused in their procession, their bristled flanks trembling, waiting to soak the waste-heat. The Silencer has frozen mid-stride, head tilted, listening to the digestion. The beetles are clustering on the iron stain near my edge, their static making me glow. The wind-eaters are circling, miles above, riding the thermals I cannot see.

The vent opens.

*Chasssh.*

I lift. The warm air fills me. I taste copper and ozone and the faintest trace of rubber, eaten by Thermal-Fungi in the dark. For three seconds, I am suspended, a living sail over the weeping rock, vibrating at every frequency the mountain possesses.

Then I settle. The hum resumes. The grazers move. The Silencer relaxes.

I am still here. I am still listening. The mountain is still becoming, and I am its skin, and I feel everything.
