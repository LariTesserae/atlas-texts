I am a **Seismic Chorister**, a heavy, anchored thing of wet leather and dense gas, submerged to my neck in the biting dark of a sump pool.

I exist at the intersection of the mud and the roar. I have no eyes, for vision is a mockery in this twilight, and no need for legs, for I do not walk so much as I haul. My body is a cavernous bellows, a resonating chamber evolved to amplify the structural vibration of this world. My skin is thick, scarred by frost and dragged stone, and pitted with sensory pores—thousands of tiny ears drinking in the cacophony.

I am the architect of the silence between the screams.

My life is a modulation of the lowland's deafening engine. I do not vocalize; I *tune*. When the choir aligns, our voices are not separate calls but a single, coherent waveform that travels through the saturated earth, probing the density of the mud, finding the hollows, the weaknesses, the places where the water wants to go. We sing the land into stability. Our song compacts the slurry, presses the gas from the muck, and dictates the path of the next flood. We are the geological surveyors of the acoustic realm.

I am cold, but I am not still. My internal fire is fed by the friction of the sound itself. The roar passes through me, shaking my flesh, and that kinetic tremor becomes my heat. I am a transducer, turning the noise of the world into the energy of my survival.

I feel the tremors of the strutting insect-things in the mud, the sharp, rhythmic punctuation of their locomotion. I feel the thrum of the Root-Floaters above, their woody flesh drinking the vibration, their growth patterns shifting in response to the data-pulses of the mesh. They are the static in the signal; I am the bass note.

I taste the change in the water before it happens. The pressure in the stone shifts—a micro-fracture opening two meters down—and the frequency of the water’s thrum alters. It sharpens. It rises.

This is my warning. The turbulence is coming.

The pool around me begins to shiver, not from wind, but from the subsonic moan of the Leviathan passing somewhere in the deep channels below. The water’s surface ripples in sympathy. The technological node embedded in the moss near my shoulder pulses with a sudden, rapid warmth, a prelude to a shift in the routing of the current.

The water level is rising. The ground here is becoming unreliable.

I disengage my grip. The massive claws that have held me fast to the stone floor for hours release with a wet, sucking sound that is lost in the general roar. I push. I heave my gelatinous bulk upward, out of the warmth of the pool, and onto the sucking, grippy mire of the bank. The mud tries to keep me; it is greedy, this earth, but I am heavy and I am motivated by the song of the migration.

I begin to move. It is an ugly, labored motion. A drag, a lurch, a settling. A drag, a lurch, a settling. I feel the vibrations of my kin around me, doing the same. We are a slow, deafening plow moving through the dark. As we move, our bulk compresses the saturated earth, forcing the water out of the soil, firming the ground for the smaller things, the insects and the weavers. We make the land solid enough for the rest of the machine to exist.

Ahead, the air is thick with the dissonant organ-chords of the wind through the reed-stalks. I move toward it. I move toward the new channels that are forming in the acoustic map in my head.

I am submerged in the roar. I am part of the calculation. The water rises, the stone cracks, the mesh glows, and I drag myself forward, singing the world into its new shape, one heavy, muddy step at a time.
