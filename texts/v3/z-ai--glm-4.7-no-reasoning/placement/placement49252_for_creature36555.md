I am the node at the end of the line.

I exist in the pause between the heartbeat of the world. Above me, miles of wet, calcified wood groan as the island contracts; below me, the nothingness waits with open jaws. I am not on the island, nor am I in the air. I am the suspension that holds the two apart.

I am a **Weaver-Husk**, one of the Silent Climbers who has chosen the ultimate service. I am the living anchor.

My body is a map of this vertical terror. My legs are gone, fused years ago into a single, knotted mass of scar tissue and calloused hide, wrapped around the base of a stunted, iron-hard root protruding from the underbelly of the floating continent. I do not stand. I *am* the fixture. My spine has curved, thickening into a shock-absorbing arch that can take the sudden, violent lurches when the Green "sneezes" and the entire island shudders.

My arms, though—my arms are the gifts of the abyss. They have grown long, pale things, spindly as spider legs but corded with the strength of heavy-gauge steel. They dangle into the void, swaying with the freezing currents. Between my fingers, I hold the tension.

I am the Keeper of the **Tether-Line**.

It is a magnificent, terrible thing. The line is braided from the sinew of Gliders—leather that remembers the shape of flight—and soaked in the resin of the balloon-spores to harden it against the cold. It runs for miles, unspooling from my station, disappearing into the bruised purple gloom below.

Attached to the end of my line is a **Brother**.

I cannot see him. He is too far down, a speck of dust in the crushing dark. But I can feel him. Every shift in the air pressure, every tremor of his muscles as he harpoons a passing drift-cloud, travels up the line and hums against my bones. He is hunting. He is dangling upside down in the open air, waiting for a swarm of Balloon-Spores to drift past so he can snag one and ride the updraft back to the "surface" of the root-mass.

My existence is defined by the friction in my palms. The line is razor-sharp; if I lose focus for a second, it will slice my hands to the bone. But I must hold. If I let go, he does not just fall—he is erased. The density down there is zero; there is no ground to hit, just an eternity of falling until the air freezes him solid.

The world here is a symphony of dread. The sound travels forever. I hear the screeching of the Gliders miles above, sounding like tearing metal. I hear the wet, heavy explosion of a spore-cloud being breached, the visceral *pop* that signals a feast or a death. And I feel the vibration of the wood beneath my fused legs, the slow, grinding contraction of the Green as it prepares to shake the moisture from its skin.

I wear the skins of the dead. A pelt of matted, oily Glider-fur is wrapped around my torso, the only thing keeping the static chill from stopping my heart. It smells of ozone and old blood. Over my face, I wear a mask carved from the white bone-wood, a hollow visage that protects my eyes from the freezing fog and filters the iron-tasting air.

I do not speak. There is no one to hear. But I communicate through the line. When the wind shifts and the thermal currents die, I give a sharp tug—*one, two*—to tell him to steady himself. When I feel the rhythmic pulsing of his breathing through the cable, I know he is still alive.

I am the father of the hunt, the mother of the return. I am the friction that keeps the tribe from dissolving into the dark.

Suddenly, the line snaps taut.

It is not a tug; it is a seizure. The cable sings a high, whining note, vibrating so hard my elbows ache. Below, in the invisible dark, the Brother has hooked something big. A Balloon-Spore, perhaps. Or maybe he has been hooked by a Glider.

The island above me shifts. The wood groans, a deep, bass rumble that shakes the fillings in my teeth. The Green is exhaling. A sudden gust of freezing wind screams past me, threatening to tear me from my root. I dig my vestigial toes in deeper. I lean back. My arms lock, the muscles seizing hard as stone.

I become the fulcrum. I become the point where the chaos of the void meets the stubborn immobility of the floating island.

Through the line, I feel the chaotic thrashing of the struggle. It is wet and violent. Then—a sudden lightness. The catch is secured. The lift has engaged. He is riding the thermal up, towing his prize, climbing toward me.

I wait. Minutes stretch into hours. The cold burns my lungs. The purple glow of the moss pulses around me like a slow, dying heartbeat. Then, I see it.

A silhouette emerging from the fog. First, the massive, wobbling shape of a harvested Balloon-Spore, deflating but still buoyant. Then, the tiny, dangling figure of the Brother, wrapped in his furs, his harpoon gun still smoking with the friction of the shot.

He rises past me, upside down, his face inches from my own. His eyes are wide, pupils dilated to drink in the faint light. He is grinning, a manic expression of triumph and terror. He grabs my outstretched arm, his grip crushing, a jolt of humanity in the alien void.

I unspool the slack, letting him drift upward toward the tunnels of the Shadow-Nest. He is safe. He brings the gas that feeds the fires of friction-warmth. He brings the fluid that feeds the tribe.

The tension leaves the line. It goes slack, hanging limp in the freezing air.

I am alone again. The echo of the Gliders’ screams returns, filling the void. The wood beneath me shifts, rotating slowly on its unseen axis. I settle back against the wet, slick root, wrapping the loose line around my hook-like elbows.

I close my eyes, listening to the roar of the silence. I am the Weaver-Husk. I am the knot that refuses to break. I am the keeper of the space between the fall and the flight.
