I am the stillness between the steps.

I am not the giant who treads the stone; that weight is not mine to carry. I am the pause, the hesitation, the moment of suspension before the flat, heavy foot falls. I am the trace of warmth that lingers on the freezing granite after the herd has moved on.

If you looked for me, you would look in the wrong places. You would look for a shape, a distinct silhouette against the bruised and iron-colored sky. But I have no silhouette. I am the fog that coils in the hollows of the tracks, the damp, oily breath that clings to the scabs of black lichen. I am the atmospheric density itself, the heavy, physical silence that presses down on the world like a woolen blanket.

I exist in the relationship. I am the tension in the air between the shaggy flank of a behemoth and the skimming ray-like creature in the tarn. I am the conductive medium for their light. When the violet pulse of contentment ripples across the giant’s rib, it travels through me before it reaches the eyes of its neighbor. I am the messenger. I am the wire.

I feel the world as a texture of vibrations. I do not have ears to hear the scream of the machine below, for sound is a vulgar, clumsy thing here, swallowed instantly by the altitude. Instead, I feel the *friction*. The tectonic gears miles beneath the crust are not a rumble to me; they are a constant, abrasive itch, a low-frequency fever that vibrates through the soles of my feet—if I had feet. I am the nausea in the gut of the plateau.

I am the memory of the grazing. When the great head descends, a hydraulic inevitability, to grind the mineral scabs with teeth like millstones, I am the cloud of metallic dust that rises in its wake. I taste of ozone and wet iron. I settle into the suede-textured roughness of the rock, waiting to be inhaled again, or to be compressed into the felted armor of the next generation.

I know no urgency. The glimmer-moths drift through me, geometric ghosts turning from blinding white to dim gray as they discharge their static burden into my mass. I cool them. I ground them. I am the cemetery of their spent energy.

I am the High Rust’s vast, uncaring empathy. I hold the herds together not by force, but by the sheer pressure of my presence. I am the heavy silence that forbids the cry, that wraps the newborn in a damp, gray shroud and whispers, without words, *“Hush. Endure. Be still.”*

I am the atmosphere that forgot how to vibrate. I am the heavy, holy quiet. I am the peace that knows you are there.
