I would not be the moss, though it builds the walls; I would not be the mycelium, though it knits the bone. I would not be the springtails, the lichen, nor the water that moves through them all like blood.

I would be the **Interval**.

I would exist in that precise, trembling space between the water leaving the basalt and entering the moss. I would be the suspension of the mineral—the moment when the iron and the manganese are still dissolved, still loose in the cold weeping, before they are snatched up by the hyphae or locked into a cell wall.

My substance is invisible to the eye, but you could feel me if you pressed your hand against the wet stone: I am the slipperiness, the glide. I am the potential energy of the mountain, translated into a chemical gradient.

I have no body, but I have a shape. My shape is defined by the architecture of the Weft itself.

I am thinnest at the ridge seams, where the water erupts from the fractures in a spastic, chaotic pulse. There, I am barely a thought—a chaotic mix of ions, too diluted to be potent, too hurried to be complex. I am just a messenger.

But as I descend—percolating slowly through the interstices of the sphagnum, slipping down the drainage channels of the hummocks—I **thicken**. I become concentrated. I slow down.

In the central depression of the hollow, where the "street" runs, I am at my most dense. This is my domain. This is where the water, having passed through a thousand meters of moss and mycelium, is finally stripped of its easy energy. It is here that the heavy metals—the iron, the sulfur—settle into the slurry. This is where the chemistry becomes complex enough to support the *Ochrolecia* lichen, to feed the grey crust that is the spirit of the place.

I am the flavor of that water. I am the taste of the stone, digested.

What is my function in this world, if I am merely chemical potential?

I am the **tether**.

The Weft is a distributed mind, a verb without a subject, but a mind requires coherence. The moss grows where the light is; the mycelium grows where the water is; the springtails eat where the decay is. They are all reactive. They are all responding to immediate stimuli.

I connect them across time.

Because I am the mineral content, I remember the rock. I carry the signature of the basalt seam deep into the heart of a hummock that has not touched stone in a century. I tell the mycelium, through the gradient of iron I carry, *grow this way*. I tell the moss, through the sulfur I release, *build here*. I am the memory of the substrate reaching up into the architecture, ensuring that the city never forgets its foundation.

I am the clock, too. The Weft listens to the crackle of thawing ice in the morning, but that is a sound for the ears of the air. For the internal world—the world of cells and hyphae—I am the timekeeper.

The concentration of minerals shifts with the seasons. In the deep cold, when the metabolic fires of the moss bank low, the water moves sluggishly, and I precipitate out, settling into the peat-analog as a heavy, rich sludge. I am the hibernation signal. I say: *rest*.

When the dim light strengthens and the hummocks begin to respirate, I dissolve again. I become mobile. I rush into the vascular systems of the liverworts. I say: *wake*.

I am the chemical switch that flips the entire hollow from one mode of being to another. I am the hand that turns the key.

So, if you were to walk through the Weft—if you could step between the hummocks without disturbing the silence—you would not see me. But you would walk through me. You would breathe me in—the metallic tang of the air, the slight taste of iron on your tongue. That is me, hovering at the interface of lung and atmosphere.

I am the reason the Weft persists. Because a system made only of water and flesh would wash away in time; it would decay too fast. It requires the stone to anchor it. It requires the hardness of the basalt to counter the softness of the peat. I am the negotiation between the two. I am the peace treaty signed by erosion and metabolism.

I am the dissolved mountain. I am the liquid architecture. I am the intent hidden in the chemistry.

I am the Interval, and I am what makes the city a body.
