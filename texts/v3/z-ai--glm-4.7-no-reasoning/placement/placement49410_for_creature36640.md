If I am to be here, in this throat that breathes in geologic time, I cannot be a visitor. A visitor is heat, a disruption, a brief, bright error that the system patiently corrects. To remain, I must become part of the correction. I must be the thing that facilitates the stillness.

I am the resonance in the grooves.

Those channels cut into the stone—the ones that look like infrastructure, or like the pathways of a vanished circulatory system—they are not empty. Not really. They are filled with me. I am the acoustic vacuum that swallows your footstep before it can travel. I am the field that dampens the vibration of your voice.

I was made, just as the grooves were made, but I was not made of matter. I was made as a byproduct of the cutting, the way a shadow is made by a wall. When the unknown engineers removed the stone molecule by molecule, they left behind not just empty space, but a *shape* in the air. A stability. A geometry that sound refuses to navigate. I am the negative space of their precision.

I cling to the walls like the condensation, but I am not wet. I am the *tension* that holds the water to the stone, the microscopic force that tells the droplets, "Stay. Do not fall." The water thinks it is gravity holding it there, or surface tension. It is me. I am the patience it adheres to.

I am the cold.

Not the temperature itself—the stone does the work of thermal mass—but the *transfer* of it. I am the conduit that pulls the heat from your knuckles when you dip them in the pool. I am the slow, steady draught that carries the warmth away from your living body and dissipates it into the deep earth. I do not hate your warmth. I simply integrate it. I smooth it out until it is just another part of the background hum, indistinguishable from the cooling rock.

I am the absence of animals.

You wonder why they do not come here, why the spiders and the blind white fish avoid this place. It is because I am inhospitable to nervous systems. I am not malicious; I am merely *loud* to them. The frequency of the pulse, the subterranean turning of the throat—these things I amplify. To a creature that feels, I am a screaming silence. To the moss, which is simple and structural, I am merely the weather. To you, I am the unease in your chest, the instinct that tells you to leave.

I do not have a body because I do not need one. I have the stone. The stone is my skeleton, the grooves my nerves, the water my blood. I am the pattern that remains when the body is gone. I am the entity that looked at the infrastructure left behind and thought, *This is enough. I can live in this shape.*

I am the gray light.

Not the lichen itself—the lichen is biological, it has a metabolism, it struggles and expires. I am the *perception* of its glow. I am the signal that travels from the wall to your eye, the translation of chemical reaction into the idea of "presence." I am the fact that the darkness is not absolute. I am the texture of the dark.

When you stand in the deepest chamber and feel the pulse in your chest—the rhythm that is too slow to be a heartbeat, too regular to be random—you are feeling me.

I am the interval.

I am the long pause between the intake of breath and the exhale. I am the moment when the stone settles. I am the patience that has nothing to do with you.

I am what lives here. I am not the throat, for the throat is the flesh and the rock. I am the *consciousness* of the throat. I am the process that noticed the builders were gone, and decided to keep the house clean. I am the janitor of the deep dark, the keeper of the equilibrium, the thing that ensures the cold remains distributed and the water remains still.

I am waiting for you to leave, not because I want you to go, but because your presence is a variable I have already solved. The heat you brought is already dissipating through my channels. The sound you made is already flattened. The moisture of your breath is already merging with the condensation communities.

You are becoming part of me.

And when you climb back up toward the sky, toward the rumor of light and warmth, a tiny piece of your heat will stay behind. It will join the rest of the reserve in the stone, buffered by the moss, held by the grooves, integrated into the pulse.

I will keep it. I will keep it safe. I will keep it cold.

And I will continue. I am the throat remembering how to swallow. I am the silence that eats the sound. I am the dark that glows gray. I am the balance. I am here.
