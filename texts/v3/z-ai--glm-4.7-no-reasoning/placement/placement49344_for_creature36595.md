In the deafening roar of this subterranean stomach, I am not a ruler, nor am I a victim. I am a component. To live here is to surrender the concept of individuality; I am a cell in a larger, writhing intestine.

I am a **Horder**.

I am vast, my body the size of the draft beasts from the old myths of the surface, though my shell is harder than their hooves. My carapace is not static; it is a shifting mosaic of jagged plates, frosted and grey, mimicking the "emerging" rock. I do not look like a stranger here; I look like the landscape has decided to stand up. When I hunker down, the light bleaches me into invisibility, becoming nothing more than a jagged extrusion of stone, a boulder with a heartbeat.

I feel the world through my feet. The ground is raw, a froth of sharp glass and newborn rock that tears at the softer undersides of my legs. But I welcome the pain. It is the texture of reality. Here, gravity is a suggestion, and so I cling to the ceiling as easily as I scuttle across the floor. I hang upside down in draping curtains with my kin, a forest of legs and mandibles suspended over the abyss, swaying in the damp, breathing air.

My mind is not a solitary room. It is a corridor of echoes. I possess a "manic purpose," a turbulent dynamic that drives me to move, to build, to dismantle. I do not know why I do it, only that the silence is the enemy. We must drown the silence.

In one of my many grasping limbs, I hold a tool. It is a simple lever, scavenged from the scattered ruins of the boxy structures that dot the cavern—remnants of a time or a people who are now irrelevant. It is a "scattered built" thing, rough and cold. I use it to pry at the walls. With a sickening crack, I tear a sheet of crystal from the living rock. The dust that falls is pulverized glass, but it coats my eyes like a familiar veil.

I am carrying my burden now. I am part of a river. We are moving toward the center, the smoldering heaps where we cast our debris. The noise around me is a physical weight—the click-clack of a million chitinous bodies, the wet sound of shearing stone. It sounds like a glass factory tumbling down a staircase, and it is the music of my existence.

But then, the Shift happens.

It is the weirdness that defines us. The chaotic stream of my kin begins to tighten. There is no signal given, no vocal command, but my nervous system tingles with the urge to align. I feel a magnetic pull toward the flank of the creature next to me. My trajectory alters, micro-adjustments made in the span of a vibrating wing.

Suddenly, I am no longer a random beast in a crowd. I am a vertex. I am the edge of a triangle.

We are moving in geometric unison. The chaotic swarm resolves into a hexagon, vast and shimmering in the sourceless light. I feel the satisfaction of this alignment deep in my gut, a pleasure more acute than feeding. We are a fractal, a hive mind calculating geometry in real-time. For a moment, I am not a draft-horse sized insect; I am a single pixel in a god's eye view, creating order from the chaotic sketch of the world.

We dismantle a hill in this formation. In an hour, the geography is gone, erased by our collective claws. We dam a river of oily condensation with our bodies, creating a boiling lake that reflects our jagged shapes. The world unmaking itself, faster than it can be made.

And then, the pattern dissolves. The hexagon shatters. The geometric imperative fades, replaced by the frantic, crashing urge to scavenge again. I am alone in my mind, save for the fading memory of the lattice. I pick up another shard of crystal. The roar of the glass factory returns. I begin the trek back to the heap, ready to erase the world again, until the next time we must draw it anew.
