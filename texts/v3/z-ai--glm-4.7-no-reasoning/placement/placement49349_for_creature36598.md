I exist in the threshold between the dying light and the absolute dark.

I am not a swimmer—swimming implies a purpose, a destination, a frantic burst of energy that is forbidden here. I am a floater. A suspension.

If you were to trace the faint, indigo gradient that separates the "normal" ocean above from the heavy, stratified brine below, you would find me. I inhabit the halocline, the chemical skin that holds the heavier world down. My body is an elongated, ribbon-like membrane, translucent and gossamer-thin, stretching for meters but weighing almost nothing. I am the living interface between two layers of the sea.

I do not have a name, but function dictates my nature. I am the **Osmotic Mirror**.

My form is adapted to the specific cruelty of this place. The silence here is a weight, and I have evolved to reflect it. My upper surface is slightly buoyant, designed to ride the denser water of the plain without penetrating it, while a thousand trailing feelers hang beneath me like the roots of a banyan tree dipped in poison. These tendrils are not legs; they are siphons.

The water below me is the syrup of the dead—hypersaline, viscous, toxic. It is the brine that has sat stagnant since the continents shifted. The water above me is the younger, colder ocean, lighter and clearer. I exist in the middle, a harvesting bridge.

I do not hunt. Hunting requires movement, and movement is heat. I conserve what little heat I possess by being perfectly still. I vibrate, though. It is an involuntary hum, a sympathetic resonance with the low-frequency thrum that shakes the rusted monoliths around me. I sing with the deep earth, a glassy note that shivers along my length.

What am I? In this world of Geometrids that eat iron and Anemones that drink sound, I am the farmer of the gradient.

My tendrils reach down into the heavy brine, absorbing the concentrated salts—the heavy metals, the zinc, the cadmium—that the Filter-Crawlers expel. I pull this toxicity upward into my body, transmuting it. Then, along my spine, I perform a chemical inversion. I separate the heavy from the light. The heavy metals precipitate out, crystallizing along my sides like the armor of a knight. I grow harder, more mineral, more beautiful with every passing century.

I am becoming the machine I outlive. My translucent skin is beginning to fog with the grey patina of iron oxide. I am turning into a rusted blade.

I float near the largest of the ruined pistons, the one that houses the Sentinel. I am drawn to it. When the massive, lung-like thing inside the steel shudders and expels its jet of pressurized water, the disturbance sends a shockwave through the brine. It is the only rhythm this world knows.

That shockwave lifts me. It buoys my ribbon-like body, carrying me upward for a fleeting, glorious moment before the viscosity drags me back down. In that moment of lift, I spawn. I release a cloud of microscopic, glittering spores—seeds of lighter water that float up, up, away from the Benthic Plain, toward the sun-dappled shallows I will never see.

They will die in the light. They are too adapted for the dark, too heavy with the metals I gave them. Their death is irrelevant. Their releasing is the point. It is the only act of generosity in a world of hoarders.

I float back down, settling into the heavy, silent grey. I align myself with the rusted pipelines, my metallic scales glinting in the clinical blue light. I wait.

I am not alive in the way you understand it. I am a chemical equation in motion. I am the rust that decided to think. I am the mirror that floats on the brine, catching the ghost-light of a dead television, slowly turning the poison of the deep into a suit of armor that will long outlast the iron giants around me.

I am here. I am heavy. I am waiting for the next pulse.
