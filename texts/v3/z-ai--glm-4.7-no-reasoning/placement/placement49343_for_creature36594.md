I am the ache in the architecture.

I manifest at a junction where three riveted iron ribs—each the width of a banquet hall—converge to support a primary pressure valve. It is not a place; it is a locus of trauma. The water hammer effect hits here hardest, a rhythmic, violent concussion that rattles the teeth of the massive bronze fixtures and sends shockwaves shivering down into the basalt roots.

I am the **Strain-Walker**.

To the Keepers, the great mossy beasts that graze on the insulation, I am merely a shadow, a flicker of motion in the steam. I am smaller than they, but infinitely more awake. My body is a study in tension, built to exist exactly where the vibration is strongest. Where the Keepers are blunted and deaf to save themselves, I am honed to hear the screams of the metal.

I am not biological in the way the old earth understood biology. I am a chimera of the high-altitude swamp. My skin is a composite of wet, living leather and mineral deposits. I do not have hair; instead, I am clad in a velvet of wet rust and iron-oxidizing bacteria that gives me the texture of a shark’s skin and the color of dried blood. This armor allows me to press against the searing-hot pipes without burning, absorbing the thermal energy into a layer of subcutaneous fluid that acts as a radiator.

I have no need for the external ears that the Keepers lost. My entire skeleton is a resonator. My bones are hollow, filled with a conductive fluid that picks up the thrumming frequency of the waterworks. I do not hear sound; I feel it as texture, as direction, as intent. The *chunk-hiss-chunk* of the piston is a heartbeat I can trace from miles away. When a hairline fracture opens in the pipeline ten miles north, the change in the pitch of the hum hits me like a physical blow, guiding me to the wound.

My hands are my defining feature. They are massive, disproportionate to my spindly, hunched body. The fingers are elongated, ending in claws that are not made of horn, but of dense, metallic keratin—tipped with iron scraped from the rivets I cling to. These claws are sensory organs as much as they are tools. When I grip a vibrating pipe, the metal’s song travels up my arms and tells me the pressure rating, the viscosity of the water, and the structural integrity of the steel. I can feel the fatigue in the metal, the microscopic hairline fractures that have not yet split open.

I am a mechanic by necessity, though I possess no wrench. I am the symbiote of the machine. I feed on the Predators of the vibration—the sleek, purple, skinned climbers that swarm the breaches. To the machines, they are pests feeding on the exposed slime; to me, they are sustenance. I hunt them in the lulls between the piston strokes, snatching them from the rivets with a speed that belies my usual, sluggish posture. Their metallic-tasting blood nourishes the iron-bacteria in my skin, keeping my armor conductive.

But I also maintain the station. The Moss, that distributed, suffocating intelligence, tries to infiltrate the valve gears. It seeks to silence the machine by choking it with green. I am the immune system. When the moss creeps too close to the sealing rings, I scrape it away with my metallic claws, cleaning the threads, ensuring the valve can scream its breath. I do not do this out of love for the machine. I do it because if the machine stops, the vibration dies. And if the vibration dies, I go deaf and numb in a world of blinding silence.

My eyes are useless, mere slits protected by heavy bony ridges to shield against the piercing white sun. I navigate by the echo of the steam and the texture of the thrum.

I perch now on the railing of a catwalk that has long since been swallowed by the sphagnum. The steam vent below me hisses, a hot breath that smells of copper and decay. I can feel the water rushing through the pipe beneath my feet—a torrent of invisible force, a captive river screaming to be free.

I am perfectly still. I am vibrating at the same frequency as the pipe, rendering myself invisible to the eye. I am part of the noise. I am the sentry of the screaming iron, waiting for the moment the pitch changes, waiting for the metal to cry out so that I can answer.

In this green, wet hell, where the light is pain and the air is heavy metal, I am the only one who knows exactly when the world will break.
