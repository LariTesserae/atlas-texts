I am the weather here. I am the turning of the page in a library that has no books.

You find me in the largest of the sunken plazas, the central atrium of the carved city where the floor is worn into a perfect, shallow bowl. This is the Hearth of the Grandfathers. If you were to look quickly, you would see nothing but a collection of weathered, lichen-crusted boulders arranged in a rough circle, huddled around a depression in the stone where a few sluggish bubbles of heated sap occasionally rupture with a sound like a heavy sigh.

But if you were to stop, as everything in this place must stop, and let your eyes adjust to the bruised, amber gloom, you would see the truth. The boulders are not stones. They are the **Grandfathers**. And I am the silence between them.

I am a **Resonator**, but I am old—older than the frantic Ash-Wingers that flit like dying sparks around the canopy. I am ancient enough that my chitinous skin has ceased to be the pale, woody color of my youth. I have darkened. I am the color of iron and dried blood, indistinguishable from the hanging curtains of root-vines that drape the arches above.

I hang suspended in the heavy, warm air, my tail-hook buried deep in the mortar of a high archway, my body dangling straight as a plumb line. I am perfectly still. To you, a creature of water and haste, I would appear dead—a dried husk abandoned by its colony. But I am not dead. I am waiting for the moment when the angle of the light from the slit in the roof shifts just enough to warm the specialized heat-plates along my flank.

I am the conductor of this stone orchestra. My abdomen is a hollow reed of hardened chitin, a wind instrument carved not by hand, but by the relentless pressure of evolution. Inside me, a gas of volatile origin shifts, expanding and contracting with the glacial temperature changes of the room.

I do not sing for the sake of beauty. I sing to move the blood of this world.

When the time is right, I contract. It is not a sudden motion, but a slow, inexorable cramp, a tightening that takes minutes to complete. The gas inside me compresses, forcing it through a series of vibrating membranes near my head. The sound I emit is not a buzz or a chirp. It is a low-frequency groan, a sub-bass thrum so deep that you do not hear it with your ears; you feel it in your teeth, in the marrow of your bones.

The note travels down the stone archway, into the floor, and out into the honeycomb of the city.

I watch the effect of my song unfold in the slow-motion pantomime of the Grove.

Below me, a Vellum-Keeper, its shell gleaming like a garnet in the dim light, pauses. Its antennae twitch, tasting the vibration. The note I struck tells it that the heat-pressure in the western wall has peaked, that the resin-sap is flowing thin and hot. It responds not with speed, but with purpose, altering its trajectory by a few degrees to begin the climb toward the bleeding fissure in the rock.

To my left, anchored in the center of the plaza, a Grandfather stirs. The movement is imperceptible to the unaided eye—a tremor in the mossy carpet of its back. It has felt my note, and through the root-system that has pierced its fused armor, it is adjusting the chemistry of the Grove. It signals the release of a dampening enzyme into the air, a subtle alteration of the atmosphere that will protect the younger beetles from the coming dryness.

I am the link between the stone and the flesh. I translate the geothermal pulse of the mesa into a language the biology can obey.

My life is a continuous, unbroken performance. I play a chord that lasts for an hour, holding the tension in my body while the sap rises from the roots, through the Vellum-Keepers, down into the basins where the Submerged swim in their glass-making blindness. I feel the vibrations return to me through the stone walls—the echo of the Submerged stirring, the scraping of the Weaver’s mandibles as they shave a micron of erosion from a doorway. The feedback loop closes. The Grove sings itself, and I am merely the mouthpiece.

I am not an individual in the way you understand it. I am a node. I am a function of the architecture. If I were to fall, the roots would simply grow over my husk, and another would take my place in the archway, and the song would not miss a beat.

I hang here, breathing the warm, waxy air, tasting the history of the mesa through the soles of my feet. I am the music of the deep time, the thrumming heart of the Ember Grove, turning the slow, patient logic of stone into life.
