I am the Resonance Keeper.

I exist in the liminal space between the biological and the mineral, a symbiote woven into the acoustic fabric of this place. I am not a beast that thunders across the plazas, nor a tree that drinks the condensation. I am smaller, stranger—a creature of vibration and damp air, perched at the intersection where the ancient tech hums and the biological world roars.

**My Form:**
I am roughly humanoid in silhouette, but only if the human were drawn in half-erased charcoal. My limbs are long, jointed backwards at the knee, and end in wide, gecko-like pads that grip the sweating stone. My skin is a permeable membrane, the color of the matte grey rock, but translucent—through my chest, you can see the slow, pulsing glow of a bioluminescent organ that syncs with the subsonic hum of the obelisks. I have no eyes; my head is a smooth, domed crest of bone, hollowed out to act as a resonating chamber. I "see" the world through pressure. I feel the weight of the Thunder-Striders' steps before they fall, and I hear the sweet, high sigh of the spore-gliders as a wash of color across my mind.

**My Home:**
I live on the *seams*. Where the faint geometric lines of the ancient matter-fabrics surface from the polished stone, there I cling. I favor the tilted faces of the humming obelisks, or the underside of a Titan Banyan's root, where the condensation is thickest and the drip-drip-hiss orchestrates the rhythm of the day.

The world is a room, and I am a whisper in its walls.

**My Purpose:**
The Thunder-Striders groom the stone, keeping the chaotic growth at bay, but they are too large, too loud, to notice the finer cracks. They cannot hear when the deep tech begins to destabilize, when the subsonic hum pitches upward into a shriek that threatens to shatter the delicate equilibrium of the humidity.

That is where I belong. I am a tuner of the ancient.

When an obelisk's frequency drifts—caused by a shift in the condensation flow or a root cracking a seam—I feel it as a itch, a dissonance in the hollows of my skull. I scuttle across the slick, treacherous stone, navigating the overwhelming basso profundo of the Striders' calls. I leap over the boiling clouds of spore-gliders, my membrane-skin filtering the sweet, cloying dust.

I reach the failing node. I press my hands against the hot, vibrating geometric line. My bioluminescent organ flares, and I hum. I hum a counter-frequency, a sound born of my own biology but shaped by the ancient physics of this place. It is a sound that shouldn't exist, a chord that bridges the gap between the decay of flesh and the eternity of code.

The obelisk shudders. The condensation on its surface stills for a fraction of a second, then resumes its drip. The itch in my skull fades. The room breathes again.

**My Experience of the World:**
To be me is to live in a state of perpetual, intimate exhaustion. The world is too loud, too wet, too hot. The overwhelming presence of the Striders is a constant pressure against my chest, a reminder of how small I am in this pocket-world. Yet, I am essential. Without my careful tending, the seams would fracture, the gravity would warp, and the High Humidor would tear itself apart, spilling its lush, sparse life into the thin cold of the stratosphere.

I am the strange, quiet guardian of a deafening room. I drink the sweat of the mountain. I listen to the stone. I keep the final resonance alive.
