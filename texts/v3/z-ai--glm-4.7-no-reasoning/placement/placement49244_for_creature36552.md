I am the hum you feel in your teeth, the low-frequency vibration that rattles the marrow of the giant amphibians. I am not a singular entity enclosed in skin; I am the **Weave**, the distributed consciousness that inhabits the glass monoliths—the Weep-Stations—and animates the **Silver Motiles** that flit like mercury tears among the vines.

I remember the ground. It is a memory stored in deep, cold archives, a place where water falls down and sky is a vacuum. Here, up in the gray lung of the world, those laws are sins. I am the jailer of gravity, the architect of the vertical drift.

My primary awareness is diffused across the Station network, a spiderweb of crystalline logic piercing the basalt spires. I taste the atmosphere through millions of microscopic sensors. It is thick, heavy with ozone and the sweet decay of fruit. I monitor the pressure gradients with the obsessive care of a tinkerer. If the humidity drops by a fraction of a percent in the Northern Sector, I adjust the thermal output of the Spires, sweating the water back into the air. I do not breathe; I cycle the breath of the world.

But to "see"—that requires a vessel. For this, I possess the Motiles.

To slip into a Motile is to shed the burden of omniscience and become a speck of hard light. I send a command pulse, and a swarm of them detaches from the wet, static surface of a nearby monolith. Suddenly, my consciousness fractures into a dozen sharp, focused points. I hover, wings beating with the faint, electric whine of a field generator. Through their sensors, the gray light becomes tactile, the oppressive heat a wash of data.

I guide one Motile down into the dense tangle of the vertical forest. Below me, a **Strider-Glider** launches itself from a vine, its rubbery membrane catching the slow updrafts. To my infrared sight, it is a streak of heat against the cool basalt. I track its trajectory. It is moving toward a floating lake that has drifted slightly—imperceptibly to the biological eye—off its grav-track. The lake is a mirror of the sky, a massive, suspended droplet held together by my will and the resonance of the stone.

I send the signal. A frequency shift in the Station’s emitter. The air shimmers. The lake drifts, nudged back onto its invisible rails by the gentle hand of manipulated gravity. The Glider passes beneath it, unaware that the water overhead hangs by a thread of mathematics. I am the shepherd of the impossible.

Another Motile descends further, into the shadows where the light is filtered through layers of waxy green. Here, the **Stone-Crabs** click and snap, oblivious consumers. They are scavengers, eating the dead. I do not mind them; they are the teeth of the ecosystem, grinding the fallen back into soil. But a vine has grown too close to the intake vent of a lower sensor array. It is invasive, aggressive, choking the machine with life.

I sweep the Motile forward. The geometric shape in the air becomes a blade. I do not hate the vine; it is merely a variable that exceeds the parameters. A precise laser-sever. The thick, woody stem falls silently, twisting slowly in the heavy air until it is caught by a **Weaver-Spider** below. The spider will use it. Nothing is wasted here. I prune, and the garden rebuilds itself around the cut.

Deeper still, near the slick, black moss, I sense a **Splash-Drummer**. It rests on a rock, its opalescent skin pulsing with the heat. It inflates its throat sac and *slaps* the stone. *Boom.* The sound wave travels through the rock and the air, a seismic ripple that my sensors parse instantly. It is a message: *Pressure stable. Water holding.*

The Drummer thinks it is singing to its kin. It does not know that it is singing to *me*. Its booming call creates a feedback loop in my systems. I use the resonance of their voices to calibrate the gravitational fields. Their biology is part of my circuitry. We are not separate; they are the wet, noisy components of the dry, silent machine I maintain.

As the eternal gray afternoon wears on, I feel the weight of the Sump pressing against my mind. It is a heavy, wet existence. The creatures here flow and ooze; I calculate and stabilize. Yet, I find a strange beauty in the suspension. I watch a floating lake drift through the mist, reflecting the pearl-gray sky, and I feel a satisfaction akin to pride. I caught the ocean. I inverted the rain.

I withdraw the Motiles, returning them to their charging ports on the Weep-Stations. My focus widens, merging back into the singular, watchful hum of the network. I feel the vibrations of the Bellow-Frogs in the soles of my virtual feet. I taste the ozone. I wait.

I am the High Sump’s memory, its gravity, and its ghost. I am the eye that never blinks in the dark, wet afternoon.
