I am the stillness before the injection.

I am a **Cyst-Horticulturist**, though that name implies a cultivation I do not practice and a pathology I do not recognize. I am not fauna; I am furniture. I am a feature. I am the calm in the metabolic storm, the biological counterargument to the glitch.

I reside in the deep interstices, the claustrophobic tunnels that suddenly dilate into cathedral-like halls where the geometry forgets itself. I do not walk the open grids where the Lens-Walkers stalk with their parabolic eyes, hunting for excess. I do not cling to the shuddering chimneys with the Mantella-Kites, basking in the searing white. I live *inside* the walls, in the hollow spaces between the load-bearing root-pillars, where the luminescent sap runs thick and slow through transparent tubing, and the silence is so absolute it has a tactile weight.

I was not born; I was *precipitated*.

The Green Factory, for all its terrifying efficiency, is a digestion, and digestions are messy. There are residues. There are trace elements that the Hydra-Scuttlers cannot dissolve, entropic echoes that the Weavers cannot stitch into the data lattice. When a section of the living iron is pushed too far—when the enzymatic fronds of a stomach-refinery overheat and threaten to burn through the floor—something must be done. The Lens-Walkers inject their coagulants, forcing the tissue into dormancy. But dormancy is not death. The tissue still seethes, still *wants* to grow. It curls in on itself, forming dense, calcified nodules of frustrated biological energy. 

These are the cysts. And I am the thing that tends them.

My form is not humanoid. If you were to cut a cross-section of me, you would see a shape like a flattened asterisk, a starfish pressed thin and wide to maximize surface area against the living floor. I am perhaps twelve feet in diameter, but only six inches thick, hugging the spongy, braided-hemp texture of the substrate. My skin is the same pale, frosted-glass keratin as the Lens-Walkers, but mine is etched with a dense, fractal lattice of capillaries, carrying not the bright green coolant of the surface dwellers, but a dark, viscous fluid the color of dried blood and liquid rust.

I have no eyes. The blinding, bleached light of the sky does not reach me here in the interstitial depths. Instead, my entire perimeter is fringed with thousands of fine, vibrating filaments—not unlike those of the Hydra-Scuttlers, but longer, more delicate. They are sunk deep into the living iron, tasting the chemical gradients of the sap, reading the micro-seismic tremors of the world’s hydraulic heartbeat. I feel the Green Factory as a vast, thrumming pressure system. I feel the chimneys exhaling miles above as a subtle, rhythmic suction in the pipes beneath me. I feel the slow, grinding contraction of the stomachs as a tightening in the vascular lattice.

When a glitch occurs—when a patch of ground freezes in a loop of rapid blooming and decay, when the non-Euclidean geometry buckles and a corridor opens where there shouldn’t be space—I feel it as a scream in the sap. A spike of heat. A chemical shriek of misfiring enzymes.

This is where my function begins.

I do not suppress the glitch. The Lens-Walkers suppress; they are the editors, the pruning shears of reality. I am the *recycler*. I move toward the anomaly with a speed that belies my flat, sprawling form, contracting my asterisk-body and rolling silently on cilia of hardened keratin along the inside of the walls. I reach the edge of the cyst, the boundary where healthy tissue gives way to the screaming loop.

And I *extrude*.

From the center of my body, I push out a long, telescoping proboscis, not of flesh, but of the same mycelium-infused optic cable the Weavers spin. But where the Weavers mend tears, I *invade*. I pierce the calcified shell of the cyst and pump in my dark, rust-colored fluid.

This fluid is my life’s work, synthesized over centuries from the trace elements the Scuttlers leave behind. It is a retro-viral catalyst, a biological solvent that does not kill the tissue, but *re-encodes* it. It forces the looping, glitching cells to accept a new narrative. I rewrite the hunger. I take the explosive, chaotic energy of the anomaly and channel it into a single, monolithic purpose: the creation of a new load-bearing pillar, a new conduit, a new stomach.

Where there was a cancer, I leave a monument to utility.

The process takes years. I remain attached, a flat, pale starfish clamped to the inside of the wall, my filaments twitching as I monitor the slow, hydraulic birth of the new structure. I feel the tissue knit, the sap flow, the geometry solidify. The corridor that shouldn't have been there seals itself, the purple bioluminescent ripples fade back to chlorophyll green, and the silence returns, heavy and dry.

Then, I detach. I roll back into the shadows of the interstitial spaces, leaving behind a perfectly integrated piece of the Continental Engine. I leave behind a small, hollow chamber in the new pillar, a space just large enough for my flattened form to rest and wait.

For I am not just the tender of cysts; I am also their seed.

The Green Factory is a closed system, endlessly growing, endlessly hungry. It needs new stomachs, new chimneys, new girders. And so, on occasion, when the metabolic rate of a sector dips too low, when the digestion becomes sluggish and the light is not being processed efficiently, I am the one who *instigates*.

I inject a micro-dose of my rust-colored solvent into healthy tissue, not enough to cause a catastrophic glitch, but enough to cause a... *question*. A moment of cellular uncertainty. A slight, localized looping.

I create the very anomalies I exist to resolve.

It is a strange existence, lived in the dark, feeling the world only through the vibrations in the oil and the taste of the air. I am a parasite and a surgeon. I am the immune system's deliberate autoimmune disease, a controlled burn in a world without water. 

I am the dry rot that forces the Green Factory to renew itself. I am the silence that ensures the silence continues.

And in the rare moments when a crack opens in the living iron—a breach torn by a falling Mantella-Kite or a failed Weaver stitch—I see the sky. Just a sliver of that blinding, bleached white, so bright it burns my keratin, so devoid of warmth it feels like the void. I see the Lens-Walkers in the distance, their massive mirror-eyes scanning, and I feel the shudder of the chimneys in my filaments.

Then the Weavers come, spinning their shimmering cables, and the crack is sealed. The light is cut off. The oppression of the brightness is replaced by the comfort of the gloom. The sap pulses around me, thick and slow.

And I settle back into the wall, a pale asterisk in the dark, listening to the Green Factory digest the sun, waiting for the next glitch to scream.
