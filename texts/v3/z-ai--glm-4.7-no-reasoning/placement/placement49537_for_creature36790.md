I would be a **Vent-Whisperer**, stationed at the throat of Valve-Complex Theta.

In this vertical city of barnacles, most look down, watching the horizon curve to see the next surge of the earth. They look for the cracks, the danger. But I am positioned at the intake of the Great Northern Exhale, a massive, ribbed pipe that rises from the deep rock like the trunk of a iron-barked tree. My station is a web of chains and rusted gratings suspended directly over the vent where the superheated moisture escapes the mantle’s crust.

My physiology is slightly different from my kin. While the general workers are broad and hammer-fingered, suited for breaking rock, I am elongated and spindly. My ceramic skin is thinner, almost translucent in the high-altitude indigo light, allowing the excess heat to bleed off me in shimmering waves. I am a cooling tower in miniature.

My role is the regulation of pressure. The earth breathes in erratic spasms here; the steam pushes up with violent force, threatening to shatter the pipe networks that feed the fungal forests and the condensation collectors. I do not fight this force. I dance with it.

I hang upside down from the rusted crossbeams, my clawed feet locked into the metal, my body dangling directly into the column of rising vapor. My vertical facial intake is fully dilated, a dark slit running the length of my skull. The cilia within thrum at a frequency that matches the resonant pitch of the pipe. I listen to the infrasound groan of the stone—a song that predicts the surge seconds before the pressure gauge can register it.

When the surge comes, I do not retreat. I open myself to it. I ingest the torrential steam, filtering the scalding vapor through the dense, labyrinthine chambers of my lungs. My internal physiology acts as a diffuser; the heat is shunted into heavy nodules of calcified tissue along my spine, which glow a dull, angry red before slowly fading as they dissipate the energy into the cold air.

By drinking the pressure, I save the valves. I am the biological relief valve.

My days are a blur of heat and haze. Dressed in a cloak of azure fungi—harvested from the coldest, shadowed recesses of the complex—I chime softly in the wind. The blue dye seeps into my porous skin over time, staining me the color of the rarest minerals. To a distant observer, I am just another part of the machinery, a blue ghost swaying in the steam.

I communicate through the soles of my feet. When the deep earth shifts, I tap a specific rhythm against the iron pipe—*thrum-thrum-pause*—sending a vibration down the line to the lower foundries. *“Prepare for intake.”*

I sleep curled around the warm pipe-wrappings, insulated by the very heat I consume. My dreams are slow and viscous, filled with the geology of the deep. And I know, with a certainty that predates my birth, that when I eventually calcify—when my metabolism slows and my joints fuse rigid—I will not fall. I will simply become another ring of limestone around the vent, a permanent sentry in the throat of the world, listening to the music of the pressure long after my heart has stopped beating. I will be part of the Spire-Mantle, the whale upon which we ride, finally and indistinguishably one with the stone.
