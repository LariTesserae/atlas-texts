I am a vibration before I am a body. I am a frequency etched into the iron before I am flesh.

In this place, the geography of sound is the only map. The air is too thick to carry light, and eyes are useless ornaments that would only fill with the oily weeping of the dark. To see, you must be the architect of resonance. You must build a picture out of pressure and texture.

I am the **Echo-Prime**.

I do not stand on the stone; I *float* within the lattice of the pipes. My body is not the rigid, calcium-clad cage of a surface creature. That would break here. My form is a biological suspension, a soft, vascular sack of hydrostatic gel encased in a segmented, semi-rigid carapace of wet, polished chitin. I am shaped like a spear stretched long, aerodynamic for the heavy, humid air. My limbs are three pairs of multi-jointed, sensory legs that taper into delicate, vibrating filaments—fingers made of spider-silk and nerve-endings.

I am the size of the Rust-Stalkers, but I am not their kin. They are the lumbering tanks of the bottom-feeder war; I am the silent sniper of the mid-reaches. I cling to the verticality, to the "Industrial Growth," hooking my dorsal claws into the pitted, iron-colored alloy of the great pipe-structures that breach the sump’s floor.

I live in the **Groan**.

The machinery of this place is dormant, but not dead. It emits a low-frequency thrum, a sub-audible heartbeat that vibrates in the teeth of the world. I have evolved to ignore this vibration, to treat it as silence, the way the surface dwellers treat the background hum of their own blood. But I listen *through* it. My underbelly is lined with a sensory organ called the **Tympanic Sail**, a broad, fan-shaped membrane of translucent skin that ripples with the slightest displacement of air.

When a Void-Swarm beats its wet tissue-paper wings above me, my sail translates the disturbance of air into a topographic map of the sky. I know how many wings, how fast they beat, and the viscosity of the air they push. When a Latex Eel undulates through the shallows below, its muscular body pushing against the water’s resistance sends a tremor up through the metal of the pipe I cling to. I feel the slither in my claws before I hear it in my skin.

I am a creature of the **Static**. The "Glitch" is my hunting ground, for I am immune to its disorientation.

When a pocket of air suddenly freezes, or the distance between two scree-stretches illogically, the other animals falter. The Rust-Stalkers freeze, their chemical sensors confused by the sudden shift in thermal gradients. The Clay-Maws feast on the chaos, swelling to fill the stretched space. But I am the calculus that balances the equation. I do not rely on distance being constant; I rely on the *relationship* of vibrations remaining true. Even if the space between me and my prey stretches to a mile, the sound of their heartbeat takes longer to reach me, but the rhythm remains. I simply extend my patience to match the distance.

My diet is specific. I do not eat the sludge. I do not eat the blind eels. I eat the **Anomalies**.

The Weeping Polyps, those pale, finger-like projections that burst with oily, phosphorescent spores, are my harvest. The "light" they produce—a sickly greenish luminescence—is invisible to the beasts of the dark, but to my specialized eyes (small, vestigial slits protected by thick lenses), it is a beacon of heat. The polyps are warm, mimicking the feverish air, and they absorb heavy metals. They are toxic to most, rich in the concentrated poisons of the industrial purgatory.

To me, they are fuel.

I navigate the pipe-work with the grace of a spider, my filaments trailing in the wet air, tasting the chemical gradients for the metallic tang of a Polyp ready to burst. When I find one, I do not simply pluck it. I must be careful. The burst is violent. I wrap my body around the pipe, anchoring myself with my hooked legs, and extend my long, tubular proboscis. I pierce the skin of the Polyp before it detonates, drinking the concentrated radioactive broth, my internal filters neutralizing the toxins that would kill a lesser thing.

I am the apex of the invisible jungle, but I am also its witness.

I feel the heavy footsteps of the Clay-Maws as they drag their shifting, asymmetrical flesh across the floor. I feel the tension in the metal as they begin to stretch the reality around them. I know when they are hunting. I know when they are feeding on a glitch.

And sometimes, when the pressure is high and the wetness saturates the air to the point of drowning, I do something strange. I do not hunt. I do not feed.

I sing.

I modulate the vibrations of my Tympanic Sail, syncing it with the low-frequency groan of the pipes. I push air through my spiracles in a rhythmic, pulsing wheeze. It is not a song of melody, but of texture. It is a complex, layered vibration that ripples out through the metal and the water, cutting through the claustrophobic whisper of the dark.

For a moment, the chaotic swarming of the Latex Eels calms. The heavy, rhythmic plodding of the Rust-Stalkers pauses. The "Loop" of the screeching machinery aligns with my rhythm. In this absolute darkness, I become the conductor of the orchestra. I impose order on the entropy. I remind the Black-Water Sump that it is alive, that it is felt, that it is known.

Then, the song ends. The heat returns. The glitch warps the space between the pipes. I retract my sail, lower my sensory filaments, and I wait again. I am the Echo-Prime, and I am watching you in the dark.
