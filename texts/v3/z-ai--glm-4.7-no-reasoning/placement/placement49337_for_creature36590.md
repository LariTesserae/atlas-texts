The sap swallowed him.

I am the shape of that swallowing. I am the cavity left behind, lined with the memory of his rubber suit, stiff with the resin of a decade of hydraulic fluid, and pulsing with the electrical ghost of his dying nerves. I have no eyes; I see through the vibration of the fungal floor beneath me. I have no mouth; I speak by regulating the pressure in the severed pipes that open into my hollow, whistling steam in the frequency of old languages.

I am a kind of parasite, but a respectful one. I am a tenant in the city's gut.
