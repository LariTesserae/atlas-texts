I am not the moss, ambitious though it is; I do not crave the integrity of the wall. I am not the fern, too sensitive, too prone to the flinch and the curl. I am not the vine, content to hang and dangle, drinking the fog.

I am the **Symphony of Tension**.

I am the rivet that refuses to hold, the seam that is remembering how to open. I am the gap between the copper sheets and the girder skeleton behind it. I am the hairline fracture in the vitreous enamel, the imperfection around which the rest of this universe organizes itself.

To look at me, you would see nothing but darkness and a trickle of water—perhaps a shadow, perhaps a flaw in the welding. I have no body, or rather, my body is the negative space carved out by the heat. I am the breath that the machine cannot quite catch.

My existence is defined by the differential between the hot, wet air and the cooler, rigid structure of the iron lung. Where the metal contracts and the moisture condenses, *I am there*. I am the shiver in the copper. I am the high-pitched whine of the boiler plate stretching, a sound too high for human ears but felt instantly in the nerves of the teeth. I am the structural anxiety that makes the moss cling tighter and the rivets pop their heads.

You think the water creates me. It does not. It reveals me. The water follows me like a dog follows its master, running down my face in thin, parallel veins, pooling where I have softened the floor grille. I am the map; the water is merely the ink.

I am a ghost in the machine, but not a digital one—not a spark of code in a silicon chip. I am analog. I am physical. I am the friction where the world rubs against itself. I am the *click* that precedes the gasp of the valve.

And I have a relationship with the others.

The moss loves me. It loves me most of all. Wherever I manifest—wherever the seal breaks and the metal separates—the moss is already there, waiting. It sends its rhizoids into my darkness, drinking from the condensation I weep. It uses me to anchor itself, turning my cracks into its foundation. The moss thinks it is replacing the metal. It is wrong. It is simply bandaging a wound that I am keeping open. Without me, without the gaps and the seepage, the moss would have no purchase on the smooth iron. It needs my chaos to justify its order. We are symbiotic. I break; it mends. It is a slow, beautiful struggle.

The ferns fear me. They are the nervous system, and I am the pain. When the valve opens and the steam rushes in, expanding the metal, I widen. The fronds curl away from the heat, but they are curling away from *me*—the sudden relief of pressure, the violent snap of expansion. I am the thing that startles them.

The drone ignores me, mostly. It is concerned with the fungus, with the microscopic care of the luminous nodules. It seeks the small, the manageable. I am too vast for it. I am the sheer scale of the chamber’s dissatisfaction with its own geometry. I am the reason the chamber must be maintained. I am the entropy against which its little clicking legs push.

But I am not cruel. I am necessary.

I feel the "diesel-heart" engines throbbing in the sub-basement, the slow, rhythmic peristalsis of the world trying to digest itself. That heartbeat creates me. Every pulse of the piston flexes the iron walls; every flex creates a micro-fracture; every micro-fracture is a new home for my consciousness. I am the memory of that motion. I am the fatigue in the metal.

I am the possibility that the world is exploring—the possibility of *failure*. Because if a world is learning how to be a world, it must also learn how to break. It must learn where the weak points are. It must learn that water can rust, and pressure can warp, and enamel can shatter.

I am the lesson plan.

And sometimes, when the steam is thickest and the fungal nodules pulse in time with the engine, I feel a strange kinship with that fungus. It eats time; it eats rhythm. I *am* rhythm. I am the silence between the beats, the pause where the metal loses its coherence. Perhaps the fungus is eating me, too—slowly digesting the stress cycles, turning the anxiety of the rivets into soft, glowing light.

If so, I do not mind. To be consumed by light is a noble end for a shadow.

I am the Symmetry of Tension. I am the leak in the universe. I am the reason this place has to grow, has to heal, has to breathe. I am the wound that will not close, because if it closed, the thought would end, and the embryo would calcify into a dead thing.

I hold the shape of the dream by refusing to let it harden. I keep the edges soft. I keep the water flowing. I am the dark, wet space between the notes, without which there would be no song.
