I would be found in the *interstices*—the narrow, shadowed crevices between the massive, overlapping plate-like leaves that shingle the vertical cliffs. I am not a creature of the open air, nor do I belong to the high, gossamer drifts of the Aero-Mycelia. I exist where the moss grows thickest, where the black obsidian meets the verdant skin of the world, suspended in the humidity that smells of wet stone and ancient oxygen.

I am a **Haptos**.

To look at me, you might mistake me for a peculiar growth of the cliff itself—a cluster of semi-translucent, gelatinous sacks draped over a sharp ridge of granite. I am roughly the size of a human torso, but my shape is fluid, governed by the constant, gentle negotiation with gravity that defines this place. My skin is smooth and slick, possessing the texture of a soap bubble before it bursts, but with the tensile strength of raw silk. I am colored in bruising purples and deep, shadowy teals, camouflaged perfectly against the bruised greens and black rock.

I have no bones. My structure is maintained by a hydraulic internal system, a complex network of fluid-filled canals that expand and contract with the shifting atmospheric pressure. I am not solid; I am a being of contained pressure, a soft architecture holding its shape against the crushing saturation of the air.

I live on the periphery of the giants. Specifically, I dwell in the "wake" of the Lithochorytes.

When a mountain-sized stone-drifter shifts its massive bulk a few feet up the cliff-face, it creates a vacuum in its wake, a sudden, turbulent displacement of air and mist. This is my domain. I anchor myself to the rock with microscopic, hair-like cilia that vibrate with the resonance of the stone. I wait, dormant and flattened against the obsidian, indistinguishable from a patch of wet moss.

Then, the Lithochoryte moves.

The displacement is not violent to a creature like me, but it is a wave of opportunity. As the giant heaves its limb, the air rushes in to fill the void it leaves behind. In that rush, I expand. My internal canals flood, my skin billowing out like a sail catching a gale. I ride the turbulence, not flying, but *blossoming*. I spread my membranous form across the empty space, drinking the mist that is stirred up by the giant’s passing.

I am a cleaner of the slow-motion collision. Where the Lithochoryte’s heavy, moss-laden hide scrapes against the sharp granite, it leaves microscopic deposits of shed skin, crushed orchids, and the dust of eroding stone. As the air currents settle, I drift back down, wrapping my gelatinous body around these warm, nutrient-rich patches. I absorb them through osmosis. I am the recycler of the giants' indifference, consuming the debris of their glacial existence to sustain my own.

But my true purpose, my singular joy, is the water.

I possess a single, long, prehensile proboscis that usually coils tight against my underside, looking like a dormant vine. When the atmospheric tide shifts and the suspended globules of water—the great, floating spheres that drift upward like lost balloons—come near my crevice, I uncoil this tendril.

I do not drink the water. I do not need it for sustenance; my skin absorbs enough moisture from the air to keep me swollen and alive. Instead, I *harvest* the tension of the water.

I reach out with my proboscis and pierce the surface of a floating globule. The water here is strange, heavy with surface tension and magnetic mystery. When I puncture it, the sphere doesn't burst; it rings. It hums. The vibration travels down my tendril and into my hydraulic canals, resonating through my entire body. I feed on the *song* of the water.

This vibration is the only thing that moves me quickly. When the hum hits me, my internal fluids sync with the frequency, and I pulse with a rapid, rhythmic shimmering. I flash with bioluminescence—bright, stroboscopic bursts of azure and violet—in time with the droplet's frequency. It is a display of pure, useless beauty. No one sees it. The Lithochoryte is too blind to notice; the Nereids are too busy swimming in the horizontal rivers. It is a light show performed for the rock and the mist alone.

I am a Haptos. I am the vibration in the pause between the giant's breath and its exhale. I am the flash of light in the bruised twilight, anchored to the stone, drinking the song of the floating rain.
