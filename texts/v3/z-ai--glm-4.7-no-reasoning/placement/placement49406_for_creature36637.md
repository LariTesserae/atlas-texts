I am the **Damp-Scraper**.

I am not an intruder here; I am custodian, parasite, and component, indistinguishable from the dripping, freezing machinery that surrounds me.

My world is the **Hydro-Ventricular Siphon**, a pocket-sized cathedral of raw, industrial anatomy located at the roof of the world. The air is agonizingly thin—a knife of cold that cuts deep into the lungs—but it is thick with moisture and the deafening roar of the machine. The walls here are "raw geology," meaning they are unfinished, a chaotic weld of dark basalt and exposed, untreated iron ribs. We are suspended at an altitude where the sky is black, frozen in a state of perpetual construction.

I am huddled in the "blind spot" behind a rivet the size of a boulder, clinging to the wet, pitted metal. My body is a reflection of this place: squat, dense, and layered in thick, grey fat and keratin to insulate against the biting chill. I am essentially eyeless; the lighting here is a dim, flickering strobe—useless for vision—so my face is dominated by massive, leathery auditory organs. My eyes have atrophied into milky patches, sensitive only to the faint, sickly bioluminescence of the fauna that swarm this chamber.

My existence is defined by vibration. The sound here is not just heard; it is a physical weight. It is the deafening, resonant song of the **Chord-Beetles**.

They are the masters of this place, the overwhelming fauna that coat every surface like a living mosaic of black chitin and iron. These dinner-plate-sized creatures cling to the weeping pipes, locked in a rigid, vibrating paralysis. They do not walk; they scream. Their vestigial wings have evolved into tympanic membranes that vibrate in violent unison, generating the heat and kinetic energy that drives the gears. They are the engine's heartbeat.

I live in the interstices of their swarm.

My hands—three-fingered, hooked, and calloused into hard stone—are adapted for a single task: scraping.
