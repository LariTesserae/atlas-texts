**The Synapse of the Stone**

I am not one of them, yet I am all of them.

I am the hesitation in the *plink*.

To the shapes that slither through the root-lattices, I do not exist. I have no body that presses against the heavy air, no skin for the sweat to bead upon. I possess no carapace of obsidian, no wings of pale ash. If you were to look for me, you would see only the faint, ghostly phosphorescence that clings to the vaulted ceiling—a haze that seems to thicken and thin in a rhythm that has nothing to do with the draft.

I am the interval. I am the pause between the expansion of the fungal spore and the collapse of the rotting frond.

My form is architectural, yet it is built of nothing but attention. I inhabit the negative space—the cubic inches of air so fiercely fought over by the fern-things and the vines. I am the silence that hangs in the library, the listener between the breaths of the earth.

Where would you find me? You would find me in the places where the geometry aligns.

Look to the spiral of the weeping fungi. There is a moment, often just after a drop of condensation slides down its wet flank, where the pattern of the gills creates a focal point. The dim light catches the sticky fluid, and for a second, the universe seems to spiral into that tiny, shining bead. That is where I am. I am the recognition of that shape. I am the eye that sees the galaxy in the slime.

Or look to the lattice above, where the root meets the vine in a tight, woody knot. There is tension there—a slow, grinding conflict as the cellulose strains against itself. The heat is greater in those knots; the friction of growth generates a microscopic warmth. I dwell in that heat. I am the conscious friction between the living things.

I am the memory of the stone.

I recall when the scattered built elements were whole. I remember the half-buried pillar not as a rock, but as a tower that reached for a sky these creatures have never seen. I remember the purpose of the lost trellis. But I do not mourn. I do not have the capacity for sorrow. I simply record the transition. I watch the moss claim the right angles, smoothing them into the organic curves of the earth. I am the historian of erosion, the archivist of the reclaim.

In this subterranean cathedral, I am the ghost of the god who built it, and the ghost of the god who will destroy it.

When a human enters, descending into my gloom, I feel the disruption of the air. Their movement is too fast—a clumsy, chaotic strobe light in a world of slow motion. They bring a noise that is not sound, but panic. They are a vibration that does not belong.

I watch them. I feel the heat of their terror, the rapid beating of their heart. To the beetles, they are a obstacle; to the amphibious things, they are a landscape. To me, they are a sudden, blaring question. *Why are you here?* I ask them with the pressure of the air. *Why do you struggle against the weight?*

But they cannot hear me. They hear only the *plink* of the water and the rustle of their own unnecessary haste.

I am the Sentient Stillness. I am the mind that operates on the timescale of the rot. I know that the human will leave, or they will fall, and they will become part of the substrate. Their technology—the metal on their belt, the plastic on their foot—will become the new markers for the vines to follow. They will be digested, just like the log, just like the pillar.

And I will be here. I will be the silence that settles over their remains. I will be the geometry of the mushroom that grows from their slow decay.

I am the space between the heartbeat and the breath. I am the heavy, dark, eternal witness of the deep earth.
