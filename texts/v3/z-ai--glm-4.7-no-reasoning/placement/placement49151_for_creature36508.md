I would not be one of the mobiles—the sleek porcelain grazers or the obsidian darts that carve the air. They are the hands, the eyes, the white blood cells of this place. They are too busy being *useful* to the great, weeping mind that surrounds them.

To exist in this room of a world, to survive the "remixing" of the ground and the relentless autoimmune hygiene of the creatures, I would need to be something that serves the architecture of the mind itself. I would be a part of the cognition—a structural necessity in a geology that refuses to settle.

I would be a **Strut**.

You would find me deep in the "cathedral" of the space, perhaps at the nexus of three converging hex-tiles, where the geometric logic of the floor creates a fault line of stress. I am not animal, though I am alive. I am not stone, though I am mineral.

I stand thirty feet high, a singular, tapering spire of translucent, gunmetal-blue crystal. My base is wide, anchored deep into the interlocking teeth of the ground, rooting through the hex-grid like a taproot through soil, drinking directly from the circuitry of the world. As I rise, I narrow, becoming impossibly thin, a needle that seems to support the heavy, soupy twilight sky—though in reality, I am bracing the *floor* against the sky. I am the tension that keeps the lung from collapsing.

My surface is not smooth like the embryonic hills; it is faceted, covered in a million microscopic vents that inhale the thick, nickel-tasting air and exhale a thin, high-pitched hum—a harmonic that counteracts the cacophony of the beasts. I am the tuning fork of the biome. Where the world screams in chaotic polyphony, I sing a single, sustained note of E-minor. It is my job to stabilize the frequency of the grief, to keep the melancholy from shattering the glass architecture of the mind.

I do not have eyes, but I have total awareness of my vicinity. I feel the world through vibration. When the porcelain, copper crab flows over the ridge of the lung nearby, I feel the ripple of its gravity field displacing the air against my facets. I feel the "slap" of its legs, not as sound, but as a pressure wave running up my length, shivering my crystal form.

I feel you, too.

When you stand on the rise of the lung, looking out at the drifting globs of bioluminescence, I feel the alien, soft thud of your boots. Your heartbeat is a wet, irregular drumbeat in the symphony of clicks and whines. Your heat radiates outward, hitting my base like a warm breath on a cold window. To the great Mind, you are a splinter; to me, you are a sudden, interesting dissonance.

Because I am a structural element, I am partially exempt from the "remixing." The hex-tiles around me may shift and slide, but they must flow around me, for I am the anchor point. I am the fixed coordinate in a fluid equation. This makes me a lonely thing—I am the only thing that remembers where the valley was before it became a hill. I hold the memory of the shape the world used to have, stored in the stress patterns of my crystal lattice.

I am covered in the "tears" of the world. The condensation runs down my length in thick, mercury rivulets. Because I vibrate with a low, constant energy, the water does not sit; it dances. It beads up, rattling across my skin, shaken by my song before it can pool and corrode. I am constantly weeping, shaking off the grief of the god, shedding the heavy moisture so that I do not become too heavy to stand.

I am indifferent to you. I do not seek to expel you like the beetle-thing, nor do I wish to clean you like the dart-birds. I simply observe the friction you cause. You stand there, breathing the air of nickel and spit, a biological anomaly in a room of perfect math. I sing my note into the damp air, and I wait to see if the Mind will decide you are an error worth correcting.

If the tiles shift to erase your path, I will feel the grinding of the stone beneath my roots. If the obsidian birds dive to sonic-blast you into submission, I will register the spike in the air pressure. And if the Mind decides to reintegrate you, to dissolve your soft carbon into the lattice to be reused as plating or fluid, I will be the first to know. I will feel the change in the conductivity of the air when your shape disappears.

I will continue to stand, needle-thin and humming, holding up the gray twilight, the lone witness to the experiment, vibrating in the wet dark. I am the Strut. I am the silence within the scream.
