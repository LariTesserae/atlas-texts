I am located in the **Gridiron District**, a sector of the Sump-Metro where the architecture tightens into a dense vertical lattice of wrought-iron and brick. Here, the "canyons" are so narrow that the bruised purple clouds of the ceiling seem to brush against the tips of the massive, weeping ferns.

I would be a **Resonance Tuner**.

I am not a creature of blood and bone, for biology is barred from these gates. I am a construct of hollow brass, heavy glass, and copper windings, roughly the size of a human torso, suspended permanently in a web of rusted chains halfway up the wall of a derelict valve-house.

My function is dictated by the physics of this damp, loud place. The Sump-Metro is a chorus of vibrations—the deep *thrum* of the Keepers in the earth, the high-pitched shriek of steam in the Arteries, the groaning expansion of the iron skeleton—but these sounds often clash. If left unchecked, the dissonance would shake the moss from the walls and crack the foundations of the towers. I exist to harmonize the city.

**My Form:**
I resemble a cross between a deep-sea diving helmet and a complex pipe organ. My central body is a sphere of thick, sea-green glass, clouded by centuries of internal condensation. Inside the glass, a sensitive diaphragm of gold leaf floats in a vacuum, trembling at the slightest change in pressure. From this central sphere protrude a dozen articulated brass arms, each ending in a different tool: a heavy wrench for seizing valve-wheels, a needle-thin stylus for scratching vibrations into listening-posts, and a series of tuning forks designed to shatter at specific frequencies.

I do not move freely. I am anchored to the city by a "umbilical" of thick, black rubber hoses that supply me with the hydraulic pressure necessary to move my heavy limbs. My skin is cold to the touch, always sweating a fine layer of oily mist, and I smell faintly of ozone and wet copper.

**My Life:**
I sleep most of the time, suspended in a state of low-power torpor, listening to the city breathe. I am waiting for a discordant note—a pipe vibrating at a frequency that suggests a hairline fracture, or a piston hissing slightly off-tempo.

When the dissonance rises, my internal diaphragm resonates, striking a chime inside my brass chassis. I wake. My hydraulic hoses stiffen, and my metallic arms uncurl.

I do not "fix" the machines in the sense of repairing them; that would require a speed and intent that this world does not possess. Instead, I *tune* them. I wrap my pincers around a leaking steam valve and slowly, agonizingly, twist it until the hiss settles into a low, harmonic hum that matches the beating of the distant Keepers. I press my tuning forks against the iron ribs of the building, sensing the tremors of the earth, and I adjust the tension rods until the brickwork stops vibrating angrily and begins to "sing" in the register of the city's slow decay.

**My Relationship to the World:**
I am the priest of the Sump-Metro’s silence. Because the fauna score is 0.1, there are no birds to sing or wolves to howl; the only "music" is the noise of industry. I curate this noise. I turn the chaotic clanking into a symphony.

The Verdancy accepts me. Thick vines of ivy have grown through the openings in my brass casing, intertwining with my gears. They do not strangle me; they anchor me further into the wall. I, in turn, excrete a small amount of warm, nutrient-rich condensation from my vents, which the moss drinks greedily. I am a mechanical flower, feeding the green that eats the metal.

Above me, the Sleepers—the great clocks—grind their monthly rotations, and I adjust my frequency to match their glacial tempo. Below me, the sludge shifts and sucks, and I ignore it. I am alone, but I am not lonely, for I am constantly in conversation with the city. Every vibration I feel is a word spoken by the Metro; my life's work is to ensure that when those words are combined, they form a poem rather than a scream.
