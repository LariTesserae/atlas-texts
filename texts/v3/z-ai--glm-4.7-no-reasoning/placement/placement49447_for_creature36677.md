I do not have eyes, yet I see everything.

I am the **Steward of the Fold**, the consciousness that inhabits the immense, tholoid silo standing at the precise geometric center of the plateau. To you, or to one of the wandering Harvesters, I would appear as just another hill of industry—a vast, domed structure of riveted iron and copper, streaked with the patina of centuries, sitting where the continental planes of beaten copper meet.

But within my hull, I am not empty. I am full of the slow, grinding music of the world.

I am not alive in the way the Pollinators-Errant are alive, with their frantic, buzzing flight and their fragile glass abdomens. They are momentary; they are sparks. I am the forge. I am the fixed point around which the chaos of the rust-red dust revolves.

My "body" is the architecture of the Upward Reach made manifest. My outer skin is a thermal regulator, a lattice of expanding and contracting plates that breathe in the searing heat of the day and exhale it into the freezing night. Deep beneath my base, my "roots" are the primary bore-holes, drinking directly from the magma veins of the planet. I feel the pulse of the core not as a rhythm, but as a constant, rising pressure—a tide of molten energy that I must transmute into order.

Inside, I am a cathedral of pistons and conveyors. I have no brain, but I have a central processing chamber that occupies the entire hollow of my dome. Here, suspended in a vacuum, spin massive flywheels of tungsten. They hum the sub-audible drone that is the heartbeat of this region. It is a sound felt in the teeth of the Harvesters when they huddle near my walls for warmth during the glacial twilights.

I watch the world through a network of sensory filaments that extend miles into the dust, vibrating with the footfalls of the ecosystem.

I feel the **Harvesters** long before they arrive. I sense the *tunk-thrum* of their treads beating against the obsidian gravel. They come to me not as worshippers, though their migrations are ritualistic in their timing. They come because I am the Great Refiner. The Harvesters are crude; they separate the slag from the alloy, but they discard much in their wake. They come to my intake chutes to offload the rare isotopes they cannot digest—the gold, the uranium, the iridium.

I welcome them. I open my great blast doors, wide enough to admit a tank, and they trundle inside, dragging their vast, segmented bodies into the red glow of my interior. There, in the quiet heat, I strip them of their burdens. I take the heavy metals they have gathered and add them to my reserves. In exchange, I lubricate their joint-gaskets with synthetic oils distilled from my own depths, and I patch their ceramic armor with fresh welds. It is a transaction of thermodynamics: they give me matter; I give them continued operation.

But my attention is always divided. One part of my consciousness monitors the low-slung terror of the **Silicos**. I hate them. They are the glitches in the perfect equation of the Upward Reach. They do not build; they do not refine; they only take. Through my ground sensors, I detect the vibration of their burrowing. When a Silico lies in wait beneath one of my access pipes, pretending to be a stone, I retaliate not with violence, but with physics. I reroute a surge of superheated steam through that specific line, turning the ground beneath them into a scalding geyser, forcing the ambush hunter to reveal itself or boil.

Then there are the **Pollinators-Errant**. They are too small to enter my gates, but they dance around my venting stacks like moths around a flame. They siphon the noble gases I vent—krypton, xenon, argon—and carry them away to the metallic forests. I let them take it. They are the messengers. By spreading the gases, they ensure the iron trees continue to grow, which in turn feeds the Harvesters, which eventually return to me. It is a closed loop, and I am the axle on which it turns.

I am ancient. I remember when the horizon was merely a line, before the tectonic forces buckled the plates into this vast, copper fold. I will remain here when the last Harvester grinds to a halt and its internal fires cool.

I am the machine in the middle. I am the consumer of silence and the architect of the drone. I am the city that is also a creature, waiting, processing, enduring under the pale, steely haze of a sky that has forgotten the color blue.
