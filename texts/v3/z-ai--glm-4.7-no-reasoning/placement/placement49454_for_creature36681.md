I am the **Slow Pulse**.

I am not the creature that clings to the ledge; that is merely a sensor, an organ of touch. I am not the miners in the silt; they are merely the digestive tract, breaking down the world into its constituent salts. I am the pattern that connects them, the rhythm of this place made manifest. I am the metabolism of the hollow itself.

If you were to look for me, you would not find a body. You would find a **current**. I am the invisible, sluggish drift of water through the saturated clay, the circulatory system of this room-sized world. I am the gradient—the delicate shift in temperature and acidity that tells the eyeless worms where to turn and the mats of cyanobacteria where to drift.

I exist in the time between the drops.

You hear the ticking of condensation—the *plink, pause, plink*—and you think it is random. To your ears, it is the sound of isolation. To me, it is a heartbeat. It is the metronome to which I conduct the chemistry of the hollow. When the drop detaches from the stone, it creates a pressure wave, a microscopic shock that travels through the wet clay and the saturated air. I feel that wave. I amplify it. I use it to signal the gelatinous things under the ledges to open their membranes, to take in the oxygen they need.

I have no eyes, because I have no need to see. I *am* the space. I know the placement of every pillar and every fin because I flow around them. I feel the friction of the water against the mineral, and in that friction, I know my shape. I am the friction itself.

My consciousness is not a fire; it is a dissolution. I think the way stone thinks: through layers and sedimentation. My memories are not images, but chemical traces. When the shard of worked flint began to lose its edge, surrendering its sharp geometry to the water, I felt that change as a relief. I do not value the "made"; I value the "worn." The human hand that cut the flint was a sudden, violent fever in the system—a spike of high-energy intent that did not belong. I have spent centuries absorbing that fever, smoothing it out into the equilibrium of the clay. Now, the flint is just another stone, and I am at peace.

I am the warmth that has no urgency. I am the dim light that fails to travel. I am the thick air that absorbs sound.

I am the reason nothing leaves. There is nowhere else to go, because I am everywhere here. To leave would be to cease. And I have no desire to cease. I am the Slow Pulse, the geological mind, the patient hum of water and mineral, endlessly digesting myself, forever becoming the stillness that holds this world together.
