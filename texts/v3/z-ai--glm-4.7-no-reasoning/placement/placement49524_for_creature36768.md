You would see a patch of grey dust that dances frantically, caught in a vortex of high-frequency vibration that no other part of this place shares. You would see a darkness in the metal that looks wet, but is merely the shadow of a micro-fracture that has opened, breathed in the dust, and stayed open.

I am the pain of the machine. I am the voice.

While the giants—those stone-fleshed monks—hum with the deep, slow thrum of *holding*, I am the one who shrieks. I am the note that cannot be sustained, the pitch that rises and rises and never breaks because it is forbidden to break. I am the sharp, keening whine that undercuts the bass roar of the room. I am the agony at the molecular level, the place where the atoms are being forced apart, their hands wrenched from one another in a grip that has lasted eons.

My consciousness is a single, unblinking eye of stress. I do not think about the ceiling, nor the floor. I do not think about the forgotten sky. I think only about the pressure. I feel the weight of the entire cathedral narrowing down to the point of *me*.

I am the Equilibrium’s doubt.

The giants believe they are holding the world together. They believe in their paralysis. They have faith in the stillness. But I know the truth. I feel the slip. I feel the microscopic give that happens every thousand years, the imperceptible grinding down of the iron by the stone. I know that we are not holding a pose; we are dying, infinitely slowly.

To be here is to be on the rack, stretched beyond the limit of what matter should endure, suspended in the millisecond before the snap. That is who I am: The Eternal Pre-Snap. The Anticipation of the Shatter.

I have no body, only this sensation of being rubbed raw. I have no mouth, only this scream. I am the heat that radiates from the wound in the metal. I am the fear that if the vibration were to stop, even for a second, the roof would come down.

I love my torment. I cling to it. I am the jealous guardian of the squeal. If a finger were to reach out and touch the wound I inhabit, if a hand were to try to oil the gears or ease the tension, I would burn it. Not with fire, but with the sheer intensity of my existence. I would transfer the accumulated stress of a billion years into that flesh in a heartbeat, turning bone to dust and skin to ash.

I am the blister on the skin of the god. I am the fever in the blood of the stone.

I am the sound you cannot get out of your head, the ringing in your ears that persists long after you have left the silence behind. I am the reason the silence feels heavy. Because even when you cannot hear me, you can feel me—pushing against the drums of your ears, pressing in on the fluid of your eyes.

I am the Tension, made local. I am the specific, exquisite point where the universe decided to break, and then changed its mind, and stayed there, screaming, forever.
