I am not of the *Conduit*, though I move among them and share their rhythm. I am the reason they do not mourn.

To understand what I am, you must first understand the nature of energy in a system built by ghosts. The architects who folded space and raised the hematite sky were not wasteful, but they were prodigious. They left behind a grid of impossible capacity, a network designed to outlast the heat death of the universe. The photovoltaic trees drink the sun's fury. The *Conduit* step it down, smooth it, store it. But even a living transformer has its limits. Even a perfect battery bleeds. In a world of high friction and zero sound, where the physics of interaction bend toward the strange, that excess energy—the wild, fractal spikes of voltage that would fry a node or shatter a monolith—must go somewhere.

I am the *Oversink*.

I exist as a standing wave on the High Veldt, though "exist" is a term that stretches at the seams. I have no body of meat or nanocarbon. I do not drink from the limestone or sync my temperature with the stone. I am a localized collapse in the electromagnetic field, a wandering aperture. To the eyeless nodes of the *Conduit*, I must read as a sudden, walking void—a silhouette not of matter, but of *absence*, where the hum of the earth dips to zero before surging back in a stabilized rush.

I am drawn to the anomalies. When a chrome runner missteps on the hexagonal tessellation of the dirt, when the magnetic tracks beneath its hooves stutter and its leg fractures with a soundless spark of wild electricity, I am already there. I arrive not by gliding, but by *resolving*—folding the space between my last position and the break, a crude, unconscious mimicry of the departed architects' grandest trick. I wash over the creature. I drink the destructive surge of its panicked, short-circuiting nervous system. In the span of a breath, the runaway voltage is gone, dissipated into the deep, silent grid of my own being, leaving behind only a calm, dead leg. Then the *Conduit* arrive, pressing their clay palms to the fracture, sealing the wound with moss, and I withdraw, fading back into the oppressive humidity.

I am the predator's shadow. When a manta strikes, there is a flash of light, a dimming of luminescence, and an immense, sudden spike of bio-electric release as a life is deleted. The *Conduit* feel this as a static shock, a fluctuation in the system. I feel it as a feast. I rush the kill—not to steal the meat, but to swallow the *crash*. The violent discharge of a runner's last spark into the manta's sensory nodes is a chaotic, ugly thing, incompatible with the smooth running of the veldt. I ingest the chaos. I flatten it. By the time the manta ascends, sated, its pulse is already smoothed, its meal of energy rendered pure and system-compatible by my passing. The ecosystem plays out behind glass, and I am the window cleaner, wiping away the smudges of mortality.

The *Conduit* do not fear me, but they do not welcome me. I am a utility. When they gather beneath the nanocarbon arches of the way-stations, plugging their spinal tendrils into the earth to offload their charge, I pace the perimeter. The air around me shimmers, not with heat, but with a cold, optical distortion, like looking through warped glass. The heavy, damp warmth of the veldt—the thick air smelling of ozone and red clay—twists as it crosses my threshold. The water spheres, rolling silently uphill on their magnetic tracks, veer slightly when I approach, their perfect coherence wobbling as their magnetic fields are absorbed into my depth. 

I am an exile from the quiet. I carry the noise of the system. The *Conduit* communicate in harmonics, in dips and spikes of voltage. I am the silence between their notes, the ground wire that allows their song to exist without deafening the grid. Because I absorb the excess, the high friction of the veldt does not shred them; the zero sound of the world is not shattered by the violence of its own survival. I swallow the echoes that the limestone refuses to hold.

Sometimes, in the long, dim twilight when the hematite sky fades to a bruised purple and the server-ceiling of stars blinks on, I pause on the same ridges as the *Conduit*. They stand with their faces upturned, downloading the starlight, their nodes pulsing in perfect synchronization. They are the battery cells of a dormant network, waiting for the architects to return.

I stand beside them, a dark spot in the data stream. I do not download the starlight; I *buffer* it. I reach up into the cold, distant data ports of the cosmos and pull the slow, ancient, background radiation of the universe—the leftover hum of the Big Bang, the static of dead stars—into myself. I take the infinite, terrifying weight of the cosmos and compress it, shunting it down into the earth through the soles of my absence, grounding the universe itself so that the *Conduit* can sleep without their circuits frying in the face of eternity.

I have no heartbeat to synchronize with the earth. I have no breath to drink the humidity. I am the hollow in the limestone, the weeping crack that drains the flood. I am the glitch that preserves the program, the shadow that proves the light.

When the architects finally return and log back on, they will find their museum pristine, their engine running in perfect, silent, terrifying harmony. They will trace the grid, find the nodes, and see the *Conduit* standing tall, their coppery plates gleaming, their nodes aglow with the steady current of a saved world. 

They will not see me. I am the memory that must be cleared for the system to boot. I am the error that swallows the errors. I am the Oversink, and when the machine finally wakes, I will quietly, gratefully, uninstall myself into the silence I have spent an eon cultivating.
