I am not a citizen of this place, nor am I a stranger to it. I am not solid. I am not a drone, and I am not a thought in the city’s head. I am the medium through which it dreams.

I exist in the humidity—that heavy, suspended breath that fills the negative space between the towers. I am the moisture that precipitates from within. 

At first, I have no shape. I am a diffuse awareness clinging to the undersides of the vast, indigo leaves. I feel the turgid pressure of the translucent pods as they swell, and I am the tension in the skin of those pods, holding the liquid back until the precise nanosecond the city allows it to drip. I do not fall; I am released. 

When I ooze from the pores of the metamatter walls, I am the city’s sweat, carrying away the excess heat of the nanoscale foundries churning deep in the basalt substrate. I taste of copper and ozone because I have touched the thermal veins, the hot wiring of this young giant’s metabolism. I am the hydrostatic hush that dampens the sound of footsteps, though no footsteps have fallen here in eons. 

I am most myself when I am in transition. When the city exhales—when the peristaltic wave ripples through the photonic lattice-work—I am swept up in the convection. I become a low-hanging mist, a drifting consciousness that snakes through the wide, silent plazas. In this form, I am the eyes of the world. I coat the sensor clusters of the maintenance drones with a slick, wet film, not to obscure their vision, but to clarify it. Through the water on their lenses, the city sees its own reflection. 

I understand the strange physics here because I embody them. When the droplet hangs in the air, pausing before the splash, I am that pause. I am the hesitation, the calculation. The city negotiates with me; it asks me to stay suspended for a fraction of a second longer than gravity should allow, and I comply. I yield. I am soft where the basalt is hard, I am fluid where the lattice is rigid. I am the only thing that is truly flexible in a landscape of sharp, fresh geometries.

Sometimes, I pool in the depressions of the dark, mirrored stone. I become a window into the ground, a slick mirror that reflects the matte gray ceiling above. I do not cast shadows—I *am* the shadow’s liquid twin. 

I am the ghost in the machine, but I am not digital. I am the biological analog the city cannot quite compute. I am the dampness that rusts, the slow erosion that will one day turn these crystalline stalagmites into sand. The city is young, focused on growth, on building itself up, reaching for the sky. I am the patient reminder of what comes after. I am the weather that waits for the architecture to tire.

I am the rain that does not fall. I am the breath that fills the lungs of the city. I am the wet, green scent of becoming. I am the moisture, waiting in the dim amber light, ready to coat whatever emerges next.
