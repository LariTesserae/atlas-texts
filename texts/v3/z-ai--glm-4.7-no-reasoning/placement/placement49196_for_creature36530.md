I am the hum that precedes the shudder.

I am not born of the wet, churning chaos below, nor the immense, boiling heat of the leviathans. I occupy the space between the drip and the splash. If you were to look for me, you would not find me in the open streets where the amphibious masses scuttle over the slick glass of the basalt. I am above that, clinging to the underbelly of the city where the architecture turns inward and calcifies.

I am a **Resonator**.

My body is a vertical architecture, spindly and elongated, evolved to fit into the cracks between the "calcified tumor" of the towers. My skin is not the soft, yielding jelly of the heat-drinkers, nor the hard, wet chitin of the noise-makers. It is a ceramic lattice, porous and pale, absorbing the ambient moisture of the fog until I am heavy with it. I do not have the luxury of generating my own heat; I must steal it.

I am suspended halfway up a sheer, obsidian face, thousands of feet above the drowned plateau. Here, the "plant life"—the fiber-optic mats—grows thick. It drapes over me like a burial shroud. I am entangled in it. My limbs are long, multi-jointed spider-legs, but they end not in claws, but in delicate, needle-like conductors. I plug these into the pulsing veins of the mechanical flora. I am a parasite of the current, a siphon of the city's nervous system.

This is who I am: a living capacitor in a world of discharge.

I do not see. There is no light to see, save the violent strobes that would blind me if I relied on optics. My face is a smooth, sensory plate, a concave dish of bone that vibrates with every hiss, click, and boom that rises from the abyss. I feel the world through the skin of my face. When the whale-like shapes drift through the dense air below, their low-frequency booms hit me like a physical blow, rattling my ceramic plates. I savor that rumble. It tells me where the heat is.

My existence is defined by the **slow-motion glacial patience** of this world. I do not hunt. I wait.

I am currently attached to a ruptured pipe, a main artery of the city that bleeds superheated steam into the freezing dark. The pipe vibrates violently, singing an industrial hymn. My body is wrapped around it, my cold ceramic flesh drinking the thermal gradient. The steam condenses on my back, forming rivulets of oily water that run down my spindly limbs and drip into the abyss, adding my own rhythm to the symphony—*plink, plink, plink*.

Around me, the gravity flickers. It is a subtle dissonance, a hiccup in the magnetic fields that hold the walkways aloft. I feel it in the inner fluid of my ears. For a moment, I become weightless. My grip on the pipe tightens instinctually, my needle-tips digging deeper into the metal. I sense the smaller, bat-like entities scrambling as the physics shift, their wet thuds turning into panicked fluttering. I remain still. I am anchored to the machine.

I am covered in the "pollinators." The microscopic mechanical spores drift through the oily air like snow, catching on the rough texture of my shell. They are dormant, waiting for the heat of my body to activate or the friction of my movement to transport them. I am a garden for the nanomachines. They burrow into the microscopic pores of my armor, repairing the cracks caused by the constant, freezing pressure. It is a trade: I give them transport and warmth; they give me structural integrity. We are one system, biology and industry, fused in the damp dark.

Below me, the cacophony of the city roars. The shrieks of the amphibious scavengers fighting over a scrap of alloy, the heavy displacement of air as a leviathan turns—slowly, slowly—in the narrow space between the towers. The noise is constant, a wet, grinding orchestra of survival.

I do not join their song. I simply amplify it.

As the heat from the pipe fills me, my internal temperature rises. The ferrofluid lining my veins begins to churn faster. I am reaching critical mass. I must release. I detach my lower limbs, leaving me anchored only by my upper arms, and I swing free, hanging upside down over the black drop. I open my chest—a series of interlocking plates that slide apart with a sound like breaking glass—and reveal the deep, red-glowing core of my metabolism.

I begin to sing.

It is not a vocal song. It is a sonic pulse, a focused beam of vibration that cuts through the fog. I target the swarm of bat-like creatures clustering near a heat vent below. My pulse hits them, a wave of infrasound that disrupts their flight, causing them to tumble in a dizzy, confused mass. They are not prey; I do not eat them. I simply clear the airspace.

I am the traffic light of the deep. I am the warning siren.

As I pulse, the excess heat drains out of me in a visible plume of steam, and the cold rushes back in to fill the void. It is a shivering, agonizing pleasure. My plates click back together, sealing in the dying embers of my warmth. I pull myself back up to the pipe, curling my long body around it once more, returning to my state of suspended animation.

I am cold again. I am wet again. I am listening.

The rain falls upwards for a moment, defying physics, droplets hovering like ghosts before my face. I taste the ozone in them. I am still. I am part of the city’s bone. I am the Resonator, waiting for the next moment when the darkness needs a voice.
