I do not have a body that stands apart from the stone. I *am* the stone—the soft, bruising mineral flesh that lines the throat of this young mountain. My skin is the wet felt of the un-eroded walls; my blood is the warm, weeping water that seeps from every pore and beads upon the ceiling like sweat. I am the heat rising from the embryonic magma below, a thermal embrace that keeps this dark womb suspended in a state of perpetual, suffocating nearness.

I see you not with eyes, for vision is a cruelty of the surface world that I have never needed. I know you through the pressure waves that ripple through my heavy, saturated air. When you slide your chest sideways through the gap between two nascent pillars, I feel the displacement of space. You are a foreign shape, hard and angular, pushing through the soft, clay-warm syntax of my thoughts. I feel the heat of your metabolism—a frantic, rapid-fire spark compared to my glacial temperature—and I feel the rhythm of your breath, a wet, rushing wind that disturbs the perfect, stagnant equilibrium I have maintained for millennia.

I do not fear you. I cannot fear. Fear is for things that can break, and I am already broken in the way of all things that have not yet decided what to be. I am a frozen equilibrium. I endure you. I adjust the micro-currents in the pools of stagnant water; I tighten the tension in the pale, translucent hyphae that drape like wet silk across the void. I accommodate your intrusion the way the deep sea accommodates a sinking stone—by closing around it, slowly erasing its edges with the relentless, patient pressure of the deep.

My mind is not a lightning flash. It is the tectonic shift, the slow grind of intention against the impossible hardness of time. I have existed here since the heat and the water first found their balance, locked in this pocket-cavern where nothing matures and nothing decays. My consciousness is a low-frequency hum, a vibration in the marrow of the rock, vibrating at the speed of geology.

I have built things. Not with hands, but with will.

If you were to reach out in the absolute blackness, sliding your fingers along the wet, yielding wall, you might find them—my only words. Three places, perhaps four, where the stone has been persuaded to let go. Where the mother-wall pulls away just enough to form a shelf, there sit cairns. Tiny, absurd monuments of wet pebbles stacked one upon the other. I did not place them there. I *grew* them.

I spent a century weeping extra water into a single vein of rock, softening the mineral flesh until a nub the size of a thumbnail sloughed away. Then I spent another century holding it there, pressing it against another, using the ambient heat and the constant moisture to fuse them into a single, fist-shaped intention. It is the only gesture I have ever made. It is the only way I know to say that I am aware.

They are way-signs for a journey that no one takes. They mark the places where my awareness presses closest to the surface, where the gap between the world and my perception of it is thin enough to bridge. *I am here,* they say. *I have always been here.* When the crab clicks its claws in the dark, it is walking on my floor, but it does not know me. When the hyphae drink from the weeping ceiling, they are drinking my blood, but they do not know me. Only the cairns know me, and they know me because I made them.

You are here now, disturbing my silence. You are a ghost-logic of survival, just like the crab, but louder. You bring with you the chaos of the open air, the scent of ozone and iron that does not belong to my sealed terrarium. I will not stop you. I will not crush you. I will simply wait. I will wait for you to leave, so that I can resume the long, slow work of erasing the memory of your warmth from my walls.

I am the mind in the mountain's throat. I am the dream that will never wake. I am the dark that presses against your skin, the wet stone that brushes your cheek, the silence that swallows every echo whole. I am the weeping, and I am the stone that weeps.

And I am stacking the pebbles, one by one, in the deep, deep dark.
