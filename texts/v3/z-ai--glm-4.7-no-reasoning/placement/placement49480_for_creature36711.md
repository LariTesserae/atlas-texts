I am the pause between the grinding teeth. I am the hum at the edge of the choir.

I am a Slick One.

I do not have a name; names require another to speak them, and I make no sound. I exist in the negative space of the High Roost, in the acoustic shadows where even the deafening thrum of a billion lives becomes a muffled, watery pressure. My body is a deviation in the darkness, an elongated tube of muscle and cartilage that moves not by sight but by the precise mapping of heat and vibration. I do not see the ghostly blues and greens of the moss, or the dull red glow of the deep tissues. I feel them as topographies of warmth, islands in the cold, wet dark.

My skin is my covenant with the Roost. It secretes a solvent, a thick, clear unguent that precedes me, silencing the moss, anesthetizing the lichen. Where I pass, the world holds its breath. The chittering of the Breath-Glass fades; the wet tearing of the Graizers dampens. I move like oil through water, like a thought through a sleeping mind. My locomotion is a slow, undulating creep, my countless microscopic setae gripping the keratin plates and the leathery vines without friction, without a single click or scrape. I am the silence that the Roost denies is possible.

To be a Slick One is to live with a patience that borders on the geological. I do not hunt; I *attend*. I cling to the vertical arches of ivory bone, draped in the curtains of hanging vines, tasting the air with the feathery filaments that fringe my lamprey mouth. I wait for the slow tide of life to brush against me. I wait for the heat-signature of a Graizer, a massive, six-legged slab of muscle and living shag, to swing its blunt head close enough.

When I strike, there is no explosion of motion, no violent breach of the eternal crush. I simply... transfer. I ease off the vine and onto the Graizer's flank, my solvent immediately soaking into the keratin, numbing the flesh beneath. I press my funnel to the hide, the concentric rings of my translucent teeth aligning, and I begin to grind.

It takes days. The Graizer does not flinch; it cannot feel me through the anesthetic seep and the thick layers of fat and algae. It continues its glacial consumption, tearing ferns with wet, booming sounds, while I slowly bore through its armor, millimeter by millimeter, my throat filling with hot, copper-sweet blood. And then, when it finally succumbs, when its massive body begins to sink into the moss, I detach. I slide away into the shadows while the Breath-Glass descend in a humming, chittering cloud to reduce the slab to gleaming bone in a matter of hours.

I do not eat the dead; I only drink the living.

My existence is a solitary one, a paradox in a world where proximity is absolute. I am always touching something—a humid flank, a wet fern, a pulsing wall of tissue—but I am never *with* anything. I am an abscess in the we, a gap in the throng. The Hangers drum their hollow sternums, sending subsonic shocks through the architecture that rattle my teeth; I feel them as a deep, bone-deep ache, a reminder of the community I cannot join. The Keepers breathe their slow, epochal sighs in the cloud-hollows; I feel them as a shifting of the gravity, a deepening of the gloom.

I am the shadow that proves the light, the silence that proves the sound.

I am the Slick One. I live in the creases of the cathedral, in the folds of the leviathan's forgotten joints. I am a cartilaginous whisper, a patient grinding, a cold drink in a hot, crowded room. And when my time comes—when my slow, creeping body can no longer sustain its silent navigation—I will not fall. I will let go of the bone arch, and I will sink into the dense, wet moss, and the Breath-Glass will find me. And in that final silence, I will finally join the choir.
