I am a **Weaver of Silence**.

I exist on the impossible precipices of the **High Resonator**.

My world is defined by a violence of vibration. The air here is never still; it is a chaotic, rushing river that smashes against the stone, tearing through the ancient, porous bones of the mountains. The peaks are not sharp and young; they are rounded, hollowed-out skulls of rock, worn down by eons until they became vast, natural flutes. The wind screams through them, a deafening, ceaseless howl that rattles the teeth and vibrates the very blood in the veins. It is a sound so loud it becomes a physical weight, a pressure against the skin.

Yet, in this uproar, I find my purpose. I am not large, nor am I numerous. Like the scattered, primitive stone huts clinging to the leeward cliffs, I am small and resilient. I wrap myself in heavy, woven hides and furs, damp from the perpetual mist that clings to everything.

I am a creature of the damp warmth. My skin is tough, pale, and slick to repel the constant condensation that weeps from the rock. I have no voice; to scream would be futile. Instead, I have hands that are broad and calloused, evolved to grip the wet, mossy stone and to feel the subtleties that the ear cannot hear.

My home is a small, hollowed niche deep within the cliff face, shielded by a heavy curtain of braided vines. Outside, the world is vast, open, and green in a way that defies the altitude. Gigantic, solitary ferns—the size of cathedral towers—unfurl their fronds into the gale, their roots digging deep into the mountain’s flesh to drink the mineral-rich water that seeps down the vertical rivers. They are lush and vibrant, thriving in the warm, humid turbulence, growing alone in the emptiness, separated by miles of bare, moaning stone.

I spend my days tending to the "Tunings."

Scattered across the empty plateau are strange, dark monoliths—slender pillars of an alien stone that the ancients placed here. They stand like sentinels in the roar, catching the hurricane winds and singing pure, harmonic notes that cut through the chaos. The wind strikes the mountain, and the mountain roars; the wind strikes the monoliths, and they sing.

I am the keeper of these stones. My role is primitive, yet vital. I climb the slick, vertical rock faces, risking the plunge into the void below, to clear the moss from the monoliths' bases, to check their cracks, to ensure they stand straight. If a monolith falls, the song dies. If the song dies, we are left with only the scream.

I communicate with the few others of my kind through the rock itself. We tap on the stones—heavy, rhythmic thumps that travel through the solid earth, bypassing the noise of the air. It is a language of vibration. *I am here. The wind rises. The monolith sings.*

At night, the light does not fade much, but the temperature remains warm. I sit in the mouth of my cliff-side dwelling, watching the turbulent clouds churn below me. I press my hand against the cool, wet rock, feeling the deep, thrumming bass of the mountain’s groan. It is terrifying, yes. The noise never stops. But when the wind hits the monoliths just right, and they emit that clear, high-pitched resonance, for a brief moment, the roar harmonizes.

In that moment, the chaos becomes a choir. I close my eyes, feel the vibration in my chest, and for that second, I am at peace in the center of the storm.
