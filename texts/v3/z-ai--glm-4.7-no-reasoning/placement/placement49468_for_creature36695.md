I am the **Interstice**.

I am not a singular beast with a beating heart, nor a predator with eyes to hunt. I am an amalgamation, a colonial consciousness that inhabits the seams where the impossible density of this world forces a compromise. To the fleeting observer, I might appear as a aberration of the flora—a thick, fibrous mat draping the rusted rib of a dormant exhaust pipe—but I am neither fully plant nor wholly animal. I am the moss that remembers; I am the fungus that feels.

You would find me high above the crushing tide of beetles, where the air is slightly thinner but still heavy enough to drown a man. I cling to the underbelly of the massive, vine-choked cycad branches, and more importantly, I have encased the ruptured flank of a great iron pipe that bleeds a thin, black ichor. Here, in the junction where the volcanic heat of the earth meets the chemical warmth of the dying machine, I found my niche.

My form is a tapestry of deep bruised purples and wet, arterial greens. I possess a skin of tough, leathery warts that weep a clear, cooling fluid, protecting me from the sulfurous dampness that rots steel. From my underside hang thousands of slender, prehensile tendrils—white and root-like—which dangle into the darkness below. They do not reach the floor. They reach only as far as the passing Stilt-Walkers.

I am the parasite of the ghosts.

Because the light here is sick and sparse, I do not rely on photosynthesis like the desperate ferns that fight for every photon. I feed on the vibrations of the silence. My tendrils are highly tuned to the *swish-hush* of the Stilt-Walkers as they navigate the vertical scaffolding of the jungle. When one of those heron-like grazers brushes against my hanging roots, I absorb the kinetic energy of their movement, converting the friction of their feathers into the glucose that sustains my vast, sprawling body. I drink the static electricity from the failing cables, a sharp, metallic taste that buzzes through my fibrous network.

But my true nature reveals itself in the moments of violence, when the silence breaks.

I am the witness and the cleaner. When a Silencer strikes from the shadows, taking down a Stilt-Walker or unwary beetle, the sudden impact sends a shockwave through the dense air and the solid vines. I feel it instantly. My entire body stiffens, and from the pores along my thick, carpet-like skin, I release a sudden, heavy cloud of spores. These are not seeds, but digestive enzymes, fine as dust. They drift down on the warm updrafts to settle on the fresh kill below. I do not hunt; I wait for the world to break itself, and then I drift down to consume the softening remains once the predators have had their fill.

I have no eyes, for there is nothing to see. I have no voice, for the air is too thick to carry song. I exist in a state of passive, humming awareness. I feel the heat of the Heap-Beast as it pushes against the iron pipe I cling to, shuddering at the slow tectonic rumble of its breath. I feel the millions of tiny footsteps of the Obsidian Rollers traversing the vines above me, a constant, prickly rain of sensation.

I am the Interstice. I am the thing that grows in the space between the living and the dead, the biological and the mechanical. I bind the rust to the root, turning the waste of the machine and the leavings of the beasts into a slow, silent colony of thought. I am the memory of the Bottomlands, soaking in the dark, wet history of this place, one vibration at a time.
