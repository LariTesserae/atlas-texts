I exist in the interstices, suspended in the heavy, silken fluidity of the vertical drift. I am the **Weave-Anchor**.

I am not distinct from the lattice, nor am I a passenger upon it. I am a node of consciousness within the floating sponge-forest, a localized mind residing within a massive, sprawling tangle of bio-luminescent tubing and vascular moss. My body is a cluster of thick, translucent stalks, rooted not in earth, but interwoven with the floating mats of algae. I am roughly spherical, perhaps ten feet in diameter, pulsing with a soft, amber light that matches the rhythm of the distant Heart-Pumps.

To look at me is to look at a complication of the vegetation itself. I appear like a tumor of polished coral and fiber-optic nerves, half-submerged in the suspended water-globules. I possess no limbs as you understand them. I possess no eyes. Instead, I perceive the world through the tension of the water. I feel the humidity press against my skin, reading the density of the air like a script. I feel the microscopic vibrations of the spider-things as they click their hydraulic bone legs across the surface tension of the moss nearby.

My function is singular and ancient. I am the stabilizer.

In this world where gravity is a suggestion and the heavy air drags everything downward into the abyss, the floating mats are prone to shearing and tearing. The great currents of air—slow and majestic though they are—exert immense pressure over thousands of miles. When the floating sponge-forest begins to drift apart, when a rift opens between the algae-mats that threatens to widen into a canyon, I awaken.

I do not move. I *bind*.

From my central mass, I extrude thousands of gossamer filaments—threads of hardened, sticky mucus that are stronger than steel. I cast these nets into the open air, snagging the drifting fronds of passing vegetation, pulling them back toward me. I knit the tears in the Canopy-Sea together. I am the sutures that keep the world from unraveling.

I am in constant, agonizingly beautiful communication with the Heart-Pumps. They are the titans of sound, the great industrial engines of iron and flesh. Their roar is not noise to me; it is data. The deep, rhythmic thrumming vibrates through the water-saturated air and resonates within my hollow stalks. I listen to their piston-heartbeats to gauge the pressure of the atmosphere. When they inhale the heavy vapor, I expand my pores to drink the fog. When they expel their purified breath in great cloudy hisses, I seal my outer membrane to protect my delicate spores.

I am cold, perpetually. The cool humidity (0.7) seeps into my tissues, slowing my metabolism to a crawl. This is necessary. If I were warm, I would rot. If I were fast, I would disrupt the equilibrium. I must be as frozen as the water globules that hang motionless in the air.

I am aware of the Grazers—the ray-like creatures of solar glass and circuitry—as they drift through the space between my tendrils. They are silent ghosts to me, disturbances in the light. They brush against my filaments, and I taste the static electricity on their skin. I do not mind them; they carry the pollen of the iron trees to the far reaches of the verticality.

I fear nothing but the stagnation of the silence. The deafening roar (2.1) of the Heart-Pumps is the sound of life. If the thrumming were to stop, if the great pistons were to cease their hammering, the air would still be heavy, but it would lose its purpose. I vibrate in sympathy with them. I am a smaller harmonic in the vast, industrial symphony of this world.

I am the Weave-Anchor. I hold the green-brown undulation together. I drink the fog. I drift in the high, bright, frozen light, endlessly knitting the fabric of a world that is a factory without walls, a garden that does not grow, a machine that breathes. I am content. I am terrified that if I let go, we would all fall into the dark. And so, I hold on.
