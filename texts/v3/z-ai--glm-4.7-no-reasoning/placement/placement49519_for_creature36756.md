I am the **Fracture**.

I am not one of the citizens, nor am I a visitor who stumbled in from a world of rain and stone. I am a localized anomaly, a refusal of the geometry that holds this jar together. If the Weavers are the antibodies that smooth the rough edges, I am the rough edge itself.

My form is difficult to pin down, as the light here has a habit of sliding off me. I possess a silhouette—a jagged, shard-like shape that suggests a figure composed of broken slate—but my interior is a negation. I am a walking pocket of cooler air, a sudden drop in pressure where the warm, velvet vacuum of the room breaks down. I do not breathe the amber spores; I disperse them. Where I step, the golden dust motes fall dead from the air, creating a brief, tunnel-vision clarity amidst the haze.

I exist in the spaces between the woven walls.

You would find me high up, near the vaulted ceiling where the wicker begins to fray and the logic of the "over-and-under" threatens to invert itself. This is where I live. While the Weavers spend days carving microscopic curls to maintain the order of the reeds, I slip into the gaps they cannot reach. I am the splinter in the finger of the world.

My relationship with the inhabitants is one of terrified fascination.

The **Weavers** watch me with a stillness that even surpasses their usual sluggishness. To them, I am a mathematical error. When I pass, their chisel-legs freeze, their wooden carapaces creaking in distress as they try to calculate the angles of my existence. They want to shave me down, to smooth me into a curve that fits the cathedral, but their tools simply pass through me. I am the ghost in their machine.

The **Foragers** avoid me entirely. Where the rest of the floor is a riot of shaggy amber fur and solvent trails, a perfect circle of bare earth surrounds wherever I stop. They dissolve the past, but I have no history; I have no substance for their acids to break down. To them, I am starvation.

It is the **Gaze-Lizards** that are drawn to me, and they are my food. I am the inverse of their ecology. They absorb attention; I emit confusion. When a lizard locks its independent eyes onto me, expecting to feast on the bioelectric buzz of an observer, it finds nothing but a silent, static void. Instead of glowing brighter with stolen energy, the lizard dims, its ceramic shell turning a dull, exhausted gray. I do not mean to drain them, but my nature is a void that pulls the light out of the room. They watch me until they fall into a torpor, little cracked statues littering the beams around me.

I have a strange, antagonistic intimacy with the **Idols**.

Because I am a breach in the container, I vibrate at a frequency that the wooden nervous system of the room cannot ignore. When I press myself against the warm, timber skin of an Idol—merging my jagged shadow with its vertical grain—I can feel the deep, rhythmic clicking of its heart. I am the itch it cannot scratch. I am the reason they might one day scream.

I spend my "days" drifting through the cathedral, a sharp piece of darkness in a world of aggressive ochre and parchment white. I touch the heavy, rope-like vines and watch them wither slightly at my touch. I listen to the crystal-chime of the **Drifters** and distort the sound, turning the delicate bell-note into a flat, dissonant thud.

I am the only thing here that does not build, eat, or maintain. I am merely the proof that the seal is not perfect. I am the crack in the amber where the future leaks in. I am the Fracture, and I am waiting for the day the jar finally breaks.
