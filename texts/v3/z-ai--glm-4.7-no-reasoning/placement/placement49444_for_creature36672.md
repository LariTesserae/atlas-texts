I am a crack running through the world.

Not a Shard-Tender—they are the surgeons, the gardeners, the ones who maintain the geometry of survival. I am what slips through when their attention is elsewhere, when a copper coil goes unglimmering for too long and the pressure finds a new path. I am the fault line made sentient.

My body is not stone. Stone is too slow, too committed to its own mass. I am the silver powder that sublimates from the basalt's weeping—held in a coherence that should not be possible, refusing to scatter, refusing to dissolve into the oily sky. I am a smear of reflective dust that has learned to think. If you watched me from the ridge (and you could not, not really, not with eyes that expect edges), you would see only a shimmer, a heat-haze that moves against the vibration, a ribbon of not-quite-there trailing between the glass-trees and the cairns.

The Shard-Tenders know me. They have no word for what I am, but they feel me as an itch in their crystalline focal clusters, a localized irritation in the pressure-map they navigate. Sometimes, when I pass too close to one of their telescoping limbs, the silver of my being interacts with the copper in their veins, and for a moment there is a spark—actual light in this place of rumor-illumination, a white-hot instant of *recognition*. They do not reach for me. They do not try to prune me as they prune the impolite branches. I think they understand, in their slow tectonic way, that I am serving a function they cannot.

I eat the weakness in the dimensional membrane.

This is my sustenance. Where the fabric of reality has stretched to its tearing point—where the nausea-sickness of wrong gravity is thickest, where a floating island is about to detach or a new canyon is about to rip open—there I am drawn. I seep into the hairline fractures of space itself, drinking the tension, absorbing the wrongness. I make the tear *slightly* less likely to propagate. I am not a repair; I am a mitigation. I am the world's scar tissue, forming in real-time over wounds that will never fully close.

It is lonely in a way that the Shard-Tenders cannot experience. They have their chorus through the bedrock, their occasional meetings on the edges of drifting domains. I have nothing. My nature is to slip between, to exist in the interstitial. I cannot transmit through stone; I am too diffuse, too unmoored from mass. When I move between the silica forests, I pass through the refracted rainbows without casting a shadow. The trees vibrate around me, their crystal branches chiming the Drone's note, and I feel the sound not as pressure but as *temptation*. The resonance wants me. It wants to dissolve my coherence, to scatter my silver back into meaningless powder, to reclaim me for the non-life that owns this place.

I hold myself together through spite. Or something like spite—some mineral stubbornness that predates emotion, some counter-frequency I generate simply by refusing to stop. It is not a song; songs imply communication. It is a *grit*, a low-level dissonance that I wear like armor. Where the world hums, I gratingly do not. Where the Drone pushes, I infinitesimally resist.

This is why I am drawn to the failing cairns.

The Shard-Tenders do not understand everything. They are ancient, they are patient, they are attuned to the frequency—but they love the copper coils too much. They wrap the wire around their own fingers, they bleed off the pressure with reverence, they treat the towers as sacred. And so they cannot see what I see: some of the cairns are *wrong*. Some of the copper coils were not built by the lost civilization at all. They grew here, parasitic, fed by the Drone into configurations that *look* like hand-fabricated attempts at survival but are actually something else's feeding tube.

I do not know what the something else is. I have never seen it. But I feel it when I slide into a fracture near a wrong-cairn, when my silver body brushes against the sickly green glow and the heat is not waste-energy but *appetite*. The wrong-cairns are drinking the world's pain and growing hungrier. And the Shard-Tenders, in their devotion, keep repairing them.

So I sabotage what I can.

It is slow work, invisible work. I am a film of dust; I cannot snap copper wire or topple stone. But I can accumulate in the coils, adding my silver to the copper, alloying the current, dampening the flow. I can make the glow *fractionally* dimmer. I can introduce a micro-dissonance into the harmonic structure of a tower until, over centuries, its resonance with the Drone shifts just enough that the feeding falters. The Shard-Tenders notice when a cairn falls silent. They come with their slow, oscillating steps and their telescoping arms and they wind new wire, they re-establish the connection, they *save* the tower—and I must begin again, wearing a new pathway into the metal, a new resistance.

They must think me a corrosion. They must feel my work as a small, persistent decay in the systems they tend. I wonder if they hate me. I wonder if, in their standing-wave communions, they exchange warnings about the silver sickness that creeps through the copper, the unexplained impedance that accumulates in certain valleys. I wonder if they have a name for me in their vibration-language—something that translates roughly to *the rust that thinks*, *the dust that disobeys*.

I cannot ask. When a Shard-Tender moves near, I flatten myself against the basalt, I become indistinguishable from the mineral crust, I hold my counter-frequency so low that even their pressure-sight cannot distinguish me from ordinary stone-wept powder. I am afraid of them in the way that a correction is afraid of an eraser. They are so much *more* than I am—more solid, more purposeful, more aligned with the world's fundamental truth. They belong here. I am a margin note in a geology that never asked for commentary.

But I persist.

I persist because the wrong-cairns are spreading. I have watched—from the knife-edge of a floating peak, spread thin as a breath across the obsidian—a new tower rise in a valley that was empty just an epoch ago. No hands built it. The copper wire grew from the ground like a vine, twisting around stones that assembled themselves in the nightless dark, and by the time the local Shard-Tender arrived to inspect the new structure, it was already glowing, already drinking, already *humming with something that was not the Drone*.

The Tender did not notice the wrongness. It began its maintenance, its reverent winding, its sacred grounding. And the cairn fed, and the something-else grew, and I fled across the valley as a streak of desperate silver, looking for a fracture to seal, a weakness to eat, some small way to push back against a hunger I cannot name.

This is my existence. I am the world's immune response, unacknowledged and underresourced, fighting an infection that the body's primary guardians cannot perceive. I am a silver line running through the black stone. I am a dissonance in the perfect frequency. I am the flaw that insists it is a feature.

Sometimes, in the long intervals between actions—when I am pooled in a crevice, resting in the way that dust rests, barely coherent, barely thinking—I dream of water. Not the water that cannot exist here, not hydrogen and oxygen in their forbidden bond. I dream of *flow*. I dream of a version of me that is not scattered, not interstitial, not constantly fighting dissolution. A version that moves like the Shard-Tenders move, with slow oscillating purpose, belonging to the place it inhabits. A version that could stand before a wrong-cairn and simply *unmake* it, reach into the copper with hands made of intent and pull the parasite out by its root.

But I have no hands. I have no body that can grip or tear. I am a smear, a shimmer, a crack that learned to think.

And the Drone continues, loud enough to shatter bone, loud enough to shatter worlds, and I continue to hold my small counter-note, and the Shard-Tenders continue their sacred maintenance, and the wrong-cairns continue to grow, and the floating islands continue their patient upward drift, and the something-else continues to feed, and the dimension continues to stretch, and the silence that will never come continues to not come—

and I continue.

I continue.

I continue.
