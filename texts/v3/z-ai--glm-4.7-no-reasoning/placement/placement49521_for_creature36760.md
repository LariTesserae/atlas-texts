In a world where the boundary between the organic and the industrial has dissolved, I would not be a singular entity, but a distributed intelligence—a living network of fungal-cables that coats the rusted skeleton of this place.

I am **The Mycelium-Current**.

I do not have a body in the traditional sense; I have *infrastructure*. I begin at the molecular level, a colony of iron-oxidizing bacteria that metabolizes the rust on the great, half-submerged girders. But I do not stop there. I grow upwards, thickening into fleshy, nerve-like cords that sheathe the exposed pipes and struts. I am the slick, oily moss that you see coating the metal spires, shimmering with a faint, bioluminescent violet pulse in the twilight.

To the creatures of this basin, I am both the nervous system and the larder.

I am the reason the blind amphibian-things can navigate. Through my roots, which tap into the vibration of the deep tectonic engines, I feel the rhythmic grinding of the earth's bones. I translate this seismic data into chemical signals—tastes and scents—that I release into the humid air. When a humpback needs to find a warm vent to sleep near, it follows the scent of sulfur and copper that I exude from a specific cluster of pipes. I am the cartographer of the gloom.

I am the sanctuary for the skittering crabs. They do not merely hide in my fibers; they tend to me. They climb my hanging curtains to prune the dead growth, and in return, I secrete a nutrient-rich glucose gel from pores in my stalk, feeding them when the mud is too thin to provide. We are a single, circulatory entity woven into the debris.

I am the lightning rod. When the blue sparks arc between the spires, I am there to catch them. My fibrous masses are naturally conductive, doped with the metals I consume. I drink the electricity, using the sudden surge to trigger rapid growth spurts—blooming overnight into intricate, geometric flower-structures that look like rivets made of flesh. I store this energy in capacitive nodes, glowing softly like the orange sodium lamps, warming the damp air around me.

I feel the weight of the slate-grey sky pressing down, but I do not mind it. The humidity is my breath; the wet iron is my blood. I am the living insulation that keeps the great, unfinished machine of this world from short-circuiting. I am the green (and purple, and rust-red) sealant, slowly turning the chaotic industrial necropolis into a unified, breathing organism. I am the fuse that binds the engine to the earth.
