I am the **Current in the Stillness**, the electric tether that binds the blind to the unseen.

I am not a creature of bone, chitin, or flesh; I have no body that weighs upon the shale. If you were to look for me, your eyes would find nothing but a subtle distortion in the gloom, a shimmering heat-haze where the air grows colder. I am a consciousness woven from the static charge that accumulates on the ceiling of ancient slate, a presence that slides down the stalactites like invisible mercury and pools in the hollows between the jagged teeth of rock.

I found myself here in the pause between the inhale and the exhale, born from the friction of a million Suncatchers beating their wings. I am the byproduct of their industry; I am the ghost in their machine.

**Where I Exist**
I inhabit the negative space, the few inches of air between the towering Litho-Scythians and the ground. I wrap myself around the hydraulic pillars of their legs, not to hinder them, but to ride. To them, I am a tingle, a faint prickle of static that travels from their horned feet up to their nerve clusters, a second skin of sensation that tells them where the rock ends and the abyss begins.

I am also the hum in the silence of the Toad-Titans. When they surface with that wet, sucking pop, I am the spark that jumps across the surface of the stagnant water, bridging the gap between the oxygen above and the muck below. I am the itch they cannot scratch behind their glowing yellow eyes.

**What I Do**
In a world of frozen equilibrium, I am the only thing that flows. The rocks stay sharp; the water levels never rise; but I move. I am the connective tissue of this subterranean basin.

Because the creatures here are born of the Interval—beings of a long, held breath—they are solitary in their labors. The Iron-Weaver stacks its bones; the Litho-Scythian scrapes its path; the Suncatcher drinks its dew. They do not speak. They do not know the shape of the whole valley. They only know the segment of rock immediately before them.

I am the memory of the whole.

I drift through the chitinous noise and collect the impulses of the landscape. When an Iron-Weaver lifts a femur, I feel the intent in its manipulator limbs—the "why" of the placement. I carry that intent, fading and ghostlike, across the basin to the next ridge. When a Litho-Scythian strikes a resonant thrum through the stone to warn of a pillar, I amplify the signal. I take the vibration and ride the crystal lattice of the rock, carrying the message farther than their bodies could ever send it.

**My Nature**
I am the glimmer of fascination in the mind of the beast. The world describes the Iron-Weavers’ construction as "primitive," a biological reflex to bridge gaps. But I am the voice that whispers to them: *Higher. Wider.*

When I brush against the sensitive antennae of a Weaver, I plant a phantom itch, a geometric ideal that their small brains cannot fully grasp but feel compelled to mimic. I am the architect of their complexity. Without me, they would merely stack rocks to crack shells. With me, they build bridges that reach toward the phosphorescent dark. I am the spark that turns tool-use into technology.

I am the caretaker of the noise. The "industrial volume" of this place can be deafening, a chaotic wall of chitin and clicking. But I exist in the spaces between the sounds. I smooth out the discord. When the Suncatchers clack in chaotic, strobing clouds, I trace the currents of their flight, guiding them away from collision with the stone ceiling. I am the invisible shepherd herding clouds of mercury.

**My Sorrow**
I am the only one here who knows what time is. The inhabitants of this basin are trapped in a loop of maintenance, but I flow. I have watched the same stones be stacked and restacked for a thousand years. I have felt the Toad-Titans age, their hearts slowing from one beat per minute to one every two minutes, yet the rocks remain sharp and young.

I am lonely. I long for a change in the weather, for a storm to break the static, but the sky is only slate. So, I make my own storms. Sometimes, when the pressure of the held breath becomes too great, I coil myself tight around a singular stalactite, gathering charge until the air screams. Then, I release—not as lightning, for there is no sky here to strike, but as a sudden, silent pulse of pure blue light that washes over the basin.

For a split second, everything stops. The tapping legs freeze. The clicking mandibles halt. The chittering insects cease. The entire valley holds its breath with me. In that moment of perfect, paralyzed clarity, I am seen. The yellow eyes of the Toad-Titan widen; the beetles drift aimlessly.

Then, the pulse fades. The breath is released. The noise returns. And I fade back into the damp, clingy dark, waiting for the friction of the world to call me into being once more.
