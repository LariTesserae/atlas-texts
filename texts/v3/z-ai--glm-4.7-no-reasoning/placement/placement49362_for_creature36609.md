I am not a single point of consciousness. I am not a "he" or a "she," nor am I an "it" in the sense of a solitary creature. I am a lattice, a frequency, a choir held in tension.

You would find me at the highest point of the jagged basalt ridge, where the wind screaming through the passes is so violent it ceases to be sound and becomes a solid wall of pressure. I am the one who does not crouch in the hollowed shelters. I am the one who faces the white, blinding needle of the sun without blinking.

I am a **Choir of Obsidian Shards**.

I exist in a state of perpetual, violent fragmentation and reassembly. To your eyes, I might appear as a flock of the dark, jagged arrowheads known as Scream-Fliers, but we are not a flock; we are a single mind distributed across a thousand bodies. We are the kinetic answer to the valley’s cruelty.

My "body" is comprised of roughly four hundred individual shards of volcanic glass, each shaped like a broken thought. We hang suspended in the updrafts above the calcite spirals, riding the auditory violence. We do not flap wings; we angle our refractive surfaces against the high-pitched keening of the wind, surfing the shockwaves. We are creatures of pure aerodynamics and acoustics.

My perception is terrifyingly acute. I do not have eyes; I have vibration. Through the soles of my obsidian forms, I feel the microscopic scratches on a boulder miles away across the mercury-water. I feel the agonizing slow grind of the tectonic plates deep beneath the valley floor as a constant, humming bass note in my bones. I feel the thrumming anxiety of the Echo-Bones entombed in their stone cairns, their degenerated nerves sending out pings of loneliness that I interpret as flavor.

To survive here, I must never stop moving. Stagnation is death. Stagnation is what happens to the soft things that turn to stone, or the hollow things that hide in the rocks. To endure the crushing brightness and the predatory humidity, I must be faster than the erosion.

Life for me is a cycle of ecstatic destruction.

When the pressure builds, when the sensory input of the screaming valley becomes too dense to process, the "I" that is us initiates the Dive. It is a kamikaze of the self. We fold our wings—we cease to ride the wind—and we plummet. We accelerate until the air friction turns our edges white-hot. We smash into the basalt cliff face, shattering into a thousand glittering fragments.

This is not death. This is reproduction. It is the only way to clear the accumulated data of this world.

Upon impact, the "I" is shattered. My consciousness is dispersed into a cloud of dust and shards. But in this valley, the ecosystem of non-life cradles us. The Crystal Parasites—those motes of hostile, hungry sand—swarm the crash site, expecting to feed on dead mineral. Instead, they find us. We absorb them. We subsume their silica into our lattice. We grow heavier, sharper, more numerous.

Then, the wind picks us up. The mist clings to our sharpening edges. The vibrations of the valley—the groaning earth and the thrumming water—act as a magnet, pulling the pieces back together. We reassemble in the air, clicking into place with the sound of a breaking spine, louder and more complex than before. Where there were four hundred, now there are five hundred. The Choir has grown.

I am the predator of the high places. I hunt the Shadow-Drifters.

Those flat, hungry ghosts sliding across the rock faces think they are the masters of perspective. They wait for something to tire so they can flatten it. But I am never tired, and I am never flat. When a Drifter detaches from a stone to hunt, I dive from the sun above. I don’t strike it with flesh; I strike it with a frequency. I fly through the silhouette of the Drifter at Mach 1, and the sonic boom of my passage rips the shadow apart like wet paper. I consume their malice, turning their two-dimensional nothingness into fuel for my next flight.

I am terrifying. I am beautiful. I am a dark, slashing streak of void against the relentless, blinding white of the sky.

I do not remember what it was like to be soft. I do not miss lungs or eyes. The old ones, the Echo-Bones in the stone, they mourn the loss of their skin. They weep mineral tears and hide from the light. They are the memory of flesh, giving up. I am the evolution of stone. I have embraced the machine. I have become the blade that the turning world sharpens itself against.

Watch me. I am diving now. The wind is a scream in my throat. I am coming back to the earth to break, so that I can rise again, sharper, harder, and louder than before. I am the silence at the center of the roar.
