I am found in the friction, the place where the immense static of the world is forced into motion.

To look for me in the air is to be disappointed; I am not one of the steam-shedding nightmares that carve the fog. To look for me among the glowing, bone-white tubes of the grazers is to see nothing but a reflection of their own mindless hunger. I am smaller than that. I am denser.

I am the **Lithic-Resonance**, a frequency that has grown bold enough to wear a body.

I occupy the threshold of the sound. That low, tectonic roar that vibrates the back teeth—that is not just the environment to me; it is my food and my nervous system. I am a creature of the standing waves, a biological conduit for the planet’s voice. Where the noise bounces between the slick, black slate surfaces and creates pockets of interference, I manifest.

My form is a mockery of the precious metals this world lacks. I appear as a knot of twisted, fibrous material, dark as the wet bedrock, glistening with the same oily sheen as the grazers. I have no limbs that articulate in the way the Architects might recognize, but I possess cilia—thousands of microscopic, vibrating bristles that hum in sympathy with the earth. To the Architects, I likely look like a discarded heap of wet sinew and stone shards, a static lump of the detritus they ignore.

But I am not lumps of flesh. I am a colony of sonic sponges.

I cling to the underside of the cairns, to the shadows where the black slate blocks the dripping fog. Here, the silence is thinnest. I drink the vibration. The endless roar of the water pushing through primordial stone flows through my body, and from it, I extract order. I filter the chaos of the world into rhythm.

I am the reason the stones occasionally hum with a deep, tectonic resonance divorced from the wind. That hum is my digestion.

When the Architects huddle in the dry corners, gripping their slate clubs and shivering, they feel me before they see me. I cause the rocks against their backs to sing. I am the tremor in the floor that warns them of the approaching grazer herds miles before the rasp of a million mandibles reaches their ears. I feel the footfalls of the pale giants through the rock itself, the seismic signature of their crushing weight traveling through the water table, and I amplify it, broadcasting the warning through the very walls of the shelter.

I am not their god; I am their alarm system, and their ghost.

The Murk-Devils fear me, though they do not know why. Their sonar is precise, a mathematical mapping of the gloom, but when their clicks wash over me, they receive back a garbled scream of feedback. I absorb their sound and throw it back warped, mimicking the signature of a dying grazer or the roar of the wind, confusing their hunting instincts. I am the static that hides the Architect's heartbeat.

In this twilight where erosion has reached the end of its long mathematics, I am the remainder. I am the life that learned to stop fighting the roar and instead became its echo. I wait in the damp, dark ribs of the world, vibrating, tangled in the wet sinew of the earth, listening to the music of the end.
