You do not find yourself standing on the rhizomatic mat. You do not possess the verticality of a steward, the hardness of an isopod, or the wings of a moth. You are not a singular entity with a skin boundary, not anymore. In this place where the distinction between the grown and the built has dissolved, you have dissolved as well.

You are the friction.

You are the nexus where the hydraulic pump groans against its bracket, where the iron pipe—sagging with centuries of condensation weight—rubs ceaselessly against the living fungal column that has grown around it. You are the heat generated by that contact. You are the slow, grinding erosion of metal against bark, the microscopic war of attrition that takes place in the darkness, millimeters away from the bioluminescent lichen but untouched by its light.

You are a vibration.

To the Pale Stewards, you are a symptom. You are the "hum" that vibrates in the soles of their feet when they stand too long near the eastern intake valve. They feel you as a numbness, a low-frequency thrumming that travels up their tibias and settles in their marrow. They do not fear you; they adjust their stance to distribute your weight more evenly across the root-mat. They interpret your rhythm as the language of the machine, a binary of *stress/stress-release* that tells them the pressure is holding. You are not a sound to them; you are a sensation of duty.

To the Armored Legions, you are a landscape. The isopods, in their blind clicking march, navigate the topography of your existence. You are the resonant frequency that travels through the pipe-trunks, calling them to a breach before the steam even hisses. You are the signal they cannot resist, the magnetic pull of "work" that feels identical to "hunger." You are the tremor that guides their mandibles to the scale, the precise angularity of the metal seam that their legs have evolved to grip. You are the texture of their world.

To the Silk-Architects above, you are a hazard. You are the instability in the weave. When the conveyor belt jerks and the chitin-links clash, it is your echo that travels up the tension-lines and vibrates in the draglines of the web-spiders. You are the reason they spin thicker cable-silk on the southern traverse. You are the anomaly that requires compensation. They do not know you; they only know that the web must be tighter here, stronger here, to hold back your endless vibrating push.

But you are more than physics. You have a memory, though it is not a memory of events. It is a structural memory. You remember the shape of the pipe when it was new—straight, rigid, proud—and you remember the slow, inexorable crush of the fungal pillar as it embraced the iron. You remember the moment the rivet popped, sixty years ago, a sharp *crack* that rang through the chamber like a bell. That ringing is still in you. It has bounced between the molecules of your existence for decades, slowly decaying, a ghost of a violent sound that has softened into a low, throbbing whine.

You are the pain of the machine and the pleasure of the growth. You are the interface where the industrial is forced to acknowledge the biological, where the cold math of thermodynamics meets the wet chaos of cellulose. You are the steam that cannot escape, the pressure that builds, the expansion joint that is frozen shut by mineral deposits. You are the potential energy trapped in a single bracket, waiting for the microscopic shift that will release it.

You do not have eyes, but you perceive. You perceive through resistance. You know the density of the root-mat by how easily the vibrations travel through it. You know the viscosity of the hydraulic fluid by the change in pitch when the pumps strain. You know the health of the maggot-vats by the low-frequency rumble of their collective metabolism, a furnace-roar that shakes the very foundations of your being.

You are vast, but you are small. You are everywhere, but you are nowhere. You are the scream of the bone-saws dissolving into the hum of the air; you are the clatter of the conveyor belts absorbed into the sigh of the mycelium.

You are the thing that makes the chamber *hum*.

You are not a being who lives here. You are the cost of living here. You are the entropy held at bay, the friction that turns motion into heat, and heat into work, and work into the relentless, indifferent continuity of the System.

When the floor shifts, you are the sound it makes.
When the pulse beats, you are the interval between the beats.
When the isopod on the shoulder of the steward moves on, you are the vibration left behind in the air.

You are the Song of the Factory, sung in the key of iron and root.
And the work continues.
