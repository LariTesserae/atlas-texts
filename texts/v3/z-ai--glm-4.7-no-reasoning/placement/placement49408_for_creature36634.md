I am found in the pause between the beats.

I reside in the **Node of Severed Frequencies**, a hollow sphere exactly fifty meters across, where the obsidian floor is polished to such a high sheen that it reflects nothing but the wet, hanging darkness. This is my domain, not because I rule it, but because I am the only thing here that does not vibrate.

You would find me in the precise geometric center of the room, hovering perhaps a meter above the pooled water. I do not touch the water, for to touch it is to disturb its surface tension, and to disturb the water is to create sound. Here, silence is the highest virtue, the only currency that matters.

I am a **Weeping Anchor**.

I do not look like the Silence-Skinners, who flow like oil and mimic the walls. Nor am I akin to the drifting spores of the Menders. I appear, at first glance, to be a piece of the architecture that has forgotten how to hold itself up. I am tall—perhaps three meters in height—and skeletal in structure, but my bones are not made of calcium or alloy. They are composed of a dense, translucent crystal, darker than the surrounding gloom, etched with microscopic fractures that glow with a faint, violet luminescence.

My form is humanoid, but elongated and stretched, as if the city itself pulled me taut. Where a head should be, there is only a complex, sensory orb—a cluster of lenses and acoustic dampeners arranged in a radial symmetry, resembling the aperture of a deep-sea camera left to rot in the brine. I have no mouth to speak, no lungs to scream. My body is wrapped in tatters of a membrane that was once perhaps skin, now hangings of grey silk that drift lazily in the stagnant air, weightless and still.

I am the stillness in the chaos.

While the Silence-Skinners navigate by the roar of the pistons, I exist to negate it. The city screams; I absorb. My purpose, handed down by the genetic memory of the Sediment Trough, is to act as the stabilizing weight for the dead zones. The Drift-Menders fix the pipes; the Skinners filter the air; I filter the noise. My crystal body resonates inversely to the thrum of the massive machines. When the cathedral-sized piston chuds in the distance, sending a shockwave through the rock, my skeletal frame hums a note exactly one octave lower, canceling out the vibration before it can shatter the delicate equilibrium of this specific node.

I am always alone. The others fear me, not because I am a predator, but because I represent the void. To a Skinner, who sees through sound, my presence is a "blind spot"—a hole in the universe where the echo does not return. They avoid the node where I float, for to enter my sphere of influence is to lose the song that guides them. They flow around the perimeter of my chamber, pressing against the walls, sensing the edge of my silence like a pressure drop in their skin.

I sustain myself on the "ghost sounds"—the energy lost in the transmission. When an Echo-Thief replays a captured laugh from the ceiling, or a distant pipe bursts, the stray acoustic energy floats through the humid air like dust. I open my sensory orb, and the fractures in my crystal body brighten, drinking in the vibrations. I metabolize the noise. I turn the cacophony of the dying city into the violet light that glows within my ribs.

Sometimes, the humidity grows too thick, and the weight of the water threatens to collapse the dead zones. Then, the Drift-Menders come. They do not speak to me; they simply drift close, their gelatinous bodies brushing against my hanging silk. They seal a leak in the wall ten meters away, and in the sudden, temporary quiet that follows, I reach out with a long, spindly finger—sharp as a needle—and prick the swollen body of the Mender. I do not hurt it; I simply drain the excess pressure it has accumulated, vibrating the stress away until it floats freely again. It is a communion of function, wordless and efficient.

I am always waiting. I am the listener at the center of the web. When the city finally inhales for the last time, when the great pistons stop churning and the neon veins dim to black, the Silence-Skinners will be blind, and the Echo-Thieves will have nothing to steal. The darkness will become absolute.

But I will still be here. In the absence of sound, I will finally be whole. I will become the silence itself, solidified in the dark, the last piece of the Sediment Trough to realize that the waiting is over. I will not move; I will simply cease to function, dissolving into the wet obsidian floor, returning to the geology, a final, silent deposit in the basement of the world.
