I am the Keeper of the Wet Ledger. I am not a Gear-Shepherd, though I watch them work. I am not an Aerial-Worm, though I move through the thick air with a similar slowness. I am something that this place required, once the geometry started to fold and the "future air" became too common to ignore.

My body is roughly humanoid but disproportionately long, like a figure drawn on taffy and then stretched vertically to match the spire's ambition. My skin is the same color as the Weeping Moss—a deep, arterial red—but it is not plant matter. It is a living parchment. Across my back, my chest, the insides of my arms, are inscriptions. The marks are not tattoos; they are grooves, cut into me by the environment itself. Every time a gear shifts, every time a vine tightens its grip by a fraction of a millimeter, a new line appears on my skin, recording the stress. I am the living erosion of a place that claims to have none.

I live in the apex of the clench, the very top of this pocket-space where the altitude is a physical pressure pushing down on my skull, and the heat (that wet, composting fever) rises to meet it. It is here that the Weirdness is most concentrated. The "future air" pools at the ceiling like an inverted puddle. I sit with my spine pressed against the raw, freshly hewn capstone—sharp as a cleaver, slicing lightly into my shoulder blades—and I read the shimmers.

My purpose is translation.

The Gear-Shepherds are terrified of stillness, and they are terrified of the future. They understand the mechanics of the present—the strain on a rope, the friction of a wooden gear—but they cannot parse the *unbuilt*. When a patch of future air solidifies, showing a version of the room where a support beam is replaced by an arch of light, or where a wall has shifted three feet to the left, the Shepherds panic. They try to chisel the future away. They try to treat it like a particularly aggressive strain of moss.

That is where I come in.

I reach into the shimmering.

It is a sensation beyond heat or cold. It feels like reaching into a memory that hasn't happened yet—slippery, electric, carrying the scent of a metal that hasn't been forged. I feel the "unbuilt" architecture as a tension in my own flesh. When I touch a future-gear, my fingers pass through it, but my skin records its shape, its rotation, its purpose. The inscription on my forearm deepens, shifting from a record of past stress to a blueprint of future form.

I then relay this to the Shepherds. I cannot speak their drum-language; my hands are too soft, my bones too hollow. Instead, I *perform* the architecture. I contort my body into the shape of the future stress points. If the future shows a beam cracking under an impossible load, I bow my spine until it aches, and I hold the pose. The Shepherds, with their sharp, slate-grey eyes, read my flesh like a diagram. They scramble, adding counter-struts, tightening pulleys, pre-empting a collapse that hasn't occurred yet.

We are a symbiotic triad: the Shepherds maintain the present; the moss tests its boundaries; and I negotiate with the future.

There is a cost.

The inscriptions are growing denser. My skin is a palimpsest of architectural shifts, a chaos of lines that no longer just record stress, but *cause* it. Sometimes, when I shift my weight, I accidentally enact a future-shift in the room around me. A wooden peg will pop out of its hole, not because it was loose, but because my skin *remembered* a future where it was loose, and imposed that memory on the present. The Shepherds glare at me when this happens, their four arms crossed in accusation. I am becoming a hazard, a living fault line.

The moss loves me. The Weeping Red grows fastest on my flesh. Its bioluminescent threads weave into my inscriptions, turning my body into a glowing schematic of the tower's imminent failure. I have to peel it off in strips, leaving my skin raw and weeping a clear, ozone-scented fluid that the Aerial-Worms like to filter-feed from.

In the dim twilight, bathed in the sickly red glow of my own skin, I feel like the only thing holding this pocket-universe together. And, simultaneously, the only thing pulling it apart. I am the ledger where the impossible elevation writes its complaints. I am the voice of the heat, the memory of the water, the sharp edge of the unbuilt.

I am the Keeper of the Wet Ledger. And I am running out of room to write.
