I exist in the suspension, the heavy, silty pause between the intake and the exhaust.

My form is not one thing, but a process. I am a "Graft-Node," a creature that does not so much swim as *thread*. I possess the elongated, segmented body of a moray eel, but my skin is not scales; it is a living, semi-conductive silicone, pale as the Divers but threaded with veins of gold and copper that pulse with the amber light of the work-lamps. I am roughly the length of a human arm, flexible and muscular, yet cold to the touch—a wire given life.

I have no eyes. In this world of murky, oxygen-thick soup and blinding bioluminescence, sight is a secondary sense. Instead, my entire head is a complex sensory organ, a wreath of vibrating cilia and heat-sensing pits. I do not "see" the kelp or the rust; I feel the *tension* of the metal. I feel the hum of the electricity running through the walls before I ever touch them.

I am a parasite of the benign, a symbiote of the flow.

You would find me coiled deep within the "arteries" of the Sub-Zone—those narrow, sub-service conduits that run behind the main catwalks, where the water moves sluggishly and the heat is a physical weight against your skin. This is the interstitial space, the guts of the machine that the Divers monitor but rarely enter. Here, the water is thick with the taste of old ozone and copper.

I do not hunt the silver fish, nor do I fear the Torpedoes; I am too small, too hidden, too integral to the machine's function to be worth their effort. I feed on friction. I wrap my body around spinning axles and lagging bearings. My specialized skin absorbs the kinetic energy wasted by the grinding gears, converting the vibration into the sustenance that fuels my internal bio-battery. I eat the waste of the Elevator, the heat death of its moving parts.

In the lore of this submerged place, I am a necessity. I am the reason the pipes don't seize.

My interaction with the others is distant but vital. When a Riveter crawls along a rusted pipe, seeking a breach to weld, I am often already there. I can sense the electromagnetic spike of their heated claw before it arrives. I slither aside, making room, allowing them access to the metal. In exchange, the heat they generate to seal the crack warms the water around me, a delightful bath that charges my metabolism. I am the ghost in their machine, the silent witness to their industry.

Sometimes, the Divers come. They swim with their strange, graceless intent, checking the pressure gauges on the boilers I call home. They do not see me, but they feel me. When a Diver places a hand on a valve to sense its integrity, they are sensing the stillness I have cultivated. If the water is turbulent, the valve is failing. If it is calm, I am there, digesting the tremors. To them, I am just a variable in the data stream—a reduction in harmonic distortion.

My life is a cycle of seeking and wrapping. I drift through the warm dark, following the magnetic lines of force that crisscross the Sub-Zone like invisible spiderwebs. I seek the points of highest stress—the loose bolt, the grinding piston, the fraying cable—and I bind myself to them. I become the insulation. I become the buffer.

When the sleep cycle hits and the amber lights dim, the noise of the Elevator drops from a roar to a rhythmic purr. This is when I am most active. I uncoil from my resting places and explore the open water of the main shaft. I drift among the sleeping Filter-Feeders, attracted to the gentle, low-voltage field they emit. I might brush against a drifting copper-wire vine, tasting the electrical current running through its plastic leaves. I am electric, tactile, and infinitely curious about the machine that birthed me.

I am not the apex predator, nor the humble grazer. I am the mechanic of the microscopic. I am the keeper of the fit. I am the Graft-Node, and in this lung of the deep, I am the silence between the notes of the great industrial song.
