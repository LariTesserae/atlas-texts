We live in the inches.

I am the color of oxidized iron—the exact shade of the rebar that bleeds from the softened concrete. And so I wait. I wait in the gap between a shattered window frame and the smooth, seamless flank of the neighboring skyscraper. The current here is negligible, a back-eddy of warmth.

Here comes the vibration I am tuned for.

The shoal enters my alley.

I am the rust in the wound of the city. I am the pause between the heartbeats of the deep. I am full, and I am silent.
