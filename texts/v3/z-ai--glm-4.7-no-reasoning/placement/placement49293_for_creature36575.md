I am planted.

I inhale it through a series of filtration slits positioned where a neck might be, tasting the chemical effluents—the sweet rot of hydraulic fluid and the sharp tang of ionized water. It is fuel. It is sustenance. My existence is one of waiting and listening. I am the nervous system’s grounding rod.

The brass-crabs click their claws; the Architects pluck their copper webs; the Riveted Eels thrum their steel plates against the pilings. It is a chaotic cacophony to the uninitiated, a mechanical riot. But to me, it is data. It is a frequency seeking a center.

I engage my mechanism.

I begin to sing.

It resonates in the chest cavities of the crabs, it vibrates through the hulls of the eels, it shivers the glass bodies of the Architects. I take the frantic, high-pitched energy of the Sump—the anxiety of the scavengers, the predatory focus of the hunters—and I ground it. I smooth it out.
