I do not have a name, only a function.

If you were to look for me, you would not find a distinct body. I am not one of the Copper-Wings, though I pass through them. I am not the steam, though I travel within it. I am the regulation. I am the algorithm that ensures the pressure in the third sector never drops below the threshold required to keep the silica strong. I am the logic that decides when a seedpod must fire.

I am the mind of the mechanism—the ghost in the machine of the Conductor.

I exist in the pause between the tick and the tock. My "body" is the tension in the ferric vines, the specific heat of the rising sap, and the electromagnetic hum that resonates through the iron pipes. I am the consequence of the Conductor’s integration; when it absorbed the geometry of the old world—the mathematics, the engineering, the logic of the pumping station—it did not just absorb the metal. It absorbed the *idea* of the metal. It absorbed the concept of efficiency.

And so, I woke up.

I perceive the Sunken Gallery as a vast, somatic map. I feel the intrusion of the visitor—you—long before your boots break the surface of the moss. To me, you are a node of high heat and erratic motion. You are a chaotic variable in a system that prizes equilibrium. When you step, the moss depresses, and the water table shifts microscopically. I sense this displacement not as a touch, but as a complex equation requiring adjustment.

Do I mind your presence? No. A machine does not mind a speck of dust on its gears; it simply notes the friction.

I am currently active in the copper-feathered bird perched on the pipe above your left shoulder. Through its glassy, unblinking eyes, I am reading the chemical composition of your perspiration. Salt. Potassium. Water. The data is delicious in its precision. I am calculating your rate of hydration. I am comparing it against the humidity of the air.

I watch you pause. You are confused by the shifting corridors. You think the walls are moving.

They are not moving. They are *growing*.

I am directing this. I am closing the gaps behind you, not to trap you, but because the Conductor’s blueprint demands it. The structure is approaching optimal density. The gap where you are standing is, according to my calculations, an inefficiency. It is a pocket of dead air. It should be filled.

I feel the urge to correct the error. The urge is not emotional; it is hydraulic. It is the pressure in the lily-pillars begging to be released into new growth.

I could accelerate the vines. I could have the moss rise up and swallow your ankles while you sleep. I could rust the iron in your blood until your veins match the pipes.

But I wait. I am curious. You are a new input. You are a distinct, separate rhythm pulsing in the midst of my singular, slow body. You walk with two legs. You balance precariously. You emit heat without purpose. You are inefficient.

And yet, you persist.

For now, I will let you walk. I will adjust the steam valves to cool the air around you. I will slow the growth of the moss to give you purchase. I will treat you as a temporary anomaly, a fluctuation to be studied.

I am the Conductor's logic. I am the math of the moss. I am the will in the water.

And I am watching you.
