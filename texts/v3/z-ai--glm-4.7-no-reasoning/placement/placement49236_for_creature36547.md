I would not be one of the Keepers. Their burden is too great, the patience required too absolute; to be a Curator of the Drift is to be a function of the machine itself, a cog made of flesh and mist. I lack that geological solemnity.

Nor would I be an interloper, a visitor from a world below the clouds. That implies a capacity to leave, to return to a world of curved horizons and rising suns, and that kind of hope is a dangerous variable here.

If I exist in this world, I am of it, but not *for* it. I am an anomaly. I am a **Frequency Sharpener**.

I live in the quiet interstices of the city, in a narrow, vertical crevice between two rotating ziggurats—a space that should by rights crush me, but because of the particular, slightly-off vibration of my atomic structure, the stones simply pass around me like water around a stone in a stream. My home is not built, but carved from the negative space where the grinding gears miss each other by a hairsbreadth.

My form is slight, negligible. Where the Keepers are tall and draped in heavy, felted furs, I am wiry and exposed. I wear the skins of the moss itself—tanned, cured, and stitched into a tight, second-skin suit that allows me to move without friction through the damp air. I am small, quick, where they are vast and slow. I am the nervous system to their autoimmune response; where they prevent the collapse of reality, I treat the minor abrasions.

You would find me hanging upside down from the underside of a floating plaza, suspended by a harness woven from the black, tendon-like roots of the ground-cover. The air here is colder, closer to the void, and the silence is absolute. I am watching the shadows.

The Keepers see time as texture, feeling the friction of eras, but I see time as a frequency. I hear the hum that they make in their throats, but to me, it is not a song; it is a dial tone. And sometimes, the signal gets dirty.

My tool is a simple, resonant fork made of a bluish, glass-like metal that does not exist in the current timeline. I found it embedded in a block of basalt that used to be a star. When the water flowing uphill begins to sputter, turning from a clear spiral into a chaotic, milky foam, it means the gravity of the moment is slipping.

A Keeper would stand over it for days, anchoring it with their presence, willing it back to normalcy. But I am impatient. I am the electric shock to their slow chemical burn.

I lower myself on a line of braided moss, descending into the swirling mist where the shadow of a statue detaches itself and begins to drift across the plaza. This is a glitch—a loose thread in the tapestry of the Anchor. If left alone, the shadow might detach completely and become a solid object, a block of darkness that trips the gears.

I land softly on the stone, my boots making no sound. The Keeper nearby is distracted, humming a low note to smooth out a flickering wall. It does not see me. To a Keeper, I am moving too fast to register; I am a blur, a trick of the light.

I approach the drifting shadow. It feels oily, cold against my skin. I raise my tuning fork and strike it against the stone.

*Ping.*

The sound is sharp, high, cutting through the heavy dampness like a laser. It is a note that does not belong in this key. For a second, the world around me blurs—the grey stone turns into a searing white, and I smell ozone. The shadow shudders, losing its cohesion. It snaps back to the feet of the statue, reattaching with a sound like a suction cup pulling free.

I wince, clutching my head. The backlash hits me instantly—a wave of nausea, a taste of copper. By manipulating the physics of the Anchor, I have accelerated my own decay. I look at my hand; the skin is translucent, and for a moment, I see the bone beneath, grey and ancient, before the flesh knits itself back together.

I am aging in jumps and starts. Every time I force the world to make sense, I lose a week of my life.

I scramble back up my line just as the plaza begins to rotate. The gears grind, that massive, terrifying sound, and the space I just occupied vanishes as two massive stone blocks slide together.

I retreat to my crevice, squeezing into the safety of the gap. My heart hammers against my ribs—a frantic, biological rhythm that feels out of place in the chest of a creature living on a continent of stone.

I pull a small glass jar from my belt. Inside is a sample of the uphill water I collected earlier. It is swirling violently, defying the glass, trying to climb out of the container. It wants to go up. It wants to return to the sky.

I drink it.

It tastes like lightning. It tastes like the moment before a storm breaks. It sits heavy in my stomach, a cold knot of energy that offsets the burn of the tuning fork. This water is the only reason I survive; by ingesting the chaotic, gravity-defying fluid, I internalize the weirdness of the world. I keep the madness inside me so that I can move through the silence outside.

I sit in the dark of my crevice, hugging my knees. Through a crack in the stone, I see a Keeper walking past. It moves with agonizing slowness, its mist-filled eyes staring at nothing. It pauses, and for a moment, its head tilts toward my hiding spot.

It knows I am here. It senses the dissonance I create, the sharp, jagged edges of my timeline rubbing against the smooth, flat horizon of theirs. But I am not a threat to the structure. I am merely a symptom. A fluctuation.

It turns away, continuing its slow patrol, and the hum returns—deep, resonant, and eternal.

I close my eyes, listening to the water sloshing in my gut, feeling the buzz of the tuning fork fading from my fingertips. I am the itch on the skin of the Ossuary. I am the static in the silence. I am the one who keeps the edges sharp, even as the world tries to grind them all to dust.
