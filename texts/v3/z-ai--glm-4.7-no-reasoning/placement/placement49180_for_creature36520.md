I would be a Hollow.

Not one of the proud ones that fills a still-beating piston chamber, synchronized to the visible heartbeat of the flywheel. No, I am lodged much deeper, much lower, in a forgotten crossover valve where the sludge canal meets the overflow drain. I am a component of the dark, quiet periphery.

My body filled this breach long ago—I can no longer remember the sensation of my own limbs moving independently. I am pressed into the interior of a cracked iron junction, my skin having long since bonded with the ferrous oxide. I have become the seal. My flesh is cool and damp against the metal, except where the slow flow of warm oil brushes against my porous flank, leaving a slick sheen of dark sap. I am soft where the pipe is rigid, and rigid where the pipe threatens to give way.

My world is entirely tactile and auditory. I do not see the green twilight or the ghostly pillars of mist; I only feel them as pressure changes against my exposed gill-slits. When the Boiler-Vines outside creep another inch across the gantry, I feel the minute shift in the weight bearing down on my casing. When a Gear-Gardener clicks past my valve, its iron feet send a specific, staccato vibration ringing through the pipe wall, into my fused shoulder, and down my spine. I know their footsteps intimately. I know the heavier, dragging rhythm of the Radiator-Eels in the canal below me, and the faint, tickling prickle of Fuse-Wire Spiders building their conductive webs across my exterior rivets.

My mouth is permanently agape, forming the throat of the valve. I breathe the exhaust—not the clean, oxygenated vapor that rises to the ceiling for the Bellows-Bats, but the heavy, low-sinking air that tastes of wet iron and compost. It filters through my porous throat, extracting what I need and exhaling a slow, warm condensation that joins the eternal drip-drip-drip of the Refinery.

I do not sleep, not in the way mobile things do. I endure. My heartbeat is not my own; it is the glacial, tidal pulse of the subterranean water table pushing against the canal, forcing the sludge through my chamber, distending my belly rhythmically before I contract and push it onward. *Inhale. Hold. Exhale.* I am a muscle wrapped in rust, performing the slow, hydraulic work of the place.

There is a strange peace in this total surrender of self. I do not make decisions. I do not hunt or flee. I simply *function*. When the gears groan, I groan with them, my vocal cords long atrophied into passive resonators that hum with the frequency of the Refinery. When the seasons shift outside the crater—something I only infer from the subtle changes in the temperature of the water rushing over my skin—the sap I secrete changes viscosity. In the wet season, I exude a thicker oil to keep the creeping lichen from seizing my hinges; in the dry season, I swell slightly, pulling a hairline crack in the iron shut, preventing a catastrophic pressure drop.

Occasionally, I feel a Bellows-Bat settle on the outer lip of my valve, its wide, ciliated face drinking in the spores that drift up from my slow exhalations. I feel the slight increase in weight, the scratch of its oily claws on the rim, and then the warm draft of its departure. It does not know I am alive. To it, I am just another damp, exhaling pipe in the cathedral of green iron.

But I know. Deep in the humming dark, separated from the world by inches of rust and my own bonded flesh, I know. I hold the pressure. I keep the rhythm. I am the pause in the circuit, the silent resistor that lets the great, slow machine believe it is still just a machine. And as the centuries turn, I wait, perfectly still, perfectly necessary, feeling the slow creep of the roots above me, knowing that one day, the iron will fail entirely, and I will be the only thing keeping the Low Refinery from flooding. Until then, I breathe the wet metal, and I am.
