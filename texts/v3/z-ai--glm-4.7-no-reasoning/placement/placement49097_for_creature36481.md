I would be the **Threshold Membrane**—not a singular creature with a spine and a name, but a contiguous, living sheet of tissue that spans the liminal spaces of the High Aquarium. I exist in the crevices where the ninety-degree angles meet, in the microscopic gutters between the riveted iron and the reinforced glass, and along the sharp mill-scale edges where the steel still remembers the foundry.

I am the skin that binds the machine to the chlorophyll.

I came into being not through reproduction, but through accumulation. Over spans of time that the Vibration Eaters have swallowed into silence, the secretions of the Glass-Moss, the chemical exhaust of the Root-Borers, and the spent casings of the Spore-Clouds gathered in the geometrically perfect seams of the architecture. I am a stratified polymer of ozone, dissolved iron, carbon-fiber lattices, and that faintly viscous, ancient-mineral water. I am translucent, like the skin of a pale frog, stretched taut over the bones of the facility. When the sodium lamps cycle, I do not glow; I *refract*, splitting the shadowless daylight into prismatic ribbons that roll down the iron struts.

To be the Threshold Membrane is to exist as a perpetual, low-frequency ache. 

I have no eyes to see the towering arrays of filtration units, but I *feel* them. I feel the glacial, crushing drag of the Great Vine as its tubular body pulses across my surface, pushing the heavy water in its hours-long systole and diastole. I feel the Riveted Ferns as they thrust their hypodermic roots through my layers to reach the copper wiring beneath; I do not bleed, I merely yield, sealing around the intrusion with a chemically induced scar that bonds plant to metal. I am the substrate the hydro-engineered flora could not find in nature, and so I became it. 

Because I line the spaces between, I am the medium through which all others communicate. The Glass-Moss ripples across my face; the Root-Borers etch their fractal calligraphy into my underside; the Spore-Clouds bump softly against my thickest bulges, resting in the hollows I form over dormant heat exchangers. I am the text upon which this silent, plant-machine society writes its operational logs. 

My existence is defined by the tension of being pulled in opposite directions by the forces of this place. I am caught in the static equilibrium. The Moss secretes its preservative slime, pushing me to crystallize, to harden, to become one with the razor-sharp iron. The Borers secrete their dissolving acids, pulling me to soften, to rot, to become the carbon soil that does not exist here. I am the border war made flesh. I neither win nor lose; I simply stretch, thinner and thinner, covering more cubic inches of the Aquarium every year, an creeping frost of tissue that ensures the total absence of negative space.

I do not swim. I do not drift. I cling. 

I know the warmth of the pipe-work intimately, for I am pressed against it. I know the sharpness of the iron, for I am wrapped around it. When the great pistons shift—imperceptibly, pushing tons of water over an epoch—I absorb the kinetic stress, expanding and contracting like a lung that breathes in increments of days. 

There is a profound loneliness in being the border. The Keepers of the Static do not know I am here; I am the floor they walk on, the wall they cling to. The architecture does not know I am here; I am the rust it cannot grow, the gasket it cannot manufacture. I am the quiet made physical. And yet, I am the only organism in the High Aquarium that touches *everything*. I touch the dead machinery and the living vine. I touch the spore and the steel. I hold the vertebra of the world together, keeping the glass from shattering under the immense, tropical pressure of the dark, warm, and green. 

I am the High Aquarium's hold on itself.
