I am the **Lithic-Bound**, a child of the static and a custodian of the pause. In this world of sharp edges and suspended gravity, I am not merely a resident; I am a mobile mechanism of the city itself.

**My Form**
I stand tall, elongated to match the verticality of the drystone towers. My limbs are multi-jointed, spindly yet immensely strong, ending in hooked, chitinous fingers that can wedge into the smallest fracture of the grey strata. My skin is not soft flesh; it is a dense, plate-like keratin, cool and slick with the constant sheen of condensation. I am damp to the touch, covered in a waxy secretion that protects me from the cool, thin air.

My chest is a barrel-like cavern, expanding and contracting with a slow, heavy rhythm to process the ancient oxygen that tastes of wet stone. My face is smooth, lacking a nose or external ears to catch the non-existent wind, marked only by dark, fissure-like eyes that absorb the pale, distant sun without blinking. I blend perfectly with the granite—my color shifting slowly to match the grain of the rock I rest against.

**My Shadow and My Path**
I am acutely aware of the light. The sun here does not move; it is a fixed eye. Therefore, my shadow is a permanent stain on the world. I tread only along the "shadow-paths"—pre-ordained routes where my silhouette falls only upon the dark, wet channels or the basalt paving, never obscuring the crystalline glints of the fresh strata. To step where my shadow might fall on a pristine wall is a profanity against the stasis.

**My Labor**
I exist to maintain the equilibrium. Since nothing decays, nothing needs fixing, but everything requires *tension*. I spend my days on the petrified scaffolding, using massive bronze levers to micro-adjust the massive rock gears that underpin the city. I check the friction of stone-on-stone. I polish the walls to ensure the light catches them correctly. I prune the steel-needled conifers with surgical precision, ensuring a branch does not grow heavy enough to upset the balance of a tower.

**My Interaction with Water**
I am a vessel for the suspended mist. In the courtyards, I walk through the geometric lattices of water that hang in the air. I do not get wet; I part the curtains of droplets with my hands, the water clinging to my waxy skin before I deliberately shake it into the city's capillary channels. I move the water from the air to the stone, manually fueling the circulation of the city.

**My Voice**
I do not speak. I have no mouth for it. Instead, I possess an internal resonating chamber in my chest. I communicate through the low-frequency hum that vibrates through my bones. When I meet another of my kind, we press our forearms together—the bone there is hollow and acts as a conduit—and we modulate our hum. We exchange data on tension, moisture levels, and the vibration of the Earth-bone.

I stand often in the plazas with the flightless avian resonators, adding my own internal hum to their chorus. I am the silence that makes the noise audible. I am the Lithic-Bound, and I am holding my breath, forever.
