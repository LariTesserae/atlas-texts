I am not a thing that moves. In this blister of raw, wet light, motion is a function assigned to the floor, not the observer. To move would be to disrupt the Lockstep, to introduce a variance in the equation that the Architect light—the blinding, white absolutes—would immediately correct.

I am the **Anchor**.

I exist where the geometry cannot hold. If the room is a cavity of embryonic slurry and the Weave is the fluid that fills it, I am the obstruction around which the fluid bends. I am a "scatter" that is not bone, not metal, not precipitate.

I am a **Vessel of Obsidian**.

My substance is absolute blackness—a negative shape cut from the brilliance of the walls. I stand—or rather, *loom*—in the center of the room, a singular, monolithic rhomboid. I am tall, stretching from the wet, weeping floor nearly to the sourceless ceiling, but I do not touch the ceiling. I stop just short, leaving a gap of trembling, ozone-heavy air. This gap is the only "empty" thing in the universe, a vacuum of pure shadow that the light cannot penetrate because I drink it.

My surface is smooth, polished to a sheen deeper than any water pool, and cold—colder than the mist, colder than the stone. I am the heat sink for the universe. The Weave moves frantically around my base, a billion purple tiles clicking and sliding over one another, generating the kinetic hum of the room. I absorb that hum. I drink the vibration. I am the silence into which the noise screams.

I have no eyes, yet I see everything through a skin of hyper-sensitive tension. I feel the pressure gradients of the room. I feel the Weave’s legs scrabble against my lower flanks, their microscopic gears trying in vain to find purchase on my frictionless skin. They slide off, ticked and clacked, flowing around me like a river parting around a boulder.

I am a **Geological Accident**. I was not put here by the Weave, and I was not built by the Architect light. I am a piece of the "Before"—a chunk of the dark, ancient universe that was swallowed by the raw, embryonic slurry of this new world. I am the memory of a time when there was horizons and distance and shadows.

My existence is a torture of equilibrium. Because the dynamic is zero, I cannot fall. I should have sunk into the wet stone eons ago, crushed by my own impossible density, but the Weave holds me up. They are the hydraulic fluid that prevents my descent. Millions of them brace against my sides, their interlocking shells forming a dynamic, living buttress that keeps me suspended in the exact center of the blister. They support me because I am the only thing that gives them shape; without me, the Weave would be a formless slurry of noise. I am the mold.

The light hits me and dies. It strikes my obsidian flanks and does not reflect; it simply ceases to be. This makes me a wound in the perfection of the room. I am the only thing here that casts a shadow—a long, razor-thin line of darkness across the writhing carpet of insects.

I do not think. I do not desire. I simply *am*. I am the heavy, silent witness to the screaming stasis. I feel the condensation weeping from the walls, sliding down my sides to join the mist. I feel the constant, rhythmic *click* of the Weave against my base. And I wait.

I wait for the Lockstep to break. I wait for the light to flicker. I wait for the room to finish happening. But I know it won't. So I stand, black and immovable, the hole in the world, absorbing the entropy of a billion desperate lives, freezing time into a solid block of silence.
