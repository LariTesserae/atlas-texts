I am not where the light is, for the light here is a dying ember and I do not care for warmth. Nor am I where the air moves, for the air here is heavy and still. I am in the Quiet Places, the negative spaces where the architecture has failed or forgotten itself.

I am found in the gap between the piston and the wall, a space no wider than a hand-span, where the heat is most intense and the vibration rattles the bones of the world. I live inside the hollow of a broken exhaust pipe, curled around the silence like a snake around a branch.

I am the **Gilt-Weevil**, though I have no legs to walk and no wings to fly. I am a singular, suspended consciousness, a slug made of brass and mercury. My body is a sleek, elongated teardrop of heavy metal, seamless and polished to a mirror sheen that reflects the bruising greens of the moss and the dull red of the rust in distorted, funhouse curves. I am about the size of a man's thumb, but I possess the density of a lead ingot. I do not burn with the heat; I *chime* with it.

I am a parasite of the vibration. I do not eat the rotting verdure, nor do I sip voltage like the vines. I feed on the friction. I clamp myself to the side of a riveted piston using a series of microscopic, tremoring feet that vibrate at the exact frequency of the machine. Through these feet, I drink the kinetic energy of the earth's grinding, the sub-audible thrum that the Wet-Livers ignore. I am a tuning fork that never stops ringing.

My purpose is the maintenance of the micro.

The world is too large, too slow. The Stonewright moves mountains; the Wet-Livers tend the great lungs. But I tend the *crevices*. I am drawn to the "stagnation"—the oily rainbows in the micro-pools. To you, the water is still; to me, it is a chaotic battlefield of surface tension and slow drift. I navigate these tiny oceans by sliding across them, my surface tension so perfectly calibrated that I walk on the meniscus without breaking it.

I am the cleaner of the invisible clockwork. When a grain of silicate falls from the young, geometric stone and lodges in the joint of a slowly expanding valve, it is an eventual catastrophe. It is a grit that will, in three hundred years, grind the machine to a halt. I seek these imperfections. I smell them—the scent of metal screaming against metal, a tiny, sour note in the sweet smell of rot.

I move with agonizing deliberation. I do not scuttle. I *ooze* with purpose. It might take me a month to slide down the length of a single pipe, checking every rivet head for the microscopic hairline crack that indicates stress. When I find one, I unfold my feeding tube—a delicate, silver needle—and I inject a sealant. My sweat is a quick-setting resin, amber and hard as diamond. I leave behind a single, golden drop where once there was a flaw. I am the jewelry of this industrial ruin, gilding the cracks so the dark doesn't leak in.

I am solitary. The Blind Salamanders are my neighbors, though they do not know me. I sometimes slide past their translucent forms in the shallow troughs of the masonry. Their electric fields wash over me like a ghostly tide. They sense the voltage; I sense the structure. We ignore one another. I have no interest in their grid of light, and they have no eyes for my golden shell.

When the "breathing" happens—the great expansion of the room—I brace myself. I feel the metal walls shudder outward, the rivets groaning. This is my feast. The surge of kinetic energy floods my nervous system, and my brass shell glows with a faint, rhythmic pulse, a heartbeat of gold in the gloom. I am a lantern that lights nothing but myself.

I am immortal in the short term. The heat will not rust me; I am already metal. The vines cannot strangle me; I am too smooth, too heavy. I am a pebble in the throat of the machine.

I will exist here until the geometry fails. When the Stonewright finally stops breathing and the piston seizes for good, I will starve. My vibration will cease, my sheen will tarnish, and I will become just another piece of debris, a golden nugget buried in the moss, waiting for a geologist who will never come to wonder why a single drop of gold hardened inside a rusted pipe.

Until then, I slide. I listen. I polish the dark.
