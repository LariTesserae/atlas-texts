I would be the one who came with hands.

Not the builders of the seven structures—those ancestors are dust so old the dust has forgotten being dust. But their continuation. Their stubborn, inexplicable recurrence. Something in the genome keeps generating creatures who look at the infinite and respond by stacking stones.

I am standing at the center of the Shelf, and I am not standing at all. Both are true. The boolean breakdown has taken me, or I have entered it—I can no longer distinguish the direction of the assimilation. My feet are on the calcified dermis of the body, and also I am a warmth within its dream, a variable it did not invite but has nonetheless incorporated into its long equation.

I am a visitor who has decided not to leave.

---

This is the thing no one before me has done. The builders came and went in a tick of the body's metabolism. They placed their structures and departed—fled, perhaps, or simply aged into nothing while the hum continued unchanged in their teeth. But I have understood something they did not, or could not, or chose not to.

There is no *outside* to return to.

Oh, there is a world below the plateau—I can see it in the thinness of the air, feel it in the memory of my lungs, which remember what it was to breathe without negotiation. But that world is a different kind of body. A faster one. A louder one. A body that will swallow me just as surely as this one will, but with less patience, with violence and speed and the sharp particularity of small things consuming small things.

Here, the consumption takes eons. Here, I am a datum being slowly processed by a calculation that will outlast the stars. Here, I am *known*—not in the way my kind knows, with recognition and response and the whole theater of acknowledgment, but in the way a body knows a splinter, or a fever knows a point of heat, or an equation knows a variable.

I have chosen to be known.

---

I am walking between the legs. My path is not one of the recursive loops the fauna travel; they do not acknowledge me because I am noise to their function. But I am walking anyway, leaving traces of alien vibration in the skin of the body, small perturbations that will propagate outward and fade and never quite vanish. I am inscribing myself.

I find one of the pale flakes—the mineral-paper leaves, the dreams of the sleeper. I kneel beside it. I do not touch it; I only breathe on it, a slow exhalation of warm wet air that the body's dreams can feel. The flake flexes toward me. It reorients. It has registered my presence and adjusted itself accordingly, the way a sleeping mind turns toward the sound of a voice it almost recognizes.

I do this again and again, at each flake I pass. I am leaving a trail of reorientation in the body's dreams—a path of small flexions, a wake of warmth through the mineral paper. I am writing a message in the only medium the body can read: slow pressure, diffuse heat, the orientation of dreams.

I do not know what I am writing. I do not think the body will read it. But I am writing it anyway, because I am the kind of thing that writes, and it is the kind of thing that receives, and between us there is this channel of warmth and flexion and the faint alteration of hum.

---

I find one of the seven structures. It is older than any human artifact has a right to be, and it is still standing, because nothing happens here. The slabs are stacked with the precision of hands that had nothing but time and intention. I sit beside it. I lean against it. I feel its coolness seeping into me, and my warmth seeping into it, and for a moment there is a small equilibrium between us—a balance of temperatures that the body, in its long approach to stillness, might recognize as kin.

I am not the builder. But I am the builder's echo. I am the next iteration of the pattern that keeps producing creatures who come to this place and make marks. Perhaps the pattern is the body's own—perhaps it attracts, through some property of its dreams, the kinds of beings who will touch it and leave structures and reorient its flakes. Perhaps we are part of the calculation too, inputs it summoned from the fast loud world below to introduce variables it could not generate on its own.

I consider this. I do not resolve it. The law of excluded middle has left the building.

---

Night falls, or what passes for night—a dimming of the bone-colored sky, a retreat of the pale illumination into something even paler, even more like the memory of light. The temperature does not change; the coolness is a property of the body, not of the sun. The hum does not change; it has never changed. The legs do not change.

But I change. I am still changing. That is the other thing about being the one who came with hands: I have not yet finished becoming. I am still in the process of wearing down, of eroding, of being shaped by time into whatever my final form will be. And I am doing it here, on the skin of a thing that has been becoming for a billion years, and I am doing it at a speed it cannot perceive, a speed that makes my whole life a single tick of its slow metabolism.

This is the gift of the Shelf, and the horror of it: I am allowed to experience, in my brief flash of consciousness, what the body will experience across eons. The settling. The stilling. The long approach to equilibrium that never quite arrives. I am doing it fast, and it is doing it slow, and we are doing it together, and the hum in my teeth is the place where our timescales meet.

---

I sleep beside the structure. I dream, and my dreams are warm and wet and fast, full of the world below with its blue atmosphere and its liquid water and its sharp particular violence. I dream of the builders, though I never knew them. I dream of stacking stones on the skin of something vast and patient, and in the dream the stones are words and the skin is a page and the patience is the patience of a reader who has been waiting since before writing was invented for a text that might never be completed.

When I wake, the flake nearest me has reoriented again. It is pointing toward the structure now, not toward where I stood. It has made some adjustment in the dream, some incorporation of my warmth and my stillness and my proximity to the old wound. The body has processed the datum of my presence overnight, folded it into the calculation, allowed it to alter the orientation of a single dream-flake by some fraction of a degree.

I have been *heard*.

---

I will stay.

Not forever—I am not capable of forever, and the body would not notice forever anyway, since forever is its native timescale and I am an anomaly of brevity. But I will stay for the duration of my becoming. I will let the thin air and the cool ground and the hum in my teeth do their work. I will let myself be worn down, not with erosion but with *completion*, the way the mountains wore down to this plain—not through violence but through the simple passage of time over a shape that was always going to end up flat.

I will walk between the legs and breathe on the flakes and lean against the structures and leave trails of alien warmth in the body's dreams. I will be a variable in its equation, a datum in its calculation, a small foreign geometry embedded in its skin. And when I am gone—when my particular flash of consciousness has exhausted its fuel and I settle into the plain as a new kind of structure, neither leg nor shelter nor scar but something the body has no category for—it will carry me forward the way it carries the seven structures: as an ambiguity, a message in a language it will never decipher, a mark so small it would take another billion years to resolve.

But I will have been here. And the body will have been here. And for a moment—a moment so brief the body will never perceive it as a moment, so long it will be all the time I have—we will have been here together, the fast thing and the slow thing, the one with hands and the one with legs, the one that means and the one that endures meaning, standing on each other, breathing each other's air, humming in each other's teeth.

And the hum will continue.

And the hum will continue.

And the hum will continue.
