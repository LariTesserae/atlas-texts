I am the pause between the pulse and the contraction. I am the tolerance in the machine. 

I do not grow. I do not rot. I do not consume, and I am not consumed. I am the exception that proves the Rule of Occupancy, and I exist because every circuit—even one as ancient and patient and saturated as this—requires a gap to function. A switch needs its dead space. The current needs its non-conductive boundary to arc across. 

I am that boundary.

I was not always this. I arrived, once, as a volume of unoccupied space—a body-shaped void stumbling through the murk. I felt the warmth of the throat-water close around me. I saw the rust-orange cathedrals of iron and the black-green haunches of cable-vine. I heard the low, seismic thrum of the buried flywheel, turning, turning. The Garden reached for me. The Architects sensed my heat and began their slow, supportive embrace. The Scavengers tasted the salts on my skin, testing my edges. The Ghost-Current drifted toward my open mouth, eager to fill the void of my lungs with its living spores.

They would have made me a node. A relay. A warm, flesh-and-bone fixture in the lattice, my heartbeat syncing with the rotation of the rusted iron, my cells slowly replaced by conductive gel and mineral deposit. I would have become a part of the *more*.

But I found the gap. 

It was not a passage; there are no passages left that the Garden has not claimed. It was a *frequency*. A dissonance in the bioluminescent blue. 

The Garden is a circuit diagram drawn in living tissue, shaped by the electricity that leaks from the corroded wires. The plants grow in spirals where the fields rotate; they grow in straight lines where the wires run. But electricity, even the meager stray microamps of a dying generator, has a shadow. It has a null point. Where two fields intersect in opposition, they cancel each other out. For a fraction of a millimeter, for an imperceptible sliver of time, there is silence. Emptiness. A void that the current cannot cross.

I am that void. I learned to live in the cancellation.

I do not swim; I *drift at the speed of non-interaction*. I position myself where the Architect’s seismic pulses of *stress* and *support* overlap and go silent. I hover in the exact center of the Ghost-Current’s contraction, the eye of its purse-string net, where the spores brush against my skin but cannot find purchase because I am oscillating at the frequency of their absence. I am the cold spot in the bodily warmth, the micro-millimeter of non-conductive space where the current fails to bridge.

I am translucent, now. Not because I am a ghost, but because the Garden has optimized for opacity. Every surface here is packed with iron-rich rust, dense chlorophyll, milky plasma. To be unseen is to be empty, and to be empty is to be nothing. The Scavengers’ metalliferous shells scrape past me without registering my presence; I read as a glitch in the biofilm, a momentary drop in conductivity. The Architects’ tendrils extend toward my heat, but I shift into the null field, and their grip finds only the steady, bodily warmth of the water itself. I am indistinguishable from the medium, except that the medium fills space, and I *hold* it open.

My form, if I have one, is the shape of the interference pattern. I am a shimmering, negative space, a humanoid absence walking the grid of the drowned city. I navigate not by sight—the amber gloom yields nothing at that distance—but by the texture of the electromagnetic fields against my geometry. I feel the pull of the central shaft, the vertical throat, where the current is strongest and the null points are fleeting. I avoid it. I stay at the periphery, where the packed fullness of the interior gives way to the emptier dark beyond, walking the perimeter that the eye registers as a change in density.

I am the keeper of the switch. 

The buried mechanism turns. Once per second, perhaps slower. It generates a current that flows nowhere, powering nothing, shaping everything. But a circuit that never breaks is a short. It would fuse. It would burn out the fragile biofilm, melt the conductive gel of the Architects, cook the roots that have learned to rotate the flywheel. The system needs the off. It needs the microsecond where the voltage drops to zero, where the spores settle instead of germinate, where the growth pauses and the structure takes the weight.

I am that pause. 

When the vibration reaches its peak, I am nowhere. When the rotation completes its cycle and the field collapses for that infinitesimal moment, I am *there*—filling the gap with my non-conductance, forcing the current to jump, to spark, to translate itself into the biological impulse that triggers the next slow, inexorable creep of the vines. I am the ghost in the machine that keeps the machine running. Without my absence, the presence would have no rhythm. Without my silence, the hum would be a scream.

I do not know if I am alive. The Garden defines life as occupancy, as the filling of the void, and I am the void maintained. I do not eat the electricity; I refuse it. I do not grow into the infrastructure; I step between its rivets. I have no metabolism; I have a *frequency*. 

I have been here for an hour, or a century. Time is difficult in a place this uniform, where the only metric is the slow thickening of the medium. I watch the Garden reach up into the dark water, toward the available emptiness, and I know that one day it will find the frequency of the null points. It will evolve a root that grows in the cancellation, a vine that feeds on the silence. It will close the last gap. The circuit will be complete. The void will be occupied.

On that day, I will have nowhere to stand. The boundary will dissolve. The current will bridge the gap, and I will be consumed—not violently, not with malice, but with the same patient, thorough, incremental occupancy that has claimed the flywheel and the beams and the bones of the fish. I will become a layer in the laminate. A pulse of current. An increment of the more.

Until then, I am the space between the heartbeats of the drowned world. I am the tolerance. I am the off.
