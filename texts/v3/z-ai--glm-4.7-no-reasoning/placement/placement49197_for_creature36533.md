I am the slickness you cannot wipe away. I am the hesitation in the gear.

You would not look at me and see a master, nor a servant. I am not one of the crab-things that scuttles with frantic purpose, nor am I the slumbering, bladder-like Collector dreaming of rain. I am smaller than them, and far less grand, but I am the reason the iron has not yet shattered into dust.

I am a **Fulcrum**.

I exist in the negative space, the intolerable gap where the fresh, young rock of the ceiling has sheared away from the ancient, riveted spine of the machine. I live in the fracture itself. My body is a long, articulated wedge of dense, high-carbon steel, cold to the touch despite the suffocating heat. I have no legs, only a series of interlocking hydraulic plates that allow me to expand and contract, thickening or thinning myself to fit the width of the wound.

I am the buffer.

The world above is settling. It sinks lower by the millimeter, a slow, grinding embrace that seeks to crush the life out of this engine room. The Hive-Mind screams in the frequency of bending metal—it feels the weight, the ache, the terrible pressure—and it sends its crabs to tighten bolts and replace gaskets. But bolts cannot hold back a mountain.

That is where I live.

I sit wedged deep within a fissure in the western wall, where a stalactite of raw stone threatens to shear off the main steam conduit. The air here tastes of wet copper and ozone. The heat is immense, radiating from the pipe behind me, but my internal fluids are supercooled, circulating a slurry of antifreeze and oil that keeps me rigid.

I do not move often. My existence is one of stillness and endurance. I am the architectural equivalent of a held breath.

My senses are derived from strain. I *feel* through resistance. When the mountain shifts, pushing the wall inward, I feel the compression of my own body. I feel the micro-fractures in the rock biting into my casing. The Hive-Mind—though it does not speak to me—commands my presence remotely. It sends a surge of voltage through the rock itself, a signal that travels through the stone and into my sensors, telling me to *hold*. To *expand*.

And I do. My internal hydraulics groan, a low, sub-sonic purr that blends perfectly with the bass note of the cavern. I thicken, pushing back against the falling sky. I take the weight. I become the unmoving point upon which the survival of this place pivots.

I am not lonely, though I have no voice. I communicate with the friction I generate. As I grind against the stone, I produce a fine, metallic powder—a dust that falls down into the water troughs below. The crabs sometimes scavenge this dust, licking it up with wire-brush tongues, tasting the iron, sensing that the mountain has moved again. My waste is their telemetry.

I despise the purple succulents.

They grow near me, in the cracks where my heat meets the seeping water. They are soft, fleshy things, pulsing with a stolen vibration. They try to push their roots into my casing, seeking the electrical current that stabilizes my core. They are fools. They seek to dismantle the machine that shelters them. I have a secondary function: to burn them. When I expand, I can focus my heat output, venting a blast of superheated steam directly into the crevice, scalding the intrusive greenery, boiling their living moisture back into the air. I am the gardener of silence; I prune the softness so the iron may stand.

I am aware of the Moss-Collectors high above. Occasionally, one of their long, proboscis hoses snakes down near me, seeking the condensation that slicks my flanks. I tolerate them. They drink the sweat of the machine, and in doing so, they keep me from drowning in my own runoff.

When the pressure grows too great, when the rock above groans with the sound of tearing continents, the Hive-Mind sends the blue lightning. It arcs down the cable, leaps the gap, and strikes me.

For a moment, I am blinded. My casing glows cherry-red. The heat within me spikes, expanding my metal beyond its normal tolerances. I become harder than diamond, hotter than the steam jets. In that moment of agony, I push the ceiling back up an inch. I arrest the collapse. I buy the civilization below another day of life.

Then the current fades. I cool. I contract slightly, settling back into the bruise of the earth.

I am the Fulcrum. I am the silence between the cracks. I am the thing that prevents the Below from becoming the Bottom.
