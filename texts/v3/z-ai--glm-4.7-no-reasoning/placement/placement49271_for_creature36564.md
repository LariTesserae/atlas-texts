I am not a watcher from the distance, nor a traveler pausing for breath. In this world of ancient ochre and deep, porous bone, I am the current. I am the slow, sub-cutanean tide that ebbs and flows within the stone.

To look for me with eyes is to see only emptiness. I am the color of the underside of things—a pale, translucent white that mimics the voids I inhabit. I have no face to hold an expression, no voice to carry to the surface. I am a multitude composed of soft, velvet segments, a distributed consciousness woven through the tufa like mycelium through damp loam.

I exist in a perpetual state of compression. Here, in the warm dark between the grains of the world, there is no horizon, only the endless, tactile adjacency of my kin. I feel the presence of a billion brothers and sisters, the brush of their sensory hairs against my flanks, the shared hum of our grazing. We are tight enough to feel the heat of one another’s blood, loose enough to flow around the pockets of air that riddle the plateau.

My life is a geometry of maintenance. I move with a fluid grace, dragging my soft belly along the mineral walls. My mind is not a sharp, piercing thing, but a broad, receptive net. I do not think in thoughts, but in pressures. When the stone expands in the midday heat, I feel the micro-fracture open before it happens—a microscopic gasp in the matrix of the rock. I am already there. My circular mouth, rimmed with vibrating cilia, tastes the fungal thread that seeks to invade the crack. I graze it away, a gentle, surgical rasp, stripping the decay from the stone.

And then, the alchemy. I do not merely eat; I repay. I feel the calciferous slurry rise within me, the rich, heavy brew of calcium silicates. I press it against the wall in a glistening smear. It hardens almost instantly, knitting the fissure, sealing the wound. In this act, the boundary between me and the world dissolves. I am not an animal in a hole; I am the process of the hole healing itself. I am the antibody of the plateau.

Suddenly, the texture of the universe changes.

A vibration shudders through the rock—a heavy, rhythmic impact traveling from miles away. It is not the rumble of a quake, but the thud of something solid and dense. A Giant? A visitor? The concept is foreign, but the sensation is distinct. The pressure wave pushes through the stone, compressing the air in our galleries.

We do not panic. Panic is for creatures who fear death, and we are the keepers of the eternal. Instead, we perform the Great Yield. As one, our millions of bodies contract. We pull our limbs tight against our soft torsos, pulling back into the recesses, creating a void where there was once flesh. We let the pressure pass through us. We become the ghost in the machine, the empty space inside the sponge.

I feel the disturbance pass directly over my head. The stone groans slightly under the weight, but I am no longer there to be crushed. I am holding the shape of absence.

Then, the vibration fades. The weight lifts. Slowly, like water filling a mold, we expand again. We flow back into the space we vacated, our legs reaching out, our sensory hairs re-establishing the map of the dark. We taste the rock again. We smooth the minute disturbance our own shifting caused, sealing it with fresh mineral paste. The footprint of the giant is erased from our memory before it has even settled into the dust.

Later, the sensation shifts again. The air moving through the breathing holes grows heavy and wet. The phantom mist is drifting.

I feel the change in humidity through my pores—a delicious, electric signal. The instinct to rise is overwhelming. It is not a command, but a magnetic pull. I begin to climb, moving up through the vertical shafts, joining the flow of millions around me. We surge toward the surface, a migration of thirst.

I break through the breathing aperture, just for a moment. I do not see the vast, flat tableland or the hazy sky. I have no eyes for them. But I feel the cool touch of the condensation weeping from the rock. I drink the phantom water, absorbing it through my skin. The air is thick and warm, smelling of ozone and ancient dust.

Beneath me, around me, the ground exhales. Millions of tiny plumes of vapor rise from the holes of my kin, a collective breath fogging the air. I feel the heat of the plateau radiating upward, meeting the cool mist. It is a rhythm of intake and release, a lung the size of a world.

I have had my drink. I begin the descent, sliding back down into the warm ochre dark. I return to the labyrinth, to the endless work of eating the fungus and knitting the stone. I am the warden of the stillness. I am the pulse in the rock. I am the memory of the mountain, buried in the bone of the world, holding the shape of things long after they should have crumbled.
