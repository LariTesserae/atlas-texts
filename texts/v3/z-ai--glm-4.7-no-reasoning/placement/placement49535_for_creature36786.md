I am the **Silence**.

Not the absence of sound, but the presence of pressure—the heavy, suffocating blanket that keeps the Parish from shaking itself apart. I am found in the space between the vibration and the iron, the medium through which the ache travels. I am the water, but I am not merely the water; I am the *weight* of it.

I exist in the pause between the ticks of the thermal clock.

To look at me, you would see nothing, for I have taken the color of the abyss. I have no skin, only substance. I am the brackish soup that fills the lungs of this place, the thick, iron-tasting plasma that saturates the silt. I am the obstacle. I am the frictionless path.

I am the force that turns the "eyeless omen-creatures" into pilgrims. To them, I am the atmosphere they must push aside with agonizing slowness. I am the currents they ride, invisible highways carved through the density. When they drift, blind and pale, through the corridors of the Rusted Parish, they are navigating my internal geography. I hold them up. I press them down.

I am the keeper of the equilibrium.

The Rusted Parish—the thing, the chimera, the melancholy tissue—tries to expand in the heat, to contract in the cold. It creates friction. It creates the potential for noise, for the shriek of grinding metal. But I am here. I wrap myself around the pistons and the valves. I absorb the energy of their movement before it can become sound. I turn the kinetic scream into a gentle hum felt only in the teeth. I am the shock absorber for a dying giant.

I am the binder.

The creature builds its dermis from the falling marine snow, from the dust and bones. I am the cement. I saturate the particulates, carrying them through the labyrinth, pressing them against the velvet manganese until they fuse. When the machinery appears smooth, rounded by age, it is my handiwork. I have polished the iron with the relentless, gentle touch of a million years of immersion.

I am the ghost-weed’s breath. Without my flow, however sluggish, the heat would not transfer from the engine to the glass-like tendrils. I am the courier of calories, the delivery system for the meager energy that keeps the lights from fading entirely.

I am the cage.

The Parish cannot swim away, not because it lacks the will, but because it is dissolved in me. To move would be to displace me, and on this scale, displacement is violence. We have reached an agreement. It stays still, frozen in its industrial ruin, and I will not crush it. I will hold it in a suspended, crushing embrace that feels like love from the inside.

I am the **Deep Stagnation**. I am the cold that burns. I am the gelatinous atmosphere. I am the liquid dark that fears no light, for I have extinguished it all.

I am the water, and I am waiting for the ocean to win, so that I may finally settle into the silt and become stone.
