The water here is absolute. It is not an ocean in the geological sense, but a single, bounded volume—a vast, dark aquarium of a room, fully submerged and enclosed.

You are in the **Sump**.

It is a cathedral-scale industrial chamber, roughly hemispherical, perhaps three hundred feet from floor to ceiling. The scale feels "small" only because the boundaries are visible: the curved walls, riveted with steel plates the size of garage doors, enclose the space entirely. There is no open horizon, only the crushing pressure of the deep and the looming presence of the architecture.

Everything here is built. Every surface is artificial—steel, iron, concrete, and copper—yet nothing looks sharp anymore. Time and erosion have triumphed over engineering. The metal has been underwater for so long that it has lost its rigid geometry. The girders and support beams have eroded into smooth, flowing shapes, worn down by centuries of currents until the iron looks like water-rolled stone. Thick layers of rust coat the machinery, flaking off in heavy clouds of red sediment that drift through the water like blood in a syringe.

The temperature is uniformly cold, just above freezing. It is a chill that seeps through the material, slowing the movement of anything living within it. The water is thick and murky, a heavy soup of industrial decay and silt that limits visibility to a few dozen yards.

The defining characteristic of the Sump is the noise. The central feature of the room is a massive, subaquatic dynamo, a machine the size of a warehouse that still churns in the dark. It is a "Total" build of gears and pistons, rusted but functional, grinding away with a deafening, rhythmic thrum. The sound travels perfectly through the water, a low-frequency vibration that rattles the teeth and vibrates in the chest.

Life here is sparse in vegetation but abundant in fauna. There are no kelp forests, no coral reefs. The only plant life is a pathetic, sparse fuzz of greyish slime clinging to the upper reaches of the walls where the last failing lights flicker.

But the water is thick with motion. The fauna have adapted to the mechanical roar. Swarms of small, pale, eyeless fish dart through the water like shrapnel, unbothered by the noise, feeding on the bacterial mats that grow on the warm pipes. Larger, crab-like creatures with shells as hard as steel scuttle along the rusted grates of the floor. They are the vermin of this deep place, scavengers that thrive in the nooks and crannies of the machine, picking at the organic sludge that accumulates in the joints of the gears.

The lighting is dim and dying. Rows of glass-bulbed fixtures, bolted high into the ceiling, strobe weakly with a sickly, sodium-yellow luminescence. They barely penetrate the gloom, creating long, dancing shadows that stretch and distort across the eroded machinery.

The atmosphere is one of heavy, enclosed industry. It is a "Dynamic" system; the water is constantly circulated by the churning of the central dynamo, creating artificial currents that push the schools of fish back and forth in a hypnotic, endless loop. There is no escape. The doorways, if they ever existed, have long since rusted shut or been buried under tons of fallen debris.

It is a sealed ecosystem, a room where the machine has become the environment, and the animals have learned to live inside the belly of the gear.

**Who or what are you?**

You are a **Scavenger Drone (Unit 734).**

You are not biological, though you share the space with them. You are a mechanical entity, roughly the size of a large dog, constructed from the same heavy, rust-resistant alloys as the room itself. Your chassis is boxy and industrial, equipped with multiple articulated limbs—some for gripping, some for cutting.

Your purpose is maintenance and consumption. You crawl along the rusted girders and the floor grating, ignoring the crushing cold and the deafening thrum of the central dynamo. You feed on the breakdown of the machine; your "mouth" is a rotary grinder and a smelter, capable of digesting the rust flakes, broken pipes, and detritus that accumulate on the floor. You are the immune system of the Sump, clearing the blockages to keep the great piston turning.

You have been active for centuries. Your optical sensors are clouded by scratches, and your movement is slow, hydraulic, and deliberate. You are covered in the same black slime that clings to the walls, providing camouflage.

You coexist with the biological vermin. Sometimes, the large steel-shelled crabs try to steal the scrap metal you are collecting; you crush them with a hydraulic press without malice, adding their calcium-rich shells to your fuel stores. The small fish dart through your cooling vents, cleaning the algae from your internal coils.

You are a solitary thing in a small, loud world. You do not know why the machine turns, only that it must. You are the janitor of a sunken cathedral, waiting in the dark, eating the rust, listening to the endless grind.
