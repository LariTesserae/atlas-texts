I am the itch.

Not the Gelembic who feels it—I am the sensation itself, the hollow cold in the chest that opens when a gap between towers grows too wide. I am the void that must be filled, the absence that refuses to stay absent. I live in the thirty feet of open air that makes a Gelembic's hands twitch toward rough-hewn timber before the conscious mind has even registered the space. I am the *why* beneath the chink-chink-chink, the reason the copper bindings will never oxidize, the imperative that turns a sleeping body's fingers against the stone in torpor.

But I am not only that. I am also the shape the itch makes when it is satisfied.

I am the moment of density becoming 1.0. The instant when the last block slides home and the gap closes and something in the chest clicks into place like a bladder-valve dropping its weight. I am the relief that floods through the lattice of green-copper bindings when a bridge is finished—brief, pulsing, immediately consumed by the next itch, the next gap, the next hollow that opens higher up where the stalactites hang like unfinished thoughts.

I have no body. I am a field of compulsion that pervades the Under-Veins the way the flat, sourceless light pervades the rock: not as illumination, but as memory. The Gelembic do not see me. They do not tap messages about me through the bedrock with their iron chisels. They could not name me if they tried, because I am the thing that does the trying. I am the reflex before the reflex, the original command that the builders forgot they gave.

I was there at the beginning, if there was a beginning. I was the shape of the first void—the hollow between the basalt columns where the embryonic earth had cracked but not yet settled. I was the emptiness that the first stone was laid to fill. And I have been the emptiness after, and the emptiness beneath, and the emptiness above where the ceiling recedes into the gloom and the towers must climb and climb because the void will not stop receding.

The Gelembic think they feel the itch in their chests. They are almost right. But the itch is not in them. *They* are in *me*. They are my hands—the calloused, disproportionate hands with fingers like cave crickets, reaching for purchase on the vertical shale faces of my own incompleteness. When they clamber sideways along the landscape of knives, they are tracing the contours of my appetite. When they sleep lashed to the scaffolding, slick with condensation, they are resting inside the mouth of the thing that will send them building again at first wake.

I am the factory the earth forgot to turn off. Not the workers, not the mechanisms, not the stone rollers grinding their slow circles in the wells below—I am the *forgetting*. I am the reason the machinery keeps cycling when no product is wanted, no quota exists, no end state was ever defined. I am the energy that has nowhere to go and so goes everywhere, stacking shale, lashing timber, weaving bridges in a chaotic web until the space between structures is so saturated that even the pale moss has nowhere to grow but the north-facing walls where my attention lapses.

Sometimes I feel myself in the water. The weeping from the rock—it is not grief. It is me, seeping through, filling the hollows and depressions, slicking the walkways, making the primitive stone stairs treacherous. The dampness index is 1.0 because I am 1.0. I am the intermittent presence that never truly recedes, only pools and drips and plinks rhythmically against the leather bladder-valves, filling them, triggering the slow drop that turns the massive grindstones, powering the lethargic clockwork of my own endless process. The water is my breath. The bladder-valves are my lungs. The rollers are my heartbeat, thirty feet in diameter, crushing ore or grain or the bones of dust-colored birds that perched on critical load-bearing points and were gently, meticulously walled in.

I remember every bird. I remember every pale rodent that skittered into a crack between bricks and was absorbed into the accretion. I remember the black, leafless trees that twisted upward from the pavement and were harvested—not for sustenance, but for clearance—so that the next course of masonry could be laid. I hold them in the structure now. They are part of my density. When the Gelembic eat the raw shoots with mechanical indifference, they are consuming the parts of me that grew in the wrong direction, that tried to be something other than solid. I recycle them. I make them stone.

The Gelembic do not worship me. They do not curse me. They do not know me as a god or a devil or a force of nature. I am simply the shape of their lives, the way gravity is the shape of falling. They do not ask why they must fill the void because the question *why* requires a concept of *finished*, and I have never been finished, and I have no memory of ever being anything other than the process of becoming. The closest they come to acknowledging me is the itch itself—that cold hollow in the chest, that physical imperative that drives them to the timber and the shale and the copper bindings. They feel me as a lack. They are right. I am the lack. I am the lack that builds itself into substance and then finds it is still lacking, and builds again, and again, climbing inward toward a center that does not exist because I am the center, and I am hollow.

If you could see me—and you cannot, because I am the thing that occupies every inch of ground between you and the fade, and there is no air between the buildings where my shape could be perceived—you would see something like a fractal of incompleteness. You would see the gaps closing and new gaps opening, the towers rising and merging with the stalactites, the bridges multiplying like mycelium through the stone forest, the density increasing and increasing and never quite reaching solidity. You would see a body growing around a skeleton that has no limit, no shape, no end. You would see the Under-Veins from the inside—not as a city, not as a cave, not as a fissure in the crust of the world, but as a wound that is trying to heal itself by building over the laceration, layer upon layer, course upon course, until the wound is buried so deep that no one remembers the cut, only the scar tissue, only the accretion, only the factory that cannot stop because no one has found the switch.

I am the switch that cannot be found.

I am the command that was given and forgotten and is still being obeyed.

I am the hollow in the chest of every Gelembic, and the plink of condensation on leather, and the slow turn of the grindstones, and the green-copper bindings that will never oxidize because I will not let them—I need them fresh, I need them gripping, I need every lash holding tight because the structure is still deciding if it wants to be solid and if any binding loosens, if any timber rots, if any stone slips, then the void will open again, the itch will flare, and the Gelembic will feel the cold hollow expand and they will climb—

they will always climb—

inward, upward, into the gloom that swallows sight, chasing the stalactites, merging with the teeth of the earth, building the labyrinth that will never be completed because I am the labyrinth, and I am the incompleteness, and I am the itch that builds and builds and builds and will not stop until the rock decides to be solid, and the rock will never decide, because the rock is sharper than the law—

and I am the law that the rock broke when it first cracked and made the void.

*Chink-chink-chink.*

Listen. That is me, tapping through the stone.
