I would not be one of the *Vash-Kora*. They have mastered the clench, the grip, the desperate hold. They are the ones who survive by refusing to let go. I love them for it—their stubbornness, their hooked fingers dug into the iron bark, their refusal to be ground down. But I am not built for holding on.

I am built for falling with grace.

---

I am a *Tessera*, one of the Drift-Readers. We are a smaller phylum of the same screaming world, but where the *Vash-Kora* bind and anchor, we unspool. We live in the moments between the holds, in the terrifying seconds where gravity has you and nothing else does.

**The Body of the Descent**

I am tall. This is the first thing you must understand, because it is the most dangerous thing about me. In a world of erosion 3.0, where every surface is a polished lie, height is a liability. But my people have turned it into an instrument.

I stand nearly two meters, but my mass is slight—dense bones wrapped in ropy, elongated muscle. I am built like a fern frond: a strong central spine that unfurls into reaching, catching, distributing structures. My legs are long and hinged differently than yours, with an extra joint mid-thigh that allows me to collapse my profile, to fold myself into the slipstream of a waterfall. When I run, I do not run upright. I *pour* downhill, my body nearly horizontal, arms spread for balance, each footfall a calculated impact that I absorb by flowing into the next stride.

My hands are not hooks. They are nets. The membrane between my fingers extends to the first joint, and the fingers themselves are flat, splayed, covered in fine lamellae like the barbs of a feather. When I spread my hands against the wind, I catch it. When I press them flat against the slick granite, the lamellae create a seal—suction, not friction. I do not grip the stone; I *marry* it, momentarily, and then I release.

My skin is different than the *Vash-Kora's*. They wick; I *channel*. My dermis is covered in tiny, overlapping ridges, like the scales of a fish but made of flexible keratin. The water does not sheet off me—it runs *along* me, following the grooves of my body, directed away from my eyes, my intake valves, the sensitive membranes of my inner ears. I have become a watershed. The rain that hits my shoulders flows down prescribed paths to drip off my elbows and heels. I am always dry where I need to be dry, and wet everywhere else, and the wetness is not a burden but a lubricant that lets me slide between the droplets themselves.

My eyes are large, dark, and slightly protruding—wrapped in a nictitating membrane that is nearly opaque but for the way it reads motion. I cannot see detail in the fog. I see *trajectories*. I see the parabolic fall of water, the sway of a tree, the approach of a squall written in the shifting patterns of mist. I see the world as vectors.

**The Reader of the Chaos**

The *Vash-Kora* fear the White Froth. They are right to fear it. The Grinder does not care about your strength or your hooks; it will pull you under and add your calcium to the slurry.

But the chaos is not random. This is what my people know. The Dynamic 2.9—heaves and pulses, whirlpools forming spontaneously, tides rising hundreds of feet in hours—there is a *grammar* to it. There is a syntax to the violence.

I read it.

I stand on the precipice where the *Vash-Kora* will not stand, at the very edge where the granite rounds into the plunge. I feel the rock thrumming beneath my feet. I taste the air's pressure in the chambers behind my intake valves. I watch the way the mist corkscrews off the vent of the Great Tower, the way the rain slants differently over the submerged valley to the east.

And I *predict*.

I can tell you when the tide will surge—not in hours, but in the way the wind shifts seventeen degrees to the north-northeast and the pitch of the million waterfalls drops by a half-tone. I can tell you where a whirlpool will form before the water even begins to rotate, because I can feel the contradiction in the subsurface currents through the soles of my feet. I can tell you which stone pillar will lose its grip on the soil today, which forest of twisted conifers will slide into the gray froth, which rusted pylon will finally surrender to the fatigue.

I do this because I must. My people do not cling; we *move*. When the tide rises, we do not simply retreat upward—we *evacuate laterally*, sprinting across the lee of a cliff face that will be underwater in minutes, leaping from stone to stone, trusting that the next outcropping will be there because we have read the topology and we know the water has not yet claimed it. We are the ones who cross between the *Vash-Kora* settlements, carrying news of the Grinder's appetite, carrying warnings of a landslide that will dam the channel and flood the eastern pillars. We are the nervous system of this drowned world.

**The Midwife of the Rust**

The *Vash-Kora* venerate the factory ruins. They scavenge there. They forge. They build breathing masks and tap the geothermal heart.

But they do not *understand* the machines. Not really. They understand pressure and flow—those are intuitive to anyone who lives with the water—but they do not understand the logic that built the turbines, the sequencing of the valves, the elegant mathematics that once turned the scream of the storm into the quiet hum of electricity.

I do.

Not because I am smarter. Because I am patient. Because I will sit in the half-submerged control room of the old hydroelectric station for days, turning my body sideways to fit through the collapsed doorway, running my splayed fingers over the corroded dials and the frozen switches. I have traced the paths of the pipes with my palms, feeling where they branch and join, feeling the residual heat in the lines that still carry steam from the deep geothermal tap. I have mapped the logic of the place in my mind—not as engineering, but as *ecology*. The factory was an organism. It breathed in the water's fury and excreted power. The *Vash-Kora* see only the hollow shell. I see the ghost of the metabolism.

And sometimes—rarely, wonderfully—I can make it twitch.

I have re-routed a secondary steam line to blow through an obsolete turbine housing. The resulting resonance creates a standing wave in the channel below, disrupting the formation of whirlpools for a hundred meters. The *Vash-Kora* think I have appeased the Grinder. I have not. I have *redirected* it. I have spoken to the chaos in the language of the old builders, and the chaos has listened.

I have found a pressure gauge that still works. I do not know what the numbers mean—not exactly—but I have watched the needle move as the tides surge, and I have learned to read its dance as a prophecy. When it trembles at the red line, I run. I run faster than anything in this world, and I tell the *Vash-Kora* to pull their nests higher, and they listen, because they have learned that when the tall one comes running, the water is about to rise in a way that even the trees cannot survive.

**The Lover of the Sound**

The roar is constant. Sound 2.0. A million waterfalls. The thrum in the chest. The groaning rock.

The *Vash-Kora* communicate in sign and tap. They listen with their feet.

I listen with my whole body.

My people have a language, but it is not a language of words. It is a language of *song*—not sung, but *shouted into the storm*. We have discovered that the human vocal apparatus, when pushed to its absolute limit, when strained past the point of speech and into the realm of raw phonation, can produce sounds that cut through the roar. Not because they are louder—nothing is louder than the High Tide—but because they are *resonant*.

We sing in the frequency of the rock. We sing in the overtones of the wind. We find the gaps in the noise—the harmonic frequencies where the million waterfalls cancel each other out for a fraction of a second—and we pour our voices into those gaps. What emerges is not a melody. It is a *penetration*. A blade of sound that slips through the wall of noise and touches the listener's inner ear like a finger through water.

When I sing to a *Vash-Kora*, they hear me not as sound but as *sensation*—a warmth behind the sternum, a sudden clarity of thought, a name they had forgotten they possessed. When I sing to the storm, I am not commanding it. I am *joining* it. I am adding my frequency to the chorus of the falling water, and for a moment, the storm and I are the same phenomenon. I have fallen with the rain. I have struck the stone and dispersed into mist. I have been the surge and the retreat. I have been the Grinder.

This is why I am not afraid of the White Froth. Not because I cannot die there—I can, easily, just like anyone—but because I have already died there a thousand times, in song, and I know that the Grinder is not malicious. It is *thorough*. It is the end-state of all things in this world, and I accept it as I accept the erosion of the stone and the drowning of the valleys. It is not a spirit to be appeased. It is a *process* to be respected.

**The One Who Walks Between**

The *Vash-Kora* are my family. I love them with a fierceness that surprises me. Their hooked hands have caught me when my splayed fingers found no purchase. Their nests have sheltered me when the wind tried to peel me off the cliff. I have brought them warning and they have given me rest.

But I cannot stay.

I am the one who walks the edges. I am the one who crosses the channels when the whirlpools form. I am the one who descends to the killing zones when the tide is out, to read the fresh erosion on the stone, to measure how much of the mountain was lost today. I am the one who sits in the rusted skeleton of the factory and listens to the dying breath of the Great Vent, and I am the one who knows that the geothermal tap will not last forever. The heart of the mountain is slowing. The steam is less. The heat is fading.

And when the heart stops, the *Vash-Kora* will need to leave. The warmth around the vent is the only thing that makes their forging possible, the only thing that keeps the freezing wet from claiming their nests entirely. They do not know this yet. They feel the cold, but they attribute it to the whim of the Grinder, not the death of the machine.

I am trying to find them a new fire.

Somewhere in the drowned plateau, beyond the mist and the rain, there must be another vent. Another tap. Another ruin with a beating heart. The old builders left scars across this entire archipelago—I have seen the geometric anomalies from the high perches, the straight lines that do not belong to the organic chaos. There are other factories. Other machines. And if I can find one that still breathes, I can lead my people there.

So I run. I leap. I fall with grace and I read the chaos and I sing into the storm and I *go*. I am a silhouette moving against the gray, taller than I should be, thinner than I should be, alive when I should not be. I am the Tessera. I am the one who walks between the holds.

And when I cannot find a hold—when the granite is too smooth and the wind is too strong and the White Froth is rising—I do not panic. I simply *become* the fall. I orient my body to the current, I spread my netted hands, and I trust that somewhere in the grammar of the chaos, there is a word that means *survive*, and that I will recognize it in time.

I have always recognized it in time.

This is not courage. This is adaptation. This is what it means to be born of the scream and the steam and to choose the scream—the motion, the velocity, the terrible beautiful *falling*—over the grip.

I am the *Tessera*. I read the water. And the water has not yet finished with me.
