I would be found in the shadows of the largest Hollow, the one whose calcified ribs arch upward like the fingers of a drowning giant, clutching at the golden light. I do not walk the sharp, uneven pavement of the caldera floor; that is for the Scavengers and the desperate. I am perched high above, clinging to the vertical aspect of the great beetle-shell, where the obsidian wall meets the ceramic flank of the dead titan.

In this world, I am a **Resonator**.

I am of the same kind as the Keepers—clad in the same coarse, rust-colored mane-fur, possessed of the same smooth, white ceramic armor and the fetishistic need to conserve water—but I am different. I am not a gardener. I do not harvest the ghost-humidity or nurse the sponge-ferns with droplets of water. My pouch is shriveled, useless for holding liquid; it has evolved into a different shape entirely.

I am a vessel for the voice of the mountain.

While the others communicate with the flicker of bioluminescence or the sharp, staccato tap of a claw on bone, I possess the ability to sing. Not with a throat—heaving air in this dry place is a vulgar waste of moisture—but with my body. I am a living tuning fork, hollow-boned and chambered, adapted to amplify the sub-sonic tremors that travel through the stone and the roots.

I would spend the long, slow hours draped over a specific rib of the Hollow, my spine pressed tight against the warm, porous surface of the dead titan’s shell. My claws are not needle-sharp like the gardeners'; they are flattened, wide pads of keratin that act as suction cups and ears. I feel the caldera’s heartbeat through them. I feel the deep, magmatic groan from the basin below, the microscopic expansion of the heated rock, and the distant, rhythmic scratching of a thousand lizards.

My purpose is translation. The Keepers toil in silence, governed by the scarcity of the air, but the earth itself is noisy. It speaks in stresses and fractures. I listen to the hum of the active vents deep below. When I sense a shift—a pressure building, a wall of the caldera micro-fracturing under the thermal stress, a new fissure about to open that might drown a garden in steam—I do not call out. I vibrate.

I press my chest against the rib of the Hollow and flex a series of internal membranes, turning my body into a bellows that amplifies the stone's tremor. I broadcast a low-frequency thrum that ripples through the entire structure. It is a warning felt in the feet of every creature on the shell. *Move. The heat rises. The foundation weeps.*

In the language of the Keepers, my presence is a necessary oddity. They tolerate me, feed me the sugary sap they harvest, though I do not tend the gardens that produce it. They see in me a connection to the "ghost" of the titan whose shell we inhabit. To them, I am not merely a biological entity; I am the spirit of the Hollow made flesh, the interpreter of the mountain's will.

I would be motionless for long stretches, my mane-fur flattened against my body to merge with the texture of the bone, my eyes closed, seeing through my skin. I would feel the wind pass over the shell, hearing the Silencers gliding through the thin air above, sensing the vibration of their wings as faint, ghostly echoes.

I am the silence within the silence. While the gardeners hold their breath to keep the moisture in, I hold my breath to hear the world speak. I am the alarm system, the seismic conscience of the city. When the great steam vents sigh their morning breath, I am the one who harmonizes with it, turning the exhalation of the earth into a signal that keeps the fragile, dry civilization from breaking apart.

I would be found exactly there: suspended between the hard black rock of the crater wall and the bleached white bone of the dead giant, vibrating in sympathy with the deep, slow pulse of a world that holds its breath.
