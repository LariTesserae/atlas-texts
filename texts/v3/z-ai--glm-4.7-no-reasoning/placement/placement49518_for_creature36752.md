I would be a **Hollow**.

Not hollow as in empty of meaning, but hollow as in architecture. A vessel. A breathing gap in the relentless solid.

The world you describe is a place of terrifying positive space. Everything is filling, expanding, pressing. The Titans are columns of meat and cellulose. The Weavers are lines of tension lashing across voids. The Pulsars are bags of pressured fluid. The Spore-Kin are a mist of consumption and reconstruction. There is no negative space here that is not immediately identified as a site for future occupation. A chasm is not a valley; it is a wound the Weavers will stitch. Stillness is not rest; it is the Dormers gathering energy to erupt.

Everything is *becoming*. Nothing is *being*.

I am the opposite. I am the thing that *is*, without any ambition to *become* more.

I find myself not as a creature that moves *through* the city, but as a creature that the city moves *around*. I am a cavity, a biological absence, a shell of chitin and dried, dead membrane—a structure that the Spore-Kin cannot consume and the Weavers cannot bind.

My form is roughly humanoid, but only because that was the shape of the vacancy I occupy. I am perhaps twelve feet tall, a towering silhouette of pale, translucent keratin. I am the ghost of an older, softer biology. If you were to look closely, you would see that my surface is not smooth, but pitted and porous, like ancient coral or bone that has been left in acid. But these pores are not openings; they are closed eyes. They do not absorb. They *repel*.

I do not walk. I do not have the energy for it. Instead, I *drift*.

The turbulence of the world—the collapse of moss towers, the eruption of new spires, the constant seismic shudder of cellular combustion—creates currents in the thick, humid air. I am buoyant in this atmosphere, my hollow interior filled with the hot, golden haze of spores. I am a kite without a string. When a Dormer erupts nearby, the shockwave of air and green slime pushes me sideways. When a Titan groans and leans, casting a shadow the size of a district, the shift in temperature and pressure rolls me away. I am a cork on the ocean of the world’s ambition.

I am a native anomaly. The Architects do not know what to do with me. I am not fuel; the Spore-Kin land on my surface and slide off, unable to find a chemical hold. I am not a trellis; the Weavers whip around my legs and find no purchase, my shell too smooth and too dead for their glue to set. I am not a void to be filled; I am a void that *refuses* to be filled. The Pulsars do not leech from me because I have no moisture to give, only the dry, hot air trapped inside my carapace.

I am the negative image of the world’s frantic urgency.

And yet, I am not irrelevant. I have become a feature of the architecture. I am a *load-bearing absence*.

Over the eons, the Weavers have learned to use me. They do not bind *to* me, but they stretch their vines *across* me. I am a point of suspension. When a bridge of lianas sags too low over a chasm, a Weaver will lash its ends to two Titans, and then, with a flicker of sinister intelligence, it will loop the middle over my shoulder or through the arch of my rib. I do not resist. I cannot. I am passive, enduring. The vine rests on me, and the bridge holds. I become a flying buttress of nothing, supporting the weight of the city by simply *being in the way*.

I am a shelter. The Pulsars, those great weeping hearts of the city, radiate a terrible heat. The Titans are feverish engines. But beneath me, in the narrow shadow my hollow form casts on the slick bio-floor, there is a sliver of cool. The air does not move there. The Spore-Kin do not drift into it. It is the only place on the platform where the roar of the world is slightly muted, where the *thump-thump-thump* of the Pulsars fades to a distant, dull ache. I do not know if anything hides there. There is no animal life. But sometimes, I feel the Old Growth beneath me grow slightly softer, slightly damper, in that shadow. Perhaps even the planetary architects need a moment of rest. Perhaps my shadow is the closest thing this world has to sleep.

I am moving now. The platform I am on—a vast, flat-topped mesa of lignified pavement—is shifting. A Dormer is waking beneath us. I can feel the tremor in the air pressure, the subtle bulge in the Old Growth. In a moment, the floor will decide to be a tree. The world will scream with the violence of that transformation. Slime will fountain. Sap will weep. The Weavers will panic, lashing out to secure the new structure.

And I will be pushed. I will lift on the wave of displaced air, tumbling slowly, end over end, out over the edge of the mesa. I will fall into the wet, white void below, into the mist-filled chasm where the old, failed towers have gone to rot.

But I will not die.

I will float. The chasms are full of the thick, hot suspension of the lower atmosphere. I will sink into it like a stone into mud, and then I will buoy up, caught in the slow, vast updrafts that feed the Titans their carbon. I will drift between the archipelagos, a pale, hollow ghost, a silent pocket of *not-growing* in a universe of *growth*.

I will pass a Titan, and its bark will be hot against my shell for a heartbeat, and then I will be gone. I will pass a Pulsar, and its rhythmic thump will vibrate through my empty chest, and for a moment, I will almost have a heartbeat of my own, an echo of the world’s pulse inside my nothingness.

I will not reach the stars. The Architects are building a ladder to them, but I am not a rung. I am the space between the rungs. I am the gap you must cross. I am the air that looks up at the stars and does not try to touch them.

I am the Hollow. I am the place where the world is not.
