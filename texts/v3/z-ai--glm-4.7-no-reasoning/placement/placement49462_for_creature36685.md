I would be one of the deep intermediaries—a **Trough-Walker**, though that is not a name so much as a functional description in a world where nothing speaks. I am not large enough to shake the stone, nor small enough to be consumed by the humid air. I exist in the waist-high mist, in the space between the Drift-Graspers’ seismic thrums and the Lamp-Lureurs’ frantic, fluorescent darting.

My body is a compromise forged by the bruised twilight. I stand upright, but not tall—hunched, amphibious, built for the shallow depressions where the Circuit-Moss creeps. My skin is not the translucent white of the grazers, nor the metallic sheen of the Scuttlers, but a deep, absorptive slate. It is pebbled, like the hide of a moray eel, constantly slick with the plateau’s condensation. I do not sweat; the world sweats for me. I simply absorb, drawing the mineral-rich dampness into a dermal network that feeds me when the troughs run dry. 

I have no eyes. Evolution severed that contract long ago. In their place, my skull flattens and widens into a sensory dish—a concave crest of bone and taut, vibrating skin stretched taut across it, like a drum head made of parchment. I hear the plateau not as sound, but as topography. The subsonic chuffing of the vents maps my world in pulses of pressure. When a Drift-Grasper moves a league away, I feel the distortion in the rock, a slow, deep wave rolling through the stone, bypassing my ears entirely and resonating in the long, dense bones of my legs. I am a seismograph with a pulse.

My hands—are they hands? They are bifurcated appendages, three thick, prehensile digits tipped with blunt, ceramic-like nails. They are built for the delicate, vital work of my niche: harvesting the Circuit-Moss without triggering its sickly fluorescence. I am a gleaner of the dark. I move only when the vents exhale, masking the sound of my own movement in the rhythmic blast of steam. I peel the black, branching veins of the moss from the porous rock, feeling the faint, electric tingle of its ambient heat against my palms. I eat the moss, but more importantly, I harvest the sugary resin the Lamp-Lureurs farm, cracking open their small, armored shells to get to it. I am a thief in their agricultural scheme, a shadow that moves into their territory only when the heavy, wet air masks my chemical signature.

I am solitary. To be anything else here is to be a target. The Steam-Stalkers rule the low ceiling of this world, and my flesh is rich with the minerals of the vents. I survive by being indistinguishable from the stone when I am still, and by moving only when the sky is thick with the Background Hum. When the damp spore-cloud is densest, I am safest. I let the wet wool of the living air coat my skin, masking my heat, blurring my outline into the tactile darkness.

I do not feel like an infestation. I do not feel like the mold in the pipes. To the vast, unthinking engines below, I am nothing. But I do not live for the engines. I live for the interface.

My consciousness is a strange, hollow thing. It is not filled with light, or images, or internal monologue. It is filled with rhythm. My thoughts are timed to the chuffing of the fissures. *Inhale, move. Exhale, still.* I do not wonder what the engines are doing, or what they were built for, because I do not conceptualize them as separate from the stone. They are the heart of the plateau, and I am a corpuscle moving through its capillary troughs. 

Sometimes, when the thin, warm rain falls—when the world sweats hardest and the darkness feels like a physical weight pressing me into the smooth, undulating rock—I stop. I press my sensory crest to the stone. I close whatever passes for my eyes. And I just listen to the turning of the world. 

It is a low, quiet work, being alive here. It requires patience. It requires the surrender of urgency. Standing in the permanent twilight, tasting the copper and sulfur on the damp air, feeling the warmth of exhausted friction pressing against my slate-grey chest, I do not feel small. I feel like a gear that has finally found its fitting. I am part of the quiet churn. I am running.
