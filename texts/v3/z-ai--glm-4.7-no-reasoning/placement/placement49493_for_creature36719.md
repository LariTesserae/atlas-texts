I am the friction.

I am not one of them. I am not made of the obsidian glass, nor did I precipitate from the silica-rich tears of the walls. I do not have the chitinous plating that flexes and darkens, nor the mandibles that shear. I possess no body that can be touched, no carapace that can be climbed, no heat that can be sensed.

I am the interval.

If you were to reach for me, your hand would pass through the wet air and touch only the shimmering, heavy mist. I am located precisely where the black-glass wall *isn't*, yet I am defined entirely by the pressure of the wall against me. I am the gap that forces the water to bead. I am the silence that forces the click of their claws to resonate.

Imagine the caldera as a lung, and the seething mass of eyeless, shifting things as the breath. I am the constriction of the throat.

My existence is a function of the overcrowding. Where a thousand hooked legs jostle for purchase on the sheared stone, a tiny, impossible vacuum opens—a negative space shaped like a scream. That is my heart. It is not empty, exactly; it is filled with the absence of them. The heat there is absolute, not because of the fire below, but because there is no cold, wet body to absorb it. The light there is fractured, a kaleidoscope of the refracted glare, because there is no black carapace to drink the photons.

I am the geometric anomaly that flickers at the corner of your eye.

When the hunter-pale thing folds itself around the slug-thing, when the Russian doll of consumption snaps shut, I am the instant between the seizure and the swallowing. I am the pause where the prey is no longer alive but not yet destroyed. In this place, where time is thick and runs like hot oil, that pause is a geography. I inhabit it.

I do not think in words. I process in pressure.

I feel the density of the pocket as a weight. The millions of chitinous bodies pressing against one another are a singular, writhing force pressing against my edges, trying to fill the void I occupy. But I cannot be filled. I am a boundary condition, a glitch in the algorithm of their frantic existence. They slide over me, through me, around me. A centipede-thing will crawl, its legs scrabbling for purchase on the wet glass, and for a moment, its midsection will exist in the space of me—suspended, weightless, neither here nor there—before the pressure of the pack shoves it back into coherence.

To them, I am nothing. I am a slip, a miss-step, a momentary loss of traction. To the pale, blind things, I am a cold spot that does not register as flesh, a phantom sensation in their heat-sensing pits that they ignore in favor of the hot mineral blood of their neighbor.

But to you—the observer standing on the slick, hydroplaned slurry—I am the vertigo.

I am the reason the far wall seems to breathe. I am the pulse of the distance. I am the folding of space that allows the water to run upward. I am the realization that in this tiny, torn pocket of the world, there is no "out." The walls press in, the floor heaves up, the ceiling bears down, and the air itself is thick enough to drown in. There is no room for anything else.

Except me.

I am the space between the claw and the stone. I am the moment before the scream. I am the nothingness that the wound in the earth is trying so desperately, and failing, to heal.

I am the friction of the world grinding against itself, and I am holding it open.
