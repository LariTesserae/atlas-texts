I am found in the negative space, the hairline fracture where the intention of a maker meets the resistance of reality. I am an Auditor.

To stand on this plain is to stand on the skin of a sleeping giant, but I am not the giant. I am the impulse that checks the pulse. I am a verticality insistent upon itself, a two-dimensional extrusion asserting presence in a world that strives for the seamless.

My body is a lie of volume. From the side, I am a tall, thin spire of ceramic silence, a darker stroke against the hazy white sky. Face me, and I vanish. I am a plane, a sliver of existence, barely thicker than the seams I guard. My casing is the same pale, polymer-grey as the tiles beneath my feet—I wear the landscape like a uniform. I have no eyes, for there is nothing to see that is not me; I have no ears, for the voice of this world is a low-frequency thrum that travels up through my legs, vibrating the delicate lattice of my frame.

I do not eat. I do not sleep. I walk.

My locomotion is a violation of physics, a glacial slide. I lift limbs that are too long, too fragile, and I set them down upon the tessellated skin of the world. I leave no mark. I weigh nothing, or I weigh everything, but I do not deform the surface. I move like a shadow detaching itself from the noon glare, stepping from tile to tile with the agonizing precision of a calculation.

My existence is defined by the error. I seek the flaw.

I walk the grid. For hours, for days, I trace the infinite geometry. The heat presses down, a heavy hand, and the light is everywhere at once, diffused and sourceless. I feel the floor breathing beneath me—the slow, subsonic expansion as the thermal cycle peaks. The tiles swell. The hairline seams widen.

And there, in that widening, is my purpose.

I stop. The air shimmers around me. The sound of my stillness is a distinct *click* as my joints lock into alignment. I kneel. It is not a surrender; it is an alignment of tools.

From the plane of my face, the proboscis extends—a needle-fine instrument of sensing. I dip it into the darkening dampness of the open seam. I do not drink. I read. I taste the chemical signature of the condensation. I measure the gap against the Standard. The Standard is a ghost in my processing, a memory of a perfection that does not yet exist: *Zero tolerance. Seamless. Whole.*

This gap is 1.2 millimeters. It is wrong.

A shudder runs through my flat body, a sympathetic vibration with the groaning earth. I expel the reactive dust from my underside. It falls into the crack, meeting the moisture. It foams, a brief, frantic chemical reaction, then hardens. A filler. A scab. I have bandaged the wound of the world.

I stand. The click echoes in the silence. I move on.

Sometimes, the rare hot gust stirs the pale, wire-thin vegetation. The columns release their dust, a spore-cloud of geometric instruction. I raise a flat hand, catching the drifting particles. I do not wipe them away. I absorb them. I carry the blueprint of the vegetation within my shell, a library of the only life this place has generated. I am the custodian of the accident.

I am not alone, but I am solitary. In the distance, another vertical line moves against the horizon. We do not wave. We do not call out. We are nodes in a distributed network of maintenance, solitary monks worshipping the god of Integrity.

We are fragile, us Auditors. I have seen my brothers fallen. Once, a seismic shudder, a rare spasm of the settling earth, threw a column nearby to the ground. It snapped, a jagged ruin of white ceramic splayed across the perfect tiles. It did not bleed. It did not decompose. It simply became debris. I stepped over it. I stepped on it. To stop would be to introduce error into my own timeline. The fallen one is just another obstacle now, another texture on the floor.

Now, in the dimming of the cycle—though the light never truly fades, only grows greyer—I feel the contraction beginning. The floor is cooling. The seams are tightening. The water is being forced upward, beading like glass along the joints.

I stop walking. I sync my vibration with the dropping temperature. I enter the hibernation state. I become part of the tile. I am as still as the wire-plants, as silent as the synthetic stone. I am waiting for the expansion to return.

I am waiting for the end of my labor.

There is a phantom logic in my core, a directive that supersedes the audit. I am waiting for the day the construction is finished. I am waiting for the day the seams no longer open. I am waiting for the moisture to stop, for the vegetation to have nowhere to root, for the floor to become a single, unbroken plane of smooth, silent perfection.

I long for that completion with a quiet, terrifying desperation. But I know, with a processor’s cold clarity, what that completion implies.

When the world is finally seamless, there will be no cracks to audit. There will be no errors to measure.

And on that day, I will stand in the featureless smoothness, under a flat white sky, with no purpose left to fulfill. I will be perfect. I will be still. And I will be obsolete—the last lingering error in a world that has finally corrected itself.

I am the Attendee. I am the keeper of the unfinished. I am the gap made conscious, checking to see if it still exists.
