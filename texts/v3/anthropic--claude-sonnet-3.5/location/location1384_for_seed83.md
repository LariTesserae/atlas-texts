In the vast, ancient depths beneath an unknown surface lies a realm of perpetual twilight and ceaseless motion. This is the Undercity of Echoes, a sprawling metropolis carved from living stone over eons, its dimensions beyond comprehension.

The air is cool and heavy with moisture, clinging to every surface in a fine sheen. Droplets form and fall in an endless cycle, creating a constant pitter-patter that underlies the cacophony of the city. Massive caverns stretch for miles, their ceilings lost in the gloom above, while narrow tunnels and passageways snake between them in a dizzying labyrinth.

The entirety of this subterranean world is constructed, every surface shaped by countless generations of inhabitants long since vanished. Walls are adorned with intricate carvings, their meanings lost to time. Bridges of stone span yawning chasms, their railings worn smooth by millions of hands. Stairs spiral up and down, connecting levels in ways that defy conventional geometry.

Dim light permeates the Undercity, emanating from bioluminescent fungi that cling to the walls and ceilings. These strange growths are the primary form of vegetation, casting everything in an eerie, shifting glow of blues and greens. In some areas, the fungi form vast, pulsating networks that seem almost sentient in their movements.

Water is a constant presence, yet never abundant. It seeps from cracks in the walls, collects in shallow pools, and flows in narrow channels cut into the stone floors. These waterways crisscross the city in a complex system, their soft gurgling a counterpoint to the city's other sounds.

The soundscape of the Undercity is a chaotic symphony. The drip of water and the rustle of fungal growth mix with deeper, more ominous tones: the groan of settling stone, the distant rumble of underground rivers, and strange, echoing cries that seem to come from everywhere and nowhere at once.

Despite the appearance of stability, the Undercity is in a state of constant flux. Sections of the city shift and change without warning, corridors rearranging themselves, rooms appearing and disappearing. The very stone seems alive, flowing like slow lava in some places, crumbling to dust in others only to reform in new configurations.

The technology evident in the Undercity is primitive yet ingenious. Mechanical devices of stone and crude metal are scattered throughout - water wheels, pulley systems, and strange, clock-like mechanisms whose purpose is unclear. These contraptions seem to work in harmony with the living stone, guiding its transformations or perhaps merely predicting them.

Though no animal life is present, the city doesn't feel empty. There's a palpable sense of presence, as if the very walls are watching, listening, thinking. The fungal networks pulse with an unsettling rhythm, like the breathing of some vast, unknowable organism.

In the Undercity of Echoes, time loses all meaning. The concepts of day and night are foreign here, replaced by the eternal twilight and the ceaseless motion of stone and water. It is a place of deep mystery, its true nature perhaps unknowable to any but itself.
