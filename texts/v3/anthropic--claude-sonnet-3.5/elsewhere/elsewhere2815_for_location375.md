At night, under the light of twin moons, the dunes shimmer with an ethereal, bluish glow.

These structures, seemingly carved from a single piece of obsidian-like rock, stand in stark contrast to the shimmering sand around them. Their surfaces are smooth and featureless, save for intricate patterns of lines and swirls that seem to shift and change when viewed from different angles. These monoliths vary in size and shape, from obelisks that pierce the sky to vast, horizontal slabs that create islands of shade in the merciless sun.

The crystal sand becomes hot enough to instantly sear flesh, and the air shimmers with heat mirages. At night, the temperature plummets dramatically. These are not green, chlorophyll-based plants, but rather crystalline structures that seem to be a hybrid of plant and mineral. They grow slowly, absorbing trace minerals from the sand and energy from the sunlight refracted through the crystal dunes.
