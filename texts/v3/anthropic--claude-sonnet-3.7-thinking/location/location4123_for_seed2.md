# The Mersian Expanses

The first thing you notice is the roar. Even through hundreds of feet of water, the sound penetrates—a constant industrial symphony of massive pumps, hydraulic mechanisms, and the distant grinding of enormous seafloor excavators. The Mersian Expanses stretch before you, a vast underwater civilization that spans an entire ocean floor.

You float at the edge of Production Hub 7, one of thousands of interconnected industrial nodes in this aquatic mega-structure. Artificial light bathes everything in a diffuse blue-white glow, emanating from enormous illumination towers positioned at regular intervals throughout the expanse. Despite being hundreds of meters below the surface, visibility extends surprisingly far—the water itself has been treated with clarifying compounds that reduce particulate matter.

The architecture is utilitarian, all sharp angles and unweathered surfaces. Most structures appear to have been built within the last decade—the metal shows little corrosion, the massive concrete foundations lack the usual encrustation of sea life. Vast pipelines connect the hubs, their massive diameter suggesting the movement of tremendous volumes of water or resources. Occasionally, a pipeline flexes slightly as internal pressure changes, the motion rippling along its kilometer-long length like a slow serpent.

Between the built structures lie vast open areas of seabed—a moonscape of excavated trenches and artificial plateaus. The emptiness is deceptive; schools of engineered fish patrol these seemingly barren stretches, their bodies bioluminescent, serving as both a food source and living surveillance system. Their movements create shifting patterns of light across the seafloor, casting ephemeral shadows against the industrial structures.

Vegetation exists, but sparsely. Specialized kelp forests have been cultivated in designated zones, their fronds swaying in the artificial currents generated by the hub's circulation systems. These plants are more functional than decorative—engineered to process carbon dioxide and generate oxygen for the human inhabitants.

Speaking of humans—they move through this underwater world in mechanized exo-suits, their forms dwarfed by the scale of the infrastructure. Transport pods shuttle workers between hubs, trailing bubbles in their wake. Massive cargo drones, resembling metallic manta rays, navigate through designated corridors, carrying harvested materials from extraction sites to processing facilities.

The temperature is deliberately maintained at a cool but workable level—cold enough to optimize the industrial processes and machinery, but not so frigid as to compromise human operation. Thermal vents at strategic locations release excess heat from the power generation systems, creating upwelling currents that draw schools of native fish from the darkness beyond the illuminated zones.

Occasionally, the seafloor trembles. The Mersian Expanses sit atop a geologically active region, chosen specifically for its energy potential. Deep boreholes tap into the planet's heat, providing geothermal power to the entire operation. These tremors are a reminder that despite humanity's monumental construction, natural forces still exert their influence.

Above it all, suspended from the surface on colossal tethers, hang the residential arcologies—massive inverted skyscrapers housing the permanent population. Their lowest levels almost touch the seafloor industrial complexes, while their highest approach the ocean's surface. Between them, transport elevators constantly move, carrying people and supplies up and down the water column.

The sound never ceases. Beyond the industrial noise lies the constant background of water movement—the artificial currents created by the massive pumping stations, the percussion of pressure equalization chambers cycling, and the rhythmic pulse of the wave energy harvesters near the surface. The cacophony becomes a strange comfort after time—a reminder that despite being below the surface in an environment hostile to human life, the machinery of survival continues its unending work.

This underwater world spans almost an entire ocean basin—thousands upon thousands of square kilometers of seafloor transformed into a functional industrial civilization. Yet for all its vastness, the Mersian Expanses represent just the first phase of humanity's ambition to colonize the planet's watery realms. Construction continues at the periphery, steel and concrete advancing across the abyssal plain, bringing light and sound and human purpose to depths that knew only darkness for eons.
