# The Tessellated Heights

The first thing you notice about the Tessellated Heights is not the buildings but their absence—or rather, where "building" as a concept ends and something else begins. What from a distance appears to be a city perched on mountainous plateaus reveals itself, upon approach, as a place where the very concept of construction has been fundamentally reimagined.

Rising from the highland mists, the Heights unfold across a series of geometric terraces that balance precariously on natural stone columns. These platforms aren't built upon the mountain—they are the mountain, reconstituted. The native granite and basalt have been meticulously disassembled and reconstructed into perfectly angular forms: hexagonal platforms, cubic projections, prismatic towers. Where natural erosion would have carved valleys, engineered canals carry thin streams of water through crystalline channels, their surfaces occasionally beading with condensation in the cool air.

The structures densely populate every available surface, layered upon one another in ways that defy conventional physics. Buildings bleed into one another, sharing walls that simultaneously face different directions. Windows open into corridors that should be external streets. Stairways spiral upward, only to continue on the underside of platforms without interruption. The entire settlement exhibits a non-Euclidean geometry that becomes more pronounced the longer you observe it.

Despite the industrial nature of the Heights—with mechanical pumps that circulate water through the system, steam vents that periodically release pressure from below, and electrical lines forming mathematical patterns between structures—the soundscape remains eerily subdued. Machinery operates with whisper-quiet precision. Gears turn against oiled surfaces with barely a murmur. The occasional metallic ping echoes across the terraces like a dropped coin in an empty hall, all the more noticeable for the background silence.

The light is unnaturally constant and bright, yet no sun is visible through the thin cloud layer that hovers perpetually overhead. Instead, light seems to emanate from the structures themselves—a cool, even illumination that casts no shadows. This creates a disorienting effect where depth perception becomes unreliable, and distances cannot be accurately judged.

Vegetation exists in precisely designated spaces—small gardens of geometric topiaries and crystalline succulents that grow in perfect symmetry. These plants don't quite match any known botanical classification; their leaves occasionally shimmer and reorganize when not directly observed, like living circuit boards slowly reconfiguring themselves.

Despite the sparse plant life, animals are surprisingly abundant. Mechanical-organic hybrid creatures move through the Heights with purpose. Beetle-like entities with copper carapaces maintain the water systems. Bird analogues with glass wings and fiber-optic plumage nest in the higher structures, their movements synchronized as if sharing a hive mind. Translucent salamanders with visible clockwork interiors bask on warm metal surfaces. None make more than the slightest sounds—a soft tick, an occasional chime.

Most disconcerting are the Places-Between, where reality itself seems thin. Walking through certain archways may lead you to exit from a different doorway entirely. Corridors occasionally extend far beyond their physical dimensions. Some residents—humans modified with mechanical enhancements—can be observed simultaneously in multiple locations, unaware of their quantum duplications. They move slowly, deliberately, as if time passes differently for them than for visitors.

The most sacred rule of the Tessellated Heights is never to speak above a whisper—not from social custom, but necessity. On the rare occasions when a loud noise occurs, ripples of distortion spread outward from the sound, temporarily warping physical space like a stone dropped in water. The residents believe their careful reconstruction of reality remains stable only in relative silence, and evidence suggests they may be correct.

Weather changes arrive with mathematical precision—condensation forming in fractal patterns at exactly the same time each day, light rain falling in perfectly straight lines every third afternoon. These changes appear to be regulated by massive machines buried deep within the central plateau, where engineers monitor gauges that measure not just temperature and humidity, but more abstract qualities like "probability density" and "causal coherence."

The Tessellated Heights exists in a delicate balance—a place where reality has been dismantled and reassembled according to different principles, yet requires constant, gentle maintenance to prevent collapse. Its beauty is in its impossibility, its danger in how easily one could become lost in its non-Euclidean geometries, forever walking corridors that fold back upon themselves in the cool, bright silence.
