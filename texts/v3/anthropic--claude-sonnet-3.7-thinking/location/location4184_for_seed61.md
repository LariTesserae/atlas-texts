# The Constant Chimes

In the forgotten depths beneath the surface, there exists a pocket realm known to few—the Naillim Enclave. This hidden warren is reached only by those who know to follow the sound of ceaseless ringing that penetrates even the densest stone.

The first sensation upon entering is always the cold damp that touches your skin like hesitant fingers. Water is omnipresent here—not in rushing streams or standing pools, but as a persistent moisture that seeps from the glistening walls, condenses on metal surfaces, and forms perfect droplets that fall in precise intervals from the low ceilings. The droplets never cease their descent, yet the floor never floods. The water simply disappears into the myriad tiny crevices etched into the stone floors, maintaining an eternal equilibrium.

The Enclave itself consists of a dense honeycomb of small chambers, interconnected by passages so narrow that shoulders must turn sideways to navigate them. Each chamber is rarely larger than a modest bedroom, yet every available surface teems with the evidence of habitation. Dwellings stack atop one another in cramped tiers, creating a three-dimensional labyrinth of copper-reinforced alcoves, stone platforms, and suspended metal baskets serving as living quarters.

There is no true natural light here—illumination comes from thousands of tiny oil lamps with curved copper reflectors, strategically positioned to cast the maximum glow from minimal flame. The resulting light is dim, perpetual twilight, tinted amber and shifting slightly as the flames react to the endless dripping water.

The Enclave was carved and constructed so long ago that no one remembers its original architects. The stone shows the patient weathering of centuries, with every edge rounded, every surface smoothed by time and the persistent caress of water. Nothing here feels new or raw—everything has settled into its final form. Copper and bronze adorn the walls in intricate patterns, all long since oxidized to various shades of verdigris that complement the gray-blue stone.

Most remarkable is the complete absence of plant life. Where one might expect moss or lichen in such a damp environment, there is none. Instead, the walls display patches of something like frozen mercury—metallic surfaces that shift and ripple when caught in the periphery of vision, yet remain motionless when directly observed. These curious formations serve the ecological role of plants, somehow processing the stale air into breathable atmosphere through means no resident claims to understand.

The sound—oh, the sound never ceases. The Enclave rings constantly with the clear tones of countless tiny bells, chimes, and resonant metal bowls, all responding to the dripping water. Each droplet strikes a different note as it lands, creating a complex, never-repeating symphony that echoes through every chamber. Conversations must be conducted at near-shouting volume to overcome this eternal concert. After a few hours, visitors cease to notice the sound consciously, though it continues to influence their heartbeats and breathing patterns.

The few residents move about with the slow deliberateness of those who have all the time in the world. They are small, pale creatures—neither quite human nor entirely other—dressed in layers of waxed cloth and adorned with intricate metal jewelry that adds to the constant chiming. They craft their tools and artifacts from copper, bronze, and stone, shaped with meticulous care using only the simplest of implements. Their fingers work with inherited precision, creating objects identical to those made by their ancestors centuries before.

Rare translucent creatures scuttle along the ceilings—something between crabs and salamanders, with segmented bodies that appear to be filled with the same quicksilver substance that patches the walls. They are tolerated, even welcomed, as they consume the rare molds that occasionally threaten to establish themselves in this otherwise stable ecosystem.

The Enclave exists in perfect stasis—neither growing nor decaying, neither prospering nor failing. The residents don't remember a time when things were different, nor do they imagine a future where anything might change. Water drips, metal chimes, lamps burn with carefully measured oil, and time passes unnoticed in this hidden pocket of existence where even the concept of change seems foreign and unnecessary.
