# The Tempo-Weavers

They call themselves the Kith-Sahl in their own tongue—a name that translates poorly, meaning something like "those who exist between moments." To outsiders who rarely glimpse them directly, they are known as Tempo-Weavers, for their curious ability to navigate the Eroded Expanse's fractured timeline as one might traverse a familiar path.

The Tempo-Weavers appear humanoid only in the broadest sense. Their bodies possess the expected symmetry—limbs, torso, head—but the similarities end there. Their skin has the same porous quality as the Expanse's stone, not truly flesh but something between matter and possibility. Depending on proximity to reality fluctuations, their forms may appear solid or slightly transparent, with colors shifting between alabaster white and deep graphite gray. These hues occasionally separate from their bodies during reality ripples, orbiting them like chromatic moons before settling back.

Most striking are their eyes—geometric pools of liquid light that change shape according to what aspect of reality they observe. Triangular when perceiving time distortions, circular when viewing stable matter, spiraling when gazing through thin places where realities bleed together. They have no pupils; instead, the entire eye shifts color and pattern to process different wavelengths of existence.

The Tempo-Weavers stand taller than humans, with elongated limbs that move with unsettling grace. Their fingers number six or seven on each hand—the count seems to fluctuate when not directly observed—and taper to points capable of manipulating the finest threads of reality itself. They don't speak so much as resonate, their language comprising harmonics that vibrate at frequencies matching the plateau's own rhythmic shifts.

The elders among them have partially merged with the Expanse itself. Their lower bodies gradually transition to the same stone as the terrain, and the distinction between elder and landscape becomes philosophical rather than physical. These anchored ones can perceive across vast distances by extending their consciousness through the stone networks beneath the plateau. They serve as living libraries, their memories stretching back to before the mountains wore down to their current rounded forms.

Tempo-Weaver society revolves around cultivation—not of food, which they don't require in conventional sense, but of stability. They tend to the fractures in reality where the verdant gardens flourish, using tools carved from reality-precipitated materials. These implements appear crude but function as sophisticated reality-stitching devices, reinforcing weak points in existence or carefully widening them when beneficial fluctuations are needed.

They don't reproduce biologically. New Tempo-Weavers emerge during rare convergences of multiple reality ripples, forming fully conscious from the concentrated potential of these intersections. These "birthings" are celebrated with ceremonies where time is deliberately folded, allowing the entire community to witness the event simultaneously regardless of their location across the Expanse. Young Tempo-Weavers begin life with translucent forms that gradually solidify as they accumulate experiences and understanding.

Their perception of time differs fundamentally from linear beings. Tempo-Weavers experience all moments within a particular location simultaneously, parsing time as spatial coordinates rather than sequential events. This allows them to predict reality fluctuations with uncanny precision and navigate the Expanse's temporal inconsistencies without disorientation. They remember future events with the same clarity as past ones, though they perceive multiple potential futures branching like crystalline trees.

Their diet consists primarily of what they call "moment-essence"—the energy released when reality stabilizes after a fluctuation. They harvest this essence using resonant stone bowls placed at calculated points across the plateau. The captured energy manifests as luminous mist that they absorb through their porous skin. On rare occasions, they consume the crystalline fruits from the geometric orchards, not for sustenance but to commune with the memories stored within these growths.

The central structure in their largest settlement—neither purely temple nor observatory—serves as what they call the "Calibration Nexus." Here, the eldest and most stone-merged Tempo-Weavers maintain what might be described as a living map of reality's erosion. This manifestation appears to outsiders as an impossibly complex three-dimensional lattice of light, shadow, and suspended water droplets that reconfigures itself constantly. The Weavers read this shifting pattern like a text, understanding not just the current state of the plateau but its past configurations and potential futures.

Their technology, indistinguishable from magic to outsiders, functions by manipulating the unstable physics of the Expanse. Their buildings rotate not through mechanical means but because the Tempo-Weavers have convinced that section of reality that rotation is its natural state. Their bridges of light and shadow are not constructions but localized agreements with physics, negotiated through complex harmonic resonances.

The Tempo-Weavers maintain ceremonial relationships with the silhouette entities glimpsed at vision's edge. These beings, neither fully present nor entirely absent, are remnant echoes of creatures that once inhabited the Expanse before reality began its long erosion. The Weavers leave offerings at certain locations—arrangements of airborne water droplets and color separated from stone—acknowledging these ancients as the first inhabitants who witnessed the plateau when it stood jagged and new.

What might appear as simple existence to outside observers is actually complex caretaking. The Tempo-Weavers understand themselves not as masters of the Eroded Expanse but as its stewards, guiding it through its gradual transformation. They believe the plateau's final state, millennia hence, will be a singular point where all reality converges—and they are preparing both the landscape and themselves for this eventual transcendence.
