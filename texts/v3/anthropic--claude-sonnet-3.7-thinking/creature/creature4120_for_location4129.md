# The People of Lichenglass

They call themselves the Luminari, though this name is never spoken aloud—it is signed with fingers that possess one too many joints and palms that emit the faintest phosphorescent glow when pressed against crystal or stone.

The Luminari stand slightly taller than humans, though their posture—a perpetual, graceful stoop—often disguises this fact. Their skin has the translucent quality of alabaster, revealing a faint tracery of luminous veins beneath. These veins pulse not with blood but with the same light that fills the grotto, as though the inhabitants have gradually absorbed the essence of their environment over countless generations.

Their most striking feature is their eyes—large, pupilless orbs in varying shades of blue and white, reminiscent of the ceiling-dwelling creatures they share their home with. These eyes never blink simultaneously, always keeping one open to maintain constant vigilance. When they focus their gaze upon a visitor, the sensation is of being gently illuminated from within rather than observed.

The Luminari are born with fully functional vocal cords but choose silence from their earliest days. It is said that speaking aloud disrupts the delicate harmonics of the crystal walls and damages their collective consciousness. Their hands speak for them—not merely through signs but through subtle variations in the light they emit, creating conversational ripples that dance across the crystalline surfaces of their dwellings.

Each Luminari lives precisely three hundred and thirteen years. When their final day approaches, they ascend to the highest reaches of the grotto where they enter a meditative state. Over several months, their bodies gradually crystallize, becoming one with the ceiling. The eldest among them can be seen there—hundreds of serene figures who now form part of Lichenglass itself, still dimly aware, still participating in the city's collective memory.

Children are rare—appearing not through conventional reproduction but when specific formations of crystals in the grotto walls reach perfect alignment. On such occasions, a new Luminari emerges fully formed from the stone, the size of a five-year-old human child but possessing all the knowledge of their society. These "birthings" occur approximately once every decade and are the only times when sound is permitted in Lichenglass—a single, resonant tone produced by striking specific crystal formations in a precise sequence.

The ceiling creatures—known as Glimmerwings—are not merely companions but distant relatives. Luminari legend tells that both species descended from the same ancestors, with the Glimmerwings choosing the path of simplicity while the Luminari embraced complexity and consciousness. The two now exist in perfect symbiosis, each providing what the other lacks.

Diet consists primarily of the crystalline fungi that grow in the deepest recesses of the grotto. These fungi absorb light rather than requiring it, breaking down the crystal itself for sustenance. When consumed, they grant the Luminari their bioluminescent properties and extraordinary longevity. Visitors who consume this food find their thoughts becoming visible as faint light patterns across their skin—a disconcerting experience that few choose to repeat.

Every Luminari fulfills multiple roles throughout their long lives, mastering dozens of crafts and responsibilities. There are no leaders as such, merely those whose current role involves coordination. Decision-making occurs through a process so subtle it appears telepathic to outsiders—a complex system of light-signs reflected across multiple surfaces simultaneously, allowing hundreds to communicate complex ideas in seconds.

Their relationship with time differs fundamentally from surface dwellers. The Luminari perceive time as a physical dimension—visible bands of light that stretch through their grotto. They can observe past events as lingering illuminations and glimpse likely futures as faint, unrealized patterns. This perception grants them a profound acceptance of their unchanging existence and explains their unhurried movements—why rush when you can see the path already laid before you?

Visitors to Lichenglass are received with neither warmth nor hostility but with a patient curiosity. The Luminari believe all beings eventually find their proper place in the universe's pattern. Those who discover Lichenglass were meant to do so, if only briefly. They make no effort to detain guests nor to hasten their departure. Some visitors, however, find themselves slowly changing after extended stays—their skin growing paler, their movements more deliberate, their need for speech diminishing. The Luminari neither encourage nor discourage these transformations, viewing them as simply another manifestation of the universal order asserting itself.
