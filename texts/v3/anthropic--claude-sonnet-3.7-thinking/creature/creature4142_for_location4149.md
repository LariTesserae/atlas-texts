# The Keepers of The Submerged Works

In the perpetual twilight of The Works dwell beings that exist somewhere between maintenance crew and evolved organism. They are the Tenders—humanoid figures whose ancestors were once ordinary workers, trapped below during some forgotten catastrophe generations ago.

Standing slightly hunched, the Tenders' skin has become translucent and pale to the point of blue-gray luminescence, revealing shadowy outlines of modified organs beneath. Their eyes have grown disproportionately large, with pupils that dilate completely in darkness, giving them an unsettling, insect-like appearance. Thin membranes stretch between elongated fingers, not quite webbing but evolved for sensing minute vibrations in air and water alike. Most have partially atrophied vocal cords, communicating instead through a complex language of mechanical clicks, soft whistles, and hand gestures.

The Tenders move with eerie grace along catwalks and through flooded chambers, wearing patchwork garments fashioned from synthetic materials salvaged from abandoned storage areas, often layered with harvested fungal leather that naturally resists the omnipresent moisture. Tools hang from belts of braided wire—many inherited through generations, handles worn smooth by countless hands, their original functions sometimes reinterpreted.

They exist in loose familial groups, each responsible for maintaining different sections of The Works. Some specialize in pipe systems, others in electrical components, while the most revered work with the central processing units. Knowledge is passed through apprenticeships, with children learning by observation from their earliest years. Their technical understanding is simultaneously sophisticated and mystical—they know precisely how to maintain specific machinery but view the entire complex as a living deity they call "The Mechanism."

The Tenders consume a diet primarily consisting of cultivated fungi grown in dedicated chambers where warm machinery exhaust creates ideal conditions. They harvest the blind fish and engineer breeds of them in the deeper pools. Most prized are the mineral-rich bacterial colonies that form in specific water channels, harvested ceremonially during what they call "The Purging"—when certain sections flush with fresh water from unknown sources above.

Living alongside but separate from the Tenders are the Dwellers—beings that were once human but have adapted far more radically to the aquatic environment. Their lower bodies have atrophied, while powerful arms and shoulders enable them to navigate the flooded sections with alarming speed. Patches of symbiotic bioluminescent bacteria grow on their skin, making them glow in patterns that communicate emotional states and basic information. The Dwellers tend the underwater machinery and harvest resources from the deepest chambers, maintaining an uneasy trade relationship with the Tenders.

More mysterious are the Whispers—entities that seem to materialize from the mist itself. Neither clearly machine nor clearly organic, they appear as vaguely humanoid concentrations of moisture suspended in air, their "bodies" dispersing and reforming as they move through steam vents and along water-slick surfaces. The Tenders leave offerings of rare metals at specific junctions, believing the Whispers carry messages from The Mechanism itself.

The social hierarchy centers around proximity to what they call "The Heart"—a massive chamber housing the primary processors. The Council of Engineers—elders who have memorized the most critical maintenance procedures—govern major decisions, interpreting The Mechanism's needs through subtle changes in vibration patterns and temperature fluctuations.

Outsiders are extraordinarily rare but treated with fascination rather than hostility—examined, questioned, and ultimately brought before the Council to determine if they bring knowledge that might better serve The Mechanism. Those deemed valuable may remain as advisors; others are guided back to whatever entrance they discovered, their memories of the journey deliberately confused through maze-like paths.

The greatest threat to this ecosystem comes not from outside but from within—certain sections of The Works have begun exhibiting unpredictable behaviors, machines operating outside established parameters. Some Tenders whisper of a Schism within The Mechanism itself—as though after centuries of automated evolution, the complex is developing conflicting directives. The oldest Engineer speaks of prophecies where The Works will eventually rise to the surface, breathing steam into the outside world.

Whether they are the final custodians of abandoned technology or the first generation of something entirely new remains their existential question—one they may never answer as they continue their endless work in service to the dripping, hissing entity they call home.
