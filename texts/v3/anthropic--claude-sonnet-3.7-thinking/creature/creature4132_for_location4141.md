# The Hushmasters of Karsaal

In the sweltering depths of the Hushing Gardens dwell a people who have not tasted water in seven generations. The Hushmasters, as they call themselves, bear little resemblance to their surface-dwelling ancestors who first sought refuge in these caverns during the Great Burning.

Their skin has adapted to the waterless environment, developing a semi-translucent quality that reveals hints of the amber circulatory system beneath – for they do not pump blood as surface dwellers do, but rather a modified version of the plant-derived thal that sustains all life here. This adaptation grants their skin a faint luminescence in the darkest corners of the caverns, patterns of light pulsing with their heartbeats.

Most striking are their eyes – entirely pupilless and colored in gradients of copper and teal that mirror the bioluminescent fungi overhead. These eyes have evolved to see perfectly in the perpetual twilight, detecting subtle variations in the plants' glow that would be invisible to outsiders. The Hushmasters stand taller than their desert-dwelling cousins, with elongated limbs and fingers that enable them to tend the highest reaching ferns without tools.

They are hairless by necessity – in the intense heat, such insulation would be lethal – and their ears have developed pronounced cartilaginous extensions that catch and analyze the symphony of plant sounds. These modified ears allow them to "hear" frequencies outside normal human range, particularly the lowest vibrations produced by the root systems that travel through stone rather than air.

The Hushmasters organize their society around seven castes, each dedicated to a specific family of plants. One is born into a caste, identified at birth by subtle variations in skin luminescence that plant-readers interpret as signs of affinity. The Copper Frond Tenders care for the massive ferns, the Gem Harvesters cultivate and collect the crystalline fruits, the Sap Channels maintain the thal circulation system, the Fungi Shepherds tend the ceiling growths, the Resonance Listeners interpret the plants' communications, the Void Keepers maintain the empty spaces between plantings, and the Memory Keepers preserve botanical knowledge through oral tradition.

Above these seven castes stand the three Symphonists – elders who have mastered all seven disciplines and coordinate the complex rotation schedules that keep the Gardens in balance. Selection of new Symphonists occurs only when one dies, marked by a ceremony where all seven castes present candidates who must undergo the Tasting – consumption of a specific combination of plant elements that induces visions and reveals the successor.

The Hushmasters communicate primarily through a language of whistles, clicks, and hums that mimics plant sounds. Their spoken language reserves words only for concepts that cannot be expressed through sonic patterns. When they do speak aloud, their voices carry the resonant quality of wind through hollow stems. In moments of great importance, they speak and whistle simultaneously, creating harmonic overtones that make listeners' skin prickle.

Their understanding of time differs fundamentally from surface dwellers. With no sun to mark days or seasons, they measure cycles by plant growth. A "becoming" marks the period from a seed's planting to its maturation. A "returning" encompasses the harvest and preparation of new seeds. Major decisions align with the rare flowering of the Timesinger plants that bloom once every forty-two becomings.

The Hushmasters do not consider themselves human, but rather "the third evolution." They believe the plants were the first life, humans the second, and themselves the third – a perfect symbiosis of both. Their origin myth tells of ancestors who, facing extinction during the Great Burning, made a covenant with the last plants of the surface world. The humans would provide protection in the underworld, and in exchange, the plants would transform them into beings who could survive without water.

Most remarkable about these people is their partial photosynthetic ability. The thal that replaced their blood contains modified chlorophyll that processes the limited light of the fungi into nutrients. While they still consume plant matter, they require far less sustenance than ordinary humans, sometimes going weeks between meals during periods of strong fungal blooming.

Yet for all their adaptations and knowledge, the Hushmasters harbor a profound fear. Throughout the Gardens, certain darkened alcoves remain forbidden, sealed with complex arrangements of resonant plant materials that create sound barriers. These "silenced chambers" contain evidence of failed adaptations – preserved remains of ancestors whose transformations went wrong. The Hushmasters believe these chambers contain something else as well – the original seeds from the surface world, kept in stasis until the prophesied time when the Great Burning ends and they can return above.

Whispers among the youngest Hushmasters speak of a final caste, unacknowledged by the elders – the Rootless Ones. These rare individuals are born without the ability to process thal and must be kept in specialized chambers where they gradually develop mineral-based bodies, eventually becoming immobile but sentient crystal formations that pulse with inner light. These beings, if they exist beyond rumor, are said to dream the future of the Gardens and whisper their visions to those who press their ears against the living stone.

In the deepest, hottest chamber of the Gardens grows a singular plant unlike any other – the Progenitor. Tended by all three Symphonists together, this massive organism resembles a translucent heart, pulsing with amber light. The Hushmasters believe it contains the collective consciousness of every plant in the Gardens and serves as the repository of all botanical memory. Only the Symphonists may approach it, and they do so only during the most significant transitions in the Gardens' cycles.

Among the Hushmasters, there is no concept of natural death. When a body begins to fail, the individual undergoes the Returning Ceremony, where they are placed in a preparation chamber with specific seedlings planted around them. Over several weeks, their body gradually transfers its thal and consciousness back to the plants, completing what they see as the eternal cycle between plant and tender. The success of this transfer is measured by how vibrantly the seedlings grow in subsequent cycles.
