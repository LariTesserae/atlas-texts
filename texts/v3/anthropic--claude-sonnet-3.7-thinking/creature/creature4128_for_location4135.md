# The Valabrine Tidal People

In the suspended waters of Valabrine dwell the Lumir, a species who long ago transcended the boundary between human and aquatic life. Standing at about five feet tall when fully extended, their bodies are slender and sinuous, with translucent skin that reveals a faint outline of their skeletal structure and major organs. Their epidermis shifts between pale blue and silver depending on their emotional state, occasionally displaying complex patterns similar to rippling water when experiencing strong feelings.

Each Lumir possesses four primary limbs and two auxiliary ones. Their arms are long and triple-jointed, ending in seven-fingered hands with webbing that can expand or contract as needed. The auxiliary limbs—slender appendages sprouting from below their shoulder blades—are primarily sensory organs that detect minute pressure changes in the water, acting as both navigational tools and emotional receptors when interacting with others.

Their most striking feature is their cranial structure: an elongated head housing an enlarged brain with deep furrows, protected by a semi-transparent cartilaginous case rather than bone. Their eyes—three of them, arranged in a triangular pattern—are large and entirely black, adapted for seeing in the dim, diffused light of their watery home. They have no external ears, instead sensing sound through specialized tissue patches along their jawline that translate water vibrations to neural signals.

The Lumir breathe through a complex network of external gill filaments that frame their necks like delicate collars, occasionally pulsing with bioluminescent light during their three daily respiratory cycles. They reproduce only once every seven years during the "Deep Confluence," when the entire sphere's currents shift inward, carrying specialized gamete cells to the central birth chamber where new Lumir slowly develop in crystalline egg cases attached to the mineral formations.

Lumir society is organized into thirteen concentric "tide rings," representing both physical location within Valabrine and social function. Those born in the outermost rings typically remain as Membrane Tenders, maintaining the water-air boundary through ritual pressure dances that strengthen the mysterious surface tension. The middle rings house the Archivists, who record community history through intricate patterns knotted into living plant fibers, and the Cultivators, who tend the three-dimensional gardens and domesticated fauna.

The innermost rings are home to the Resonance Elders, who have developed the ability to communicate directly with what they believe is Valabrine's consciousness. These ancient Lumir (some living over 200 years) have skin so transparent they appear merely as shimmering outlines. They rarely leave the proximity of the central birth chamber, where they telepathically commune with the emerging crystalline structures through a form of deep meditation they call "water-thinking."

Lumir communication is multi-layered. Their primary language consists of clicking sounds produced by specialized chambers in their throats, complemented by subtle electrical pulses conducted through the water that convey emotional context. For complex concepts, they use choreographed movements—a form of physical syntax that, when combined with their vocalizations, allows for nuanced expression impossible in either medium alone. For permanent records, they employ a tactile writing system pressed into specialty-grown kelp tablets.

Recent generations of Lumir have developed a concerning adaptation: some children are being born with rudimentary lungs alongside their gills. The Resonance Elders interpret this as Valabrine preparing its children for an inevitable change, though whether this represents imminent catastrophe or evolution remains fiercely debated among the tide rings.

The Lumir's awareness of the world below is limited. Some believe they were once land-dwellers who were saved by Valabrine's ascension; others maintain they have always been water-born. Their mythology tells of a time when "the beneath" will rise to meet them, initiating the "Great Remerging." Annually, small expeditions of volunteers intentionally exit through temporary membrane weaknesses, riding the raindrops down to explore the below-world. Few return, but those who do bring stories that become incorporated into their evolving understanding of their place in existence.

In the deepest center of Valabrine, beyond even the birth chamber, dwells the entity the Lumir call the First Swimmer—an enormous, ancient being visible only as a shadow that occasionally passes through the central waters. Even the Resonance Elders speak of it with hushed clicks. Some believe it is the original consciousness that drew the waters upward and suspended them; others suggest it may be the last of whatever species the Lumir themselves evolved from. What is certain is that its rare appearances coincide with significant changes in Valabrine's internal currents and the emergent crystalline growths.

The Lumir perceive time differently than land-dwellers, measuring it not in days or years but in pressure cycles and nutrient flows. A Lumir might speak of events occurring "three nutrient tides ago" or "during the seventh pressure ebb." This perception shapes their patient, unhurried approach to crafting, building, and decision-making, with some projects spanning multiple generations.

As Valabrine continues its slow rotation among the mountain peaks, the Lumir evolve with it—adapting, observing, and preparing for whatever transformation their living home might eventually undergo.
