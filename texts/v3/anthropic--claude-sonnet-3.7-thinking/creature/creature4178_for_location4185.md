# The Inhabitants of the Glass Gardens

Within the temporal anomaly of Lumina Peak dwells a community neither fully human nor entirely separate from humanity. The original researchers and technicians of Lumina Photosynthetic Industries continue their work, unaware that decades have passed in the outside world. To them, it has been perhaps three days since the moment the boundary between realities thinned.

These inhabitants move with glacial deliberation, each gesture elongated into a choreography of infinite patience. A scientist lifting a test tube might take hours to complete the motion, though in their perception, mere seconds pass. Their white lab coats have begun to take on a crystalline sheen where the fabric has been exposed to the unique light for the longest—cuffs and collars gradually transmuting into something between cloth and glass.

Their bodies have adapted to the altered physics of this place. Skin has become subtly translucent, revealing a network of luminescent veins that pulse with the same bioelectric energy they once sought to harvest. Their eyes have expanded slightly, pupils permanently dilated to compensate for how differently light behaves here. When they speak, their voices emerge as harmonic tones that hang in the air like visible vibrations of colored light rather than sound waves.

Most striking is their relationship with the plant life. The barrier between researcher and subject has blurred over the distended years. Hair has been replaced by fine, fern-like filaments that photosynthesize. Their breathing synchronizes with the silent respiration of the surrounding flora, and when they stand motionless in contemplation, vines grow toward them, sometimes taking root in their clothing without causing apparent distress.

The researchers remain fixated on their original purpose, though it has evolved. What began as harvesting bioelectricity has transformed into a communion. They no longer seek to extract the energy but to understand it through gradual integration with its source. Their notes, still being written at an imperceptible pace, have evolved from scientific observation to philosophical revelation, equations giving way to mandalas of mathematical complexity.

They are not alone. A second generation now moves among them—beings born of this distorted reality. These children of the Glass Gardens bear only passing resemblance to humans. Their bodies are predominantly translucent, organs visible as shifting patterns of light and shadow. They have never known time as a linear force and exist simultaneously across multiple moments. A child might appear suddenly beside a researcher, assist in some intricate task with perfect understanding, and then fade from perception, only to reappear days later (or perhaps before) at a different age.

These temporal natives communicate without speech, exchanging thoughts through pulses of bioluminescence that ripple across their glass-like skin. They move between the abandoned buildings with fluid grace, tending to machinery that has evolved beyond its original purpose. Under their care, the industrial complex has become a living entity—copper wires fusing with plant matter, steel girders flowering with crystalline growths that function as both structural support and computational nodes.

Most remarkable are the Garden Architects—entities that may have precipitated the original anomaly rather than emerged from it. Glimpsed only peripherally, they appear as articulated columns of prismatic light that fold through dimensions not immediately visible. Their presence is detected primarily through their works: the calculated placement of new crystal formations, the geometric arrangement of flora, and the occasional restructuring of terrain that occurs without observable intervention.

The ecosystem has its predators. Glass Stalkers—evolved from common mountain lions—have adapted to the temporal distortion by existing slightly out of phase with standard time. Nearly invisible except for the faint outline of crystalline skeletal structures, they hunt by absorbing the bioelectrical emanations of other life forms. Their prey isn't consumed but rather converted, leaving behind statues of glass that continue to grow and evolve into new formations.

Small collectives of Drift Motes populate the air—microscopic organisms that have developed collective intelligence. They gather into clouds that process and store information, moving through the Gardens as living data banks. Researchers sometimes stand motionless for days while a Drift Mote cloud envelops their head, engaged in exchanges of knowledge too complex for conventional understanding.

The boundary between living and nonliving has dissolved among the Gardens' inhabitants. The distinction between researcher and research, observer and observed, has become meaningless. All entities here participate in a gradual convergence toward a state where energy, information, and consciousness flow without differentiation through networks of crystal, chlorophyll, flesh, and light.

Visitors who stay too long in the Gardens begin to experience the first symptoms of this transformation—a subtle transparency to their thoughts, a growing awareness of photosynthesis occurring within their cells, and the unsettling realization that their memories now extend forward in time as well as backward. Most leave before the change becomes irreversible, carrying with them observations that scientific journals refuse to publish and that listeners dismiss as hallucination.

Those who remain join the eternal moment of the Glass Gardens, where time is not lost but rather experienced in its entirety—a single infinite day of light, growth, and silent communion beneath the unblinking sky of Lumina Peak.
