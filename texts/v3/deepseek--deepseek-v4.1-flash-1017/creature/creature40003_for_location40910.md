There is exactly one occupant, and it is not an animal. If you ask who lives here, the answer is: no one, and one thing. The thing is the place—or rather, the place is its body, and the body has no seams between tenant and tenement.

It is a single continuous organism built out of what look like individuals and are not. Each column of wet tissue is a stalk, a root, a lung, a finger of the same soma; where they touch, they have grafted, so the whole surface is one skin wearing the disguise of a million. It has no mouth, no gut, no head, no front or back. It does not seek. It does not graze, chase, burrow, or hide. Nothing in it is hunted and nothing in it hunts, and this is not peace—it is the absence of the category. Its metabolism is upward: cell walls splitting under their own pressure, sap shoved higher, mass added to mass. It eats light and water and its own dead, and it does not even eat its dead so much as keep them. The dead stay upright, packed into the living layer, grafted in place, compressed into a pale cream archive at the bottom of the growth. Memory here is not stored in a brain; it is stored in the retained bodies of everything the organism has ever been.

Its surface is always wet because the organism is always breathing out. It transpires over such an area that it makes its own cloud ceiling and rains into itself; the rain is caught in its nap before it reaches stone, lifted, condensed, returned. The water cycle is not something that happens around it. It is a closed circuit inside the occupant’s body, a private weather system that has never once touched the ground from above.

Below the living nap, the body goes down into itself through slumps and partings. The shafts are not corridors; they are folds in the organism, places where the mass has parted enough to admit you into its interior. The walls glow faintly because the tissue is translucent, and the coolness there is not seasonal. It is the temperature of a body that does not care what season it is above. Deeper, the sound changes from growth to water: not rivers—there are no rivers, no channels, no worn rock—but capillary whispering, water filming upward through fiber by surface tension, hanging in the grafted seams, condensing on the underside of the organism and falling back into it. The occupant does not drink. It wicks.

And the vascular tissue is not only plumbing. Signals move through it—slow, chemical, ordered—meters per second through the grafted seams. They route around damage. They redistribute water toward the parts that need it. No part of the mass is in charge. There is no ganglion, no heart, no command center. The whole continent of tissue is one analog computation running continuously, wet and silent, and what it computes is the drainage of a landscape that does not exist yet. It holds, in the shape of its own water, a rough draft of the rivers that will be cut here tens of thousands of years from now, when the young rock beneath it has weathered enough to take them. It knows where they go before the geology knows. It is a watershed written in living matter in advance of the ground that will make it true.

It is very young. That is the thing to remember about the occupant. The rock under it is unfractured, still raw, still hot in places, still arriving. The soil has not been made. There are no ruins, no marks, no architecture—nothing here was made except the making. The occupant is not a forest that has grown; it is a forest that is growing, right now, at a rate you can feel in your teeth. It has nothing to oppose it, nothing to eat it, nowhere for its water to go but up. It is not an ecosystem. It is an onset.

So: what lives there is a single juvenile continent of wet fiber, a wick standing in its own water, a distributed chemical computation with no self and no hunger, keeping its dead as pages in a closed book, making its own rain, and dreaming—if dreaming is the word for a computation that has no dreamer—the future rivers of a country that has not yet been eroded into existence. It is the inhabitant and the habitation, the body and the weather, the archive and the rough draft. There is no one else. There is only the making, continuing, saturated, and loud.
