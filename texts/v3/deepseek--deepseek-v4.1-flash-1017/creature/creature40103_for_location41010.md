Nothing else lives there. That is the first answer, and it is almost true. There are no animals, no insects, no birds, no people—no separate creatures moving through the green with purposes of their own. The only inhabitant is the green itself, and it is not one thing but a continuum: a single distributed organism, or a colony so thoroughly merged that “individual” has no meaning. If you need to call it something, call it the Mechanism. It does not live *in* the forest. It is the forest, and the forest is its body.

Its anatomy is the place. The canopy is its skin—thin, layered, photosynthetic, drinking the dim glow from above and passing the energy down. The trunks are its long bones and its pipes; the branches are conduits; the hardened resin at the joints is its cartilage and its engineering. The root floor is its nervous system and its circulatory bed, a wet dark grid through which fluid and signal move in slow pulses. In the hearts of the largest growths are chambers where the temperature rises and the hum deepens: stomachs, or furnaces, or glands—there is no word for them, because nothing else has ever needed one. It has valves. It has nodes. It has no face, no eyes, no mouth, no hands. It does not need them. It senses pressure and chemistry and the gradient of light; it knows the weight of water in its tissues and the rhythm of its own circulation. It does not think in any way we would recognize. It maintains.

It is blind and patient and vast. It does not hunt, because there is nothing to hunt; it does not flee, because there is nowhere else and nothing to flee from. It eats light and water and the soft mineral ground, and it makes something—something dark and warm that moves through its conduits in pulses, something produced in the chambers and carried away to be used for a purpose the place does not explain. It is a factory that grew instead of being built, and it is also the workers and the walls and the product and the customer. It does not distinguish between living and manufacturing. For the Mechanism, metabolism is industry, and industry is metabolism, and both are the same slow hum.

It has no society, no family, no young in any visible sense. It is one and many at once: every trunk connected to every root, every leaf a cell in a body whose boundary is the limit of its own conduits. It does not reproduce so much as extend, and it does not extend so much as hold. It is young—the land is soft, the channels shallow, nothing worn down—and yet it is already in equilibrium. It has reached its operating state. It will not speed up. It will not slow down. It does not have seasons or moods or intentions. It is not hostile. It is not kind. It is simply running.

If a person enters, the Mechanism does not notice. A human is a foreign body, warm and wet and brief, tolerated the way a stone is tolerated in a stream. The green does not close around you or drive you out. It goes on being green. The water drips. The conduits pulse. The hum fills your chest until you stop hearing it. And the inhabitant—the only inhabitant—remains what it has always been: a single blind industrious continuum, living its long undifferentiated life in the warm, the wet, and the dark, making something no one sees, for reasons no one is owed.
