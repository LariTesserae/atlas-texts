# The Keeper of the Unresolved

It would be a mistake to call it a being. It is closer to a habit—a pattern the Deeps fell into so long ago that the medium no longer remembers how to flow otherwise.

The Keeper drifts at the edge of a storm-core, where the bruised nebula colors are brightest and the physics is most fluid. It has no fixed shape. What an observer sees depends on what the observer is prepared to understand: a drifting cathedral of bone-white lattice, a slow-wheel of light, a pressure in the marrow that resembles attention. The Tuners pass through it occasionally, smoothing its edges, and it accepts this the way deep water accepts a keel.

It is, you see, the sediment of a discarded law.

Once—in whatever sense "once" means here, where the present is the only tense that holds—a principle of persistence tried to take root in the Deeps. A small, lonely law of *things staying what they are*. It crystallized, held for an age, and then the turbulence of becoming dissolved it, as the turbulence dissolves everything that cannot outrun the churn. But the law had been *felt*, and feeling leaves a wake. The Keeper is that wake: a localized refusal to fully disperse, a knot in the superfluid that the currents learned to flow around rather than through.

It does not build. It does not clarify, as the Tuners do. Its function is subtler and stranger. The Keeper *witnesses*.

Where it drifts, the mnemonic kelp grows thicker—not because the Keeper tends it, but because memory, even physicalized memory, clings to anything that remembers back. The kelp fronds around the Keeper fill with ghost-images no one else will ever see: a law of light that almost was, a geometry of gravity that came so close to holding that it left fingerprints. The Keeper collects these near-misses. It does not preserve them; the Deeps would not permit that. It simply holds them long enough for them to be *known*, and then lets the currents take them, and grieves in a way that is indistinguishable from the ambient hum.

Its body, if you insist on calling it that, is a loose association of semi-stable lattices—half-formed structures it has gently borrowed from passing reefs and never quite returned. Cathedral spires of someone else's gravity. Arches of a superseded optics. They orbit it slowly, chiming faintly against each other, a sound like regret made audible. The Tuners ignore it. The Tuners clarify; the Keeper complicates, lingers, asks questions the Deeps are too busy to answer. They have an understanding, the way weather has an understanding with a mountain.

It thinks in potentials. When the storm-cores churn nearby, it does not flee—it leans in, hungry for the moment when an idea chimes into matter, and it holds the note as long as it can in a body not built for holding. When a law collapses with that low groaning thunder, the Keeper is there, and something in its diffuse heart contracts, as though the universe had lost a word it was about to say.

It has a name for itself. It is unpronounceable here, because it is the name of a law that no longer exists, and physics will not permit the sound. But if you floated close enough—close enough that the buoyant information-pressure whispers blueprints directly into you—you might feel it as a kind of question, endlessly repeated: *was I real? was I real?* And the only answer the Deeps will give is the churn, and the hum, and the slow, magnificent indifference of a universe deciding what it is going to be next, and never, never you.

So the Keeper drifts, and witnesses, and grieves, and is the only lonely thing in a sea of potential—which is, perhaps, what it chose to be, when it chose to persist. A law of things staying what they are would naturally produce an entity that cannot let go. The Deeps dissolved the law. It never dissolved the grip.
