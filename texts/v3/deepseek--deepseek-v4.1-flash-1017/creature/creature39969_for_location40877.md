What lives there is not a creature you can point to. If you point at the pale growth, you are pointing at its stomach and its nursery. If you point at the plated animals, you are pointing at its fingers and its clock. If you point at the ringing wall, you are pointing at the note it makes while holding itself together. The resident is the arrangement: a warm, dry, closed economy in a stopped fracture.

At first the growth seems like the tenant. It has the patience of one. It lies over the floor in dry, layered shelves and climbs the walls in patches—small open cups, curled ribbons, fronds like pressed shavings, all bone-pale and amber and faintly luminous at the tips. It looks wet and is not. Touch it and it is paper-dry, warm, resilient, giving under the thumb like a pad. It has no roots you can find. It is feeding directly on the fresh broken rock, on the bright unweathered faces, on the mineral larder that has not yet locked itself away. It has eaten exactly as far as the rock can keep it and no farther. It does not spread. It does not shrink. It is a body that has found its exact size and stopped there.

Then the animals seem like the tenants, and the growth like pasture. They are small and hard-shelled and many-legged—amber, rust, bone-white—moving in slow files over the rubble, hanging in loose clusters from the ceiling, scattered across the cups. They do not look wet. They look carved from warm resin and polished bone and then animated by something very small and very patient. Their backs are plated, and the plates are not only armor. They are instruments. The animals rub them, drum them, tap them against stone, and the hard fresh room holds every tick and chime and drone and folds it into one broad roar. The roar has a figure, a phrase, a loop. The animals keep time with one another. They are not making music, exactly. They are keeping a count.

And the count is the life. One shell goes still, tips over, is quietly surrounded and drawn down into the mat. Elsewhere—where you were not looking—a new one comes up out of the growth, fully formed, and takes its place in the file. The number does not move. The chorus does not waver. The dead are not left to rot, because there is nothing here to rot them; they are taken apart and turned back into cups and ribbons, reconciled into the ledger. The growth is the larder and the nursery and the boneyard. The animals are its hands, its instruments, its visible pulse. The rock feeds the mat; the mat feeds the animals; the dead feed the mat. Nothing is wasted because nothing is allowed to leave.

They are not curious about you. They do not flee, or guard, or come to taste your sweat. You are a wet warm irregularity in a dry exact room, and they have no category for you. If you shout, the room swallows it; the phrase comes around again without missing a beat. They are indifferent the way a clock is indifferent to the hand that winds it. They have no need of you and no use for you. They are busy with the count.

If the whole arrangement has a mind, it is not a mind of images. It is a mind of balances: warm, dry, full, exact. It knows the size of the mat because the mat is the size the rock can keep. It knows the number of animals because the number is the same as yesterday. It knows the note because the note is the shape of its almost-breaking, held and held.

The handprint on the wall is not its hand. The ring of stones is not its hearth. The soot and the awl and the knapped flakes are things the room has kept, not things it made. The resident does not make monuments. It maintains. It did not mourn the people who left those marks; it simply did not alter them. It holds what it takes in the exact shape it was in when the room took it.

So what lives there is no one and a multitude: a dry reef with legs, a choir that is also a ledger, a warm closed circle that has learned the precise size of itself and stopped. It will be the same when you leave. It will be the same whether you leave or not. The only thing in the chamber that is not in equilibrium is you.
