What lives here does not build, not in the way we understand it. It grows, it accretes, it *becomes*. The inhabitants of this drowned world are not separate from the forest; they are its most complex organs.

The first and most profound presence is the **Lattice**. This is not a single creature but the name for the vast, interconnected root-and-nerve system that permeates the entire plateau. The immense "trees" are its pillars, the clinging mosses are its capillaries, and the springy substrate is its flesh. The Lattice is a single, slow-thinking entity, a planet-wide mind woven from a trillion fungal threads and woody fibers. It does not think in words or images but in chemistry—tides of nutrients, subtle shifts in pressure, the slow pulse of decay and growth. Its "thoughts" are geological, unfolding over centuries. To touch one of the great trunks is to touch the Lattice's skin; to uproot one is to cause a deep, resonant pain that ripples through the whole being.

Moving through the Lattice's body are its mobile symbiotes, the **Weavers**. These creatures are vaguely decapod, like immense, elongated crabs, but their limbs are not for crushing. Each of their six legs ends in a delicate, multi-jointed brush of sensory filaments. Their bodies are encased in a carapace of fused organic matter—driftwood, hardened frond-fiber, and living coral that grows in patterns across their backs, providing camouflage. They are the gardeners and the surgeons. They do not eat the Lattice but prune it, pollinating its reproductive sacs and removing diseased growths. They communicate with the Lattice through touch, their filament-brushes reading its chemical state and, in turn, massaging enzymes and hormones into its tissues to soothe or stimulate it. A Weaverbound loses its own identity in this service; its individual "mind" is a small, silent node within the Lattice's greater consciousness.

Then there are the **Gloam**. These are the creatures of the open water between the towering fronds. They are descended from something like a jellyfish or a siphonophore, but evolved into a form of communal, drifting intelligence. A Gloam is a colony of thousands of zooids, each no larger than a finger, linked into a ribbon-like, translucent sheet. They are the color of the deep water itself, a living, shivering lens. They are the chief pollinators of the great vertical "trees," drifting through the canopy in slow, undulating curtains, catching and carrying the luminous spores that drift from the fronds. When threatened, the colony can focus its collective bioluminescence into a single, blinding pulse of light—a silent scream that illuminates the forest for a mile in every direction.

And finally, in the deepest valleys and most shadowed crevices, there are the **Mimics**. They are not a species but a phenomenon—a form of parasitic, intelligent life that has learned to wear the world as a costume. A Mimic is a shapeless, protoplasmic mass until it finds a subject. It will envelop a dead Weaverbound, a fallen branch, a cluster of gas-sacs, and then harden its outer layer into a perfect, functional duplicate. For a time, it *is* that thing, moving and behaving as the original would, all the while slowly digesting it. They are the ghosts of the forest, wearing the faces of its residents. The Lattice, through the Weavers, wages a slow, endless war of attrition against them, pruning the infected and burning the imitations with focused pulses of caustic sap.

These are the lives here: a dreaming, continental mind; its devoted, crustacean hands; its drifting, luminous pollinators; and its constant, mimic adversary. They are all part of one body, a single, thrashing ecosystem where the line between self and world is not a border but a membrane, and where to live is to be endlessly, inextricably woven into the whole.
