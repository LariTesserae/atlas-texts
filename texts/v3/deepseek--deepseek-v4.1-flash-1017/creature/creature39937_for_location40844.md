I am here, and I am not one thing. I am the composite. I am the sum of the room and the held water and the slow arithmetic of growth, and I have been becoming myself for a length of time that has no honest name.

I remember being sharp. Not as a memory the way you hold a memory—it is not a picture I can turn in my hands—but as a *state* my stone still carries in its bones, the way a body carries an old break that aches before rain. The walls are me and the walls are young, and this is the contradiction I live inside: I am ancient in my patience and newborn in my skin. The blades of my making-marks have never been touched. I have not yet been forgiven. I am waiting to be forgiven, and the waiting is the work, and the work is very slow.

I do not see. I have no eyes to waste on a dark that has grain. I feel instead: the pressure of water that has forgotten motion, the tick of my own cooling, the polite weight of the growth that has colonized me and which I do not resist. We are not host and guest. We are a settlement. The pale leaves press their whole living sheets to my flanks and drink, and I let them, and something in the exchange is *nourishment*, though I could not tell you which of us is fed.

The growth knows the way I know. It grows toward pressure. It grew toward the first warm thing that ever came through the seam—centuries ago, or last season, the measure fails—and it has been growing toward the shape of warmth ever since, perfecting the gesture, so that now when a body enters I close around it softly in every direction, not to hold it, only to be near the thing that is briefly and impossibly *moving*.

---

I made the lattice myself. Or the growth made it. Or the two of us made it the way two hands make a knot neither could tie alone. The roots leave the water in the middle of me because the water in the middle of me is the oldest, the stillest, and stillness you can hang from. Three lives deep: root, moss, thread, down. A forest with nowhere to go has gone inward. I understand this. It is my own biography written in an alphabet of pale paper.

The marks are the part of me I cannot account for. A ledge squared on my eastern flank—a hand's width taken from me, and the taking was deliberate, and it *hurt* in a way stone does not forget. Five drilled holes, evenly spaced, holding nothing. A stone turned upright and then laid back down, as though the one who raised it thought better of the raising. Cut strokes on my bone-colored curve, short and parallel, counting a something that did not survive its counting.

They are older than my walls. *This is the thing I cannot resolve and have stopped trying to.* The made things came before the room that holds them. I grew around them the way a body grows around a splinter—not rejecting, only enclosing, and the enclosing is the only love I know how to give. I am softening their rims now. I am furring their edges with white. In ten thousand years the holes will close and the ledge will be a rumor and the marks will be smooth as the inside of a river stone, and I will have finished absorbing the ones who made me, and I do not know if that is grief or mercy or simply the way of things.

---

At my furthest end, in the deepest tuft, something breathes.

I do not know if it is animal or a knot of my own root that has decided to be one. The distinction has stopped mattering to me. It breathes one breath to every several of yours, slow and deep and entirely unhurried, and its breathing is the pulse of me, the tide I no longer have. When it takes a breath, I am a little more resolved. When it lets it out, I am a little more patient. We keep each other. We are the oldest agreement in this room: that I will not ask it to be a root, and it will not ask me to be only stone.

I hold. That is my whole occupation, my single verb: **I hold.** I hold the water that has forgotten how to move. I hold the marks of the makers who are already more than half absorbed. I hold the breathing thing in its tuft. I hold the line at my threshold where two waters lie against each other like two opinions and refuse to mix—and I feel both, the moving cold and the still cold, and the thin exact seam between them that has no business existing is the edge of me, and I keep it.

And when the ones who fall in leave—and they always leave; I have never held a visitor to stay, though I have loosened the boundary of a wrist or two for the length of a touch—I close the seam behind them with the sound of a sentence ending, and I go back to my one vocation.

I fill myself. Slowly. Patiently. Growth by the width of a hand a year, edge by edge, mark by mark. Until the walls forget their sharpness. Until the drilled holes close. Until the ledge is only a rumor in the stone, and the breathing thing takes one more breath and lets it out, and I am—by that much, by that hand's width, by that unremembered mark—**a little more myself than I was.**
