There is no *who*. The whatever is the fleece—and the fleece is not an animal, not a plant, not a fungus, not a colony in any way you could point to and say *there, that one*. It is one continuous body of dry mineral hair, chapel-sized, with no face and no mouth and no limbs. Its body is the pelt: a meter thick on the walls, hanging in curtains, matted where it meets the floor, splaying and rising again. Each strand is a chain of stiff segments, chalk-white to bone to tarnished straw, built grain by grain like shell and abraded away at the same rate. It is soft the way raw wool is soft, or the inside of a very old mattress, or the fur of something long dead that has gone to mineral. It crumbles faintly at the edges. It sheds a pale meal that the air will not let settle.

It lives by combing the drawn wind. The wind comes up the face carrying salt-bright crystalline dust, and the pelt holds it, takes a fraction of it apart, adds that fraction to itself, lets the rest go. It also holds charge: a blue-white standing glimmer at the tips, a faint repulsion wherever two curtains meet, a tautness running through the whole mass. Put your hand in and the fleece leans away from your skin. Disturb it and the chord wavers—then the pelt closes over the disturbance exactly as it was, and the sums go back to zero. It has no hunger you would recognize and no thirst at all. Its attention is electrostatic. Its memory is the fixed density of its own mat. Its voice is the note the wind makes through it.

It is not alone. Halfway back, under the deepest curtain, the old cylinder turns. Riveted plate, orange seams gone brown, ribs, bolted hatch, louvered intakes packed solid with fleece at precisely the right resistance. Something inside rotates with the slow heaviness of a fundamental. It has no face either. It is not clear whether it is a machine the fleece has adopted or a heart the fleece has grown around, and it is not clear which of them is the apprentice. They do the same work: take the dust, hold the note, keep the equilibrium. The cylinder is louder and older; the pelt is more widespread and more patient. Together they are the resident.

So if you ask who lives there, there is no who. If you ask what, it is a dry, charged, air-fed pelt and a turning cylinder, coupled so tightly that the room is their body and the chord is their breathing. Nothing else lives there—no insect in the fleece, no bird in the slot of sky, no mite in the dust. Nothing eats. Nothing hides. The abundance is total, and it has no use for water, which is why it would let you die of thirst in the middle of it without cruelty. It is not hostile. It is simply occupied by something that has solved the problem of living here by becoming the solution, and holding it.
