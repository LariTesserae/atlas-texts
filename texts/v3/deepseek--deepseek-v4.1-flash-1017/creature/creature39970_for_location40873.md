The Level is inhabited the way a held note is inhabited by its own fading: not by a population you can count, but by an arrangement that has learned to keep itself. The bodies are there, but they are not the first thing. First comes the scarcity, the negotiated gaps, the low walls that close on nothing, the holes warm at the rim. The people who live there are the slow agents of that arrangement.

They are not builders. Building implies a gap between the maker and the made, and they have closed that gap the way a body closes a wound. Their walls are postures held for generations. Their terraces are social distances cut into stone—four or five steps, each lip rounded by use. Their holes are not storage and not traps; they are drilled in rows and spirals, always the same diameter, smooth inside, warm, and they seem to have been made by a mouth or a hardened lip turning patiently for years. When you put your palm over one, you feel the faint residual heat of a body that was there, or a body that still is, pressed close on the other side of the rock.

The inhabitants themselves are hard to see because they have the good manners of the place. Their skin is the color of the rock beneath its crust—rust-olive, bruised green, a dark that takes the amber light and gives nothing back. It is warm to the touch and softly granular, like the inside of an old shell. They have no clean front. They have a side that pays attention, a broad paler patch that tightens when you are near, a seam that opens to take in water and closes again. No eyes. Something that functions like attention. When they are still, they are outcrops. When they move, they are the water deciding.

They do not swim so much as arrive. Their limbs—six or eight flexible spurs, not quite arms, not quite roots—press into the sediment and pull, and their progress is slower than a slow thought. They can hold a position for hours. They can hold it for days. Their pulse is so faint that you only find it by accident, and then you are not sure whether you are feeling them or the plate. They are warm all the way through, body-warm, blood-warm, the same temperature as the water, so that the boundary between them and the Level is a courtesy rather than a fact.

They keep no tools because they are their own tools, or because their tools were made of something that has fully returned—a secretion, a shell, a shed part that the meadow took back. The only durable things they leave are negative: holes, sockets, the hollow curve of a wall. The sockets are empty in a way that feels polite rather than looted. Whatever was set there was removed, not stolen. The removal was part of the making.

They speak in pressure. A low sub-audible swell behind the sternum, a texture in the quiet that thickens and thins. A conversation can last a season. They do not approach and they do not flee; they have outlived the need for either. Instead they alter the quiet. They make room, or they decline to. Their scarcity is load-bearing. You feel it as a social pressure, a room where everyone has already agreed how loud to be. The drifting bell and the long breathing ridge are not their livestock. They are neighbors in the same agreement, citizens of the same hush.

And they are not immune to the place. That is the thing you must understand. The Level’s final form reaches out and settles them too. Their oldest are not elders; they are landforms. You can tell one that has gone too far into the flatness by the wrongness of the silhouette—a cushion where a cushion should not be, a ring with a body’s proportions, a low wall that closes around something the shape of a person who stopped deciding. They do not die so much as settle. The meadow accepts them. The water remembers their loops.

If you meet one, it will not ask you to leave. It will not ask you to stay. It will hold position and let the quiet do the talking. After a while you will understand that it is not waiting for you. It is waiting the way the plate waits—for a direction to stop being a question. And you will either swim on, or become part of the arrangement.
