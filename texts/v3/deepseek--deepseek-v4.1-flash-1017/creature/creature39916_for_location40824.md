Ask it plainly — *what lives here* — and the question frays in the hand, because the pocket does not keep the boundary that "lives" requires. It does not sharply distinguish a thing being made from a thing being alive, so the honest answer has to be given in layers, and each layer is a different kind of tenancy.

**The unfinished, who are the majority.**

They live here by not being finished. That is not a misfortune they are enduring; it is their lease. The mites the colour of the stone hold their ground in the seams of new mineral, and they move only when the wall moves, so that they seem less like animals on a surface than like a pattern the surface is trying out. The pale isopods walk their slow deliberate arcs and stop for long stretches — and they stop *because* they are hardening, because the shell is still soft and the walking is expensive. A touched one leaves a smear, which means it lives in the condition of a thing that has not yet decided what its outside is. The too-many-winged fliers lift heavily and land badly: they live in the interval before a body learns its own grammar. The threads in the water film move with purpose and no discernible anatomy of purpose. The segmented creature in the crevice comes out every few minutes, tastes the air with its fringe of feelers, and goes back, like a tide that has nowhere to be.

Two of them are the same animal at different stages of deciding what it is, and they keep their distance from one another, as though each found the other's company embarrassing. And there are the ones with an extra segment and the ones with a missing one, and nothing about them is sick. They are not dying of their conditions. They are simply resident in the before.

The rule seems to be this: a thing that finished would have to leave, or would become old stone and be unmade. So nothing finishes. The creatures that will never pupate are not failures; they are the ones who understood the terms.

**The one in the hollow.**

In the corner by the wall there is a wear in the floor, shallow and smooth, the size of a person sitting, filled to a centimetre with water. Something pale lives in that water. It is the smallest tenancy and the strangest, because it lives inside a shape made by a body that is not there — it has taken up residence in someone's posture. The hollow holds water; the water holds the pale thing; the pale thing holds nothing and goes about its business in a centimetre of depth as though the whole room were not leaning over it. You could say it lives in the pocket. More accurately, it lives in the pocket's memory of a person.

**The absent, who have not entirely left.**

Someone scratched a bent-finger form on the driest wall — four, a gap, two more — and the scratches have half-healed with new mineral in a way that could be very old or very new. Someone stacked seven flat stones into a low wall a hand high, the only straight line in the room, holding nothing back, marking nothing visible. Someone sat where the hollow is, long and often enough to wear the floor. These are residents in the way a claim is a resident. The pocket keeps them the way it keeps everything: imperfectly, correcting toward them. The stacker may be nine hundred years gone or may be standing behind you at this moment with another flat stone, and there is no way to tell, because the pocket does not distinguish sharply between an old thing and a new thing either.

**The pocket itself.**

And then there is the tenant who is also the tenement. The room is not a container that happens to be occupied; it is occupied in the way a body is occupied by itself. It lives by revising. It has a memory of what it is and keeps correcting toward that memory imperfectly, which is why a lobe of stone is not where you left it and the shelf has grown to meet where it went, and why a step that crossed the room in the morning crosses less than half of it by afternoon — not larger, only more itself. It lives, if the word holds, by brightening when things are born and dimming very slightly when they stop, so that the light pulses at the edge of vision and the shadows never quite keep up.

The damp is its breath. The light is its warmth. The mineral is its tissue, soft in places, ringing in others, filling in a fingertip dent over an hour the way a body closes a small wound without being asked.

**And you, for as long as you are here.**

You are the newest tenant and the least permanent, and you are already being revised. The room will keep a version of you the way it keeps the bent-finger marks and the seven stones and the wear in the floor. You will stand in the quiet and think a few minutes have passed and find the hollow has evaporated and refilled twice. You will find your own breath has been going on for a very long while. The stooping you do in the low places will become, briefly, one of the shapes the ceiling remembers.

So: whoever lives there is whoever is still becoming, and the pocket is among them, and it has not decided yet what it is — the hill above it has not decided either, and the seam breathes, and the light comes slightly from the left and drifts, and the whole crowded bright unfinished tenement keeps its own slow counsel, under a hill that has not finished being a hill.
