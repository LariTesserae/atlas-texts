# The Dwellers of the Warm Engine

## The Drift

They are not born so much as *condensed*. The Drift begin as a thickening in the water, a local density where the milky soup of the basin gathers itself into something that remembers having a shape. No one has witnessed the moment of quickening. The water simply becomes *more* in one place, and then it moves with purpose.

They are colonies, not individuals. A single Drift might span the length of a drowned bus, its body a translucent membrane stretched between knotty nodes of calcified tissue. Inside, you can see the water passing through them—not filtered, not consumed, but *processed*. The Drift are living pumps, and their interiors glow faintly with the bioluminescence of the symbiotic algae they cultivate in their gut-cavities. They drift with the churning currents, extending fine, hair-like tendrils to taste the chemical gradients, to feel the pressure waves of the Engine's distant clanging.

They are not intelligent in any way a human would recognize. But they are *aware*. When a Drift colony passes near the boiling nimbus of a live electrical cable, its tendrils retract in slow, coordinated waves, like the flinch of a sea anemone. When the great pistons pound, the Drift orient themselves toward the sound, their membranes rippling in sympathy with the rhythm. They are the closest thing this world has to grazing animals—filter-feeders of heat, sound, and dissolved organic matter.

They reproduce by *fission of attention*. When a Drift's body grows too large, too diffuse, it splits not into halves but into *fragments of behavior*. One piece might retain the memory of the northern current; another might inherit the habit of feeding near the great rusted tower. The new colonies are born without parents, without lineage—only with a set of instincts inherited from the water itself.

## The Verdant Mechanists

The vegetation here is not passive. It *listens*.

The coral-thicketed plants that dominate the basin are not a single species but a symbiotic network of at least a dozen. Their root-cables snake through the muck and find their way into the shattered machinery. They are drawn to heat. A vent that has been scalding for a thousand years will be wrapped in thick, pulsing vines that funnel the steam through their hollow interiors, using the pressure to circulate sap. A pump that still shudders with ancient life will be colonized by sac-plants that feed on its vibration, their membranes stretching and contracting in rhythm with the machine's futile labor.

The plants do not merely tolerate the Engine. They are *complicit* in it. They have learned to exploit every leak, every hiss, every catastrophic energy discharge. The blue-lit cable that boils the water around it? Its base is a garden of phosphorescent fungi that feed on the stray current, glowing brighter when the arc is stronger, dimming when it weakens. The fungi are the plants' early-warning system—they communicate the cable's state to the surrounding vegetation through a network of bioluminescent pulses.

There is a plant that hunts. It is a balloon-sac, tethered by a root-cable to the rusted hulk of a turbine. It drifts in the current, its membrane thin and inviting, its interior warm and dark. It emits a low, sweet hum, a frequency that resonates with the Drift's bodily rhythms. When a Drift colony drifts too close, the sac contracts—*snaps*—a hundred tiny sphincters closing along its surface, sealing the Drift inside. The sac then slowly digests it over the course of a week, its interior glowing brighter and brighter as it feeds on the bioluminescent algae of its prey.

The plants are the closest thing this world has to predators. They are patient. They have all the time in the world.

## The Echoes

There are things that were once human.

Not alive, not dead—*caught*. The Engine does not kill; it *preserves*. A worker who fell into the coolant channel a thousand years ago might still be there, preserved in a matrix of scalding, mineral-rich fluid, their body slowly calcifying into something that resembles the coral-vegetation. Their eyes are open. Their mouth is frozen in a scream that the water carries as a faint, high-frequency tone.

These are the Echoes. They are not ghosts, not spirits—they are biological artifacts, bodies that the water has *remembered* into permanence. They drift in the deeper channels, where the light is dimmest, their skin the color of old ivory, their hair still moving in the current. They do not decay. The bacteria that would consume them have themselves been consumed by the water's peculiar chemistry.

The Echoes do not move on their own. But sometimes, when the great unseen piston strikes, the water carries the shockwave through their bodies, and they *twitch*. A hand might curl. An eyelid might flutter. The Drift avoid them. The plants grow over them, sometimes forming a perfect outline of a face in the moss.

They are the only thing in this world that is still, and their stillness is the most disturbing thing of all.

## The Bunker-Keeper

In the concrete bunker, half-sunk in the mire, something lives.

It is not human. It might have been, once. Now it is something else—a slow, patient thing that has learned to survive in the silence. It has no name. It does not think in words. It thinks in *tasks*.

The Bunker-Keeper tends the bunker. It sweeps the floor with a broom made of dried vines. It patches the cracks in the walls with mud and moss. It has arranged the interior with a careful, obsessive symmetry: a rusted cot, a metal shelf, a single, intact lightbulb that has not worked for centuries. The Keeper polishes the lightbulb every day with a rag made of its own shed skin.

The Keeper does not leave the bunker. Outside is chaos, sound, *too much*. Inside is quiet. Inside is *manageable*. The Keeper has learned to read the vibrations in the floor, to predict the rhythm of the Engine's clanging. It knows when the great piston will strike. It knows when the electrical cable will flare. It knows the patterns of the Drift, the flowering of the sac-plants, the slow drift of the Echoes.

The Keeper is lonely, but it does not know the word for loneliness. It knows only that sometimes, when the silence outside is particularly deep, it stands in the doorway and looks out at the churning, beautiful nightmare. It watches the lights. It listens to the scream. And then it turns back inside, closes the door, and polishes the lightbulb again.

It has been doing this for a very long time. It will do this for a very long time to come.

## The Pulse

There is something else. Something larger.

The water itself is a mind, but the mind has a *heart*. Deep in the basin, where the Engine's largest remnant lies—a vast, tilted sphere of corroded metal, half-buried in sediment—there is a pulse. It is slow, slower than the thrum of the water, slower than the clang of the piston. It is a rhythm that takes hours to complete a single beat.

The plants orient toward it. The Drift drift in its orbit. The Echoes twitch in its rhythm. The Bunker-Keeper feels it through the floor and does not know what it is.

The sphere is not alive. It is not dead. It is *running*. It is the last, greatest piece of the Engine, and it is doing something—something that requires energy, something that requires patience, something that has been happening for a thousand years and will continue for a thousand more.

No one knows what it is doing. Perhaps it is thinking. Perhaps it is dreaming. Perhaps it is *waiting*.

The inhabitants of the Warm Engine do not ask. They simply live in its shadow, in its rhythm, in its deafening, ceaseless, beautiful noise. They are the children of a catastrophe that never ended, and they have learned to call it home.
