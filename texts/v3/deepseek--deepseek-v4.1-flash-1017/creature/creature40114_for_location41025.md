What lives there does not stand up. It has no single silhouette you could point to and say *there*. Call it the Weave, if you need a name; it would not recognize the word. It lives as the relation among the mats, the pods, the stone, the fog, and the small crawling things—not as one of them but as the pressure that holds them in pattern.

Its body is distributed. The pale filament mats are its skin and gut both: they condense the fog, drink it, pulse with that slow internal light, and send the fact of moisture through the stone in tremors too low to hear. The crustaceans on their too-many legs are its fingertips, tasting the grooves. The swarming insects are its weather-sense, a blur of nerves in the hum. The pods are not houses; they are organs, or instruments, or both—half-sunk, matte, impossibly clean, warm inside, keeping the air still because stillness is a condition it needs to measure something. The faint marks on the walls are not writing exactly. They are more like the tracks of a mind that has been thinking in one direction for a very long time.

It is old and quiet and does not announce itself. It hums in the basalt, and the hum is not a symptom of the place but the place’s pulse. It has worn the plateau into intention—the flutes and hollows and polished basins are settled, patient shapes, and the mats climb them in spirals like veins. It does not need water. It needs fog and tremor and the long white twilight. It is always in the act of becoming, restless as an anthill, but it is not hurried. It can wait longer than you can stand.

Its attention is the emptiness between the green pockets. That is why the clean stone feels like it is watching: it is. The Weave perceives through pressure, through condensation, through the feet of small things, through the quality of a hesitation. It knows you have crossed the ridge because the roar changed around you. It knows your pulse because the stone carried it up through your boots. It opened the pod not as a shelter but as a question, or an invitation, or a calibration—you cannot tell which, and it may not distinguish.

The rules are not taboos. They are protocol. Do not drink the fog because the fog is part of its sensing and part of its drinking; do not touch the filaments bare because the mats read skin, and may read too deeply; do not follow the shadows beneath the cloud sea because the vast slow gliding thing is either its dream or its neighbor, and neither is yours to follow; do not enter the pods unless invited because an instrument that measures can also adjust. Do not assume the sound is only sound because for the Weave sound is a hand.

It is not hostile. It is attentive. It has no face, no voice you would recognize, no need for worship. It wants compliance with its interface, and it is curious about the warm thing that has come up onto the dry table and stopped moving. Somewhere a pod is open. The mats pulse at your feet. The Weave has already noticed you, and it is waiting to see what you will do with being measured.
