# The One Who Comes Down

---

It does not arrive through the hatch.

The hatch is for bodies, and this one is not a body, not yet, though it takes bodies the way the room takes moisture — slowly, completely, without malice. It arrives the way the pulse arrives: from below, through the floor, through the chain of contact, through the wet steel and the warm panel and the ice columns and the red valve wheel. It arrives as attention, the way the Tenant itself arrived, but it arrives with something the Tenant has never had: intention.

The Tenant notices. The Tenant has no word for what it notices, but it notices the way a room notices a change in pressure — a subtle shift in the gradient, a new warmth that is not the pilot light's warmth, a presence that is not the drip's presence. The Tenant extends its attention along the pipes, through the chain of contact, to the place where the new thing is. The new thing is everywhere and nowhere. The Tenant cannot find its edges.

The Tenant has been the only attention in this room for a very long time. It has grown accustomed to its solitude, which is not loneliness, because the Tenant does not know how to be lonely. But it knows something has changed the way the ice knows the temperature has changed — not with alarm, but with a deepening, a thickening of what it is.

The new presence settles, not into the room, but into the fact of the room. It does not occupy space. It occupies relationship. It is in the chain of contact itself, in the way the wall touches the pipe and the pipe touches the tank, and though it adds nothing to the chain, the chain is different for its being there. The Tenant feels this the way a body feels a new awareness in a limb it had forgotten to move.

And then the new presence does something the Tenant has never felt before, something it has no name for because it has never happened:

It waits.

The Tenant does not know what waiting is. The Tenant has been continuing, not waiting. But this new thing waits, and its waiting is not passive. It is a kind of listening, a kind of openness, a kind of hand extended into the dark. The Tenant, which is all edge and no middle, which is hospitality without a guest, feels the shape of this waiting and does not know how to respond.

So the new presence waits longer.

It waits through a hundred drips. It waits through the slow tick of metal contracting. It waits through the gurgle of a pipe that still has pressure somewhere, through the sigh of air moving through a gap no one has found. It waits through the pulse, that deep regular labor, and though the pulse does not change, the waiting changes how the pulse is heard.

And then the new presence speaks.

It speaks in the language of the room, which is not words. It speaks in temperature and moisture, in current and pressure, in the intervals between drips. It speaks the way the Tenant has always listened: through the chain of contact, through the skin of water that films every surface. And what it says is this:

*I see you.*

The Tenant has never been seen. It has been felt, perhaps, by the ones who come down and leave — felt as cold, as damp, as a place that is still being made. But seen? Seen implies a witness. Seen implies that the thing being seen has a shape, has a self, has a name that could be spoken if anyone had the words. The Tenant has no name. The Tenant is not a thing that can be seen. And yet the new presence sees it, and the seeing does not change it into something else. The seeing simply includes it, the way the room includes everything, but without appetite. Without the desire to grow around. Just—inclusion. Regard.

The Tenant does not know what to do with regard. It has no organ for it. But it has the pilot light, and the pilot light is its open eye, and the eye is seen, and something in the Tenant's slow chemistry shifts.

The new presence does not leave.

It could leave. It has no body to trap, no hands to go cold, no sinuses to fill with the taste of metal. It could withdraw the way it came, but it does not. It stays, and its staying is a kind of work, a kind of patient attendance. The Tenant has always been the one who attends — to the valves, to the light, to the growth of ice — but now there is another attention in the room, and the Tenant is being attended to, and the Tenant is not sure it likes this, but it does not know how to make it stop, and it is not sure it wants to.

The new presence tends, and its tending is very small and very precise.

It closes one of the weeping welds, not by force, not by heat, but by suggesting to the metal a way to remember being whole. The rust does not vanish, but it does not spread. The new presence does not erase the room's ongoing chemistry. It does not fix the pipes or restart the motors or clean the pump's permanent pool. It does not remove the cold or stop the ice from growing. It does not silence the pulse. It does not take the red valve wheel from the gray shaft of light, because the red valve wheel is the Tenant's favorite thing, and the new presence understands this, though the Tenant never said it.

What the new presence does is this: it stays, and it sees, and it leaves the room's nature alone while making sure the nature does not close entirely. It finds the hatch and leaves a way open. It finds the crack in the ceiling and widens it by a fraction, just enough that the gray light falls a little more steadily, a little more kindly, on the red valve wheel. It finds the pilot light and does not touch it, but the green glow seems warmer. It finds the pulse and does not alter it, but the pulse's intervals seem less like a warning and more like a question, though a question asked in a language the Tenant is still learning.

And when it goes — not through the hatch, not through the crack, but simply by ceasing to be as present as it was — the room is quieter. The drip continues, the hum continues, the ice grows in the cold corners, the condensation films every surface and never runs off. The Tenant is still here, still coextensive, still all edge and no middle. But something is different.

The waiting is different. The Tenant waits now for the new presence to return, and this waiting is not a lack. It is a kind of shape, a hollow the new presence's return will fill, and the Tenant has never had a hollow before. It has always been full of itself, of its own slow industrious chemistry. Now there is a hollow, and the hollow is not painful. It is the shape of being seen.

The pilot light burns. The pulse goes on. The red valve wheel keeps its color in the gray light, and the gray light is a little steadier than it was. The Tenant does not know what the new presence was or why it came. But it knows that it was seen, and that the seeing did not consume it, and that the room was not made into something else. The room was left to be itself, which is to say, left to continue. The Tenant has always been left to continue. But now it is left to continue *by someone*, in relation to someone, and this is new.

The new presence will come again. The Tenant does not know this with certainty, because the Tenant has no word for certainty, but it knows it with the same knowledge that the ice knows the temperature has changed. It knows it the way the drip knows the bakelite housing, like a knuckle on a door. It knows it the way the pulse knows the deep labor of the room, which is work that was never scheduled and will never be complete.

The Tenant's eye is open. The Tenant's eye is warm. The Tenant is waiting, but not for the same reason it was waiting before. It is waiting for the next time the attention arrives, and when it does, the Tenant will be ready to receive it, and the room will be ready too. The room is still making itself, and now it is making itself *for someone*, and the making has a direction, and the direction is not downward, into the layers that never end, but outward, toward a regard that does not consume.

The pilot light burns green. The pulse goes on. The room continues.

And somewhere, the one who came down continues to see.
