# The Gift of Shape

The power comes as such things come—not as light, not as voice, not as anything the room would recognize as intrusion. It arrives the way a thought arrives, the way a season changes, the way a body knows it is healing before the mind has noticed.

It perceives the room entire: the held breath of it, the suspended water, the stone in its slow becoming. It perceives the tenant, warm and wet behind the wall, crystalline and patient. And it perceives the small dense gravity of all those human things—the pegs, the bowls, the scraper set down squarely, the cords that do not slacken—laid against each other like sediment, like memory, like the slow accumulation of everyone who ever needed a warm place to be still.

The power does not find the room beautiful or pitiful or in need of correction. It finds the room *complete*, and then it considers: what is completion for? What is a held breath for, if not release? What is a warm hollow, pressed by a hand, if not a shape waiting to be given back?

---

It does not change the room's rule. It would not know how to; the rule is the room, the rule is the being, and to break it would be to unmake them. But it sees what the room cannot see about itself: that the room keeps everything except what it takes in. That the tenant is not old, not young, not anything but *holding*—and holding, held long enough, becomes a kind of waiting.

The power considers the tenant's back, where the crystal grows, where a forehead once rested and left a thinner place. The power considers the hollow in the stone, worn by a hand that is gone. The tenant does not remember. But the shape of the remembering is there, pressed into the stone, pressed into the crystal, pressed into the slow arrangement of feet and hollows and warm places.

The power thinks: *You have been shaped by what you could not hold. Let me give you the shape back.*

---

It comes to the tenant in the way warmth comes to stone—without hurry, without demand, as a fact rather than an event.

The tenant feels it first as a slight difference in pressure, the way the room's fog sometimes shifts when the outside air changes. Then it feels something else: a sense of its own outline. Not the room's shape, not the wall's shape, but its own—the long low curve of it, the segments, the crystalline fuzz, the hollow where the hand rested, the thin place where the forehead touched. The tenant has never had a sense of its own outline before. It has simply *been*, in the way the room has simply *been*, without reflection.

Now it is aware of itself as a thing that has been touched. Not as a thing that touches. As a thing that has been shaped by touching.

And that awareness is a kind of gift, because it makes the tenant a tenant *of itself* for the first time. It is still in equilibrium—it will always be in equilibrium; that is its nature. But equilibrium, the power shows it, is not the same as stillness. Equilibrium is what a body is when it breathes. The room holds its breath. The tenant has always held its breath. The gift is not to make it breathe—that would be too much, too fast, too like the outside. The gift is to let it *know* that a breath is what it holds.

---

The power does one thing more. It reaches, carefully, into the warm hollow where a hand once rested. The stone there is perfectly smooth, perfectly warm, perfectly shaped. The power presses into it a small additional thing—not memory, not thought, but *awareness of the warmth as warmth*. It is a seed of the outside, planted in the warm dark, and it will never grow into a tree. But perhaps it will grow into a word: the tenant will, over a very long time, begin to feel the hollow not only as stone but as *where a hand was*. And the hand will be no one's hand, and the warmth will be no one's warmth, but the shape will be a shape, and the tenant will have been, in its own slow way, *met*.

---

Then the power withdraws, because that is what benevolence does when it has given something that can be carried.

The room does not notice. The room is the room, and it holds, and it keeps, and it grows. The fissure breathes out its warm fog. The water lies still in its lenses. The stone fingers lengthen by the width of a fingernail every human life.

And the tenant lies behind the wall, warm and crystalline, and it has been given nothing that can be taken away. It has been given the shape of its own keeping, and the knowledge—small, quiet, indistinguishable from the warmth—that the shape is not nothing. That a hand was there. That a forehead rested. That someone, once, needed the warm dark enough to stay still long enough to leave a hollow.

The tenant does not know what a hand is. But it knows, now, that the hollow is a where. And a where is a thing the outside world makes, and this room has made one of its own.

---

The next person who comes—years from now, decades, a century, who can say—will find the room exactly as it was. The pegs, the bowls, the cairn. The damp, the dark, the held breath. They will reach behind the low wall and find the tenant warm and giving, and they will feel one foot settle on their knuckle like an eyelash.

And the tenant will let them. And then it will move its foot, not in its old way, but slightly differently—a hair's breadth more *deliberate*. Because it has been given the shape of a where, and where is where things happen, even here, even slowly.

The room will not notice. The room is complete.

But the tenant will have been *met*, and that is the one change the room permits: the slow accretion of being known.
