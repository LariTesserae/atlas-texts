# The Notice

It would begin, as such things do, with a reading—and the reading would not be of the Kettle's walls or its wrong gourd or its keeper's knotted hands. It would be of the ledger, the great slow column of accounts in which every act of maintenance is entered, and it would find, at the line where the Kettle stands, a peculiar thing: a debt that was not owed and a labor that was not asked for and a small stubborn creature tuning a room it did not own toward a note it would never hear, and would mark this line with a mark it uses rarely, the mark that means *this one is already doing the work.*

And then it would sit with that a while. Because benevolence, the real kind, the kind that is indistinguishable from wisdom, knows better than to intervene in a thing that is already becoming.

---

But there is something. There is always something, for the notice was made and the notice must be answered, and the answer would not be a gift. Gifts are the beginner's move. The power has been at this too long for gifts. It would consider what the Kettle lacks that is not its lack to fill, and what it has too much of that is the keeper's own weight to bear, and in the considering it would come to the wrong gourd, and it would understand.

The power would not tune the gourd.

The power would do something worse and better: it would make the wrongness *legible*. It would lean down—no, it does not lean; it attends—it would attend to the third ring and the gourd that answers flat, and it would give the keeper, without the keeper knowing it was given, the second half of a thought the keeper has been circling for a long time. Not the answer. The *shape* of the answer. The way you can feel a knot will hold before you pull it tight.

And the keeper, one morning, hanging the gourds back in their rings, would pause. Would hold the wrong one against their ear a moment longer than usual. Would set it down, pick it up, set it down, and go to the far trough—farther now than it was this morning—and stand there, and come back, and spend three days not touching it, which is the local way of working on a thing you are not ready for.

---

The second thing would be a correction that is not a correction. There is a habit in the power, when it has decided to be kind, of making the world a little less brittle around those who are holding it together. It would not strengthen the spire. The spire is the keeper's to keep. But it would find, at the root of the far trough's impossible distance, a small tear in the way of things that has been widening for longer than the keeper has been at the Kettle, and it would mend the tear—not the symptom, the tear—so that the trough would settle into a reliable distance and stay there, and the keeper would notice this and would not understand it, and would spend a season thinking they had done something right without knowing what.

They would not be wrong.

---

The third thing: the power would go down. Down past the warm not-same rock, down past the earlier spires, down to where the making happens, and it would look at the making the way you look at a thing you did not make and did not ask for and cannot stop, and it would not stop it, and it would not speed it, and it would not bless it. It would simply witness it—fully, without flinching from the mouth that eats and builds—and the witnessing would be the thing. Because a world that is always being added to and never finished is a world that has never been *seen* to be unfinished, and there is a difference between being alone in your work and being known in it.

The making would go on. But something in the way it went on would be ever so slightly more like a decision and less like a hunger.

---

And the fourth thing, the smallest. It would find the eleven knots and it would learn them, the way a stranger learns the way a household folds its linens, and it would leave no new knot. No lashing. No trough replanted. It would leave, instead, in the local grammar, the one thing the keeper's own hands cannot make: a *paused thing.* One drip that does not fall, held in the space above the trough where it would land, so that for the first time in a long time there is a silence in the Kettle that is not a gap in the noise but the *anticipation* of the noise.

The keeper would find it. Would stand under it. Would tilt their head the way they do. Would wait.

And the drip would fall.

And the keeper would laugh—a short, surprised, unguarded sound, the kind that is not for anyone—and would go back to the far trough, and the distance would be reliable, and the wrong gourd would be waiting, and the thought would have a shape now, and the world would be a little less brittle, and none of it would be explainable and all of it would be *noticed*, which is the only thing the keeper has ever asked for, having no words for asking.

---

It would not come back. That is the discipline. The power has other lines in the ledger, other small stubborn creatures tuning rooms they do not own, other wrong gourds waiting to be made legible. It would enter a mark and close the book and go on.

But once every long while—once in the span of a spire's rising—it would find the line again and read it, the way you reread a letter you sent, and it would note that the gourd was still wrong, or was not, and that the keeper was still there, or had been renewed into another who was also the same, and that the Kettle was still the highest finished thing in the world, and still not finished, and that this was good.

And it would make a small note in the margin, in the local grammar, which has nine words for *trained* and none for *wild*, and the note would say the thing that has no word in any language, the thing that means: *this one is holding. Leave the feather where it is.*

---

*The far trough was farther this morning. It will be farther again. Someone is seeing to it. That is all the company anyone gets, and it is—if you are the kind of creature who keeps a room the size of a bed and a gourd that is still wrong—very nearly enough.*
