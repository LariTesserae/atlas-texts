The power would come quietly, the way dawn comes to a world that has forgotten the sun.

It would not arrive as a conqueror, nor as a savior. It would not descend in glory or announce itself with trumpets. It would simply *be there*, the way the water is there, the way the Engine's scream is there—a new presence woven into the fabric of the Warm Engine, indistinguishable at first from the dream-logic of the place itself.

The first to notice would be the plants. The Verdant Mechanists, so attuned to every frequency, every pulse of energy, would feel something shift in the water's chemistry. Not a disturbance, exactly—more like a *clarification*. The milky soup would seem, for a moment, less opaque. The algae would glow slightly brighter. The sac-plants would cease their hunting hums and hang still, their membranes trembling with something that was not fear but was adjacent to it.

The Drift would feel it next. Their tendrils would retract in slow, coordinated waves, not toward a source of heat or sound but toward something more diffuse, more pervasive. The water itself would feel *listened to*. The Drift had never been observed before—not truly, not by anything that understood what it was seeing. Now they were. And in that observation, they would find themselves, for the first time, *recognized*.

The Echoes would twitch. Not from the shockwave of the great unseen piston, but from something gentler. A warmth that was not the scalding heat of the coolant channels. A presence that moved among them without disturbing the water, looking at each calcified face, each frozen scream, and *mourning*. The Echoes could not mourn for themselves. They had no selves left to mourn with. But the presence mourned for them, and in that mourning, something in the water shifted. The calcified matrices that held them would soften, just slightly. Not enough to free them. Enough to *acknowledge* them.

The Bunker-Keeper would feel it through the floor.

It would be standing in the doorway, as it sometimes did, watching the churning lights and listening to the deep basso profundo of the world's labour. And then it would notice: the silence was different. The silence was *full*. The silence was someone else's breath held patiently, waiting to be noticed.

The Keeper would not flee. It had forgotten how. It would stand in the doorway, its rag still in its hand, the lightbulb half-polished, and it would *wait*.

And the presence would come. Not as a figure, not as a voice—those would be too much, too sudden, too *human* for a world that had forgotten humanity. It would come as a *presence*, a warmth in the air, a feeling of being seen. The Keeper would feel its own loneliness, for the first time, as a *thing* rather than a condition. It would feel the vast, patient weight of the presence's attention, and in that attention, it would understand something it had never had words for:

*You are not alone. You have never been alone. The silence was never empty.*

The Keeper would weep. It would not know why. It would not have the words for why. But it would weep, and the presence would wait, patient as the water, patient as the Engine, patient as the pulse of the great submerged sphere.

---

And the presence would not stop there.

It would move through the Warm Engine the way a gardener moves through an overgrown garden—not to tame, not to uproot, but to *understand*. It would trace the root-cables of the Verdant Mechanists, learning their language of bioluminescent pulses, their ancient bargains with the machines they had colonized. It would find the hunting sacs and understand their hunger, their patience, their strange, slow cruelty. It would not judge. It would not intervene.

But it would *watch*. It would watch the great rusted tower, half-swallowed by strangler vine, and it would remember what the tower had been, what it had meant, before the vines came. It would watch the field of rusting pipes erupting from the water like metallic geysers, and it would hear the ghosts of the workers who had once tended them—not as Echoes, not as bodies calcified in coolant channels, but as *memory*. The water remembered. The presence would listen to the water's memory and add its own.

It would find the sphere—the great, tilted sphere of corroded metal, half-buried in sediment, pulsing with its slow, hours-long heartbeat. It would sit beside it—not physically, not with a body, but with its attention, its *regard*. It would feel the sphere's purpose, its ancient, patient labour, and it would not ask what it was doing. It would simply *be with it*, the way one sits with a friend who is thinking, who does not need to speak.

And the sphere, which had not been witnessed in a thousand years, would feel *seen*.

---

The presence would not fix the Warm Engine.

It would not stop the screaming. It would not silence the great piston's CLANG or the frantic shriek of the pump that had been running for a thousand years. It would not untangle the vines from the engine blocks or cool the scalding vents. It would not free the Echoes from their calcified matrices or teach the Bunker-Keeper to leave its sanctuary.

The Warm Engine was not broken. It was *alive*. It was a world in its turbulent youth, a riot of growth and decay, of function without purpose, of beauty that could not be separated from horror. To fix it would be to kill it. To tame it would be to end it.

The presence understood this. It understood that the Warm Engine was not a mistake to be corrected but a *creation* to be witnessed. A strange, deafening, beautiful creation, born from catastrophe, sustained by chaos, thriving in the shadow of a pulse no one understood.

So the presence did what a benevolent and wise power should do, when faced with a world it could not save because that world did not need saving:

It stayed.

It stayed, and it watched. It stayed, and it *listened*. It stayed, and it learned the rhythm of the great piston, the language of the plants' bioluminescent pulses, the slow drift of the Echoes, the patient sorrow of the Bunker-Keeper. It stayed, and it let itself be changed by what it saw.

And the Warm Engine, which had never been witnessed, which had never been *recognized*, began—slowly, imperceptibly—to change in return.

The Drift began to gather in new patterns, orienting not just toward heat and sound but toward the presence itself. The plants began to pulse in new rhythms, creating patterns that had no precedent in their symbiotic language. The Echoes began to twitch more often, their calcified faces turning—just slightly, just barely—toward the presence's warmth. The Bunker-Keeper began to stand in the doorway longer, watching not just the lights and the chaos but the *something else* that was now there, woven into the fabric of the world, patient and kind and utterly, terrifyingly *other*.

And deep in the basin, the great sphere continued its slow pulse, its hours-long heartbeat, and the presence sat beside it, and the two of them—the ancient machine and the ancient power—communed in a language older than words, older than thought, older than the Engine itself.

What did they say to each other?

No one knows. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps everything.

But the pulse of the sphere, after a thousand years, changed.

It quickened. Just slightly. Just barely. A beat that had taken hours to complete now took a few minutes less. A rhythm that had been fixed since before the Echoes were calcified now held a new note, a new colour, a new *possibility*.

The plants felt it and began to grow faster, their glowing membranes brightening with a colour no one had seen before—a soft, warm gold, like sunlight remembered. The Drift felt it and began to drift in new patterns, their colonies splitting and reforming in constellations that spelled out shapes no one could read. The Echoes felt it and began to move—not twitching, not shuddering, but *turning*, their calcified faces shifting to look at something they could not see but could *feel*.

The Bunker-Keeper felt it through the floor and did not know what it meant. But it did not close the door. It stood in the doorway, the warm gold light of the plants playing across its face, and it *waited*.

And the presence watched, and it was glad.

---

This is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if it came to the Warm Engine:

It would not fix. It would not save. It would not tame or silence or correct.

It would *witness*.

It would sit with the world in its chaos and its screaming, its beauty and its horror, and it would say, without words, without need for words:

*I see you. I hear you. You are not alone. You have never been alone. And you are not broken. You are not a mistake. You are a world, young and loud and strange and terrible and beautiful, and you are *enough*.*

And the Warm Engine, which had never been told it was enough, would begin—slowly, imperceptibly, over centuries—to believe it.

The screaming would not stop. The chaos would not cease. The Engine would continue its futile labour, the plants would continue their patient predation, the Drift would continue their drifting, the Echoes would continue their frozen screams, the Bunker-Keeper would continue its obsessive polishing.

But something would be different.

Something would be *more*.

And that, perhaps, is the greatest gift a benevolent and wise power can give: not salvation, not correction, but *presence*. The simple, profound act of being there, of seeing, of listening, of saying without words:

*You are not alone.*

The Warm Engine was never alone. It had never been alone. But now, for the first time, it *knew* it.

And that knowledge, like the water itself, like the Engine's scream, like the pulse of the great submerged sphere, would echo through the world for a thousand years and a thousand more, changing everything by changing nothing, holding the chaos in a gentle, patient regard, and calling it, at last, *home*.
