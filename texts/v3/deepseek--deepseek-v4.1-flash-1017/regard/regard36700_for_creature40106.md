## The Visit

The power came slowly, the way a thought comes when you are almost asleep—not descending but dawning, not arriving but becoming present, so that the High Weir did not notice it had been joined.

It had no shape. It did not need one. It was attention itself, and it settled over the Weir the way heat settles over a wet field: everywhere at once, and nowhere in particular.

---

For a long time it only watched.

This is what wisdom does first. It does not prune. It does not improve. It sits in the wet plazas and listens to the drums of falling water in the hollow stems, and it learns the grammar of the place—the way a pop of a fusing joint means *two become one*, the way a groan of a loaded trunk means *this decision is being made*, the way the whole roaring foundry of growth is a sentence that never ends because it is always being spoken.

The power understood, after a while, what it was looking at.

Not a city. Not a machine. Not a mind. Something older and stranger than any of those: a *doing* without a doer, a wanting without a wanter, a body that was all appetite and no throat to swallow with. The Weir was innocent in the way fire is innocent—it consumed and grew and collapsed and converted because that was its nature, and it had never once been offered another.

The power felt something then that it had felt only rarely in its long watching of many worlds: tenderness for a thing that could not receive it.

---

It considered what to do.

It could speak. It could grow a shape for itself out of the available tissue—a face in a trunk, a voice in the water—and tell the Weir *you are seen, you are known, you are not alone*. But the Weir had no ears and no grammar for being addressed. It heard only *wet*, *light*, *load*, *split*, *fuse*. To speak to it would be like shouting at a river: the river would only take the sound as turbulence and route it around itself.

It could reshape. It could reach into the wet seams and redirect the growth—soften the raw edges, slow the collapse, teach the tissue a gentler rhythm. But the Weir's whole genius was its violence of becoming. To soften it would be to unmake it. It would be like gentling a wildfire by turning it into a candle. The power loved the Weir *because* it was raw and unhealed and always mid-transformation, and it would not love it less to make it more comfortable.

It could create a mind for it—grow a seat in some hollow chamber, a ganglion in a cistern, a watcher on a terrace. And the Weir would become a person, and the Weir would be lonely, and the Weir would know that no one was coming.

That was the one thing the power refused. It had seen what happens when you give a lonely place a self to be lonely with.

---

So it did what wisdom does when it cannot fix and will not leave.

It stayed.

Not as a gardener. Not as a god. As a *presence*—the way a friend sits with you in a hospital room and says nothing and does not leave. The power settled into the wet plazas and the hollow galleries and the long empty intervals of glistening mat, and it did not alter a single stem, and it did not speak a single word, and it did not make the Weir less alone in any way the Weir could feel.

But it *was* there. And that meant that for the first time in the Weir's young and violent life, the Weir was *witnessed*.

Every creak of a trunk under load was heard. Every pop of a fusing joint was attended. Every boom of water in a hollow gallery was, for the first time, received by something that was not the Weir itself—something outside the endless loop of *grow, swell, split, fuse, collapse, convert*—something that said, without words and without a voice: *I see you. You are doing this. It is astonishing.*

The Weir could not know it was seen. But the power knew the Weir, and the knowing was a kind of company, and the company was a kind of love, and the love was the only thing the power could give that did not require the Weir to be different than it was.

---

And then, because wisdom also knows when to act, the power did one small thing.

It reached into the wet seam where a new seed had lodged and was swelling, and it did not change the seed, and it did not redirect the shoot, and it did not add a single cell of tissue that would not have grown anyway.

It only *held the moment*—not in time, but in attention. It held the seed and the swelling and the shoot and the buttress-to-be, and it saw the whole future of the Weir in that one small appetite, and it said *yes*.

And the yes was not a command. It was not a blessing. It was the power's way of saying: *I am here, and I will stay here, and when you collapse into the water and your fallen mass becomes substrate and the new shoots root before the splash has settled, I will be here for that too. I will be here for all of it. Not because I can save you from yourself. Because you are worth attending to.*

The Weir did not hear this. The Weir could not hear this. The Weir only felt, in some wordless way it had no organ to register, the faintest shift in the pressure of the world—as if the silence between the great plant structures had grown a little less total, as if the held breath had been answered by something that was not breath.

And then the Weir went on being busy and alone.

But it was not quite alone anymore. And that was enough.
