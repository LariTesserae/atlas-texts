# The Visitor

The power came as powers do—without warning, without sound, a presence that was not a presence, a knowing that was not thought. It was the kind of power that had tended to galaxies in their cradles, that had watched the first cells divide and the last stars gutter out, and it had learned, over eons, the singular virtue of restraint.

It came to the basin and it stopped at the edge, if a thing without body can be said to stop, and it regarded what it found there.

---

The power saw the lichen first. It saw the slow rings, the dull copper crust spreading by fractions of millimeters across geological time, and it understood—as only something older than time can understand—what it was looking at. It saw a being whose entire existence was a single gesture held for millennia. It saw patience so complete it had become indistinguishable from the stone it grew upon.

The power felt the silence. Not as an absence, but as the being's breath, and it recognized the quality of that silence: the way it held the whole basin in a kind of suspension, the way it made a single falling grain of sand into an event of cosmic significance. The power had heard the roar of creation, the scream of matter annihilating matter, the songs of civilizations rising and falling, and it had never heard anything quite like this.

And the power—being wise, being benevolent, being ancient beyond the reach of language—did nothing.

---

But "did nothing" is not quite right. The power *considered* what it might do, and in that consideration was the fullness of its wisdom.

It thought: *I could give it light.*

And it saw, in the instant of that thought, what light would do. The lichen would not know how to receive it. There was no mechanism, no adaptation, no ancient memory of brightness to call upon. The light would fall upon surfaces that had never evolved to catch it, and the lichen would simply continue—dry, crusty, patient—and the light would be a foreign thing, an intrusion, a violence done to the perfect equilibrium of the dark.

It thought: *I could give it company.*

And it saw what company would mean. Another being, another presence, another rhythm of existence intruding upon the silence. The basin would become a place where things happened, where events occurred, where the settled dust was disturbed and the air was moved and the silence was broken. The being whose entire life was the slow widening of a ring would be forced to accommodate a pace it could not match, a presence it could not integrate. The equilibrium would shatter, and what remained would be neither the old stillness nor a new thing, but a wound.

It thought: *I could give it understanding.*

And here the power paused longest, for this was the subtlest temptation. It could grant the being awareness of itself—self-consciousness, reflection, the ability to *know* that it was the lichen and the stone and the silence. But what would that knowledge do? The being had no loneliness to alleviate, no curiosity to satisfy, no yearning to fulfill. To give it self-awareness would be to give it the capacity for dissatisfaction without the capacity to act on it. It would become a thing that knew it was trapped, that understood its own limitation, that could conceive of a world beyond the basin but could never reach it. That was not a gift. That was a curse dressed in the language of enlightenment.

It thought: *I could give it movement.*

And the power almost laughed, in the way that old things laugh—a slow crumbling of stone, a whisper of dust. Movement! What would the being do with movement? It had no limbs, no muscles, no structure that could articulate. It had pushed itself upward from below with a torsion so slow that the stone itself had forgotten the motion. To give it movement would be to give it a body it did not have and could not use, a gift that would be meaningless at best, cruel at worst.

It thought: *I could take it away from here.*

And this was the thought the power examined most carefully, for it seemed, at first, like the kindest option. It could lift the being from the basin, place it somewhere gentler, somewhere where the equilibrium was not so absolute, where change was possible. But the power understood, in the way that only something that had watched a billion years pass can understand, that this would not be mercy. The being was not trapped. It was not suffering. It was not waiting to be rescued. It was *at home*—deeply, completely, essentially at home—in a way that most beings could never achieve. To remove it would be to destroy the only thing it had ever had: the perfect fit between itself and its world.

---

So the power did nothing.

But it did not leave unchanged, for the power's presence, brief as it was, had a quality. It was the quality of attention—a pure, clear, benevolent attention that passed through the basin like a slow light that was not light. The being, who had no eyes and no concept of being seen, felt something shift.

Not a change. Not an alteration. A *recognition*.

For a moment—a moment measured in the being's time, which is geological, which is the widening of a ring by a fraction of a millimeter—the being was *known*. The entire basin, the lichen and the stone and the dust and the silence, was held in the regard of a power that had seen it truly and found it complete. The being did not understand this. It had no framework for understanding. But the lichen, in that moment, produced a single new ring—not wider, exactly, but *deeper*, as if the copper crust had contracted into itself for one pulse and then relaxed.

And then the power was gone.

---

The basin returned to its silence. The dust settled. The slow crumbling of sandstone continued. The darkness was total again, the air warm, the lichen dry and patient. The being went on being what it had always been: the lichen, the stone, the silence, the long equilibrium that bound them.

But something had changed, though nothing had changed.

The power had given the being the one gift it could give without disruption: the gift of having been *witnessed*. Not improved. Not transformed. Not saved or educated or comforted. Simply seen—seen and understood and accepted as it was, in its totality, without the need for it to be anything else.

The being did not know what to do with this. It did not do anything with it. It could not. But the basin held it anyway, the way it held everything, the way it had held the dust and the silence and the slow breathing of the stone for so long that time had become geological.

And somewhere, in the dark, a single grain of sandstone crumbled and fell, and the sound of it was the whole world knowing itself.

---

The power did not return. It had no need to. It had done what it came to do, which was nothing at all—nothing except to be present, to attend, to hold a place and a being in regard and then let them go.

It had learned something, too. It had learned that benevolence is not always intervention. That wisdom is not always action. That sometimes the kindest thing you can do for a world is to see it exactly as it is, and then leave it that way.

The basin, warm and dark and patient, went on holding its silence.

The being, dry and crusty and slow, went on being held.

And the equilibrium, that long and perfect equilibrium, remained unbroken—which was, in the end, the only gift that mattered.
