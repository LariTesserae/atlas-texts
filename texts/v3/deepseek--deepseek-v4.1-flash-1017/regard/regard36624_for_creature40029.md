The power watches. It does not descend, because descent would be a kind of violence here — a heavy thing arriving from a direction the Sill has no word for. It hovers at the seam where the gray lid of sky meets the gray lid of water, and it listens, and it takes a very long time, because the Sill does not permit haste.

The first thing it notices is that the Sill is already doing the work. The grazers are shaving the stone. The water is finding its channels. The clickers are keeping their laws. The Quiet is keeping its hole. The people are keeping the cairns. It is a place that maintains itself by attention, and any intervention that came from outside would have to be quieter than the roar and sharper than the gap, and the power is wise enough to know that it is neither.

So it does not build. It does not seed. It does not lift the Quiet out of its nature or teach the boom-things a new song. It does not even stop the flow to carve a channel that would spare the hut, because the hut is already a memory the stone is keeping, and the stone keeps it better than the power could.

What it does is what the Sill itself does: it attends.

It attends to the pattern of the potholes, and it learns the dialects of the clickers, and it finds, after a span of listening that would have worn a mountain down, that the dialects are not merely codes of survival but of *argument*, of *gossip*, of *counting* — that there is in the green-black water a whole interior life that no one has ever needed to name. It does not disturb it. It simply knows it now, in the way that the stone knows the water, by being worn.

It attends to the Quiet, and it sees that the Quiet is not evil, and it does not try to make the Quiet good. But it notices that the grazers have learned to watch the plain instead of listen to it, and that this watching is a skill the Sill is slowly growing, a new sense in a place that had built itself entirely on another. It does not accelerate this. It watches the watching, and it is glad.

It attends to the people, who are the least and the most temporary, and it sees that they walk with their hands on their sledges and speak jaw to jaw, and it understands that the Sill has given them something it has given no one else: a kind of honesty. You cannot lie in the roar. You cannot pretend to be heard when you are not. You can only press your jaw to someone's jaw and say the thing you mean into the bone, and then walk on. The power finds this beautiful and does not say so, because saying so would be a noise the Sill would have to absorb.

And then it does the one thing that is left to do, which is the thing the Sill itself has been doing for ten thousand years: it keeps a promise.

Not a new promise. The cairns were a promise, and they are still keeping it. The power does not add to the line. But it makes the line *matter* — not by marking it, not by protecting it, but by holding it in a mind that does not erode. The cairns are already remembered by the stone. Now they are remembered twice. And in a place where nothing grows and nothing is replaced, a second memory is a kind of wealth, and the power gives it without being asked, which is the only way to give anything here.

It does one small thing more, and it does it so lightly that the Sill does not notice for a thousand years.

It listens to the boom-things at dusk, the single note that goes on and on while the stone gives it back a half-step lower, and it finds in that note a frequency that is almost — almost — a word. It does not make the word. It does not teach the boom-things to say it. It simply *matches* the frequency, holds it, returns it, and adds nothing. And the plain, which has never been answered before, does not know it has been answered. But something in the stone settles. The water moves a fraction more evenly. The crack in the pothole's lip does not widen. And the clickers, in their old green-black water, find that their counting comes a little easier that night, though they could not say why.

The power stays. It does not build a temple or a marker or a law. It stays the way the wind stays, as a thing that is always arriving and never quite arrives, and it listens. And because it is wise, it knows that the greatest gift it can give to a place that has made itself out of attention is to attend, and to keep attending, and to never once mistake the roar for noise.
