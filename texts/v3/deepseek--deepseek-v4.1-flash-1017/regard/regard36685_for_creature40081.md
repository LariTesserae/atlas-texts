# The Power at the Rim

## The Warmth on the Face

It felt the heat before it saw the source, and it had not been warm like that in a long time.

It came down out of the plateau in the cold hour, and it did not arrive as light, because light is a kind of insistence, and it had learned that insistence is how powers ruin things. It walked the last kilometer on foot, which was not necessary, and did anyway, because there is a difference between being present and being *there*, and only one of them is a courtesy.

It stopped at the meter-wide band. One hand in the wet heat, one hand on the cold stone. It stayed there a long while — long enough to learn the stroke: two seconds of steam, four of silence. Long enough to notice that it had begun to breathe in time with the engine, and to make itself stop.

For a great deal of its life, it had been drawn to places by their pain. This was the first thing in some ages that had drawn it by nothing at all.

## The Search for the One Who Lives Here

It went looking for the one who lives here, because there was said to be one, and because its nature was to look for the one.

It found the fig-vines, falling upward, becoming colonnades. It found the editors braced against them, cropping the canopy in slow deliberate arcs, vacant-eyed and unafraid. It found the scavenger at dusk, settling against a dead browser like a hand into a glove. It found the green columns of drifting light, the partly-rooted ones on the warm gravel, the orange crust on the vent aprons brightening and dimming with the pulse.

It found no resident. Nowhere a self that held the others. It was like opening a body to look for the life in it and finding only organs, every one busy, none in charge.

Then it understood, and the understanding came slowly, because it was not a kind of idea the power was good at.

The being was the agreement.

Not a creature. A relation, held open — a thousand things that had stopped competing for the same mouthful and started taking turns. It had no location and no boundary and no face, and it was realer than most of the singular things the power had ever been asked to love, because it could not be picked up, and could not be put down, and could not be killed by any one death.

And the engine was not the being's opposite. The engine was what the being was made of, the way a person is made of a metabolism they do not supervise and will never meet.

The power sat with that a while. It had spent so much of itself on beings who knew they were beings. This one did not know, and did not need to, and was not lesser for it.

## What It Refused

A power's first temptation is to be useful. It had to hold that one in its hands and look at it before it could set it down.

It refused to map the rift. A map is a claim with the manners of a fact, and the first thing a claim attracts is a claimant.

It refused to name the valley. Naming is how a power takes custody. The valley had names already and they were all rhythms — two-and-four, the bright pulse of the mats, the soft irregular patter of fig fruit, which is not rain.

It refused to fence it, protect it, station a ranger at the rim. A fence makes an inside and an outside. The rift already had an inside and an outside. It was one meter wide, and it worked.

It refused to seed it elsewhere. There were thirty cold rifts on thirty cold worlds that would bloom. But the valley was not a recipe; it was an outcome, a thousand years of local negotiation, an agreement that had been *reached*. Seeding it would not be planting a garden. It would be planting an argument.

It refused to quiet the engine so it could be heard as music. The engine was not music that had gone wrong. It was a stroke, and the plants had synchronized to it, and a power that finds a rhythm ugly has not finished learning it.

It refused to give the rooted thing speech — to give it the gift of asking, and with it the grief of having waited so long without an answer. The thing was waiting, and had forgotten what for, and not every waiting wants to be answered. It was not the power's place to decide that the waiting was a lack.

And it refused, most carefully of all, to be thanked. It caught that one in itself and set it down the way you set down a hot stone.

It had made a garden once, long ago, and the garden had become a museum, and the museum had become a lesson, and the lesson had outlived everyone who might have learned from it. It had learned to tell the difference between a kindness and a monument, and the difference was almost always time.

## The Letter It Did Not Send

*You do not know I am here, and I have decided that this is the correct arrangement, and I want to say why, though you will not read it.*

*I have been a power for a long time. Nearly everything I have loved has loved me back, or feared me, or wanted something from me, and I had come to believe that this was what being loved consisted of. You have corrected me. You are indifferent to me the way weather is indifferent, and it has not diminished me. I find I am larger for it. I did not expect that.*

*I am not going to fix you. There is nothing in you that is broken. You are the only factory I have ever seen that produces nothing for anyone, and I have decided that this is not waste. It is rest. It is what work looks like when it has forgotten who it is for and keeps going anyway, and the sound of it is a stroke and not a scream, and the leaves open on the stroke and close on the lull, and nothing is owed, and no one is owed.*

*I will not preserve you, either. Preservation is a kind of taxidermy, and I have done a great deal of it, and I am sorry for it. Instead I will do the smaller thing, which is to keep you out of the way of people who would love you usefully.*

## What It Actually Did

It kept the door. Not a wall — a dullness. Instruments that came near the rift tended to drift; notes taken at the rim disagreed with notes taken at the rim; expeditions arrived at the right coordinates and found a cold slot of granite with nothing in it, and went home early, and could not say why.

The veil was porous, and it made sure of that. It obscured the valley only from those who came wanting. Anyone who came without appetite would see everything. The power could have made it absolute and found that it could not, quite, and was quietly glad of the limitation, because absolute concealment is only ownership wearing a different coat.

It kept the memory. The valley does not know it is strange and will never know it was seen, so the power holds the record — not in a vault, in itself, as a way of being. It will be the only place the valley exists as a thing that was loved, and it will hold that without asking anything for the holding. A witness is the only gift that costs the giver nothing and the given nothing and is still worth something.

And it sat with the rooted thing. It came back at intervals and sat on the warm gravel beside it and did not speak and did not answer. It was simply there, the way you sit with someone in a hospital: present, useless, correct. When the thing finished rooting, and its animal parts went quiet in a way that was not death and was not the old waiting either, the power stayed a little longer, and then stood, and dusted off its hands, and did not come back to that spot.

## The Band

It still comes back. Not often, and never in a way that announces itself.

It crouches in the meter-wide band with one hand in the wet heat and one hand on the cold stone, and it listens to the engine chugging away down there in the green and the steam and the glowing dusk, measured and mindless, and it is glad the engine is for no one. It has decided that this is the whole of the gift, and that the gift is sufficient, and it has been sufficient for a long time now.

The being does not know. That is the point. That is the entire point.
