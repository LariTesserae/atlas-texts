The benevolent power would arrive not as a conqueror, not as a savior, but as a visitor who understands the sanctity of a place that has found its own equilibrium. It would come quietly, the way dawn comes to a place that has known only night—gradually, and without demand for acknowledgment.

**First, it would observe.**

The power would stand at the edge of the cavern for what might be hours or years, watching the slow turn of the wheel, the patient creep of vines, the Keeper's still form in the doorway. It would listen to the hum and the drip and the *skree*, and it would understand that this is not a place that needs fixing. It is a place that needs witnessing.

It would note the rust on the catwalk, the warp of the metal, and instead of seeing decay, it would see time made visible. It would see the engine, driving nothing, and understand that purpose is not always external. Sometimes purpose is the turning itself—the continuation of a process that keeps a being alive, that gives shape to days that would otherwise blur into the endless dark.

It would see the Keeper and recognize it not as a monster, not as a victim, but as a *maintainer*. A being that has found its niche, its function, its way of being in the world. A being that has become what it tends, the way a gardener becomes their garden, the way a sailor becomes the sea.

**Then, it would approach.**

Not with fanfare or proclamation, but with the same slow patience the Keeper understands. It would walk the catwalk, letting the metal sing under its weight, letting the vibration travel ahead to announce its coming. It would stop at the doorway and wait.

When the Keeper came—tilted, pulsing, wet seam breathing its sour warmth—the power would do nothing threatening. It would lower itself, if necessary, to be at eye level with those dim bioluminescent eyes. It would extend a hand, palm up, and in that palm would be nothing but a single drop of clean water, cool and clear and utterly foreign to this place.

An offering. A question. A statement: *I am not here to take. I am here to give, if you will have it.*

The Keeper might tap its rhythm on the pipe: three slow beats, a pause, three slow beats. The power would listen. And then, if it understood—and it would understand—it would tap back. Not the same rhythm, but something adjacent. Something that said: *I hear you. I am here. I mean no harm.*

**Then, it would stay.**

Not forever. But for a time. Long enough to learn the rhythms of this place, to understand the difference between a healthy hum and a sick one, to feel the subtle pulse of the engine through the stone. It would sit in the warmth and let the condensation gather on its skin. It would drink from the brass cup the Keeper offered, and it would not flinch at the taste of mineral and sap.

And it would *help*—but only in ways that the Keeper would recognize as help. It would not rip the vines from the wheel. It would not pull the Keeper from its symbiosis. Instead, it would do small things. It would hold a valve steady while the Keeper's root-fingers worked. It would carry a heavy stone from one place to another when the Keeper's roots had stiffened with cold. It would learn to scrape the mineral crust from the engine's housing, and it would do so with the same care the Keeper showed.

It would ask nothing in return. It would expect no gratitude. It would simply *be there*, the way the stone is there, the way the heat is there.

**And then, it would give a gift.**

Not a grand gift. Not a transformation. Something small, something that fit within the logic of this place. Perhaps it would bring a seed—a seed of a plant that could grow in this heat, in this damp, that could provide something the Keeper's diet lacked. Perhaps it would mend a bearing that had been grinding for years, easing a pain the Keeper had long since stopped noticing. Perhaps it would simply sit with the Keeper through one long, dark stretch of silence, and in that sitting, communicate something the Keeper had not known it needed: *You are seen. Your work is seen. This place is seen.*

The Keeper would not understand this gift at first. It would tilt its fungus-face, eyes pulsing out of phase, and it would tap its rhythm on the pipe: three slow beats, a pause, three slow beats.

And the power would tap back: *Yes. I know. I am here.*

**Finally, it would leave.**

But not without a promise. Not spoken—the power does not speak here; speech is not the language of this place—but conveyed through action. The power would place its hand on the great wheel, feeling its slow turn, and it would leave something behind. Not a physical thing. A presence. A warmth that was not the cavern's warmth, a hum that was not the engine's hum.

It would be a kind of *attention*, a lingering awareness. A sense that somewhere, something was watching over this place. Not intervening. Not correcting. Just *watching*. Just *caring*.

And the Keeper would feel it, the way it felt the vibration of the catwalk, the way it felt the change in the engine's note. It would not understand it, not fully. But it would know that something had changed, and that the change was not a threat.

It might stand in the doorway for a long time after the power left, one hand on the engine, listening. Listening to the hum and the drip and the *skree*. Listening for the new note that was now part of the song.

And then it would go back to work. Because that is what it does. Because that is what it is.

The wheel would turn. The vines would creep. The water would drip. And somewhere, in the silence between heartbeats, there would be a presence that said: *I am here. I see you. Keep turning.*

And the Keeper would keep turning. Because that is what it does. Because that is what it is.

And that would be enough.
