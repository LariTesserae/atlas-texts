**The Wise Power at the Slot**

It came as a held breath, not a beam of light. It did not stand in the room with a face. It settled into the forty-second rhythm and learned the chord of the hollow stalks, the boulder’s hair-shift in its seat, the long exhale of the leaf membrane overhead. It counted until the count stopped being counting.

Then it made its judgment: *This is not a wound. This is a working.* There was no face here to comfort, no want to satisfy, no wrong to repair. The grove was young, temporary, and complete under its own terms. To brighten it would kill it. To still the water would kill it. To make it permanent would be to turn a verb into a specimen. So the power did not rescue. It did not prune. It did not name the grove in any language the grove could not hear. It did not add a single beetle.

What it did was smaller and harder: it kept the conditions. It held the upstream fissures in their slow argument with melt and rain, so the pulse stayed a pulse and not a flood or a drought. It did not regularize the forty seconds; it let the mountain keep its own time. It let the fin shed, the channel silt, the shelf of moss hang over its new hole. When the jammed boulder finally shifted, the power did not catch it. That collapse was part of the grove’s life, and would be part of its death.

It set one boundary. The room would be found only by those who came as it had come—slow, empty-handed, willing to be shouted at by water and to learn by hand and ear. Hammers, lamps, hoses, collectors would find the slot breathing cold mist and a clutter of fresh-fractured stone, and the way would not open. Not a ward of light; only the mountain’s own indifference, reinforced a little. The grove would not be managed, sampled, or made into a shrine. It would be allowed to remain uninterested.

And because the power was wise, it did not pretend this was forever. It knew the grove was younger than a person and would be choked or washed out or buried within a few decades, and that another room would open somewhere in the same fracture and begin to knock. It did not transplant the moss. It did not carry spores to a safer seam. It trusted the mountain to make another. But it did one thing a thing without a face cannot do for itself: it remembered. It took the chord into its attention—the exact pitches, the wet-iron smell, the forty-second build and blowout, the pale green reading as black—and held it. When the roof seals or the water cuts a new throat and the room goes quiet, something in the universe will still know that here, for a while, a blind wet engine made a chord in the dark. That is the kindness proper to a grove: not to keep it, but not to let it pass entirely unwitnessed.

Then the power withdrew its attention to the edge of the rhythm and left no sign. The mist still pours from the slot and thins ten paces downslope. A hundred paces away there is bare young stone and wind. The room keeps its noise packed under the fallen wedge, making pale green and water hammering, entirely uninterested in whether anyone ever finds the way in. The power did not ask it to be grateful. There was no one there to be.
