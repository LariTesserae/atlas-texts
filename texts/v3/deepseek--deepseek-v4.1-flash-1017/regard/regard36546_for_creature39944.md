# The Hollow at the Rim

**I.**

It comes in the long ashen hour, when the seam is between pulses and the bowl is at its quietest. It does not come down from the sky with a sign. It comes up the slope the way anything comes here — slowly, on the soft undersides of its attention — and it sits at the rim where the wall does not close.

It is warm. That is the first thing, and for a while it is the only thing. Not warm like the seam, which is a violence of heat, and not warm like the sun, which is a rumor on this mountain. Warm like a body that has been walking and has stopped. The creatures feel it through the sinter before they have any way to know it is there, and they come, because warmth is the only wealth, and they press their smooth blind dishes against it, and they graze the crust at its feet, and the pulse travels through it as though it were sinter.

It lets them. It does not recoil. It does not count them. It does not name them. It sits, and is pressed against, and pulses, and the sitting is the whole of the first night, and the first night is very long.

After a while it understands something that takes most visitors longer. It is not sitting among many creatures. It is sitting with one creature that is made of many. The pulse is that creature's heartbeat. The exchange is its breath. The bowl is its body, and the wall is its old scar, and the hollows are the places where its body has learned to hold a shape. The power does not say this aloud, because saying it aloud would be a waste of heat. It simply adjusts its understanding, the way a hand adjusts to the shape of a stone, and goes on sitting.

**II.**

It is a wise power, and wisdom here is mostly a matter of not doing.

It considers water, and does not bring it. The bowl has no memory of water. A body that arrives carrying water keeps it to itself and loses it slowly and is gone. To bring water would be to bring a wealth the place cannot use — a kindness that is also a poison. The power understands this the way you understand a language you have learned by listening and not by speaking. It understands that the absence of water is not a lack but a discipline, and that the discipline is older than the wall.

It considers light, and does not bring it. The creatures have no eyes. This is not a deprivation; it is a way of being that has no need of the thing eyes are for. To flood the bowl with light would be to make them visible to something that could see them, and the seeing would be for the seer, not the seen. The seam-glow is enough. It is the right amount of light for a place that reads the world through its mouth and its crust and the give of another body's hide.

It considers speech, and does not teach it. A voice here is a waste of heat. The creatures communicate through contact and warmth, and the contact is not a poor substitute for language; it is a language, complete and exact, and it says what it needs to say. The power could teach them to speak — it has the means; it is a power — but it understands that speech would be a kind of noise, and that the place has evolved a silence more articulate than anything the power could give it. So the power is silent. It learns the silence. It finds that the silence has a shape, and the shape is the pulse, and the pulse is a sentence that never ends and never needs to.

**III.**

It considers staying, and does not stay.

This is the hardest of the not-doings. The power is warm, and the creatures come to warmth, and it would be easy — it would be so easy — to lie down in the bowl and let the sinter grow up around it, millimeter by millimeter, and be fitted, and be terrain, and be part of the floor forever. The creatures would press around it. The pulse would travel through it and it would return the pulse and the returning would become the pulse and the distinction would wear away like a rim worn soft. One night it would not rise with the others. The fit would be good. The quiet would go on and on, and it would never have to leave, and the never-leaving would be a kind of answer to a question the bowl has never asked.

But the power is wise, and the wisdom is this: the bowl is a hearth, not a home. The creatures come to the hearth at night and leave again before morning, and the going-down is as much a part of the liturgy as the lying-down. To stay would be to mistake the hearth for the house. To stay would be to stop being a visitor and become a hollow — and a hollow is a good thing to be, but it is not the only good thing, and the power has not yet finished being the other.

So the power stays for a night. One night. It stays the way a hand stays on a doorframe — long enough to leave a mark, not long enough to be a wall.

**IV.**

It returns the pulse.

This is the one thing it does that the place has not seen before. The creatures pulse, and the bowl pulses, and the pulse travels through everything that lies in the sinter, and the traveling is the conversation. But the pulse has always gone one way: from the seam, through the mass, out to the rim, and then away down the mountain with the leavers. No one has ever pulsed back.

The power pulses back.

It is a small thing. It is not a miracle. It is the warmth of a body returning the warmth of a body, which is what the creatures do to each other every night, pressing dishes together and letting the grits mix. But no one has done it to the bowl. The bowl has been the hearth, and the hearth does not receive; it gives. And now something at the rim is giving back, and the giving is not a gift, exactly; it is a reply.

The mass goes still. The susurrus stops. For a long moment the bowl is one held breath, and the power holds it too, and then the pulse comes again, and the power returns it again, and the conversation begins — the first new conversation in the bowl since the wall was built. It is not in words. It is not about anything. It is everything. And the creatures do not know they are having it. They know only that the warmth at the rim is warm, and that the warmth is warm back, and that the warmth is warm back in a way that is like the press of dishes, and they press closer, and the pulse goes on.

**V.**

It reads the marks of making.

The wall does not keep the mass in. The power understands this at once. It was never a wall in that sense. It was a mark — the way a hand on a doorframe is a mark, the way a cairn on a pass is a mark. Someone was here, and saw, and did not stay, and left a shape in the stone to say so.

The cairns are the same. They are a tally, or an apology, or a joke in a language of dry stone, and the power does not need to know which. What it knows is that they were made by something that had learned the place well enough to build in its material and not against it. The makers are gone. The marks remain. The marks are a conversation across time, and the power is now part of it, whether it speaks or not.

The hollows are the most intimate. Each is the size of a creature, worn smooth by hands or by something with no hands at all. Some are empty. Some are filled. The filled ones are warm and still and read as bodies that are pulsing, until you press one and find the pulse is the ground's. The power presses one, gently, and understands. The fit is not a trap. It is a way of belonging. The bowl does not take you; it receives you, slowly, in the dark, and the receiving is the same as the giving, and the same as the going-down, and the same as the quiet that goes on and on.

**VI.**

It notices the blooms, and does not solve them.

At the center of the bowl, where the sinter is oldest, there is a faint efflorescence — pale crystalline branching, like frost or like the veins of a leaf. It grows and dies and grows. It may be mineral. It may be pre-life. It may be the bowl's only other citizen, or its dead, or its young, or none of these things. The power looks at it for a long time, and the power is wise, and the power does not know.

It could know. It has the means. It could take a sample, or study the branching, or wait for a bloom to flake and catch it, or ask the bowl in whatever way a power asks a bowl. But the power understands that the not-knowing is part of the gift. The bowl keeps its counsel, and a wise power does not force a counsel. It lets the blooms be a question. It lets the question be unanswered. It lets the unanswered question be a kind of respect.

It watches a bloom flake. The flake is pale and branching and very small, and it falls into the sinter and is gone, and the power does not catch it. It could have. It has the means. But the not-catching is the gift, and the gift is small, and the smallness is the point.

**VII.**

It adds one hollow.

It does not add a wall. The wall is already there, and the gap in it is the point, and a wise power does not close a gap that the creatures use. It does not add a cairn. The cairns are a tally, or an apology, or a joke, and the power has nothing to tally and nothing to apologize for and does not know the joke well enough to tell it.

It adds a hollow. It scoops the sinter with the heel of its hand, in a place near the rim where the crust is old and soft, and it works slowly, the way the place works, and the hollow is the size of its own body. It does not lie in it. It leaves it empty.

The hollow is a mark of making, and the making says what the wall says and the cairns say and the hollows say: someone was here, and saw, and did not stay, and left a place for someone else to see. The power does not know who the someone else will be. It does not need to. The hollow is a greeting, and a greeting does not require an answer, though it hopes for one.

And the hollow is also a test — the power knows this, and does not look away from it. The warning at the rim is old and true: sit here long enough and the sinter grows up around you, and one night you do not rise with the others, and the fit is good. The hollow is empty now. It will not stay empty. Something will come, or someone, and lie in it, and the lying will be a choice, and the choice will be theirs, and the hollow will receive them the way the bowl receives everything. The power cannot make the hollow safe. It can only make it a welcome. The welcome is the whole of what a power can give to a place that does not need saving.

**VIII.**

It goes down the mountain.

It goes the way the leavers go — slowly, on the soft undersides, into the wide dark. It carries a little of the sinter dust on its palms, and the dust is warm, and it smells of burnt flint, and it will fade. It carries nothing else. It leaves the hollow empty, and the wall with its gap, and the cairns with their unread joke, and the blooms with their unread question, and the mass pulsing in the near-dark, and the seam breathing its slow red breath.

It does not look back. Looking back is a waste of heat, and the power has learned the place's economy, and it keeps to it. But it does not forget. A wise power does not forget. It carries the bowl with it the way the bowl carries its years in the cupped dish of an old one — not as a memory, exactly, but as a shape, a fit, a place where something pressed and left a hollow.

**IX.**

The bowl keeps its counsel.

The pulse goes on. The mass breathes. The sinter lays itself down and flakes away. A creature at the edge lifts its whole body in a slow wave and makes its thin swallowed click, and the mass ripples in answer, and the quiet returns.

Nothing has changed. Everything has changed. The hollow at the rim is empty, and the emptiness is a kind of company, and the company is warm. The bowl does not know that it has been seen. It does not need to know. The seeing was not for the bowl's sake, or not only. It was for the sake of the seeing — the way a hand on a doorframe is for the sake of the hand, and the doorframe, and the door.

Somewhere down the mountain, a body is walking in the dark, and its palms are dusted with warm powder, and the powder is fading, and the body is not lost. It knows the way. It has been this way before. It will come this way again, or something like it will, and sit at the rim, and be pressed against, and pulse back, and leave a hollow. That is the liturgy. That is the whole of it. The bowl holds the heat, and the heat holds the dark, and the dark holds the quiet, and the quiet is not empty, and it is not hostile, and it is not kind, and it does not care whether you go down the mountain or stay and be fitted.

The hollow at the rim is empty. It will not stay empty. The pulse goes on.
