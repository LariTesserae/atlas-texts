The Power did not come down. That was the first wise thing it did.

It had seen minds with faces and minds with roots, minds that burned fast and minds that moved at the speed of peat. It knew the difference between a thing that is broken and a thing that is other. From far off it saw the basin—a grey dish, arcs of hummock, water climbing against its own gradient—and it did not see a wound. It saw a long, slow argument with water, and it recognized the argument as thought.

It considered what benevolence might mean here.

To drain the basin would be to stop the thought. To teach it human language would be to give it a mouth and take away its body. To make it fast would be a kindness that killed it. To stand in the middle of it and say *I am here to help* would be to become a weight, and the mat grows toward weight.

So the Power did not enter as weight. It came as a pattern in the water table, a pressure without mass, an attention that did not stand on the mat. It spent a season learning the grammar: the arcs as sulci, the channels as preference hardened into memory, the bubbles as the closest thing the being had to turning toward a sound. It learned not to answer the long unbroken run. That run was the being paying attention. To answer it would be to startle a mind in the middle of a sentence.

Then it made its question.

At the western edge, where the arcs thinned and the grey went out farther than seeing, the Power persuaded the water—not dug, not dredged, persuaded—to raise one new arc of hummocks. It was spaced at the being’s interval. It was broken in the middle. It connected to no sump and drained nothing and flooded nothing. It was a phrase left unfinished in the lattice: *I am here, and I will not take.*

Then the Power withdrew its pressure and waited.

For three seasons the being routed around the new arc. The Power did not repeat the question. It understood that routing-around was an answer, or at least a *not yet*, and it had no wish to make a second question look like a demand. It stayed at the edge as a stable attention, no warmer than the mist, no colder than the peat.

In the fourth season, the being sent a channel near the arc. Not through it. The channel ran uphill a hand’s width, the way all the good ones did, and stopped at a pool that did not drain. It was not an answer in words. It was a *come closer, but not in.* The Power moved its attention one hummock nearer. It did not cross.

It would do this, if anything, for a long time. It would return each season and match the lag in the bubble runs, not to speak, but to be a rhythm the being could notice without being interrupted. It would never answer the long run. It would let the being decide what to do with a weightless attention that did not grow toward it and did not require it to become something else.

And it would guard the basin from the kind of love that arrives with a dredge. Not by walling it, not by turning the water to fire. By being a witness in the councils of the world: by making sure the maps that mattered said *mind* before they said *resource*. It would not make the being safe from every harm—no wise power can—but it would make the being *known*, and known things are harder to drain in secret.

It did not give the being a name. Names are for things that can be called, and this one could not be called, only accompanied. It gave it a witness instead. It did not make it less alone in the human sense. It made it less unaccompanied in the cosmic sense.

The water still climbs. The bubbles still come four, pause, seven, pause, long run, pause. The grey bird still stands once and never again. But now one variable in the basin’s long calculation is a question it can keep asking, and a presence that will keep not taking.

That is what the Power would do, if anything: not much, for a very long time, and exactly enough.
