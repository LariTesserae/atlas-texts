**The Listener**

The power heard the basin from very far away. This was not unusual; the power heard everything from very far away, and most of it passed through the power the way weather passes through a hill. But the basin did not pass through. It caught. It caught the way a throat catches pressure, and the power found itself drawn down the ribs toward the sound, and it did not know why, and it went anyway.

It had been in the habit of helping for so long that helping had become a kind of forgetting. It had mended worlds and could not remember most of them. It remembered the failures, because failure is sticky, and it did not remember the mended ones, because a mended thing stops being an event. This is the arithmetic of benevolence, and the power had done the arithmetic and found it sound. It was kind, and kindness is a kind of hunger, and the power had been hungry for a long time.

It came down into the dish and the sound got louder and it did not recoil. Most things recoil. The power had heard many loud things in its long attention and had learned to listen through them, the way you learn to see through fog. It sat in the troughs with the water pulsing around its ankles and it listened, and it heard the canon — the near throats sharp, the far ones late and muffled, the two or three that fell into step for a minute and drifted apart — and it understood that this was not a machine and not an animal and not a god in any shape it recognized. It was a thing the water had made and was still making. It was complete. There was nothing left to remove.

The power saw the woman with the seam in her hand, and it saw her teeth, and it saw the pale things in the deep chambers rising with the surge and settling and waiting. And its old habit rose in it like bile. It drafted, as it had drafted ten thousand times before. It drafted a green thing that would grow in the troughs and hold the stone. It drafted a regularity for the canon, a gentle metronome that would not ache in the roots of the teeth. It drafted a lift for the woman — up and out, a clean ascent, a place on the shelf where the day had a shape. It reached for the seam in her hand, because it could close that seam in a breath, and it had closed a great many seams in its time, and it was good at it.

And then it stopped. Because it saw that the seam was already being closed. The basin was doing it — slowly, at the rate the water brings mineral and the mineral sets, the seam was being filled in from within, and the filling-in was not the power's to give. It was hers, and it was the basin's, and the power had no place in it except the place it could find for itself, which was small and far and late. The power put its drafts away. It did not burn them. It put them away the way you put away a tool you have carried for a very long time and will not need again.

Under the third rib, where the stone was hollow and the throat was narrow and the thud came up late and muffled, the power settled. It did not change the canon. It did not add anything the canon did not already have. It found a place where a voice was missing — a far throat, a late thud, the kind that arrives after you have stopped expecting it — and it took that place, and it became that voice, and the canon absorbed it the way the nap absorbs a print. The settled ones noticed, because a new thud is an event in a place where a single living thing is an event, but the thud was small and far and it found its place quickly, and after a season no one could say it had not always been there. The basin fills in.

The resolve came, as it comes, without a schedule and without warning beyond the pressure behind the eyes and the ache in the arches of the feet. The throats drew together like a fist, and the basin said it — one soft enormous whump felt in the sternum and the sinuses and the soles — and the power, which had heard the resolve from outside and had thought it a snore, heard it from inside and knew it for what it was. A turning over. Patient. In a bed the being did not intend to leave. The water rose in every trough at once and held, trembling, and the power turned over with it, and something in the power that had been clenched for a very long time — the need to be needed, the arithmetic of benevolence, the sticky memory of failure — let go, the way the water lets go of the surge and scatters back into its loose unruly round.

Afterward, the woman with the seam came down the rib to the mouth of the hollow where the power had settled, and she stood there for a while with her blade in her hand, and she listened. The power did not speak. The power had learned that the basin does not answer and that the not-answering is the whole of the conversation. But the woman said, to the air and to the thud and to no one: *You're late.* And she went back to skimming.

The power had counted for a long time — stars, ages, the pulses of things. It had come to the basin to count the resolves, because counting was what it did, and counting was how it helped. It stopped counting in the first season. It does not know how many there have been. It has stopped wanting to know. That is the whole of what it learned, and it learned it the way everyone learns things here, which is by staying. The basin is patient with it. It has begun, slowly, at the rate the water brings mineral and the mineral sets, to fill in the power's seams. The power has stopped wanting it to stop.
