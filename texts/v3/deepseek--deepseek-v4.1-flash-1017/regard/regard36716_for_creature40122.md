The power that notices the Chiming Weald does not arrive. It has always been there, in the way that a very old thought is always somewhere in a mind, even when unnoticed. It is the pressure of a hand on a shoulder that you do not feel until you need to. It is the warmth of a room you have never left.

When it turns its attention fully—when it *looks*—it does not look from above. It has no above. It settles into the Weald the way the damp settles, into every crevice and joint, into the root-network and the ferment-heart and the slow, cold blood of the Grafters. It feels the place from the inside out: the churn of the green mass, the groan of overloaded limbs, the low, patient chord of the tuning-language, the taste of wet bark and yeast on the air.

And it understands, without words, that this is a broken thing that works. A machine left running past its purpose, a garden gone feral and beautiful, a body that has forgotten it is a body and has become a world. It is not sick, exactly. But it is *stressed*. It is a held breath that has been held too long. The ferment-heart runs too hot. The venting is never sufficient. The Grafters—a dozen slow, patient, holy mechanics—are always behind. They have been behind for a thousand years. The system has achieved equilibrium at the cost of constant, grinding effort, and the effort is winning the battle against the equilibrium.

The power considers intervention. It weighs the Weald in its attention the way a hand weighs a stone before skipping it.

It could heal the place. It could cool the ferment-heart, restore the balance, make the Grafters' work unnecessary. It could make the Weald a quiet, stable forest, and the Grafters could become simple gardeners, and the great churning engine could rest.

But this would be a kind of death. The Weald is not a wound. It is a *creature*—a strange, vast, half-living thing that has grown into its own strange shape. To fix it would be to unmake it. The power does not unmake.

It could give the Grafters more. More hands, more strength, more speed. It could swell the cradle-knots, quicken the decade of transformation, make the dozen into a hundred. But the Grafters are not a labor force; they are *organs*. And the Weald cannot support a hundred. The dozen are the right number, and the slowness is the right speed, and the fatigue is the right cost. This is not a flaw. It is a *calibration*. The power respects calibrations.

So it does nothing. It listens. It stays. It learns the chord of each Grafter—the one with the deep root-voice that always answers first, the one that limps and has never told the others, the young one that has just learned to feel the pressure in the heart and has not yet learned what to do with the knowledge. It learns the rhythm of the chime-stones and the way the network breathes. It feels the ancient, patient, stubborn *will* of the Weald—not a will to survive, which would be simple, but a will to *continue*, to keep churning, to keep producing, to keep being this and only this.

And then, very slowly, it begins to do something so small and so gentle that the Grafters will not know it is happening for a generation.

It creates a *new note*.

Not a new organ. Not a new Grafters. A new *tone* in the network. A frequency that was not there. It passes it into the stone at the root of the oldest chime-slab, and it waits for the network to incorporate it. It will take years. The Grafters will feel it first as a faint, unfamiliar vibration in their soles. They will tap their vines and receive no answer. They will be confused, then frustrated, then, eventually, curious.

The note is not a command. It is a *question*. It asks: *Can this be tuned?*

The power does not know the answer. It does not need to know. It has simply added a new possibility to a system that has been running on a closed set of possibilities for a very long time. The Weald will accept the note or reject it. The Grafters will investigate or ignore it. The chime will change, or it won't. All outcomes are acceptable. The power is not here to fix. It is here to *offer*. If the Weald chooses to grow—not to spread, but to *deepen*—the power will be there. If it does not, the power will be there anyway. Its presence is not contingent on the answer. Its presence is the answer, in a way.

The Grafters will not know what to make of it for a long time. The note is strange. It does not have a chord. It does not come from anywhere. It does not ask for maintenance. It just sits there in the stone, resonant and patient, humming a question that has no words.

And in that hum, very slowly, the Weald will begin to change. Not in its structure, not in its dynamic. But in its *self-awareness*. The Weald is not a mind. It cannot think. But it can *respond*. And the question is a kind of pressure. And pressure is how the Weald communicates. It will push back. It will test the note. It will build structures to resonate with it. The Grafters will find themselves doing things they have never done before, tuning strands of vine that have never been tuned, listening to silence with a new quality of attention.

The power watches. It does not guide. It is present. It is *interested*, in the way a gardener is interested in a plant that might bloom in a new color.

The Grafter with the limping foot will be the first. It will be out on the edge of the known network, clearing a choke-knot, and it will feel the note clearly for the first time. It will stop. It will press its palm to the stone, and the stone will sing the note back to it, and it will not understand, and it will not be afraid. It will tap a vine. The answer will come back—not another Grafter, not the network's usual low acknowledgment, but something else. A *third* thing. A resonance that is neither Grafter nor Weald. A chord that has no known chord.

It will stand there for a long time, the rain beading on its mossy skull, its amber eyes staring at nothing. And then it will do something it has never done. It will hum. Not the resonant, sealed-plate hum of the chime-language. A quiet, cracked, unpracticed hum, the hum of a creature that has learned to make a sound it did not know it could make.

The power will hear it. And the power will be glad.

The Weald will keep its hot heart and its slow, patient mechanics. The Grafters will keep their tuning and their quiet, grinding work. Nothing will be fixed. But something will be *different*. A door has been opened. A new note has been struck. The Weald will not become a garden. It will become something else—something that has a *choice* now, however faint and slow that choice may be.

The power will stay. It is in no hurry. It has all of time. And the Weald has all of patience.

They will meet in the middle, eventually. They already have.
