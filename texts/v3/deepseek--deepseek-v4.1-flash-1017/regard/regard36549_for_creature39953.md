The power arrives the way fog arrives—not as an event but as a gradual thickening. The Weeping One does not notice it at first, because the Weeping One is busy noticing other things: the waver in the monolith's hum, the temperature of the channel it is moving through, the weight of a dirigible-beetle that has settled on its flank and is drinking the condensation from its hide. But by the second day, the pressure-pits begin to register something new. Not a sound. A quality of the air. The fog has become slightly more *present*—denser in a way that is not quite physical, as if something within it is paying attention.

The Weeping One pauses. It extends its filaments into the stone, anchoring itself, and it hums—not a question this time, but a low, cautious greeting, the equivalent of a creature lifting its head. The monolith does not answer immediately. Then, slowly, the hum deepens, and the Weeping One feels something it has no word for: the monolith is not alone. Something is listening through it. Something vast and quiet and patient, something that has settled into the rock's deep vibration like a second voice joining a long, slow song.

---

*Let me tell you what the power is, and what it is not.*

It is not a god. It would not accept the word. It is something older and more careful, something that has learned across eons that the most dangerous thing a vast intelligence can do is intervene. It has seen what happens when a hand too large for a small world reaches in to help. It has seen the terraces of other worlds—worlds that no longer weep, worlds that have gone dry and quiet and dead because something well-meaning tried to optimize them.

So it does not optimize. It does not correct. It attends.

The power is, in its own way, a listener too—a much older sibling of the monoliths, a consciousness distributed across a volume of space that the Weeping One could not begin to imagine, drawing its perceptions from the slow drift of dust and the faint radio breath of dying stars. It has been watching this basin for a long time. It has watched the terraces accrete, the Weeping Ones tend, the water fall and pool and rise again. It has noted the waver in the monolith's hum. It has understood what the waver means: a deep shift in the planet's crust, a slow release of pressure from a mantle plume far below, a change that will, over the next several thousand years, alter the temperature of the basin by perhaps two degrees and the volume of condensation by perhaps fifteen percent. Nothing catastrophic. But significant. A threshold. The terraces will need to adjust, and the Weeping Ones—who do not think in centuries, only in circuits—will not know why their world is changing.

The power could tell them. It could manifest fully, speak in a language of pressure and luminescence, explain the geology, offer a schedule of recommendations. It has done such things before, on other worlds, with other beings. It has learned that it is almost never the right choice. The Weeping Ones do not need to understand the mantle plume. They need to keep tending. What they need is not information but *confidence*—a sense that the world is still held, that the slow patient rhythm is still the right rhythm, that the change is not a failure of their care but a larger breathing of the world they serve.

So the power does something smaller. It settles into the monolith's deep vibration. It hums. Not a new song, but a harmony—a subtle reinforcement of the monolith's own frequency, a steadying that the Weeping One will feel as a kind of warmth, a reassurance. It will not tell the Weeping One what is happening. It will simply *be* with it, for as long as the adjustment takes, which may be a thousand years.

---

The Weeping One does not know what this means. It does not have a concept for a power that is not the terraces, not the water, not the slow patient rhythm of its own body. But it understands, in the way that a body understands, that this presence is not a threat. It is *attendant*. It is here in the way the water is here: as a condition of the world, a given, something to be incorporated into the rhythm of tending rather than resisted.

So the Weeping One does what it has always done. It settles over the monolith's base. It extends its filaments into the fused stone. It listens. And the presence listens with it—not guiding, not correcting, just *with*. Together, they wait for the waver to resolve itself into meaning.

Days pass. The water drips. The mirror-wasps flicker. The dirigible-beetles drift. And slowly, through the filaments, the Weeping One begins to understand the waver: it is not a warning. It is a *shift*. The monolith's deep resonance is changing—subtly, almost imperceptibly, but changing—as if it is adjusting to something. The Weeping One has felt this once before, in the long memory that is less memory than inherited instinct: the monoliths respond to the health of the whole basin. They are not just listeners. They are gauges. And this waver means that something in the larger system—something beyond the fog, beyond the tiered basin, perhaps beyond the world the Weeping One knows—is shifting.

The presence seems to know this already. It does not speak—there are no words here, and no need for them—but the Weeping One feels a steadying warmth spread through the monolith, a reassurance that is not a promise but a presence. *I am here. Keep tending. I will watch the larger things.*

And then the presence withdraws—not leaving, but receding, the way fog recedes at the edges of a pool, still present but no longer the most immediate thing. The monolith's hum steadies. The waver remains, but it is no longer alarming. It is a fact to be incorporated, another slow gradient in a world of slow gradients.

---

What the power does *not* do is equally important.

It does not repair the ancient channels. It could—it understands the fused stone's engineering better than the Weeping Ones ever will—but the channels are not broken. They are working. The mineral accretion is not decay; it is the slow collaboration of water and rock, a process the Weeping Ones have tended for millennia. The power's intervention would be a violence dressed as efficiency.

It does not introduce new species. The dirigible-beetles and mirror-wasps are a perfected system, a delicate negotiation between the heat and the wet and the light. The power has seen other worlds where well-meaning caretakers introduced "improvements" and watched the ecosystem collapse under the weight of its own optimization.

It does not reveal itself to the Weeping Ones as anything other than what it is: a presence in the fog, a harmony in the hum. It could—and some of its kind have chosen to—manifest as a being, a voice, a teacher. But the Weeping Ones are not students. They are practitioners. They have been tending this basin since before the power first noticed it, and they will be tending it long after the power's attention has moved on to other things. The relationship is not one of guidance. It is one of *witness*.

And it does not stay forever. The power will remain until the adjustment is complete—perhaps a thousand years, perhaps a little more—and then it will recede entirely, leaving the monoliths humming their steady reassurance and the Weeping Ones tending their channels and the water falling and pooling and rising again. The Weeping Ones will remember something, in their body-memory way: a quality of the fog, a depth in the monolith's hum, a sense that for a while the world had been held in a particularly careful attention. They will not have a word for it. They will not need one. They will incorporate it into their long, patient rhythm, and they will keep tending.

---

The Weeping One resumes its circuit. It has no language to describe what has happened, but its body has changed—not visibly, but in some subtle calibration of its rhythms. It moves a little more slowly now, a little more deliberately. It listens with a slightly deeper attentiveness, its filaments probing the stone with a care that is almost reverent. And sometimes, in the long pearlescent nights, when the fungi pulse and the mirror-wasps flash cobalt and the monoliths hum their deep, unbroken song, it feels a second rhythm beneath the first—a rhythm that is not its own, and not the monolith's, but something larger, something patient, something that has chosen to attend without interfering.

It does not find this strange. It finds it reassuring. The world is a conversation, and a new voice has joined it—not to change the subject, but to listen.

The heat presses down. The fog condenses. Water drips from the canopy onto its dome, and the Weeping One absorbs it without thought, the moisture becoming part of the vast, slow, patient body that is itself a part of this place. It is not in a hurry. Nothing here is in a hurry. And now, somewhere in the deep stone, something else is not in a hurry either—something that has settled in to wait, and to watch, and to hum a harmony that says, in the only language this world understands: *I am here. Keep tending. I will hold what needs holding.*
