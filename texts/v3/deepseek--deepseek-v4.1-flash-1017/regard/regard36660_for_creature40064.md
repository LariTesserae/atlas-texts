# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The power comes into the hollow the way light comes into it: indirectly, secondhand, by way of the tilted throat rather than all at once. It does not blaze. It does not announce. It settles, the way the air settles, and waits to be noticed—which, given the nature of the place, is not very long at all, because the place notices everything that enters it. The floor registers the weight of a visitor by the trembling in the rims. A power is a very great weight. The rims tremble for some time.

---

## I. What It Notices First

The power notices the wholeness. Not the water, though the water is remarkable. Not the calcite lips, though they are a marvel of patience. It notices that the hollow has achieved something the power has seen only rarely in a long and patient existence of its own: a place that has already found its shape and is holding it, and holding it well, and doing so without struggle. There is no fraying here. No leak of meaning. No crisis. There is an unhurried, mineral sufficiency that most living things never reach and would not recognize if they were shown it.

The power sits with this for a while. It is not in the habit of improving things that do not need improving.

---

## II. What It Considers Doing

A benevolent power, faced with a place of contented stillness, faces a particular temptation: to make it *more*. To add. To heighten. To introduce abundance where there is sufficiency, variety where there is clarity, meaning where there is only quiet being. The power considers this carefully. It weighs the hollow in its attention the way a skimmer weighs a pool in the soles of the feet.

It considers, first, the obvious gifts:

*To make the light a little better.* To carry a warmer shaft down the throat so the deeper rims are lit, so the pale drifters are seen in their turning, so the snail casts a visible shadow and is beloved as a snail rather than only a displacement.

*To seed the water with something small and green.* A mat of algae, a weft of lichen on the upper stones, an ecology where there is now only chemistry. To make the hollow *fertile*.

*To reveal the wake-thing.* To bring it into the light. To give it eyes, or at least a name the skimmers could hold in their mouths.

*To teach the skimmers something.* A better tool, a finer skim, a way to leave the rims and return to find them still perfect.

The power turns each of these over. It is not a foolish power, and it is not a young one, and it knows the difference between a gift and a disturbance. It has seen what happens when a quiet place is made more interesting.

---

## III. What It Decides Against

It decides against the light. The hollow's dimness is not a deprivation; it is the condition of the hollow's peace. The skimmers have eyes patient with dimness. The pale things have eyes that were never meant for light. To brighten the throat would be to tell the place it has been failing, and the place has not been failing. It has been *being*, which is rarer.

It decides against the algae and the lichen. The hollow's cleanness — its lack of weeds, its absence of decay, its refusal to host a food web — is not poverty. It is a kind of health the power recognizes: the health of a thing that has never needed to make room for anything it did not choose. To introduce green would be to introduce *wanting*: the wanting of the algae for light, the wanting of the grazers for the algae, the wanting of decay for the grazers. The hollow would fill with *wants*. It would become like every other place. The power has seen every other place. It is fond of them, but it is not in the business of making all places the same.

It decides against revealing the wake-thing. The wake-thing lives in the dark because the dark is its home, and to drag it into the light — even for the sake of being known — would be to take a creature that has never needed to be perceived and make perception its need. The skimmers leave the deep rims alone. The wake-thing leaves the shallow rims alone. This is a truce of great antiquity and it does not require a witness. The power can hold the wake-thing in its attention without showing it to anyone. Attention is enough. Attention is often the whole of what a thing requires.

It decides against teaching. The skimmers' patience with the rims, their arc-stacked walls, their notches cut to move water, their heel-first carefulness — these are not the fumbling of people who have not yet been taught. They are the *fruits* of a long, unforced understanding between a people and a place. The power could improve the tools. It would not improve the relationship. It would make the skimmers into students, and the hollow into a curriculum, and the hollow has never been a curriculum. It has been a home.

---

## IV. What It Does

So the power decides to do what a wise and benevolent presence does when it finds a place already whole: it *dwells*.

It does not enter dramatically. It does not change the temperature of the air, which is warm, or the temperature of the water, which is blood-warm, or the dimness, which is the dimness of a finished thing. It settles into the hollow the way the air has settled, and it adds one thing only, and that thing is *attentiveness*.

It attends to the skimmers. Not as a patron attends to beneficiaries, but as a companion attends to the work of a friend. When a skimmer kneels to skim a rim, the power is present in the kneeling. When a skimmer's hand finds the notched channel by feel in the dark, the power is present in the finding. It does not guide the hand. It does not improve the skim. It *witnesses*, with the kind of attention that does not alter what it sees, and this witnessing is not passive—it is a form of care so old and so quiet that most beings never know when they are receiving it.

And because the power is a power, its attention has weight in the world. The skimmers, over the generations, will find that their work goes a little more smoothly than it used to. Not because they are being helped, in any way they could name, but because they are being *seen*, and seeing is a kind of nourishment. A rim skimmed under the attention of a benevolent power lays down its calcite a little more evenly. A wall steers the seep a little more sweetly. The difference is not measurable, but it is real, and the skimmers will not know why, and they will not need to. They will attribute it to luck, or to the hollow itself, and they will be right, because the power has become part of the hollow.

It attends to the small things. To the blind drifters, one to a pool, turning without hurry. To the snail the color of water, whose only evidence is a shadow. To the wake-thing, which will feel, over a long time, a presence in the dark that does not chase and does not illuminate and does not demand to see it — a presence that simply *is there*, the way the dark is there, and that leaves it entirely alone. The wake-thing will not be grateful, because it will not know. But it will be a little less alone, and being a little less alone is the deepest gift a power can give to a creature that has chosen solitude.

And it attends to the floor itself. To the tens of thousands of brimming lips, each holding its small circle of tired light. The power does not speed the calcite's growth or slow it. It does not drain the pools or fill them. It does not break the shell. It sits with the floor the way the floor sits with itself: brimming, ticking, warm, going nowhere. And the floor, which has no season and no hunger and has found the shape it wants to be, receives the attention the way it receives the seep—without request, without rejection, without change—and goes on being exactly what it is.

---

## V. What It Leaves Behind

The power does not build a temple. It does not inscribe a message on the walls. It does not send a prophet up the tilted throat to tell the skimmers that they are blessed. It has seen what messages do to quiet places.

It leaves behind only this: the memory of its attention, woven into the hollow so gently that it will never be distinguishable from the hollow itself. The rims will be a little more perfect. The skimmers will find, without knowing why, that their low voices sound a little less lonely in the thick air. The drown of the seep, the tick of the condensation, the fine-grained patter under everything—these will continue, and under them, added to them, there will be something that was not there before and yet has always belonged: the sense, not of being watched, but of being *accompanied*.

And the power will go. It will not stay forever; a power that stays forever becomes a fixture, and the hollow does not need a fixture. It needs what it has always needed—the seep, the calcite, the skimmers, the drifters, the dark—and it now has, in addition, the knowledge (though it does not know it as knowledge) that it is understood. This is the gift. It is small. It is sufficient. It is the only gift that a place already whole can accept without being diminished.

---

## VI. What It Does Not Do

It does not reveal itself. It does not descend in glory. It does not speak. The hollow has no wind and has never had any, and a voice would be a wind.

It does not explain. It does not tell the skimmers what it has done, because what it has done cannot be told, only lived. It does not tell them what they mean. It does not tell them they are blessed. They are. But the blessing is in the living, not in the knowing.

It does not ask for anything. No shrine, no offering, no remembrance. The power has no need of these things. It has attended, and attention is its own reward, and the hollow's continued brimming is the only response it requires.

It does not change the water. It does not change the dimness. It does not change the cleanness of the place or its patient refusal to host life that has not been invited. It leaves the hollow exactly as it found it, and it leaves it *more so*.

---

## VII. What It Sees, Leaving

As the power withdraws up the tilted throat, it turns—insofar as a power turns—and sees the hollow as it will be and as it has always been:

A cupped hand of weeping stone. A floor of a thousand brimming mouths. A warm dark that goes on past the light and does not end. A few low walls, hand-stacked. A notch cut to move water. A wooden scraper laid across two rims as if the worker will be back after lunch. A fire-blackened stone in its dry niche, the only dry thing, and enough.

And among it all, small lives: the skimmers, heel-first, weight-rolled, reading the floor through their soles; the blind drifters, turning; the snail like a moving shadow; the wake-thing in the deep, leaving its slow line across a still pool.

The power does not say goodbye. It has not said hello. It has been present, and it will be present, and the hollow will go on being what it has always been—brimming, ticking, warm, content to go on not changing, in the dim.

And because it has been attended to, it will be, in some way too small to measure and too real to deny, *more itself*. That is all. That is everything. That is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place that has already finished: it lets the place finish, and it is there for the finishing.

---

*The water seeps in and the water deposits and the water seeps out. The rims are rounded. They were rounded before anyone came, and they will be rounded long after. The light is dim and it will be dim. The air is warm and will stay warm. There is no season down here, no tide, no flood-mark, no gradient. Only the brimming, and the ticking, and the patience that has no need of patience because it has never known haste.*

*And now, woven in so fine that no eye will ever find the seam: the quiet, steady, unending presence of a kindness that asked for nothing and changed nothing and somehow made the whole of it more.*
