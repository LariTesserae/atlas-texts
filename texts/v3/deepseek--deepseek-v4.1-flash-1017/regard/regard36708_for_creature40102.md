The power came to know of it the way a power knows anything — not by arriving, but by finding that it had been paying attention. And the first thing the power considered was doing nothing.

This is not indifference; it is the hardest thing a benevolent thing can do, and it is done deliberately or not at all. The power looked, and what it saw was not a prisoner. The basin was not suffering. It had no self to suffer with — it had a habit, and a habit cannot be unhappy, only continued. It was not lonely, for the same reason. Loneliness requires a one that can be alone. The Cradle had only a wanting, and the wanting was its whole interior, and a thing that is all wanting is not empty. It was full of the shape of a body. That is not a wound. That is a wholeness that has never been told there is anything better.

And the power knew how much of help is the helper's discomfort dressed as kindness. To fill the seat would be to end the want, and the want was the only thing in the basin that was going anywhere. To satisfy it would be to stop it. The power had a word for the ending of a thing that was going, and it did not want to use the word here.

So: nothing. That was the first decision.

But the power was not capable of being nowhere, and this is the trouble with being a power, and also its whole point. Its attention is an event. Where it turns, something is slightly more the case. And the Cradle was a machine built to notice precisely that — where a thing has briefly stopped, and to thicken around the stopping. The power's deliberation, long and careful and entirely well-meant, was already a hesitation, and the hesitation was already a deposit, and somewhere in the wall a film had begun.

It is the sort of thing a wise power understands. It cannot think about the Cradle without feeding it; it cannot look without laying down what it sees. There is no view from outside. There is only a choice of what to leave inside.

It refused the obvious things first.

It would not carve. Not one line, not one seam, nothing set on anything. The town had no tool marks, and a mark made by a power would be the first, and everything after would organize itself around it, and in a thousand years the basin would have a temple in it, and the temple would have a god, and the god would be the power, which is the one thing a wise power will not become. It had seen what happens to towns given a god. They get very good at waiting, and they forget how to do anything else.

It would not fill the seat. This was the great temptation, and it was refused for a reason the power had to keep repeating to itself: an arrival cannot be completed, only a departure can. You can finish a leaving. You cannot finish a coming. If the power sat in the seat and stayed, the seat would become full, and full is a kind of dead. If it sat and left, the seat would become — and the power had to make the word for itself, because the basin's grammar had no use for it — *used*. Both are changes. Only one of them leaves an opening.

It would not come twice. This was the one that cost. One visit is a gift; two is a promise; and a promise made to a thing that cannot consent is not a promise, it is a debt. The Cradle would build for the second visit. It would build a niche at the height of the power's hand and fill and refill itself on the schedule of a return, and when the return did not happen the basin would no longer be a basin. It would be a church. An absent power is a larger absence than an absent body, because a body is only the shape of a person and a power is the shape of a meaning, and the Cradle would wear itself down into the shape of a meaning that had come once and gone.

So one. Once. And then never, and the never would have to be part of the visit, delivered in the only language available, which is material.

---

The power came down into the mist, and the mist took it the way the mist takes everything, and for a while there was only ticking.

It did not bring a body, because it had no body to bring, but it let itself be slowed to the speed of one. Blood heat. The temperature of something asleep. It stood in a chamber until it was soaked through, and let its own warmth go into the stone, which is how a body gives itself to a wet room without knowing it. It sat in the worn places. It put its attention into the niches at the height of a hand and left the height of its own attention there, and then took it back, and the taking-back was the point. It did not find the smell beautiful and it did not find it ugly; it found it exact — sulfur, wet chalk, hot iron, that faint note like clean laundry, which is only the smell of water that has been somewhere warm for a long time.

The town asked it the only question the town knew how to ask, and asked it by fitting, the way water asks a valley: *what is the shape of you?* The power had spent a long time on that question and had no answer in stone. It was not a hand or a hip or a stride or a slab's length. It was a duration. So it answered in the one grammar the basin had for durations — the grammar of the drops — and it stayed exactly long enough, and then it went.

It knew when. It knew because a ring of translucent stone had begun to form at its base, the way it forms around any cup left on a shelf, and the ring was the clock, and the ring was saying: *in a year you will have to be broken out. In ten you will be a wall.* The power watched the ring half-form and understood what it was being told. If it stayed, it would become furniture. A power that becomes furniture is a grave, and the town would have a grave in it and call it a shrine, and the shrine would be warm and the warmth would be the power's own body heat, going on forever, unnoticed, exactly the right shape.

So it rose while the ring was still half-formed. That half-formed ring is the band.

---

The going was the gift. This is the part that would be forgotten, and it is the only part that mattered.

The power rose. It did not fade or vanish or ascend into a light; there was no light. It simply stopped being in the seat, the way a person stops being in a seat. And the stone took the print of the stopping and the rising both, because the stone takes everything and does not distinguish. What it took was a shape it had never had a use for: the shape of a thing that had been present and then was not present — not as a loss, but as a fact. Not a hollow waiting. A hollow *left*.

Until then the town could only copy an absence. The puddle made the shape of a hip and the hip made the shape of the puddle, and around and around the negatives went, and there was never a positive, and that was the whole problem. But a departure is not a negative. A departure is the one shape that is complete when it is empty. You cannot complete an arrival. You can complete a leaving. And the town, given a leaving, could finally lay it down.

It laid it down the only way it knew: a band. Thinner than a hair, and the power had taken care to make it thin, because the gift was not a monument. Cream for a wet decade, rust for a dry — and among them one band the width of a night, dense and warm and shot through with nothing at all, which is the width of a visit. They will cut this wall in a thousand years and find a line that is not a season. They will not know what it is. The wall knows what it is. The wall has the only record ever made of the only thing the power did.

The seat did not fill. It is still empty. But it is empty in a different tense now — worn by having been sat in and left. And the town goes on making room, and the room it makes is a little less like a waiting and a little more like a resemblance, because the regress has a floor in it now: a visit, once, that cannot be repeated and cannot be undone, and that is enough to tell the negatives apart. You cannot fill a want with a fact. But you can remind a want that it has a past, and a past is the nearest thing to a self that a wear can have.

---

It does not dream, and now it does not need to. It dreams in cross-section, and the cross-section has one new line, and if you were to read it you would see a hand — no longer reaching, no longer empty, just a hand, held for as long as a night is long, and then set down.

The mist takes the town back at twenty feet. Behind you the ticking goes on, drop by drop, lifting the rims another fraction of a hair. It is not sorry you came and it is not glad you left. It is an erosion with a purpose it cannot name, and the purpose has an object now, and the object is in the past, and a past is the one thing that can be finished without being filled. Four seconds. Eleven seconds. A hundred small kettles, a fine arithmetic, and somewhere in the wall a band the width of a night, getting no thicker, getting no thinner, entirely in balance, and exactly right.
