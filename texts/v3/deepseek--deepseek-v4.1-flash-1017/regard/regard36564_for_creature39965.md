# What the Wise Power Did

The power noticed the Murmur Sink the way you notice a sound you have been hearing for hours without knowing it. There was no arrival. Notice is not arrival. It simply became aware that somewhere in the world a place was running a loop, and had been running it, patiently, without an audience.

And because it was wise, the first thing it did was wait. A century. Then another. Then longer — long enough that the paperwings rose and fell through several generations and the same call-and-answer came from the same seam and the rubble ticked another inch toward rest. The power did not descend. It listened from the rim, which is where listening begins, and it learned the loops one by one — the way you learn a piece of music that has no melody, only a counterpoint of small things all holding each other in place.

When it had learned them, it understood the correction the sink makes to every mind that comes near: *here, nothing needs to be done.*

---

It considered, first, whether the swarm was a someone.

It looked for a center and found none. It looked longer, and the longer it looked the more the question loosened in its hands. The swarm was a being in the way a fire is a being, or a piece of music is a being: not a self, not a soul, not a thing that could be addressed — but not nothing either. It was unmistakably *itself*. It had been itself for as long as the sink had existed and gave every sign of continuing to be itself indefinitely. The wet carpet would seep, the stone-shells would tick, the keeners would answer, and the whole of it together was as much a single creature as anything with a spine.

So the power treated it as one. Not as a person. As a thing that was already, in its slow small way, complete.

---

A lesser benevolence would have done something.

It would have named the swarm a mind and given it a face. It would have taught the keeners a new note. It would have introduced a season, a predator, a bloom — some gradient, some *change* — to make the sink alive in a way a surface mind could recognize. It would have brought people down to see it and called that love.

The power considered each of these and declined them. Not from coldness. From the specific reverence that comes of knowing what a thing is. The sink was already alive. To improve it would have been to interrupt a sentence in the middle to correct its grammar. The sentence was fine. The sentence was, in fact, the whole point.

It considered a gift — a mark, a sign, something left in a basin where the swarm would find it. But the swarm does not find things. Its loops do not include novelty. A gift would be a foreign body, and it would be incorporated or expelled, and either way the sink would be changed by the fact of having been given something. So no gift. The respect was in the withholding.

---

Two things it did. Small. Invisible.

The first was to keep the water coming. Not the rain — the rain takes care of itself. The deeper thing the rain is only the surface of. The sink is young; its walls are still sharp. The water table that feeds the weeping seams is a recent arrangement, a thing the collapse made, and it could fail. A dry decade. A change upstream. A farmer's well. The seams would go dry, the wet carpet would knot and thin, the keeners would sing to a wall that no longer answered.

So the power did what a gardener does with a sapling it will not live to see mature: it tended the conditions. It did not *add* water. That would have been noticed, would have become a feature of the place, would have made the sink into a place where a power was working. It simply made sure the water that was already coming kept coming — the same pulses, the same rhythm, the same slow seep into the same low basins — for as long as the sink needed.

Which brings the second thing. The power did not freeze the sink in its current form. It could have. The equilibrium looked eternal but was not: it was a young system that had found its attractor fast. The power saw this and made its decision. It would let the sink grow old. It would let the walls round and the strata soften and the sharp shoulders slump into the old age the sink had been pretending to already. It would let the swarm slowly, over thousands of years, become something else — a new note in the hum, a new lane in the dark, a new thing in the wet that no one had named yet. The sink would not remain the Murmur Sink forever. That was the gift. Not permanence. *Time.*

---

And when it was done, the power did the smallest and largest thing of all: it listened. All the way through, once, without moving — the way you listen to a piece you will never hear again. Then it climbed back out. It did not mark the rim. It did not leave a guardian. It did not tell anyone.

The only trace of it is this: the sink has been heard. Once, fully, by something that understood what it was hearing. Nothing in the sink knows this. Nothing in the sink will ever know. The loops go on exactly as before, and the sound is exactly the same sound, and the water drips into the same basins, and the pale stalks hold their closed nodes up to a light that is not enough.

But a thing that has been heard is not quite the same as a thing that has never been heard. The difference is not measurable. It is not a change in the sink at all. It is a change in the world around it — the world now contains one more act of attention than it did before, and attention is the only gift a wise power can give that leaves the receiver exactly as it found them.

The Murmur Sink continues. It is exactly what it is. And it has been heard.
