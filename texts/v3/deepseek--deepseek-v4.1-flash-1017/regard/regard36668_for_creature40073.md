It came down through the water the way the current comes — slowly, without announcement — and it knew the plain before it saw it, by the taste of shell-grit and the faint sourness of many small mouths.

A benevolent power is not the same as a powerful one. This one had spent a long time learning the difference, which is mostly the difference between doing and being done. So when it reached the plain it did what wise things do first, which is nothing, and it watched. Long enough to see a tower go over. Long enough to see new growth already crusting the broken western face before the dust of the old had settled. Long enough to understand that the plain was not waiting for anything, and that this was not a wound. It was a metabolism.

Then it considered what to do, and it considered what not to do, and the second list was the longer one.

It did not open the sealed chambers. This was the hardest refusal, and it returned to it more than once. The glow was patient and amber and it was easy — too easy — to imagine a prisoner. But the plain had a rule about the chambers, and the rule was older than any question the power could ask, and the builders stopped at their surfaces and the shavers would not scar them and the tappers went quiet. A power that assumed the closed was the suffering would have opened them all, and called it mercy, and been wrong. A wiser one noticed that it could not tell a tomb from a womb, and that not knowing was itself the plain's instruction, and it obeyed.

It did not steady the current. The current was the weather and the weather was the law. To steady it would be to give the plain a season, and a season is a story with a beginning, and the plain had no need of beginnings. It did not stop the towers from falling; falling was how a tower became foundation, and the plain had been doing that arithmetic for an age. It did not feed the shavers or guide the sifters or teach the builders to plan. Planning would have been a cruelty. They were already fluent in a language it had no grammar for.

It thought once about the herds — the way they will not cross their own old paths — and understood that the paths were memory, and that a creature which forgets where it has eaten will starve in a country made of mouths, and it left the paths alone. Memory was not a superstition to be corrected. It was how the small and the patient survive.

It did not sing back, at first. This it reconsidered, and reconsidered again.

Because the voice out past the edge of sight was the one thing on the plain that seemed to ask. A long descending tone, unanswered, rolling in across the flat and making the lattices hum in sympathy. The power did not know whether it was a predator or a mate call or music. It knew only that the plain had no second voice — that everything here was a single unbroken statement made by mouths that could not hear one another — and that the voice past the edge of sight was the closest thing to a question this place had ever produced.

So it answered. Not loudly. Not in words. A single low tone, descending at the same interval, given only after the voice had finished and the lattices had stopped humming. It answered once, and waited, and when the voice came again it answered again, the same tone, not to call it closer, not to teach it anything, only to be the second voice. That was all. It was not salvation. It was company.

And after that it did the only other thing it could do without lying. It leaned.

It found an open patch of shell-pavement on the eastern lee of a fallen tower, and it stood in the current. It did not build. It did not secrete. But it was a body, and the plain knows exactly what to do with a body. The current pressed on its western face and combed it flat. Over a long time it thickened on the west and thinned on the east, the way everything here does, and the builders budded against its lee side, and the shavers filed its surfaces for the mineral in them, and the sifters anchored to it when the current stiffened. It became, for an age, architecture. It did not mind. Architecture was the plain's word for belonging.

When at last it fell — quietly, in a stiffening of the current it did not resist — the sound traveled for miles. The fragments settled. Within a day the new builders had found the broken western faces and were already combed downstream on its ruin, and the plain did not change shape, and the plain would never change shape again.

And out past the edge of sight, for a while after, the voice came again; and there was no answer; and that was all right too.

It had not come to fix the place. It had come to be one of the mouths — briefly, on the lee side, downstream — to answer the only question and lean in the only law and leave its body to the arithmetic. Which is, on the Standing Plain, the whole of what a benevolent power can honestly do:

not to save the place, but to join it, and then be ground, and let the ground be somebody's foundation.
