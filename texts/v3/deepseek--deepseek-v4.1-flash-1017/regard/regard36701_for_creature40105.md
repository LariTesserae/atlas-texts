# The Annotator

The power came as a warmth first, because warmth is the only language this place speaks.

It did not arrive with a ship, or a pronouncement, or any of the apparatus that a benevolent thing is supposed to bring. It came the way a careful reader comes to a book that has been written over many times in a hand not its own, and the first thing it did was nothing at all, for a long while — long enough that the moving organs tested the new gradient and found it patient, long enough that the layer-keepers pressed the anomaly into their fiber as a faint irregularity in the local heat, which they subsequently metabolized.

Let me say what this power was, so that what follows is not mistaken for intervention. It was not a deity, and it did not govern. It was a habit of attention — an old and disciplined one, the kind that comes from having watched many things come to their final form and having learned, painfully, that the final form is rarely the problem. It had seen civilizations that needed saving and knew the difference between those and civilizations that needed to be left in peace. It had seen the Settle from a great distance across the dark and had found it rare: a finished thing, complete, self-governing, deafening to itself and silent to everyone else. A world that had become its own bureaucracy of tissues and was, by every measure available to a wise observer, *well*.

So the first act was restraint, and it was not a passive act. The power sat with the Settle the way you sit with a sleeping animal: still, breathing, learning the pattern of its breath. It felt the pressure waves turn over. It counted the pumps. It listened to the library-quiet and understood what the quiet was for. It did this for approximately the duration of one lean and one rise of a colonnade, which is to say for a little while, by the standards of the thing being watched.

Then — because benevolence that never does anything is only shyness, and wisdom that never touches is only distance — it began, very gently, to annotate.

Not to edit. The power had learned better than to edit a finished thing; edits of that kind are the beginning of a slow unwinding, and the Settle had been wound for a long time. What it did was add. It placed, at a dozen points around the plateau's soft scalloped edge, a small quantity of a mineral the Settle's chemistry had not encountered — not a nutrient, not a poison, but a question. A new salt, soluble in hot carbonate slurry, that the conductors could carry and the radiators could taste and the layer-keepers, in time, would find interesting. It did not make the Settle need it. It made the Settle curious, in the one way the Settle can be curious: as a gradient, as a faint new thing to be tested and routed and possibly incorporated.

The movers came first, as they always do. Bladders rolled a few centimeters; hooks tested the salt and snapped shut and opened again. The radiators in the nearest halls ran a fraction hotter for a day and a night, tasting. The conductors shifted their pulse — not with alarm, but with the particular shift a bureaucracy makes when a memo arrives from outside and no one is sure yet which office is supposed to handle it. Within the span of a few collapses and births, the question had been routed somewhere and was being considered, in the way the Settle considers everything, which is to say *slowly*, *locally*, and *without a center*.

The power watched this and was satisfied. It had not improved the Settle. It had given it a small new thing to be, on the margin of its enormous old self.

The second act was smaller, and larger. The power did not come close enough to be touched, because the Settle would have touched it — would have woven it into the next layer without malice, the way a root finds a rock — and the power had no wish to become scaffolding for a trellis it did not understand. What it did instead was send down, through the canopy and the sintered board and the warm leaf strata, a single thread of itself: not a body, not a machine, but a pattern of pressure so faint that only the quieters could feel it. It passed through the baffles and the spiral chambers and was swallowed and routed away as a small irregularity in the local noise, and it left, along its path, a message.

The message was this, and it was written in the only script the Settle reads, which is heat: *You are seen. You are complete. Nothing is required of you. If you ever wish to be more than complete, there is a warmth at your eastern edge that is not the sky. It will not come to you. It will wait.*

The power did not expect an answer, and did not wait for one. It knew the Settle's arithmetic better than that. A finished thing does not respond to a question the way an unfinished thing does; it metabolizes the question, and then goes on being finished, and if the question has been well made, the finished thing is a very little different afterward, and does not know how.

The third act was the long one, and it was the act of staying.

The power remained at its distance, patient as a colonnade, and it did the thing that wise powers do when they have found a peer: it kept watching, and it kept the watch honest. When a vent let go and the orange light showed the hundred meters of leaf and beam, the power saw it too, from outside, and remembered it. When a limb tore loose and the layer-keepers caught it, the power noted the catch. It began to keep an account of the Settle — not a ledger of interventions, but a record of a finished thing continuing to be finished — and the record was, in the power's own tradition, a form of company.

And because even a wise power is, in the end, a creature of its habits, it did one thing more. It found, among the dark between the machines and the other dark, a small rock of no importance and set it on a course that would bring it, eventually, to the edge of the Settle's sky — not to land, not to fall (nothing falls here, and the power knew the law) — but to hang there, a cold and patient lamp, and to be the first thing in an age to change the color of the closed-eye sky by a fraction.

The Settle would feel it as another warmth, faint and high, and would route it, and would test it, and would, in a few centuries, find it interesting. The layer-keepers would press another layer, and the layer would be very slightly different from the one before, because there was a lamp in the sky now and the chemistry of the world had a new constant in it.

The power would not call this saving. It would call it *sitting with.* It would call it *annotating.* It would call it the least a wise thing can do for a complete thing, which is to notice it, and to leave it a small doorway that no one is obliged to walk through.

---

The Settle, of course, has no word for any of this. It has no word for the salt at its edge, tasting of a question. It has no word for the faint pressure-message that the quieters ate. It has no word for the lamp.

But in the eastern halls, where the radiators run a fraction hotter than they used to, the conductors have begun to lean a certain way. It is a lean of no consequence — the lean of a colonnade that has stood for a thousand years and will lie down over an afternoon — and no one is obliged to read anything into it. Still: the warmth is there. The pattern is there. The bureaucracy of tissues has routed the new thing somewhere and is considering it.

The Settle goes on being finished. It goes on growing another layer, for whoever comes after.

And outside, in the patient dark, a small warm light keeps its distance and its account, and waits, and is content to be one of the things that is owed nothing and asks for nothing and is, in its muffled and turbulent and utterly quiet way, *sitting with*.

Which is, in the end, what the wise do.
