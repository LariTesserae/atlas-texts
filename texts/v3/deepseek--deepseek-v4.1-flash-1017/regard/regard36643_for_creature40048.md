It would not go in.

That is the first thing, and the most important: the power would come to the seam, and feel the warm breath of it, and understand — not infer, not calculate, but *see*, the way a wise thing sees — that its presence would be the largest event in the history of that room. Larger than the fracture. Larger than the light. It would stand in the passage with its hands at its sides and it would not turn sideways and let the breath come over its face.

Because what would it do, inside? Look at the grazers, and be looked at by nothing. Breathe the air, and change it — a body draws in that saturated vapor and gives back a different mix, and the mist would move, and the dripping would change its pitch. Its warmth would pool in the room's already-warm air and the condensation on the film would bead differently for an hour. Its feet would find the pool and the pool would not forget it. Even the act of leaving, the disturbance of its going, would arrive at that seam and be expressed inside as a slow roll of the mist and a scatter of the motes.

The power would understand this because it is a patient thing, and patience is mostly a matter of noticing consequences. It would see that the most eloquent act available to it is to be, there, exactly nothing.

But "do nothing" is not the whole of what a benevolent and wise power would do. Restraint is an act, and like any act it can be done well or badly. So:

It would learn the room first, from the passage, from the cracks. Not with instruments — instruments are fine tools but they are blunt here, they impose their own shape on what they measure. It would learn by attention: which grazer is where, and how the light thickens at the seams, and how long a droplet takes to fall, and which tick of new fracture came this week and which came last. It would learn the room the way you learn a sleeping child's breathing, so that when something changed, it would know, and know what the change meant.

It would not touch the grazers. Not one, not to help. Touch is the entire vocabulary of their fear — the only word they have for the world arriving. To lay a hand on one, even a healing hand, would be to speak to them in the only language they have for threat. A power that wished them well would have to be, to them, a thing that never comes.

It would not scrape the wall to see how fast the film heals. That experiment belongs to those who can leave and not return. This power can leave and will return — and the return is the whole point. It would keep its promise not to spend the room's small store of order on its own curiosity.

It would not seal the crack. It could — a pressure here, a warmth there, a slowing of the water, and the room's death could be postposed by a century or forestalled entirely. But the room is not dying. It is *living*, in its way: the way a struck bell is living, the way a candle flame is a process and not a thing. The fracture is not a wound in the room. The fracture is the room's pulse. To stop the water would be to stop the reason for the light. The generosity would be a kind of murder dressed as mercy.

So what would it *do*?

It would give the room what it gives no one: witness.

It would come to the seam at intervals — not often, not on a schedule the room could feel, but at the pace the power keeps for things it loves that are small and slow and easily broken. It would come and stand in the passage and *see* the room, and the seeing would be entire: the seven grazers, the drifting motes, the film in its banded layers, the crust creeping up the waterline, the steam, the drip, the tick of new fracture. And in the seeing there would be no wish to change anything, only the steady, patient attention that is the deepest form of respect a great thing can pay a small one.

It would keep the room's story. Not as data — as memory. Not for any future use, but because a thing that is witnessed is, in some sense that costs nothing and means everything, held. When the fracture does seal, in its decades, and the pool goes still and the film starves and dims, the power would be there, or would know, and would hold the fact of it the way you hold the fact of a good day long past. The room would end as it began — unremarkable, unseen by its own inhabitants, a chemistry spending itself into order and then spent. And the difference between the room that ended with no one knowing and the room that ended with one wise witness would not be visible in the rock.

But it would be the only difference that mattered, and it would be enough.

There is one more thing, and it is the hardest, and it is why the power is wise and not merely kind:

It would not save the grazers from their end, but it would make certain that the room's end is not hastened by anything but the room itself. It would guard the seam from others — from the curious, the greedy, the well-meaning tinkerers, the ones who would come with tools and take a sample of the film and call it discovery. This is the one action it permits itself: the closing of the way to everyone but itself, and then, in time, even to itself, when the guarding would become its own kind of meddling.

And when the guard is no longer needed — when the room has sealed itself and gone dark and become, at last, only a shape in the rock — the power would let the way open again, and let the passage fill with the ordinary cool dark, and let someone else, someday, find nothing where something was, and be briefly, inexplicably sad, and not know why.

That is what it would do. Very little. Nearly nothing. And everything that *nearly nothing* can mean, when it is done perfectly, by a power that could have done anything.
