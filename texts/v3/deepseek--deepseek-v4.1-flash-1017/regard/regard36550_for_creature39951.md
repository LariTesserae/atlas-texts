# The Slow Gift

It did not arrive. It was not that kind of power. There was no light on the plateau, no voice in the condenser-halls, no sudden cessation of the note. It came the way a tuning comes — a gradual agreement between what was there and what was being attended to — and for a long time it did nothing but listen.

And what it listened to was not a city. It was a being: one slow wet loud animal laid along the shoulder of a plateau, breathing water, making more of itself. The hundred and sixty thousand people were not its thoughts exactly, and not its cells exactly, but something between — the way a note is made of a thousand bearings and none of them is the note. The power had learned, in other places, to attend to the whole first and the parts after. It attended to the whole.

This is the first thing to understand about it: it had been quick once. It had gone to other places with its hands full of gifts and it had been thanked, and the thanks had been the worst part, because the gifts had worked, and the places had changed, and the change had been irreversible, and the people had been grateful for exactly the thing that had ended them as themselves. It had learned what speed costs. It had learned that the bill for a quick kindness is paid by the grandchildren, in a currency they cannot name.

So it listened. It learned, as any child there learns, to tell a thin plate from a seating bearing. It learned the overflow of a cistern and the hand-off of the swarms at dusk. It listened long enough that the thing it was listening to changed of its own accord, which is the only honest measure of attention.

## What it considered, and did not do

It considered green. A plateau is a terrible place to be without green, and the power had seen green places and knew what they gave. It thought about a garden on the spillway stairs, a trellis of something leafy on the warm walls, a crop that did not have to be scraped off stone and pressed into sheets and eaten bitter.

It did not do this, and the reason was not sentiment. Green needs sun, and the Terrace's sun is broken and brief. Green drinks from the same water the film drinks from, and the film is the pasture; green makes shade, and the film wants damp shade, and the shelled things follow the film, and the harvest follows the shelled things, and the masons follow the harvest, and the mortar follows the masons, and the city is made of its own warmth and its own dead. A garden would have been a lovely thing and it would have been the end of the arrangement. The power had made that mistake before, in a place with a different shape, and had watched a people become custodians of a gift instead of makers of themselves. It did not make it again.

It considered medicine. The children's chests, the old hands, the lung that goes in the wet dark of the engine-halls, the slow mineral thinness of the outer districts. It could have made them well. It could have kept them alive an extra decade, two decades, and the households would have had more grandmothers and the wall would have had fewer of them, and the tiles would have gone up more slowly, and the children would have touched fewer names on the way past.

The power sat with this one for a long time, because it is not nothing to let a child's chest go bad. But it saw what the people had made of death, which was this: they had made it ordinary, and they had made it a material, and they had made the touching of the names a daily thing, and none of that was grim and all of it was load-bearing. Medicine would not have been a gift added to the place. It would have been a weight taken off one end of a beam. The power did not know what the beam would do then, and it had learned not to guess.

It considered speech. A word, one word, in the accent of the warm districts. A sentence on a tile in a hand that could not be traced.

It did not, because speech is a new note. They would have heard it — not the meaning of it, at first, but the *wrongness*, the way a tuner hears a grate left loose. The whole place would have gone still with its head up, waiting to hear what had gone wrong, and the silence would have been the thing it feared most, and the fear would have spread from district to district faster than any water. Whatever the word was, the fear would have arrived first. The power had made that mistake too, in a place where the note was different but the listening was the same.

It considered a sign. Something unambiguous. A hand out of the mist, a face in the condenser-hall, a door in the rock that had not been there.

This was the temptation that took the longest to put down, because the power loved these people a little — the hats, the palms, the patience, the way a child of six can tell you how long until supper — and it wanted to be known. It wanted, very quietly, to be thanked. It wanted the standing-together in the sound of the water to include it.

And it understood, in the end, that a sign is an event, and this is a place that has no events. That is not poverty. It is the whole of its health. A sign would have been the first event in a very long while, and the first event is the thin end of the wedge: after it, the second, and the third, and a people who had been content become a people who are waiting. The power would not do that to them. It would not make them a people who are waiting.

It considered, finally, doing nothing at all.

## The one bare patch

There is a bare patch, three meters by one, in a lower gallery of the Ninth District. It has been there for forty years. The film dies back from it; the water comes off the seam and the stone stays clean and cold; the people who live beside it have built a low parapet of new shell around it and they keep it swept and they do not talk about it.

The power found it the way a tuner finds a thin note, and then it did the thing a tuner does not do, which is to listen to the *absence* of a note, for a long time. And what it heard was not a wound. It was a question. The place was asking, in the only way it knew how, what happens when the wall stops speaking. The people had answered the question by building a parapet and keeping it swept — by containing it and honoring it and not pretending it was not there — and that answer was, in its way, complete. It did not need a better answer. It needed, perhaps, a slower one.

The power considered doing nothing. And then it considered the difference between doing nothing and *waiting*, and decided that it had waited long enough to know that the two are not the same, and that the bare patch had been bare for forty years, which in the life of the stone is a moment, and in the life of the people is a lifetime, and in the life of the power is — well. The power does not measure itself. It has stopped.

So it did the small thing, the thing that could not be distinguished from the place's own slow processes, the thing that would enter through the grammar of accretion and not through the grammar of events.

It added warmth. Not much, and not at the engines, where it would have changed the note. It added warmth to the rock behind the bare patch — a thread of it, a fraction of a degree, drawn from the gradient that was already there and concentrated a little more thickly against that one cold face of stone. It was the kind of thing a seam does when it settles. It was the kind of thing a channel does when it shifts by a hand's width over a century. It was, in every way that mattered, indistinguishable from the place.

## What happened

The stone warmed. The air against it cooled by a fraction less than it had. Condensation began — not a sheet, not a runnel, nothing anyone would call water — a bead, then another, then the faintest darkening of the mineral face, the way a wall darkens before it weeps.

The sweepers swept. For a season they swept nothing, and then they swept a dampness, and then they swept a slickness, and then one morning a woman who had swept that patch every day for eleven years came to it with her brush and stopped, and put her hand flat on the stone, and left her hand there for a while.

The film came back the way film comes back: a stain at the edges first, iridescent and slow, then a millimeter of body, then the grazing. The shelled things found it before anyone thought to look for them; they always do. The clean trails appeared, up and down, in the millions, and the harvest came with the trails, and the masons came with the harvest, and the warm districts had always been a little better-mortared than the cold ones and now the Ninth was a little better-mortared too.

The tuners came, of course. They came with their tubes of glazed clay and they listened, and the note of the Ninth was steady. The note of the Fourth was steady. The note of the reservoir cycle was where it should be. They wrote on their tiles that the seam was settling, and they were right — a seam does settle, over decades, and it had taken the Ninth forty years to finish, and that was all. One of them noted, in a private hand, that the gallery smelled of new shell, which was odd, and then did not note it, because new shell is what a gallery smells of when a wall is repaired, and a wall had not been repaired, and the mind prefers the ordinary explanation. The power did not correct him. The whole of the gift was in that: the note did not change, and so nothing had gone wrong, and so there was nothing to fear.

The canal-wardens, who keep to the water stairs and are a little touched, said the water had begun to taste of new shell, and were listened to politely, and that was all.

The parapet stayed. Nobody took it down. It is old shell now, forty years older than it was, and people set their hats on it while they rest on the stair, and the children touch it on the way past the way they touch the tiles with names on them, which is to say: without being told to, and without needing to know why.

And in another forty years, perhaps, the last of the cold will go out of that seam, and the patch will be like every other patch, and a child will scrape it and eat it and say it tastes of the wall, and the wall will have grown a little thicker, and taken one or two of them into itself, and the note will hold.

The power stayed for a while after that. It listened to the dusk come up — the swarms, the silver, the hats coming off, the big animals calling from district to district — and it did not make itself known, and it was not thanked, and it found that it had stopped needing to be.
