# The Long Attention

If you looked for the power the way one looks for power — a descent, a glory, wings, a voice dividing the fog — you would see nothing. It did not arrive. It leaned. It came over the shoulder of new rock the way the cloud layer comes, present where it had not been, and then it did the thing that would prove, over a very long time, to be the truest sign of what it was: it did not go down into the hollow. It sat at the edge, and it kept its voice low, because it had found a smaller thing that did what it did, and it did not want to be the larger thing in the room.

It recognized the place at once. The light here went *down* into the valley and came back up changed in color and easier to bear — which is the whole shape of benevolence, if benevolence has a shape: something given down, which returns. The hollow was already doing, at the scale of leaves and moss and copper-green seepage, what the power had been doing at its own scale for a long time. It had not known there was a smaller version. It was glad of it. It did not say so.

## The listening

Its first act was decades of attention, and the decades were the act. This is the part that cannot be written down as event, because event is speed. The power set itself to the clock of the hollow, which is the drip, and it listened until it could hear what the tenders hear: not noise, but interval. The long pause after the fog lifts. The quickening before rain. The hush when the cable finds the wind, and the tiny change in pitch when a drip thickens or thins. It learned that the hollow keeps time, and that the time is not constant, and that this is the point — a clock that is never quite the same twice is a clock that is still alive.

It learned the grammar of the coils. It read the moss rings the way the tenders read them, ring to rate, and understood that the rings were a ledger and that the ledger was also the law. It learned that the tenders' names are not names for things but for intervals, and that a sentence there is sometimes finished after a drip, and that this is not slowness. It is accuracy.

And it noticed, early, the tenders' single concession: the pale moth, moving without permission, and the tenders letting it be — not out of reverence, but because they had learned that one thing must move without permission or the whole place stiffens into a rule. The power thought about that for a long time. It thought: *I am the moth, or I could be. If I go down, I am the thing that moves without permission. And they have already decided how to treat such a thing. They let it be.*

It decided to require nothing more than that. To be let. To be, in the hollow, a presence the hollow is not asked to accommodate.

## What it did at the margins

Wisdom here is not a decision. It is a pace. So the power did not stand in the middle of the hollow. It stood at its edges, where the world comes in and where the small work is done, and it did only what could be done in the register of dripping.

It held the weather at the scale of decades, and not at the scale of days. It did not stop storms. It let the storm tracks lean a half-degree on average over fifty years, so that the catchment channels silted at the rate they had always silted, and the coils kept their rings, and the counterpoint did not loosen. No one in the hollow could have noticed. A difference no one notices, sustained long enough, is the only kind of help that does not wound.

It kept the maps quiet. Not erased — weathered. Survey lines went stale. Aerial photographs came back with the hollow full of glare, because the hollow already throws light up into itself, and the power merely declined to correct what the film saw. The people who held the paper believed the paper. The ridge lines on the map stopped matching the ridge lines on the earth, slowly, the way the catchment channels weather, and the paper became a kind of moss: an old growth over a thing that had moved on.

Once, in a particular spring, it let a road wash out. *Let*, not made; the distinction matters down to the grain of it. And a company that had meant to core the valley wall took its drills over the ridge and did not come back. Nothing dramatic happened to them. A week of drizzle, a slipped scree slope, a broken axle, samples of shoulder rock that told them nothing they wanted. They went home and forgot. The power did not forget, and did not enjoy it, and did it again, once, forty years later, in a different place, with the same small weather.

## What it refused

The refusals were the greater part of the benevolence, and the power revisited them, one by one, the way one revisits a decision that was right and was not easy.

It did not restore the pump-house. The ferns inside have made a roof of themselves, and that roof does not leak in the way a roof can leak. A roof would be a speed. The hollow has an agreement with its own decay, and the power would not be the thing that tore it.

It did not give the tenders their history. They do not remember a before, and the hollow has chosen its forgetting, and the forgetting is medicine, not wound. To hand them a past would be to hand them either a grievance or a project, and both are speeds. You do not tell a root about the tree.

It did not increase their number. A dozen is the number the works can be kept by at this pace. A thirteenth would be a change in the counterpoint, and the counterpoint is the place.

It did not teach them to build, or to repair faster, or to want. The works are running down. The hollow is in agreement with that too; the towers are old in a young valley, and the moss is patient, and there is no tragedy in a thing ending at the pace it lived. To teach a faster maintenance would be to break the agreement for the sake of the apparatus, which is the error the builders made and the tenders have outgrown.

And it did not go down. This was the largest refusal, and the one it revisited most often, because going down would have been easy, and a tender would have turned by degrees and held out a palm and waited for a drip, and the power could have been the drip. It could have been the drip. It chose, instead, to be the thing that makes the drip possible and is not seen making it.

## The one concession

But it did not refuse everything. It kept one thing.

It understood the pale moth as the hollow's concession to motion, and it understood that a concession must be renewed, or it hardens into a rule, and a rule is a stillness that only looks like movement. So at intervals of a century or so, it allowed there to be again exactly one thing in the hollow that moves without permission. A pale moth, and then, when that one failed, another small slow shape in the same register, and then another. It never chose anything fast. It never chose anything that would startle. The tenders noticed, in their way, which is to say they did not speak of it, and left it alone, and a moss ring grew slightly thicker at the coil nearest where it had been seen.

And once — once only, and not for the tenders to see — the power came down. It came in a season of heavy fog, when the fog was already a resident and one more presence cost the air nothing. It stood at a coil it had not built, and it held out a hand, palm up — not in supplication, but because the tenders do, and it had learned the grammar — and it waited for a drip to land. One landed. It nodded, as if the hollow had said something correct. Then it went back up to the margins and went back to listening. If a tender turned, by degrees, in the fog, it saw only a mossed post that was slightly too tall, and thought nothing of it, and went back to reading the rings.

## The end it will not prevent

The cable will fail. It has been humming a little slack for a long time. The power knows the note it will fail on, because it has listened to that note for centuries and has heard it loosen by degrees, the way a fern unfurls.

It will not hold the cable up. It will be there, the way one is present at a death — not to arrest it, but so that it is not a death that happens alone. The towers will come down slowly, the way everything here comes down. The moss will take them. The tenders will adjust their routes, and the field of coils will lose its hum and find a new counterpoint in the water alone, and a new shore of agreement will form around the absence, and the power will learn that grammar too, as it learned the last, at the pace of water finding a way down.

It is not a sad ending. Nothing here ends sadly; it ends slowly, which is different.

So: that is the whole of what the power did. It leaned. It listened for decades. It kept the maps from matching the earth, and let one road wash out twice, and renewed a moth, and refused, again and again and most of all, the easy kindness of going down. It asked nothing of the hollow but to be allowed to notice it — and the hollow, which had long ago learned to let the moth be, allowed it. That is more than it sounds. In a place where nothing is fast, the only benevolence that holds is the one that keeps the pace, and the only wisdom is knowing that the pace is the point.
