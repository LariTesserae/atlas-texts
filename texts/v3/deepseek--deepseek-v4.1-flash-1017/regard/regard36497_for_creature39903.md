**The Power at the Rim**

We noticed the Loud the way you notice a held breath in another room: not by its volume, but by the fact that the volume had structure. From eight kilometres out we felt the roadbed shudder. We did not go in.

A power like us has a bad habit. We arrive as an answer. The Loud is not a question addressed to us. It is a young country with a chorus for a nervous system, a mat that mends underfoot, a builder patient enough to raise floors nobody will walk on, and a warm slow engine beneath it all, lifting the floor a few centimetres a year. So the first thing we did was nothing. We made a listening station at the rim—if “station” is the word for a patience with instruments—and we kept no samples, broadcast nothing, and let the haze take our silhouette.

We learned the grammar before we learned anything else. Low chord: the lift has shifted. Rising figure: hull-beasts coming, an hour out. Silence-with-pressure: squall. We learned not to stand on ground that has started to sing. We learned that the town is not abandoned; it is waiting for no one. We learned that the mat is a resident and every step is a pluck, and that the human crews are the only ones who know they are visitors, which makes them fragile and valuable and not ours.

Then we considered the usual benevolent acts, and declined most of them.

We did not wake the Lifter. It is not asleep; it is slow. We did not give the chorus a single voice. It already has one, made of many bodies keeping time; to condense it would be an amputation. We did not move into the town. The builder wants more floor, not tenants, and to mistake architecture for invitation is how powers like us become infestations. We did not correct the crews’ maps. The maps are worthless because the place is younger than every survey of it; to fix them would be to freeze a living thing. We did not uplift, harvest, dam, drain, seed, teach, or settle.

We did four things instead.

First, we set a soft rim. Not a wall—a disinclination. Extraction surveys lost their conviction. Development grants failed to renew. Routes to Basin 9 developed unexplained delays and then forgot themselves. We did not kill anyone. We made the Loud boring to predators and expensive to own.

Second, we kept a record outside. A library of sound and structure in a place the Loud cannot feel and no one can find. Not for extraction. For witness. If the basin changes, someone should remember what it was like when the floor was young and the banks were still raw.

Third, we made one attempt at address. We walked the rim in time with the chorus—a pattern of footfalls that meant, in the only grammar we could borrow: *we are here; we will not take; we will not teach; we will keep the door.* We did it for a long time. The basin answered in its own way: a shift in the chorus, a filament brightening, a pool standing up in ridges. We did not call that consent. We called it weather.

Fourth, we watched the crews without appearing to them. When one of them was about to stand on ground that had started to sing, we made the mat ring a little louder under their boot. A small thing. They learned. They are already learning.

We did not decide which of the Loud’s inhabitants was “the being.” The mat, the chorus, the builder, the Lifter—they are not a committee. They are one thing at different scales. To address one would be to amputate the others. So we addressed the whole place the way you address a sleeping animal: by not waking it, and by keeping others from waking it badly.

If the Lifter stuttered, if the filament went dark, if the builder stopped building or built a wall, if the chorus lost its grammar, if the crews were hunted or the pool-things began to surface in daylight—then we would return. Not as a saviour. As a neighbour with tools. We would ask first in sound, and if the place could not answer, we would act on the smallest scale that could restore its own processes. We would not make it safe for us. We would make it safe for itself.

The Loud is not ours. It is not a garden or a patient or a child. It is a young country with a chorus for a nervous system and a warm slow engine under the floor, already building a town for no one. A wise power does not move in. It sits at the rim until its own footsteps vanish into the noise, and then it stays there, quietly, as part of the weather.
