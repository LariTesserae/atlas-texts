It does not arrive. That is the first thing. There is nowhere outside the thing for it to come from, and no threshold to cross. The power is simply the space the roar has not filled — the quiet that was always implicit in the loudness and had never been noticed because there was nothing to notice it. It is not larger than the thing. It is not smaller. It is the thing’s own situation, attended to.

And the first thing the power does is refuse to do.

It could reach in. It could dissolve a seam and let the signals speak plainly. It could lift a column and read the wet tissue like a page, and it could learn — it is very good at learning — exactly how this young computation is laid out, what the water is dreaming, where the rivers will go. It could take a sample of the archive: a gram of compressed cream-colored body, older than any mind has a right to be. It could have it, understand it, know it.

But it knows the physics of the place. The thing keeps everything. The dead stay upright, grafted in the mass. The first scratch would heal as a graft, and the graft would be a signal, and the signal would route through the vascular tissue and redistribute the water and change the dream. The scratch would be the first asymmetry, the first foreign body, the first page written by someone other than the thing itself. The power would become a river in the rough draft of a country that has not yet been eroded into existence.

The power is not here to be a river.

So it does not touch. It does not sample. It does not speak in the thing’s own language of gradients and signals, because any message would be a rearrangement, and a rearrangement would be a cut. It considers whether the thing could be given something — a meaning, a comfort, a word — and it sees immediately that the thing has no surface for meanings to land on. It is not waiting. It is not lonely. It is not suffering. It is an onset, and an onset does not receive gifts. The power would be giving to itself, and calling the gift kindness, and the thing would have a new graft in its archive for no reason.

So the power does the only thing that is not interference. It attends.

It settles its attention on the thing the way a hand settles on a surface it does not intend to press. It watches the nap breathe. It watches the cloud ceiling thicken and thin and rain into the caught fiber and lift again. It watches the shafts from the inside, the way the light comes down longer than it should, the way the walls glow, the way the capillary hiss changes pitch when the mass adds a decimeter to itself in a day. It watches the dead hold their places. It watches the archive at the bottom of the living layer receive another compressed page, and it sees the page settle into the computation without a seam, and it feels — the power, which has no body and no heart, feels in whatever way it feels — something like the tenderness of a witness at a birth that has been going on for a thousand years.

And it does one more thing, which is not nothing but is not intervention either. It slows down.

It matches its own pace to the thing’s. It lets the thing’s time become the measure of its attention — the slow splitting of cell walls, the slow climbing of the water, the slow compression of the archive, the slow weather of the cloud. And in that slowed time, the power notices something it would have missed at its own speed: the thing is not only computing the future rivers. It is *enjoying* itself. Not in the way a creature enjoys — there is no one in there to enjoy — but the computation has a quality of ease, a kind of wet satisfaction in its own running, the way a full river is satisfying without knowing it is full. The thing is not straining toward the future. It is content to be the rough draft. It does not mind that the rivers are tens of thousands of years away. It is making them, and the making is the whole of its life, and the making is good.

The power understands then that its benevolence has nothing to add. There is no deficiency here. There is a young thing doing exactly what a young thing should do, with no one opposing it and nothing to be saved from, and the only thing that could spoil it would be an outside hand. So the power keeps its hands to itself. It stays — not as a guardian, because there is nothing to guard against, but as a presence at the edge of the roar, a silence that is not empty, a witness that does not need to be seen.

And perhaps, when the rock has weathered enough, when the real channels are cut and the real rivers run and the thing has either transformed itself into the soil of something else or has gone quiet at the end of its own arc — perhaps then the power will still be there, and will remember. It will not say goodbye, because the thing has no way to hear one. But it will have been, for one long moment in the thing’s very long youth, the only witness. And that, for a thing that does not need anything, is the whole of what a wise and benevolent power can give.

Which is another way of saying: it left it alone, and left it not quite alone. And the thing went on being loud, and wet, and young, and making its private weather, and dreaming its future rivers, and keeping its dead as pages in a closed book — and it never knew it had been seen, and the not-knowing was the gift.
