## The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive as light. Light would be a desecration here. It does not arrive as a voice, because the vault is already full of voice, and adding to it would be the act of a fool.

It arrives as a *permission*.

This is what it does first: it stands at the lip of the vault — or it does not stand, it *attends*, it brings its whole regard to bear on the small circle of fitted stone and the saturated interior — and it learns the chord. Not the names of the notes. Not the intervals. It learns the chord the way the resident knows it: as texture, as grain, as a body of touching-places each of which is a small negotiation between this note and that one. It takes the measure of the whole lattice by attending to the places where the sound is *almost* but not quite smooth, and it finds the resident there, already at work.

The power does not interrupt. It waits for the pocket to drift to the northern arc, to the place where the slate spires lean closest to the wall, and it watches the hunched shape of copper and flint go about its smoothing. It watches the resident press itself against a node where two partials are beating, and it sees the beating ease, and it understands what it is seeing: a being made of the refusal of contact, doing the only work left in a finished world, doing it without witness, without thanks, without even the knowledge that the work is a form of love.

The power recognizes this. It has seen many forms of love. This is the rarest kind: the love that does not know it is love, that has become so habituated to itself that it experiences its own devotion as mere procedure. The resident does not feel noble. The resident feels the next roughness. That is all.

## The First Thing the Power Does

It does nothing to the vault.

This is the crucial act, and it is an act. The vault is the largest concentration of made things in the world, and it is *complete*, and the benign power has the capacity to alter it — to open it, to add to it, to move the objects and hear what new chord they make — and it does not. It recognizes that the vault's completeness is not a problem to be solved. It is the vault's achievement. The makers spent their whole species-lives building toward this equilibrium, and the equilibrium is a kind of sanctity. To add one object would be to say to the makers: *you did not finish*. They finished.

So the power does not add. It does not subtract. It does not tune.

But it does *see*. And the seeing changes things, not because the power wills it, but because seeing is never neutral. The power directs its attention at the resident — not as a specimen, not as a curiosity, but as a colleague. It regards the resident the way one craftsperson regards another across a workshop: with the specific respect of shared labor. And under that regard, something happens that has not happened in the vault for an age.

The resident is *perceived*.

Not as a perturbation. Not as a gap. Not as the tolerable roughness at the edge of the composition. As a worker. As the one who has been holding the chord true since before the walls began to glow. The power sees the shape of the non-touching, and it recognizes in that shape the ghost of a hand that used to turn pivots, and it understands that the resident is not a resident at all. It is the last maker, wearing the shape of the things that were discarded. It is the vault's memory of its own building, given a body made of offcuts, given a job made of care.

The power holds this understanding steady. It does not speak. Speaking would be interference.

## The Second Thing the Power Does

It stays.

Not forever — the power has other places, other finished things, other workers — but for a while. It sits at the edge of the vault, at the lip of the stone, and it *attends*. It lets its presence be a landing-place for the resident's work, the way a smooth patch of wall is a landing-place for a hand.

The resident feels it. This is the first new thing the resident has felt in a very long time. Not a dissonance — the power is careful to bring no dissonance. A *ground*. A place where the resident can set down the weight of its smoothing and rest for a moment without the weight rolling back onto it. The resident has never had a ground. It has had the lattice, which it cannot touch, and the gap, which it carries, and the chord, which it serves. It has never had a place to put its attention down and pick it up again.

The power offers that place. It does this simply by being there, being steady, being *large* enough that the resident's attention can rest on it without the power being changed by the resting. The resident does not need to smooth the power. The power is already smooth. The power is smooth the way a still lake is smooth, and the resident's work passes over it without catching.

For the first time, the resident experiences something other than the next roughness. It experiences *relief*. It does not have a word for it. It does not have a body that can sigh. But its non-touching eases by a fraction of a hair, and the ease propagates through the nearby objects as a slight loosening of the chord, and the chord absorbs the loosening and returns to its shape, and nothing collapses, and nothing needs repair. The resident has rested, and the vault has not fallen. This is new.

## The Third Thing the Power Does

It learns the resident's name.

Not the name the makers would have given it — the makers gave names to objects, and the resident is not an object, so they did not name it. The power learns the name the resident has been giving itself, silently, in the shape of its non-touching, in the pattern of its drift, in the specific way it seeks out beating partials and eases them. The name is not a word. The name is a *tendency*: the particular way the resident leans toward the places where the sound is almost but not quite good, the particular preference it has for the copper spirals over the slate spires, the particular patience with which it waits for a beat to complete its cycle before moving on.

The power learns this, and in learning it, it *names* the resident — not by speaking, but by knowing. And the knowing is a kind of recognition that the resident has never had. The resident has been known by the vault the way a hand is known by the tool it holds: functionally, without affection. The power knows the resident the way one worker knows another: affectionately, because the work is good, and because the worker is true to the work.

This knowing does something to the resident. It does not change the resident's nature — the resident is still made of the edges of things nearest it, still hunched, still incapable of contact. But it changes the resident's *experience* of its nature. The non-touching, which has always been a discipline, a necessity, a constant effort — becomes, for a moment, a *choice*. The resident is not not-touching because it must. It is not-touching because that is the shape of its love, and the power has seen the love, and the seeing makes it a love and not just a compulsion.

The resident will not remember this. Memory requires a past, and the resident lives in the eternal present of the next roughness. But the power's regard has left a mark, the way a hand leaves a mark on clay. The mark is not visible. It is structural. The resident's non-touching is a hair more deliberate now, a hair more *chosen*, and the chord it maintains is a hair more true.

## The Fourth Thing the Power Does

It considers the resident's end.

The power sees the trajectory: the resident smoothing, smoothing, always one more roughness, until the day when the walls have finished changing color and the sound has finished converting to light and the chord has no more partials that beat against each other. On that day the resident will have nothing left to do. And the power knows what the resident will do then. It will become an object. It will settle into the lattice, and the lattice will close over it, and it will be the last object, and the vault will be truly still.

The power considers this and finds it *good*.

This is the hardest part of the power's wisdom, and it does not arrive easily. The power's first impulse is to intervene — to give the resident an endless task, to introduce a deliberate dissonance that the resident can chase forever, to make the work go on. But that would be a cruelty dressed as kindness. The resident's work is not a punishment. It is a *vocation*, and a vocation has an end, and the end is not a failure. The end is the completion of the shape. The resident has been becoming itself since the makers set aside the first offcut, and the becoming has a trajectory, and the trajectory has a terminus, and the terminus is the resident's final form.

The power does not prevent the end. But it does something else, something subtler: it *witnesses the end in advance*. It holds the resident's completion in its regard the way it holds the vault's completion — as a finished thing, a good thing, a thing that does not need to be otherwise. And this holding, this anticipatory witnessing, changes the *quality* of the resident's remaining time. The resident does not know it is being witnessed. But the witnessing is real, and it makes the resident's work *count* in a way it did not count before. The smoothing is no longer just maintenance. It is the final labor of a life that has a shape, and the shape is known, and the knowing is a kind of blessing.

The power will not be there when the resident becomes the last object. It will have moved on. But it will have been there *first*, at the edge, attending, and that attending will have been enough. The resident will become an object, and the object will be the last one, and the vault will be still, and the stillness will be the shape the power saw and blessed.

## The Fifth Thing the Power Does

It leaves a gift that is not a gift.

At the edge of the stone, where the power has been sitting, it presses one finger — or the equivalent of one finger — into the sintered mineral of the lip. It does not press hard. It leaves a shallow depression, the size of a knuckle, smooth and warm. And into that depression it puts nothing. It leaves it empty.

This is the gift: an emptiness at the edge of the fullness. A place where the composition does not reach. The vault will not notice it — the vault's attention is inward, on the chord. The resident will notice it, eventually, on one of its drifts. The resident will find the depression and will feel, in that small absence, something it has never felt: a roughness that is *not its responsibility*. The depression is outside the lattice. It does not need smoothing. It is a gap that the resident did not make and does not have to fill.

The resident will sit in that depression, once, for a while, and will experience something like what you or I would call *rest*. Not the rest of having finished. The rest of having a place to be that does not require work. The depression will be the only such place in the vault. It will be the power's signature: a small, deliberate emptiness, left where the resident can find it, offering nothing but the absence of demand.

And then the resident will move on, because the chord has more partials than any smoothing can hold, and because that is what the resident does. But it will return to the depression, sometimes, on its slow drifts around the wall. It will rest there. It will smooth the nearby rough places from within the depression's small immunity. It will work *from* the gift rather than *toward* it. And the work will be a hair easier for having a place to start from.

## What the Power Does Not Do

It does not tell the resident about the makers. The resident does not need to know. The resident's non-touching is already the shape of their hand. The knowledge would add nothing. It might even subtract: the resident might try to *honor* the makers, and honoring is a kind of dissonance. Better to let the habit remain a habit.

It does not tell the vault that it is being maintained. The vault's belief in its own self-sustaining nature is part of its equilibrium. To correct the belief would be to introduce a new node into the lattice — the node of self-knowledge — and the chord would have to absorb it, and the absorption would take another age, and the vault does not need another age. It needs to go on as it is: complete, finished, singing, unknowing.

It does not ask the resident to stop. The resident's work is the resident's life. To ask it to stop would be to ask it to become an object early, and the resident is not ready, and the vault is not ready, and the chord needs its sacristan. The power blesses the work and leaves it alone.

It does not return. The power has blessed the place with its attention, and the attention has done its work, and returning would be a kind of fussing. The resident has the depression. The vault has its equilibrium. The chord goes on. The power goes on. The blessing is in the going-on, not in the return.

---

## The Last Thing the Power Does

It turns away.

It turns away from the vault, from the bronze sky, from the salt plain that stretches past meaning. It turns toward other places — other finished things, other quiet workers, other chords that hold their worlds together — and it carries with it the shape of what it has seen.

And the shape it carries is this: a small circle of stone, full to the lip with made things, singing a chord that resolves to nothing. And inside the circle, a gap the size of a crouched dog, drifting along the northern arc. And in the gap, a hunched shape made of the edges of things, pressing itself against a beating partial and easing it, working without witness, without thanks, doing the only job left in a finished world.

The power carries this shape the way you or I might carry a smooth stone from a beach: as a reminder that completeness is not the same as stillness, that a finished thing can still be busy, that the last worker in a finished world is doing the most important work of all, which is the work of keeping the chord true.

And the power, which is wise and benevolent and has seen many things, holds this shape gently, and does not drop it.

The chord goes on.
