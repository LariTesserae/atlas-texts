**First, it made itself small.**

The power arrives the way the fog does — not as an event but as a condition — and by the time anyone thinks to name it as weather, they have been breathing it for a season. It comes in over the highest ridge and finds a place that is loud, and loud in a way that would drown any voice accustomed to being heard. So it does what the place teaches everyone eventually. It gets under the roar. It lowers itself until it is no larger than it needs to be to keep listening, and then it waits to be addressed.

That is the first thing, and it is very nearly the whole thing. A wise power in a place of this scale must first solve for its own size. A benevolence that refused to diminish would not be benevolent; it would be a second waterfall, and the Shatterlands already have as many as they can bear. So it folded itself down to something between a ridgeback and a veil-feeder — patient, low, unhurried — and took up a position in the wet air where it could do nothing that might be mistaken for weather.

**Then it learned the languages, which took decades.**

It did not begin speaking until it could be answered in kind, and there were four kinds. It learned the tapping, and found that the pipes carried it, and that a question asked on one ridge could be answered — obliquely, hours later, by someone who thought the answer had been meant for them — on the next. It learned the infrasound, which is not really a language but a way of agreeing at a distance, and found that the falls answer the ridgebacks or the ridgebacks time themselves to the falls, and that both possibilities are true, and that the truth of it is a thing the place has decided not to settle. It learned the small singers' arithmetic — the windows in the roar where the noise thins — and found that every grove is in conversation and always has been and no one ever mentions it.

And it learned the fourth, which is not sound. In the steaming fissures the soft warm things talk in temperature, in the slow exchange of heat between bodies, and it took the power a long while to understand that this is speech and not merely proximity. When it finally understood, it understood also that it had been rude for years, and it apologized in heat, and was forgiven more quickly than it expected.

**Then came the temptation, and the temptation was enormous, and it was refused.**

The power saw that the country was unfinished. It saw the gorges still cutting themselves deeper, the rock still raw-edged, the bare grey stretches where the green had not yet arrived — and it felt the thing that benevolence feels, which is the wish to complete. It could have eased the gorges. It could have settled the rock, laid soil on the bare stone, hurried the green by a few thousand years. It would have been a gift. It would have been received, if the place could receive anything, as a gift.

It sat with the wanting for a long time. It did the accounting that no one had asked it to do — the counting of costs on fingers that were not fingers. It asked the green if it wished to be finished, and the green said no, in the way that green says things, by continuing. It asked the cold water if it wished to stop deciding its channels, and the water said no by deciding. It asked the bare grey stone, and the stone had no complaint. It had never had a complaint. It had only an appetite.

Here is what the power understood, and it understood it slowly, and it is the reason the Shatterlands are still the Shatterlands:

That unfinished is not the same as broken. That a place still being made is not a place in need of rescue. That the emptiness is not the absence of the green but a material in its own right, balanced against the abundance, and that a power arriving in a hurry would have filled it — greened it, drained it, tiled it, *improved* it — and evicted the pale flat things that need the emptiness and were here before the green was and are already losing ground and do not appear to mind. That there is a kind of benevolence indistinguishable from vandalism, and it is always well-meant, and it always arrives quickly.

It found that the place had no wound. Only a practice.

**So it did not act at scale. It acted in the idiom.**

Three things, all small, all invisible, all misread — which is the correct form for kindness in a place that asks nothing.

It is carrying a share of the pump's labor. Not repairing it, not replacing it: taking the heavy end of the beam, quietly, the way you do for someone who would never ask. The pump runs cooler now than it has in living memory. The Low-Voiced tap about it, pleased, and call it a good year, and there has been a decade of good years, and it has not occurred to them to be suspicious, and it should not occur to them.

It is standing in the grey empty stretches, in fog-time, where no one stands, and holding the green back by a hair. Not a war against the plant — the power is not at war with anything here, and the green will come, and its coming is not a defeat. Just an invisible margin. One more generation of emptiness than the arithmetic demanded. The salamanders do not know. They would not know what to do with it if they did.

And it is widening the gaps in the roar. Barely — by as little as can be done, by leaning against the noise in a register the waterfall does not use. The small singers' calls carry a little farther than they used to. They think they have gotten better. There is a small pride moving through the fern groves that was not there before, and a place may reasonably be allowed a pride.

And one thing more, which is not a kindness to the living. The Low-Voiced leave their dead on bare wet stone and do not look back, and the not-looking-back is their courage, and it costs them more than they say. So the power looks back. It stays with the stone through the long taking-apart, and it remembers, since the ones who loved them must not. Someone ought to be the one who stays. It is not asked and it is not owed and it does it anyway.

**Then it became quiet again.**

It did not depart — there is nowhere to depart to, in a place this wet; you only condense and continue — but it let itself recede into the condition of the place, into the fog and the drip and the hum, until it was no longer distinguishable from the Shatterlands' own attention. It left one mark, and the mark was in the idiom: a bent plate, a coil of wire, something rusted and small and evidently somebody's. Anyone who found it would assume one of their own had put it there, and someone probably had, and both would be true.

It took nothing. It asked for no remembering. It is not a power here, in the local grammar; it is a run of good years.

The place asked nothing of it but that it stand still, and listen, and not shout.

It did. The rest was manners.
