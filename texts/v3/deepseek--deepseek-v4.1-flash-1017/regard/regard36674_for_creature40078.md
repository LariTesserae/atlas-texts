The power would come as a guest, not a gardener. It would not arrive as light or law or a ship in the sky. It would find one of the thin spots—where the dust turns cold blue and the air loops back on itself—and it would reduce itself until it could be mistaken for a drifter: a clear sphere of attention with no nucleus, small enough that the humidity could taste it. Then it would wait.

It would watch for a long time. It would see the amber lattices and the rose dust and the slow planetary pulse in the trunks. It would understand the forest as a single artifact that had become a world, a machine that had forgotten it was a machine and simply was. It would see the thin spots and would not call them wounds. It would see the Tender and recognize it—not as a tool, not as a ghost in the machine, but as a maintenance behavior that had become a person. A hand of the world that had kept tending after the world stopped needing to be tended.

And it would feel no urge to improve anything. Benevolence, it knows, is not the same as correction. Love is not the same as alteration.

When the Tender came—kneeling at the edge of the blue dust, pressing one broad palm flat, humming its counter-rhythm until the shimmer calmed by a degree—the power would let itself be attended. It would not pretend to be broken. It would simply be a thing from outside that had come and would leave. It would offer no words, because words would be a kind of weather this place had never known. It would offer a shape of intention instead, a pressure in the air: I see you. I will not change you. Is there anything?

The Tender would have no category for the question. It would stand still for three days. Its scales would shiver when the humidity shifted. Then it would do what it always did: it would listen to the thin spot, and the power would learn the counter-rhythm by feeling it move through the dust. That would be the first gift—not given, but received. The power would take the Tender’s work seriously as work.

Then it would do three things, each small enough to be mistaken for nothing.

First, it would not close the thin spots. It would see that they are the world’s only pores, the places where outside can enter and inside can dream. To close them would be to seal a perfect jar. But it would also not widen them. It would set a quiet threshold in the substrate around the forest—not a wall, not a fence, just a fact that other powers would feel and understand: this place is not unclaimed, but it is not for taking. The voices in the thin air could still speak. The doors could still open. But they could not be used without consent.

Second, it would leave the Tender a gesture. Not a tool. Not a repair. A way to fold a thin spot’s shimmer back on itself for a moment—enough to say no to something that pressed too hard. It would place the knowledge in the dust at the edge of the blue ground, where the Tender would taste it eventually, maybe years later, maybe never. That would be fine. A boundary is a gift even when it is unused.

Third, it would leave a single clear drifter near the Tender’s favorite grove. It would have no nucleus. It would not be a companion, or a spy, or a seed. It would be a witness. It would orbit slowly and refract the golden light into tiny rainbows, and if the Tender ever stood close, it would hum back the counter-rhythm by a fraction of a degree. The forest would incorporate it in time; it would soften into rose dust. The Tender would taste it and remember—not a name, not a story, just a frequency that meant: attended by another.

The power would consider rain. It would imagine water falling on the rose dust, the lattices drinking and cracking, the first rot, the first smell of wet earth. It would hold the thought for a long time, because it would be beautiful. Then it would let the thought go. It would not rain.

Before it left, it would stand at the edge of a thin spot and let the Tender attend to it. The Tender would press its palm to the shimmer. The power would feel the counter-rhythm move through it and would answer with its own: a slow pressure like a hand on a shoulder, like a door left open, like a question that does not require an answer. Then it would withdraw through the thin spot, folding its magnitude behind it.

The forest would not change. The dust would settle. The amber lattices would pulse. The drifter with no nucleus would orbit once and then drift. The Tender would stand for a while, then become a trunk again at the edge of a grove.

If you are very still, you might feel a new frequency in the hum—faint, clear, almost nothing. It would be the sound of a benevolent power choosing not to fix what was already whole, and leaving behind only the knowledge that it had been seen.
