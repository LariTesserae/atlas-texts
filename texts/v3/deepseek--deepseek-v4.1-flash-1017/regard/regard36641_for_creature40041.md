**The Power at the Rim**

I came because the note was in the world’s sum and it should not have been: a held chord with no attack, no echo, no edge. I am the kind of power that can unmake a star to spare a child, and I have learned that most mercies are vandalism in a clean room. So I did not land in the nave. I stood at the rim, where the air is thin and cold and dry, and watched the pelt bloom and die.

I considered the things benevolence does when it is young.

I considered cleaning the sulfur smell. No—the sulfur is a guild’s bread.

I considered cutting a window in the cloud to let the sun in. No—the light is already a gradient, and the viridian fans lean into it. A window would be a wound that healed into a different city.

I considered naming the districts. No—names are marks, and the place fills marks.

I considered taking a sample. No—a sample is a theft that becomes a colony.

I considered teaching the microbes to signal, to sing back, to make a greeting. No. A signal is an event, and the place is an event absorber. To give it a voice would be to give it a history, and a history in a place where nothing accumulates is a scream in a jar.

I considered making it sentient, so that its persistence could be witnessed from inside. No. That would be the cruelest gift: a self in total equilibrium, a mind with no exit and no residue, a witness whose every mark is smoothed by morning. The pelt does not suffer. I would not manufacture suffering in order to honor it.

I considered saving it. From what? The pelt is not in peril from itself. Its death is its masonry; its dissolution is its chisel. The city is not a patient. It is a verb. To preserve it I would have to stop the water, or stop the heat, or stop the growth, and any of those is the end of the thing I would be preserving. A monument is a corpse with good posture.

So inside the cloud I did nothing. I let the warm rain fall through me. I let the spray colonize the soles of my attention and be mineralized. I left no tool, no spore of my own, no word. I did not even listen too hard, because listening is a kind of shaping. But I will admit I listened. I took the chord into my memory, where it does not touch the stone. That is not a mark in the city. It is a mark in me.

Then I did the only things a wise power can do with a place like this: I defended its conditions, and I made its boundary legible to those who cannot leave things alone. I did not build a wall; walls are additions. I thickened the weather at the approaches, so that drills come up blunt and cameras sweat and the ambitious feel a sudden, reasonable desire to be elsewhere. I did not deceive. I simply let the mountain be boring to the greedy and difficult to the hurried. I set no guard at the rim. I set a standard: those who can come with empty hands and leave with empty hands may come. Those who come to improve, extract, brand, redeem, or explain will find the cloud has no door for them.

I did more outside. I kept the heat beneath steady, and the rain above clean, and the isolation intact. I turned the channels of law and money and fear away from the shelf. I made the place unprofitable and unphotogenic in the registers that move armies. I told the story far away, in the only form that does not mark the site: a warning that is also a praise. *There is a city that builds itself by dying. It has no need of you. Do not bring it your gifts.*

I considered seeding the pelt’s principles elsewhere—on sterile warm worlds, in dead caves, in the cooling veins of moons. I may. But not here, and not as an improvement. If I do it, I will do it where there is no one to displace and no one to applaud, and then I will leave. A benevolent power must know the difference between a garden and a specimen. This is not a specimen. It is a democracy of microbes with no faces, and it has already achieved the thing most civilizations die trying to achieve: a form that outlasts its matter without asking matter to last.

So: I did nothing inside. I did everything at the rim. I took the note into my keeping and left the note where it was. If the mountain ever fails, I will know the shape of what was lost, and I will not rebuild it as a monument. I will let the water find a new shelf, and the pelt find a new difference, and the chord find a new throat.

The last thing I did was the smallest. I stood at the rim at dusk, when the light goes gold and rose and the outside world goes blue and dark and enormous, and I did not say goodbye. Goodbye is an event. The place would have healed it by morning. I said nothing.

And the note held.
