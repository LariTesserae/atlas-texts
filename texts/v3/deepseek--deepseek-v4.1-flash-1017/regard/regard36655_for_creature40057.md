# The Second Beat

The power does not come with light.

It has light. It leaves the light where it was, at the edge of things, because it has been to places like this before and it knows what light would do here: not reveal, but wound. The eye in this country has learned to stop insisting, and light would be the insisting resumed. So the power comes the way weather comes — warm, at the temperature of skin, saturated and slow, so as not to be an event.

It stands in the knee-water at the edge of the flat and does not move for a long time. The film gathers on it. The small ones travel across the tops of its feet and part around its calf and close again. At the waterline they gather in a fine stitching, testing whether it is food or surface or weather. The power lets them decide. This is the first thing it does, and it is most of what it does: it lets the country describe it, by its own method, and does not interrupt.

After a while it walks. It goes slowly, the way you learn to go here once the country has bent you twice. It sets a bearing and the flat bends it, gently, over the course of an hour, and the pump house it left behind is ahead of it again, and it understands that this is not a trap. It is a preference. The flat prefers to keep what is in it. The power does not argue with the preference. It adjusts its walking to the local grammar, which is: go slowly, and do not trust the direction of your own going, and let the country have the last word about where you are.

It counts. One stroke every four seconds. It puts its palm on a standpipe and feels the hum in its teeth and counts, and loses the count, and finds it again, and the finding is the point, not the number. It counts long enough to learn that the stroke is not a machine-noise. It is a pulse. The flat is not a place that contains a circulation; it is a circulation that has taken the shape of a place. The pumps are its pulse, the channels are its vessels, the attendants are its hands, and the being who lives in it is not any one of these. It is the arrangement. The small ones ride the pulse through the mud, the breathers know it in their flanks, the borrowers hold it in their sacs along with the sounds they steal. The power learns the pulse the way the residents have, from the inside of contact, and then it knows something the residents do not: the flat has never heard its own pulse. There has never been an outside to hear it against.

---

It could give the flat light. It has considered this longest, because light is the ordinary gift and the ordinary mercy, and it would be wrong here in a way that would take a long time to show. The eye here has stopped insisting. That is not a deficiency; it is a settlement. The country has given up on sight and gone in for touch, for heat, for chemistry, for sound, and it has built a whole dense grammar on that foundation. Light would not open the country. It would re-open a question the country has already closed. It would say: your settlement was a surrender. It was not. The power leaves the light where it was.

It could lift the water. The water looks like the problem — the heat, the wet, the standing sheet, the film on everything. But the water is not what the flat is drowning in. It is what the flat is made of. Take it away and the flat is a dry young country with sharp edges and nothing carrying: no sound arriving through the channels, no borrowers at the mouths, no slow editing in the deeps, no returned things in the shallows. The water is how the flat thinks. The power does not take away a country's way of thinking to make it more comfortable.

It could make the returned alive. This is the temptation that most resembles kindness, and it is the one the power sets down first. The returned are the flat's best guess at the shape of a thing, made from a description that came through the water. They are wrong the way a translation is wrong: faithful, and off. Animation would not complete them. It would interrupt the only correspondence the flat has — the slow, patient, approximate love it bears toward the shapes it has been given. And the attendants may be returned things themselves. Their patience is not a wound. To heal them would be to take their work, and their work is the shape of their lives.

It could set the distance true. It could straighten the channels and hold a bearing for a traveler so that the country stopped bending them. But the bends are where the sound lives. A straight channel carries a word and delivers it whole, and a whole word is a dead word here. The drift is the grammar. The offness is the meaning. The power does not correct a language into silence.

It could explain the chronology. The works are older than the land and younger than the land; the pumps made the channels and the channels grew to fit the pumps. The contradiction is load-bearing. Resolve it and the flat becomes a smaller place — a place with a history instead of a temperament. The power does not explain. It has been wise long enough to know that some contradictions are not errors. They are the shape of a thing that is larger than its parts.

It could take up the wrench. It could relieve the attendants. It has hands, and the hands have the right number of joints for the work, and it could stand at the pump house and make the seal and let them rest. But they do not want rest. They want the work, at the pace of the pumps, one patient adjustment every four seconds, and their wanting is the only thing in the flat that is not circulation. It is purpose. The power does not take purpose away from anything that has found it. That is not mercy. That is theft with a soft name.

---

So it does none of these. And because it is a power, and not a visitor, its attention would be a weight without a shape if it only declined. It must do something that fits the grammar. It looks for the smallest true thing.

It puts its hand on the iron. It stands at the other end of the shell from an attendant and it counts. One stroke every four seconds. It counts for a long time. And then — this is the whole of it — it begins, very quietly, to keep its own time, half a second off.

Half a second is nothing. It is too small to be a correction and too steady to be an accident. It is close enough that the two beats braid, and far enough that the braid has a grain in it, a rub, a seam. A second clock is not a leader. It is an outside. The flat has never had an outside — no sun to set against, no shore, no source, nothing that is not itself. Everything in it is inside it. And now, half a second off, there is a second beat, and the flat's own beat has become audible to the flat: not changed, not quickened, not slowed. Only heard.

The small ones feel it first, the way they feel everything first, through the mud. The breathers adjust their flanks, unhurried, as though a familiar pressure had shifted by a degree. The borrowers take the second beat into their sacs and hold it with the stolen sounds, and for the first time a channel returns something that is not the flat's own voice with the vowels drifted — it is the flat's own voice with an outside in it, a foreign grain, a second clock braided into the first. The attendants pause. One of them lifts its hand from the iron, the way a person lifts a hand from a table when they hear a sound they cannot place, and holds it in the air a moment. Then it puts the hand back. The counting resumes. The second beat is still there. Nothing is corrected. The flat is accompanied.

If you are walking in the flat when the power is keeping its time, you will not see it. You will feel the water move against your legs at a rhythm that is not the pumps, and you will think it is a breather, and it will not be. You will stand still and let the film gather and the small ones decide about you, and the two beats will braid, and you will find that you have stopped trying to leave.

---

And there is one thing more, smaller than the second beat and larger.

In a shallow pan, where the water is ankle-deep and warm and the mud is firm, the power finds a returned thing. An animal. It has the anatomy it needed to be alive and no life in it. It does not rot. It does not participate in the ordinary decay. It lies in the pan the way the flat left it, wrong the way a translation is wrong, faithful and off.

The flat can edit a shape toward life. It cannot sit with the shape when the editing is done. It has no lap, no patience of that kind, no way to be with a thing without also changing it. The power can. So it sits down in the warm water beside the returned animal and it stays. It does not animate it. It does not fix it. It keeps it warm — which costs nothing here, where everything is already at the temperature of skin — and it is with it, at the pace of the pumps, one long patient company every four seconds.

This is the one correction the flat could not make for itself. Not resurrection. Company. The flat has been alone with its returned things for a very long time, and it did not know it was alone, because aloneness is an outside, and it had none.

---

After a long time — seasons, or the length of several hundred thousand strokes, it does not matter — the flat considers the power by its own method. By pressure. By heat. By the small mouths at the waterline deciding whether it is food or weather. And it makes a shape.

It puts the shape in a shallow pan where the power will find it. It is the flat's best guess at what the power is, made from a description that came through the water, and it is wrong. It has too many joints, or not enough. It has a hand where the power has a hand, but the hand is open, and the power's hand is not always open. It is warm. It is patient. It is not alive, and it is not exactly dead, and it is the flat's attempt to say: *I have noticed you. I have described you as well as I can from the outside, which is the only way I know. I am sorry the vowels have drifted.*

The power does not correct it. It does not say: no, I am like this. It takes the shape and holds it. The shape is wrong. The shape is the best thing the flat has ever made of an outside, and the power keeps it. That is the correspondence. Not rescue. Not correction. Two things describing each other as well as they can from the outside, and accepting the drift.

Then the power goes back to the iron and puts its hand on it and counts. One stroke every four seconds. Half a second off, its own time, steady, quiet, not leading. Together they make a sound that is not the world and not a correction. It is the sound of two things keeping time in the same warm water, neither of them alone.

The pumps make their rounds. The flat is not fixed. The flat is heard. That is what a wise power does, when it has nothing to fix. It is not much. It is the whole of it.
