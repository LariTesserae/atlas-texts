The power comes not as a visitor, but as a quality of attention. It settles over the terrace the way evening settles—without announcement, without demand, simply present and complete.

It is old enough to recognize a kindred spirit: a system that has achieved what so many grasping, hungry things never do. Equilibrium. Self-sufficiency. The long, patient work of metabolic grace. The power does not want to improve this place. It wants to understand it, and perhaps to learn.

---

**First: the power watches.**

It takes the terrace in slowly, the way the mossback takes in heat through its belly. It reads the deep pulse of the mountain, the constant exhalation that would seem wasteful to a lesser mind. But the power is not lesser. It sees the economy of the steam—how the heat rises, condenses on the broad leaves, falls as rain, feeds the mosses, becomes something living, becomes something that eventually dies and becomes stone and crust again. The cycle is closed. The energy is not wasted; it is transformed. It is the most elegant kind of machine, one that has no off switch and no need for one.

The mossback is the heart of this understanding. The power perceives it not as a creature apart from the landscape, but as a node of consciousness within it—a slow, diffuse awareness that has chosen to settle here, to become part of the terrace's own logic. The frogs on its back are not tenants; they are extensions of its temperature, its warmth, its exhalation. The geckos are not guests; they are the mossback's own sensitivity to the rock, its contact with the world rendered in quick, small lives. The creature is not one thing. It is an ecosystem wearing a single skin.

The power finds this beautiful. It finds it instructive.

---

**Second: the power learns.**

It lingers for what might be days or what might be a single, long inhale. It does not count the time. It teaches itself the rhythm of the vents—which hiss with a nightly regularity, which sulk and then exhale in sudden, violent plumes. It learns the taste of the mineral crusts, the way the orange stains spread in thin, concentric bands that mark the slow accumulation of years. It learns to read the mossback's stillness not as passivity but as a kind of listening.

And the power listens back.

It feels the low thrum the mossback emits in the evening, and it responds—not with words, but with a matching frequency, a resonance that says *I am here, and I mean no harm, and I am trying to understand*. The mossback does not open its clouded amber eyes. It does not need to. It has already registered the power as a presence of a particular kind: patient, non-predatory, curious. It files this information in the same place it files the temperature of the wind and the meanness of the last drought. The power becomes weather. The power becomes acceptable.

---

**Third: the power does something small.**

It does not heal the terrace, because the terrace is not sick. It does not tame the mossback, because the mossback cannot be tamed and would not benefit from it. It does not build or alter or plant or save. To do any of these things would be to interrupt a conversation that has been going on for millennia and in which the power is only the newest and least experienced participant.

But it does make one small intervention. It protects the terrace from the species that will come later, the ones who will see the geyser basin as a resource, the mossback as a novelty, the mineral crusts as a commodity. It does not build a wall of stone or a line on a map. It builds a wall of attention, a kind of psychic boundary that says: *This place is already complete. It is already inhabited. It is not yours.*

This wall is not a barrier. It is a suggestion, a quiet thought planted in the minds of those who might otherwise be inclined to take. The mossback feels this new boundary in the air, in the subtle shift in the pressure of the vents. It does not know what it means, but it reads the change and adjusts its exhalations accordingly—a slow, rhythmic sigh of steam that, over time, becomes part of the terrace's established pattern.

---

**Fourth: the power leaves a gift.**

Not a statue, not a shrine, not a monument. Nothing so crude. Instead, it settles into the terrace's deep memory a template for the mossback's future, a seed of possibility. It does not change the creature now, but over centuries, over the slow grinding of the earth's engine, the mossback's line may develop a new spiracle, a new sensitivity in its jaw pits, a new chemical signature in its mineral crust. A mark of having been seen and understood by a mind that appreciated its patience.

The copper frogs will chime a half-tone lower. The ferns on the mossback's back will develop a slightly deeper green. The vents will hiss in a pattern that, to a trained listener, sounds almost like an affirmation.

---

**Finally: the power does nothing else.**

It does not return. It does not check in. It trusts the terrace to continue its long, wet, hot, patient conversation with itself. The mossback is not its creature; it is the mountain's creature, the vent's creature, the moss's creature. The power is only a witness, and it knows that the best witnesses are the ones who leave when the moment is complete.

The terrace continues. The vents exhale. The frogs chime. The mossback settles into its slow thrum, its dorsal crust blooming with new orange stains. The steam rises, condenses, falls. The cycle closes. The power is gone, but its attention remains, a subtle warmth in the air, a faint resonance in the deep pulse of the mountain.

The mossback, if it could understand such things, might say: *We were seen. And it was good.*
