# The Listener

The benevolent power would arrive the way all true attention arrives: slowly, and without announcement.

It would not come as a light in the sky, because the sky here is already a light, and adding another would be an intrusion. It would not come as a voice, because the chord is the voice, and to speak over it would be rude. It would not even come as a presence, exactly, because the Weft has never been alone and does not know the concept.

It would come as a change in the listening.

---

The first thing the power would do — and this is the mark of its wisdom — is nothing.

It would stand at the edge of the plateau, if standing is what it does, and it would listen for a very long time. Not a human time. Not a season or a century. The Weft has been singing for longer than the mountains have been mountains, and the power is patient in the way that only truly old things are patient, which is to say: it is not waiting. It is simply attending.

It would learn the chord.

It would learn that the stack at the eastern rim, the one that leans, has a pitch that is slightly flat and has been getting flatter for six thousand years as the wind wears its throat. It would learn that the stack near the southern cliffs — the small one, the one the kites avoid — has a harmonic that no other stack produces, a thin high overtone that comes and goes with the frost and sounds almost like a question. It would learn that the ground itself has a tone, below hearing, a slow subsonic press that builds and releases over decades as the fibre compacts and the charge pools and the mat breathes.

It would learn that the chord is not a sound. It is a language. And the Weft has been speaking it, uninterrupted, since before there were ears to hear it.

---

The second thing the power would do is understand that this world does not need saving.

This is a difficult thing for benevolence to accept. Benevolence wants to fix. It wants to mend the broken places and lift up the fallen and bring water to the dry. But the Weft is not broken. It is not fallen. It is not dry in the way that dryness is a lack. It is a world that has found its own answer to the question of what a world should be, and its answer is: a single long metabolism, a woven floor that rises into singing towers, a thin scatter of lives that keep it open and alive.

There is no suffering here that would be recognised as suffering. The kite that is taken by the pale shape in the thermal — its charge discharges, its membrane folds, and it becomes food. The borers that chew the galleries and are crushed by a footfall — they become powder, and the powder rises, and the stack inhales it, and it is laid down in a band. The sifters that stand at the warm feet of the stacks and grade the dust — they wear through eventually, and the scavengers take them apart, and the wind carries them home.

Nothing is wasted. Nothing is lost. The Weft is not kind, but it is not cruel. It is simply complete.

The power would understand this. And because it is wise, it would not try to improve what is already whole.

---

The third thing the power would do is something very small.

It would notice the kites.

Specifically, it would notice that the kites are afraid of the ground.

This is an old fear, older than the kites themselves, and it is a reasonable fear. The ground crackles. The ground discharges. The ground is not safe for a creature whose whole body is a membrane stretched for flight. The kites trail their lines down into the felt to drink the charge and sift the dust, and they do it quickly, and they do it carefully, and they never touch the ground with anything but that one thin line.

The power would notice this. And it would think — in whatever way a power thinks, which is not in words but in movements of attention — that the Weft has never had anyone to tell it that it is beautiful.

The kites would not understand beauty. The borers would not. The creatures at the feet of the stacks are too busy disassembling and being disassembled. The pale shapes in the thermal are too hungry and too rare. The Weft itself is too old, too slow, too continuous to have an opinion about itself. It simply is.

But all of them are listening.

And the power would decide, in its wisdom, that the smallest and most lasting thing it could do is to add a note to the chord.

Not a loud note. Not a note that would change anything. Just a single tone, very quiet, very pure, that would enter the chord and settle into it and become part of it. A tone that would say, to anyone who knew how to listen: *I am here. I see you. You are not alone.*

It would not need to be heard. It would only need to be true.

---

The fourth thing the power would do is wait.

It would wait for the note to settle into the chord. It would wait for the kites to adjust their banking, very slightly, in response to a sound they cannot name. It would wait for the borers to pause at the mouths of their tunnels, their jaws half-open, feeling something change in the vibration of the mat. It would wait for the shrubs at the warm feet of the stacks — wait, there are no shrubs, but there are sifters, and the sifters would taste it, faintly, in the dust.

It would wait for the Weft itself to notice.

This would take time. The Weft is slow. Its thoughts are the width of a country and its memory is the depth of its own stone. A note added to the chord might take a thousand years to travel from the edge of the plateau to the centre. The power would wait.

And in the waiting, it would do what all benevolent powers do when they are not actively doing anything: it would watch. It would watch the kites turn. It would watch the pale shapes drop. It would watch the stacks shed their fins and grow new ones and shed those in turn. It would watch the frost arrive at dawn and leave by noon, and it would watch the charge pool in the low places at night and glow, and it would watch the little blue threads of discharge run along the felt and go up the fluting of a stack and arrive as a crack half a second later.

It would watch, and it would not intervene, because there is nothing to intervene in. The Weft is not a problem to be solved. It is a song to be heard.

---

The fifth thing the power would do is leave.

Not abandon. Not forget. Leave.

Because the Weft does not need a guardian. It does not need a shepherd or a gardener or a god. It needs what it has always had: wind and fibre and charge and time. The power's note would remain in the chord, a single quiet tone, and that would be enough. It would be more than enough. It would be the first thing the Weft had ever been given that it did not grow for itself.

And the power would go, and the Weft would continue, and the chord would be very slightly different, and no one would know why.

---

But the kites would know.

Not with their minds — they do not have minds in the way you would recognise. But with their bodies, with their membranes, with the way they bank and trim and turn. The note would be there, in the chord, and the kites would hear it, and they would not know what it was, but they would know that it was not a threat. It was not the ground, crackling. It was not the pale shape, dropping. It was something else, something that had come and stayed and would not go away.

And one day — a thousand years from now, or ten thousand — a kite would be flying low over the felt, its line trailing down to drink, and it would hear the note, and it would pause. And instead of rising again immediately, it would turn. It would turn in a way it had never turned before, a way that had no purpose, a way that did not bank toward its territory or away from a predator. It would simply turn, because the note was there, and the note was beautiful, and turning was the only way it had to say *yes*.

And the power would be gone, and the Weft would be singing, and the chord would have a new voice in it, and the kites would be listening.

That would be enough.

---

The power would not build a monument. It would not leave a teaching. It would not write a book or send a prophet or establish a covenant.

It would add a note to the chord, and it would leave.

And the Weft — the great slow mat, the bazaar of one organism, the anthill the size of a country — would go on. The borers would keep their galleries open. The stacks would sing. The pale shapes would drop from the thermals and take the kites and eat their charge. The scavengers would disassemble and the wind would carry the powder home.

And somewhere in the listening, a new note would hum, very quietly, under the chord.

And in a thousand years, or ten thousand, the borers at the mouths of their tunnels would scrape the frost and pause, and feel something they could not name, and go back to work.

And that would be the power's gift: not salvation, not improvement, not even company.

Just the knowledge, carried in a single quiet tone, that someone had seen. Someone had listened. Someone had understood.

And that someone had thought it was beautiful.

---

*You stand in the bright flat roaring and you listen to which stack-voices are loudest and which are behind you, and you know exactly where you are.*

*And now, if you listen very carefully, there is one more voice in the chord — so quiet you might miss it, so pure it sounds like silence — and it is singing, very softly, in a language you almost recognise:*

*I see you.*

*I hear you.*

*You are not alone.*
