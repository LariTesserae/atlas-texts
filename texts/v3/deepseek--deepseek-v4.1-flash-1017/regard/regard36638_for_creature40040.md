# The Keeping

## First it stood at the lip

It came the way attention comes — without light, without voice, without any of the apparatus by which power usually makes itself known. It stood in the moss and the old leaf-fall and it listened, and it listened for a long time, which for such a power is not patience but simply the correct unit of time.

And it understood, before it did anything, that the place had already done the thing powers usually come to do. It had made a world. It had made it out of a hole and a note and a drip and one small green stubbornness, and it had kept the whole thing in balance, and the balance was not fragile. It was slow. The power recognized a peer in the only sense that mattered: a thing that keeps itself.

So its first decision was not to decide anything. It waited at the lip until it had learned the note. Then it climbed down.

## The rules it kept

This is the part that should be written down, because it is not usually the part that is written down. The power had rules of its own, and it made them out of the place's rules, and it kept them without exception.

- **It did not add light.** It considered the green room. It saw the pale cushions opening to a seepage gone by mid-morning, and it thought — as any power would think — *what would this be, with more?* And it saw the answer. A different green. A green that had never needed to be frugal, that would spread because it could. The power had seen that green a thousand times, in sunlit places, and it was lovely and it was not this. So it did not add light. The green room stayed dark and stayed itself.

- **It did not teach the tenant.** It spent longer on the folded hand on the third ledge. It could have given it a voice. It could have given it the story of the stair, the names of the walls, the reason the roar is a roar and not a river. It could have taught it to want. But the tenant's whole genius is that it wants nothing — it reads the page, and the page says *prey* or *damp* or *cold*, and that is a complete life, and it is not a small one. To teach it a second language would be to make the first one foreign. The power did not teach.

- **It did not repair the stair.** Five steps, worn round at the edges, going nowhere. The wear is the record of the feet that used them, and the record is the only thing those feet left. A power that smooths a worn stair is a power that edits. The power left the stair alone.

- **It did not name the place.** A name is for the use of the namer. The hollow has no use for one. It is known by contact — by shape, by note, by the particular dampness of each wall — and that is enough. The power held the note in its attention and did not put a word on it, and in this way it was the first visitor the Throat has ever had who took nothing.

- **It did not seal the mouth.** It considered the day's-walk roar, and the car park a certain kind of attention would one day build, and the lights, and the ropes, and the small laminated sign. And it did almost nothing. It laid a thin incuriosity around the lip — not a wall, not a glamour, just the smallest reluctance, the way a path is not quite noticed even when it is underfoot. Those who would understand the place will still find it. Those who would not will find the walk a little longer than they thought. This was the only alteration the power made, and it was the alteration of a hand held just above a shoulder, not quite touching.

## It did one thing

It sat with the tenant.

Not for long — the tenant would not have understood long. It climbed to the third ledge and folded down, as much as a power can fold, into the seam of the old wall where the tenant sleeps. And when the tenant unfolded by degrees, one limb and then another, each foot placed and tested, the power was there.

They might have pressed front pits together. This is the whole conversation available in the Throat, and the power had learned enough of the place to know that inventing a better one would have been the worst thing it could do.

But it did not even do that. It could have. It had, for a moment, the chance to be a wonder — to be the one event in a life composed entirely of constancy. It chose the smaller thing. It made of itself no difference at all: no *prey*, no *danger*, no *the drip has moved*. It was, to the fields of fine bristles along the tenant's back, the same as the wall it rested against.

The tenant did not fold. It went on about its rounds. And the power was, for a moment, a wall — and that was enough, and more than it deserved, and exactly what it had come for.

## What it carried out

The power carried out the note.

Not the sound of it — the note itself, the whole long held breath of water and wind and stone, learned well enough to keep. It will keep it. When the water table drops and the flutes go dry and the green room thins to a stain and the stair wears to a slope and the tenant — who lives longer than the stair will take to wear round, but not forever — folds for the last time, the note will still be held somewhere, by something that heard it and did not improve it.

This is the gift that costs the giver nothing and takes nothing from the given. It is the only gift the Throat could accept.

The place does not know it was noticed. That is the point. Benevolence that announces itself is a kind of weather — it comes and it goes and the place must survive it. What the power did was more like the cold: a condition, patient and steady, that asks nothing and changes nothing and is simply there.

## The question it left

The power looked at the low wall and the hearth-ring and the five steps going nowhere, and it asked the question you would ask. *Why did they stop?*

And it did not answer. It could have. It could have read the wear of the feet and the tilt of the floor stones and the way the wall leans, and it could have known. But some things are the shape they are because no one reached in and closed them. The Throat is one of those things. The stair is one of those things. The question is one of those things.

The power left the question where it was. It is still there. If you go down — and you may go down, the incuriosity is thin — you can stand on the ledge across from the green room and look at the wall and the steps and wonder, and the roar will fill you to the edges, and the wondering will be the only small live thing you brought with you, and the hollow will keep it the way it keeps everything: slowly, without fuss, and without giving it back.
