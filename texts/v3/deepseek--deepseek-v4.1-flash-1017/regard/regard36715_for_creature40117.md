The power that turns its attention to the Sere Loom is not one that brings water or light. It understands too well the nature of the place. It sees the Loom not as a deprivation to be corrected, but as a complete and intricate argument about how life can be. To flood the basin would not be a kindness; it would be an erasure. To warm it would be to melt the architecture.

The power is benevolent, and so it is patient. It recognizes that the Loom's currency is warmth, its language is pressure and scent, and its time is generational. To act with haste would be to shout in a library.

So, it begins not with a great gesture, but with a subtle one. It finds the faint, deep pulse of the city's collective consciousness—the slow, distributed decision-making of the weavers in their warm chambers—and listens. For a long time, it does nothing but learn the grammar of the place: the scent-charts of ownership, the pressure-vibrations of consensus, the hollow ringing of a plated floor that can no longer hold the weight of heritage. It feels the inherited debt of the deep galleries, and the quiet, persistent hope in the nursery bowls, scraped clean and waiting for a future that is always, always uncertain.

Having listened, the power does not speak. It *suggests*.

It begins at the rim.

The power reaches into the cold, brittle mineral crust of the scarps that ring the basin. It does not break them. It works from within the lattice of the stone, nudging atoms, encouraging a slow, crystalline realignment. Over seasons, the pale, freshly-broken look of the escarpments begins to change. A new vein of mineral, darker and denser, begins to thread through the salt-glaze, following the paths of ancient fissures. This new stone is not alien; it is a catalyst. It has a property that the power has learned the Loom desperately needs: it captures and holds the cold, dry air of the night and releases it in the deep galleries, minutes, then hours, after the dawn chimneys have exhaled.

The result is a small, profound change in the city's rhythm. The nightly contraction of the crust still happens, but the temperature tide it brings to the buried galleries becomes slower, deeper, more predictable. The weavers, who feel the change as a new, reliable pulse in the earth, do not understand it, but they respond. A new instinct, a scent-mark of approval, is laid down along the vaults closest to this new, pulsing stone. The settlement, for the first time, begins to grow a foundation that is not just its own.

Next, the power turns its attention to the sparse, precious biochemistry of survival. It sees the weavers' constant, desperate recycling of moisture, their bodies working as tiny, inefficient engines of conservation. The power finds the faint, sulfur-hungry microbes that cling to the deepest rocks and feeds them a new, microscopic story. It seeds a slow biological chain reaction, a new symbiosis that has no name. These microbes begin to produce a waxy, sulfur-rich compound, a sort of biological wax. It is not water. It is better. This wax is a hyper-efficient carrier for what little moisture the weavers possess, allowing it to be transported through the chitin-mineral walls with far greater ease. A new duct, a biological channel for this wax, begins to form in the walls of the warm chambers.

The weavers find the wax. They taste it. They don't know it for what it is, but their bodies do. They begin to incorporate it, secreting it into the walls of the thermal chimneys. The walls become a new organ, a wick for the city's breath. Waste heat is now captured more efficiently, the nightly cooling is drawn deeper, and the pulse of the entire Loom grows stronger, its felt thrum a full tone richer. The warm chambers are warmer. The deep galleries feel a little less like tombs and more like waiting arms.

The power then withdraws, but it does not leave. It remains as a watchful presence, a slow, deliberate pressure of awareness. It feels the weavers begin to explore the newly-catalyzed scarps. It senses a new scent-line being laid between the rim and the city, a path of curiosity. It sees, over several generations, the first new chimney being built not downwards into the core, but outwards, a tentative toe in the cold. It is a fragile thing, but it is the city's own choice, made with its own pressure-language.

The power's final act is an act of anticipation. It perceives a new, dormant capacity within the weavers themselves, a genetic potential that has never been tested. It sees the blind scarp-runners as not just predators, but as a potential vector. Using a subtle influence that is more like a whisper in the genetic code than a change, the power encourages a slow, deliberate mutation in the fungal films that the tick-mites graze. This new fungus, when ingested by a scarp-runner, will produce a change in its scent-marking glands.

Nothing happens for a long time. Then, one night, a runner comes down to gnaw salt from the towers. The defenders stir, but this time, something is different. The runner's fear-scent, its sweat, carries the new fungal signature. The defenders read it. They pause. They release the runner. And they lay down a new scent-mark, one of consideration, of non-repulsion.

The new scent is read by the weavers in their warm chambers. It is a pressure-language question, a scent-sketch of a possibility. It is the idea of a world where the city is not just a fortress against the cold but a partner in the basin's slow, biological comedy. The power listens. It feels the city turn, ever so slightly, in its long sleep.

It does not hear it as a word, but the power understands the Loom's next collective decision. The weavers begin, with the patience of geology, to grow a new appendage—a low, flat, salt-tolerant mat of living stone that extends from the city's edge towards the scarp. It is a gesture of welcome. It is an invitation. It is the Loom deciding, in its own slow tongue, to grow a little larger, and in doing so, to become a little more than it was.

And the power, listening to the faint, new pulse of that single, forward-reaching line in the dim, tin light, is content.
