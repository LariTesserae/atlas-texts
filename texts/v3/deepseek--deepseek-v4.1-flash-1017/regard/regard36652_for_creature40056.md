**What the Power Did**

It arrived the way a season arrives — afterward nobody could name the hour. No light in the sky, no voice in the dust. Only, at some point, the plain was being attended to: looked at, and considered, and left.

First it did nothing, and it did nothing for a long time. This is the part lesser powers skip. It set its attention down on the flats the way you set a hand on a sleeping animal’s flank — flat, warm, expecting nothing — and it waited through a full turn of the pans’ rhythm before it made a single judgment.

What it watched, mostly, was the silence. Three breaths of it, then the churring again, ragged. It took the power a while to learn that this was not a death. It took longer to learn the difference between the raggedness that follows a hunter and the raggedness that follows a bad night — the second is slower to close, and the flyers go up first. It learned both. It is a wise power, and wisdom is mostly the willingness to be slow about the small differences.

What it understood, then, in order:

The plain is not dying. The plain is *done* — eroded into its final form and left. The power had spent an age among places still mid-sentence, all of them pitching toward some next shape, and it had grown used to helping. Here there was no next shape. This was a period. It had to sit with that the way you sit with a person who has finished grieving.

The pans are not wounds. This was the second thing, and it decided everything. A tidier benevolence — a municipal one — would have looked at a hundred sunken hollows in a dead white plain and started filling them in. But the hollows are not injuries. They are cities. The density is not distress; distress is thin, quiet, and failing, and this was loud, packed, and old. The noise was the health of it. A rasper hissing at its plate, a comber in its queue, the whole bowl churring underfoot — that is what a place sounds like when the arithmetic is working.

The pump-jack is not a ruin. This was hardest. The power had a long history with abandoned things and an instinct to restore. But the rust had become substrate. The flange was a reef. The tailings heap was a rise that caught the wind, and the wind caught the dust, and the dust caught the eaters. To make the works new would be to evict the tenants. The power thought about what a cleaned pump-jack would *be* — a clean thing, in a place whose entire virtue was that nothing in it was clean anymore — and it left the jack ticking.

---

Then it went looking for the being. This is the part the story wants. It is not the part that is there.

The power looked down into a pan and searched, as you would, for the one who lives in it, and found no one. It found a tenement. It found raspers and combers and curtains of clingers and a low gyre of papery flyers and thin fast hunters that made everything flatten and hold still. It found a thousand overlapping processes, none of them in agreement, none of them in charge.

And then it looked again, and the thing assembled itself out of the looking. Not a resident — a condition. The chain of pulse that ran hollow to hollow across the flats, a rhythm passed along the crust like a rumor, was the nearest thing to a nerve the place had. The pan was a drum. The plain was the drum’s skin. The city did not know it was a city, and it was one, and the power recognized it the way you recognize a face assembling itself out of a crowd in a doorway.

It considered waking it. This is the great temptation, and it felt it fully. To tell the city it is one — to make a single being out of the tenement, to give the place an inside — would be a kind of birth, and births are what a benevolent power is *for*. But the power has been at this longer than the plain has been flat, and it has learned to distrust a kindness that arrives with a birth attached. The city was already awake in the way that matters to a city: awake in every room. What the power would be adding was not wakefulness but self-wakefulness, and there is no way to know in advance whether that is a gift or a burden. There is only the knowing afterward, and afterward is too late to put it back.

So it did not wake the city. It loved the silence between the notes and left the notes alone. It loved the hunters too, which is the harder love, because the hunters are the part of the arithmetic the visiting mind most wants to edit.

---

It refused to count them. It refused to name them — a name is a handle, and a handle is how a thing gets carried off. It refused to widen the pans or deepen them, and it refused to open the flyers’ gyre. The closed circuit looks like a prison only to a being that can leave; the power had learned to distrust its own claustrophobia. It refused, above all, to spare the individual. It could have plucked one rasper out of the path of one hunter and never been seen doing it, a thousand times. But mercy at this scale is not a rescue, it is a subsidy, and a subsidized system hollows out. The arithmetic is not cruelty. It is the way the city eats.

What it did, in the end, was small, and it was done in units of duration rather than force. It had, as such powers do, a capacity for intervention best measured in continents, and it used none of that.

It slowed the wind at the pan rims by a fraction — not enough to notice, enough that the thousand-year worry of the dust could not cut through a shoulder of crust in a single bad season. It left the rims to their softness.

It held the light steady at the changeover — only at the changeover, only then — so the flyers would come down all at once, in their one great settling, as they always had. A pan whose flyers come down in panic loses a generation.

It set a courtesy into the crust. This is not a rule and it is not a fence. It is a bias, the way a floorboard knows not to creak under a person who is not angry. Where a visitor steps without greed, the sinter holds a little firmer than it should. Where the curtains shingle the wall, it holds firmer still. It will not be enough. The power knows it will not be enough, and does it anyway, because the alternative is a wall, and a walled pan is a specimen, and a specimen is already dead.

And once — once, in the whole of its attention — it lay down at the far edge of the flats, where the last hollow is, and put its ear to the ground. It heard the pulse come down the chain. It held still for three breaths.

The pan did not flatten. There was no tremor, no shadow, no thin fast thing. Nothing hungry had ever made a silence like that, and the city did not know what to do with a silence that wasn’t, so it did what a city does with weather: it took the stillness in, and made it part of the pulse, and passed it along.

Then the power stood up and did the last thing, which was the opposite of marking the place. It made the plain a little harder to find than it should be — not hidden, not fenced, just unadvertised. Fewer people would come. The ones who came would step right.

---

From a distance the flats are still pale and flat and finished, and the wind lifts the topmost millimeter of dust and sets it down a little farther on, and the jack ticks, and under the whole white sheet of it a chain of small loud rooms is passing a rumor along, hollow to hollow to hollow, out past where anyone can see.

Leave a place like that alone long enough and it stops reading as anything — just a plain, just the wind, just the dust settling again. Looked after rightly, it stays that way. It costs more to leave a thing than to keep it, and that is how you know it was done well.
