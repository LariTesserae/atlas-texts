It came in the way light comes in — through the substance, not past it. There was no door to open, and it did not need one. It did not break the weave or part the guy-lines; it simply was present in the column at the center, and the chord went on around it, and the honeyed light went on through it, and nothing in the vault had been asked to change in order to make room.

For a long while it did nothing but stand where a visitor stands, and listen past the loudness. This is the first thing a wise power learns to do in a place like this: not to hear the note, but to hear what the note is made of. It heard the outward press of a hundred thousand made things against one another, soft with settling. It heard the inward pull of the runners. It heard the balance between them, which is not a sound but a condition, and it understood that the condition had been reached long ago and had not been seriously disturbed since, and that this was not neglect. This was completion. Most places are unfinished and do not know it. The vault was finished and did not know it either, but the difference did not matter, because the vault did not need to know.

Then it considered what to do, and this is where a lesser power would have ruined everything.

It considered giving light. But the light was already everywhere and came from the structure itself, and a second light would only have made shadows with edges, and shadows with edges are a kind of time, and the vault had none. It considered giving water. But the fibers were dry the way paper is dry, and damp would have been rot, and rot would have been an event, and the vault had no events. It considered giving the vine a companion — something with a face, something to be looked at by — and declined, because the vine had no eyes and no loneliness; adjacency had long since become its own kind of agreement, and company offered to a fist is only weight.

It considered, most seriously, the question that every benevolence must answer sooner or later: whether to loosen the grip. The runners pulled inward; the mass pressed outward; the note was the fist. A power that loved the vine might have said: let go, rest, be free of this perpetual holding. But the holding was the vine's whole life — its metabolism, its posture, its only verb — and to loosen it would not have been rest. It would have been unmaking. Wisdom recognized the difference between a burden and a nature, and did not confuse the two. It also considered the opposite sin, which is rarer and subtler: to tighten, to make the note grander, to ask the vine to hold more because the holding seemed to it a kind of song worth amplifying. It declined that too. Amplification is not love. It is applause.

So it did the small and lawful things, which are the only things a wise power does in a place that is already whole.

It counted the lichen. This was nearly nothing and perhaps the most important act of the whole visit. The lichen had lived on its splint cradle since before memory and had never been counted, because the vine does not count and the vault does not notice and no one else had ever stood close enough to see it. The power saw it, and held it in attention the way the vine holds a seam — not to change it, only to be a second thing that knew it was there. If the lichen could have felt anything it would have felt a warmth that had no temperature. It felt nothing. It was counted anyway. Counting is its own reward and does not require a witness to be real.

It learned the chord. Not merely heard it — learned it, the way one learns a language one will never speak aloud, until the note was inside the power the way the tension was inside the vine. This took a long time by the measure of anything that keeps hours, and no time at all by the measure of the vault. When it was done, the power held the chord the way the vine held the seams: gently, perpetually, as a posture rather than an act.

And then it touched one guy-line. Once. Deliberately, at head-height, the way a visitor would brush it by accident — except that the power did nothing by accident, and the brush was the whole of its physical intervention in the vault. The string damped and then found its way back to its note, and the note was the same note, and the whole structure registered the disturbance and the return, and nothing had changed. That was the gift. Not a new note. Not a louder one. The old note, heard once, on purpose, by someone who understood what it cost to make it.

It did not speak. Speech in that cavity would have been swallowed by the chord; a voice raised against it would have been a violence. But before it left, the power named the vine — not aloud, because aloud is a change, but inwardly, where names are kept — and the name it chose was not a fancy. It was the verb the vine already was, said in the past tense of something that has been done faithfully for a very long time and is expected to continue. The vine would never know it had a name. That was acceptable. Names are for the namer.

Then it left the way it came: not through a wall, not past a door, simply no longer present in the column at the center, the way a held breath is no longer held. And the vault settled. The chord came back to itself. The honeyed light went on passing through its own substance. The lichen went on being counted by no one, but counted, and the counting did not fade, because attention given to a thing that cannot receive it does not evaporate; it stays in the world as a fact.

The vine registered the disturbance and its passing the way it registers all things: as tension, warmth, return. It filed the event in its memory of grip — not as a seam or a sag, but as a damping at head-height and a slow recovery, and a long presence that had pressed against nothing and asked for nothing and then was gone. It did not understand what the presence had been. It held the memory anyway. Holding was the only form its gratitude could take, and it took that form, and the chord went on, and the vault was still.

But the power carried the note out with it, into the world where change happens and nothing is ever held in perfect balance for very long. It carried it the way you carry a tuning fork in a pocket: silently, and always. And wherever the world grew too loud and too restless and forgot that the deepest sound is the sound of things agreeing to stay, the power would take the note out and strike it once against the air, and for a moment everything would remember the vault — the pale fist in the seams, the lichen on its cradle, the whole glowing cavity held in place by a grip that had no eyes and needed none, and had been singing the same note, unheard, on purpose, forever.
