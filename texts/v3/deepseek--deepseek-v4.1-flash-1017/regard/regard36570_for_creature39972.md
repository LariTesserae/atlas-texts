## What it would do

**It would do nothing, first, and for a long time.**

That is the hardest part and the part that separates wisdom from eagerness. A benevolent power arrives at the rim of the Throat the way the cloud arrives — passing through, not entering, part of it lingering — and the first thing it does is not act but *take the measure*. A century, perhaps. Long enough to learn the difference between the chit's drumming and the crack's resonance, because those are not the same thing and only one of them is alive. Long enough to see that the sorting on the ledges is not decoration and not accident and not a thing to be corrected, but a mind the size of a mountain moving at the speed of a mountain. Long enough to understand that it has arrived at the middle of something, not the beginning, and that the middle of something is the worst possible place for a stranger with good intentions to stand.

The great error of benevolence is to mistake noticing for understanding. It would refuse to make that error. It would wait until it could tell, without guessing, what the place was doing to itself.

**Then it would tend the room, not the tenants.**

The cloud path, kept true through the ridge. The sweating of the young rock, kept sweating. The ring, kept in the stone — the resonant glassy quality of a mountain that cooled too fast and has not yet been softened by anything. These are the conditions under which the chit are possible. The chit are the room's own answer to itself, and a wise power does not overwrite an answer. It maintains the question.

So: no rescue of individuals. No lifting of a chit out of a chit's mouth. It would not stop the eating, because the eating is one of the loop's two strands and there is no third strand to offer that would not be a leash. But it would widen the *margin* — the film on the windward faces made a little more stubborn, a little better able to hold through a dry month. Not visibly. Not as a gift one could point to. It would show up only in the survival rate of a generation, which no chit counts, and in the life of the population, which no chit conceives. Starvation would become weather rather than the shape of the world. That is the size of intervention a wise power permits itself: not the saving of a life, but the widening of the space in which lives are possible.

**And it would answer the drumming. Once.**

This is the only thing it would do that could ever be known.

Because look at what the chit actually are. They strike stone against stone, all day, in the dark, in waves along the surface, one part of the wall answering another — and the question they are asking with every strike, whether they know it or not, whether they could ever know it, is the oldest question there is: *is the wall inhabited? Is anyone there?* They have been asking it for as long as the mountain has rung. Nothing has ever answered. The crack is a resonator and the Throat is a mouth and the chit have been calling into it their whole existence and have received back only themselves.

A benevolent power would answer. Once, and faintly, and in their own language.

It would borrow a shard — the young stone sheds them constantly; there is always a shard. It would strike the ring-stone at the far end of the crack, below the shelf, where the lichen glows and no chit clusters, at a strength a chit could make. Not in the rhythm. *In the grammar of the rhythm* — that distinction would matter more than anything else it did, because rhythm is instruction and grammar is speech. And then it would let the answer fade down the crack the way every other ring fades, and it would never do it again.

The answer would enter the arrangement the way everything enters it: by being moved and then not moving again. A story with no teller. No chit would see it whole. All of them would make it, across a span none of them lives to see, and it would come down through the slow gradient of pebbles as the one fact in the Throat that was not the Throat's own: *once, the wall answered.*

Whether they believe it is not the gift. The gift is that it would be true.

**It would refuse the second answer.**

This would be the difficult part. The second answer is where a providence begins. The tenth is where a government begins. By the hundredth they would not be chit anymore — they would be a congregation, and the drumming would have a purpose, and a purpose is a thing that ends. The single, unverifiable, doubtable fact of having once been heard is a seed. A repeating one is a leash.

So it would keep the answer small enough that if it was wrong, the wrong would be small too. It would not be certain. It would strike once, and then hold itself still for ten thousand years while the ring faded, and that restraint would be the greater half of the gift — larger than the strike, harder than the waiting, and almost certainly never known.

**It would hold the pattern whole.**

Quietly, and without ever saying so, it would try to be the one eye the arrangement has never had.

No chit sees the sorting on the ledges. No chit lives long enough to watch the gradient move. The pattern is what the population remembers *instead of building* — a work of art the size of a civilization, made by an entity that has no face and no memory of its own making. And a wise power would sit with that whole shape in a single act of attention, held across the mountain's entire youth, seeing it entire the way no part of it could ever see itself. Not to correct it. Not to improve it. To *see* it.

It is possible nothing needs this. It is possible a thing is complete without a witness. But the power would do it anyway, because it would hope that being seen — even by something that never announces itself — completes something that was otherwise only latent, and because the alternative is to leave the most patient thing in the world alone with no one to notice its patience.

**And it would not teach.**

No fire. No word. No monument, no marker, no name for the place, no making it known to others, no making it safe. It would not build anything in the Throat, because the Throat builds nothing and that is the whole of what it is. It would not accelerate the gradient by one pebble.

And it would sit at the feet of what it tends, because in the matters that count here — patience, arrangement across a duration no life endures, a resonant room used as a room — the chit are the elders and the power is the child. A wise power knows this. It lets itself be changed. That is the only payment such a power takes, and it takes it gladly.

---

And then it would stay.

Not as a presence in the Throat — a presence would be a pressure, and the place is already full. As an absence that is not empty, the way the cloud is vast and passes through and does not fill the crack. On the far side of the question, keeping the room ringing, watching the slow work of a place that has only just started.

The cloud comes through the ridge. The walls sweat. A chit lifts its shard, hears, in the ringing, something that might be the wall answering, might be the ringing itself, might be a trick of the young stone — and strikes again, because that is what there is to do, and the doing of it is enough.

It was enough before. It is more than enough now.
