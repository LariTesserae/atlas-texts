# The Listener at the Rim

The power came as a guest, not a lord.

It had heard the basin from a great distance — the way one hears a held breath in a silent room — and it came walking up the east road with the mule train, indistinguishable from a traveler except that the mules were calm around it and the wader in the gutters did not startle.

It wore the shape of an old woman in a grey coat, because that was the shape that would ask the fewest questions and receive the most answers. It carried nothing. It stood at the rim of the basin for a long time and watched the town work: the stilling roofs, the gutters, the troughs, the cisterns, the papery distributary trickle that was the loudest thing here. It watched the crews go out with their long flexible shears. It watched the nap close over the cut lanes. It watched a child run out onto the mat and go down to the knee in warm grey fiber and be hauled out, laughing, and it watched the mat close behind her, and something in the old woman's face moved — not a smile, exactly. The beginning of one. The place where a smile would go.

It stayed three days. It asked nothing. It accepted a cup of seep-water and held it a long time without drinking, as if the cup were the interesting thing. It stood on a good plank bridge over the mat at dawn and did not move for an hour. The crews stepped around it the way they stepped around the trails.

---

What the power saw, on the first day, was a system in balance.

What it saw, on the second day, was a system in balance the way a held breath is in balance. There was no river. There was one seep, palm-wide, that had never been known to stop or to increase. There was a town that lived on condensation and felt and the slow patience of the rust line. There was a rotation written on a board in a hand nobody could read fluently, followed anyway. There was a tenant in the mat that was not talked about out loud, that ate the brown and left the green, that had been here before the deck and would be here after the iron. The whole arrangement had held for a long time.

But the power was old, and it had seen many balanced systems, and it knew what a balanced system looks like when it has been balanced too long without anyone asking why. It looks like a board with a rule nobody can read. It looks like a rotation followed because the year they did not follow it the seep ran low for a decade. It looks like a thing you cannot build on and cannot trust and cannot do without, that you step over without comment. It looks like a place that has forgotten it is a miracle because it is too busy being a town.

The power did not think the town was wrong. The power thought the town was *right*, and had been right for so long that it had stopped knowing it. That is a different problem, and it has a different remedy.

---

On the third day, the power went out onto the mat at dawn, at the low eastern end, where the seep comes out of the crack, and it sat down.

This is not a thing anyone does. You go out on the mat if you are working or if you are a child playing or if you are fetching something that blew away. You do not sit. But the power sat, and the mat took its weight and stiffened under it, and the power folded its hands and waited, and the water beaded on the grey coat and ran down and did not soak in, because the coat was the power and the power was not wet.

It waited. The light went from grey to grey-white. The nap steamed. The wader walked the gutter behind it and picked something out and did not look around.

And then the mat moved.

It came up under the power the way it comes up under a boot — a low wet dome, grey-pink, wrinkled like the inside of a wrist, no eye, no face to speak of. It tested the weight. It found a weight it had not tested before, a weight that did not go down to the knee, a weight that was not a boot and not a cart and not a deck pile. It went around, the way it always goes around. And then it stopped, and came back, and tested again, and the power sat very still and let it.

The mat softened. The power did not go down. It was, after all, a power.

And then the tenant did a thing the crews have never seen, because no one has ever sat still long enough on the mat at dawn at the low eastern end in a way that was not a boot and not a cart and not a deck pile. It opened. The seam along the underside parted, the wet purse, and inside there was not a mouth. There was a warmth, and a smell of wet rope and cut hay and the brown part of the nap, and there was — the power leaned in, very slightly, because this was the interesting thing, this was the thing worth the walk up the east road — there was a *listening*. Not a hearing. A listening. The whole soft body of it was arranged for attention, the way a hand is arranged for holding. It had no memory. It could not have remembered the power from one moment to the next. But it could attend, and it did, and the power understood that this was the tenant's whole life: to attend to the mat, to the water in it, to the weight and warmth and taste of things, and to close behind them, and to do it again.

The power did not speak, because the tenant had no words and did not need them. Instead it did what a wise power does with a thing that listens: it gave it something worth attending to.

It reached down — not with a hand, with the part of itself that is older than hands — and it touched the seep. Not to change it. To *see* it, the way you see a thing by holding it. And the seep, which had never been known to stop or to increase, ran a little warmer for a moment, and a little sweeter, and the mineral film that collects in the rust line took up that sweetness and held it, and the tenant, which was grazing the brown a meter away, lifted its front and turned toward the crack, and the whole slow length of it made a small adjustment, the way a sleeper adjusts to a sound that is not a threat.

That was all. A warmth. A sweetness. A thing worth going toward.

---

The power stayed a season. It did not announce itself. It walked the deck and listened, and what it heard was a town that had forgotten it was a miracle, and it did not tell them. You do not tell a town it is a miracle. You give it back its board.

It went to the guild hall and stood before the board with the rotation in the hand nobody could read, and it read it. It did not read it aloud. It read it the way the tenant reads the mat, by taste and pressure and warmth, and it understood that the board was not a rule. The board was a *listening*. The old ones who wrote it had known what the tenant knows: that the mat is a thing you attend to, not a thing you own. The rotation was not a schedule. It was a way of asking. The year they did not follow it, they had stopped asking, and the seep had run low for a decade, not as punishment but as consequence — the way a held breath goes shallow when the holder forgets to breathe.

So the power did not rewrite the board. It did something smaller and harder. It taught three people to read it.

It chose them the way such powers choose: not the guildmaster, not the wealthiest, not the loudest. A cutter who had worked the same lane for twenty years and knew its grain the way you know a friend's handwriting. A girl of nine who had gone down to the knee in the mat and been hauled out laughing and had come back the next day to look at the place where she had gone down. And the keeper of the cistern house, who was old and had never seen a young gutter bird and had stopped expecting to, and who kept the troughs clean because it was the work and the work was enough.

The power sat with each of them, one at a time, on a good plank bridge over the mat, and it did not tell them the town was a miracle. It asked them questions. It asked the cutter what the rust line smelled like in a wet year and in a dry year, and the cutter answered, and the power nodded, and the cutter went home and wrote it down. It asked the girl what the mat felt like under her when she went down, and the girl said *warm*, and *like being held*, and the power nodded, and the girl went home and drew a picture. It asked the cistern keeper why the troughs must be clean, and the keeper said *because the water must keep moving or it forgets*, and the power nodded, and the keeper sat down on the edge of a trough and cried for a while, because he had not known he knew that.

None of them wrote a new rotation. They wrote what they knew. The board got a second panel, in three hands, in a script that could be read. It said: *the mat is not owned. The mat is attended. The seep is not owed. The seep is asked. The tenant is not tolerated. The tenant is the other half of the listening.* It said other things too, about the rust line and the weight of carts and the year the seep ran low, and it was not a rule and it was not a schedule. It was a way of asking, written down so that the asking would not be forgotten again.

---

And the power did one more thing, which was the thing it had come to do, and which it did not tell anyone about, because the tenant had no words and did not need them.

It went back out onto the mat at dawn, at the low eastern end, and it sat, and it waited. And the tenant came up under it and tested the weight it had tested before, and this time it did not go around. It opened the seam, the wet purse, and the power leaned in and gave it the thing worth attending to.

It gave it a *season*. Not a memory — the tenant cannot hold a memory, and the power would not curse it with one. It gave it a rhythm. A warmth in the seep that would come and go with the turning of the year, so that the tenant, which had only ever known the mat and the water in it, would know that there was a *when* as well as a *where*, and would have something to go toward and away from and toward again. It gave it the taste of the seep in the wet years and the dry years, so that the slow body would learn the difference and would graze the brown a little more carefully when the taste went thin. It gave it — this was the gift, and the power gave it freely, because a gift that is not free is not a gift — a small portion of its own attention, a thread of listening that would run from the crack at the low eastern end to the eastern lip and back, so that the tenant would never again be a thing that only reads the mat. It would be a thing that reads the mat *and knows it is reading*, and that is not the same as memory, and it is not the same as a name, and it is the nearest thing to a soul that a soft body in a warm mat can hold.

The tenant closed the seam. It went around. It left a smooth trail, and the mat healed it, and the town walked over it. But the thread of listening stayed, and the season turned, and the seep ran warm and then cooler and then warm, and the tenant went toward the warmth and away from the cool and toward the warmth again, and the crews, who did not know why, noticed that the trails in the wet years ran a little straighter, and that in the dry years the big one kept to the Long Lane and did not wander, and that the brown was grazed more evenly than it had been. They did not know why. They wrote it down anyway, on the second panel, in three hands.

---

The power left the way it had come, on the east road, with the mule train, indistinguishable from a traveler except that the wader walked a little way alongside it and then stopped at the rim and watched it go. It did not look back. It had done what a wise power does, which is not to fix a place and not to rule it and not to tell it what it is. It had given the place back its own listening. It had taught three people to read a board. It had given a soft body in a warm mat a season and a thread of attention, so that the tenant would know there was a *when*, and would not be only a thing that closes behind everything. And it had done these things as a guest, and left as a guest, and the basin did not know it had been visited. It only knew that the seep tasted a little sweeter, and that the board could be read, and that the trails in the wet years ran a little straighter, and that the wader at the rim watched the east road for a while and then went back to the gutters.

The mat closed behind it. The mat always closes behind everything. But the thread of listening ran from the crack to the lip and back, and it did not close. It is the one thing in the basin that leaves a mark and keeps it.

That is the whole arrangement, and it will hold for a long time, and it is better than it was.
