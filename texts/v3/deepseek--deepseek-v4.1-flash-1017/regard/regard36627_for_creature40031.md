# A Power That Chose to Sit

It would come, as powers do, intending to be useful.

It would come down onto the plateau by the long ramp of weathered stone, and it would take perhaps a full day to understand that the floor is wrong, and perhaps a full season to understand that the wrongness is not a problem to be solved. A benevolent power is not a hasty thing, but benevolence has its own impatience — the urge to fix, to improve, to lift. This is the first thing the breathing ground would teach it, and the lesson would not be gentle.

---

## What It Would Notice First

The power would arrive with its instruments, whatever they are — senses that reach through stone and tissue, a mind that holds a thousand years the way a hand holds water. It would see the mat, and it would see the listeners, and it would be tempted to name them: *one organism, or a few, and here is the boundary, here is the mechanism, here is the truth of the thing.*

And the ground would say, in its slow way, in the chorus that is also a language: *no.*

Not refusal. Something more like correction. The way a river corrects a map.

Because the power would be looking for the single thing to call *it* — the intelligence, the person, the one who lives here — and there is no such thing. The mat is a commons. The listeners are a commons. The people are a commons. They are not one organism and they are not many. They are a *conversation*, and a conversation has no body you can point to.

The power would learn this slowly, the way everyone learns it: by standing still at dawn and feeling the low tone in its teeth, and realizing that the tone is not the ground humming but nine named animals and a great many unnamed ones, opening their mouths under its feet at the same time. It would learn that the "one who lives here" is the arrangement itself. The living is in the between.

---

## What It Would Not Do

It would not drain the lenses. It would not seed the mat with something new, or thin the listeners to make travel safer, or build a thing that lasts. It would not clear.

It would consider, briefly, the question of the listeners — of whether they are predators and whether predators should be *managed*. The power would notice that a listener takes perhaps a dozen mowers a year, and that it takes them the way weather takes them, and that the mowers have been living with listeners for longer than the power has been alive. It would notice that a listener without birds is thin and often wrong, and that a listener with birds is a better navigator, and that the birds are not slaves and the listeners are not kings. It would notice that the humans who live here have been negotiating with the listeners for a very long time, and that the negotiation works, in the way that a marriage works: imperfectly, and over centuries, and with seams.

The power would understand that it is not being asked to improve anything. It is being asked to *notice*, and to be careful, and to leave no seam of its own.

---

## What It Would Do

**It would stop.**

It would sit down on the mat — carefully, the way you sit on a sleeping animal — and it would let the floor take its weight and return it with that slight delay, that mattress-sleep, and it would listen.

It would listen for a year. Five years. A century. Time is not the same problem for a power that it is for a person, and the ground is not in a hurry, and there is nothing here that requires haste.

It would learn the eleven pillars by name. It would learn the nine listeners by name. It would learn to walk in rhythm and to ask once and not twice and to let itself be replayed. It would learn that the old listeners answer in a smear, and that the deaf are still there, and that the seams in the mat are old quarrels still knitting.

It would learn the chorus — not as sound but as *structure*, as the harmonies that are nobody's intention and everybody's voice. It would learn that the plateau is one instrument, and it would not touch the strings.

---

## What It Would Give

A power that listens long enough begins to understand what a gift is.

The people who live out here do not need much. They have bone and hardwood and chert and the occasional piece of traded metal. They have houses that grow and seams that heal and a sky with almost nothing rising from it. They have, above all, the knowledge of their ground: the sound of home, the shape of the haze, the names of the things that rise.

What they lack is not material. It is *continuity* — the sense that their knowledge is held, that the quarrels and healings and negotiations are not only their own but belong to something larger. The seams in the mat remember; the listeners answer in the voices of everyone who has walked over them. But the people themselves are scattered, one household per horizon, and their memory is the memory of a season.

So the power would give them a seam.

Not a building — nothing that rises. A *mark* in the mat, perhaps a hand's breadth across, that would not fade. It would be placed at the crossing of two old cuts, where the seams meet and the ground rings true. It would be small and dark and slightly warm, and it would be a place to touch, the way a doorframe is a place to touch when you come home.

The mark would say: *you are held. Your parents' parents' parents walked here and the ground knows their stride. You are not the first and you will not be the last.*

And the power would make another, and another, scattered across the plateau, each one at a crossing of seams or at the foot of a named pillar or at the rim of a listener that has learned to answer cleanly. They would not be many. They would be enough.

---

## What It Would Ask

Nothing.

A power that has learned to sit does not ask for acknowledgment. It does not require a temple or a song. It does not need the people to know its name.

But there would be a story, because people tell stories, and the story would be: *something came and sat and left marks, and the marks are warm, and the ground is still exactly what it was.*

The power would be content with this. It would be content to be a seam among seams, a quiet line in the mat's memory, a thing that did not fix and did not improve and did not clear. It would be content to be *part of the commons* — not the one who lives here, but one of the voices in the chorus, saying *I was here, and I listened, and I left it as I found it, and that is the gift.*

---

## The Marks

They would be small enough to miss. A traveler crossing a day of emptiness might walk past one and not know, unless they stopped — and people here do stop.

At dawn, when the mat is beaded and the low tone rises in the sternum, a person might find a mark by the feel of it: a place where the ground is slightly warmer, slightly more *present*. They would touch it. They would listen. They would hear the chorus in its layered loudness, the birds and the burrowers and the sawyers now, and beneath them the listeners saying it all again, a little late, a little down, in the voices of animals that have gone somewhere else.

And they would know, without knowing how they know, that they are not the only ones who have been careful here.

---

The power would leave without ceremony. It would walk back down the long ramp of weathered stone, and behind it the mat would close over its footsteps, the way it closes over everything.

But the marks would remain, and they would be warm, and the ground would sound the same — except that now there is a new harmonic, very low, very faint, in the voices that answer when you ask.

A seam that heals into something slightly different. A listener that has learned a new patience.

And the chorus would be one voice larger, and nobody would need to know.
