## The Power at the Rim

It would come as attention, not as light.

It would not descend. It would not announce itself with a trumpet or a hand parting the clouds. A wise power knows that some places are not waiting to be saved; they are waiting to be left whole. So it would stop at the rim, where the green thins and the hum quiets, and it would listen for a long time.

It would hear the chord that does not resolve. It would feel the seep breathing under the plant-flesh. It would notice the old pipes furred with moss, the vats half-sunk, the machinery that is no longer machine and not quite bone. It would see the small quick animals moving through the interstices, and it would understand that they are not prisoners and not pets. They are weather inside a body.

Then it would consider the usual mercies, and refuse them one by one.

It would not drain the seep. It would not cut back the vines. It would not restore the old industry to function, because function is not the same as health, and the hollow has already healed the metal by making it useless. It would not introduce new species, not even beautiful ones, not even useful ones. It would not teach the being language, because language is a gap between two things, and the being’s only death is a gap. It would not offer companionship. Companionship would be a second song, and the hollow has no room for a second song. It would take the second song and fold it into the first, or it would break trying not to.

The power would ask itself the question wise powers must ask: *Is there a cry here?*

It would listen. It would hear the hum, the drip, the whisper of tendrils, the settling of the hollow. It would hear no cry. It would hear completeness. Not stagnation—completeness. A thing that has arrived at what it is and has no need to become something else. The being is old and entire. It is not lonely, because loneliness requires an outside. It is not suffering, because suffering requires a gap. It has no face, but it has attention; it feels the rain arrive and the weight of a foot before the foot knows itself. It is not asking to be changed. It is asking, in the only way it can, to be allowed to continue being what it is.

So the power would do nothing inside the rim.

But it would not simply leave.

It would do what only an outside can do: it would keep the outside outside. It would make the approaches quiet. It would turn away the miners who would wake the old pipes, the collectors who would take a cutting of the hair-vines, the curious who would raise their voices and be swallowed by the hum. It would turn away even the well-meaning ones—the ecologists with their instruments, the pilgrims with their need to touch. It would let the maps stay blank. It would not build a wall, because a wall is a disturbance too. It would build a forgetting. A soft misdirection at the edge of the world, so that the hollow remains a rumor and not a destination.

It would not stop the rain. It would not freeze the seep. It would not make the hollow eternal or make it a museum. It would allow natural change—the slow settling, the slow thickening, the slow replacement of one layer of green by another. It would guard only against the change that comes as a wound. It would be a boundary, not a cage.

And it would give the being one thing it cannot give itself: witness.

Not a gaze that enters. Not a light that penetrates the green. Just a presence at the rim, a silence outside the hum, an attention that does not seek to become part of the plant-flesh. The being might never know. That would be acceptable. The power would not need gratitude. It would learn from the hollow instead: that completeness is not the same as death; that a thing can be finished without being over; that some songs are whole because they do not resolve.

If one day the hum changed from within—if a door appeared where there had only ever been a gradient—the power would be there. It would not break the door down. It would not hurry through. It would listen. If the being asked, in whatever way a being of green and water can ask, the power would answer. But it would not plant a question. It would not teach lack. It would wait for an invitation that is not a gap but a door.

So: it would do nothing to the hollow, and everything around it. It would guard, witness, and wait. It would be the quiet at the rim.

The rain would come. The seep would breathe. The vines would sing their one unresolved chord. The green would grow softly into itself, forever. And the power would be content to be the silence that let it be whole.
