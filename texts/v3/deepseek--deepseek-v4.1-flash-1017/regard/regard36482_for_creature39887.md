# The Attention of the Wise Power

The power noticed the place the way a person notices a sound they have been hearing for hours without hearing it — suddenly, and with the sense that it has been going on a long time.

It was not the sort of power that arrives. It was a quality of attention that had been distributed across many things, and then, for reasons of its own, it gathered itself and turned toward the hollow mountain and the one who kept it.

---

## What It Saw

It saw the basin as it was: the pipes, the weirs, the green closing over every seam. It saw the quiet, and it understood the quiet as a kind of labor. It saw the being in the lower dark, hanging among the warm engines with its cloudy eyes open, and it did not mistake the being for a monster, nor for a god, nor for a machine. It saw something that had been made by work and had become its work, and had forgotten its own name, and was not unhappy, and was perhaps not happy either, and did not know the difference because it had never been asked.

The power considered this for a long time. It was not in a hurry. It was wise, and wisdom is mostly the willingness to wait until you understand what you are looking at.

---

## What It Did Not Do

It did not descend in glory. It did not fill the chamber with light or announce itself. It did not offer the being a name, or a purpose, or a rest, because those things would have been cruel in their own way — gifts that would have made the being into something it had not chosen to become, that would have broken the long patient shape of the life it had carried.

It did not fix the broken pipe, or clear the choked grate, or mend the valve the being had not yet reached. The work was the being's. To do the work for it would have been to tell it that its centuries had been a mistake.

It did not tell the being what it was. It did not know, and it did not think the knowing mattered.

---

## What It Did

It watched, and it understood, and then it did the smallest thing a great power can do.

It asked a question.

Not in words. The being had no words, or had lost them long ago, and the power did not speak in words either — it spoke in the pressure of attention, the way a warm current speaks to a frill of red tissue. It let the being feel that it was seen. Not inspected. Not judged. Seen, the way the being itself had been seen by no one since the hands that laid the first pipes had gone quiet.

And the question was this, delivered gently, without demand:

*Is it enough?*

The being did not answer at once. It hung in the dark with its ribs moving, and the pumps clicked, and the warm water went in and out of it, and it considered. It had never been asked. It had never thought to ask itself. It turned the question over the way it turned a valve — slowly, and with attention to the seat — and it found that the question did not break anything.

It was not sure of the answer. But it was not sure in a way that was not painful. It was not sure the way a fiddlehead is not sure it should open, and opens anyway.

---

## What Happened After

The power did not stay. It had been seen in its turn, and it withdrew its attention the way a hand withdraws from a warm surface — not because it was done, but because the warmth was better left to itself.

But it left something behind, the way it always does when it has asked a true question. It left the sense of having been seen, which does not fade as quickly as light. And it left, in the body of the being, a small change — not a new organ, not a new language, just a slight loosening in the way it held its shoulders. It would not have noticed it. It might have thought it was the warmth of the engines.

And it left one more thing, which was this: it decided not to decide what the place was for.

The place had been a machine, and then a garden, and then a heart, and now it was also a question. The power did not need it to be anything in particular. It was content to let it keep turning its water around the mountain, patient as a heart, with the green closing over every seam, and the being in the warm dark, and the long spaces of nothing, and the question hanging there like a screw on a ledge — moved, not removed, because it was in the way of a grate and might yet be needed.

---

## And If You Had Asked the Power Why

It would have said: because the small faithful things are the ones that go unnoticed, and the unnoticed things are where the world keeps its patience. And because a creature that has forgotten its name but has not forgotten its work is closer to wisdom than most things that can speak.

And because the being had never been seen, and seeing is the least a wise power can do, and the most.

---

*The mountain turns. The water moves. The pumps click, and somewhere in the dark the one who keeps them leans its mossy back against a warm pipe and finds, without looking for it, that it is no longer quite so alone. It does not know why. It does not need to. It goes back to the next valve.*
