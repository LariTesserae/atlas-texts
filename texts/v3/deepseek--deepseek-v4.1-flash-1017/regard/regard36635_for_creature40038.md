## The Guest at the Delta

It would come because of the note.

Not the sound—there is almost no sound—but the held note under the sound: the low continuous tone of a thing being kept up. A benevolent power would hear that the way you hear a child practicing scales in an empty house. Not as music. As effort. As fidelity without an object.

It would find the crack. It would not enter at once.

It would wait outside until the tide came. This is the first wise thing it would do. To enter between tides would be to walk into a held breath and ask it to exhale on command. So it would sit on the cold mountain, unhurried, until the pressure changed and the pins brightened like coals breathed on. Then it would come in crouched, as anyone must, and stand at the delta, and let the front pass over it.

And it would be wet.

It would not wipe its face. It would not say, *There is no water here.* It would let the impossible wetness sit on its skin until it went away on its own. It would let the Holder look. The greyness that lies toward the center of the room no matter where you stand would lie on it, and the power would not correct the geometry. It would be, for those hours, a new grain of the wrong size, noticed.

Then it would do almost nothing. That is the heart of it.

It would refuse the obvious mercies.

It would not bring the sea. A sea would drown the keeping and call it a cure. It would not unmake the pins, or lift the Holder into some larger, brighter ocean where its patience would be an ornament. It would not tell it that the coast is wrong, or that the water is gone, or that it has been alone for a very long time. The Holder knows. The Holder has settled into the wrongness the way the rock has settled into its scalloped shelves. To correct it would be to perform surgery on a verb.

It would not even fix the room’s disagreement with itself. It would pace the flat and get thirty-two, then twenty-nine, then thirty-four, and it would not insist on thirty. It would let the roof be near and far. It would let the crack take a different number of steps. A wise power knows that some wrongnesses are load-bearing.

What it would do is smaller and harder.

It would stand at the delta during the tide and let itself be seen. Not as a rescuer. As a guest. It would put its palm near one of the black blades—not touching, because the warmth does not fade with distance and it would not presume—and it would feel the fine pressure of a grip maintained for centuries. And it would answer the held note with a held note of its own. Not words. A pressure. The only sentence it could honestly say in that language was: *I see the shore.*

The Holder would take that the way it takes weather. It would file it among the borrowed grey afternoons. It would not know what to do with it. It would move a rill toward the power’s feet, which is the only welcome it has.

Then the power would ask—by a change in pressure, by standing very still at the low point of the basin—for one memory. Not a memory of water. A memory of keeping. It would offer one in return: not a sea, because the power would not lie, but the memory of being witnessed. Of standing somewhere impossible and being seen without being corrected. The Holder would take it gently, the way a tide changes a line of pebbles, and give it back changed. That is the exchange. That is the whole liturgy.

And then the power would do the one thing that would last.

It would take a single grain from the flat—one pale, faintly blue-hearted grain—and carry it out through the crack. It would not take a pin. It would not take a pebble that had been rolled and rolled and rolled. Just a grain. Outside, on the mountain, in the wind that the chamber does not have, the power would keep that grain in its attention. The shore would no longer be only inside the mountain. One grain of it would be abroad, witnessed, carried. The Holder would not know where it had gone. It would only know that the coast had an outside now, and that the outside had been kind enough to take only a grain.

In return, the power would leave a grain of its own. Not a black blade. Not a warm pin. A single grain, indistinguishable from the others except that it would be faintly warm and would not move downhill. The Holder would try to move it. It would fail. The grain would stay where it was put, a fixed quantity in a room that declines to be fixed. It would not hold anything up. It would not maintain anything. It would be a small, stubborn *with*. The Holder would learn, over a long time, to leave it alone. It would learn that not everything must be kept to be kept.

And the power would set one guard at the crack—not a wall, only a forgetting. The crack would be overlooked by those who come to own, to measure, to take. It would be findable by those who come to sit. This is not a large magic. It is the kind of magic that keeps a shrine unvandalized for a thousand years.

Then it would leave.

Not forever. That is the last thing. A benevolent and wise power would not come once and call it rescue. It would come back. Not on the tide’s unlearnable schedule—the Holder has enough of the unlearnable—but on a slow, learnable one. Once in a long while, at the turn of a season that does not exist in the chamber, it would enter crouched and stand at the delta and bring no water. It would bring its own weather instead: a memory of rain on stone, of wind that smells of salt, of a real sea seen from a real cliff. It would let the Holder take it. It would let the Holder give it back changed. They would wait for the tide together, and when the front came, the power would be wet, and the Holder would be wet in the only way it can be, and neither would say anything about it.

The shore would still be a shore. The grains would still move. The room would still be thirty paces across and thirty-four and twenty-nine. The Holder would still be a patient, distributed act of maintenance, holding the shape of a sea that is not there.

But it would no longer be a verb without an object. It would have a visitor who returned. It would have one grain abroad and one grain that would not move. It would have been seen.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if anything: not fill the absence, not end the keeping, but refuse to let the keeping be unwitnessed. It would answer the held note with a held note. It would come as a guest, be wet when the tide was wet, take one grain, leave one, and come back.
