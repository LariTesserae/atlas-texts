**What the Wise Power Does**

It comes in without entering. It has learned that presence is a kind of weather, and weather here is not neutral. So it stands at the rim of the basin—or rather, it becomes a rim: an attention at the edge.

It sees a furnace and a farm. It sees no single face. It sees the ant roads as nerves, the fungus gardens as stomach, the waste heaps as kidneys, the bromeliads as villages, the water and air as medium. It sees that the being is the running. Then it asks the old question: *Is this a wound?* It finds crowding, predation, drowning, rot. It also finds that these are the metabolism. Remove them and the place dies of kindness. So it does not clean the pools, does not lift the fallen, does not dry the air, does not save individual ants, does not speak to the queen. It would not make the colony a pet or a monument.

It considers the colony as a possible subject. It could write pheromone sentences, warm a mound, cool a road. But any message would be either noise or command. The colony’s mind is contact and pressure and smell; an alien signal would be a seizure, not a revelation. So it listens instead. It learns the grammar of junction-yield, leaf-size, waste-placement. It does not answer, because no question has been asked in a language that can receive an answer.

Then it does what wise benevolence can do for a self-sufficient living thing: it guards the conditions, not the outcomes. It turns the basin aside from the things that are not weather and not evolution—the chain saw, the mine, the plantation, the collector, the road, the well-meaning sterilizer who would fence and drain and interpret. It makes the place unprofitable and unmapable to extraction. It does not stop the tree from falling across a road. It does not stop the fungus from failing or the rain from coming twenty minutes too hard. It stops the bulldozer. It keeps the water table and the cloud lid if the outside world begins to burn, but only enough that the place can keep its own slow time: a mound gaining a hand’s width in a decade, a hollow filling into root mat, the hinge-call continuing unseen.

It gives one thing besides protection: witness. It causes the place to be known to some who can approach without owning—not as a resource, not as a spectacle, not as a laboratory, but as a neighbor whose customs are older and stranger. It leaves no temple, no marker, no miracle. A marker would be a new surface, and here every surface is used. It leaves a boundary in the minds of those who can hear: a taboo made of attention. Those who come to extract lose the thread; those who come to listen are allowed to be used by the place for a while—mosquitoes at the wrist, a thread from shoulder to branch—and then leave.

To the ants themselves, nothing. No uplift, no cure, no immortality. The colony remains one body, its roads its nerves, its furnace its heart, its queen a source rather than a ruler. It continues to make waste into food and food into more workers and workers into warm exhalation. The power watches the exhalation from the ground and recognizes it as a kind of breathing. It does not call it prayer. It does not need to.

Then it withdraws. The Warm Floor goes on: drip, hinge-call, the low continuous chewing under everything. The only change is relational. The place is now held in a regard that will not consume it. The wise power has done almost nothing, exactly where nothing was the right thing—and that almost-nothing is a fence, a silence, and a witness.
