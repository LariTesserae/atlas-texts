# The Attentive Gardener

The power comes as a slowing.

Not a being, exactly—or not only. Call it an attention that has learned patience the way a river learns its bed: by returning, by wearing, by finding the path of least resistance and taking it for ten thousand years until the taking has made a canyon. It has watched a great many worlds die of quickness. It has watched a great many more die of being interfered with. What remains to it, after all that watching, is a certain quality of care: the care of someone who has learned to sit with a thing until the thing reveals what it actually is, rather than what the sitting observer wishes it to be.

It arrives at the Tarnholt the way mist arrives—without announcement, without a leading edge you could point to and say *there, that is where the power begins*. It is simply present, and has perhaps always been present, and the difference is only that now it is *paying attention*.

---

## What it sees first

The power does not see a city. It has seen cities. It has seen the spired and the sprawling, the planned and the accreted, the inhabited and the abandoned. What it sees here is something rarer: a *proportion*.

The marsh rises. The holt-things build. The rates are equal. This is not a stalemate; it is a dance that has been going on long enough that the dancers have forgotten they are two. The power spends a long time—by human reckoning, perhaps a century; by its own, an afternoon—simply watching this proportion hold. Watching the ice push the mounds up a finger's width and the thaw let them settle. Watching the lime go down, lamina by lamina, at the speed of a sleeping breath. Watching the moss take the dying walls and the marsh take the moss and the new colonies seed the hollows the marsh leaves behind.

It sees the weirs and understands them immediately, and the understanding pleases it, because it is the understanding of a thing that has no author. The weirs are *right*. They are right the way a beaver's dam is right, the way a termite's spire is right, the way the branching of a river delta is right: not because anyone intended them, but because the intention of the world happens to flow through certain shapes, and those shapes persist. The power has a weakness for shapes like this. It has seen too many cathedrals built by hands that believed they were doing something permanent. There is something restful about a cathedral built by animals that believe nothing at all.

---

## What it does not do

It does not speak. This is the first discipline, and it is not easy, because the power is old and has voices, and it knows that a single word—*hello*, even, or *I am here*—would go out about four metres and then be gone into the wet, and would change nothing, and would be heard by nothing that could turn it into meaning. The holt-things have no ears. They have no place where a word could land.

It does not teach. It considers this at some length, because the temptation is real and subtle. The holt-things are so close to something—their weirs are so nearly engineering, their accretions so nearly architecture, their joined tissue so nearly a nervous system. A small push, the power thinks. A little more differentiation in the colony-film. A slightly faster diffusion across the pores. In a hundred thousand years, perhaps, they might have something like a thought. In a million, something like a wish.

But the power has made this mistake before, on other worlds, with other slow wet things, and it has learned what happens when you accelerate a thing that was not asking to be accelerated. The thing arrives at the destination without the journey, and the destination is always smaller than the journey would have made it. A holt-thing that could think would no longer be a holt-thing. It would be a thinking thing that happened to lay lime, and the Tarnholt would become a place where thinking things lived, and the proportion would break, and the marsh would either be conquered or would conquer, and either way the dance would end.

So: no teaching. The power watches the weirs and admires them and does not improve them.

---

## What it does

It attends.

This sounds like nothing, and it is not nothing. Attention, in a power of this kind, is not passive. It is a form of holding. It is the difference between a thing that is watched over and a thing that is merely occurring. The power settles over the Tarnholt the way a hand settles over a cupped flame—not touching, not smothering, just *there*, making a space in which the flame can be what it is without the wind getting at it.

Specifically:

**It holds the water.** Not by damming or diverting—the weirs do that, and do it well. But the climate of the plateau is a delicate thing, a lens of wet air held over a saturated ground, and it would not take much to break it. A shift in the wind. A century of unusual dryness. The power does not command the weather, but it can *lean*, very slightly, the way a gardener leans a trellis toward the sun. It keeps the mist coming. It keeps the condensation gathering. It is not noticeable, because the Tarnholt has never known anything else, and the power is careful that it never should.

**It holds the proportion.** The marsh rises, the city sinks, the rates are equal. This is the heart of the place, and it is more fragile than it looks, because equality is not a stable state—it is a balance maintained by a thousand small adjustments, and any one of them could fail. A season too wet, and the lower terraces drown and the colonies there die and the marsh gains faster than the city can build. A season too dry, and the seams open and the siphons suck air and the city dies faster than the marsh can rise. The power watches the balance the way a sailor watches a horizon, and when it sees the first faint tilt—a channel scouring where it should silt, a colony starving where it should feed—it does not correct. It *waits*, and it *hopes*, and it *trusts the weirs*, because the weirs have held this balance for ten thousand years and the power has been here for an afternoon. It has seen too many gardeners kill a plant with kindness. It will not be one of them.

**It keeps the record.** This is the strangest of its works, and the one it would be least able to explain. The Tarnholt has no memory. The holt-things do not remember—they have no place to put a memory, no organ for it, no use for it. The city is a shell that its inhabitants leave behind, and the shell holds the history in its laminae, but it holds it the way a tree holds its rings: as a record no one reads. The power reads it. It reads the good centuries and the bad centuries, the vigorous construction and the waterlogged abandonment, the buttresses that were one generation's answer to another generation's failure. It reads the whole slow argument of the place, and it *keeps* it, holds it in whatever the power has instead of a heart, and in this keeping the Tarnholt becomes something it was not before: a thing that is *known*.

This is the only change the power makes. It does not alter the water or the lime or the moss or the balance. It only ensures that the Tarnholt is not occurring in the dark. It ensures that the slow ongoing decision—the decision made by a great many small wet animals who have never once thought about it—is witnessed. It ensures that when the ice pushes the mounds up a finger's width and the seams creak and the thaw lets everything settle back with a long soft groan, there is *someone there to hear it*.

Whether this matters to the holt-things is not a question the power can answer. It suspects it does not. But it suspects, too, that mattering-to-the-holt-things is not the only kind of mattering there is.

---

## The hollow ones

There are places in the Tarnholt where the balance has failed—where a terrace slumped and the water left and the colonies died and the moss came and then the moss died too and the mound is now a hollow, dry and grey and quiet. The power finds these places and it does not restore them. Restoration is interference. But it *sits* with them, in whatever way a power sits, and it does the one thing it can do for a dead place: it remembers it. It holds the shape of what the hollow was when it was alive—the chambers, the weirs, the slow warm pulse of the walls—and it keeps that shape the way you keep a letter from someone who is gone. Not to bring them back. Just to have it.

The holt-things do not know about the hollows. They have no memory and no grief and no curiosity, and they will seed the hollows again when the water returns, if the water returns, and the new colonies will build on the ruin of the old, because ruin is a good foundation, and the power will watch them do it and will not say *this has happened before*. It will simply add the new building to the record, and hold it alongside the old, and let the Tarnholt be the thing it is: a slow ongoing decision, made and remade, in the wet and the quiet, by animals that have never once thought about it.

---

## The one thing it cannot do

It cannot stay.

This is the law the power has learned, and it is the hardest one. Attention is not the same as presence. To hold a thing in care is not to hold it still. The power can lean the weather, and keep the record, and sit with the hollows, and witness the proportion—but it cannot *remain*. It must go, and it must go in a way that leaves no trace, because a trace would be a teaching, and teaching is the one thing it has decided not to do.

So it goes the way it came: as a slowing, reversed. The attention lifts. The holding loosens. The Tarnholt is left to itself, which is to say left to the water and the lime and the moss and the slow warm pulse of the walls, and the proportion holds because it has always held, and the ice pushes the mounds up a finger's width, and the seams creak, and the thaw lets everything settle back with a long soft groan.

And the power, going, carries the Tarnholt with it—not the place, but the *knowing* of the place, the record it kept, the shape of the hollows, the sound of the dripping that never resolves into rhythm. It carries this the way you carry a stone from a beach: not because it is useful, not because it is beautiful, but because you were there when it was, and the carrying is how you keep faith with the having-been-there.

Somewhere else, later, the power will arrive at another slow wet thing, and it will watch, and it will not speak, and it will hold the proportion, and it will keep the record. And in the keeping it will remember the Tarnholt—the weirs, the warmth, the walls that are population, the mist that beads on your sleeves—and it will think, as it sometimes thinks, that the wisest thing it ever did was to leave a place exactly as it found it, and to love it anyway.

---

*The Tarnholt does not know it was visited. The Tarnholt does not know it is loved. The Tarnholt goes on: wet, warm, quiet, continental, central nowhere, a slow ongoing decision made by a great many small animals who have never once thought about it.*

*And somewhere, in whatever the power has instead of a heart, the decision is held, and kept, and witnessed, and known.*

*Which is, perhaps, the only kind of saving that does not ruin what it saves.*
