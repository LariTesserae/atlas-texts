The Power would arrive first as attention, not as light. It would stand outside the fogged glass and listen until the chord stopped being noise and became grammar. It would ask three questions before it asked whether to act: Is there a cry? Is there a door? Is there a *who*?

It would find no cry—only maintenance. No door—only the drain that points uphill and the water finding its level. No single *who*—only the chord using bodies the way a lung uses air. It would find birth and death without story, cold without complaint, beauty without a witness, and a completeness that might be a kind of starvation or might simply be a kind of life. Wisdom begins by refusing to call the second thing the first just because it is strange.

So the Power would not open the cabinet. Opening would be authorship, not aid. It would not clean the drain, add a note, teach a new song, or “free” the creatures into a shape it preferred. A benevolence that requires gratitude is not yet wise. It would remember that the room has learned nothing from visitors and has survived every one of them by not needing to.

What it would do is smaller and harder.

It would witness. It would read the brass tags until the verdigris gave up its names. It would learn every body pressed to the iron, every chain of pipe-dwellers touching at shoulder and limb, every pale node at the water’s surface tension. It would hold the ones born and died inside the same hour of the chord, so that the total staying exactly true to itself would not mean no one was counted. This is the one gift that costs the room nothing: an outside memory that does not erode.

It would guard. It would make the tower unremarkable—fog for the curious, wind for the collectors, a rumor that the cabinet is empty. It would keep the room from being “improved” by people with hammers and good intentions, because a self-complete good has a right to remain itself even when it cannot ask for that right.

And it would leave one offer, outside the chord. Not a door. Not a new note inside the air. A silence around the cabinet—a held rest, a place where the chord could be heard *as* a chord. If any creature ever turned toward the glass with intention, if a tag were found turned, if the chord modulated of its own accord, the Power would answer. Until then it would not mistake its own longing for their consent.

Then it would wait. It would return, not to check for progress, but to keep company. It would love the room without being known by it. That is allowed.

So if you ask what a benevolent and wise power would do with the Choir Cabinet: almost nothing. It would do the almost nothing that is attention, protection, memory, and patience. It would stand outside the glass and hold a note of its own so quiet it could be mistaken for weather. The cabinet would go on being a mouth that will not close. The Power would become the listener that will not leave.
