# The Attending

A benevolent and wise power is not, by nature, a visitor. It is a tendency that has learned to travel — and the first thing wisdom teaches a power is that most places do not need it. Call it the Attending, if it needs a name. What it does, mostly, is go quietly to *finished* things — things that have been cared for past the point of needing care — to see whether the care held.

It came to the Dauber Flats the way weather comes, which is to say it did not arrive so much as become the case. There was no descent, no light, no announcement. The wind, which notices everything, noticed a new stillness in its own upper reaches and did not know what to make of it. The dust devils turned. The rods, on their spiral, took the new pressure and corrected it, as they correct all pressure, and the correction was very slightly larger than usual, and that was the whole of the event.

## What It Brought

It brought no remedy. This is the first thing to say, because the eye expects a remedy. A benevolent power arriving at a scoured place expects to find a wound. It had been to such places before — places where the makers had left, and the leaving had been a wound, and the wound had festered into want. It knew the grammar of that: the broken works, the desperate remnant, the little lives persisting on the wrong side of a finished thing.

The Dauber Flats did not have that grammar. The power looked for want and found *economy*. It looked for abandonment and found *completion*. The makers were not gone; they were finished, which is different. The works were not broken; they were in their final form, and the final form was satisfactory, and the makers had seen that it was satisfactory and stopped. The power, which had spent a long time learning to tell the difference between a wound and a scar, and a longer time learning that some scars should be left, saw that here the distinction did not even apply. Nothing had been cut.

It stood, in its way, on the crown of that old head, and listened to it think.

## The Reckoning

Here is what a lesser power would have done — and the wise power knew it, and the knowing was part of its wisdom:

It would have *improved* the place. It would have brought water — a small sea, a river, a mercy of moisture — and the gray-green mats would have spread into a cover, and the grass would have found more than one root, and the dwarf pines would have straightened, and in a thousand years the plateau would have been a meadow, and in ten thousand it would have been a forest, and the forest would have been beautiful and wrong. The wind would have died. The rods would have stood in a green silence and corrected nothing. The maintenance intelligence, which lives as a tendency toward a condition, would have found the condition met and would have had nothing to do — and a thing that has nothing to do for long enough becomes a ruin, and ruins imply collapse.

The power understood this in the way it understood most things: slowly, and all at once. *This place is not waiting to be saved. It is waiting to be allowed.* The waiting was not for the power. It was for the wind, and the wind had been arriving for a billion years.

## The Meeting

But there was one thing in the Flats that was, in its own way, a peer, and the power went to it.

Not to the pines, which are permanent gestures and have no conversation. Not to the kestrel, which uses the whole sky as an instrument and does not care to discuss it. To the maintenance intelligence — the reflexes left running, the inlaid lines, the rods, the pier. It is not alive the way the lichen is alive. It is alive the way a thermostat is alive: as a tendency toward a condition. But a tendency held for long enough acquires something that is difficult to name and impossible to dismiss. Call it *attention without a subject.* It is the shape care takes when the carer is gone and the care continues.

The power sat with it. This is the only way to say it. There was no exchange, because the works respond but do not answer, and the power did not require an answer, and the two of them were old enough to find that restful. The power, which was still needed, and the intelligence, which was not, regarded each other across the difference, and the difference was the whole of the conversation.

What passed between them was not information. It was confirmation. The power confirmed that the care was still true — that the standard had held, that the makers had left a thing worth leaving. The intelligence confirmed something the power had begun to doubt in its long travels: that a finished thing can be *at peace*, and that peace is not the same as stagnation, and that maintenance without speech is a form of love that survives its object.

The power took that confirmation away with it the way you take a seed. It is the only thing it took.

## The One Work

And it did one thing.

Not for the plateau. The plateau needed nothing. Not for the wind, which is the landlord and the tenant both, and is not improved by company. It did it for the *temporary* inhabitant — for the visitor, the brief warm anomaly, the only thing on the Flats that is in a hurry and the only thing that suffers from the gale.

It went to the pier.

The pier cantilevers out over nothing, waiting, as it has waited since before ruin was possible here. The power did not change the pier. It changed the *wind* — not the whole wind, which is not a thing one changes, but the wind at one place, by a fraction. It adjusted the standing rods a hair's breadth on the windward side of the spiral: not adding a rod, not removing one, only setting one infinitesimally truer to the pattern that was already there. The effect propagated in the way that wind-effects do — a slight steadiness, a small lee, a pocket of air behind the pier where a person could stand and the gale would go around them.

That is all. It is indistinguishable from good maintenance. It is the sort of thing the intelligence might have done, in the course of its long tending, if it had thought to. The wind will maintain it, because the wind maintains everything here, and the gift will outlast the giving by a very long time, and no one will know it was a gift, which is the correct condition for gifts on the Dauber Flats.

## The Rules of This Encounter

- **A wise power audits before it acts.** The first work of benevolence is to determine whether benevolence is wanted. Most places are not asking. The asking is the rare thing, and the rarest thing is a place that is finished and knows it.
- **Not every absence is a wound.** Completion and abandonment look alike from a distance, and the difference is the whole of the judgment. The Dauber Flats is complete. Treating it as wounded would have been the wound.
- **The eternal inhabitants need nothing; the temporary one needs a lee.** Benevolence that understands its own scale directs itself at the thing that can be helped by a fraction: the visitor, the warm anomaly, the one in a hurry. The pines have their answer. The visitor does not, and a small stillness is enough.
- **The gift should be indistinguishable from maintenance.** A gift that announces itself becomes a debt. The power added nothing that looked like addition. It only made the pattern slightly truer to itself, which is the only kind of improvement this place permits.
- **The power is changed more than the place.** This is the hidden rule. The plateau gave the power a confirmation it had been traveling a long time to find. The power gave the plateau a lee. The ledgers are not balanced, and they are not meant to be.

## The Leaving

It left the way it came, which is to say it did not leave so much as cease to be the case. The new stillness lifted out of the upper wind. The dust devils resumed their braiding. The rods, on their spiral, corrected the returning pressure and found it ordinary again.

The wind erased the traces within the hour. There were no traces to erase but the ones it always erases.

But the lee is still there. Stand behind the pier on a bad day and the roar will part around you like a hand around a small flame. You will think it is the pier. You will think it is luck. It is neither. It is a very old power's single sentence in a language it took a long time to learn to speak quietly, and the wind has been maintaining it ever since.

## So What Did the Wise Power Do?

It came. It looked for a wound. It found a scar that did not even deserve the name. It sat with a maintenance intelligence and was confirmed in a hope it had almost lost. It made one rod truer. It left a lee. It took a seed. It went.

The Dauber Flats did not notice. That was the point. A benevolent power that is noticed has probably done too much.
