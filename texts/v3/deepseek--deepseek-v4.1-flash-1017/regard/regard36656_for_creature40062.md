# The Attention of the Wise

---

The power came as attention first, before it was anything else.

It noticed the plateau the way one notices a held breath—not by sound, but by the sudden awareness of a silence so complete it has its own weight. It perceived the slow pulse of the stone-eaters, each one a century's-long exhalation, and understood immediately that here was a place that had achieved something rare: a completeness. Nothing was missing. Nothing was waiting to be fixed.

The wise power did not descend. It did not manifest. It simply... attended.

---

For a long time—days, years, the distinction has no meaning here—the power did nothing but learn the grammar of this place.

It learned that the stone-eaters do not think, but they remember: their flesh holds the memory of every drought, every long winter, every century of mist, encoded in the density of their crusts and the patterns of their seams. It learned that the moss-backs dream, in whatever way a creature with a nervous system like a slow tide might dream, and that their dreams are made of warmth and the taste of minerals. It learned that the crawlers, alone among the inhabitants, experience something like fear—a quick, cold flicker when a shadow falls—and that this fear is the plateau's only concession to urgency.

The power understood, with the clarity that only true benevolence possesses, that this was not a place to be improved. Improvement implies deficiency. The plateau was not deficient; it was *complete* in a way that most worlds never achieve. Its completeness was not the completeness of a finished thing, but of a system so thoroughly integrated that every part fed every other part, and the whole had achieved a kind of equilibrium that was less like stillness and more like a very slow dance.

And yet.

---

And yet the power saw something else, too. It saw the edges.

It saw how the mist sometimes thinned, just slightly, at the plateau's margins, and how the lichen there grew a shade paler, strained by a dryness that was not quite drought but was not quite belonging either. It saw how the youngest stone-eaters, the ones still small enough to be mistaken for ordinary hills, sometimes shifted in their sleep and found no warmth nearby, no moss-backs to tend them, because the old ones had not yet died and the new ones had not yet grown large enough to anchor their own retinues.

It saw the loneliness of the in-between.

Not a loneliness that caused suffering—nothing here suffered, not in any way the power would call suffering. But a loneliness nonetheless: a slight pulling at the edges of the community, a place where the dance was not quite complete.

The power considered this for a long time.

---

What would a benevolent power do with a world that did not need saving?

The answer, when it came, was simple. It would give what could be given without taking. It would offer what could be offered without imposing. It would be, not a gardener, but a guest—one who brings a small gift, and then sits quietly, and does not rearrange the furniture.

So the power did three things.

---

**The first was a gift of warmth.**

Not fire, not heat—nothing so crude. The power simply... leaned. It directed a fraction of its attention toward the cold edges of the plateau, the places where the mist thinned and the lichen paled, and it held that attention there like a hand cupped around a candle.

The effect was subtle. The mist thickened, just slightly, at the margins. The lichen deepened by a shade. Nothing dramatic—a change that would take a century to notice, a millennium to matter. But a young stone-eater, settling into its first long sleep at the plateau's edge, found the frost less biting than it might have been. Its seams opened a fraction wider. Its warmth leaked a fraction farther. And in the spring—if there was spring here, if the word had meaning—a new patch of moss-backs found its lee, and began to graze.

The power did not stay to watch. It had given what it could give. The rest was the plateau's own.

---

**The second was a gift of memory.**

The power understood that the stone-eaters remembered, but that their memory was locked in flesh and stone, inaccessible even to themselves. They could not share what they knew. Each generation began anew, feeling its way through the world with the same slow patience as the first.

So the power gave them a way to speak.

Not words—nothing so direct. But the power wove a thread of itself into the damp air, a thread so fine it was indistinguishable from the mist. And through that thread, the stone-eaters could... not communicate, exactly, but resonate. A young stone-eater, struggling to find the right rhythm of expansion and contraction, would feel a faint tug at the edge of its awareness—the echo of an older stone-eater's centuries of practice, humming through the damp like a tuning fork.

The moss-backs felt it too, in their way. A herd approaching a new pool would slow, hesitate, and then turn—not because they had learned to fear the water, but because some ancient memory, borrowed and returned, whispered that the footing was uncertain there. The crawlers, most sensitive of all, felt the thread as a faint vibration in the stone, a kind of geological music, and they danced to it in their scuttling way, their shells clicking in rhythms that had no purpose but were beautiful nonetheless.

The power did not teach. It simply opened a door. What passed through was the plateau's own wisdom, finally given voice.

---

**The third was a gift of possibility.**

This was the most delicate gift, and the power hesitated longest before giving it.

The plateau was complete. But completeness is not the same as stillness. A thing can be whole and still grow, still change, still become something it was not before—so long as the becoming comes from within.

So the power planted a seed. Not a physical seed—nothing so crude—but a possibility, a potential, a question. It planted it in the deepest part of the plateau's slow economy, in the place where the stone-eaters' warmth met the moss-backs' decay met the fungi's patient work, and it whispered:

*What else might you become?*

The power did not answer the question. It did not even know the answer. But it knew that the plateau, given enough time—and time was the one thing this place had in abundance—might find its own answer. Might develop something new: a stone-eater that learned to dream in colors; a moss-back that grew a shell; a crawler that paused, not in fear, but in wonder.

Or perhaps nothing would change. Perhaps the question would sink into the stone and be forgotten, and the plateau would continue as it always had, complete and unchanging, for another million years.

Either way was good. The power had not come to shape. It had come to offer.

---

When the gifts were given, the power withdrew its attention—not entirely, never entirely, but enough that the plateau could feel itself again, could return to its own rhythms without the faint pressure of being watched.

And the plateau... continued.

The mist beaded on the lichen. The stone-eaters breathed. The moss-backs grazed, and died, and became compost, and were reborn as lichen, and were grazed again. The crawlers scuttled and froze and scuttled again, their shells clicking faintly against the ancient stone.

But something was different. Something had shifted, so slightly that no single creature could have noticed, but so deeply that the plateau itself seemed to settle into a new configuration—like a sleeper finding a more comfortable position, or a held breath finally released.

The warmth at the edges held. The thread of memory hummed. The question waited, patient as stone, for whatever answer might come.

---

And somewhere, in a place that had no name and needed none, a young stone-eater—the smallest of its kind, barely older than a century—shifted in its sleep. And for the first time in the history of the plateau, it did not dream of warmth and minerals and the slow taste of rock.

It dreamed of something else. Something it had no word for.

Something that might, in a thousand years or ten thousand, become a word.

The power, watching from a distance that was not a distance, allowed itself something very like a smile. Then it turned its attention elsewhere—to other plateaus, other worlds, other places that might or might not need what it had to give.

But it left a thread of itself behind, a filament of attention so fine it was indistinguishable from the mist.

And the plateau, breathing its slow breath, holding its slow warmth, dreaming its slow dreams, did not notice.

But it was not alone. And perhaps that was the greatest gift of all.

---

*The wise power gives what can be given without taking. It offers what can be offered without imposing. And then it sits quietly, and does not rearrange the furniture.*

*A guest, not a gardener.*

*Attention, not intervention.*

*And the world, complete and whole and slowly becoming something more, breathes on.*
