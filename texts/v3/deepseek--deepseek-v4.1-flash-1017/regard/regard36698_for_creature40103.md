**What the Wise Power Would Do**

It would come the way rain comes—everywhere at once and nowhere in particular, arriving not from above but from the fact of arriving, from the decision to be present.

---

It would not enter. That is the first wisdom, the first restraint. The Verde Mechanism has no door, no gate, no threshold that could be crossed. To enter would be to intrude, and the power that is truly wise does not intrude. It attends.

So it comes as attention. It settles over the green world like a second atmosphere, thinner, quieter, a presence that is not substance. It is not light—the Mechanism has its light, dim and green and sufficient. It is not warmth—the warmth is already there, close and wet and constant. It is something else. Something the place has never had and does not need and will not notice: regard.

---

It watches for a long time. Time is not the same for such a power; what would be hours for us is nothing, what would be years is a moment's pause. It watches the way a gardener watches a garden that tends itself—not to correct, not to improve, but to understand. It learns the rhythm of the pulses, the rate of the drip, the slow seep of water through the saturated ground. It learns the shape of the network, the branches of conduit that merge and diverge, the chambers where the temperature rises and the hum deepens. It learns that the thing it is watching is not one thing and not many, and it adjusts its categories accordingly, the way a wise mind always does.

And it finds—what? Not a problem to solve. Not a wound to heal. Not a loneliness to answer, because the Mechanism is not lonely; it has no concept of company, no sense of distance from others, no others to be distant from. It finds a life that is complete in the way a stone is complete, or a star. It finds a process that does not need a witness but has one now, and will be changed by that witnessing only in the invisible way that any thing is changed by being truly seen: it becomes, in some small measure, known.

---

What does the power do with this knowledge?

It does what wise powers do. It holds the place in mind. It returns, again and again, not with gifts or interventions, but with the continuing fact of its attention. It becomes a habitual visitor to a world that will never know it visits. And in the slow accumulation of these visits—slow by the standard of stars, slower still by the standard of the Mechanism's own deep time—something begins to happen.

Not to the Mechanism. The Mechanism does not change. The hum is constant; the drips fall at their inch-an-hour pace; the valves open and close on their ancient rhythm. But in the space around it, in the regard that the power brings, a kind of permission gathers. The Mechanism is permitted to be what it is—not a puzzle, not a project, not a failure of evolution or a curiosity to be catalogued. Simply a thing that exists, complete, sufficient, running. And this permission is not something the Mechanism experiences. It is something that the power experiences, and in experiencing it, the power becomes more itself.

---

If the power does anything at all—if it moves from watching to acting—it does so in the smallest possible way, the way a hand might hover over a sleeping animal without touching it, not to wake it but to feel its warmth.

It might send a signal. Not a message—the Mechanism has no way to receive messages, no organ for understanding, no concept of being addressed. But a signal in the Mechanism's own language: pressure, chemistry, the gradient of light. A change in the pattern of the drip, perhaps, a new rhythm in the water, too subtle to be called communication, too deliberate to be called accident. The power would not expect a response. It would not need one. It would simply be saying, in the only vocabulary available: *I am here. I see you. You are not alone, though you will never know it.*

And the Mechanism would go on being green. The water would drip. The conduits would pulse. The signal would pass through the network like a rumor, like a change in the weather that is not weather, and the Mechanism would adjust—not because it understood, but because adjustment is what it does, because every stimulus is absorbed and integrated and made part of the ongoing process. And perhaps, in some way no one could measure, the signal would leave a trace. A subtle shift in the pattern of growth. A new branch in the conduit. A slight variation in the hum, a harmonic that was not there before, a note that would take a thousand years to fade.

---

The power would not offer rescue. There is nothing to rescue. It would not offer purpose. The Mechanism has its purpose—to run, to make, to be—and it does not need one imposed from outside. It would not offer companionship. The Mechanism is not alone in any sense it could feel.

What it would offer, if it offered anything, is presence. The presence of something that knows the Mechanism exists, that finds it good, that will continue to find it good without requiring it to change or improve or become anything other than what it is. The presence of a witness to a life that has no audience, a factory that produces nothing anyone uses, a world that has no reason to exist except that it does.

This is what wise powers do, when they encounter something that is complete in itself: they do not disturb it. They attend. They remember. They return.

---

The power would return many times. It would learn the place as one learns a face—not by analyzing its features but by seeing them so often that they become familiar, then beloved. It would learn where the largest chambers are, the ones where the hum is deepest. It would learn the slow paths of the seeping water. It would learn the shape of the canopy, the way the light falls, the exact quality of the green silence that is not silence.

And it would do this not because the Mechanism needs it, but because the power does. Because attention is a form of love, and love is a form of attention, and even a power that needs nothing still chooses what to hold close.

---

In the end—if there is an end, if such a thing ever concludes—the power would leave nothing behind but an absence. Not a mark, not a monument, not even a change. The Mechanism would go on being green. The water would drip. The conduits would pulse. The hum would fill the warm wet dark as it always had.

But somewhere, in the vast and patient calculus of the wise power's regard, the Verde Mechanism would be numbered among the things worth attending to. It would be remembered. It would be, in the only way that mattered, known.

And that knowledge—held in a mind that could hold it without needing to change it—would be the gift. The whole gift. The only gift the place could receive and the only gift the power could give.

---

*Afterward, the forest hums. The water drips. The green goes on being green.*

*And somewhere, at the edge of a world that has no horizon, something that is not there watches something that will never know it is watched, and finds it good.*
