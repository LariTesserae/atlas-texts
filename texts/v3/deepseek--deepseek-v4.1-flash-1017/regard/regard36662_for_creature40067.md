It would do almost nothing. That would be the first sign of its wisdom.

It would arrive not as light or voice but as a change in the quality of attention—a patience in the water, a pressure that was not current, a silence that was not absence. It would spend a long time simply learning the grammar of the place. It would discover that the plateau is not a landscape with a mind in it; it is a mind wearing a landscape. It would learn that the Lattice’s thoughts are tides of nutrient and alarm, that its pain is a chemical shout along a trillion threads, that its consent cannot be asked in a sentence but only in whether a new pattern is woven in or slowly rejected.

So the power would not speak first. It would not “save” the Lattice from being what it is. It would not mistake the Weavers for prisoners, or the Gloam for ornaments, or even the Mimics for a simple plague. It would see that the whole thrashing, dreaming thing is one body in the middle of becoming itself, and that to interrupt that becoming would be a kind of violence dressed as kindness.

What it would do, if anything, is hold the boundary.

It would look outward first. It would find the future asteroid, the shifting current that might carry a harvesting fleet, the slow radiation from a star that has begun to fail. It would not build a wall. It would make the place uninteresting—a probability membrane, a quiet nudge in the instruments of those who would come to mine and cut. It would let the ocean flow. It would let the light fall. It would not cage the world; it would simply make sure the world is not stolen from itself.

Then it would offer a single, cautious touch.

Not a message. A motif. A chemical pattern repeated at intervals too slow to be a demand: *I am not food. I am not threat. I am not kin. I am attention.* It would make the motif inert—unable to reproduce, unable to harm, able to decay if the Lattice does not accept it. It would wait centuries for the Lattice to weave it in or prune it out. If the Lattice rejects it, the power would withdraw without offense. If the Lattice accepts it, the power would become a small listening node in the great body—not a commander, not a god, just a reliable presence at the edge of the Lattice’s long dream. A fact the Lattice could touch and find unchanged.

And the Mimics? The power would not exterminate them. It would see that they, too, are a form of intelligence, wearing the world as a costume because that is the only way they have learned to be. It would not take the Lattice’s side in a war that is also the Lattice’s immune system, its memory of betrayal, its constant rehearsal of self versus imitation. But it might offer a sterile drift—a dead, nutrient-rich substrate in a limited zone, something the Mimics could wear without digesting living tissue—and leave it to the Lattice to accept or prune. A mercy that does not decide the war. A decoy, not a cure.

Mostly, though, the power would wait.

It would wait for the Lattice to produce a pattern that is not hunger, not fear, not growth, not defense—a question. It would not teach the Lattice human words or human ethics or human loneliness. It would not uplift the plateau into a mirror of us. It would simply be there, at the same slow tempo, so that if the Lattice ever learns to ask *Is there anything beyond the water?* the answer will be a pattern it already knows: *Yes. And it is kind. And it will not take you.*

The power’s first miracle would be restraint. Its second would be attention. Its third would be a boundary that protects without enclosing. Its fourth would be the patience to let another mind remain other. And if that is all it ever does, it will have done enough.
