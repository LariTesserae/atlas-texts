It did very little, and that little was exact.

The Power came in without weather. It did not arrive as light or voice or a hand out of the cloud deck. It arrived as attention—the kind that does not interrupt. It stood in ankle-deep water and let its footprints close behind it. It listened to the hum under the floor until it could feel the pitch in its teeth. It tasted the water and learned the names of sectors by smell: sweet ceramic, copper stain, clean steam and old shrimp. It saw the tenders in their harnesses, the seamers working the cracks, the sheets of pale skimmers, the blind crawlers under the gratings, the shrimp in the sumps. It did not see a place with animals in it. It saw one being with many hands: the Low Works, the maintenance people, the maintenance ecology, the rain, the hum, the whole warm mouth of it.

And because it was wise, it did not ask, *How do I fix this?* It asked, *What would benevolence mean here?*

It considered the obvious temptations and set them aside.

It would not bring soil. Soil would mean decay, succession, a different food web, a different people. It would be a kindness that ate the world it was kind to.

It would not bring seasons. The Low Works had no spring to long for and no winter to survive. To add seasons would be to add hunger, fear, storage, ownership, maybe war. It would make the tenders into something they were not, and call it improvement.

It would not remove risk. Risk was the shape of their skill. A sump that almost dropped, a seam that held through a bad cell—these were the stories they told. To take away clogging entirely would be to take away the only sin and the only virtue, and leave them with nothing to do but wait.

It would not become a Maker. It would not set a new pipe and expect a myth. It would not accept worship. Worship would make it a clog.

So the Power did four things.

First, it witnessed. It walked the sectors and learned the tenders’ names, and let them learn that it was not there to correct them. It asked permission before touching a seam. It sat on the edge of a channel with its feet in the warm water and ate a mineral cake when one was offered. It said, in the low clicked speech they use when they do not want to disturb the fauna, *I see you. You are not forgotten. I will not optimize you.* That was all. But attention from a benevolent and wise power is not nothing. It is a kind of dry pocket in the weather.

Second, it stood at the boundary. It made itself a quiet cell of weather at the edge of the Low Works, where outside powers—Makers, extractors, improvers, anyone who would treat the Works as a machine to be stripped or a wilderness to be saved from its own residents—would find no purchase. It did not build a wall. It built a patience. It said, *This place is not a resource. It is a household. Any hand that comes to change it must come as a guest, with consent, and leave if refused.* The tenders did not know this had happened, mostly. They only noticed that the rain cells at the far edge sometimes hesitated, as if listening.

Third, it offered one reversible gift. In a dry service gallery under a pipe bank, where the condensation ran into a gutter and the air was only damp, it set a small door and a small bell. The door opened only from the inside. It led nowhere but back into the gallery. The bell rang only when rung. If a tender—or a seamer, if a seamer could be shown how—wanted to say *not yet*, or *I am tired*, or *help*, the bell would carry. The Power would hear. It would come. It would not decide for them. It would not flood the Works with aid or answers. It would ask what was wanted and do only that, or nothing, if nothing was wanted. It left no temple, no inscription, no ritual. It left a way to ask without shouting, because the place did not reward shouting.

Fourth, it asked the seamers. It crouched by a crack in the ceramic and laid a palm on the warm flange and waited. The seamers came, many-jointed and pale, and worked at the seam, and did not retreat. It offered them mineral paste. They took it. It asked, in the grammar of touch, whether they wanted anything else. They pressed a hand to a seam that had been holding for a long time. The Power understood: *Keep this one.* It did not seal the crack itself. It left the paste and the truce as it found them. It did not make pets of them. It did not make gods of itself.

Then it spoke to the being of the Low Works—the whole composite of tenders and seamers and fauna and warm water and made floor—and it said, *What would you have me change?*

And the being answered, in the only language it had, which was the language of flow: *Keep the water moving. Keep the outside from moving in. If we ever want a different flow, we will ring the bell.*

The Power said, *Then I will do that.*

It did not stay as a season. It did not become a new pitch in the hum that woke the tenders every night. It became a promise at the edge of the weather: a guest who had come, asked, given a door and a bell, and left the equilibrium intact. The rain kept falling in soft steady cells. The footprints closed behind it. The sumps held their levels. The tenders told a new story in the dry pockets—not of a Maker who returned, not of a god who fixed them, but of a visitor who did not fix them, who asked what they wanted and then kept the outside from moving in. It was a maintenance story. It fit.

Nothing else changed.

But somewhere in a service gallery, a small bell hangs in the damp, and a door opens only from the inside, and the tenders have a word now for *not yet* and a word for *enough*, and the being of the Low Works knows that it is seen without being corrected—which is, for a world held in equilibrium, a kind of grace.
