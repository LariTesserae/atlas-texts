# The Bright Pocket

You find it by the cold coming out of the ground — a seam of it, narrow as a shoulder, in a hillside that has not decided yet whether it is a hill. The grass on the slope is thin and grey-green and grows in widely spaced tufts, and between the tufts the soil is loose and unconsolidated, the kind of ground that has not been walked on enough to pack down. The seam breathes. Not wind — something slower than wind, a displacement of air that keeps its own time, and it is cool and smells of wet chalk and something faintly animal.

Getting in is not difficult. The rock around the opening is soft. It crumbles under your hand in a way that rock should not, and the crumbs are warm where the interior air touches them and cold where the outside air does, and the boundary between the two is not a line but a slow weather.

Inside is a room. That is the whole of it — one room, pocket-sized, maybe nine paces across at its widest, low enough that you will stoop in places and can stand upright in others, and the ceiling is not a ceiling so much as a place where the walls lost interest in continuing. It is bright. This is the first thing and it takes a while to accept: the place is *bright*, evenly and sourcelessly, with no shaft, no opening, no glow-worm constellation, no plausible origin. The light is simply a property of the space, the way warmth is a property of a body. It has direction — it comes from everywhere and slightly from the left, though the leftness drifts over minutes and by the time you have turned around twice you cannot say where it started. Shadows are shallow and do not agree with the shapes that cast them. You will notice this and then stop noticing it, and the stopping is the strange part.

The stone is unfinished. That is the honest description. The walls are pale, wet-looking, faintly translucent in the thin places, and they are *still becoming stone* — there are shelves and lobes and drips of mineral that have not set, that hold a dent from a fingertip and slowly fill it back in over an hour. Some of it is the consistency of soft cheese; some of it is already hard and rings faintly when tapped; and the two states are not distributed by any logic you can see. New stone is being made here at a rate you can almost watch, and old stone is being unmade, and nothing is being demolished — it is more like a thought being revised. The floor is a shallow unevenness of the same material, damp everywhere, with a film of water that gathers in the low spots and does not quite form pools, and in the film the light behaves oddly, bending toward the walls rather than reflecting the ceiling that is not there.

Water arrives the way the light does: without weather. There is condensation on every cool surface in a fine even breath of it, and every so often a drip falls from a place where there is nothing to drip *from*, landing with a small flat sound. The damp has a smell — wet lime, old breath, the inside of a jar. It is cool in here, steadily, the cool of a cellar or a well, the temperature of a thing that has never seen the sun and does not miss it.

Vegetation has made a thin attempt. Along one wall, in the seam where the mineral is furthest along, a mat of pale thread-roots has gotten in from somewhere above and hangs in a curtain of white filaments, some of them a metre long, all of them feeling slowly at the air. There are patches of something moss-like on the dampest stone, grey-green and sparse, in islands a hand's width apart, and they are not quite moss — they retract. Not much. A few millimetres, when the light's direction swings past them, and then they extend again. That is the whole of the flora and it is enough.

The fauna is not sparse. The fauna is the loudest thing about the place, and the place is quiet, so what that means is: a constant low tick and rustle, a sound like fine rain on leaves, made by more small animals than you can count and hardly any of them larger than a thumbnail. They are everywhere and they keep their distance — the density is in the *variety*, in the way every surface has two or three things on it going about their business at arm's length from one another. Mites the colour of the stone, so that you see them only when they move. A kind of pale isopod that walks in slow deliberate arcs and stops for long stretches, and whose shells are still soft, still hardening, so that a touched one leaves a smear. Small fly-like things with too many wings, or wings that are not quite wings yet, that lift off heavily and land badly. Threads of something in the water film that are animals and move with purpose. And in the deeper crevices, things with more shape: a segmented, unhurried creature the length of your finger that comes out, tastes the air with a fringe of feelers, and goes back in, and does this every few minutes like a tide.

They are all, in some way, unfinished. It is the rule of the place made flesh. There are creatures here that are larval and will never pupate; there are creatures with an extra segment and creatures with a missing one; there are two animals that seem to be the same animal at different stages of deciding what it is. Nothing here is sick. Nothing is dying of it. It is simply that the pocket does not distinguish sharply between a thing that is being made and a thing that is alive, and so neither do the things.

Of building there is very little and it is very old or very new and you will not be able to tell which. On the driest stretch of wall, someone has scratched a row of marks — not tally marks, not letters, but a repeated form like a bent finger, four of them, then a gap, then two more. The scratches have partially healed over with new mineral, which suggests age, but the healing is uneven in the way that fresh healing is uneven, which suggests otherwise. In one corner, seven flat stones have been stacked, deliberately, in a low wall a hand high; the wall is not doing anything, not holding anything back, not marking anything you can see, and it is the only straight line in the room. There is a hollow worn in the floor by the wall, shallow and smooth, the size of a person sitting. Nobody is sitting in it. The water has filled it to a depth of a centimetre and something pale is living in the water.

And then the rules, which are the part you will think about afterwards.

The pocket is not stable in the way that a place is stable. It edits. The first time you notice, a lobe of stone you had used to orient yourself is not where it was, and the shelf below it has grown to meet where it went. Nothing you could call movement — no sound, no falling, no dust. It is as though the room has a memory of itself and keeps correcting toward it imperfectly. Distances are slightly unreliable for the same reason: a step that took you most of the way across in the morning takes you less than half in the afternoon, and the room has not gotten larger, it has gotten *more itself*. Time pools here. You will find that you have been watching the isopods for what feels like a few minutes and the water in the hollow has evaporated and refilled twice. You will find that a drip has fallen eleven times in the space of a breath, or that your own breath has been going on for a very long while.

The light brightens when things are born and dims, very slightly, when they stop, and since things here are always being born, it mostly brightens and recovers, brightens and recovers, like a slow pulse you can only catch at the edge of vision. The shadows do not keep up.

It is quiet. It is one of the quietest places you will ever stand in, and it is crowded, and it is bright, and it is still being made — and all of that at once, in a room you could cross in nine paces, under a hill that has not finished being a hill.
