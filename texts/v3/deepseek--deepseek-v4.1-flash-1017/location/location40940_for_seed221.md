# The Choir Cabinet

The highest thing is also the smallest thing: a room nailed to the top of the tower like a nail driven from below, iron and glass and copper, and inside it the whole world has been sorted into jars.

The room is a mouthful of a building. Six meters across at its widest, an octagon of riveted iron plate, and the rivets are the size of knuckles, and they weep. Water beads on every seam — condensation, dew, the slow sweat of a cold metal box in a cold high sky, and the water hangs in lenses and then lets go and falls, and the falling is the only event that has ever happened here. The floor is a shallow copper basin, dished, with a rim that catches everything, and a drain that does not drain because the drain was fitted before the room arrived at its final idea of itself and now points slightly uphill. So the water pools. Ankle-deep in the low side, a lens of dark liquid lying over the copper, and the copper beneath it has gone the green of old bread.

You have to understand the sound before you can see anything, because the sound arrives first and arrives all at once. It is not a chorus in the way a chorus is — it does not swell or fall or resolve. It is one sustained weaving, thousands of voices, and the voices are so close to one another in pitch that the air itself feels combed. There are pipes here. There are formerly-pipes here — a great acoustic organ of industrial ducting, oil drums sawn open and bolted in rows, lengths of chimney stacked like glasses, and in every open vessel and every sealed vessel there is something living and it is singing. The sacks are not sacks. The drips are not syncopated. The whole cabinet holds one chord and holds it, and the chord does not change, because nothing in this room changes, because the room is a machine that has achieved its final state and now only maintains it, and the maintenance is a sustained note.

The creatures are everywhere and there is no place for them and so they have layered. On the walls, flat to the iron, with their bodies pressed like coins, a crust of them. On the pipes, in the pipes, hanging from the pipe-hangers in loose chains of them, touching. In the drain's pooled water, a skin of something with many small faces just at the surface tension. Some are the size of a thumb, some the size of a hand, some so small you notice only the cold. They are not one species. They were brought here maybe, or rose here, or are being slowly made here — it's hard to tell, and the room does not encourage telling. They are furred and chitinous and one of them is vaguely feathered and none of them have names, or the names are stamped on brass tags riveted beside their vessels, and the tags are illegible with verdigris.

The light is dim. The light is a single industrial lamp on a swan-neck from the ceiling, gone down to ember-orange, and it is not the main light — the main light is the animals' own, faint and scattered, the way the sea is bright with things that make their own small reasons to be seen. The glass panes are fogged and then cleared by drips and then fogged again. Outside is nothing you can see and nothing you need to: high air, no horizon, cold pressing on the glass like a palm.

Vegetation is a rumor. There is moss, but it is not moss so much as archive — a thin fur on the copper rim, three or four species of it, holding the water, feeding nothing. There is a forgotten sack of something in the corner that has gone soft and grey and is being eaten slowly by the animals, and this is the only plant in the room and it will never be replaced and it does not seem to matter.

The room is old in a way that has stopped, because the room does not erode. Its rust does not spread; the rust reached an agreement years ago with the damp and the two of them now hold the same border, unchanged. The paint on the iron is flaking only where it was flaking when you arrived. The animals are not aging, or the aggregation of them is not — some are born and some die and the total stays exactly true to itself. The water rises? No. The water has found its level. The song does not modulate. The lamp does not flicker.

This is what it is: a sealed cabinet on the top of the world, cold, damp, dense past comfort, full of invented life in invented vessels, singing one unvarying chord into one unvarying air, and the only movement the slow falling of water off the rivets, endlessly, the same water, or water indistinguishable from it, and the copper basin receiving it, and the copper basin giving it back up to the ceiling as vapor, and the ceiling condensing it again onto the rivets.

You could stand in it for an hour, or a year, or an imagined lifetime, and you would learn the room exactly, and the room would learn nothing from you. It was built complete. It is still being built, in the sense that the animals are still being made here, but they are being made along a line that was drawn before they arrived, and the line is a chord, and the chord is a held note, and the note is the room, and the room is a mouth that will not close.
