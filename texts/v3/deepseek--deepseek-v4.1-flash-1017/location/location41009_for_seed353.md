# The Settle

There is no ground here, only older canopy.

Stand anywhere on the plateau and you stand on leaves that died before the last of the machines was young. They have not rotted — there is nothing to rot them, no water, no bacteria worth the name — they have been pressed and sintered and pressed again until they are a substance like dark glass fiberboard, and beneath that board another layer, and beneath that another, each one a former surface, each one a season of a world that has been having the same season for a very long time. If you cut down — or rather, if you find a place where the weave has torn and you can lower yourself into it — you descend through strata the way a miner descends through rock, except that the rock is leaf, and the leaf is warm, and somewhere far below, at a depth nobody has reached twice, the layers give way to the machinery that made them.

## The weave

The whole of it is plant. That is the first and last fact. Everything you see is leaf and stem and runner and holdfast, and everything you do not see is also plant, and the plant has been persuaded — over an age of persuasion — into shapes that are not quite its own. There are colonnades of it: trunks the girth of furnace stacks, cored with a pale conducting pith, marching in ranks across the flattened top of the world for a distance it would take you days to cross. There are halls where the growth has been trained into roofs. There are conduits — vegetal, yes, but conduits — through which something hot and mineral moves with a slow peristalsis, and the conduits branch and re-branch and eventually end in a leaf the size of a door, which is not a leaf but a radiator, and the radiator is doing the work a factory does.

Because this is a factory. It has always been a factory. The plant is the substrate and the architecture and the product and the staff, and the machines are threaded through it like bones through a body that has grown around them and half-digested them and will, in some centuries, finish the job and grow new ones from the pattern it kept. Nothing here is natural. The mound itself — the elevation, the high place, the fact that you are standing above a plain you cannot see — is manufactured: a heap of sintered tailings, a mountain built on purpose, worn now to a soft-shouldered plateau, its edges slumping into the dark in long scalloped skirts of overgrowth. It is old past any useful counting. It has reached its final form, more or less, the way a very old man has reached his final face.

## The heat, and the dark

It is hot. Not brightly hot — there is no sun to be bright, and the sky, if the thing overhead is a sky, is the color of a closed eye. The heat comes up through the soles of your feet from the layers below, and it comes out of the leaves themselves, which run a slow exothermic chemistry the way other plants run photosynthesis: they are not eating light, they are eating heat and mineral and, in some way that the engineers who built the first of this would have recognized, they are eating *movement*. Their chemistry is not water-based; there is no water anywhere on this world, not a drop, not a memory of a drop. They drink a hot carbonate slurry that moves through them like blood and crystallizes at the edges of every wound, so that a torn stem glazes over with a white salt scab and goes on living.

The light is almost nothing. You navigate by the warmth of surfaces, by the faint long-wave glow of the canopy, which is very slightly brighter than the dark around it — a floor of coals seen through smoke. Now and then, somewhere out in the packed and woven distance, a vent lets go: a furnace throat clearing itself, and for two or three seconds there is *light*, orange, and you see the architecture of the thing you are standing in, a hundred meters of leaf and beam and conduit stacked into a night that has no ceiling. Then it closes. The image stays on your eye like a stain.

## Quiet, and why

The strange thing is how quiet it is.

This place is in constant upheaval. It is never still. Runners surge a meter in an hour; a colonnade that has stood for a thousand years will lean and fold and lie down over the course of an afternoon, and where it lies down something else is already rising. There are pressure waves in the mass, slow and enormous, like the turning over of a sea that has no water in it. Somewhere a great many things are being crushed and some great many things are being born, continuously, all across the plateau, and the whole of it is the noise of a bazaar at full cry —

— and you hear almost none of it. A creak. A click of cooling metal, deep down. A sound like a person breathing in another room. The far, patient *thum* of a pump that has been pumping for an unimaginable time.

The vegetation eats sound. It is one of the things it is for. The leaf-flesh here is a dense fibrous foam, absurdly absorptive, and the growth has been trained — or has trained itself, which amounts to the same after an age — into baffles and traps and spiral chambers that swallow the turbulence as it happens. The world's chaos is its food. Every collapse is absorbed, metabolized, turned into another centimeter of warm wet-adjacent tissue. To the plants, this place is deafening. To you, it is a library.

## The rules

Nothing falls. This is the law of the place, and it is enforced. If a limb tears loose, the weave below catches it within a few meters; if a machine sheds a plate, the runners find it and swallow it and it becomes ore again. There is no floor for anything to reach. The Settle is its own floor, all the way down, and the deeper you go the older and hotter and more mineral it becomes, until at some depth the leaf gives up and the machinery gives up and there is only the manufactured mountain, sweating quietly in the dark.

No animals live here. Nothing with eyes, nothing with legs. There are things that move — a bladder that inflates and rolls a little and anchors again, a hooked organ that snaps shut on nothing — but they are plant, all plant, organs of the one organism or of its many negotiating parts. If you stay long enough you will begin to feel that the place is watching you in the way a forest is watching: not with attention, but with the total, patient, absorbing presence of something that has outlasted every alternative and knows it.

It is not hostile. It is simply *complete*. It has been finished for a very long time, and it is still, quietly, in its muffled and turbulent and utterly dark way, doing its work — inhaling the noise of its own changes, exhaling heat, growing another layer for whoever comes after.
