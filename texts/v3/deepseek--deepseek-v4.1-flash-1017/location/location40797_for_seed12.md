The world is a high, drowned valley, a mountain basin that the sea climbed into and never left. Here, the water is not a thing you look at; it is the air you move through, a warm, mineral-thick atmosphere that holds you in its arms. It is a world of perpetual, luminous twilight, where the sun is a distant, hazy bloom of light far above, its rays fractured into shifting, god-like columns that never quite touch the valley floor. This is a place of emergence, a geology still in its infancy, raw and soft. The mountains that ring the basin are not ancient, weathered stone but pale, chalky structures that crumble at a touch, their slopes slick with a fine, constant silt. The whole landscape feels like a sculpture left out in a warm rain, its features softened, its final form not yet decided.

Through this submerged basin, the city of Aethelburg rises. It is not built on the ground, but woven into the water itself. The people, the Aethel, are a lithe and patient folk who have long since adapted to their liquid world. Their city is a dreamlike lattice of platforms and towers grown from a black, coral-like substance, harvested from the deeper vents and coaxed into shape with patient, manual craft. The architecture is organic and layered, a dense, chaotic accretion of dwellings, walkways, and communal halls that spiral upwards towards the light. There are no sharp corners, only curves and flowing lines that follow the paths of the warm currents. Bridges are not structures of stone but living vines, thick as a man's thigh, coaxed to span the gaps between districts. The city is a dense, high-saturation place, its every surface encrusted with life, its spaces filled with the constant, low thrum of activity.

The vegetation is lush and predatory. Great forests of kelp, their fronds broad as sails, sway in the slow currents, their roots gripping the crumbling mountainsides. The water is thick with drifting spores and luminous plankton that glow with a soft, internal light, providing the valley's primary illumination. The Aethel cultivate gardens of bioluminescent fungi, their caps pulsing with a slow, rhythmic light, and tend to the snail-like creatures that graze on the algal mats, their shells smelted for simple tools and ornaments. The air—for it is still air, just thickened and heavy—is thick with the scent of wet stone, salt, and the sweet, decaying perfume of the jungle.

Life here is rare and strange. The valley is not a place of great beasts but of small, secretive things. You might see a shoal of glass-winged fish that are nearly invisible until they catch the light, or a creature like a giant, slow-moving sloth, its fur a matted garden of moss and tiny crustaceans, that clings to the underside of the great kelp fronds. The most common sound is the low, resonant thrum of the water itself, the creak and groan of the coral structures, and the occasional, haunting call of a far-off creature that sounds like a whale's song played on a cracked wooden flute.

The rules of this world are simple and patient. Time moves with the tides, a slow, cyclical rhythm of rising and falling water levels that dictates all activity. Metal is scarce, a treasured material found only in the eroding mountainsides and worked with crude, hand-powered forges. The Aethel are a people of wood, bone, and woven kelp. Their tech is primitive, their lives a constant, careful negotiation with the warm, buoyant world that surrounds them. They have no concept of a world without water; the sky is a story, the dry land a myth. They live in a state of constant, low-level maintenance, their lives a slow dance with a world that is always, gently, trying to reclaim them.

The atmosphere is one of profound, dimly-lit peace. It is a quiet place, the sound dampened by the water, the light soft and diffused. The city of Aethelburg is a dense, slow-beating heart in the center of a world that is still being born, a place of strange beauty and quiet, patient life, forever on the cusp of becoming.
