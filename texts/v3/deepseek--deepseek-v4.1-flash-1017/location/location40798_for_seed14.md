The water is the first and final fact of this place. It is not a sea, for there is no surface, no shore, no sky above—only a cold, black immensity that extends in all directions. It is a world entirely submerged, a volume of water so vast it has its own geography. The pressure here is immense, not with the crushing weight of depth, but with the sheer, settled presence of absolute fullness. The cold is a deep, penetrating chill, a temperature that has not changed since the world's first long night. It seeps into everything, a constant, silent companion.

The landscape itself is a riddle of motion and stillness. The ground, far below on a scale one can barely perceive, is a plain of rounded stones and fine, grey silt, worn down by an eternity of gentle, inexorable currents. This is the most ancient of erosions—the end of a process so long finished that even the memory of the original rock is gone. Yet, above this placid floor, the world is in constant, chaotic motion. The entire water column is a turbulent medium. Not with the crashing violence of waves, which belong to a shallower world, but with slow, immense, and endlessly complex currents. These are not rivers; they are moving mountains of water, great gyres that twist and shear past one another, creating internal storms and silent, swirling tempests that can last for years. The water is thick with suspended matter—a perpetual, fine-grained "rain" of ancient minerals and organic detritus that drifts downward, never settling for long before being caught in another current.

This water is thick and saturated, not just with particles, but with life itself. It is a "snow" of the deep, a constant, gentle blizzard of microscopic organisms, tiny scavengers, and fragments of dead things. To move through it is to move through a living, breathing medium, a primordial soup that sustains a dense, scattered population. The life here is sparse in the sense of large, distinct forms, but the sheer number of tiny, individual presences makes the water feel packed, occupied. There is no room for emptiness. Fauna is rare in any singular sense; one might drift for hours without seeing a single large creature. But when one appears, it is a creature of this deep and ancient world: a slow, armored thing like a crab of polished obsidian, or a translucent, ribbon-like serpent that is felt as a disturbance in the current long before it is seen. These are not beasts of frantic energy, but of quiet, patient purpose.

There is no built environment. The only structures are the chance arrangements of stone, the occasional cluster of pale, fungoid growths that are not true vegetation but something stranger—a life that is almost mineral, a few sparse, pale, reach-like forms that cling to the rock. The "vegetation" is a rare and ghostly presence, the barest hint of something sessile and hungry, living not on light but on the chemical whisper of the deep. No tools, no craft, no hands have ever shaped this place.

The light is almost nothing. It is not the total darkness of a cave, but a profound, ancient gloom, a darkness that has weight. The only illumination comes from the inhabitants themselves. The slow, drifting "snow" is spangled with the cold, blue-green sparks of bioluminescence, flashes of light that are as silent as they are momentary. A passing creature might trail a faint, ghostly aura. These lights are the only events in a landscape of near-perfect silence. The sound is a deep, ubiquitous, and sub-audible hum—the sound of pressure, of immense water moving against itself, a sound felt in the bones more than heard. It is a silence so profound that the faint, distant crackle of a million tiny organisms and the low moan of a distant current are as startling as a shout.

The world is dynamic, but it is the dynamism of a slow, inevitable chaos. It is the "anthill" of the microscopic, the endless, turbulent dance of the currents and the drifting life. It is a world of constant, quiet, and primordial creation and destruction, where the only rule is the slow, grinding turn of the water and the deep, patient life it carries. It is a place that has been this way forever, and will be this way forever more, a final, settled, and turbulent state, hidden in the cold and the dark.
