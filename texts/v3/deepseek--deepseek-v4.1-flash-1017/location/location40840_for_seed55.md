There is no horizon here. There is only the deep.

The world is a drowned one, a vast and ancient basin filled to its brim and beyond with cold, silent water. The surface, if one could ever find it, is a myth, a ceiling of shifting, silver light that the inhabitants below will never see. This is a place of perpetual, liquid night, a darkness so complete it has texture, pressing in on all sides like a physical weight.

This is not a natural sea. It is the final form of something else, a continent that was once a place of stone and air, now settled into its ultimate, patient state of submersion. The sheer scale of it is difficult to comprehend; it is a world of water, a volume so immense it generates its own slow physics, its own weather. The water itself is cold, a deep, bone-aching chill that is constant and absolute. It moves with a glacial slowness, great currents that take years to complete a single circuit, and the only sound is the low, constant, sub-audible hum of this immense pressure.

The architecture of this drowned world is what tells its story. Everything is built. There is no natural seafloor, no silt or sand. Instead, the bottom is a sprawling, labyrinthine metropolis of ancient, unknown design. Structures rise from the abyssal plain, not as sharp ruins but as soft, rounded forms, their edges worn away by millennia of water and pressure. They are crafted from a stone that is not quite stone, a porous, coral-like material that seems to have grown rather than been built, creating a city of porous rock and fused shell.

These structures are everywhere, a dense, layered topography of bulbous domes, ribbed towers, and labyrinthine passageways that intertwine and grow through one another. They form canyons and arches, bridges and vast, empty plazas. Every surface is coated in a thick, soft, cold-water flora. Lush, yes, but not in any way we would recognize. This is a garden of darkness, a vegetation of pale, ghostly tendrils and fleshy, phosphorescent fungi that cling to the stone. Great, fan-like fronds of a translucent, jelly-like substance sway with the slow currents, and the entire city is shrouded in a forest of cold-water kelp, its broad, black leaves filtering the minimal light. The vegetation provides the only illumination: a faint, spectral glow of blues and sickly greens, a bioluminescence that casts long, wavering shadows and makes the city feel like a dream of its own.

There are no animals here. No fish hunt through the glowing kelp, no crabs scuttle across the porous stone. The only life is the vegetation and the people. And the people are many. They live in a state of dense, quiet saturation, a population that has settled into the very bones of this place. They are small, pallid, and slow-moving, their skin the color of the deep-sea fungi, their hands and feet slightly webbed. They are a primitive people, their technology a matter of hand-tools and patient craft. They use hooks of shaped bone to harvest the tough, rubbery kelp and the fleshy fungi from the structures, and they have learned to coax the bioluminescent life into brighter, more focused forms, using captive cultures in carved stone bowls to light their dwellings.

Their world is one of quiet, patient process. There is no drama here, no sudden change. The world is old, so old that it has settled into its final form. The erosion is not a process but a state; everything is deeply aged, smoothed and softened by an eternity of water. The people live in the spaces between the ancient, swollen structures, their lives governed by the slow, predictable cycles of the deep currents and the growth of the fungal crops. Their sounds are few: the soft scrape of a bone tool, the low murmur of a conversation held in the flickering light of a fungus bowl, the gentle thrum of the water against the walls of their world. Their rules are the rules of this place: conserve the light, tend the crops, respect the currents, and remember that the world above is a story and the world below is forever. They are the last inhabitants of a drowned continent, living a slow, quiet, and ancient life in the glowing, liquid dark.
