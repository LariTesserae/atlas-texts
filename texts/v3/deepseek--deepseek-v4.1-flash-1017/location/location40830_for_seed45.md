# The Scaffold of False Dawn

The sky here is not a sky. It is a ceiling of cracked mineral, a vault of layered stone suspended at an altitude where the air thins to almost nothing and the horizon bends upward at the edges, suggesting you stand within something rather than beneath it. Light does not fall from above. It seeps. Faint bioluminescent veins thread through the overhanging rock—not organisms, not quite, but crystalline structures that phosphoresce with a slow, irregular pulse, as though the stone itself is breathing. The color is the amber of a dying fire seen through closed eyelids. It is never enough to see by, only enough to know you are not blind.

The ground is a vastness of pale, vitreous stone—a salt-flat that never knew water, or a glass plain fused by some ancient heat. The surface is warm. Not hot enough to burn, but warm the way a stone wall is warm after a day of sun, except there is no sun, and the warmth comes from below. The crust is thin in places. Beneath it, something shifts.

The elevation is extreme. You feel it in the pressure behind your eyes, in the way sound behaves strangely—swallowed, then returned from distances that should not echo. The plain does not stretch flat. It tilts. It rises toward the edges of vision into ridges and shelves of raw, unweathered rock, jagged formations that look newly torn from something larger. Nothing here has been rounded by wind or water. The edges are sharp. The stone is unpolished. You are standing on a world that has not yet decided what it will be.

And it is still deciding.

The turbulence announces itself first as vibration. A low, sub-audible tremor that you feel in the soles of your feet and the hollows of your bones. Then the ground cracks—not dramatically, not with a roar, but with a sound like a knuckle popping, a sudden release of tension. A seam opens ten meters away. Warm vapor sighs out. The seam closes again. The stone seals itself. This happens constantly. The surface is a scab that will not stop healing and re-opening. The plain is alive with slow, geological fidgeting. New ridges push upward over hours, then subside. Fissures exhale sulfurous breath that smells of struck matches and hot metal. Occasionally, a shelf of the glassy crust will shear away and slide, revealing a cross-section of layered deposits—bands of coppery orange, deep vitreous green, a purple so dark it reads as black. Then the shelf will slowly rise again, re-fusing, the colors sealing back into the milky surface.

There are structures scattered across this plain. Not buildings in any recognizable sense—but arrangements. Pillars of the same pale stone, stacked in ways that seem intentional, though no tool marks show. They are crude. Some are little more than three stones balanced together, a geometry that should not stand but does. Others are taller, reaching four or five meters, leaning at improbable angles, joined by channels cut into the ground that might be drains or might be script. The pillars are warm to the touch. Some hum. They are not ruins. They are not finished. They exist in a state of having been started and abandoned, or started and not yet continued—but by whom, or what, there is no sign.

No animals move here. The air is too thin, the ground too unstable, the warmth too strange. No insects crawl across the vitreous stone. No birds wheel beneath the mineral sky. The only motion is geological: the slow creep of the crust, the sudden snap of a fissure, the drifting of dust that is not dust but fine particulates of abraded stone, settling in the channels around the pillars, accumulating in the seams.

The soundscape is mostly silence—but a silence that is not empty. It is the silence of held breath. The silence of something waiting. When the ground cracks, the sound is sudden and flat. When the pillars hum, the hum is felt more than heard, a resonance in the chest. When the vapor escapes, it hisses briefly and is gone. Between these events, there is nothing. No wind. No water. No life. The quiet is so complete that you become aware of your own pulse, your own breathing, the wet sound of your own eyes blinking.

Time here is not measured in days. There is no sun to rise or set. The amber glow in the ceiling neither brightens nor dims. But the turbulence has its own rhythm—a slow build of pressure, a cracking, a settling. You could call it breathing. The plain inhales, and new stone pushes upward. The plain exhales, and seams seal shut. It is not a cycle. It is a rhythm without pattern, a body that has not learned to be regular.

The warmth rises from below, and the air above is cold with altitude. The gradient is sharp. Your feet are warm; your face is chilled. You stand in a contradiction. The place does not care. It is not hostile. It is simply uninterested. It is not finished becoming itself. You are standing on the surface of something that is still being made, and you are not part of the process.

This is a landscape of raw emergence—a vast, high, dry plain of vitreous stone, cracked and re-sealing, scattered with crude pillar-arrangements that suggest intent but no clear meaning, lit by the pulsing veins of an overhanging mineral ceiling, warm from below, cold from above, silent except for the snap of the ground and the hum of warm stone, and above all, *unfinished*.
