# The Kettle

There is no ground here. There is the room, and under the room the making.

The room is small enough that you can stand in the middle and touch both walls with your elbows bent. It is called the Kettle by the marks cut into the doorpost, and it is the highest finished thing in the world — which is to say, the last thing anyone has finished, in a place where finishing is the rarest act there is.

## Position

You reach it from below, which takes a long time, or you do not reach it at all. The spires come up out of a cloud sea that never breaks — a white floor going away in every direction, boiling slowly, lit from beneath by something the colour of an ember in a fist. The spire the Kettle sits on is one of many. Others stand off in the white at every distance: thin dark columns, each with a small churn of green at the top, each with its own little room. Some of them are lit. Some have ropes strung to their neighbours, slack catenaries disappearing into the cloud, and something crosses them now and then, unhurried, hand over hand.

The Kettle's spire has no foot you can find. Descend for a day and the cloud is still below you and the rock is still going down, and it is warm, and it is not the same rock it was when you started.

## The Room

Everything in the Kettle is held. There is nothing here that was found. No stone was quarried, no beam was felled — the walls are woven, split cane and twisted fibre and folded leaves, and the weave has been planted, vines threaded through the warp and rooted into it, so that the wall is simultaneously basket and hedge and is tightening year by year. The floor is a lattice of lashed bundles that give under your heel and spring back wet. Between the bundles, in the gaps, you can see down: a red-orange glow, faint, the colour of a coal three rooms away, where the spire's newest courses are still curing and sweating.

No metal to speak of. One blade, dull as a spoon, kept in a gourd. A bone awl. Wedges. Hands, mostly. The knots are the local architecture — there are eleven of them and they do eleven different things, and a wall is a knot repeated until it is a wall.

It smells of hot clay, bruised leaf, and something sweetish, like sugar just before it burns.

## Water

It is wet the way a mouth is wet. The air does not so much humidify as condense; you can watch it happen on the underside of a leaf. The spire sweats — water comes out of the warm new rock in beads and runs down the inside of the walls in the channels that have been left for it, split reed and folded leaf, gutters that carry it to the troughs.

Every vessel is a gourd, and every gourd holds water at a different pitch. Tap one and it answers with a note. The room is tuned. Somebody tuned it, over a long time, and you can tell that the tuning is not finished, because one gourd at the end of the third ring is still wrong.

The channels run down and also, in three places, up. Do not follow the ones that run up past the third ring. They are carrying water to something that is still growing and it is not finished and it is not yours.

## Growth

Lush is too mild a word. The Kettle is *packed* — there is no bare surface anywhere in it. Troughs are stacked above troughs; gourds hang in tiers; the walls are green and the ceiling is green and the floor is green wherever a root has found the lattice. Things flower in the dark and are pollinated by hand, by a feather, by a fingertip. Things fruit and are eaten. Things are pruned to shapes that have no name in any language but the local one, and the local one has nine words for *trained* and none for *wild*.

The plants grow toward sound. Not light — they will lean away from a lamp to lean toward a dripping gutter, toward the hum of the weave, toward a voice. Speak in the Kettle and by morning a tendril has come around to find where you were standing.

## Light and Noise

Dim. Two lights, both poor. Green-gold comes through the weave in a haze, so that the far wall is a suggestion. Red-orange comes up through the floor in a fainter haze, so that the undersides of leaves are always faintly lit from below. Nothing in here has a shadow; everything has two half-shadows in different colours, and they cancel.

It is loud. This is the thing visitors do not expect of a room the size of a bed.

A hundred distinct waters: trickle, slap, gurgle, the tick of a drip finding a new path. The weave itself, singing — the whole structure is under tension and the fibres hum, a high continuous whine you stop hearing after an hour and then hear again, all at once, and it is like standing inside a plucked string.

And under that, the mountain. The spire is still being made, and the making has a sound: a low grinding churn, kiln-like, patient, punctuated now and then by a boom from somewhere far below when a new mass goes on. It is not a collapse. It is the opposite of a collapse. It is the sound of something being added, and it never stops, and after a few days you cannot sleep without it and after a few more you cannot sleep with it.

## The Rules

Nothing here is old. In a place this hot and this wet, decay should be furious, and it is — a leaf rots in a day, a lashing softens in three — but it is always outpaced. The rotted thing is replaced before you notice it went. There is no patina in the Kettle. There is no worn step. Everything is new and everything is maintained and the maintaining happens mostly when you are not looking.

The top is always the top. The spire rises; the room rides up with it; the summit is the growing edge of the world. Down is not a direction here so much as a date. If you climb down far enough you will not reach a base, you will reach an earlier spire, and it will be warm, and it will not yet have a room on it.

And the far end of the room is farther than it looks. Not always. Not predictably. But there is a trough at the eastern end that is four paces away in the morning and a morning's walk away in the afternoon, and if you go to it in the afternoon you will arrive having crossed something, and you will not be able to say what.

## Fauna

Rare, and mostly not here. A few soft pale things live in the troughs, blind, slow, tolerated because they eat what would otherwise need eating. The ropes between the spires carry their own traffic and it is not for you. Once, while you are watching the cloud sea, something long and pale crosses to the next spire, hand over hand, without hurry, and does not look down, and is gone.

You will find, some mornings, a fresh lashing on a beam you did not lash, and a trough replanted, and the dull blade put back in its gourd the wrong way round. That is all the company you get. Whoever keeps the Kettle is very good at it and does not care to be thanked.

## Last

Stand in the middle. Touch both walls. Listen to the water find its note and the weave hold its one high siren and the mountain grind away underneath like a mouth that is eating and also, somehow, building.

Then look at the gourd that is still wrong. It has been wrong since before you came. There is a feather beside it. You could tune it. The feather is there for that.

You will not manage it before dark, and dark does not come here, and the far trough is getting farther.
