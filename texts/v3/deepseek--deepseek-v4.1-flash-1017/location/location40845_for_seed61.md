The city is called Glisten-Reach, though no one there calls it that. Its true name is a resonance, a chord struck on wet stone that you feel in your teeth and the small bones of your wrist. It is a place of perpetual, dripping dusk.

You arrive by descending. There is no sky here, only a ceiling of fused, basalt-like rock, slick with condensation and studded with the fossilized remains of some impossibly ancient, non-plant life—great calcified fans and spirals that never knew sunlight. The air is cool, a constant, skin-clinging damp. It smells of wet mineral, of ozone, and of the sharp, clean scent of something akin to galvanized metal. The only light is a soft, murky glow from the city itself, a bioluminescence of a non-living kind.

Glisten-Reach is built into and of the living rock. It is not constructed so much as *coaxed* into being. Over millennia, the inhabitants have learned to weep a mineral-rich water from the stone and guide its flow. As the water evaporates in the still air, it leaves behind deposits that they shape with simple tools of hardened stone and shell. The result is a city of organic, flowing forms, a warren of slick, rounded towers and bulbous domiciles that seem to have grown from the cavern floor. Streets are not paved but are deep, water-worn channels, worn smooth by a million years of flow and foot traffic. Every surface is wet, and everything glistens. The city is a single, continuous, slowly-accumulating accretion, a structure that is in a state of perfect, unhurried equilibrium with its environment. It has always been this way. It will always be this way.

The people of Glisten-Reach are small, pale, and nimble, their skin devoid of pigment from generations in the dark. They are artisans of water and stone. Their technology is one of leverage, filtration, and exquisite patience. They do not mine the rock; they persuade it. They use a type of slow-acting acid, harvested from the digestive tracts of the rare, blind, isopod-like creatures that scavenge the deepest crevices, to etch away the stone, and they use the constant drip of water to polish their creations. A single new chamber might take a person's entire life to complete, and they consider this a reasonable timeline. They are born, they coax a room into being, they live in it, and they die in it, and their children continue the smoothing.

The sound is the city's most defining feature. It is never silent. The primary, ever-present sound is the water: a million different drips, a constant, overlapping percussion. It drips from the ceiling into gutters, it flows in rivulets down the walls, it cascades through carved channels that act as the city's plumbing and its music. But this is a din, not a melody. The true sound is the Resonance. Because of the enclosed space and the layered, hollow construction of the city, the sound of Glisten-Reach reflects and combines into a dense, low-frequency hum. It is the sound of a place that is a giant, imperfect musical instrument. The people have learned to navigate by this sound, to use its shifts and echoes to find their way. A changed note in the hum can mean a new crack in the roof, or a stranger from the deep ways. They communicate across districts by striking specific, quartz-veined pillars with resonant stones, sending complex messages through the stone itself. The air is alive with this silent, thrumming conversation.

The only movement, other than the people, is the water. The environment is static, a perfect, frozen equilibrium of stone and moisture. The people are the sole agents of change, and they enact it with a glacial pace. There are no seasons, no day or night. A "year" is a period of time measured by the slow, predictable rise and fall of a particular subterranean river that feeds the city, a cycle of perhaps forty of our years.

The rare fauna are a part of the city's machinery. The isopods are farmed in deep, wet pits, their chemistry harvested. Motes of luminescent, non-biological dust drift on the air currents, providing the faint, ambient glow. The people themselves, in their quiet, patient way, are the dominant life, their settlement a singular, enduring feature in a world of deep, damp, and silent stone. It is a place of profound, settled, and unending sameness, a city of water and patience, humming a chord to itself in the dark.
