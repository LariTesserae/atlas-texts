The sound arrives before the place does. It is not one sound but a pressure—a continuous roar that fills the skull and makes the teeth hum. There is wind in it, yes, but also something beneath the wind: a low grinding pulse that seems to come from the stone itself. You feel it in your sternum before you hear it. By the time you crest the final ridge, you have stopped hearing it at all; it has become the medium you move through, like water.

The plateau is small—a table of dark basalt perhaps three hundred meters across, lifted so high above the cloud sea that the air is thin and cold and tastes of iron. The edges drop away into white nothing. There is no water here. Not a puddle, not a rill, not a bead of dew. The stone is dry as bone, scoured by wind into flutes and grooves and polished hollows. Erosion has had its way with this place for a long time; the shapes are settled, patient, worn into their own state. Nothing here is raw. Everything has been worked by weather into something that looks almost intentional.

And yet the plateau is lush.

Not with grass or trees—nothing so ordinary. The vegetation grows in pockets, in basins where the wind slackens, in the lee of the larger rock formations. It is a kind of mat, low and dense, a weave of pale filaments that pulse with a slow internal light. It draws its moisture from the fog that rolls over the plateau in waves—thick curtains of it that condense on the filaments and are drunk in before a single drop can fall. The mats are soft underfoot, almost spongy, and they smell of ozone and wet chalk. Where they grow thickest, they climb the stone in spirals, following the grooves, making the plateau look veined.

Between the pockets, the stone is empty. Vast, clean, hollow space. You can walk for a minute and see nothing but dark rock and white sky. The emptiness is not barren—it is full of attention, somehow. The place feels like it is watching.

The fauna is everywhere. Small things, mostly—crustaceans that scuttle over the stone on too many legs, insect-like creatures that swarm in the fog, their wings beating so fast they are just a blur, a hum within the hum. There are larger things too, though you rarely see them whole. A shape moving at the edge of vision. A sound that is not the wind. Once, a shadow passed over the plateau—something vast and slow, gliding beneath the cloud sea. You did not look up. You are not sure you wanted to.

The built structures are scattered—a handful of small pods, half-sunk into the stone, their surfaces smooth and matte and impossibly clean. They are not ruins. They are not abandoned. They are simply there, like they grew from the rock. One of them is open. Inside, the walls are warm to the touch and the air is still—the roar of the plateau does not reach in here. There are marks on the walls, faint lines that might be writing. You do not recognize the language. You are not sure it is a language.

The technology here is old and quiet. It does not announce itself. It hums in the stone, in the filaments, in the fog. It keeps the air breathable and the mats alive. It might be doing other things too. You cannot tell. The pods are not shelters—they are instruments. You are not sure what they measure.

The light is normal—daylight, pale and cold, filtered through the fog. It does not change. The sun is a white smear behind the clouds. Shadows are soft and slow. The plateau is always in twilight, even at noon.

The dynamic is the storm. The fog rolls in and out in waves, the wind gusts and screams and then drops to a whisper, the mats pulse faster and slower. The stone itself seems to shift—not visibly, but you can feel it in your feet, a tremor that comes and goes. The place is not stable. It is not falling apart. It is in a state of constant, restless becoming. Solaris-like. An anthill. A bazaar of weather and light and small crawling things.

The rules here: do not drink the fog. Do not touch the pulsing filaments with bare skin. Do not follow the shadows beneath the cloud sea. Do not enter the pods unless invited. Do not assume the sound is only sound.

You stand at the center of the plateau. The roar fills you. The mats pulse at your feet. Somewhere, a pod opens. You do not move. You are not sure you can.
