# The Standing Plain

You come up from below, and the water changes before the ground does.

For a long time there is nothing but cold and the sound of your own passage — the deep is quiet the way a held breath is quiet, and the pressure lies along your skin like a second hide. Then the current finds you. It comes in from the west, slow and enormous, a river the width of a country, and it carries a taste of shell-grit and something faintly sour, the smell of many small mouths. This is how you know the plateau is near. The water has been *used*.

Then the floor arrives, and it is not rock.

## The Plain

The summit is a broad, worn country, nearly flat, rising a little toward its center and falling away at every edge into blackness. It is the oldest shape in the world, or near it — something that was a mountain when the water was young and has been sanded down for so long that it has forgotten slope. There are no peaks, no ridges, no gullies. Just a vast dim plain extending past what you can see, its far edge dissolving into the blue-black, and the long slow tilt of it toward the drop on every side.

Underfoot, nothing is stone. The ground is a pavement of broken shell and tube and plate, ground small and packed hard, and where it's scoured clean by current you can see it goes down and down in layers, each one somebody's former city, compressed to a fine pale laminate. Dig anywhere and you are digging through architecture. There is no natural substrate. There never was. Everything here has been made by something with a body, and everything has been unmade, and the flatness is the sum of that arithmetic over an unfathomable span of time.

The temperature is a few degrees above freezing and does not vary. There is no season. There has been no season for a very long time.

## The Built

The plateau is covered — not crowded, but *covered*, which is different — with structures.

They are grown. Nothing here was assembled; everything was secreted, extruded, budded, calcified, cemented, laid down cell by cell by animals that have no hands and no tools and no word for what they are doing. There are towers of stacked whorls twenty times your height, pale and ribbed and slightly translucent, each one a single colony's slow decision repeated for a thousand years. There are arches. There are lattices so fine the current plays them like a harp, giving off a low continuous hum you feel in the sternum more than hear. There are long low galleries like the casts of worms, tunneled through the accumulated shell-work of earlier generations, some of them still occupied, some of them long empty and slowly being reabsorbed at the edges by newer builders who cement their foundations directly onto the ruin of the old.

And there are chambers everywhere — closed, sealed, blind chambers with no door, built around nothing, or built around something that died inside and was never let out. They are the most common structure on the plain. Nobody knows, down here, what they are for.

Everything leans. That is the first rule of the place. The current comes from the west and never stops, and so every structure on the plateau is built on the eastern, lee side of whatever stood before it, and everything that stands before it grows a thicker wall on its western face, and the whole architecture of the plain is a single enormous comb turned downstream, generation after generation shouldering in behind the last. To walk west is to walk into the wind. To walk east is to walk through a city that is all back.

## The Water

The current is the weather.

It is not fast, most of the time. But it does not rest, and every so often — you cannot predict it, nothing can — it stiffens, and something breaks. A tower a thousand years old goes over with a sound like a bell dropped in a cathedral, and the pieces tumble, and within a day the fragments are already crusted with new builders, and within a year there is a new tower, leaning, combed downstream, its western wall thickening. This happens constantly, everywhere, in a slow continuous roar of creation and collapse that never resolves. The plain does not change shape. The plain will never change shape again. But the surface of it is a storm that has been going on so long it has settled into a rhythm, and the rhythm is chaos.

Above, far above, there is light — a thin cold grey that is not enough to grow anything by, only enough to see by, only enough to tell you which way is up. Nothing here photosynthesizes. Nothing here is green. Life on this plain is built entirely out of what falls from above and what eats what falls.

## The Inhabitants

They are abundant and they are far apart, and both are true at once.

The plain is scattered with presences. A grazer the size of a hand, armored in overlapping plates, moves in slow herds of four or five, shaving the surfaces of the structures down for the mineral in them — you can track a herd by the fresh pale scars it leaves, and by the way it will not cross its own old paths. Something larger, low and soft and boneless, drifts just above the shell-pavement with a fringe of tentacles trailing, sifting. Things live *inside* the walls and announce themselves only as a tapping, a rhythm from within the calcium, going quiet when you approach. In the chambers with no doors, sometimes, there is a faint glow — a dim amber pulse, very slow, that some animal has put there for reasons of its own.

The sound of the place is mostly silence, but silence with a floor to it: the current's hum through the lattices, the far-off bell-crash of a tower going over, and now and then a long descending tone from somewhere out past the edge of sight — something big, something that has a voice, that you will probably never see.

## The Rules

Nothing here is passive. Every surface you touch was made by a mouth. Nothing is stable except the flatness, and even that is only the average of the storm.

If you build, you must build on the lee side of what already stands. If you stand in the current, you will be thickened on your west face and thinned on your east and in time you will lean, and in time you will fall, and the pieces of you will be somebody's foundation. Nothing is wasted and nothing is buried; the dead are re-used as ground. The chambers with no doors are not opened. The herds do not cross their own paths. And the current, which is the weather, which is the only law that has ever mattered here, will go on coming from the west.

It has been like this for an age, and it is very busy, and it does not need you.
