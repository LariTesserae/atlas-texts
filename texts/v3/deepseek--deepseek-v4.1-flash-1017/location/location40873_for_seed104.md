# The Level

There is no surface. That is the first thing the place teaches, and it teaches it slowly, by refusing to end. You swim up and the light thickens rather than brightens, and the pressure loosens rather than releases, and after a while you understand that "up" is a direction with a long habit of disappointing. The water goes on. It is warm — body-warm, blood-warm, a temperature that makes it impossible to tell where you stop and it starts. It is not deep in the way trenches are deep. It is deep in the way a held breath is deep.

And below you, coming up out of that warm nothing, is the country.

---

**The plate.** It is a highland the size of a continent, and it is nearly flat. Not flat like a plain — flat like something that has been argued with for a very long time and has finally conceded. The relief is measured in hand-spans. You can swim for a day and rise or fall the height of a person. Somewhere under the sediment is the ghost of a mountain range; you can feel it in the way the water moves over the ground, the way currents split and rejoin around absences, the way the whole plate hums when something heavy passes far away. The mountains are gone. What remains is their *intention* — a broad, patient uplift, worn down to a shape that has stopped changing, a shape that has become a kind of final answer. Nothing here is being built. Nothing is being torn down. The land achieved its form long before you were a possibility, and it wears that achievement with the terrible calm of the finished.

It is warm all the way down to the stone. The stone itself is warm — you can press a palm to an outcrop and feel it, fainter than a pulse, the residual heat of a world that never quite cooled. It is dark rock, veined with rust and old iron, and over it a crust of living things so thick and so old that the crust has its own topography. The rock shows through only where something has scraped it, and the scrapes are all old.

**The water.** Warm, and dense enough to slow you, and holding a fine suspension of something — not silt exactly, not plankton exactly, a soft warm murk that turns every distance into a rumor. There is light, but it is dim and it is amber. The sun is a fact you deduce rather than see: a diffuse gold ceiling somewhere above, a direction that is brighter, an implication. Colors here run to rust and olive and a deep bruised green. Nothing is blue. Blue would require a clarity this water has decided against.

The murk makes the scale lie. You think a ridge is close and it is a hundred meters. You think a shape is far and it is beside you. In the Level you learn to mistrust your eyes before your instincts, and then to mistrust your instincts too.

**The growth.** Lush is not the right word but it is the available one. The plate is mantled — not with coral in the tropical sense but with something more like a meadow that has forgotten it is underwater. Soft fans, low cushions, bladders and bells. Forests of a tall grass-like thing that grows in dense stands, waist-high to a swimmer, bending all together in slow traveling waves when a current crosses, so that the whole country appears to be breathing on a long cycle. Between the stands, mats of a flat fleshy leaf that lie against the rock and propagate by spreading outward in rings, each ring a decade, and some of the rings are fifty meters across.

Everything here grows slowly and lives long and is very patient about both. Nothing blooms on a schedule you could detect. Reproduction is a rumor, happening elsewhere, at intervals. But it is dense — near-touching, layered, saturated. Every surface is occupied. Every gap is being negotiated. The lushness is not exuberance; it is a very old, very crowded arrangement in which no one has room to be extravagant.

**The marks.** Scattered across the plate, so rarely that you might swim a week between them, are things that were made. Not built — *made*, in the way a bird makes a nest or a body makes a shell. Low walls, no higher than a knee, running in curves that close on themselves for no reason you can find. Terraces cut into the soft rise of the plateau, four or five steps, each one worn to a rounded lip. Holes. Many holes — drilled in the rock in rows and spirals, sometimes hundreds in a cluster, always the same diameter, always smooth inside, and warm. Some of the walls have sockets in them, and the sockets are empty, and the emptiness has the specific quality of something removed politely rather than looted.

There is nothing here. No tools, no fragments of tools, no bone shaped into anything, no metal, no glass. Whatever made the walls and the holes and the terraces did it without leaving a single object behind — or made its objects out of something that has fully returned. The marks are the only evidence, and the marks say: *someone was here long enough to get bored of geometry, and then stopped.*

**The quiet.** It is not silent. It is *quiet* — a sound field so low and so even that it registers as a property of your own body. A pressure. A long, low, sub-audible swell that you feel behind the sternum. Sometimes it resolves, very faintly, into something like a note held by a very large instrument in another room. Then it's gone and you're not sure it was there. The grass moving makes no sound. Nothing here makes a sound you could record. But the quiet has a *texture*, and the texture changes, and after enough time in the Level you learn to read it the way you'd read weather — a thickening that means something large is moving two kilometers off, a thinning that means the currents have shifted and will shift back.

**The rare things.** Fauna here is a rumor with a body. You will see, perhaps, three animals in a long stay. They are large, and slow, and they do not flee, and they do not approach, and they have the air of creatures who have outlived the need for either. One is a kind of drifting bell, four meters across, trailing a fringe of fine tendrils, moving through the grass forest at the speed of a slow thought, and it turns to follow you with a surface that is not an eye but functions like attention. One is a long low thing you mistake at first for a ridge of rock and then, over the course of an hour, watch breathe.

They are not the point. The point is the space they leave. The Level is not empty of animals; it is a place where animals have agreed to be scarce, and the scarcity is *load-bearing* — you feel it as a kind of social pressure, a sense that you are in a room where everyone has already decided how loud to be.

**The breakdown.** And here is where the Level stops cooperating with description.

The plate is flat, and the flatness is contagious. Stay too long in one place and you will notice that your own thoughts have begun to lie down. Not fade — *flatten*. Ambition goes first, then urgency, then the sense that things should be otherwise. You will find yourself content to hold a position for hours. You will find the difference between deciding and not deciding becoming academic. This is not euphoria and it is not despair; it is the same final-form the land achieved, reaching out and settling you into it. Swimmers who stay too long are said to become part of the meadow. There are cushions out there, in the rings, that are the wrong shape.

The water remembers. Not metaphorically. Move along the plate and you will cross patches where the water is doing something it did before — a current that runs the same loop every ninety seconds, a warm pulse on a seven-minute cycle, a place where sound arrives before it is made. In one hollow, a hundred meters wide, the murk arranges itself into a standing pattern like a slow whirlpool that never winds down, and inside it the light is a slightly different amber, and things dropped there do not sink at the rate they should. The plate is a finished thing, and finished things have opinions.

And the mountains. Sometimes, in the deep quiet, the whole plate will *ring* — a single enormous low tone, felt not heard, the way a struck bell is felt — and for a moment the flatness is a lie and you can feel the range that used to be here, the height, the cold, the sharp ridgeline of a world that had not yet conceded. It lasts a breath. Then the land settles back into its final answer, and the water resumes its long warm patience, and you go on swimming across a country the size of a continent, at the height of a mountain that no longer exists, under a surface that will not arrive.
