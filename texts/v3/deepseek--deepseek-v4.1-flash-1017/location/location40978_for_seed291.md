The place is called the Sump, though no one who lives there calls it that with any affection. It is a city built into the slow, wet collapse of a river delta, where the river itself has forgotten its name and now spreads out in a hundred shallow, braided channels through a forest of black alder and drowned machinery. The whole region feels like a single, vast organism mid-breath—a damp exhalation that never quite finishes.

**Terrain and Materials**
The ground is not solid. It is a mat of roots, mud, and the compressed remains of centuries of industry, a spongy crust that trembles underfoot. Everywhere, water seeps. It isn't a river or a lake but a pervasive dampness, a constant condensation that beads on every surface and gathers in the countless depressions between the tangled roots. The air itself is wet, tasting of iron and peat and something faintly electrical, like the air before a storm that never breaks.

The city is built on this unstable skin. Structures are not founded; they are anchored. Buildings are lashed to the massive, buttressed trunks of the alder trees, or they are stilted platforms driven deep into the muck, leaning against one another for support. The architecture is a layered, chaotic accretion of materials: rusted corrugated iron, slabs of concrete salvaged from some older ruin, panels of synthetic polymer, and always, wood—living wood, woven branches, great beams of alder that groan and shift with the ground. The overall impression is of a settlement that is less constructed than *grown*, a metallic and wooden reef clinging to a living, shifting sea.

**Atmosphere and Sounds**
The air is cold, a wet, penetrating chill that settles into the bones. It is never truly day or night here, but a perpetual, grey-green twilight filtered through the dense, dripping canopy and the ever-present mist that rolls off the channels. The light is flat and shadowless.
It is quiet here, but not peacefully so. The quiet is a held breath. It is the quiet of a thousand small, dripping sounds—water falling from leaf to leaf, from pipe to puddle, a constant, arrhythmic percussion. Underneath it is the low, almost sub-audible hum of the city's industrial heart and the deep, resonant creak of wood and metal under stress. Occasionally, a sharp crack will ring out across the water, the sound of a support beam giving way somewhere in the maze, followed by a distant, muffled splash. It is the sound of a place slowly, patiently dismantling itself.

**Ecology and the Weird**
The vegetation is not merely lush; it is aggressive. The black alders are the undisputed masters, their roots forming the very ground, their branches a dense, interlocking ceiling. But between them, in every available space, grows a riot of strange flora. Pale, phosphorescent fungi bloom in the damp hollows, throwing a sickly green light onto the undersides of the platforms. Vines with leaves like wet leather crawl over everything, their tendrils seeking out warm pipes and engine exhausts. There are floating mats of vegetation in the slower channels, dense with a kind of bladderwort that is said to hum faintly when it feeds.

The fauna is abundant and adapted. Blind, pale crabs scuttle in the mud beneath the boardwalks. Insects are a constant presence—not just mosquitoes, but great, sluggish moths with wings like oily rags, and beetles with carapaces of a dull, metallic sheen that feed on the rust. The most notable creatures are the "mud-larks," amphibious, long-limbed scavengers that are a cross between a heron and a salamander, which nest in the discarded machinery and whose eerie, whistling calls are often the only sign that anything is alive in the deep, foggy channels. The strangest thing is the water itself. In certain pools, the water is warm to the touch and shimmers with a faint, internal luminescence, a phenomenon blamed on the "seep," a chemical runoff from the old factories that is said to have its own peculiar, semi-liquid life.

**Rules of the World**
The primary rule is impermanence. Nothing is fixed. A building that was stable last week may be listing today. A channel that was navigable yesterday may be silted up tomorrow. The inhabitants have adapted, valuing mobility and salvage over permanence. They are a people of narrow boats and rope bridges, of quick repairs and deep knowledge of the shifting geography.

The city is defined by a constant, low-grade industrial hum. The old factories are not dead; they are dormant, cannibalized, repurposed. Great water-wheels, driven by the current, turn slowly in the deeper channels, powering winches and pumps. The air is punctuated by the hiss of steam vents and the rhythmic, metallic thud of a stamping mill that no one remembers how to turn off. The settlement is saturated with a sense of frantic, chaotic energy—a bazaar of human and mechanical activity—but it is all conducted in a hushed, careful manner, as if a loud noise might bring the whole world down. There are constant, small emergencies: a breach in a hull, a snapped cable, a fire that must be smothered before it can take hold in the damp. The town functions as a single, nervous system, responding to crises with a shared, unspoken urgency.

The greatest mystery, the core of the place's weirdness, is the "coal-spirit." It is a local belief, half-superstition, half-observation, that the concentrated industrial runoff and the damp, decaying organic matter have birthed a kind of elemental consciousness in the deepest sumps. It is not malevolent, but it is immense and slow. It expresses itself in the sudden, inexplicable changes in the city's structure—a new path opening where none existed, a building collapsing without warning, the water in a channel becoming suddenly warm and luminous. The people of the Sump have learned to read its moods. They know that when the water-levels begin to rise without rain, the spirit is stirring, and it is time to move their homes. They build around it, not against it, their entire existence a frantic, temporary negotiation with a power that is always present, always shifting, and always watching from the depths of the damp, green, breathing dark.
