# The Verdigris Vents

## The Shape of the Land

A rift valley without end, or so it seems, cut into a high cold plateau that runs toward no visible horizon. The walls of the rift rise sheer and pale—glacier-scoured granite with fresh fracture faces, sharp as the day they split, because they are: the rift is young, still tearing itself open a few centimeters a year. The valley floor lies maybe two hundred meters down, and it is the only warm thing for a thousand kilometers in any direction. You feel that warmth on your face before you see its source.

The scale defeats the eye. The rift runs long enough that the far end dissolves into a standing haze of its own making—a perpetual weather system that the valley generates and keeps, like a held breath. Clouds form along the rim and never quite leave. Rain falls somewhere down the valley at all times; you can watch curtains of it walking the floor kilometers away, silver-gray, while your own boots stay dry on an outcrop of frozen scree. The valley has its own climate. It does not consult the plateau.

## The Warm Engine

The warmth comes up through the floor. The rift sits over an active spreading zone—a crustal suture still open, still hot—and heat pours out of it in vents and seeps and broad shallow steaming pools. Everywhere the cold rock meets the rising heat, water condenses, runs, gathers, drips. The air is heavy with it: not raining on you, not quite, but beaded on every surface, running in threads down the rift walls, hanging as visible vapor that the light turns into something you could almost climb.

And here is the thing the eye cannot reconcile: the floor of this cold high rift is green. Not moss-green, not lichen-green—*green* green, saturated, glossy, a jungle packed into a slot canyon. The vegetation roots in warm gravel and drinks from condensation and thermal runoff, and it does not stop. It fills every square meter it can reach, and where it cannot reach it hangs—vines descending the walls in ropes, mats of creeping growth on every wet ledge, canopies pressing against canopies until the valley floor is a single continuous surface of overlapping leaves and moving stems. The density is the overwhelming fact of the place. There is no negative space. Your eye finds nowhere to rest, no gap, no sky at floor level—only the packed architecture of plants growing on plants growing on the fossil bones of older plants, a hundred generations deep, saturated.

## What Lives Here

Nothing about the fauna is modest. The warm air is loud with it—a constant layered roar of insects and frogs and the low choral groaning of things you never quite see, a sound that never stops and that you stop hearing after an hour, and then notice again, and are startled by. The canopy moves constantly, and not from wind. The valley holds more animals per hectare than seems geometrically possible; they are stacked through the layers, ground-dwellers and climbers and things that live in the wet air between leaf and leaf. Every pool of thermal water is occupied. Every warm rock is occupied. When you walk, you displace a volume of life at every step.

They are strange, many of them—the rift's isolation and its heat have been at work a long time on the local lineages. Things with too many legs, things that glow faintly in the understory shade, things that seem to be part plant, rooted and mobile by turns. But nothing here hunts you. Nothing here has reason to. The density is its own defense; the valley is so full that you are simply one more warm surface in a place already crowded with warm surfaces, and you are ignored.

## The Light and the Air

The light is relentless. Even under the perpetual cloud, even in the rain, the sky is bright—a white glare off the snow-rimmed plateau, a diffused radiance that fills the rift and turns every wet leaf into a mirror. At the hour when the sun clears the rim, the whole valley lights up like something being turned on: every droplet, every glossy surface, the standing mist itself. You squint constantly. There is no shade worth the name; the shade is bright too.

## The Industrial Question

You will notice, walking the floor, that the valley sounds like a factory. The comparison is exact and unsettling. The vent fields hiss and roar, steam under pressure shrieking through fractures in rhythmic pulses. The great shallow pools boil and thump. Somewhere up the rift, a massive vent field produces a low, measured, chugging roar that carries for kilometers—the rhythm is so regular you will catch yourself looking for the machinery. There is no machinery. The valley built none and needs none; the thermal engine runs on its own geology, and the sound is the sound of the planet's own scaled production, endless and mindless. The longer you listen, the more you understand: this *is* industry, complete and self-operating, rock and water and heat in place of pistons. It has been running since before there were ears to hear it.

## The Cold Above

Climb fifty meters up the rift wall and the world flips. The green stops dead. The warmth stops dead. You are on the plateau again, in thin cold air under a washed-out sky, and below you the rift is a steam-filled slot of impossible green running away toward its own private weather. The contrast is the strangest thing in the landscape: a warm jungle in a deep wound, packed and roaring and bright, held inside a frozen silence that does not notice it at all. The transition is a meter wide. You can put one hand in the wet green heat and one hand in the dry cold of the plateau and hold both at once.
