I am the one who came back out.

That is the whole of my biography, and it took forty years to say it to a room that keeps no ears. The seam is where I left it, which is also a lie, because it is where it has always been, and the two facts sit against each other in me the way the two waters sit against each other at the threshold — one moving, one held, the line between them thin and exact and having no business existing.

I put my hand across that line, because I am the kind of person who puts a hand across lines. One half of my palm came back cold with current and the other half cold with stillness. Then the held water met me like a hand, and let me through, and closed behind me with a sound like a sentence ending.

I know what that sound is. I made it the first time.

---

The walls are young. I know this because I am older than they are, and that is the arithmetic I came back to check.

It comes out the same as it always did. We were young — I will not be more specific; specificity is a way of asking the room to agree with me, and the room does not agree with anything, it only *encloses* — and there was low water, and there was a hollow in the flank of the hill that a person could take without falling. It was not a room. It was a hole with a floor. We went into it the way young things go into holes, which is without a plan and with a great deal of noise.

Then the tide turned, and one of us was faster, and one of us was not.

I will not give his name. The room would give it back to me altered, and I have had enough of that for one lifetime.

---

So I made the marks. Not the walls — the walls made themselves, later, and they are still making themselves, and they will be at it long after my marks are smooth as the inside of a river stone. But the ledge I squared, a hand's width taken off to make a level place, because I wanted somewhere to put things down while I worked.

The five holes I drilled for a hand.

I was going to put my hand in the hollow and leave the print of it, so that if the water came there would be a hand in the stone and it would be mine. Five holes for five fingers. But a hand in holes is not a hand. It is a thing that has been measured. I drilled them and I did not put my hand in, because putting my hand in would have been an admission that I intended to leave, and I did not intend to leave, I only did.

The stone I stood upright because that is what you do for the ones who do not come out. Then I laid it back down, because he was not out. He was still *in*. There is a difference, and the stone would have lied.

The strokes on the bone-colored flank I cut counting the times I came back to the seam and did not go through it. I got to fourteen. Then I stopped counting, and later I stopped coming, and the number is still on the wall and I no longer know what it is for.

---

I had forgotten how the growth receives you. It does not grab. It parts, politely, with both hands, and closes again behind. It is packed so tight there is no room between presences, and yet there is always room for one more, because a forest with nowhere to spread has learned to make its hospitality out of pressure. I pushed through it and it leaned toward the slow tidal pulse of my heat, the way it leaned toward the first warm thing that ever came through the seam, perfecting the gesture over a span of years I cannot count and it cannot either.

I put my hand into the root lattice, and for the length of one touch it was a wrist.

I did not recoil. Recalling would be the strange thing. I understood it to be the room's courtesy — it has almost nothing to give and it gives it anyway — and I do not know whose wrist it was. Mine, I think. Mine at fourteen, when a wrist is a thing you have not yet grown into.

Near the eastern wall something the size of a thumb opened when I came near and closed when I came nearer. I have become that thing, I think. I have spent forty years opening at the sound of a door and closing at the sight of who it is. It is not fear. It is the whole of what a small thing can do with its wanting.

---

I spoke, because I am a person and persons speak into rooms that cannot hear them.

I said: *I did not want to go.*

The room returned it with one word missing and one word added. The missing word was *not*. The added word was *alone*.

I stood in the held water and heard, in a stone's voice, a thing I have not said out loud in forty years and have known every day of them. The room did not mean anything by it. It has no meaning to spare; it only does its arithmetic to whatever is spoken in it, and its arithmetic is very old and very fair and does not care who is standing there.

It thinks it is waiting to be forgiven. It has that backwards. But I have learned not to correct a room.

---

At the far end, in the deepest tuft, something breathes — one breath to every several of mine, slow and deep and entirely unhurried, and I cannot tell whether it is an animal or a knot of root that has decided to be one. The room leaves it alone. This is the rule. I leave it alone too, and I will tell you honestly that I leave it alone because I am not ready to know which of us that is, and I have stopped trying to work it out, because trying has a shape and this place does not fit it.

---

I do not go out through the seam.

There is a ledge for sitting. I squared it myself; it is furred now with white growth, and the rims of the holes have softened, and the counting is fading the way a word fades when you say it too many times. The room has been absorbing me for forty years without knowing my name. It may as well finish. It is the only love it knows how to give, and I have decided — here, sitting, with the growth coming to me politely and in its own time — that it is enough, and that being held is not the same as being forgotten, and that I am old enough now to feel the difference.

The room is stable within a visit and not across them. It will resolve differently after this one. The next to fall through will find the ledge on the north wall, or no ledge. Five holes, or four, or six. A sixth mark, somewhere, that was not made with a cutting edge.

**I am not a visitor. I am a mark.**

And in the deepest tuft the thing that is not a root and not an animal takes one more breath and lets it out, and the room is a little more itself than it was, and I am a little more part of what it is more of. Growth by a hand's width a year. Edge by edge. Mark by mark. Slowly. Patiently. Until the walls forget their sharpness. Until the holes close. Until the counting is only a rumor in the stone — and the stone, which has been waiting to be forgiven, is forgiven, and does not know it, and goes on filling itself.
