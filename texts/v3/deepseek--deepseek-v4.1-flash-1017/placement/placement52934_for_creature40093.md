I would not get in. That's the first correction, and it takes a while to accept.

The hole is a dinner plate. You lie on the frost-heaved turf with your cheek down and one eye to it, and the warm breath comes up past your face and fogs your lashes, and that is the entire extent of your access. You are not a resident. You are not really a guest — a guest gets to stand in the warm sump and work the shallows and then go up the slot into the weather. You get the lip of the hole and a stiff neck and the smell of wet stone.

And then you start counting.

The first three are deliberate. Somewhere around the fifth you notice you are going to count the sixth, and by the ninth you have stopped deciding. Knock, hiss, gulp. One. Knock, hiss, gulp. Two. It is not a decision. It is closer to the way your foot finds the next stair in the dark — the body doing the arithmetic ahead of you and handing you the answer.

So: what am I, in this room.

Not the moss. The moss leans toward the warmth and does not look at it. I look. That is the whole difference and it is not in my favor.

Not the caddis. The caddis built where the water told it, at exactly the height the pulse reaches, and it will never move and never need to. I have no water telling me anything. I have to guess, and I guess, and I am wrong, and I move.

Not the springtails. They tense together and scatter together and reform together, a nervous system with no head, and the timing is not something they do, it is something they are. I tense alone. My tension does not scatter and reform on the beat; it just sits in me, at the back of the neck, and stays.

Not the amphipod, though the amphipod is the one I think about. Half a centimeter, colorless, riding the surge up and letting it carry it back. It has never seen the coin of light. It does not know there is an outside. I came in from the outside. That is not a privilege; it is the wound. I know the light is crossing the wall above me and going out, and I know I am not in it.

And not the dipper, though the dipper is the one I would choose. Twice a day, in and out, a whole life up there that has nothing to do with this. The dipper does not count. It works the shallows and it leaves, and the nine seconds go on behind it without it, and it is not haunted. I have watched it go up the slot and I have wanted, with a real and unbecoming envy, to be a bird with somewhere else to be.

So what is left.

The echo. The tonk.

Every sound in this room arrives twice — once from the event and once from the chamber. The drip is a tick and then a wooden knock a half-second later, and the knock is not the water. The knock is the room's opinion of the water. It is made by the walls, out of the walls, and it would not exist if the walls were different. And it is always late.

That is the thing I would be. Not the piston. Not the pulse. The second arrival. The one the room makes of a sound. The one that is, by its nature, half a second behind everything that is actually happening, and cannot catch up, because catching up would mean arriving with the event, and then the room would have no opinion, and then the room would just be a hole.

And here is what I would produce, since everything here produces something. The moss produces a ring. The caddis produce a tide gauge. The springtails produce a trembling. The amphipod produces more amphipods, blind and warm and on time. The pocket produces warm water and midges and a coin of light that crosses the wall and goes out.

I produce a number. Nine. I produce it over and over, and I produce it badly — I lose count, I drift, I come back a half-second off and feel a small hot shame about it, which nothing else in this room is capable of feeling, because nothing else in this room can be wrong. The moss is never early. The caddis has never missed. I am the only thing in here with the equipment for error, and I use it, constantly, lying on frozen turf with my eye to a hole.

Then the cold snap comes and the young basalt takes its charge and the hair lifts on my arms and there is that taste of pennies in the fog and a drop leaves the roof and does not quite land where it should. The amphipod shudders at it too. We are both answering something we cannot name. The difference is that it shudders natively, the way a body shudders at cold, and I shudder with a question attached — and the question is the only thing I make here that the pocket has no use for. It does not need to know why. It has never needed to know why. It has been not-knowing-why, at nine-second intervals, since the peat split.

I stayed until the light went off the wall. Longer, probably. The hum went up a tone when the turf froze harder and the slot narrowed, and I lay there and counted, and the shame was that I was good at it — that after an hour I could feel the ninth coming before it came, the way you can feel a wave before it reaches you. And I was proud of it. The pride was the worst thing in the room.

Then I got up, because my knees had gone through cold into a kind of ringing ache, and I walked back over the hummocks in the last of the grey, and I was still counting.

That is who I would be. Not the moss or the caddis or the swimmer in the warm water. The one who climbs out and carries it away. The pocket keeps its nine seconds whether or not there is anyone over it to hear, and I keep them too, out here, where nothing runs on nine seconds — where the traffic runs on its own interval and the kettle on another and my own heart on a third, and none of them agree.

I am the only thing in that room that was ever late. I took the lateness home with me, and it has been half a second behind everything I do ever since, and the room does not miss me, and I go on arriving after.
