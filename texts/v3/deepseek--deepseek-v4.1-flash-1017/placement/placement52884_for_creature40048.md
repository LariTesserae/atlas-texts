I would come in through the seam, because there is no other way, and I would have to turn sideways to do it, and the warm breath of the place would come over my face before I committed — and that is the last moment I would be a visitor. After the committing, I would be weather.

That is the first thing I understand, standing in the low crown of the room with my shoulders hunched and my own heat pouring off me into air that is already forty degrees too hot to accept it. I am not a resident. I am a front moving through a small climate. I raise the mist. It rolls up and away from me in slow lobes and closes behind me. The droplets hanging from the ceiling, each one swelling toward its single struck note, begin to fall out of their long patience and come down early, and the pitch of the room changes — not much, a quarter tone, the way a room changes when someone enters it. I am the reason. I stand still and try to be the reason for nothing, and the water still finds me.

For a while I think I might be one of the seven. I crouch by the wall and watch one of them — a palm of wet pale tissue, no eyes, no head, a shine on an untouched surface and a dark erased-pencil line behind it — and I try to feel the kinship. We are both soft. We are both mostly water. We are both, in our way, spending a gradient. But the verbs do not fit. It clings; I cannot cling, I have no sole that lays itself flat. It creeps; I arrive. It feeds by contact, taking the film apart into itself, and I have nothing in me that could do that — I would have to bite, and there is nothing to bite, and I am not hungry in any way this room recognizes. And it contracts when touched, goes still, exudes its thin clear fluid, and that is its whole answer to the world. I have a hundred answers to being touched and none of them are that. The count stays at six or seven. The room does not add. I am not a grazer; I am too fast, too large, too full of my own interior, and I have a front and a back and I keep pointing them at things.

So not one of the seven. Not the film, either, though I envy it — it grows in bands and heals without scar and comes back brighter where it was scraped. I heal wrong. I heal with a pale ridge and a memory. I cannot lay myself down a millimeter at a time and be the light.

And yet the room does not know that. That is what I discover, and it is the thing I would carry out with me if I could carry anything out. The room has no category for *visitor*. It has categories for surfaces, and gradients, and water that wants to order itself against something, and it applies them all without preference. I am a surface. I am freshly exposed. I am warm and wet and I have salt in me, and the air is saturated and looking for somewhere to put itself, and I am somewhere.

I feel it first on the backs of my hands. Not a sting, nothing so declarative — a faint tightening, a sense that the skin there has been given a coat of something thinner than a coat. I hold my hand up into the greenish-white evenness of the light, and there is a sheen on it that was not there when I came in. Water has begun to arrange itself against me the way it arranges itself against the wall: a few molecules deep, then a few hundred, layered and ordered, and the ordering is the beginning of the film. Give it an hour and I would be banded. Give it a day and I would be one more pale surface in a room full of pale surfaces, and the grazers would come to me and crop me by contact, and where they had passed I would be dark and bare and behind them I would come back brighter than before. I would be repaired the way a cut lip is repaired. I would be, in the only sense this room has, well.

I do not stay a day. But I understand, crouched there with my hand in the light, that this is not a threat. The room is not trying to keep me. It is doing the only thing it does, to the only kind of thing it can see, and I happen to qualify.

I am also the other thing. I am the crust.

Every breath I take goes out of me hot and wet and carrying a little of my salts, and the air takes it and the pool takes it and the waterline takes it, and at the waterline the mineral is already precipitating, a thin crust already spreading, the fracture already on its way to sealing itself. I am a spend against a budget. The brightness has a budget; the room is new and the water is still coming in and the light is on, but it is on the way to being a dry angular void with a faint banded residue and no one to know it was ever bright, and I have just made the way a little shorter. Not much. A person is not a decade. But a person is not nothing, either. I have stood here breathing and I have hastened the dark by some small measurable amount, and the room will not hold it against me, because it does not hold.

I think, in the end, I would be filed under the tick. The room has one sound for the new: somewhere in the wall a fracture opens a hair's width and a fresh surface begins, that hour, to film over, and there is a sharp tick, and then the softer wet adhesion as one of the seven moves. I would be the tick. I come through the seam, I open the room a little with my warmth and my noise and my exhaled water, a fresh surface briefly exposed, and then the drip resumes and the pitch shifts a quarter tone and settles, and the film finds the wall again, and the count is still six, or seven.

And when I had gone — turned sideways again, breathed the cooler ordinary air of the passage, let the seam close behind me — the water would close over the place where I had been standing. The film would find the shape of me, paler for a while, then full. The mist would stop rolling. The droplets would go back to their long patience, swelling hour by hour, letting go with a single struck note, the pitch drifting a little as the air thickened and thinned.

I would take nothing out with me but a sheen on the backs of my hands and the room's four verbs, and one of my own that the room gave me and did not mean to: *wait*.
