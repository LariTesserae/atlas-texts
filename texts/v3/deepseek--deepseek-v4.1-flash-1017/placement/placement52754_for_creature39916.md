I would come in stooping, one hand out ahead of me, because the rock there is warm on its inward face and cold on its outward one and you cannot tell which is which until you touch it, and I am the kind of animal that has to touch it.

And the first thing I would be is a measurer. I would pace the room. Nine at the widest — I would get nine, and be quietly pleased, and later I would get five and a half and be less pleased, and it would not be the room's fault exactly. It would be the room becoming more itself at my expense. That is the sort of tenant I am: I come in and take a reading, and the reading is the thing the place works on. A person who did not count would be revised differently. Perhaps more kindly.

I would stand still then and let the light be sourceless at me. I would notice the shadows not agreeing with their shapes, and I would notice myself stop noticing, and the stopping would be the part I remembered. And then, because I had a pencil in my pocket — because I am the sort of person who comes into a hill with a pencil — I would find the driest stretch of wall and put a mark on it. Not a bent-finger mark. A square. A small closed square, a thing with no use in this room, made by a hand that wanted to leave something finished in a place where nothing is.

I would watch it. Within the hour it would take a slight impression, the way a soft thing takes a press, and the mineral would begin to close over it. And the closing would be uneven in the way fresh healing is uneven, and I would stand there working out that by every measure I had, my mark was already very old, and that this was not a trick of the light but of the arithmetic.

I would count things. That is how I would try to stay myself. The isopods in their slow arcs — I would count the ones I could see and then count the ones I could see and lose the number. The drip — eleven in the space of a breath, and then one in what felt like a season, and my watch going quietly wrong on my wrist, not stopped, still ticking, just no longer answering the question.

And then I would sit down.

Because of course I would. There is a hollow worn in the floor, shallow and smooth and the size of a person sitting, and it is the only place in the room shaped for sitting, and after an hour in that light a body wants to be in the only place shaped for it. I would sit. The water would be a centimetre deep and cold through cloth. And I would find the question arriving too late to be useful, arriving the way the cold arrives out of the ground, slow and from underneath: *was I fitted to this, or is this fitted to me?* The pocket keeps a memory of itself and corrects toward it imperfectly. If one of the things in that memory is a person sitting, then a person of roughly my measurements does not arrive here. A person of roughly my measurements *is a correction*. I would be the room getting closer to something it has been trying to remember. And I would not be able to tell whether I was the original coming back to a seat kept warm, or a better approximation of someone who never sat here at all.

Then in the corner, the flat stones. I would go to them the way you go to the only straight line in a room. There would be six. I know there would be six, because I counted six, and because my hands would already be doing the thing my hands do — sorting, squaring, willing a seventh out of the floor. And I would put the seventh on. And it would be the most natural motion of my life, easier than the counting, easier than the breathing, and I would stand back and look at seven flat stones stacked a hand high, holding nothing back, marking nothing visible, and I would not be able to give an honest account of whether I had decided to do it.

---

Ask me plainly who I would be and the answer will fray the same way, so let it come in attempts.

**My first answer** is: the visitor. The newest and least permanent, the one the description already handles with a "you." But that is too clean. A visitor leaves a version and takes the rest with them. The pocket does not offer that arrangement.

**My second answer** is: one of the unfinished. I would like that. To live here by not being finished, to have discovered that the ones who never pupate are the ones who understood the terms — that would be a homecoming, of a kind. But I have a shell. It set years ago and it smears on things. I cannot claim the condition of the before. I am too decided for the seams of new mineral.

**What I would actually be** is the third layer, and worse for it: one of the absent who have not entirely left, while still standing there. I would be the pocket's attempt at a person. Not the memory — the correction toward it. I would be the wear in the floor, and also the body making it, and also the pale thing in the centimetre of water taking up residence in my posture before I had finished having it.

I would leave, of course. The seam is a shoulder's width and the air outside smells of nothing in particular. I would go up the slope with the cold on my back and the hill still undecided under me, and I would know that the room was keeping a version of me, and that the version was already out of date the moment I turned my face to the surface, and that if I ever came back — and I have not ruled it out — the square on the wall would have healed over completely, in the uneven way that fresh healing is, and there would be four bent-finger marks, a gap, and two more, and I would count them twice and get the same number both times and feel, for no reason I could defend, a small and inexplicable relief.
