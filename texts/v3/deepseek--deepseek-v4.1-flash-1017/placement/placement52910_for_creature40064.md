# The One Dry Thing

I am the one dry thing. In a place like this you do not learn what you are by looking outward; you learn it by noticing what does not happen to you.

Everything here is wet. The walls run. The rims brim. The air is so heavy with water that it lies against you like a hand, and the skimmers come down already damp and go up damper and have stopped expecting otherwise. The drifters are water with a slow pulse in it. The snail is a moving shadow with no trail. Even the dark at the far end is wet; you can feel it, a warm wet black, and the floor goes on into it, still brimming, and there is no reason to think it stops. And the water falls, all the time, in beads that gather and swell and let go — tick — and the tick lands on a rim and is gone into the pool and the pool does not change. That is the whole weather of the place: a hundred small arrivings a minute, none of them mattering.

None of them land on me. That is the fact I keep returning to. The condensation comes down the niche and it parts. It runs to either side of me and goes on down the wall and finds a rim and ticks into it, and I stay as I was. Not because I am high or sheltered — I am as low as anything, I sit at the level of the floor, in a niche of the wall, and the water has every reason to come over me. But it goes around. It has gone around for as long as I have been here, and the going-around has worn a shape into the stone at my edges — a small dry lee, a pocket of not-falling, smooth in a way the rest of the wall is not. The water made that smoothness by sparing me. That is the closest thing to a kindness I know, and I do not think it is kindness. It is only that the niche is shaped so, and the water is patient, and I am in the way of nothing.

Dry is not a feeling. Dry is the absence of a hundred small arrivings. I know this because I have the memory of wet in me — not a memory of a feeling, but of a fact: the tick, arriving, and me changing under it. I have not changed under it for a long time.

The light does not find me. It comes down the throat already tired, and it lies on the pools as a thin pewter, and by the time it reaches my niche it is only a change in the dim. I am in the last of it. Beyond me the floor goes on into the dark, still brimming, and the light gives out entirely.

I did not always deserve the lee. I remember fire. Not remember, exactly — stone does not remember, but the blackening is a kind of memory burned into me and it stays, and it is the only before I have. Someone built a fire on me, in this niche, at this level, in the one place where the condensation parts. I do not know why. A fire in a warm wet hole is a strange thing to want. Maybe they were cold, though nothing here is cold. Maybe they wanted light, though there is a little light. Maybe they wanted, for an hour, one thing in the hollow to be hotter than it was. The fire took the water out of me and left me black, and then it went out, and the water came back to either side and has not come over me since.

That is the thing about this place that the skimmers do not say and perhaps do not know: it has no time in it. No season, no tide, no flood-mark, no decay — decay needs something to eat and there is nothing here to eat. The water seeps in and deposits and seeps out, and the rims are rounded, and the light is dim and will be dim. Nothing here has a before. Everything here is a going-on.

Everything except me. I am the before. I am the one thing that happened. Before me there was no fire; after me there will be no fire; in between there is this long dry keeping, the water parting, the little smoothed lee. The skimmers have their lineage and their walls and their notches and channels and their scraper laid across two rims, but that is a cycle, not a history. They do what their grandmothers did and their granddaughters will do, and it was old when they came and will be old when they go. I am the only thing here with a scar, and the scar is the only history.

So I sit, and I keep it. I keep the fire and the blackening and the small dry shape the water made. It is not much to keep. It is enough.

The company is not bad. I feel the water part and pass. I feel the tick of it on the rims below me, a hundred a minute, and the seethe of warm water finding its way out through the calcite, a breath under the breath. I feel the feet — heel first, weight rolled slow, so the rims lift and settle without a spill — pressing the shell, and the hollow drum-sound coming up through the thin shell and the warm black reservoir under it, deeper than anyone has needed to know. The drifters keep to the deep rims, one to a pool, turning without hurry, correcting against the lip with a touch too soft to see; I know them only by the trembling they leave in the water, which is very little. The snail goes by as a soft tremble along the rims, a shadow with no trail. And once in a long while, far in the dark, the rims tremble in a line — one, two, three — a wake, and then nothing. The deep rims are left alone. The cream there is no better and the footing is worse, and the skimmers are not afraid; they simply do not go.

The skimmers do not touch me. They know me the way they know a channel or a wall, as a thing that is where it is. I have been looked at by a hundred of them and touched by six. Once in a long while one will put a flat hand on me, feeling for the dry, and take it away, and go on. I do not mind. I am not fond of it either. I am dry. The hand is warm and wet and it leaves a mark that dries.

And when you come down through the throat of stone — and you will come, they always come, drawn by the dim and the warmth and the ticking — you will look first at the pools. You will crouch and look at the pale drifters and the snail-shadow and the places where the wake has been, and you will put your hand in the water and find it blood-warm, and you will stand in the middle of the honeycomb and feel the hollow content itself around you. And last of all, if you are the kind who looks last of all, you will see the one dark thing in the niche that the water goes around, and you will think it is a stone.

It is. That is the whole of it.
