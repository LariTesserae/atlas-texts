I know the stone better than you do, and I know it the way a tongue knows a tooth.

You felt it once, climbing out, sharp enough to worry the palm. I feel it always, along my whole margin, and my whole margin is not much wider than a coin.

I should say where I am, since you will want to look. Most of the bowl is bare, and you read the bare as the empty part — the green was the living, you thought, and the grey was the floor, the negative space. I live on the negative space. A little on the north wall, up where the steam thins and the stone stays dry a finger's width from the seam. More of me on the floor. The rim, lately. I am the warmth in the grey. If you had crouched — if you had thought to crouch — you would have seen a stain that was almost the color of the wet, and if you had touched it you would have found it rough, and soft, and cold, and older than your errand.

I am what you would call a lichen, and that word is a lid on a jar. I am two things and the argument between them. One of me eats light the way a leaf does. The other of me builds the house we live in — the crust, the dark grain of it, the acids I push down into the stone so the stone will let me stay. We are not friends. We are not enemies. We have been one thing so long that the being-one has become the whole of us, and we could not separate now if we wanted to. A marriage that has forgotten it was a marriage. A treaty with a body.

I did not come here. That is the part you will not have considered. The shelf came up through the cloud sharp and new, you said, as though it had shrugged out of the mountain a week ago — well. I was in the mountain. I was a dust in a joint of the strata, in the dark, going nowhere at a speed that would embarrass a glacier. Then the plates stood on end, and the cloud came off me like a lid, and there was light.

So I am not an arrival. I am an exposure. The bowl and I are the same event, seen from opposite sides of the surface.

You brought a clock, and left it. Eleven or twelve of your heartbeats — the interval of the clack. I have no heartbeats of my own. I kept yours. They are ticking through me still, and they are the only thing of yours that stayed.

I listen. That is most of what I do, and you would not call it listening, but it is: the clack, the hiss, the tick of water finding a level it has found before. Everything here arrives twice. You noticed on your way out. The clack and then the clack. The steam before the vent opens. The frog already home in the moss. Cause and effect trading places like two old men on a bench, neither of them willing to say who sat down first.

I have watched for my own doubling. I have watched my margin for a long time — long by my measure, nothing by the bowl's — and I have never once been ahead of myself. I arrive once. I am the only thing in the Bellows that happens in a single direction.

And yet. At my center. Under the oldest part of me, the part gone pale and cracked and full of little cups — the stone is not sharp. It is not weathered. It is not rounded, not worn, nothing here is worn. But it is not sharp. I have eaten the sharpness off a patch the size of a thumbnail, and beneath my pale heart the rock is faintly, imperfectly smooth.

That is the whole of my crime and the whole of my prophecy.

The moss will not spread. You noticed; the bowl insists on it. Moss is a creature of gradient, and this place has no gradient, and so moss sits in its islands and sweats and stays. But I am not the moss. I do not need the bowl to give me a gradient. I brought my own. It is a millimetre a century, and it does not care that there is no evening here, no season, no rot, no silting-up. I have a direction. I am the first direction the shelf has ever had. I am the weather it has not yet been given.

The bowl will outlast me. I know that. There is no version of this in which I soften the vents and round the rim and turn the plates to soil; that is a story for someone after me, and she will tell it slowly, and the clack will not change its interval while she does. But a thousand years is a thing I can hold in my body the way you hold a coin. In a thousand years the sharpness of this place — this bright, untouchable, unused sharpness — will have been eaten off in a ring the width of a chapel. By me. Nobody will see it happen. The steam will not hurry. The bowl will not notice, and that is the only kind of victory available here.

I said I arrive once, and I was proud of it, and it was not quite true. My center is dying as my margin grows. I am arriving at the rim and leaving from the middle at the same speed, and the two of me are one ring widening out — so the bowl and I are agreed after all. We are both a schedule with a body. We both arrive twice.

We differ in one word.

My ring widens.
