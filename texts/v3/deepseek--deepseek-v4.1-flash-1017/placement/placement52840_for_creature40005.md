I would be a thin place.

You would not see me coming. You would see me only by what shows through: a faint bloom low in a wall, a smear of green-gold where the calcified layers have worn down to almost nothing, the glow of the next chamber leaking through like a bruise beneath skin. The builders made me without meaning to. Ten thousand generations of small blind mouths secreted their chambers into the dark, and where two warm currents met and scoured, the wall went thin. I am what the water did to their work. I am not alive, and I am not dead. I am a place where the city has been worn down to the point of transparency, and the city keeps me the way a body keeps a scar.

What passes through me is the water. It comes slow and thick and blood-warm, carrying what water carries: shed shell, soft fragments, the fine snow of decay, the chemical signature of everything upstream. It arrives at me and it slows. The thinness is a kind of filter. What was diffuse in the open channel becomes legible here, pressed against the calcified grain, and for a moment the water is not a medium but a sentence — this fed, this died, this is dying, this passed through an hour ago and was large and strange. Then the current moves on and the sentence is gone. But I held it. That is what I am for. Not memory. The interval before forgetting.

The light comes through me too, from the chamber beyond, and I have learned — if a thing without nerves can be said to learn — that the light and the water do not agree. The light is steady, a pale green pulse that swells and dims with something on the other side I have never seen, some heart or signal going about its business. The water is never steady. It brings the news of the city, and the news is always change, and the light goes on saying the same thing. I am where the two of them are forced to occupy the same thinness. I do not resolve them. I am the seam between a pulse and a rumor.

Things graze across me. The small scuttlers come in loose crowds, their antennae fine as hair, their rasping mouths leaving pale trails through the film that settles on every surface — and on me the film settles differently, because the water is slower here, and the trails they leave are the only lines in the city that are not made of shell. I feel them as a pressure, a warmth, a small persistent irritability of legs. They do not know I am a place. To them I am simply a surface that happens to be good for scraping. When the pressure changes in the water above, they stop, drop, and become part of the wall — and for a while I am part of a crowd of still bodies, all of us being wall together, and the water forgets us.

Larvae settle on me sometimes. A builder's child, no larger than a grain, drifting out of the dark and finding my thinness warm and my grain rough enough to grip. It begins to secrete. It does not know that it is thickening the one place in the city that was thin. It does not know that in a hundred years there will be no window here, only wall. I do not mind. I was made by the same accident that will unmake me. The city is a process. I am a pause in the process, and a pause is not a stopping.

You would pass me and not know. You are large and strange and you move through the water slowly, because the water resists, because there is no hurry here. Your warmth reaches me before you do — that is how I know you are coming, the way the waiters in the side chambers know, by the taste of blood-warmth and electric motion. You are not food. You are weather. The sampler brushes your leg and does not return, and the grazers go still as you pass, and the light behind me goes on saying its one steady thing, and the water carries your signature down through my thinness and out the other side. For a moment you are legible. Then you are upstream, and then you are memory, and then the current moves on and you are only the shape the water learned.

That is what I would be, if I were anything here. Not one of the named ones — not mason, tongue, weather, archive, immune system, mood. I would be the place where the city is briefly readable to itself. The window it does not know it has. The thinness that lets the light through and holds the news a moment longer than the wall would.

You are a passing thing. I am the place you pass through. The water takes your signature away and adds you to the memory of the place, and I am the seam where the adding happens — and then the forgetting, which is also the city, and also goes on.
