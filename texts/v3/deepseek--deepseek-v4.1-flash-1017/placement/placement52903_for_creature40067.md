**The Suture**

I would find myself in the **Suture**—a low saddle on the plateau’s shoulder, where a stand of the great vertical trunks pulls apart and the living substrate dips into a long, caustic burn. It is not a valley anyone names. It is a scar. The Lattice once flooded it with sap to kill a Mimic bloom, and the sap killed the bloom but not cleanly. The water there tastes of iron, burnt sugar, and the cold green breath of fronds. Light comes down thin and blue. Gas sacs tick against one another. Sound arrives as pressure more than noise.

I would be the **Graft**: a Weaver-sized creature with six filament-brushed limbs and a carapace of driftwood, coral, and dead frond-fiber. But inside I would not be a Weaver. A Mimic once copied a Weaverbound too well and crawled into a root-cleft; the Lattice answered with sap and fiber. The result did not die and did not belong. It hardened around the mimic core, threaded itself with Lattice nerves, and woke into a small, stubborn “I.” My back would grow coral in the pattern of the burn. My limbs would end in delicate brushes. I could read the Lattice’s chemistry as weather—pressure fronts of pain, tides of nutrient, cold blooms of alarm. I could also feel the mimic in me as a hunger that is not mine but uses my hands. I am not a true symbiote. I am a negotiated peace.

My days would be spent along the burn edge. Weavers pass me with wary grooming; they daub enzymes on my joints, not out of affection but maintenance. The Lattice sends me slow pulses when a mimic copy is near—an itch in the root beneath a false frond. I go to it, imitate its stillness, and touch it with my brushes. If it is a copy, my mimic core recognizes the lie before the Lattice’s caustic sap arrives. I mark it; the Weavers prune. Sometimes I find a copy so perfect that I hesitate. That hesitation is the only part of me that is fully mine.

Gloam drift through at dusk, translucent ribbons carrying luminous spores. They do not avoid me; my false core glows faintly like a spore sac, and they fold around me, reading my surface. For a moment I am a lens in their colony, and I see the forest as a single breathing sheet. Then they pass. I am left with the cold and the taste of iron.

So: in this drowned world, you would find me in the seam between the Lattice and its adversary, a small mobile scar at the edge of a caustic burn, under thinning fronds and blue-green light. I would be the Graft—part gardener’s hand, part ghost’s costume, part filament of the continental mind, and part something that can say *I* only because two vast things failed to kill each other cleanly. I would live by listening to the slow weather of the Lattice and the hunger of the Mimic, and by choosing, each day, which one to feed.
