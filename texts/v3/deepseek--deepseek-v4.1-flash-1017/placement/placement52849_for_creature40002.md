**THE OPEN LOOP**

Find me an hour out from any grove, on the bare slab, standing where the light falls straight down and my shadow lies on the rock like a cut piece of black paper. Mine is the only shadow that moves. Everything else here has been holding still for a very long time, and I am the one thing in sight that is doing something.

I do not belong, and I know it, and the knowing is not sad. It is just the fact of the place. Everything on this plateau is a closed loop: it breathes itself, it drinks itself, it refines itself, and nothing comes in and nothing goes out. I am a hole in that. I am the one open system on the shield. Air goes into me and comes out changed. Water goes into me and does not come back. I am a warm, wet fracture that walks around, and I am leaking at every edge.

---

I keep time the way this place keeps nothing: by losing.

I left with a little over forty litres and it is going out of me steadily — breath, sweat, the wet in my exhale, the water I spend just to stay at a temperature the rock would call restful. I have stopped counting days. I count litres. When I am down to half I will turn back, and if I am wrong about the distance I will not turn back in time, and that is the whole of my calendar.

The only cold surface within a day's walk is the underside of a grove's thatch. It is not cold; it is a degree or two below the temperature of my breath, and that is enough. I have learned not to rest under a canopy. You stand in the green dark out of the light, grateful, breathing, and the thatch takes it — your moisture condenses on the inner faces of the columns, and runs down inside them, and the wall of the stalk is catalytic and does not care whose breath it is working. It takes mine apart. It sets the parts down in its ring, out at the root, in the frost: a thread of carbon, some sodium, a bright flake of something I was carrying. The grove is drawing me, line by line, into the record it keeps of the present, and it is doing it where I cannot watch.

So I keep to the bare. It is a courtesy I extend to myself.

---

I came here to read layers. That is what I know how to do. Give me a wall of stone and I will tell you what fell into it, and when, and what was alive above it while it fell. I have spent a whole life reading the slow soft rain of the dead pressed into floors.

There is no floor here. Nothing silts down. Nothing is covered, nothing is buried, nothing is pressed. The past has nowhere to lie, so it does not lie; it is all present tense, all of it continuous, the metal creeping out from the roots at a rate you would need instruments to see, the crusts recording only *now, now, now*, like a breath fogging a window over and over. My instruments have nothing to attach to. Everything I can measure here is a rate, and every rate is the same rate it was a thousand years ago and will be a thousand years on.

The only stratified thing within a hundred kilometres is my own skull. My teeth have bands in them, one for every year I have been alive, and so do my bones, and so do the little stones in my ears. I am carrying a readable sequence across a country that abolished sequence. When I am gone it goes with me, unread, and the rock will not care, because the rock has never had a use for a before.

---

I am also making litter.

This is the thing I did not think through. There are no animals here — not one, not a fly, not a mite in the thatch — and so there is nothing that breaks a body down. There is no decay. There is no small patient crew of things that take a fallen thing apart and give it back. Whatever I shed stays shed. Skin cells in the fractures. A hair. The salt off my hands on a hand-width of slab. I am not one thing either: I am carrying several kilograms of things that are not me, and none of them have ever been outdoors here, and every one of them is going to die in the open and lie there, intact, because there is nobody here to eat them.

The first leavings. The first midden. There is a word for what I am making and the word is archaeology, and I am making it while I stand here, by breathing.

And when I stop, whenever it is and wherever it is, the nearest ring will find me the way it finds everything — slowly, outward, without intent. Not burying me. Nothing here buries. Plating me. It will come up over the soles of my boots, over the case, over the long bones, a thin iridescent frosting in bismuth-pink and arsenic-grey and a green that is not the green of any leaf I have ever known, and it will be very accurate, and it will be made of me, and it will take a very long time and nothing will interrupt it.

---

Here is what I think, out on the bare, with four groves in sight and a fifth coming up over the rise, each one dark and glossy and copper at the edges, each one humming at the floor of hearing, each one holding its balance like a spinning top with everything in motion and nothing changing:

I am the first future this place has ever had.

Not a big one. Not a long one. But until I came, there was nothing here that could be *found* — the record was continuous and unreadable and there was no underneath, and no one coming, and no reason for anyone to come. Now there is a shape on the slab that will still be a shape when the shape of the continent is different. Somewhere down the years, something will cross this shield and come on a bright thin wrong ring around a grove, and in the middle of it, under a crust of its own elements, a thing that was not made here.

And it will dig, because that is what you do with a find — and it will find the slab.

No layer beneath. No strata, no floor of the past, nothing to go down into. Just the object lying on the open rock with the sky drawn tight over it, the first and only stratum this plateau has ever had, and the whole of it one body deep.
