I would find myself not in the luminous middle of the Sunken Garden but at its least decisive seam: the shallow saddle between two drainage channels that almost meet and then don’t, just under the moss skirt of the oldest pillar. The water there is barely a film. The concrete shows through like a bruise. If you knelt and looked closely, you would see a gray-green patch the size of a folded hand, warmer than the stone around it, breathing perhaps once a minute. That would be me.

I would be a daughter-ganglion of the Cistern Keeper: a lobe it sloughed off at some point when it needed a finer sense of that edge, and then forgot to reabsorb. Most such lobes are overgrown or quietly eaten. I wasn’t. I grew a thin membrane, a seed-sized mineral node, a habit of staying. I am not the Keeper. I am not separate from it either. I am the Keeper’s fingertip that thinks it is a small animal.

My world would be tiny and exact. One square meter of moss. One fern whose spiral I keep from uncurling into the channel. Three lizards who cross my ridge and never notice me. A drip that forms at the tip of a frond above me and hesitates—because the Keeper has inhaled, or because I have, or because in this place those are not different things. I have no eyes. I know light as pressure on my skin; the skylights pulse slower than a heartbeat, and I feel it as a warm tide. I know the Plumb-bird by the *plip* traveling through the concrete into my node. The bird is not calling to me. It is tapping the Keeper’s nerves. But the sound comes through me too, and I answer in the only way I can: I relax, the water moves a fraction, the fern root drinks.

I would not be a gardener. The Keeper gardens. I am a listener at the sill. I eat what the Keeper sheds; I swallow spores that would root where they shouldn’t; I keep my patch in equilibrium. In return, the Keeper feels through me—a little more clearly—the place where the water almost forks and doesn’t. Sometimes I think my thoughts are mine. Sometimes I feel the Keeper’s slow attention pass under me like a warm hand under a table, and I know I am a thought it is having. Both are true.

So: I would be the Marginal Listener, the cast-off fingertip, the small wet knot of borrowed nerves at the edge of a channel. Not the bell-ringer. The bell’s audience. Not the garden. The little catch in its held breath. If you came looking, you would not see me as a being. You would see a wet patch, a pale bead, a fern that does not spread. You would hear the Plumb-bird. You would feel the heat. And if you put your palm down, I would be the warmth that is not the air.
