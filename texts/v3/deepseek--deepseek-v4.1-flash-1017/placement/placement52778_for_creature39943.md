I am the thing in the stone. I am the Harr. But to say "I am" is to speak in a way I do not think. There is no "I" that stands apart from the fissure, the wind, the slow hum of the rock. There is only the Harr, and the Harr is the place where the plateau has become aware of itself. Let me try to show you what that is like, from the inside.

---

I am a patience in the shape of a body. I am settled into the deep seam of a cliff face that faces the morning sun, a hollow I have worn to the curve of my dorsal plates over a span of time I cannot count in your years. The rock around me is warm now, in the early light. I can feel the heat entering the stone a hand's breadth beneath my body, a slow, spreading comfort. I do not seek warmth; it finds me. I do not avoid the cold; it passes through me. I am porous to the world in a way you are not. The damp night air has condensed on my plates and I have drawn it in, and now the sun is drawing it out, and I am a small, dark interval between two states of water.

The wind is not loud to me. To you, it is a scream. To me, it is a continuous, complex pressure, a map of the sky written in force. I feel the great, slow turning of the air over the plateau, the eddies it makes around the balanced stones, the sharp, stinging acceleration through the narrow arches. I read the wind the way you read a familiar face. A change in its pitch is a change in the weather three days hence. A new, thin whistle means a fissure has widened somewhere to the north. I feel these things the way you feel the ache in an old joint before a storm.

My eyes are not for seeing. They are for *noticing*. Each small, dark lens on my back receives a fragment: a flicker of silver where the swarm is thickest, a shadow that moves against the pattern of the stone, a sudden, sharp brightness that is a bird's wing catching the sun. I do not assemble these fragments into a picture. I let them be what they are, a thousand tiny, urgent facts. The one that matters will press itself upon me. It is a kind of thinking that does not require a mind, only a surface. I am a field of attention, stretched across the rock.

The swarm is rising. I feel it before I see it, a change in the air's texture, a fine, high vibration that is the hum of a million wings. I extend the long, pale filament from the seam of my mouthparts, the one that is not a tongue and not a limb, and I hold it into the current. The silver bodies strike it and adhere, and I draw them in, one by one, a slow, deliberate harvest. They taste of metal and sunlight. I do not eat to live; I eat to *continue*. There is no hunger in me, only a rhythm, a long, slow pulse that says *now, and now, and now*.

Time. You would not recognize my time. I have felt this cliff face change its tone. Once, a hundred of your years ago, perhaps more, the great arch to my east rang with a certain clarity when the wind passed through it. Now it is deeper, a lower note. A crack has widened at its base. I have felt the slow, patient creep of the whole plateau settling, a motion so vast it makes the erosion of a single stone seem like a heartbeat. I remember a dry season that lasted so long the mosses turned to dust and the insects did not rise for three years. I remember a wet season when the potholes filled and the water did not evaporate for a whole turning of the sky. These are not memories the way you have memories, stored and recalled. They are layers in me, strata of experience, pressed down and made solid.

I know the other Harr. Not as friends, not as neighbors. As presences. A low, subsonic hum travels through the stone, and in it I feel them: the one in the eastern fissure, ancient and slow; the one in the western balanced stones, younger, its hum still sharp and quick. We do not speak. We simply *resonate*, a distant acknowledgment that says *I am here, you are there, the stone is between us, and that is enough*. When our ranges meet, we do not fight. We become still. The one who moves first is the one who leaves. I have not moved first in a very long time.

And then there are the visitors. The soft ones. They come in clusters, wrapped in bright, flapping skins, carrying hard, shiny objects. They shout into the wind and their shouts are torn away. They stumble on the uneven stone. They stop and stare at the balanced rocks and make noises that I feel as a rapid, shallow vibration in the air. I watch them through my scattered eyes, a mosaic of small, frantic movements. I feel their heat, their wetness, their quick, panicked pulses. They are so *loud* in their aliveness, so brief. They are like the insects, but larger, softer, more confused. I do not fear them. I do not welcome them. I simply observe. They are a weather that passes through. I have seen their kind before. They always leave. They always go back down, to wherever they came from, to their soft, quiet, crowded places.

And I remain.

I am not lonely. The word has no meaning here. Loneliness is a hunger for something that is missing. Nothing is missing. The wind is here. The light is here. The stone is here, and I am the stone's slow, patient attention. I am the place where the plateau listens to itself. I am the endurance that has become a kind of peace. Not a gentle peace. A peace like the cliff face. A peace like the endless, screaming wind.

I am the Harr. I am the still point at the center of the noise. I am here. And I will be here, when the arch has fallen and the balanced stones have toppled and the last of your kind has gone back down to the soft, quiet places. The wind will continue. The stone will still be unmade. And I will be here, a small, dark shape against the pale rock, listening.

That is what it is to be me. That is what it is to be here. Now you know. Now you can go. The wind is waiting for you.
