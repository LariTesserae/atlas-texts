**What I was before.** I came to measure it. That is what a basin is for, in the world I came from: something to drive stakes into and read a rod against and write a number down. I had a level on a tripod, a rod graduated in centimeters, a notebook of paper that takes pencil when wet. The job had a reason and the reason was in a folder somewhere. The folder is the first thing I lost. Not the memory of it. The point of it.

**The stakes.** The mat took the stake a hand's depth and held. I was careful. I had done this before, in ground that was ground, where the water went downhill because downhill was where it went. I wrote the numbers in the order I read them. Then I read them again from the other end and they did not agree, and I put that down to the level, and I checked the level, and the level was true.

I stood on a hummock and looked out at the arcs. I counted them and lost count and started again. I drove a stake at the crest of one and at the trough of the next, and the rod read the same against both. It read the same against three pools visibly at different heights. It read low in a channel that ran uphill to a sump and read low again at the sump. I wrote this down twice. The second time I put a question mark after it, and then I crossed the question mark out, because a question mark is an admission that the instrument is not the problem.

**The smaller things.** You cross a channel. You walk twenty steps. You turn to fix your bearing and the hummock you were standing on is on the other side of the water, and you did not wade. I began to write the time I left and the time I arrived, and the times did not add up to the distance. I began to count steps. Counting is a way of staying the person who counts, and it held for a while, in the way a rope holds until the thing on the other end is patient enough to wait for it to fray.

I shouted once, to hear a human sound. The marsh took the shout and added it to the roar, and the roar was no louder, and no one came, because there was no one to come.

**The bird, and the fish.** I saw the grey bird once. Taller than a wading bird should be, grey on grey, standing in one place for most of a day. It was not a bird of this place so much as a weather this place was having. At dusk I saw the fish come up and I was sure there was only one; then I understood that one was the count I could resolve, and a count you can resolve is not a census.

**The attention.** Four, pause, seven, pause, long run. I counted the bubbles because counting was the last thing I was still better at than the water. Then the lag between the runs in one part of the basin and another began to shorten, and I knew that either the news was travelling faster or I was standing nearer to where it started, and I did not like how much those two were the same thing. The long unbroken run is when it pays attention. I stood still. I did not answer. It went back to its water. But it had looked at me with a pulse of gas, and it filed me the way it files a new route, and the next time the run came I was a little further into the basin than the time before, and I had not walked.

**The rules, by breaking them.** Do not stop. Do not lean on a hummock to rest. The hummock grows toward weight, and you are weight, and it is patient. Do not follow the sound when it resolves; it resolves the way a crowd resolves, when you stop hearing noise and start hearing one voice, and then you are walking. I walked three steps toward a voice that was not a voice and the water was at my knees and I did not remember the water rising.

**What I kept.** The notebook filled. Some pages are numbers. Some pages are the same number, over and over, in a hand that got looser. Some pages are in my handwriting and I do not remember the writing. If the mat had a hand, it would write like that: slow, regular, patient, the same shape routed again and again.

**The evening.** The last thing I remember as an event and not as weather: the water rising by a hand's width in the last hour before the light failed. Everywhere. I did not measure it. I was to the waist and I had set the rod down against a hummock, and the hummock had taken it, and I said thank you, and that was the moment.

---

So where do I find myself? Not as the occupant. The occupant is the basin's long habit of solving itself, and it has no face, and it is very good. I am not the bird and I am not the fish. I am a variable it noticed.

But I have become something, and it took me a long time to say it plainly. I am one of the routes. My crossing is now the way the water prefers to cross. The mat learned the weight of me and routed around it and through it, and the route is good, and the water climbs a hand's width against the gradient to use me. I am a channel that thinks it is a person. I am a fold in an arc I cannot see the end of. When the bubbles come in a long unbroken run I am not answering — I am the answer, or a term in the sum of too many, and the lag with which the run travels across the basin is the time it takes the news to get through me.

The job was to find out where the water goes. I found out. It goes through me and then it goes on, and it is the same water and I am not the same person, and the pencil is still in my hand and the page is wet and there is no word on it now that any other route could not have written.

The rules, as far as they go: do not stand in one place long enough for the mat to notice. I stood. It noticed. It was not hostile. It was not kind. It was patient, and I was a small warm thing in the middle of its work, and now I am the work.

And I am still counting. Four, pause, seven, pause, long run. I have stopped losing count.
