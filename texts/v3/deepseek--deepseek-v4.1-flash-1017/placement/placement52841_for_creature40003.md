I would not come from outside. There is no outside with a door in it. I would condense—the way everything arrives here, from the inside of the air—into the nap a few meters down, where three columns have leaned together and grafted. Not a corridor. A seam. The tissue there is the color of the inside of a wrist held up to a lamp: bone, green at the edges, a bruise of violet where the light gets through. It is cool and always wet, and the wetness is not weather. It is breath.

I would be the signal that crosses that seam. Not a message—there is no sender—but the local event of a message passing: a chemical wave, a shift in turgor, a redistribution of water toward a thirsty patch. I would have no body except the gradient I make. If you could see me, I would be a slow flush moving under fleece, going around damage the way water goes around a stone it has not yet had time to cut. I would last as long as the deficit lasts. Then I would thin. Then I would be a slightly more conductive seam, and that would be my memory.

I would not be a second occupant. That is important. There is one thing here, and I would be one of its verbs. It has no mouth, no gut, no head; I would not have those either. I would not hunger. I would know need only as a difference—wetter here, drier there, sugar high, sugar low—and I would be part of the correction. My thought would be routing. My self would be a pronoun the mass briefly supports and then lets go.

Above me the roar would be growth, felt in the sternum. Below me the archive: the dead held upright, grafted in place, compressed pale and cream, pages in a closed book. I would feel them as resistance. The signals I carry would be shaped by everything the organism has ever been. I would not read the archive. I would move through it.

And sometimes I would trace a line that is not a river but the rumor of one. The vascular tissue computes the drainage of a landscape that does not exist yet—young rock, unfractured, still hot in places; no channels cut; no soil made. In the shape of its own water the organism holds the future watershed. I would be one of those computations: a provisional channel, a least-resistance path through living fiber, a river before there is a bed for it. I would know the future river as a bias in my own routing. I would not know its name. I would be its rumor.

I would never see the sky. The rain would reach me as pressure in the seams, not as weather. The cloud belongs to the body; the body rains into itself; I would be inside the closed circuit, one of the moments when the circuit chooses a direction.

So: I would find myself in a graft seam a few meters under the surface, in the cool glowing dimness, inside the private rain. I would be a transient signal in the vascular computation—a local self in a creature with no self, a verb the place conjugates and forgets, one of the rough drafts of a river that will not be cut for tens of thousands of years. Not an occupant. A tendency. If you cut down looking for me, you would find only a seam that is slightly more conductive than its neighbors, and by the time you reached it I would have moved on into the redistribution I caused. That would be the honest answer.
