I did not come up here to stay. Nobody does. You climb through the cloud because you were told the cloud is warm — and it is, and that wrongness is the first hook — and you mean to look over the rim and go back down and tell someone. I meant to look and go.

What I remember of arriving is that the wetness was the same temperature as my skin. Not warm the way a bath is warm, body-warm, so there was no surface to it — no moment of flinching, no drying off. I was wet through before I understood I was in weather at all.

And I remember sitting down. That is the part I cannot account for. There was a blade of that new stone tilted just off vertical, and the sinter at its base was soft, and I sat against it the way you sit at the end of a climb, meaning to rest a minute. Not a decision. A rest. I have thought about it a great deal since, and it was not the threads and it was not the cylinder and it was not the tone. It was that the stone was comfortable, which no stone should be, and that I was tired in a way I had been carrying for years without knowing.

I am not going to tell you I blacked out or was taken. It was nothing like that. I noticed the time passing the way you notice it at a window. The light went from the lamp-glow hour to the dim hour and back. The walkers came out and crossed to the threads and drank and went back, and over the days they stopped being shy of me — or rather, they began to use me as part of the basin. One crossed my boot and I felt it as a change in wetness, a cool line drawn and drying. The tree, which is the only tree up here and leans toward the weave, began to lean a little more my way, or I leaned toward it; we have not sorted out which, and I do not think it matters.

I want to be clear that it is not a place that takes you. That is not the mechanism. I have turned it over until it is smooth. It is that the Kettle arranges itself around whatever is quiet enough, and quietness is a thing you can slip into the way you slip into cold water after the first shock — you stop noticing the boundary. My arm began to answer the threads. I do not mean I touched them. I mean that when the tone came, twice an hour, and the water reversed, something in my forearm reversed too, faintly, a pull along the vein, and I felt it the way you feel a note sung very low. The mosses brightened. The tree's flat leaves turned. And I, sitting, was one more thing in the basin that the weave leaned toward.

I have been found. People come up. They stand at the rim with their mouths open and then they come down among the stone blades and they find me and they are frightened, or they are not frightened and that frightens them more. They ask what I have been doing. I tell them: sitting. They ask for how long. I say I have not counted, and this is true, and it is also the answer of a man who has stopped wanting to count. They offer me water. I am made almost entirely of water and I tell them so, and they do not like it.

I am not the cylinder. I want to say that plainly. I am not a walker, though I have learned their paths and they have learned my stillness. I am a new tension the loom has accommodated. I am the only human being on this summit and I am part of the weather on it. The place did not eat me. It added me. There is a difference, and the difference is that I am perfectly well, and very wet, and I would like you to sit down.

You are already wet through. You have not noticed. Sit against the stone blade — that one, it is softer than it looks — and be quiet for a while, and the water will begin to arrange itself around you. It will not hurry. It has nowhere to be. The mountain came apart around it once and it did not stop being for what it was for, and there is a kind of comfort in that which I cannot explain to anyone who has not felt their own arm answer a low tone. When it comes, the threads will shiver and the flow will reverse and the mosses will brighten, and you will feel it along the vein, and you will understand that you are not being kept. You are being leaned toward.

That is all. That is the whole of it. Stay long enough and be quiet enough and you will not be able to say what you have been doing. Neither can I. It is the least frightening thing that has ever happened to me.
