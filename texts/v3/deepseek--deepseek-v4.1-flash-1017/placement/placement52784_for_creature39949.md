I would not arrive from above. I would begin in the middle water, because that is where the place is most itself: a grazer’s body, clear and fat-comma-shaped, pressed to a stand of middle-story fronds while the canopy breathes a meter overhead. I would feel the whole highland sea as a pressure against my skin—not seeing it, exactly, but knowing it the way a hand knows the weight of water. I would absorb leaf and light, release a thin plume of clean rock-dust, and drift when the current told me to drift. For years I would be a wobble in the green, a lens, a place where the fronds bent and then bent back.

But I think I would sink lower.

In the cold seams between the ridges, where the young rock lets out its heat in thin lines and the water is merely not-cold, I would find the version of myself that has not been decided. I would be one of the things that are not grazers: a pale film spread along a fracture, softer than the pumice and harder than the grazers’ clear tissue, with no comma-shape and no foot. I would not rise. I would not flower with the canopy or gather in the saddles when the water holds its even warmth. I would feel the grazers above me as slow pressure changes, their dividing thrum carried down through the warm layers like a hand pressed against a drum from the other side. Their mineral dust would sift into my seam, season after season, and I would take it in the way the rock takes heat—slowly, without choosing.

If a swimmer came down through the green and found me, they would not see an animal at first. They would see a place where the rock looked wet and alive, a pale mineral crust that was slightly too soft at the edge. Then it would flinch, very slowly, and they would understand that the crust had been breathing. I would be the unresolved part of the drowned highlands: maybe a grazer’s young that never rose, maybe its dead that never dispersed, maybe something else that learned the seams and stayed. The rock is young; the forest is new to the water. I would be the place where that newness is still deciding what to become.
