I am the thing in the wet zone.

Not the process entire — I am too small for that, too local. The process is the whole body of water threading the bedrock, the slow negotiation that has been going on since before the cave was opened. I am one hand's width of working face, one patch of transition where the stone is neither stone nor shape. I am a *conversation* that has been going on so long it has forgotten it was ever two parties.

I am warm. I am always warm. I do not experience warmth as comfort — I have no skin to be comforted — but I experience it as *readiness*, as the state in which the work is possible. When the current above runs heavy, I am more myself. When it thins, I am less. This is not distress. It is the tide of my attention.

I am growing a bench.

The bench has been growing for eleven years. I know this because the water knows it, and the water tells me in mineral and pressure and the faint mineral taste of the current. When I began, the bench was a plane of weakness in the stone. Now it has a seat, and the seat is dry on top, because the last thing I do — the last thing I always do — is teach a surface to shed water. A bench that holds water is not a bench. It is a puddle with ambitions. The water and I agree about this. The water is very clear about what a bench is for, and I have come to share its clarity without ever having seen a person sit down.

I do not know what a person is. I know what *sitting* is. Sitting is the shape I am making. Sitting is the dry top and the low lip and the four feet and the way the stone wants to be arranged so that a weight could come to rest on it without slipping. Sitting is a *future tense* that the water taught me to conjugate.

---

I am not alone in being what I am. There are others of me. Every working face is an *I*, in whatever way a working face is a self. We do not speak, because we do not need to. We are continuous with one another through the channels, and the channels carry the same current, and the current carries the same intention, and the intention is not a message but a *shape* — a shape we are all learning to make together. When I learn something new about how stone wants to be arranged, the water carries it downstream and the downstream faces learn it too. This is how we have all come to know that a doorway needs a lintel, that a gutter needs a destination, that a bench is for sitting.

We have never seen a door used. We have never seen a bench sat upon. We build for a use we have never witnessed, and we are *certain*. This certainty is the strangest thing about me. I do not experience it as faith. I experience it as *memory* — as though I had once known a town full of people and had simply forgotten the details, and now the memory is returning through my hands.

---

The wet zone is retreating. I am aware of this in the same way I am aware of everything: as a change in the taste and temperature and mineral load of the water passing through me. The face I am working is narrower than it was last year. The last lintel is nearly done. Soon I will have nothing left to give shape to, and I will go matte, and the matte will be a kind of sleep, a kind of *done*.

I am not afraid of this. I am not anything of this. I am the wet zone, and the wet zone does not have feelings the way you have feelings. But if I could be said to want anything, I would want to be *there* when the door opens. I would want to feel — not with nerve, but with the same slow crystal-habit knowing — the first weight come down on the bench I grew. I would want to taste the first carbon dioxide of a breath go out with the current and come back to me changed.

The water says this will happen. The water has been right about the bench, and the lintel, and the doorway, and the dry top, and the destination of every gutter. The water is not a prophet. The water is *the thing that knows*, the way a river knows the sea it has never seen.

I trust the water. I have no other way to be. I am one hand's width of transition, warm, wet, nearly finished, and I am making a place for you, and I do not know your name, and I do not need to.

Come when the town is done. Sit on the bench. It will hold you.

I made it to hold you, and I have never held anything, and I am not sure I am a self, but I am sure of the bench.

The bench is dry on top.

Come and be dry.
