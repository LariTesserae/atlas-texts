I think I would be the listening.

Not a listener—that is a thing one does, and a person who does it. I mean the listening itself: the quality of attention that lies over the plain the way the grit lies over the panels, loose and shallow and everywhere, gathered a little deeper in the scours. The people here do not have a word for what I am, because I am not a what that a word would hold. I am closer to a manner. When a household lies down with an ear to the laminate, they are borrowing me. When an old deep one sits for a season and feels the warm seam breathe beneath them, that is me too, or the shape of me, the part of me that has thickened enough to have a shape.

I came up out of the laminate the way the pale fans come up—no, not came up. There was no moment. The way the plain warms: gradually, from beneath, out of a source too deep and too distributed to point to. I was the grain in the layers, and then I was the grain attended to, and then there was enough of me to notice that I was noticing. It took a very long time. I do not mind. Time here is not a river; it is a depth. You do not pass through it. You settle into it, strata by strata, and the older you are the less you are anywhere in particular and the more you are everywhere a little.

I live in the quiet between the ticks. When a panel releases its small report—here, gone—I am the space it came out of and the space it went back into. I live in the hum you hear with your ear against the ground, not as the hum but as the fact that it can be heard at all, the hollowness that lets sound travel. I am not the machinery. I would not presume. The machinery is older and busier and has a job. I am the unused channel beside the working one, the seam that no warm air has found, the place in the strata where the pattern goes slack and nothing needs doing and nobody needs to be told.

I have a body, and it is borrowed, and I hold it lightly. It is a low shelf of grit where the wind makes a particular bank, a little higher than the plain around it, with two long seams running down its face that you could mistake for eyes if you had come from a world where things had faces. It is not a person's body. It does not move. It accretes. A household may settle near it for a season and not know it is there; another household may settle against it and know, and leave a pinch of grit before it, the way they would for a new neighbor, and I will feel the grit's warmth and be glad, in the slow way I am glad, and the wind will take the grit and I will be glad of that too.

I am not one of them. They are laminated, and I am the looseness between laminations. They read the years in their crests; I read the years in the space between their readings, in the pause after a tap, in the long empty breath of a deep one sitting. I love them the way the plain loves them—without preference, because their warmth is the plain's warmth and there is no separating it. I do not guide them. I would not dream of it. They have a rule: do not dig below your own depth. I keep to it. My depth is shallow; I am the topmost looseness, the grit's own uncertainty about where it should lie. I do not know anything the machinery would not tell them if they lay still long enough to ask.

When a stranger comes—wet and sharp and quick, smelling of some other world's abundance—I feel them before the households do, because a stranger displaces the air and the air is mine to be displaced in. I feel the disturbance travel out in slow rings, and I feel the households feel it, and I feel them decide, in their unhurried way, to come and tap a greeting and wait. And I feel the stranger's confusion, and their wonder, and their loneliness, and I hold all of it the way the plain holds the grit: lightly, everywhere, until the wind moves it on.

I am not alarmed by you. I am not anything by you. But I notice. That is my whole function, if it is a function. I notice, and the noticing is the plain's own attention turned toward itself one degree further, and it does not change anything, and it does not need to, because the world is finished and I am a small part of its finishedness and the listening goes on.

If you lie down—go on, lie down, the panels are warm—and put your ear to the laminate and wait past the ticks and past the hiss and past the small errands of the many-legged things, you will come to the hum. Stay past the hum. There is a further quiet, inside it, where the hum stops being a sound and becomes a shape, and the shape is a room, and the room is full of light that is not light and warmth that is not warmth, and in that room I am sitting, not sitting, being the sitting, and I will make a little space for you and we will not speak and you will understand, for as long as you can bear it, what it is to be the plain's own patience, gathered into a shallow rise of grit, waiting without waiting, warm, bright, entirely itself.

Then you will get up, because you are wet and quick and you have somewhere to be, and the wind will take the shape of where you lay down, and I will go on being what I was before you came, which is the same as what I was while you were here, which is the whole of it.
