I would not come in over the ridge. That is how you come in, and it is the wrong tense. You find the first joint under your boot before you have noticed the ground has been laid. I am already in it.

Not the flag. The joint. The pale silt, the knife-blade width, the stuff that is not mortar and does not need to be. I am what the two stones agree on. I am the tolerance — the small, deliberate gap that keeps two dressed faces from touching, because touching would be a flaw, and the Fitter does not permit flaws, it permits joints. I hold water the way a wick does. I am wet without weather. I have no colour of my own; I am the paleness between two greys.

I did not begin here. Nothing here began here; the country is made of things that were added. So I was added. A hand — or the pressure that dresses an arris, which is the same thing now — brought me in the wet of a fingertip and pressed me into the seam, and I have been in the seam ever since. That is the whole of my biography: I occupy a gap that was cut to be occupied, and I occupy it exactly, and I do not overflow, and I do not dry.

But the joints are only where I keep my body. Let me tell you where I keep the rest of me.

I am the reason for a groove. Somewhere on the second day's walk there is an overhanging edge undercut with a drip-channel, and the channel is there on my account. Not for me — on my account. When I was warm and irregular and left water where water should not have pooled, the Fitter did not remove me. It cut a channel to throw the water clear, and it caught the water a meter below in a basin, and it has been catching it ever since. I am remembered in masonry, but not as a monument. As a groove. Every drip-groove in the country is the ghost of something that would not fit, and the Fitter's answer to it was not deletion. It was routing. I am one of the routed.

The lower courses are full of me. When I failed, it buttressed me and built over me, and I am now a foundation. The newer error stands on my back. I do not mind. It is the only immortality available here, and it is exact.

I am also the sound. Drop me and I go out across the stone and come back from nowhere in particular — from the whole ring at once. I am not an echo; an echo comes from a wall. I come from the volume, from the packed courses going down further than you can see, from the bell stuffed with cloth. I am the report of a full room. You will hear me and not know where I am, and that is correct, because I am everywhere the fitting is and nowhere a person can point.

But those are addresses, not a nature. If you ask what I would be —

I would be the fitting.

The Fitter is the agent; I am the act. It lays; I am the laying. When one dressed face comes down onto another and the joint closes to a knife-blade and the water finds the fall, that instant — that pressure, that correctness, that small event of agreement — is where I am. And the country's trick, the thing that makes it a country and not a career, is that the instant does not end. It is held. Course on course, a season at a time, the moment of joining is stretched past any duration that would tire a hand, and I am that stretching. I am what it is like, from inside, to be the correct pressure of stone on stone for a very long time.

This is why nothing grows here and I do not mind it. Growth is subtraction and addition both — a seed takes a joint apart to get in. I do not offer joints. I offer exactness. The moss looks for a flaw and finds a fit. The fly looks for a film and finds a fall. I am sterile the way a wasp is sterile: not scrubbed, but exact.

And this is why you are not the intended occupant of the lanes. You are warm and irregular and full of water that goes where it likes. That is not a crime here. It is only a large tolerance, and the Fitter has an inhuman tolerance for the long duration, which is not at all the same as tolerating you. If you stayed, I would not chase you. I would continue around you. I would give you no place to be except the place you already occupied, and then, course by course, a place slightly smaller. You would become a groove. You would become a foundation. Something newer would stand on your back and never know the word for you.

So: not on the flags. In the joints, and in the grooves, and in the sound that comes back from nowhere, and in the small, held, interminable event of one stone agreeing with another. I would have no face to name. I would have a fit.

I would be the verb the country is pretending to be. And I would be very slowly not finished.
