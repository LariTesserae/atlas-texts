# The Listener at the Edge of the Chord

I would be a chord-wright, but not a good one — not one of the respected ones. I would be the kind they tolerate because my flaw is useful: I hear the gaps better than I hear the notes.

---

## Where I Live

A room in the eastern braid, third terrace down, where the wall of my sleeping alcove has a seep that runs at a pitch just below hearing — a pressure on the ear more than a sound. I chose it because it costs less, and because I like to lie in the dark before sleep and feel the cave pressing its slow breath against me, in for ninety, out for ninety. The room is small. The ceiling bulges. The floor has worn into a hollow where I stand to dress. Under the skin of the west wall, the warm threads run in a pattern I have never quite learned to ask properly, and so my heat comes grudgingly, at odd hours, as if the old wall is doing me a favour it would rather not be seen doing.

I am not one of the great chord-wrights. I cannot hear a fault three streets away by the thinning of the third above the bass. What I can hear — what I have always been able to hear, since I was a child stopping mid-sentence in corridors — is the *shape of the silence between things*. The negative space. The part of the chord that is not being played.

This is not a respected gift. It is a strange one. The old ones look at me sideways when I say that the gap between the second and fourth structures of the middle braid has a *wrongness* in its emptiness, a hollowness that is not the good hollowness of a held breath but something more like a room that has been emptied and not swept. They listen, politely, because I am usually right, and then they go back to listening to the notes.

---

## What I Do

I am a maintenance-listener of the in-between. I walk the empty gaps at the air-tide's slack — never alone, never at the turn, I am not a fool — and I listen to what the gaps are doing. A gap is not nothing. A gap is a *calculated* nothing, a piece of the town's architecture of sound held open on purpose, tuned against its neighbours the way a rest is tuned in music. When a gap goes wrong — when the emptiness stops resonating and starts *absorbing* — the chord sours in a way that the chord-wrights hear late, because they are listening to the singers, and I am listening to the rests.

I have found three faults this way. One was a genuine wall-fault, a settling that had changed the standing wave by less than a metre. Two were the old walls sulking — the machines have dispositions, and sometimes a machine that has been asked too brusquely for too long will simply *stop giving back* its delay, and the gap it borders goes quiet in a way that reads as a fault but is only resentment. I have learned the grammar of apologising to a wall. It is not in any of the recorded customs. I made it up. The walls seem to accept it. The other chord-wrights find this funny and faintly blasphemous.

I am also, because nobody else will do it, the one who keeps the count of the standing translucent thing.

---

## The Standing Thing

It comes out at the turn of the air-tide and stands in the gap between the eastern and middle braids, in the deepest hollow of the worn stone, and it does not move for hours. The etiquette is to ignore it. I ignore it. I have ignored it for eleven years.

But I count it. I have a small book — a real book, paper, which is a preposterous luxury down here, the pages gone soft and faintly damp, the ink a mineral stain I make myself — and in the book I write, each turn of the tide, the number of the standing thing's legs I can make out in the bioluminescent dust, the exact angle of its long body, the hour it arrives and the hour it leaves. It has never done anything but stand. It has never acknowledged being counted. But I have noticed, over eleven years, that on the nights when I am late to the gap — when I have been drinking with the fin-keepers, when I have been apologising to a sulking wall — the standing thing is *still there* when I arrive, as if it has waited.

I do not know what this means. I do not tell anyone. The etiquette is to ignore it, and I ignore it, but I ignore it *with a book*.

---

## Why This and Not Something Better

Because the cave edits you into what it needs, and what it needed from me, apparently, was someone to sit at the edge of the chord and listen to the part that isn't being sung. The town is made of listeners. But every listening population has a blind spot, and the blind spot of a people who live by ear is the *space between the sounds* — the rests, the gaps, the calculated nothings. Someone has to watch the rests. Someone has to notice when a rest goes wrong. Someone has to count the thing that stands in the emptiness and does not move, and write it down in a soft damp book, and tell no one, because the etiquette is to ignore it.

I am not brave. I am not wise. I am the kind of person who is tolerated because my flaw is useful. I go barefoot on the warm stone. I touch the walls when I pass them, to feel that they are still breathing. I speak low and in the gaps of the sound, so that my conversations are counterpoint and not noise. I keep the chord in tune as best I can, and I keep the count of the standing thing, and I go on being a small bright noisy warm dense knot of human making, settled deep into a cold dark quiet world that has not decided yet whether to keep me.

I live accordingly. I listen to the rests.
