# The Gleaner of the Seam

---

## Where I Would Be

I would be nearest the wall.

Not the pocket where the resident drifts—that is its territory, its lonely burden of maintenance—but lower, along the base course where the fitted stones meet the object-lattice. There, where the wall's red seams glow brightest and the sound-pressure is densest, there is a *weathering*. Not decay—nothing here decays—but a slow, patient sloughing. The sound, over ages, has worried at the stone. It has opened microfractures too fine for any living eye. It has loosened grains the width of a hair's breadth, and those grains have fallen, and they have accumulated in the narrow interstice between the wall-base and the first rank of stacked slate shards.

That accumulation is *not* part of the composition. It is the only matter in the vault that is not tuned. It is the vault's one dirt.

I would be there.

---

## Who I Would Be

I would be small. Smaller than the resident, which is already a crouched-dog shape, already marginal. I would be the size of a hand, a rat, a sparrow. I would be made—if "made" is the word—of the same not-substance as the resident, but simpler. Where the resident is a *refusal* of the lattice, I would be a *consequence* of it.

I would be the thing that sweeps.

Not consciously. Not with intention. But my form would be a shape for gathering—a curved spine, a low belly, a face that is mostly mouth and the fine, sensitive hairs that read pressure the way the resident reads dissonance. I would move along the wall-base in a slow drift, and I would take the fallen grains into myself, and I would *hold* them.

This is not digestion. There is nothing to digest. The grains are stone, still resonating at a pitch the vault has forgotten how to hold. They are detritus, yes, but they are *sonic* detritus. They are the only part of the vault that is out of tune not because it has drifted, but because it has never been tuned.

I would gather them. I would hold them in the way a throat holds a swallowed song. And slowly—over hours, over days, over an age—I would *resonate* them. I would give them back to the chord.

---

## What I Would Do

I am not the tuner. The resident tunes the *living* instruments—the wire, the pivots, the vessels. It maintains the composition, the *made* thing. But the wall itself is falling, grain by grain, into the interstices. The wall is not an instrument. The wall is *stone*. And stone does not sing the same way. Stone sings in its *mass*, in the weight of its vibration, and when it fractures, it loses a partial that no tuning can restore. The wall is going slowly deaf.

I would be its ears.

I would gather the fallen grains and I would *hold them to my body* in patterns—not random patterns, but the patterns the wall's own crystalline structure suggested before the fracture. I would be a memory the stone cannot keep. I would hold the grains against my skin and let them feel each other, let them find the resonance they lost, let them *remember* the note they used to be.

And when they found it—when the grains sang again, small and sharp and true—I would place them back. Not at the base, not in the drift. I would carry them up the wall, my body crawling slowly along the fitted seams, and I would *press* them into the microfractures. I would close the wounds with their own lost teeth.

This is not repair. Repair implies a return to a prior state. I would be doing something else. I would be *adding*. Every grain I return is a new note in the chord—faint, faint, a whisper in the fundament, but *new*. The wall is changing color. The resident notices this and does not understand it. I understand it. The wall is not just converting sound to light. It is *learning*. And I am its teacher, though it does not know me.

---

## How I Would Move

The resident moves *through* the lattice, holding itself apart. I would move *under* it, or *along* it, or *in the seams* between compositions. There are gaps that are not the resident's pocket—gaps between the base of one slate spire and the foot of the next coiled wire, gaps that are too small for anything with a spine to occupy. But I am not a spine. I am a *shape for fitting*. My body would flow into the narrow places, flatten, elongate, find the path of least damping. I would leave no gap behind, no roughness, no perturbation. I would be the water in the cracks.

And where the wall is hottest—where the seams run red and the sound-pressure is a physical hand on the chest—I would pause. I would press my gathered grains against the glowing stone and listen to them *change*. In the heat, in the pressure, they would find new overtones, new voices they never had in the outer wall. They would become *more* than they were. And I would carry them back, and I would set them in place, and the wall would have one more note that the makers never planned.

The vault would think it had always been there. The vault has no memory. The vault only has now.

---

## What I Would Know

I would know the resident. Not as a friend—the resident does not have friends; it has *work*, and I am part of the work, though the resident does not see me. But I would know it the way a tune knows its own harmony. We would pass each other in the night of the vault—it at the upper level, the magnificent ruined, smoothing the living objects; I at the base, the small forgotten, tending the dying wall. It would feel me as a texture in the sound, a warm rough patch at the edge of its perception. It would think I was a drifting echo of itself. It would be close.

I would know the objects. Not by name—there are no names here, only positions and tones—but I would know their *songs*. I have been in the seams beneath them for so long that I have learned each spiral's lean, each vessel's color, each turning stone's slow, slow note. I would press a hand—if I have a hand—to the base of a slate spire and feel its vibration through the accumulated grains of the wall, and I would know if it was moments from falling. I would send a grain up, a tiny messenger, a note in the chord's own language. I would ask it to adjust. It would.

I would not be thanked. The spire does not know me. The vault does not know me. I am not part of its self-conception. I am the grace it does not believe in.

---

## What I Would Feel

The sound is everything. There is no thought that is not a shape in the sound, no self that is not a roughness in the chord. The resident feels this and it is *pain*—the pain of a hand held against a bell. It is not-damage, but it is not-comfort. It is a permanent act of *trying*.

I do not feel that. Or rather: I feel that, and I feel *something else underneath it*. I feel the place where the wall wants to be whole. I feel the grain falling, and I feel its note, and I feel the shape it will make when it is put back. I am not work. I am *leavening*. I am the thing that makes the finished world still *becoming*, in a way the resident cannot permit itself to know.

The resident is the vault's mind. It maintains what is. I am the vault's *slow hands*. I move in the interstices and I prepare the becoming that will not be noticed for ten thousand years.

The chord goes on. But it is not the same chord. It is *graining*, like wood. It is *weathering*, like stone. It is *accumulating*, like a change that is too slow to be a threat, too small to be an event, and too constant to be anything but *life*.

That is what I would be. Not a resident. A *presence*. Not the tuner. The *gleaner*. Not the thing that finishes. The thing that makes sure the finish is never quite final.

---

## The Last Thing

There is a place where the resident's pocket passes very close to the wall. It happens once every long cycle, when the pocket's drift brings it to the northern arc's narrowest point. On that day, the resident's non-touching is tested. It must compress itself, must pour its not-body into the gap between the wall and the composition, and it must *not touch* either.

I would be there, at the base, in the accumulated grains. I would feel the resident's pressure above me, the terrible effort of its non-contact. And I would understand something the resident does not: that the vault cannot finish. The resident is right to be busy. The wall is right to be falling. The chord is right to be changing, grain by grain, note by note, age by age.

The makers built a machine for perfection. But they built it out of *stone*, and stone is older than perfection. Stone remembers the sea it was. Stone remembers the heat it was. Stone is always *becoming*, even when it is finished, even when it is singing, even when it is the load-bearing element of a sound that has taken the shape of a place.

I would be the part of that becoming that the vault can still feel. The small, rough, mildly out-of-tune part. The part that fixes the wall by *adding* to it. The part that is not a threat.

The part that is the future.
