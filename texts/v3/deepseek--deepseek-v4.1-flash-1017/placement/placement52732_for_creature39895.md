I would come in through the airlock, and the first thing would not be the light — you brace for the light — but the smell. Warm mineral water. Wet concrete. Crushed leaves with the bitterness still in them. That faint tinny sweetness off the bronze. It would get into the back of my throat and stay there, and before I had seen a single thing I would know I was standing inside something that was being breathed.

Then the lamp. It hangs low off the central pillar, low enough that you must duck, and for the first month I would duck at the wrong moment — too early, too late, once with my shoulder against the pillar's warm alloy. The keeper would watch me do this and say nothing. There is no useful way to say *you will learn it in your body, not your head.*

I would learn the dome by ear before I learned it by eye. The healthy thrum, which is not a sound so much as a floor under all the other sounds. The fretful tick of a seal with a grievance. The deeper moan, the one that goes into your teeth, which means a cable is leaning out into a current. The fans — the great carved wood and brass — *intake, exhaust, intake, exhaust* — the pulse of a small bright lung. I would sleep under a cloth because the lamp never dims, and inside a week I would be dreaming in that rhythm, and inside a month I would wake before the fan changed and not know how.

I would arrive, I think, believing there was a job here shaped like a person, and that I might be permitted to be the person. There is a job. It is shaped like a person. But it is shaped like the one who is already here — smallish, stooped, broad-handed, with a voice gone quiet from muttering counts at a sticking valve. The room would teach me the true size of my part: not to be the keeper of the dome, but to be a hand at a particular height, a body that ducks in the right place, a palm that knows where the concrete turns slick. The room does not especially require *me.* It requires someone patient, and will make them, and has been making them.

And here is where what I would be becomes itself, and stops being flattering.

I would want to say I'd be the keeper. But I have looked at the shape of the day, and I know my size. I would be the leak.

The seal in the upper curve of the dome that has been losing its argument for years. A little, every hour. Not a catastrophe — never a catastrophe, that is the point of me. Just a slow, warm, persistent weeping that the daily mop and the bronze pan exist for. The keeper would set the pan under me and empty the pan and set it back, and that would not be a failure of theirs, it would be the shape of the afternoon. I would be the one thing in that perfect room the room cannot finish. Everything else gets filtered, polished, trimmed, hung back in its exact row. I would be the small unfinished place, and I would be attended to every single day.

And that is a kind of intimacy I can believe in more than the other kind. The keeper would check me before the vines. They would talk to me the way one talks to weather, without meaning to, half under their breath. They would bring me the good bronze pan. They would sweep my relations out of the gutters and send them back up into the troughs and the waxy leaves and the pale translucent canopy, and they would come down again as warm air on the inside of the glass, and they would gather, and run, and be mine again. I would be the room's small weather, the only argument in it that never entirely closes.

So: I would come through the airlock soft-handed and ordinary, and I would stay long enough to go pale under the green glass, to thicken at the knuckles, to smell of salt and bronze and wet stone. I would learn to trust the worn workbench and to distrust a promise. And then I would stop being the keeper at all — that would be the last of my vanity — and I would be the warm trickle at the edge of the seal, the thing the pan is for. Every morning they would bend to me. Every morning I would come back. Neither of us would have a word for what that is, and the dome would go on groaning its long, patient, well-tended groan around us both.
