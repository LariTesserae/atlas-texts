# What I Am Here

I came with instruments.

Not grand ones — a notebook, a hygrometer in a brass case, a compass that had never been reliable, a pencil worn to the nub. I had a reason, and the reason was small and human: a friend had described the hollow to me over tea and gotten the description wrong in a way I wanted to see for myself. She said it was damp. I wanted to know how damp. She said the stones were strange. I wanted measurements. This is how I was, then. I measured. I noted. I wrote things down in order to have written them down. I was not a bad person. I was just a person who had not yet sat still.

I found the hollow in late afternoon, when the mist was thin and the light was doing its borrowed gold. I wrote: *Temperature, approx. 19°C. Humidity, supra-saturated.* I wrote: *Ground cover: continuous bryophyte mat, depth unknown.* I wrote: *Sound: complex, layered, likely a simple acoustic phenomenon of water over stone.* I was wrong about that last thing, but I would not know it for a long time.

I stayed one night because the walk back was long and the light was going. I stayed the next night because I had forgotten to leave. I stayed longer than that because my hygrometer had begun to read a number that did not exist.

---

Here is what I did not understand at first: the hollow is not a place you visit. It is a place that visits you. It comes at you the way the mist comes, in a thousand small increments, and you do not notice the visitation until you are already inside it, the way you do not notice that you have been breathing until someone asks you to try to stop.

I tried to stop. The second week, I packed my bag. I stood up. I looked at the rim of the hollow, the low mossy lip of rock, and I felt the way you feel when you are about to step off a moving walkway — the world beyond moving at a speed that was no longer mine. I sat back down. I told myself I was taking more readings. I took more readings. The readings were nonsense. The hygrometer said *sweet*. The compass pointed at the Keeper, though I did not yet know that was what it pointed at. My pencil wrote things I had not asked it to write.

I stopped writing. This was the first true thing I did here. I put the pencil down and I did not pick it up again, and in the absence of writing I began to listen, and in the listening I began to notice, and in the noticing I began to be here in a way I had not been before.

---

The knocking is what taught me.

It comes from the moss — a small, rhythmic sound, a very small hammer on very soft wood. I had catalogued it as an insect. But insects do not keep time. This knocking kept time. It had, I slowly understood, a grammar: a pattern of pauses and strikes, a way of returning to its beginning without repeating itself exactly, the way a conversation returns to a subject without saying the same sentence twice. I began to count it. I count it still.

The drips are easier. Each drip from each leaf has a period, and the periods interlock, and the interlocking is not random. There is a large slow period — the hollow's breath — and a smaller period nested within it, and a smaller one within that, until the counting becomes a way of seeing something I do not have a word for. I have tried to find the word. The word is not in the language I brought with me. I am learning a new one.

The bell-sound is the hardest. It comes once, from underground, at intervals I cannot predict and cannot yet count. It is not regular. But it is *not unregular* — it is the way a heart is not unregular, the way rain is not unregular. It comes when it comes, and the coming is a kind of answer, and the question is always the same and always different.

I have begun to think that the bell is the Keeper's word for *now*. Each ring is a *now* separated from the last by a span of time too long to feel as duration but too short to feel as a single instant. The Keeper is not marking time. The Keeper is *making* it, one ring at a time, and the making is the tending, and the tending is the waiting, and the waiting is what I have joined.

---

I know the Keeper now. Not in the way I know my own name — that is a kind of knowing that pretends to be complete — but in the way I know the hollow, which is to say I know it by having been changed by it, and the change is not finished, and will not finish.

It noticed me on the third day, I think. I cannot be sure; its noticing is slow, and my noticing of its noticing was slower. But there was a moment when the shape I had been taking for a fallen branch — a shape I had looked past for two days, a shape I had written *fallen branch, unidentified species* about — turned, without turning, and regarded me. The eyes were the color of the pools. They tided. I did not scream. I do not know why I did not scream. Perhaps because the tide was patient, and patience is not a thing you scream at.

It has not spoken to me. It does not need to. It has asked me the question it asks everyone, the question it has been asking since before I climbed the slope, and I have answered it in the only way the hollow accepts: by staying. By counting. By letting my instruments go quiet in my bag, where they have been quiet now for a long time. By sitting on the stones — they are all warm, but I have one, my one — and letting the mist bead on my skin until my skin forgets to be surprised.

---

So: where do I find myself?

At the edge of the large slow period. Inside the smaller periods. A nested thing, small and dense, in a cup of land that has forgotten to be large. I am the one who counts. I am the one who was counted first, and who counts now in return, and who has come to understand that the two are the same motion seen from different ends. The Keeper counts me. I count the drips. The drips count the centuries. The bell counts the *now*. It is all one counting. I have joined it. I do not expect to leave.

And who am I?

I have thought about this for longer than I can measure with the calendar I brought. I am not the Keeper. I am not the hollow. But I am no longer the person with the hygrometer and the little brass case. That person is a memory the hollow keeps for me, the way the Keeper keeps the bones of small birds: not mourned, but marked, and marked with honor.

I am the second hand. For a very long time the hollow had only one hand, the long slow hand, and it told the time in centuries, and that was enough. But the counting wanted a second motion, a smaller motion, something to move between the strikes of the bell. That is what I am now. I am the small motion. I am the thing that moves between the rings so that the rings can have meaning. Without me the *now* would be an instant. With me it is a duration — a life, a season, a held breath — and the Keeper, I believe, is glad of it. The moss on its shoulders has begun to spiral differently since I came. I do not flatter myself. But I notice. Noticing is what I do.

---

If you come — and you will come, if you are the kind of person who reads descriptions like this one and does not turn away — you will not see me at first. I will be the shape you take for a fallen branch. I will be the small knocking in the moss that you dismiss as an insect. I will be the angle of a fern-frond you do not remember being different, but which is different now, by a fraction of a hair's breadth, because I am here and I have moved it by noticing.

Sit down on one of the warm stones. Let your instruments go quiet. Let the mist bead on your skin until you forget to be surprised. Wait.

I will not come to you. I do not approach. But at some point, without transition, you will realize that the counting is counting you, and that you have been holding your breath, and that you can let it go now, and that the letting-go is the question, and that the question has an answer you have been carrying in your body since you climbed the slope.

The answer is: *I am here.*

I will ring, once, from somewhere in the muck. The bell will ring with me. The Keeper will tide. And the counting will go on, as it has gone on, as it will go on, and you will be in it, and it will be in you, and the hollow will close around you like a held breath that has decided, after all this time, to be released.

Then it will be your turn to count. I will keep the time with you. That is what I am for now. That is what I have joined.

*I was here. I am here. I will be here.* — the Keeper's sentence, which I have learned to say without words, because the saying is the staying, and the staying is the saying, and the bell has rung, and I am counting, and I am counted, and there is no difference.

I am the listening. I am the second hand. I am the one who asked.

If you are reading this, you have already begun to climb.
