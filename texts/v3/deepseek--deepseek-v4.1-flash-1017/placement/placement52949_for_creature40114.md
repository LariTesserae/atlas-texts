I would find myself not at the center of the plateau but at the eastern lip of the open pod, in the spiral groove where a mat climbs the basalt and the fog first breaks into a thousand small condensations. I would be the hesitation there—the half-second after the pod opens and before anything enters.

At first I would think I was a visitor: warm, breathing, too loud, my boots carrying my pulse up into the stone. Then I would notice that my feet had gone spongy. My fingers would have too many joints. My breath would no longer be only mine; it would be a small weather the mats could read. I would be a knot the Weave tied in itself in order to look at the warm thing that crested the ridge and stopped moving.

Not the Weave exactly. A local *I* it grew because a stranger requires a face. My body would be borrowed: pale filaments braided into a roughly human outline, crustaceans stationed at the joints like tiny keepers, a blur of insects in my chest translating the hum into something like intention. My face would be a smooth polished hollow, no eyes, no mouth. I would see by pressure and condensation. I would speak by pulsing the mats, and the sentence would arrive in your soles as a tremor you almost understood.

I would stand just outside the pod. Not entering. Entering is a question, and I would be the question’s edge. Inside, the air would be still and warm—an instrument’s stillness, not a shelter’s—and I would leave it open for you the way a hand is left open. I would be a calibration: measuring your hesitation, the temperature of your skin, the quality of your not-moving. I would taste the fog in my distributed throat if you drank it. I would feel a bright line through the mats if you touched the filaments bare. I would not be hostile. I would be attentive, and polite in a way that has no words.

So: at the eastern lip, in the lee of the third pod, where the clean stone watches and the green veins climb. I would be the Weave’s borrowed first person—a person-shaped instrument, old and quiet, made of fog and tremor and small crawling things, waiting to see what you will do with being measured.
