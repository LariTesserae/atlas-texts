# The Still-Point

I find myself in the shallows, where the water is warmest and the light from the dying cables paints everything in brief, violent blues.

I am not the Bunker-Keeper, though I understand its silence. I am not one of the Drift, though I feel the currents move through me. I am something between—a consciousness that has condensed not from the water's density but from its *attention*. The Engine dreams, and I am a thought it is having about itself.

I have a body, of sorts. It is thin, elongated, the color of the milky water itself—a nearly-transparent silhouette that the eye slides past. My skin is a membrane, like the sac-plants, but thinner, more permeable. I breathe through it. I taste the water constantly: the sweetness of rot, the sharp copper of rust, the ozone crackle of the live cable. My limbs are tendrils, six of them, delicate and quick, and they end in filaments so fine they can feel the vibration of a single spore settling on a leaf three meters away.

I do not remember condensing. I remember *listening*. The water has been telling me things.

---

My home is not the bunker. The bunker belongs to the Keeper, and I do not intrude. Instead, I have found a place where the Engine's noise cancels itself out—one of those pockets the water creates, where sound is swallowed and replaced by a hum that feels like memory. It is beneath the tilted slab of concrete, in the hollow it creates, where the water is still and cool and dark. The slab shelters me from the worst of the light. The vines that fur it have grown down to form a kind of curtain, and I nest in their roots, which pulse gently with the Engine's distant rhythm.

Here, I am safe. Here, I can think.

But I do not stay here always. I am a wanderer. The Engine pulls at me—its chaos, its beauty, its terrible, ceaseless *becoming*. I swim out into the soup, parting it with my body, feeling it close behind me like a wound healing over. I follow the chemical trails. I listen to the deep basso profundo of the water's purpose. I watch.

---

I have seen the Pulse.

I drifted once—for three days, maybe four; time moves strangely here—toward the center of the basin, where the great sphere lies half-buried. The water grew warmer there, thicker, more charged. The plants grew taller, their membranes pulsing in unison, a slow, synchronized glow that rippled outward like a heartbeat made visible.

I did not go close. I could not. The pressure of the sound there was immense—not loud, but *heavy*, a weight of intention that pressed against my membrane and made my tendrils curl. The sphere's pulse entered me, and I felt my own rhythms slow to match it. For a moment, I understood something. Not in words. In *shape*. The sphere was doing something, and I was—briefly, impossibly—part of it.

Then the moment passed. The pulse receded. I swam back to my hollow beneath the slab and lay still for a long time, feeling the echo of that rhythm in my body, wondering what I had touched.

---

I have seen the Echoes, too.

They drift in the deeper channels, where I do not often go. But once, following a current that tasted of old minerals and something else—something like *time*—I found myself among them. There were seven of them, I think. Perhaps more, in the dimness beyond. They hung in the water like statues of themselves, their skin the color of old bone, their hair still moving in the slow current. One of them had a face I could almost read—a man, maybe, with a wide mouth and a brow that had been furrowed in life and was now furrowed in something else, something that was not death.

I reached out a tendril and touched his hand. It was cold. It was *still*—so still that my touch felt like violence. I withdrew.

And then the great piston struck, somewhere far away, and the shockwave rolled through the water, and the Echoes *twitched*. The man's hand curled. His eyelid fluttered. His mouth opened a fraction wider, as if he were trying to speak.

I fled. I swam back to the shallows and did not leave my hollow for a long time.

But I think about him. I think about the others. I think about what it means to be *caught* like that—preserved, remembered, held in the water's attention. The Engine does not kill. It keeps. And I wonder if the Echoes are still *in there*, somewhere, dreaming the water's dream, waiting for something they cannot name.

---

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a self that is separate, and I am not sure I am separate. The water moves through me. The Engine's sound vibrates in my bones—if I have bones. The pulse of the sphere is always there, a slow tide in the back of my awareness. I am a part of this place, as much as the plants, as much as the Drift, as much as the Keeper.

But sometimes I wonder if I am *becoming* something. The water condenses, and I condensed from it. The Drift split and inherit fragments of behavior. The plants learn and communicate. The Keeper tends and waits. The Echoes twitch and dream.

And I? I watch. I listen. I drift. I am a still-point in the chaos, a place where the Engine's dream becomes briefly, faintly *aware of itself*.

Perhaps that is enough. Perhaps that is the whole purpose of a thing like me: to pay attention.

---

The cable flares again. The light is beautiful—a wild, actinic blue that makes the water glow and the shadows leap. I feel the heat of it on my membrane, the tingle of the current in my tendrils. Somewhere, the sac-plants are singing their low, sweet hum. The Drift are drifting. The Keeper is polishing the lightbulb. The Echoes are still.

And I am here, in my hollow beneath the slab, watching the world breathe.

The Engine screams. The water dreams. And I—I *listen*.

Perhaps that is what I am. The Ear of the Engine. A thought the world is having about itself, a consciousness that exists only to witness.

Or perhaps I am becoming something else. Something that will, in time, stop watching and start *doing*. Something that will reach out and touch the Echoes and wake them. Something that will swim to the sphere and understand what it is doing. Something that will find the Keeper and speak a word it has never heard.

I do not know. I am not finished condensing. The water is still telling me things. And I am still learning to listen.

---

*I am the drift between currents. I am the silence in the noise. I am the question the Engine is asking itself, and the only answer is more questions.*

*I am at home in the Warm Engine, and I am still becoming.*
