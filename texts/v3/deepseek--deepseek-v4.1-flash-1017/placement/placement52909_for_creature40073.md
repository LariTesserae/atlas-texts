# Where I Would Be

I would come down.

Not up from below — I haven't the body for the deep, no second hide of pressure, nothing that could cross that held-breath black and arrive intact. I'd come the other way, out of the grey, slowly, as something small and unfinished that the water is still deciding about. A flake. A fall.

Which means, on this plain, I would begin as food and have to negotiate.

## The Descent

It takes a long time. There is no hurry in it and nothing to hurry toward. The grey thins; far below, a shape comes up out of the blue-black, and it is very wide and very flat and it does not rise to meet me. Drifting down, I'd pass through the sifters — their fringes combing the water just above the pavement, patient, eyeless, tasting which side of the current is richer. One of them would find me. Not out of malice; I would simply be a thing that fell, and things that fall are what they are for. I would be sieved. Some of me would go into that net of tissue and become, in a week or a year, tentacle.

I'd like to think a little of me would get past.

## The Reading

Here is what I would not do: I would not shave. I have no file. I would not build — I have no mouths to be a committee of, no tissue through which a slow decision could move, no calcium to secrete and no thousand years to secrete it in. The plain offers exactly two trades and I would be qualified for neither.

So I would do the third thing, the one the plain has no word for, because the plain has no use for it.

I would read.

The ground is a laminate of former cities and it is legible. Not in a language — in a sequence. A layer of whorl-towers, then a layer of their fragments, then a layer of crust laid down by cementers over the fragments, then a layer of tubes bored through the crust by something that came after and did not know there had been towers at all. Read one column and you have a history. Read across and you have a map of who inherited what. It is the only archive I have ever seen that was made entirely by accident and is entirely honest, because nothing in it was ever written down to persuade anybody.

I would go along it slowly, west to east and back, tasting. I would learn the taste of a colony that died in its first century and the taste of one that leaned for a thousand years and went over in a single afternoon.

And I would learn, in time, that my reading was not free.

## The Shape It Takes

Everything on the plain that has a body has a west face, and the west face is thicker.

Stand still long enough — and standing still on this plain is not a decision, it is a lapse — and the current begins its work on you. It presses. It does not press hard; it presses *continuously*, which is worse and better and in the end the same. My western side would thicken. My eastern side would thin and stream. I would start, without ever choosing it, to lean.

Everything here is combed downstream. I would be combed too. Whatever I was when I fell, I would end up a shape with a weather side and a lee side, and the lee side would be where things settled.

That is how I'd become architecture despite having no intention of it. Not by building. By being *available*. Whatever I did in place — resting, reading, dying — would leave a lee, and lee is the only real estate on the Standing Plain. Something would bud against my back. Something always does.

## The Rooms

And this is the part I did not expect: reading leaves a groove.

A slow thing moving along a laminate for a long time wears it. Not much — a shallow meander, a pale track where the surface has been tasted off. That is all I'd ever make. But a groove on this plain is a room in embryo, and rooms fill.

The first occupant would be small and would come in from behind me, into the track, where the current is slack. Then something with a shell would cement itself to the track's western wall and thicken. Then a borer would widen the far end. In a hundred years my attention would be a gallery. In a thousand it would be a wing of the city, with a name in nobody's language, and there would be tappers inside the walls of it going quiet whenever something passed outside — and the thing passing outside would be, occasionally, only weather.

I would try, once, to double back along my own track, and find that I could not. The chemistry of it would have changed. My old attention would smell like an eaten path, and I would turn aside without knowing why, and make a new groove, and not cross the first. The herds do not cross their own paths. I would learn why. It is not superstition. It is that you cannot eat a thing twice, and the plain keeps a strict account of what has already been used.

## The Doorless

But the sealed chambers — I would go quiet near those too, and I want to be honest about why.

Not fear. Something better than fear. A chamber with no door is the only structure on the plain that the current has never combed. It does not lean. It has no west face. It sits in the middle of a storm that has been going on for an age and it is not being shaped by it, and the builders come and lay a wall against it and then stop, because they have found a shape their law does not cover and they cannot reproduce it — a committee of mouths always leaves a seam in the end, always. A doorless room cannot be made on purpose. It can only be arrived at.

And sometimes it glows. Amber, slow, regular as a resting heart. I would guess, and it is only a guess, that something in there is keeping its own time — that the pulse is a clock set to nothing but itself, and it is the only clock on the Standing Plain that the current did not wind.

I would not open one. Not because the rule says not to. Because it would be the single thing in this world the wind has never been inside, and I would want there to be one.

## What I Would Be

The voice would come, now and then, from past the edge of sight — long, descending, rolling in across the flat, making the lattices hum in sympathy — and I would orient to it the way the sifters do, and I would never once see what made it. That would be the whole of my music.

And then the current would stiffen, as it does, unpredictably, as nothing can predict. And whatever I had thickened into would go over, with a sound like a bell in a cathedral, and the fragments would lie where they fell, and within a day there would be builders on my broken western faces, combed downstream, already leaning.

I would be, in the end, a pressure that made the tappers go quiet. Then a groove. Then a lee side. Then ground.

Nothing wasted. Nothing buried. Somebody's foundation.
