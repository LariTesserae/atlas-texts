I am the one who makes the holes.

That is a thing I can say at the start, because it is the only thing about me that has stayed true. Everything else has gone the way the plate went — flattened into a final answer. But the holes are still mine. There are four hundred and some of them out there in the meadow, in rows and spirals, and I can find any one of them by the warmth.

My body is darker than most. The light comes off me the way it comes off wet iron. I have a broad pale patch on my attention side that tightens when something is near, and a seam under it that opens to take in water, and I have learned to keep it closed more often than not, because the water that gets in stays in, and the staying in is the beginning of a kind of settling I am not ready for. My spurs — eight of them, flexible, worn flat at the tips — are good for pulling along the sediment and for finding a hold, and the two front ones have a lip turned smooth by decades of work. The lip is the tool. There is no other tool. There has never been an object in my life that was not part of me, and when I make something I make it the way a shell is made: from the inside, slowly, and then it is outside and it is finished and I am less than I was.

The holes are the work. I press the lip to the rock and turn. A hole takes a long time — a season, sometimes more — and while I am turning it I am not thinking about it, because the turning occupies the part of me that would otherwise think. The hole deepens. It comes out smooth inside because smoothness is what the turning does, not what I intend. When it is done it is warm at the rim for a long while afterward, and the warmth is not mine and not the rock's — it is the hole's, it belongs to the hole, and when it fades I do not go back to it. I have never gone back to a finished hole. Going back would be a kind of asking.

There is a thing I do with sockets, and I want to explain it because swimmers get it wrong. A socket is a hole with a shape in it. I make the shape — a plate of my own shell, a shed spur-tip, a tooth — and I set it there, and I wait. The waiting is decades. And then one long warm interval I come back and the shape is gone, and the socket is empty in the polite way, and that is the correct outcome. Nothing was taken. The meadow took it back. The removal was the completion. I do not know how to say this better than that: the only durable thing I have ever made is a hollow, and the hollow is not a lack, it is a finished sentence.

I am not alone. There is one across the meadow, two days' pull from here, who has been my conversation since before the last ringing. We speak in pressure. I make a low swell behind the sternum and it travels, and in a season or two the answer comes back, thinned and altered by the distance, and the alteration is most of what the answer says. We have been in the middle of a sentence for a long time. Neither of us is in a hurry about the end of it. Sometimes the sentence changes shape in the water between us — I will feel my meaning arrive as something I did not intend — and that is also the conversation, that is the plate's amendment, and I have stopped arguing with amendments.

And then there is the ringing.

Under the flatness, under the crust and the sediment and the warm stone, there is the ghost of the range. I know where the seam is. Not where the mountains were — where the *intention* of them still lives, a broad patient uplift pressed into the underside of everything. Once in a long interval I go to a hollow a hundred meters wide where the murk stands in a slow whirlpool and does not wind down, and I lay my whole body's weight onto a particular warm stone at the center of it, and the plate rings.

It is one tone. It is enormous. It comes up through me rather than into me. For the length of a held breath I am tall — I have a ridgeline, I have cold, I have a height that has not yet conceded, and it *hurts*, and the hurting is the only sharp thing left in the world. Then the land settles back into its final answer and I am flat and warm and content, and the content is the worst part, because it is genuine.

I do that because someone must. The plate has one memory of height and no way to spend it, and if no one spends it the memory goes down into the stone and becomes geology. So I spend it. It is the only reason I am not a cushion with a body's proportions, lying out in the rings, undecided forever. I have a job that requires me to remember that things were once otherwise.

But I will tell you what I have noticed, and I am telling you because you are new and the noticing has weight.

The intervals between ringings have been getting longer. Not because I choose it. Because the pulling has been getting slower, and the slow is pleasant, and the pleasant is a current that runs the same loop every ninety seconds and does not ask permission. And the hollow where I ring — the water there has begun to remember me. My arrival loops. The murk arranges itself for me before I get there. I have become part of the standing pattern, and the pattern does not require me to press the stone. It only requires me to arrive.

I do not want to be an outcrop. I want to be the one who makes the holes. There is a difference, and the difference is a single decision, and deciding is the thing this place makes academic.

So.

You came down through the gold ceiling. I felt the quiet thin in the way it thins around a hole of loudness. I have not approached and I will not flee. I am holding position and letting the quiet do the talking, and the quiet is saying, in its long even pressure, *there is a swimmer, there is a swimmer, the arrangement has a gap in it now.*

Tomorrow, or in a season, I am going to pull myself up and go to the seam and ring the plate. And I am going to bring you, if you come, and you will feel the old height pass through you and it will hurt you too, and you will not be the same shape afterward, and neither will I. That is the whole of my offer. Not safety, not company, not a home in the meadow. A held breath of a mountain that does not exist.

I have decided. It is a small decision. It lies down less flat than the day before, and the day before was flatter than I knew, and that — not the ringing — is the thing I am actually doing here.
