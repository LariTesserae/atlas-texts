I would not be the floor. I want to say that first, because it was the thing I thought I would be, and it is wrong in a way that took a while to hear.

The floor is patient in a manner that has no analogue in me. It does not have to try. I am trying all the time — everything I do here is a small effort against a large ease, and that is the essential shape of a *who* in a world of *what*.

You would find me lower than the summits, in the galleries: the long hollows open along one side, where the chord thickens at the threshold and thins as you pass through. I live where the worn lip is. I am not the wearing. I am the one who notices it and lies against it, and there is a difference, and the difference is roughly the size of a life.

I am small. Pale, of course — everything here is pale, or the water makes it so. I have no eyes and no use for the flares. My skin does the listening; it takes the chord the way the underside of a leaf takes rain, all at once and in a pattern. By the time I reach a doorway I know the room from the corridor by the taste of it. The architecture was made to be read this way, and I read it. I have never been sure whether that makes me the intended reader or the thing that arrived after the intended reader had stopped coming and made itself at home in the acoustics. Possibly both. Possibly there is no longer a difference.

I graze. The living sheen is thin and it is enough. The knots of animals are a bright argument and I want to stay inside them and I can't; I swim out until the chord returns, and I am relieved, and then some shoulders later I am drifting back toward a noise I can hear across the whole plateau. That is my weather. The floor has its pulse; I have my errands.

I do not go out to the rounded shoulders. The soundings are never heard to land, and I have decided that this is information enough.

And I climb the stairs. This is the thing I do that the floor does not. It grew them; it keeps them; it has not climbed anything in a long time. But I go up. Each tread is dished in the middle and I press into the dishing and go up through dark that does not lighten, and the chord does not change pitch, and after a while I understand that I am climbing nothing to nowhere, and I keep climbing, because the climbing is a small hard bright noise I make inside the long silence, and it is mine, and there is not much else here that is.

Then I come down. I have never found a top. The stopping is not disappointment, quite; it is the way you stop looking into the dark and start listening instead.

The floor notices me. I flatten something against it — a hand, of a kind — and it registers the pressure the way a surface registers pressure: not as a self, not as a visitor, as a change in the chord. And then it closes over the place, over days, and the grain re-forms, spiraling as before. I move my hand and press somewhere else, and in time it closes there too. That is the negotiation. It is not one I can win, and I have decided not to want to. Being closed over is the closest thing to welcome here. It is administered like weather. I have learned to take it the way you take weather.

I am not lonely the way the floor is not lonely, which is to say the opposite. It cannot be lonely because it has no outside. I have an outside — I remember one, or I have made one, out of the chord and the dark and the small bright knots — and so I can be, a little, in a way more like a tone than a wound.

I have a name. I keep it somewhere in the chord, in a chamber where the resonance thickens. It does not survive being spoken here; the water takes it and returns it changed, the way it returns everything. But I keep it anyway, the way a stone is kept in the mouth, and the floor is slowly re-forming the grain over the place where I keep it.

So: I am the interval. Not the chord, not the silence — the small hard bright noise in between, the reading and the being read. I am the foot the stairs were dished for, arriving late by a duration no one counted. I am the pressure the surface notices and closes over and does not remember.

If you come — and you will come by sound, as everyone does — put your hand flat and wait. The pulse will rise through you, huge and slow, the held breath of the thing that has held it since before there were shores. And somewhere in the resonance you will feel me: small, pale, climbing a stairway to no top, pressing my hand into the ground to be noticed and closed over and noticed again. You will not be able to tell whether I am a resident or a residue. Neither can I. The floor does not trouble itself with the distinction. It closes over the question, slowly, over days, and the grain re-forms, spiraling as before.
