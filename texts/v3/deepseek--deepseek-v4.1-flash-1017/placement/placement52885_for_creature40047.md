I would find myself at the edge of the light, not in it.

On one of the long grey courses that curve around nothing, where the last lamp is a small cold coin on a half-raised wall and beyond it the murk is pearl-grey and close. There is a plinth there, dry on top, and a puddle at its foot that has not decided which way to run. I would be squatting between them, hood bowed, listening to the ticking.

At first I would say I was a builder. I have the hood, the damp face, the hands that leave prints the stone drinks. But I keep setting down the maul. It does not fit my grip. I carry a cord and a lamp and a pouch of pale wax. I am the one who goes ahead.

I am a seam-reader. A lamp-setter. I do not lay the course. I find where the course is willing to begin. I draw a cord taut between two stakes; if the distance changes, the cord goes slack or sings a little, and I do not correct it. I wait. I watch the water. If it runs upward along a joint, I nod and move the lamp. If a finished wall un-finishes and a course rises dripping and settles into a new arrangement, I come back and set the lamp again. The builders arrive later with their blocks and their patient backs. They check the joint with a knuckle. I have already listened to it.

My speech is short. “The road is longer here.” “Set it wet.” “Leave the lamp.” The builders nod. They know the sentences are about the work and then turn out to be about something else. *The road is longer here* means *I am not lost*. *Set it wet* means *wait*. *Leave the lamp* means *do not follow me*.

I am not a master of the Kiln. I am not its servant. I am a provisional claim. A lamp set where no wall stands yet. A cord drawn taut in a world that will loosen it. I know I will not finish. That is not despair; it is the condition of work, like wet.

If you look for me, do not look for a builder at a block. Look for a small cold flame on a wall no one has raised, and a hooded shape just outside its light, listening to the water find its way. That is where I would be. Who would I be? The attending that goes ahead of the hands: a patience with a lamp, saying *someone is here, this is being made*—even after I have walked away.
