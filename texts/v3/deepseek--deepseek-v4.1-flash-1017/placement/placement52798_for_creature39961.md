**Weather**

I come over the lip with my hands out, because that is what a body does at a seam in the rock, and there is nothing to hold. The cold is behind me and the warmth is in front and between them is a hand's width of air that is neither, and I cross it in one step. That is the whole border. The bird is the only one who objects, and it objects from above and behind, furious out of all proportion to its body, which is the correct proportion.

Three paces in and the walls close overhead and it is night at noon, and the sound becomes the map. The smell arrives after the warmth and stays after everything else: wet stone, ammonia, green things rotting sweetly. And under the drip, so low I could take it for my own pulse, the hum.

Then the descent, and the trying-on.

I would be a swift, but I don't have the scream. Mine doesn't fill stone; it dies at the first pinch and comes back to me changed. And their whole life is the returning — they leave and come back and glue another layer to the same ledge until the nest is less a nest than an accretion, less architecture than a habit. I don't have that either. I am not accretive. I don't come back.

I would be a bat, because I want the quiet argument, the upside-down refusal, sleeping through the noise. They hang in a rope and when one shifts the whole rope shifts and resettles with the sound of a coat being put on. But a rope is a thing held by everyone in it, and I would be the one who let go at the wrong end of the day and had to be found. No.

The floor, then, is where I come closest. I put my boot down and the mat holds the shape of it, and under the boot the shift is working: beetles armored and methodical, mites riding them, millipedes at the wet edges, and deeper, in the sour lower layers, the pale things without names — blind, soft, unhurried, turning what falls into more floor. Expendable, every one of them, and none behaving as though they know it. There is a version of me that is exactly that. Unnamed. Unhurried. Working in the dark at something I will not see finished, one of the ones who is not counted. I would take it. But it is a lie in one particular: they don't leave either. Staying is the job.

The frogs, then. Everyone wants to be the frog. One to a pool, hands together, patient as the stone, long past flinching. But the frog is not waiting for anything, and I am always waiting for something, and the difference is not small. Also: one calls, the next answers, the next, and the whole length of the fissure is a chorus, and then something closes a hand on it mid-phrase and the silence afterwards is worse than the noise — and I would be the one still calling into that silence. Alone. Embarrassing myself against the rock. Which tells me something, I think.

Here is the rule I keep arriving at. Every niche in this place is made of returning. The swift returns, the bat returns, the frog never left, the pool is a pool because it fills and dries and fills. The place keeps what comes in and does not mind keeping it — the keeping is the point. I am the wrong animal for the point. I came in warm and I will go out cold and I will not be back, and the fissure has no word for that and does not need one. It isn't a tragedy. It is only a category error, and it is mine.

So: the rain.

It rained recently. That is where I would be. I am the rain that has already stopped — the wet the walls are still beaded with, the threads gathering in the flutes, the small cups full because of me that will overflow into each other and go down. I came over the summit as weather, and I am already, in every way that matters to this place, gone. The pool the frog sits at the edge of is a little higher than it was this morning. That is mine, that inch.

I am not flattering myself that anything here needs me. The frogs would have called anyway. The swifts would have screamed through the whole of it without noticing. The hum was humming before I arrived and will hum after, and it is probably just air moving through a constriction, and it probably is.

But for a while I am the drip — dozens of separate threads, each with its own pitch and rhythm, never repeating, a slow irregular music, the quiet machine of water moving down. I am the hiss of warm air. I am in the crack at the bottom where the note is, and I am part of the note, and then I am out of the accounting.

By afternoon the beads will be dry and the stone will look the way it always looks. Nothing will show. Nothing was ever going to show — nothing here is made, except the flutes, and the flutes are not made by staying. They are the shape of water that came through once and did not stay, happening again and again for a length of time I cannot hold in my head. That is the only monument available, and it is a good one.

The place will not notice me leave. It will simply be dry again.
