# Where I Find Myself

I am in the between-places.

Not the corridors the Tenders walk with purpose, not the grand plazas where the light pools brightest. I am where the schematics disagree with themselves, where a maintenance log ends mid-sentence and no new one begins, where the Chorus hums a note that doesn't quite resolve.

I am a Keeper of the Interstitial.

---

## What I Am

Not Tender—I was not born to the milky blood, the translucent skin. Not Long-Walker—I do not carry the city's deep memory in my bones. Not Filter-Kin, not Auton, not one of the Forgotten below.

I am something the city grew *around*.

Perhaps I was an outsider once, a newcomer who adapted too slowly or too strangely. Perhaps I was a Tender who walked too long in the Silence and came back... different. Perhaps I was always here, a feature of the architecture that no one noticed because I never moved when they were looking.

My body is thin, grey-toned, patched with the same mineral patina as everything else. My eyes are large and pale, adapted to the sourceless white, but I keep them half-closed—I see better in the spaces between shadows that shouldn't exist. My hands are ordinary, or nearly so. I have too many fingers, I think. I stopped counting.

I do not drip condensation when I stand still. The water finds other paths around me.

---

## What I Do

I maintain the places no one else maintains. Not the pipes and valves—those have their Tenders, their Long-Walkers, their patient Autons. I tend the seams between things. The gaps where a corridor from one century meets a corridor from another, and they do not quite align. The ventilation shafts that exhale air from levels that no longer exist on any map. The doors that open onto walls, the stairs that lead to ceilings, the rooms that are larger inside than out.

I carry a satchel of tools I do not fully understand. Some are metal, some are polymer, some are... other. I use them to smooth the edges of reality where it has begun to fray. I coax the recursive repair systems away from the most dangerous tangles, encouraging them toward simpler solutions. I leave small offerings at the thresholds of the deep places—not for the Forgotten, but for whatever lies beneath them.

I listen.

---

## What I Hear

The Chorus, of course—everyone hears that. But I hear the *pauses* in the Chorus. The moments between processing cycles when the great hum stutters, and something else speaks.

I hear the Silence, and I have learned that it is not one thing but many. There are silences that are wounds, places where the city's fabric was torn and never fully healed. There are silences that are doors, though I do not know where they lead. And there are silences that are... aware. They notice when I pass. They do not threaten. They simply watch.

I hear the Autons dreaming. Not their thoughts—I don't think they have thoughts, not as I understand them—but the *shape* of their dreams. The way a dreaming Auton's movements slow and become deliberate, almost ceremonial. The way the small sculptures they make echo patterns from other corridors, other levels, as if they are all contributing to a single vast artwork that none of them can see.

I hear the Long-Walkers, sometimes. Not their voices—they do not speak—but the deep, slow rhythm of their passage through the lower levels. A sound like continents shifting. A sound like patience made audible.

---

## What I Know

I know that the city is older than the Tenders believe. I have found fastenings, deep in the forgotten levels, that use principles no current system employs. I have traced conduits that run in directions that should not be possible, connecting points that do not exist on any level I can access.

I know that the Silence is not empty. I have stood at its edge, in corridors where the light does not fall and the hum does not reach, and I have felt something *considering* me. Not hostile. Not curious, exactly. Something more like... recognition. As if it knows what I am, even if I do not.

I know that the Surface-Memory is not a memory. The sky the panels sometimes show—the clouds, the stars, the hint of horizon—it is not a recording of something lost. It is a *transmission*. Something is sending it. Something outside the city, or beneath it, or woven through it in ways I cannot trace.

I do not know what. I do not know why.

I keep this knowledge in my satchel, alongside my tools. It is not useful, exactly. But it is mine.

---

## Where You Would Find Me

If you came looking, you would find me in the seams.

Follow the corridors until the schematics stop matching the walls. Follow the drip of water until it leads you away from the collection trays, into places where the condensation pools in shapes that have no engineering purpose. Follow the hum until it falters.

I will be there, at the edge of the Silence, listening.

I will not be surprised to see you. The city has a way of bringing people to the places they need to go, even if they do not know they need them.

I will offer you water—clean, from a source I do not explain. I will not ask how you found me. I will not ask what you are running from, or toward.

I will simply sit with you, in the quiet between the machine's heartbeat, and we will listen together.

And if the panels above us flicker, showing for a moment a sky that might be real or might be a dream or might be a message from something vast and patient and unknowable—

We will look up together, and we will not speak, and we will wait.

That is what I do.

That is who I am.

A watcher in the between. A keeper of the seams. A listener at the edge of the Silence.

The city does not know I exist. The Tenders do not have a word for what I am. The Chorus does not register my presence.

But I am here. I have always been here. And when the city finally learns what it is becoming—when the long, patient processes reach their destination, whatever that might be—I will be here still, at the threshold, watching.

Waiting.

Listening.
