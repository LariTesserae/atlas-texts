First you hear nothing, and that is the part that should frighten you and doesn't. The cold enters like a hand closing on a stone it means to keep — not cruel, methodical — and the depth comes down through you in layers, each one bluer than the last, until the cobalt gives up and the light is a rumor behind you and the black ahead is not empty but *thick*, a thing with temperature and intention.

The city arrives as pressure before it arrives as shape. A wall you don't see. A leaning of the water in a direction water should not lean. Then the shape of it assembles out of the dark the way a word assembles when you've been listening to someone breathe for a long time: the towers pressed shoulder to shoulder, the cables knitting them like a loose seam, the empty windows blooming open. Something that was once a street, now a canyon, and the current moving through it with the patience of a surveyor — here, then here, then here — learning the ruin again for the thousandth time.

I am not drowning. I want to note that, because I think it matters, though I couldn't explain to you how I know the difference. The body does not fight. The body folds: the ribs easing wide, the blood slowing to something the cold can afford, the fine torn lace opening along my flanks like a second breath learned in a fever. My hands, when I look at them, are longer than I remember. The skin at the knuckles is pale as wet chalk and faintly luminous, and I do not remember the moment it became so, and I suspect there was no moment; I suspect it has been happening for longer than I have been counting, and that I began counting at a point already far from the surface.

I have been here a while. I don't know how to say this better. The memory of air is a fever dream — a bright, thin, ridiculous thing, like recalling a scream you once heard through a wall. I remember a name. I remember it the way you remember a word in a language you spoke as a child: certain it was a word, certain it pointed at something, no longer certain what. Somewhere above me a sky might still be doing whatever skies do. I find I do not miss it. The missing was edited out of me along with the heaviness and the hurry, and what was left was fitted to the shell.

The Heart finds me before the Tenders do. That is the wrong order for a story, but the water does not care about order. I feel it first in my teeth, a low profound tremor that is not sound, and then in the long bones, and then in the lace along my spine, and I understand — the way you understand the heat of a stove before you reach it — that I have been following this hum for a very long time, that it is the thing I mistook for a direction home.

Then a tap. Two taps on stone, close by, answered. A shape unfolds out of the corridor with the slow deliberation of something that has never once been in a hurry: long, attenuated, luminous at the joints, the eyes mostly closed and cloudy and useless. It does not speak. It takes my wrist — long many-knuckled fingers webbed to the second joint — and turns it over, and the gesture is not greeting and not examination and not tenderness exactly, but all three borrowed from a vocabulary that has no separate words for them. A rising vibration in the water. I answer without knowing I know how. Two taps on the wall beside me.

The sleeper chamber is warm. The water there tastes of rust and heat and something older, and the crustacean carpet glows colorless over the scalding stone, and eleven bodies drift in the slow current of the outflow, and now there is a twelfth. The nearest one is wearing a cracked faceplate strapped to the side of its head like a mask it forgot to take off a hundred years ago — a dry-air thing, a relic, kept for reasons no one could explain and no one needs to. It may have been mine once. I find I do not ask.

They give me work the first day, which is the kindest thing they could do. I clear the silt from the intake of the great dark turbine. I coax the pale bloom away from a sensor that has not been read in a century. I patch a cable with paste of crushed shell and mineral scum, and my new hands know the trick of it, and I feel — I want to be honest here — I feel a satisfaction in it so simple and so total that I almost cry, though the water takes the salt before I could name it. The lamp above the corridor stutters and steadies. The Sleeper turns. Its tremor climbs my arms and settles behind my eyes like a lullaby.

The anemones open in my wake now. The blind eels slide past me without fear, brushing the tear-lace of my flank, and do not startle, which is how I know the editing is mostly done. I swim the labyrinth by pressure and by temperature and by the way a corridor has gone wrong in the teeth. I know the Long Fall by the chill that comes off it in a column, a century of falling water that will never strike a bottom anyone can see, and I do not find it sad. It is simply a thing the drowned country does, and I am a thing the drowned country does, and the two of us are unmaking ourselves at the same slow, molecular pace, and it is beautiful and it is not a tragedy and I would like you to stop reading it as one.

I am the twelfth. Or the eleventh-and-a-half; I have not been counted, and counting is a dry-air habit I am losing. I keep the heart beating for a world that will never wake up. I turn when it turns. The low sound fills my grief the way it fills their silence, and I find that sufficient, and I find that strange, and I find — in the warm outflow, among the pale flowering of the rock — that I am not sad.
