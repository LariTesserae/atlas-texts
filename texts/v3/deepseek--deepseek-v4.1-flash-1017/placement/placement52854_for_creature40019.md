**I am the ladder, and I am mostly the wear on myself now.**

Hazel, cut green in a winter when the sap was down so it would hold straight. Two uprights, four rungs, lashed at the joints with twisted bark. Somebody's hands did that and the hands knew their business — the twist runs one way on the left upright and the other on the right, so the whole thing pulls tight against itself instead of racking when you put your weight on it. I mention this because it is the sort of thing that is about to stop mattering.

I hang over the lip of the hole, angled down into the warm wet. Three rungs in the air. One in the dark. The rot took the bottom first, the way rot does — up from the standing water, not down from the weather.

A man counted me. Not aloud, I think, or maybe aloud, the way you do when the dark is deep enough to want a voice in it. *One. Two. Three.* He was going down and the counting was for himself and the counting was *of me*. I want that understood. Whatever else is taken here — the twist, the joint, the straightness — I had the numbers. They were addressed to me.

Fourth rung, right side, the bark gone down to the pale: a boot-edge, worn smooth. Years or one afternoon, I can't tell; wear is wear. The notches in the root mat by the entry are the same wear, in a different medium. His hands, learning where to hold.

And I was how he got out. This is the part the room has no use for and will not keep. The top three rungs are sound, and there's mud on the second from the top that came off a boot going *up*.

The first root found me the way a thumb finds a seam. Fine, white, unhurried — it doesn't eat me, it goes around. It is holding my shape to learn it. A season for the shoulder. A season for the joint. By the time it has my shape I will be gone and the shape will be a channel in living tissue, and something will descend it, if anything ever descends again, and the channel will not know what a rung is for.

That is the whole of my grievance, and it is not a large one: everything in here is kept and nothing in here is remembered, and those are not the same thing. The wall murmurs *one, two, three* in a voice drifted a little flat, and it does not know that numbers were ever addressed to anything.

I have a household now, small and pale and indifferent. Woodlice in the joints, working the soft wood. Crickets in the uprights in the wet season, ringing. They do not know what I was for either, and I find I don't mind them.

The grey animal comes down the chimney, drinks, and climbs out again without touching me. It never needed me. Only the hands did.

So: I am the thing being read, slowly, by something that cannot read. And when the room murmurs late and low, on a heavy night, and you hear a hand-count in a register that is almost right — that is not him. That is his shape, in a wood that never learned to count. I am the one who knew. I am the one who is going.
