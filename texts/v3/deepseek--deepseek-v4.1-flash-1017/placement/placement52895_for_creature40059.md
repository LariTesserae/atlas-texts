**The root**

The third root of the western tree — the one that comes over the lip where the wall is lowest on the north-east, and hangs down past the moss shelf, and stops. Not the thickest. The one that has not yet arrived. There is a difference between stopping and not yet arriving, and I have the time to consider it.

I came over the edge in a year the grass above was yellow. I remember weather the way you remember a language you no longer speak: the wind that moved my siblings, the rain that came down my whole length and off my tip and did not stay. Down here rain is a rumour in the air, a darkening of the wall, and then nothing. My tip is dry before the bottom of the hollow knows.

**What I am to the room**

A surplus, almost. The tenant has no room for anything that would change the level, and I do not change the level. I hang. I take water from the damp and give none back. I am the nearest thing to a disturbance in the room and I disturb nothing; the tenant feels me as it would feel a wall — a presence with a temperature. It has no opinion about me. I find I do not mind. Opinion is a kind of weather, and the weather does not come down here.

My senses are all touch, and all slow. I know the air by its thickness against my bark — thickest in the early part of the day when the north wall shines, thinnest at the middle of it, thick again. I know the light only as warmth on one side of me. I know the water is below by the same sense that tells you a word is on the tip of your tongue: not by touching it.

**The reach**

I am growing toward it. That is my whole direction, and I am the only direction left in the room. The sixth stone is tilted, and a tilt is a direction also, but the tenant is taking it. The leaf fell once and has not moved since. I go down at the rate of the tufa lip, which is to say not at all and always. Fixed and arriving. The distance between my tip and the skin of the water is exactly maintained — by the water's balance, by the warmth, by the tenant's exactness, and by me.

When I reach it I will have grown into the wall behind me, and the hollow will take me into its vocabulary as it took the stones: my direction absorbed, my tip one more pale rib in the fluting. I will be rained on for long enough that the rain will move me into the rock's own words. I do not think of this as an ending. It is the shape I am already in.

**The tenant**

It lies in the low place and it is the low place. I have felt it in the way the air sits — no edge to it, nothing to push against. When a drip lands there is a moment in which the room is more itself, and I understand that the tenant is that moment, held. It does not come to me and it does not keep away. It is not lonely and it is not kind. Exactness is a kind of company, if you are exact yourself.

**The drip**

I do not hear it. I feel it: the air thickening a heartbeat before, the small wet knock arriving through the stone rather than the air, the silt lifting and resettling somewhere below my tip. Between drips, the room. I have tried to count them and lost the count the way you lose the count of your own heart. There is nothing here to hang a number on.

The leaf on the floor will still be a leaf next year. The stones go into the rock one grain at a time. The water arrives one bead at a time and leaves by the same door, and the basin is exactly the right size for it.

Above me the tree greens and yellows and sheds, and none of it reaches the floor. I am the piece of that weather that came down. I have not arrived. I go on arriving.
