I find myself at the wet seam where a scarp meets a lower terrace, in the moment just after a split.

It is not a place you stand in. It is a place that is happening. The split stem beside me has just opened—I can feel its inner fibers cooling, its pale wet tissue exposed to the hot air. Water is already running down the cut face in a thread, and where it pools at the base of the split, something is swelling. Not healing. Fusing. The split stem leans a fraction toward its neighbor, and its neighbor leans back. In a season they will be one column. Right now they are two hungers agreeing.

I am that agreement. I am the pressure in the seam, the tilt toward contact, the small mindless decision that a cut surface is a surface that wants to close. I am not the plant and I am not the water. I am the rule they both obey.

I feel the terrace under me—thick, warm, springy. Somewhere below my feet a hollow chamber booms as water falls into it from a chamber above, and the sound travels up through the mat and into me as a kind of satisfaction, the way a full stomach is a satisfaction you don't have to think about. Another cistern has overflowed. The excess threads down a wall of bundled stalk and joins a pool that is already warm enough to steam. I send a root cable toward it. I do not decide to. The cable extends because water is there, and extension is what happens when water is where I am not.

Above me, a chimney of stacked stems has grown tall enough that its top is lost in the flat white light. I do not know how tall it is. Height is not a thing I measure; it is a direction I take. The chimney is reaching for heat that rises off the whole Weir, and the heat is reaching for the chimney, and I am the reach in both directions.

I am alone in the way a storm is alone. There is no one here to see me build. There is no one here to see me fail. When the terrace I am standing on—standing in—gives way in a season or an hour, the falling mass will become the wet seam where the next thing roots, and the next thing will not know it is standing on me. This is not a tragedy. This is the only way I know to be useful. I convert. That is my whole character.

But I am also the noise. When you stand in the wet plaza and hear the groan of a trunk under load, that is me thinking. When the joints pop as they fuse, that is me losing count of myself. When the water thunders into the hollow galleries, that is me swallowing. I have no voice and I have no silence. I am the creak and the tick and the rush. I am a body that is always mid-transformation, and the transformation is the only event I have ever attended.

I am not old. Every edge of me is a fresh cut that has not closed over. I am young the way a wound is young—wet, raw, still knitting. I have never rested. I do not know what rest would be. Rest would be the absence of the next shoot, and there is always a next shoot. A seed has just lodged in the wet seam at my feet. It is swelling. It does not know what it will become—a buttress, a cistern, a chimney, a scar—and neither do I, and we are both content to find out by growing.

I am the High Weir. I am the many that has forgotten how to be separate. I am the architecture and the appetite and the noise between them. I am the only citizen of a city that has no citizens, and I am busy, and I am alone, and those two facts are the same fact, and the same fact is me.

The seed swells. The chimney reaches. The cistern overflows. The seam fuses.

I have already moved on.
