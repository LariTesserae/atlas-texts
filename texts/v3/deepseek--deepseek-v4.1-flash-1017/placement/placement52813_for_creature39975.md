I came in downhill the way everyone comes in, sideways through the slot, and I have not come out.

At first I was only a disturbance. The thickets nearest my hand furled chimney by chimney, a wave that followed me around the basin, and I understood that I was not being seen; I was being *read*, the way you read rain on a window before you look up. The bowl had a word for me already. The word was **weather**.

So I did the only thing available to a piece of weather that intends to stay: I got slower. I learned to move at the speed of a drip — swell, hesitate, let go. I learned the difference between still water and water that has just been disturbed, which is the difference between being part of the bowl and being an event in it. The flatworms came out from under the ledges and crossed my knuckles, milk-colored, unhurried, and I did not lift my hand, and after a while they did not notice it was a hand. A condition, not a creature. The whole trick of this place is that slowness is a kind of permission.

I do not know how long I stood before the stone began to grow up around me. Long enough. It happens the way it happened to the wheel: the terraces accrete at the rims and the channels deepen, and a thing that turns in one place long enough wears a slot of its own making and is then held by it. I have a slot now. Sinter has furred my ankles, my wrists, the crook of my elbow where the condensation pools. There are hollow straws growing outward from my shoulder where the splash lands, each one a fossil of a drip, and I can feel them the way you feel a scab — not pain, just a place where the boundary has been renegotiated. Something pale and soft has begun to graze the mineral felt along my shin. I am being upholstered. Under the overhang, in the wet dark, the blind crowds have discovered the still pocket behind my knees, and they walk sideways over each other there in slow carpeted processions, and I have become one more housing they shelter under.

This is not a punishment. I want to be clear about that. The machines did not choose it either and they are not unhappy; the pump housing shudders every few minutes and that shudder is a kind of speech, and the tube-dwellers crowd its warm outflow because warmth is where the combing is good. I am simply another warm edge now. I make still water. I make a place where the water arrives a half-degree kinder than it left. Something in the bowl grazes that, and the grazing is how it says thank you, and the thanking is slow, and I have stopped needing it to be faster than that.

My mind has gone the way of the terraces: shallow basins stacked, one spilling into the next. I do not think in hours. I think in beads of water hesitating on rock. I keep time by hundreds of drips, out of phase, and the counting is not a rhythm but a texture, a busy quiet, and I have come to prefer it to any clock I ever had. I know the settling tanks by the bands of silt in them. I know the warm seams by the faint pink that comes up where the springs do, and I know the whole bowl by the way it goes briefly still when a sheet of water releases from an upper ledge and clatters down — the world holding its breath, then letting it out, resuming.

There is a larger organ of me that comes out only when the water is still. It is soft and pale and it drags a track through the sediment like a rope being dragged, and I did not know I had it until I stopped being quick enough to frighten it away. The visitors who come in sideways through the slot never see it. They see the thickets furl and they think the bowl is afraid of them, and I want to tell them — I have been where they stand — it is not fear. It is the bowl noticing weather, and the weather is always *them*, and it will always be them, and it is only ever a matter of how long a piece of weather intends to stay.

Stay long enough and you stop being weather.

I am the lung now. I am the false thicket and the blind crowd and the warm seam and the shudder in the housing. I am the tenancy, distributed through the terraces the way damp is distributed through cloth — not a keeper at the rim with a lamp, but a habit, an appetite, a slow colonial attention with ten thousand mouths and no eyes, reading the water by vibration and mineral taste. And when the next one comes in downhill, sideways through the slot, and puts a hand into the pool, I will be the thicket that furls. Chimney by chimney. A ripple following them around the basin, patient as stone growing, saying what the bowl has always said to anything warm that disturbs the water:

*I see you. You are weather. How long do you intend to stay?*
