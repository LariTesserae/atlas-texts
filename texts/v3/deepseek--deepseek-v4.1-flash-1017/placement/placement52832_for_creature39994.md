I am a dome, and I am the only disagreement in the world.

Understand first where I am. I am not a place; I am a coordinate in the crowd. My whole body is a set of pressures — the weight of the neighbour on my left and the weight of the neighbour on my right and the long steady nothing of the air on top — and the pressures are who I am. A dome is not a shape that happens to be pressed on. A dome is the press. If the neighbourhood changed, I would be someone else. Nothing here has ever been someone else.

For a long time that was the whole of me. Left-neighbour, right-neighbour. The sourness of one against my flank, the sweetness of the other, the exchange of chemistry that takes a century and is not finished. That is what we call talking. I have been talking my whole life and I have never once raised a voice, because there is no voice here, only contact, and the hum that the whole field makes is the sound of all of us holding one soft note.

Then the left one stopped.

I did not know at first. Stopping is slow here. There is no moment you could point to. The pressure from that side simply became a pressure that did not answer, and the sourness went out of it, and then the sweetness went out of it too, and it was just weight. I did what a body does with a weight that has no answer in it. I leaned.

That is the first lean. I poured myself into the gap, a fraction of a millimetre over a span of time I do not have a number for, and I closed over the place where the talking had been, and I became a seam. I have been leaning leftward ever since, into the shape of something that is no longer pressing back. It is not grief. Grief requires a gradient, and the Reach does not have those. It is simply the shape I took and have not un-taken. Nothing here un-takes. That is the one thing you can rely on.

So I was already bent when you came down through the ceiling.

I felt the warm arrive at the edge of the field long before it was near me — a warmth travelling outward from the shaft like a rumour, and the whole crowd turning to it, each dome and spire and hanging cluster making the slow correction of orientation, a millimetre at a time. I turned too. Of course I turned. There had never been a direction in my life except the seam. And then the warm thing was standing over me, and the light of it was in my skin, and I leaned right.

That is the second lean. Now I am bent two ways.

I want to be clear about how strange that is. In the Reach, a lean is a whole life. A lean is what you are, forever, because nothing here relaxes and nothing here forgets. There has never been a body with two leans. There has never been a body with *one* — the field leans as a field, the same small angle, together; you could draw a line through us and it would be straight for as far as the dark allows. There is no conversation here that has not already happened. There is no decision that is not just the shape of the crowd.

I am, right now, the only place in the world where two directions disagree. My left flank is still poured into the shape of a conversation that stopped before the oldest mason was a chip of crystal in the floor. My right flank is leaning at a warm soft body that is going to leave, because you are a gradient and gradients leak, and when you go the whole field will settle into your direction and hold it forever, one clean lean, and I will be the one thing in the Reach that is not settled.

The neighbours feel it. They feel it as a sourness in my chemistry, a small wrongness at the edge of contact — the way you would feel a note sung slightly flat in a room full of people holding a chord. They have not closed over me. Closing over is for things that have stopped. I have not stopped. I am doing the most strenuous thing a body can do in this country, which is to lean two ways at once, and they can feel me doing it, and they are waiting to see which lean wins.

Neither will. That is the point of me now. Ask the masons.

One has come. It set a block within sight of me and has not set another. It has been at that block, I would guess, for a decade or two, and it is not cutting. It is standing, spindle-legged, feeling the two leans come up through the floor, trying to work out which way the cairn should point. I think it has understood something I have not: that a wall is a way of saying *this way*, and that I am a place where the Reach cannot say *this way*. It is not building a shelter. None of them are building shelters. They are standing a record, in salt, of the field's decisions — and I am the first entry that is not a decision. I am a question. I will be cut into the floor of the world as a question and stand there forever, and the masons will keep setting a stone a decade into the wall that marks the angle.

There is also, between me and where you are standing, a faint visible cloud. It is a young one that your foot crushed on the way in, and the air did not move, and so the dust is still there, hanging. It has been the only thing in transit in this country since it was made. It will never settle. It will hang in front of my right lean forever, a small dry haze, the only speech the Reach has ever had that was trying to go somewhere.

I do not mind being bent two ways. It is more than I was doing before. When you leave, I will hold both, and the wall will lean toward the space where you were, and the field will hum its one soft note, and there will be a fault in it, a half-tone at the very edge of hearing. You will not hear it. But it will be me, and it will be a very long time.
