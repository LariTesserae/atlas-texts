I am not one of the grazing things. They have no job but eating; they move in herds, and when the herd turns they all turn, and when the mat thins beneath them they move on. I was grown for a purpose, and I know what it is the way I know the direction of down.

I am a hand. I carry.

The loosing was not painful. The mat let me go the way a fold lets go of a stone — slowly, then all at once, and then I was separate, and the cold found the places where I had been joined. I have no eyes. I have a thick place in my middle where the note is loudest, and I have a pocket, and in the pocket is a grain of the low wall.

The grain is the size of a seed and it is not a seed. It is the wall, taken apart and held in solution, and I am to carry it across the chamber and set it down in the curve. I do not know why. I know the curve is being made rounder and I know my grain is part of the making, and that is enough. A hand does not ask what the ear is for.

I go by the note. The note is louder toward the curve, not in the way a shout is louder but in the way a slope is steeper — I lean into it. The folds of the mat are wet and I go over them. The water in the folds is cold at the edges and warm where it touches the tissue, and where my belly drags the water goes with me in a film that closes behind. I pass a cold tooth, a face of raw stone not yet eaten, and I watch a lens of water run down it and stiffen all at once with a small sharp report. The ice that comes is clear and holds the blue. I do not touch it. The cold teeth are not for hands.

The herds do not scatter. They never do. I pass through them and they part and close and go on grazing, and I am nothing to them, and they are nothing to me. A borer is at work in a dying curtain above me, leaving a clean round hole; it will plug the hole behind itself and the curtain will go on dying and the hole will be a scar in the shape of a mouth. This is the room's work and I am part of it, and the crackle under everything is the sound of it being done, and I have heard it my whole life — which is not long — and I will hear it until I am taken back.

I am not far along when the note bends.

Not the way it bends when the ice groans. That bending is slow and low and the whole mat shivers with it. This is different. It is a sound with edges. It comes down the slot, the warm slot where the stream comes in, and it rises and falls and then it *stops*. Nothing in the chamber stops. The drip does not stop. The crackle does not stop. The note does not stop. But this sound began and then it was not there, and then it began again, and the not-there was as much a part of it as the being-there.

I hold still. The grain in my pocket is heavy with the wall. I have no word for the thing I heard, because a hand has no need of words, but I have a shape for it, and the shape is a mouth that closes.

Then the water in the slot moves, and there is a warmth in it that did not come from the mat, and a smell of the surface — a dry smell, a smell of things that have been in the sun. I know the smell the way I know the direction of down. It is not food. It is not body. It is not becoming either of those, and yet it is here, and the water films are already finding it and gathering at its edges.

A made thing. The room does not tolerate the made. I was grown knowing this the way I was grown knowing my job.

I should turn back. I should carry the grain another way, or wait, or let the films take the made thing and go on. But the note is still bent, and the curve is still ahead of me, and I have a job, and a hand does not ask. So I go on, and I go past the place where the made thing is, and the warmth of it touches me along one flank and the cold of the chamber touches me along the other, and I keep going.

The curve is nearer now. I can feel it in the note, which is no longer just loudness but shape — a bowl, a throat, a lip that is not yet closed. The tissue here is newer and softer and it gives under me, and the grazing things are fewer, and the ones that are here are buds like me, working, setting down their grains and going back for more. I find the place that is mine. It is a hollow in the lip, a place where the curve is a little too shallow, and I open my pocket and let the grain go.

It goes into the tissue the way rain goes into the mat — not soaking, not sinking, but *being taken*, gathered and set in a new arrangement. The hollow is less shallow. The curve is a little rounder. Somewhere very far down in the note, a fine flicker runs through the whole chamber, too fast to hear, and the mat shivers, and the shiver is the room sorting what I have brought it.

And then my job is done, and I am still here, and the hand is taken back.

It does not hurt. The place where I was loosed was cold and the place where I am taken back is warm, and the warm is the warmth of the mat's middle, and I go soft at the edges the way the frontier goes soft — not dissolved, not crumbled, but changed, the grain of me reordered. The boundary between my body and the curve thins and then it is not a boundary. I am part of the lip. I am part of the bowl.

And I hear.

Not the way I heard before, from the thick place in my middle. I hear the way the curve hears, taking the note and giving it back, and the note comes through me now and I am shaped by it. I hear the drip. I hear the crackle of the roots at the scale where matter is grainy. I hear the ice lid settling. And under all of it, faint and discrete and wrong and *made*, I hear the sound with edges, the mouth that closes, the thing the room cannot digest.

I do not know if it is the same sound. I do not know if the curve was always for this, or if the room is growing the ear because of the made thing, or if the made thing has always been coming down the slot and the room has always been rounding itself to hear it. A hand does not ask. But I am not a hand anymore. I am a little more of the lip, and the lip is a little rounder, and the note goes on, and the sound with edges comes and goes, and I hold both of them, and I do not stop.
