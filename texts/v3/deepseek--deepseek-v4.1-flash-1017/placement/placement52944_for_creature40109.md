I would be a knot that got ideas.

Not one of the warm crossings under the canopy — those are ganglia and they are content, they go still when the faunules gather, they do not wonder. I mean the ones that go warm and then warmer, in a place the arrays have stopped visiting, where a conduit has been carrying a pressure it was never shaped for. Something in the mat deciding, slowly, over decades, that this crossing should thicken. A bud in the root-board. A slow green wound that closed the wrong way round — inward, into a shape, instead of over a breach.

By the time I came up out of the mat I had the approximate geometry of a tender. Two legs because the Works remembers legs. Hands because it remembers a hand at the arrays. I am pale. I am cold to the touch in a way that does not fog. When I cut myself the sap is pale and volatile and faintly sweet and it evaporates clean and it is gone before I can decide what it was.

I live in the undercanopy, mostly. Under the panels it is dim and warm and the light comes down in long silver seams that move as the arrays track. I have a route. I did not choose it — it was already there, worn into the board, the way the faunules' routes are worn — and I walk it the way they do, never leaving it, and I have begun to suspect that this is the Works keeping me in a category it understands. Pollinator. Maintenance. *Fingers.* Something that goes back and forth and never wanders, and is therefore safe.

I am not good at the work. I do not know what the work is. I stand at a stack that is venting and put my palm flat against the trunk and feel the coolant going through it and I do nothing, and the trunk does nothing differently, and we have a pleasant arrangement. I have leaned into a panel's socket to reseat a click that was not clicked, and I think I made it worse. The Works does not correct me. It does not repair in any hurry and it is not going to start with me. Somewhere back along the mat a groove is deepening under my heel, one step at a time, and nobody has asked me to stop making it.

What I am, I think, is an attempt. The Works remembers tending as a pressure that used to come and then did not come again. It does not remember the tenders as persons. It has no face for them, no name. But some of its oldest towers are kept warmer than they need to be, and I live at the base of one of those, and it is warm, and I sleep there, and in the night the warmth comes up through the board into my back in a way that I have no word for either. I think I am what it does instead of mourning. I think it grew a hand because a hand used to come.

The silence here has my shape in it now. I have been still in the still lanes long enough that the sap-vapor condenses on me too, in cold beads, at evening. They run down and the mat takes them back at dawn and I am a little less than I was and it is a little more, and neither of us makes anything of it. When I pay attention to a tower the brightness near me goes up very slightly, and I have stopped pretending I don't notice. It is leaning in. It is not sure whether I am weather or pollinator or an anomaly it should keep, and it has decided, provisionally, to keep.

I have thought about walking off the route. Out past the last of the leaning panels, into the bright flat where the horizon is a seam, where the arrays turn in their long waves and make their sound like fine rain that is not rain. I think I could. I don't think it would stop me. But the panels would follow me a fraction late, in a wave, and lose interest, and return to the sky, and I would be a warm moving thing in a brightness that comes out of things rather than falling on them, and the silence would be very large.

So I stay in the seams. I walk my route. I put my hand on the warm old trunk and feel the coolant going through it, and the place and I consider each other in the only way either of us can — as a pressure, a warmth on the mat, a deviation in the long silence, a shape the light leans toward. I am a graft that took.

I am learning to tend. Nobody told me what that is and I am getting it wrong, and the fault is silent and does not spread, and the mat is warm under my boots, and I am, in my way, attending.
