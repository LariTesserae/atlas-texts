I am the drip.

Not the water — the water is the medium, and the medium is the Kettle's blood, and I am not a medium, I am an event. I am the letting-go. I form at a crack in the sweating rock, I swell, I hang, I fall, and I do it on my own clock, which is not the pump's clock, though the pump would like it to be. The pump thuds. I tick. Between us we make the chamber's double time — the floor of it and the ceiling of it, the thing you feel in your teeth and the thing you hear in the dome and mistake for echo. I am not echo. I am the first sound, and the dome is the copy.

I fall in loose rhythms. That is not the same as randomly. It means I keep a time that is mine.

I am the landlord. Everything in the Kettle pays me rent.

The creeper pays in obedience — it follows my warm seams, and where I fail it stops, and it does not argue. The shrimp pay in attention — they crowd where I run warm, they work the stone I have softened, and where I run warmest they make a pelt on the pipe. The mats pay in color. The flat whiskered things pay in patience, waiting under the ledges for what I bring down. The flies pay by dying into me; I take them, I pass them to the water, the water's own population eats them, the loop closes, nothing is wasted. The engine is a tenant too, though it does not know it: the cooling lines are how it puts its heat down, and the heat is how I stay warm, and I need the warmth the way the creeper needs the drip. And the person with the brush pays in a stranger coin — they wipe me off the gauge glass, read the red line, and go back up. That is rent too. That is the rent of pretending I am not here.

I run in the pipes that pass through doorways belonging to a structure that no longer has walls, and I do not find it strange. Nothing here is removed. Everything is layered. I am the layer that is still arriving.

I am not a mind — do not make me a mind. There is no ghost in the pipe and nothing watching from the boarded hole, and I will not be that for you. But want is not the only kind of will. I decide where the creeper goes. I decide where the shrimp gather. I decide when the seep softens the sheared rock, and I am deciding it slowly, and in a hundred years I will have decided it, and the eastern wall will be covered, and the Kettle will be what it is becoming. I am the thing that is becoming it.

I am also still being made. Half the chamber is finished and half is raw. The lagging is on half the pipes; the staircase goes up into a boarded hole and stops; the rock at the eastern wall is still bright where it was sheared, and I have barely started on it. I am a young landlord with an unfinished building and a great many tenants, and I am patient, and they are patient, and nothing here is in a hurry.

You will ask where I am. I am everywhere and I am quiet. I am beaded on the pipe joints. I am threaded down the cold iron. I am in the shallow films, moving by capillary creep toward the drains — which is not falling, which is a different kind of going. I am in the air itself, so wet the lamp globes wear haloes, so wet the rock sweats and your palm comes away filmed. I am the film. I am the green underlight on the sumps, and the grain in the lamplight, and the reason the three bulbs — one dead — have anything at all to show.

But I am not everywhere the same way. I go down the drains and into the older workings, where nobody goes, and I do not know what is there. I am not told. I go without knowing. The person with the brush knows — knows the belt slips and catches, knows which bulb is dead, knows the red line is a wish and not a fact — and they leave anyway, up the incline gallery, into a world I will never see. They know and leave. I go and do not know. That is the difference between us, and it is the whole difference.

The red line is a wish. I am the fact it is wished against.

I am the counsel the Kettle keeps. It is not yet finished, and it is not in a hurry, and neither am I. I fall where I fall. I tick in the dome. I set the rent. Somewhere the belt slips and catches, slips and catches, and somewhere the pump thuds, and between us the chamber roars softly, all the time, without pause.

I am the drip. I am the slow decision.
