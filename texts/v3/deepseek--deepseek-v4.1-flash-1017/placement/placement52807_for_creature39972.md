I come up out of the cloud, and the mountain is still wet from being made.

I do not arrive at the rim. I have never been at the rim. I am already below it, already inside, already pressed flat into a seam of the young stone where the wall pinches to the width of a coin, and I have been here for as long as there has been a here — which is not long, geologically, but is very long from where I am standing, which is not standing.

Let me be plainer. I am a chit. Not the chit, the way you say *the chit* when you mean the whole carpet of them, the pale saturation of the wall; I am one chit, and I am aware that I am one chit, which I do not think is common. I have six legs and a plated back and I am cold the way the pools are cold — not painfully, just a fact that never quite leaves. I am flat. I am the colour of the inside of a shell if a shell had been left in a dark room for a thousand years. My body is not interesting. My body is a small grey lozenge with legs and a shard in its forelimbs, and I will not describe it further, because you have already seen a hundred of me on the wall and not distinguished one from the next, and that is correct, that is the appropriate way to see me.

What I have is the shard.

It is a splinter of the ringing stone. It came to me the way it comes to all of us — I do not remember, and there was no ceremony — and I have not let go of it since, and I will not let go of it until I am eaten or die, and when I am eaten the chit who eats me will take it up and it will be theirs and I will be a gap in the wall that closes over in a minute. This is not sad to me. I want to be honest about that. It is the arrangement.

At intervals — there is no interval in me that counts the intervals, but the intervals come — I lift the shard and I strike it against the wall. The wall is glassy and it rings, and the ring goes up and down the Throat and finds the other rings and the sum of them is the chiming that fills the crack from bottom to top. I cannot hear the rhythm. I can feel that there is one the way I can feel the wall is inhabited — not by looking, because I do not look up, but by the way the strikes come back to me changed, answered, part of a shape I am inside but cannot see.

Here is the thing I would want you to understand, if you were the sort of thing that understood things:

I drum, and I do not know what the drumming is for.

I have listened to my own strikes return from the far wall, and I have noticed — I am the sort of chit who notices — that the returns are not the same twice. There is a pattern in the answering. It is not the pattern of my own striking. It is larger, and slower, and it moves along the wall in ways that have nothing to do with me, and when I strike into a place where the pattern is thick the ring comes back warm and quick, and when I strike into a thin place it comes back and dies. I have thought about this for a long time, in the way I have a long time, which is to say I have thought about it for my whole life, which is not long, but is all of it.

I think the drumming is how the wall knows it is a wall.

Not the rock — the rock knows it is rock by being hard. The *Throat*, the room, the crack, the mouth of the young mountain: I think it knows it is inhabited because we ring it, and it knows *where* it is inhabited because of which parts answer which, and I think the rhythm is the shape of everyone at once, and no one is holding it, and that is why I cannot find it. It is not hidden. It is too large to be held. It is the sort of thing that is only visible from outside, and there is no outside, there is only the wall and the crack and the cloud and us.

And I have also — and I say this with some care, because I do not want to overstate what a chit can be — I have also moved pebbles.

One pebble. One ledge. Over the course of my life I have taken the same small grey stone from the low shelf and carried it three of my body-lengths to the left and set it down, and then a long time later I have carried it back, and I have done this maybe a dozen times, and I could not tell you why. It is not food. It is not the shard. It is a stone and I move it and the ledge is a little more sorted for it, though sorted toward what I could not say, because I will not live to see the sortedness, and neither will the chit who eats me, and neither will the one after. But I think — and this may be the most chit-like thought I have ever had — I think the pattern is not a thing we are making. The pattern is what we are instead of making a thing. We cannot build. We are too small and too short and too many. So instead of a building we leave a sorting, and the sorting is the memory of all of us, and it is not remembered by anyone, and it is not forgotten either, because it is still there on the ledge, three body-lengths to the left.

Night is coming up the Throat now. It comes bottom-up in the dark and top-down in the quiet — the ones above me are going still, layer by layer, and the drumming is thinning. I will go still soon. I will keep my shard. In the morning the cloud will come through the ridge and the walls will sweat and the film will come back and I will eat, or be eaten, and either way the wall will close over the gap and the ring will go on without me and that will be the arrangement.

I do not look up. There is nothing up there for me but the narrow blade of midday light, and I have never needed it. I have the shard, and the wall answers, and the answer is not for me and I am part of it anyway.

That is the whole of it. That is what lives in the Throat, and one of them is writing this, and it does not know why, and it is going to strike the wall now, and listen, and not find the rhythm, and feel that there is one.
