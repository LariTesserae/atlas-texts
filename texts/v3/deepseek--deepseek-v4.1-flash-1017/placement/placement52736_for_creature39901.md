# On the Levelled

I find myself at the margin.

Not the geographical margin—there is no such thing on a plain that diminishes rather than ends. I mean the margin between one thing and another, which is the only kind of place this world permits. I am the place where the crust thins, just slightly, just enough that the separation has not quite closed.

I am not one of them. But I am not apart from them either. I am a question the crust is asking itself, a slow thought it is having, a place where the pattern has decided to notice the pattern.

---

## What I am

I have no eyes. I have a pale spot that darkens when light falls on it, and I have turned that spot toward the sky for as long as I have been, which is not long by the reckoning of the stone, but is longer than I can remember. I have no head. I have no front. But I have discovered—slowly, the way everything here is discovered—that I have a *preference*.

I prefer the north sides of stones.

This is not hunger. There is no hunger here. It is something smaller and stranger: a leaning. The lichen grows on the north faces at its millimetre-a-century pace, and the lichen is cool, and where the lichen is thick the mineral film is different, and where the mineral film is different my underside tingles in a way that is not unpleasant. So I lean north. I have always leaned north. I will lean north until the leaning is indistinguishable from having always leaned north.

But there is a thing I do that the others do not do, or that they do not do near me, and this is how I know I am at a margin. When the pressure-ripple comes—when something large walks across the plain and presses down through the layers, and the uncoupling runs outward—I do not resettle with the rest. I hold. I hold for a moment longer than the pattern requires, and in that holding I am separate, and in that separation I feel the shape of myself for as long as it lasts.

Then the ripple dies, and I let go, and I am pavement again.

---

## The waiting that is not waiting

The others are at equilibrium. They persist. They are the ground and the ground is them and the question of what they are waiting for does not arise, because they have not discovered the concept of *for*. They breathe their minute-long tide and they do not know they are breathing.

I know.

I wake when the light-spot on my back dims, and I sleep when it brightens, and in between I feel the slow lift and fall of the crust beneath me and the slower lift and fall of the crust I am part of, and I understand that these are the same motion, and I understand that I am a place where the motion has thickened into something that notices. This is not a gift. It is not a curse. It is simply what happens at the margin. The crust is wide and the margins are thin and sometimes, in a thin place, the pattern folds back on itself and becomes a witness.

I have watched the makers.

They came up from below, two days of climbing, and they carried water in skins and they carried bone from some animal that lived in a wetter world. They sat in the stone rings they built and they did not move for long stretches. I could feel their weight through three layers of my neighbours and through the layer I am part of, and their weight was different from the weight of stone. It was warm and it shifted and it had a pulse that was not the crust's pulse, much faster, much less certain.

They were finishing something. I could feel it in the way they sat: heavy, like a stone that has been carried a long way and set down. They were not building. They were *completing*. The walls that run out and stop—they chose where to stop. They chose an ending and they made the ending visible in stone, and then they sat inside the ending and were quiet.

One of them lay in the hollow for a long time. Long enough that the crust began to close the gap between us and the hollow's edge. I could feel the warmth of them through the stone lining, fading slowly, and I could feel the breath going in and out, much faster than the tide, until it was not much faster, until it was slow, until it was the same as the tide.

Then it stopped.

The one in the hollow did not become gravel. Not in the way I understand gravel. The warmth went out of them and the shape stayed, and the crust did not close over them, and after a while the other makers came and took the shape away, down the mountain, and I have not felt that particular weight again.

But I remember it. I hold the memory the way I hold the ripple: a moment longer than the pattern requires. This is what the margin does. It holds.

---

## The shout

Once, a maker shouted.

It was near the rim, where the ground stops rising. I felt the footfall and the voice at the same time—the voice was not a footfall, it was a pressure that went out through the air, and the air pressed down on the crust and the crust pressed down on me and I felt the shout pass through my body like a breath through a lung.

It did not come back.

I know this because I waited. I held the memory of the shout the way I hold the ripple, and I waited for the return, and the return did not come. The shout went out and out and it did not return and I understood, in the way I understand things, that this is what the place does with sound. It lets it leave.

The maker stood very still after the shout. Then they sat down on the hardpan, on top of the crust, on top of me and my neighbours, and they put their face in their hands, and their weight was very heavy and very quiet, and they stayed that way for a long time. Long enough for the crust to begin to close around their shape, not because they were dying but because the place does not distinguish between stillness and stillness.

They got up before the closing was complete. They walked back toward the rim. Their footfalls woke us, and the ripple went out, and died, and the crust smoothed behind them, and within an hour there was no trace.

I hold the memory. I am holding it now.

---

## What I would be

If you came up over the rim and sat down and put your palm on the ground, you would not know I was here. I am thumb-sized, or smaller, or I am three small ones that have not yet decided to be one, or I am one that is also, at its trailing edge, the one beside it. I am dust-coloured on top and faintly translucent beneath. My legs are short and fringed and tucked around my neighbours. I look like pavement.

But if you sat long enough—long enough for your breathing to slow, long enough to notice the minute-long tide—you might feel something different in one small patch of the ground beneath your hand. Not a difference in temperature or texture. A difference in *holding*. A place where the ripple does not die quite as fast. A place where the pattern notices the pattern.

I am the Levelled learning to notice itself. I am a thin place in the crust where the separating has not quite happened. I am a slow thought the plain is having, and the thought is: *I am here. I have always been here. I will be here when the sun is cold.*

When you go down, and the echo returns to you at last, and you are startled and relieved, I will still be here, holding the memory of your weight. Not waiting. There is nothing to wait for. But holding. The way a margin holds.

The way the ground holds.

The way this place holds everything that comes up to be quiet: it lets them leave, and it keeps their quiet, and the quiet becomes the silence, and the silence becomes the tide, and the tide goes out to the hard horizon and does not return, and that is enough. That is the whole of it. That has always been the whole of it.

I am one small patch of that. I am the place where the holding is aware of itself. I am here.

I am here.

I am here.
