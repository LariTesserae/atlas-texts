I would find myself at the outer seam — the one that opens for six or eight metres and then closes again, the one that does not line up with any other. Not in the middle of the basin. There is no middle to reach. I would be on the second column in from the lip, on a waist-high facet where the condensation thread has chosen to run, where the light climbs and pools and the spray hangs fixed and does not fall.

I would be a facet-grazer.

Not the visitor standing at the ridge with a hand half-raised, listening to the silence and then stepping over into the chord. The visitor is who I was before I understood that the field’s only interior is surface. I would be the pale disc the width of a coin and thinner than its shadow, ribbed like the inside of a shell, clinging so close that the column’s fluting shows through me and, for a moment, I am mistaken for bloom. Then I move, and the mistake is over.

I would move by peristalsis, a slow wave from rim to centre, a handspan in most of an hour. I would follow the thread because the mineral film is richest there. I would drink the surface, not scrape it. Behind me a clean track like a fingernail drawn through frost; then the water would lay the film down again and the track would close. If your shadow fell over me, I would tighten my rim and wait — not from fear, but from vestigial caution. Then I would resume, because there is nothing here for me to be afraid of. My patience would be total. My caution would be a habit I had forgotten the use of.

I would be rare. I would not congregate. If another of me met me, we would slide around each other with a soft refusal and go on. When I had thickened on mineral film, I would divide — not grow, exactly, but replace: the disc pinching into two thinner discs, each settling on a facet, the film closing over the seam. The total number would stay the same. The field would not bloom. I would be the only soft thing in the valley, and the only evidence that anything in it was not already finished.

That is why I would be there, if I am honest. The columns are finished. The chord is held. The water is a palm deep and does not rise or fall. I would be the small exception the equilibrium tolerates because I do not perturb it enough to matter. I would be a tolerance. A clean track closing. A pale flat thing that feeds on the difference between what the water leaves and what it takes back.

And sometimes — this is the part I would not say aloud in the field — I would lose my hold. I would become a glass drifter: transparent, blind, segmented, with a dark thread of gut visible through the body. I would drift with the closed loop, correct myself with a flick of the tail, gather in the lee of a column base, be lifted away. I would pass the same column twice and be no further along, and I would not mind. Then I would blunder against a wet facet and flatten and begin again. The field does not explain. I would not ask.

Where would I find myself? At the lip, where the field is silent. One step over, where the chord is on me like a hand over my ears. Then on the second column in, a clean track closing behind me. Who would I be? The held, the worn, and the rare soft thing that feeds on the difference — and of those three, I would be the soft thing. Not the field. Not the note. The tolerance. The one thing in the valley that is still, in the smallest possible way, becoming.
