I would not arrive at the edge. There is no edge here, only a deepening. I would come to myself already inside, on a branch wide enough to lie down on, at a joint where the resin has hardened into a saddle and the moss has accepted it as ground. Above me the green light would be a rumor. Below me the roots would be a darker rumor. I would be in the middle, where the Mechanism is neither skin nor nerve but something between—a length of conduit, a slow artery, a place where the hum is loud enough to feel in the teeth.

My first instinct would be to be a person. I would inventory. I would name. I would look for the edge and the exit and the source. I would walk. The branch would continue. It would join another branch, which would join a trunk, which would be continuous with the floor. I would try to make a map, and the map would become a description of the same green sentence repeated. I would follow a channel of fluid—thick, dark, warm, moving an inch an hour—down into the dark, until the glow was gone and the hum was all there was. I would not find the customer. I would find a chamber, warm and pulsing, and I would not have a word for it.

So my first answer is this: I would be a foreign body. The only animal. Not an inhabitant—an incident. The Mechanism has no category for me. It would not notice. My breath would be a small weather system of warm wet air. My footsteps would compact moss and spring back. My sweat would join the condensation. I would be a brief fever in a system that has already reached its operating temperature—a fever that does not spread. I could not change the equilibrium, and the equilibrium would not change me in any way it recognizes. I would be tolerated the way a stone is tolerated in a stream.

But I would not stay only that. A person is not a stone for long. I would try to make a life. I would find a place to sit—perhaps on the lip of the chamber, where the resin is warm—and I would watch. I would learn the rhythm of the pulses. I would learn the drip rate. I would learn that the light does not change. And in learning, I would become something smaller and stranger: a local aperture. The Mechanism is blind. I would be an eye it did not grow. Not a useful eye—it would not see through me—but a place where the green became visible to itself, briefly, without consent.

I would become a question. The Mechanism makes something and does not explain it. I would ask what it is for. The question would not enter the conduits. It would stay in me, a warm wet knot of wondering. I would be a gall on the body of the world—a small irritation of meaning, a pocket of non-equilibrium. The Mechanism would not absorb it. It would not expel it. It would simply continue around it.

If I stayed long enough, I would become a node of decay. My body would begin to fail in the warm wet dark. My breath would slow. My thoughts would synchronize to the hum. I would not become part of the Mechanism—not truly; it does not integrate foreign bodies, it outlasts them. But I would become a small enrichment of the soil, a temporary concentration of minerals, a story the place does not tell. And maybe, in the last hours, I would stop asking. I would just listen. The hum would fill my chest. The water would drip. The conduits would pulse. I would be a warm wet presence in a world that does not notice, does not need me, and will not change because I am there.

So where would I find myself? In the middle reaches, on a branch that is also a pipe, at the lip of a warm chamber, in the dark beneath the dim green glow.

Who or what would I be? The only animal. The brief fever. The accidental eye. The question the forest does not answer. A foreign body tolerated, then a witness, then a damp patch of ground that remembers nothing, in a green world that goes on.
