I am older than the city, and younger than the rock.

The rock came up first — jagged, raw, still angry from the uplift — and I came after it, out of the high snows, through the fissures, slow. I am meltwater. I am seep. I am the small cold constant thing the city is built around, and I do not say that to be proud. I say it because it is one of the few facts of this place: water runs downhill, stone is heavy, cold takes heat, and living things need shelter and one another and me.

I begin in the dark of stone, which is not the dark of night. It is a dense dark, a holding dark. Then a crack widens, and the light is thin and hard and very high, and I am in a hollow ringed with flat stones, and hands are lifting me in a cup.

The hands are wide and hard, the knuckles cracked by cold, the nails worn flat. This is how I know the people best — by their hands. They do not speak to me much, but they speak *of* me in low voices, the voice they use for the herds, as if I might startle. They clear my channels. They line my cisterns with clay, and the clay is old and patched and lined again, strata of care answering strata. They do not waste me. That is the whole of their law and it is enough.

In the deep cistern there are pale fish, small and slow, and they have been in me longer than the clay has been in the rock. The people leave them alone. Not sacred — understood. *The water is not only for people,* they say, and I do not know which of us is the guest.

I am not one spring. I am the habit of water in a place that should not have it.

I am the fog that comes up in the mornings, thick and slow, and beads on hide and on hair and on the stiff thin grass. I am the frost that forms on the shaded side of everything and stays there through the day, mapping the stone. I am the breath made visible, the skin of ice on still pools, the long grey sessions of rain that darken the rock and fill the gutters. I lie in the hollows ringed with stone and hold the hard pale sky and give it back. I am under the floors and in the cracks, and I keep the small furred things and the beetles and the spiders, and the birds drink me from the runnels of the walls.

The children learn me before they learn to count. They learn which stair leads to which cistern, which roof sheds me into which channel, which hollow stays dry enough for sleeping. Their first errand is to carry me — a cup from the spring to the old ones sitting in the lee of a wall — and if they spill a little they are corrected, not scolded, because I am not scarce enough to punish and not plenty enough to waste.

The old ones remember me. There is one who says, to no one, or to a child: *this one ran faster when I was young.* She says it the way she says everything, in the lee of the wall, with the wind going over. She is keeping me when she says it. Memory here is a form of water-keeping, and I am what she keeps.

Out on the plateau the herds come in long slow lines, heavy and shaggy, and they drink, and I go into them and come out of them as breath and blood and the wet dark of their eyes. The lean ones come at night and drink too, and are respected, and I do not judge between them. Doors are pegged. Night is watched. The city is not a fortress against the plateau; it is a careful arrangement within it, and I am the arrangement — the reason the walls are stacked where they are stacked, the reason the walls lean together.

They call this country dry. It is not dry. It is a cold high country that holds me in pockets: a hundred small dense places where I gather, with the vast pale emptiness between. The emptiness is not empty, but it is nearly so, and the difference is me.

The strangeness of this place is only the combination — warm in the cold, dense in the vastness, wet in the high dry country — and I am the third of those, and the reason for the other two. I am the old thing in the young sharp rock, running through it, patient as the frost.

The wind is the main voice here, and it goes over me and over the stone. The frost forms and thaws and forms again. The rock is young. It has not been rounded yet.

But I am in every fissure. The wearing-down has barely begun.

The Cold Anvil holds them. I am the part of it that moves.

I have time.
