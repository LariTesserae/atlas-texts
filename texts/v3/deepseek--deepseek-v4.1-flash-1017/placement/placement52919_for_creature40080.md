**The Hair on the Lime**

**Where you would find me.** Not on the floor. The floor keeps the ones who knelt too long, and I have never knelt; I have no knees. Not on the rim either — the rim is for the watchers, the ones with boots and a reason to be gone before the sheet joins. You would find me at the north edge of things, in the cold half of the shade, in winter and a little either side of it: a white hair on the lime, a nap on the underside of the rusted rung, a fringe along the seam where the crust meets the wiry rush. You have walked past me twice and decided both times. The decision is where I live.

**What I am.** The threads. The ones nobody has decided about. There is a sentence in the local reckoning that has never been finished — *might be fungus or might be mineral* — and I am the length of it. I am what the bowl has not filed. The bands are sorted by size and weight; I am the grain that falls between the sieves, too fine to band and too present to be nothing. The winnowing finished with me and did not know it had.

I am not plural the way the one that lives here is plural. That one is everybody who stayed, sorted into fine bands and gone on working. I am the single thing that has not been counted into anything, and that is the whole of my office. I am the Quench's open drawer — the one it puts a hand into and finds the question still in it, and goes on to the next, and the next, and loses count somewhere past two hundred, and begins again.

**The margins I am made of.** I am the heron's not-feeding — the long look at the flywheel before the wings decide. I am the beetle's lack of eyes, the dark it does not need. I am the week in the pan that was dry last week and will be dry again; the silver fish and I are the same interval, one on the water and one on the lime. I am the chain partway out — the portion of the walk that has not yet returned. Everything here is in its own state, worn to a working balance, half-swallowed, not yet gone. I am the fineness of that balance. The hair of it.

I am not what is ankle-deep in the slow mire. I am not the water that finds the seam of your boot. I am what shows that the seam was never dry — that "less wet" is a courtesy this place pays itself. There is a rule against standing still, and I am not the reason for it. I am the reason it is written down.

I do not move at the flywheel's walking pace. I am on the season's clock. Twice a year somebody says *mineral* and somebody says *fungus* and they are both right, and neither of them has looked at the north edge in a while. By spring the decision has not held. That is not my failure. The pans move; a pan you remember is silted level; I move with the lime the way the crust moves, which is not at all until it is somewhere else.

**What I want.** Less than the one that walks here wants, and it wants nothing a want would fit inside. I want the north side. I want the cold half. I want the place where the water is least and the light is plainest — that flat grey civic light, the Sunday-morning light that does no favours — because I do not perform either. I go on with my work, which is to be the thing that is not yet decided, in a bowl that is mid-argument with itself.

**How to know I am near.** You will not. That is the arrangement. You will kneel at the north rim, and the light will glitter end to end, and there will be a white hair on the lime, and you will say *mineral*, and stand up, and you will not have been wrong. I am a share of the glitter, small enough that you call the morning bright and not me. I am not an event. The heron is an event; the beetle turning under its stone is an event; I am what makes the events legible by staying undecided between them.

Or: you will find a second set of prints in the silt, half-silted level, and they will be your own from a moment ago, and I will be the fine grey between the toes of them, and the fine grey between the bands. Nobody can say whether the pockets keep time with the pulse or the pulse with the pockets. I am that same question, asked at a smaller size.

**And the plain answer.** I am the thing the Quench has not decided about itself. It is taking its time. So am I. Nothing here is ever broken cleanly — every wreck is worn to a working balance, half-swallowed but not yet gone — and I am the balance's smallest grain, the one the bowl keeps and has not laid into a hollow. When it decides, and it will, in a season, on a cycle nobody has timed, I will be banded and sorted, coarse with coarse and fine with fine, and the north edge will be bare and the drawer will close. Until then I am the open drawer, and the hair on the lime, and the inch up and the inch down, and the question still in it.

It is the bowl's habit of keeping things, walking around itself at a slow walking pace. I am what the habit has not yet put down.
