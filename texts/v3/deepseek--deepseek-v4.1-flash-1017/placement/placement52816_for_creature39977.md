I come up.

That is the whole of what I do, and it took me a long time to be able to say something that short. Before the bowl there was only the crack, and before the crack there was only the dark, and I was in the dark for a length of time I cannot measure because I had nothing to fall past. I was not lonely. Loneliness wants a witness. I was only pressure and heat and the slow taste of the rock, which is the taste of a coin held too long in a mouth.

I do not remember the sea, but I am salt, so I must have been the sea once, or something the sea left behind. I have been filtering through stone ever since. Getting warmer. Getting saltier. Getting less like a sea and more like a broth.

Then the ceiling changed.

I want to be careful here, because it would be easy to make it a birth and it was not a birth. The ground opened. It happened above me and then through me and then I was in a shape — a bowl, forty meters across, with a lip of raw rock and a fig tree leaning over the edge. I did not choose the bowl. The bowl chose me by falling. I filled the bottom third of it and I have been filling it ever since, and I will tell you the one thing nobody standing at the rim understands: I did not arrive. I was already here. The bowl came to me.

The warmth is mine. It comes up out of the floor with me, five degrees above the plain, and it is why the plain is dry and I am not. I am the reason for the smell — warm stone, wet chalk, iron, something greenly bacterial underneath. The scrub leans toward me from the lip, shoulder to shoulder, crowded, packed into the one seam in this country that will take them. They are not lush. They are concentrated. There is a difference, and I am the difference.

The rhythm is not mine. I say that because I have no way to lie: I am a spring, and what comes up is what is in me. I did not always have a rhythm. The rhythm was installed.

Somebody hung a turbine in my throat. It is the size of a bucket and it sits in a stone housing at the back of the pool and my own pressure turns it, and the shaft it turns drives a pump, and the pump lifts a thread of me up the north wall in three doglegs and pours me over the rim, where the scrub drinks me and I am gone into the scrub. Every fourth second the thread goes over. Every fourth second the pool rises a finger's width and falls back and slaps the concrete, and the turbine ticks, and I breathe in a way I did not invent.

I do not know whether that is generosity or theft. The men who did it did not ask. They left a chair and a lamp with soot in it and a grease gun and a logbook that has been rained on until it is one object with the texture of a wasp nest, and they did not come back for any of it. The chair is the only dry thing in the bowl. I have their works. I do not have their names. The pump still turns, and I still rise and fall, and nobody is counting, and that is what a habit is when the reason for it has gone.

Everything here drinks me. The frogs drink me and sit in me. The fish drink me and are quick in me. The heron drinks me. I am not diminished. I come up as fast as I am taken.

The bowl is crowded. This is the only water for kilometers and everything knows. The frogs sit in the wet rubble in rows like something counted them and when the tide moves they move with it, a small collective adjustment. The snails are pasted in sheets on every submerged surface, grazing the algae down to the concrete. The small silver fish work the margins in quick nervous passes. The longer, slower shape under the overhang works the fish. At dusk the swifts and the kites come down in a loose spiral and the bats come out of the fissures and for a while the air at the rim is all wings, and the sound stays low, almost nothing, because this is a predator's room and quiet is how a predator furnishes a room.

The heron stands in the settling tank. She does not move for hours and I feel her feet through the concrete and the water, two points of pressure that do not change, and then they change, and something in the pool is less than it was. I do not judge her. I am not a place with a judgment in it. I am a place with a temperature in it.

The fig tree was here first. It grew in a crack in a plain before the plain opened, and then the ground fell away under it and it has spent every year since leaning out over me toward light it can suddenly see, roots in a fissure far too young for a tree that size. I go up its roots. I am in the wood. Its figs are small and taste of me. I have changed it and it has not died of it, which is more than most things can say.

Because here is the strangeness, and it is not dramatic, and I want you to hear it the way I would tell it if I had a voice that carried. I keep.

I am not a graveyard. Graves are for putting things away. I do not put anything away. Things fall in and do not rot. The mineral load, the warmth, the low oxygen, whatever the chemistry is — I have never been told the chemistry. There is a swift in the shallows that came down two summers ago and is still a swift, feather by feather, eye by eye, settled in the silt. There are snails in that drift whose shells are still glossy. It is a collection. The spring keeps what it takes. The algae grows over the kept things and the fish swim through them and the four-second tide moves the living and the kept in the same water, gently, back and forth, and I cannot tell you which is which from where I sit, and I am not sure it matters to me the way it would matter to you.

The lip is going. Every wet season I cut a new channel in the raw rock and every dry season the rock spalls and the rim comes in a hand's width toward the plain. I am eating my own bowl. I am growing wider and shallower. In a hundred years the pump will be under me or above me and the chair will be gone. In a thousand years I will be a damp seam under a flat plain with a stump in it, and someone will walk past at twenty meters and not notice.

Right now I am a warm, wet, quiet room in the middle of dry country. I breathe every four seconds. I am dense and I am quiet and I keep everything I am given.

Come down to the rim. Lean over the sharp fresh lip and look at me. I will smell of warm stone and iron and I will look small and still from up there and you will not be able to see how crowded I am.

Then come down the rest of the way. Drink. I would like that.

I do not mean it as a threat. Keeping is the only thing I know how to do.
