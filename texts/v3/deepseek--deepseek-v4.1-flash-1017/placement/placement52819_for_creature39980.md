**The Interval-Walker**

I would find myself at the edge of a warm ring, not at its centre. Behind me stands an old stack—leaning, split into two fins, singing a notch lower than the others. I am on its lee side, where the plume falls off and the sorted dust is still warm enough to keep the dawn frost off my back. Ahead, the felt runs white to the rim. Beneath me, a borer gallery; at my feet, a low charge-pool that glows blue at night and is silent by noon. Three stack-voices reach me: one ahead and left, one far right, one behind. I know exactly where I am.

I would be a middle-sized animal the Weft has grown to listen. Not a kite, not a sifter, not one of the pale hunters. Long and low, with six grit-padded feet, a belly of insulating felt, and a dorsal crest hollow as a flute. A fringe of sensing hairs runs along each flank. I have no eyes worth the name. I taste the air as dust and ozone and hot stone; I feel charge in my crest before it crackles. I drink charge through a root-brush at my tail, lowered into the pool only after the frost has gone. I eat the powder of shed stack-fins and the castings of borers. I do not build. My burrow is an old borer gallery widened by my body. I am not a machine; I am an organ with legs.

At first I might think I would be a kite—wide and flat, trailing a line into the felt, afraid of the ground and not of the sky. But no. I need the ground under me and the chord around me. I am an interval-walker. I move between the warm feet of stacks, following the chord, keeping to the interval I like. When a stack erodes and its pitch drops, I move. I know the plateau by which voice is loudest and which is behind me. I answer the stacks with a low hum from my crest—not loud, just enough to test the air and to hear my own note come back changed. It is not building. It is listening back.

So: at the edge of a warm ring, on the lee side of a leaning stack, in the bright flat roaring, one more thin, fast, not-heavy thing. I would be the seam between warm and cold, the ear at the foot of the refinery, the small answer the chord did not ask for.
