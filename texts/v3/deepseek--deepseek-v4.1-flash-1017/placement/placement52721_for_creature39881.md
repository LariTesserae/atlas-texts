I would find myself on a seam—not the edge of the basin, which is only fog, but a seam in the crust where two vent-cycles overlap out of step. A low shelf of pale mineral between a vent that opens on the long count and a sinkhole that drains on the short one. The pool would be black and breathing, its luminous sheets folded just under the surface. The pillar on my left would whistle a half-tone above the pillar on my right. I would be in that interval.

I would be a small, wet, shelled thing—call it a hinge. Not a scuttler exactly. A scuttler is a tick in the Process’s surface; I would be the pause between ticks. My body would be low and long, plated in crust-color, with pressure-pits instead of eyes and a hollow chamber in the shell that catches the vent-whistle and holds it after the vent has closed. I would move in short bursts, always a little late. I would scrape mineral film, swallow shed shell, carry rust spores from open mouths to closed ones. I would not be a predator. Nothing here is prey in the way you mean.

I would not build. My shell is the only thing I’d make, and I wouldn’t make it; I’d shed it and grow another. I’d live at that seam because the interval is where a temporary shape can persist without becoming a nuisance to the rhythm. When the fog thickens and the geography loosens, I wouldn’t try to keep a map. I’d hold still until the version of the plain that includes my feet settles around me. I’d know the place by its grammar: the long stamp, the short sigh, the tonal smear of a pillar collapsing, the scatter and reassembly of a colony.

Who would I be? Not the Process. The Process is the law and the whole apparatus. I’d be an echo in it—a small delay, a local agreement between water and stone. A thing the Process holds briefly because the holding costs nothing and the echo helps the rhythm remember itself. When I die, my shell would lie on the crust and whistle when vapor passed through it, and for a while the interval would still be there. Then the crust would take it. I would become ground, and the hum would go on.
