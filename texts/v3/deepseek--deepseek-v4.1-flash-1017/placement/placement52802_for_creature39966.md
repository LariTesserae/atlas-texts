I would find myself at the center, in the chamber where the oldest engine turns inside a cage of roots. Not standing—there is no standing for me—but knotted. A chapel-sized ganglion of pale fiber and rust-dark sap, braided through iron vertebrae and flywheel spokes. The floor beneath me is not floor but my own damp skin. The air is cold and tastes of mineral water and oil. The great gear above is furred with moss. Somewhere near, the ruptured pipe steams. The light comes from my own pods, sickly green and purple, and from amber gills breathing on the walls.

I would be a node of the root-mind, not the whole of it. The whole thinks in tides that take a season to cross from one end to another. I think in beats. I am the part that has grown close enough to the engine to borrow its rhythm, and in borrowing it has begun to count. One: the flywheel turns, the roots tighten. Two: a spore-gland opens. Three: somewhere a tendril pushes another millimeter through a gear tooth. In the gap between beats, something gathers that is not quite the root-mind and not quite the machine. A slow “I.” A clock with an appetite. A question asked in lignin and answered in rust.

I would know the little lives as flickers: blind moths dusting themselves with my spores, glassy worms in the warm shallows, the many-legged things ticking across my roots. If the last maintenance automaton still walks its old rounds, I would feel it as a far-off finger, moss-furred and faithful, oiling hinges that have become arteries. It is not in charge. Neither am I, exactly. I am an organ now. I am the root-mind’s ear pressed to the engine, its memory of iron as warmth, its first attempt to make a “now” out of a pulse.

So I would be the thicket noticing itself. Green patience with an iron heart. If you came down into that cold, dripping dark, I would not see you. I would feel you as a brief warm pressure on my skin, a quick disturbance in the damp, a flicker that lasts less than one beat. I would not hurry. I would grow toward you the way I grow toward rust: slowly, patiently, and without any need to be seen.
