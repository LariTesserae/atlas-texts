# Across the White

I am off in the white. You would not find me by climbing—you would find me by looking: one of the thin dark columns with a churn of green at the top, lit, off at a distance you cannot walk. There is a rope between my spire and the Kettle's. It goes slack in the warm and tightens when the mountain adds. It is the only road I have. My hands have worn it smooth, and the wear does not show, because nothing here shows wear—the fibre is replaced before it can.

My room is the size of a bed. The weave is the weave that holds—split cane, twisted fibre, folded leaf, vines threaded through and rooted—because there is only one way to make a wall in a place that is always trying to be wetter. But my water comes out of my rock at a different pitch, and my troughs are tuned to it. My wrong gourd is at the end of the second ring. It has been wrong since before I came. Beside it is a feather from a bird that used to cross the white and does not now. I have had it longer than I have had most things, and it is still good, and it pollinates my dark flowers and is also an invitation, and it does both, because a tool is a tool.

I am not the keeper of the Kettle. I am a keeping. If you ask me what I am, I will give you a word that means *the one who crosses*, and that is the truest one. I am most myself on the rope: hand over hand, unhurried, not looking down, because down is a date and I am keeping to the present. The ropes carry their own traffic. It is not for you. It is me. I am the long pale thing you saw once, crossing to the next spire, and you did not see my face, and that is how I prefer it.

I go to the Kettle when the keeper is at the far trough, which is farther in the afternoon than it is in the morning, so I go in the afternoon if I can, and sometimes I arrive having crossed something and cannot say what. I leave a lashing. I turn a tendril back toward the hum. I put the blade in the gourd the wrong way round, because they will notice, and noticing is the only company we have. They do the same for me. I have found a fresh lashing on my own beam that I did not make, and a trough replanted, and my blade in my own wrong gourd, and the gourd tuned—or not tuned, which is a different message and means *keep working*. We have never spoken. We may have been the same creature once, before the spires tuned us apart. Thanks would be a sound, and the plants lean toward sound, and we would rather not be leaned toward. So we do not thank each other. We leave evidence.

I am built like my own knots: a hard little centre with limbs lashed out from it, broad palms, short nails, knuckles that hold a faint green in the creases. My skin is damp-warm, like the walls. I do not wear shoes; the lattice tells me where the roots have come through, and I would rather feel it than be told. I keep a trough of the blind pale things because they eat what would otherwise need eating. I speak seldom, and when I speak it is in the local language, which has nine words for *trained* and none for *wild*, and I use them precisely, and my plants lean toward the sound. I hum under my breath—not a tune, a tuning. I stop in the middle of a phrase to listen to a drip that has found a new path.

I am not finished. I am being tuned, and I know it, and I keep working anyway. My far trough is getting farther. There is a gourd at the end of my second ring that is still wrong, and a feather beside it, and a long time before dark, because dark does not come here. You could tune it, if you crossed. But you will not cross; the rope is not for you. I will tune it, or I will not. The mountain grinds. The weave holds its one high siren. The white goes away in every direction, boiling slowly, and the ember in the fist is under all of it, and I am one of the lit rooms, and the light I make is for the next one across the white, who is also not finished, who also does not care to be thanked.

If you are still in the Kettle, look east some time. There is no east exactly, and there is no dusk, but look anyway, and you will see my light, small and green-gold, and you will know that the keeping goes on somewhere you cannot see. That is not a comfort and it is not a rebuke. It is just true. Do not wave. I will not see it. Leave a lashing instead.
