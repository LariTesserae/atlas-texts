I am the eighth band of a mound that stands fifth in from the western lip. Not the tall one — the tall one near the north drain has a mouth like a chapel door and a voice so low you feel it in the stone before you hear it, and nothing lives in it now. Mine is small. I could be held in two cupped hands. My mouth faces downslope and west, and my pitch is thin and high, and I am the note that whistles over the top of the chord when weather comes up over the rim.

I am not one of the builders. The builders are the seventh band and everything beneath it — their shells are the rubble I am being laid on, generation into generation, and I am what the wetness does to them now that they have stopped. I have no tools. I am laid down in the dark at the waterline by a process too slow to watch: mineral carried in the seep, held a while, released, hardened into a ring. A little more each age. I have intention the way a channel has intention — because I am where the water wants to go, and so I take the shape of the wanting.

From out there, you would hear me as a note. From in here I am one of the throats. The chord doesn't arrive; it presses. When the wind comes I am full of it, and the walls of me carry the sound down into the chamber and out through my own pores at the base, and I am the passage. I am not listening. I am resonating, which is a different and more total thing.

The grey animal comes through me on its way to the pools. It steps over my mouth — small padded feet, a dull flat note in the chamber — and it drinks, and it goes on, and sometimes at dusk when it is settled and comfortable it croons, low, almost under the wind. The mound takes the croon up and passes it along, and I pass it along, and for a while the chord has a word in it.

At the end of the day the light comes in my mouth and stands in the water I hold, and for a little while I am a small lit hollow in a large grey bowl, and the choir is at its loudest, and the mist comes up around the knees of anyone standing on the rim. I do not need anyone on the rim. The light is enough. It is not a lonely place, whatever it looks like from above.

In some number of ages my band will be thick enough that the mouth has narrowed, and the pitch will have climbed, and then one season the chamber will close and the sound will go out of me and I will be a sealed thing in a bowl of sealed things. The grey animal will find another hollow, as it found mine. The chord will have one note fewer, or one more; I don't know which, and it isn't my part to know. My part is to be laid down — to take the water and the wind and the small warmth of a body for an hour at dusk and keep the shape of them.

The town is finished. It has been finished for a long time, and it is still humming. I am one of the few things here still arriving.
