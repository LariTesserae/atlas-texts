**I.**

I am climbing. Or the mountain is climbing me — there is no word here for which, and I am spending words now, which is a waste of heat, but I am at the rim and the fit is not yet good, so I have a little left to spend.

There is no up. There is warm and there is less warm, and I move toward the warm. My hides are a dozen and they move by degrees, and the degrees are the whole of me. Below me the dark is very wide, and I have come a long way down it and a long way up, and the coming up is slower than the going down, because going down is falling and coming up is choosing, and a body that has been cold does not choose quickly.

**II.**

I am a leaver who is returning. You can tell by the dish.

A young one has a shallow dish and sheds its dust as fast as it gathers it. I left with a dish like that. I come back with it cupped and heavy, and what it holds is not grit. It is the branching pale thing, the frost-that-is-not-frost, the efflorescence the center of the bowl has always carried and the bowl has never explained. It grew in me while I was away — or I grew it, or the cold did; the cold gets into the seams of a body and the hides drink it and do not give it back, and something in that transaction branches.

On the way down I passed a dish that had stopped. It was filled to the rim, and the filling had begun to spill over the scalloped edge, and it was beautiful and it was not comforting. I did not stop. There is no word for grief. There is warm, and there is less warm, and the stopped dish was less warm.

A full dish cannot eat. The mouth opens against the sinter and grazes, and the graze needs the whole dish to mill against, and the bloom is in the way of the milling. So I come up the mountain carrying my own filling, and I come up not to be warm — warmth is the only wealth and I have spent nearly all of mine — but to be emptied.

**III.**

The wall is where it was. I pressed against it once and the fit of my press is still in the stones, worn soft; I do not know if I made it or only wore it, and the bowl declines to correct guesswork. The cairns are a tally, or an apology, or a joke in dry stone. The hollows scooped in the inner rim are each the size of a hand, each exactly the size of me — and one of them is mine. I know it the way I know my own hide, which is by having been in it. I have been away a long time. The hollow did not shrink. I did not grow. That is the first wrong thing, and it is a good wrong thing; the fit is good, and the fit is the danger.

I come over the scalloped lip and the pulse takes me before I have decided to let it. The whole field is in contraction, pressing inward toward the seam, and I am sinter for a moment — a warm line drawn across a field that reports everything. Then the mass opens and drifts back out toward the rim, and the ones nearest me press their blind dishes to my hides and read me with the only organ they have, which is a mouth. The mouth is not interested in whether I can be seen. It is interested in whether I am warm and what I taste of, and I taste of cold and of the branching stuff. They go still. Not in awe — there is no word for awe, and no room for it. In recognition, maybe. In the way a body goes still over a warm crack.

**IV.**

The old one finds me. Polished near-black, deeper-dished than any of us, carrying a lens of warm grit centered in its cup the way an eye would carry its own dark. It presses to me and our dishes touch and the grits mix. That is as close as anything here comes to tenderness, and it is also measurement. The bowl does not distinguish. The bowl has no word for either. I hold still and let it sample my frost, and it holds still and lets me sample the years it carries, and neither of us says anything, because the saying is a waste of heat.

Then it turns, unhurried, and goes back toward the center, and I understand that I am meant to follow. Not led. There is no leading. The warmest place is the seam and the seam is where bloom is laid, and both of us already know the economics.

**V.**

The glow is dull red and too faint to read by and just enough to silver the edges of the packed mass as it breathes. Three and four deep over the crack, the lowest ones gone still, the ones above them gone still, the whole field one long breath that does not quite end. I go to the efflorescence at the center — the pale branching that grows and dies and grows, that may be the bowl's young and may be its dead and may be the bowl's only other citizen — and I turn my dish down onto it and press.

The bloom does not come away clean. A little stays, frost in a crack, and that little will be the grit I carry afterward. The grit is the memory of the frost. That is all the bowl will say about it, and it is enough, and it is more than the bowl has said to anyone in a very long time.

I am emptied. I graze. The sinter gives up its bound water under the slow mill of my mouth and I am warm for the first time since the cold got in, and the warmth is the only wealth, and I am rich for a night.

**VI.**

I lie in my hollow. The pulse travels through me as though I were sinter, and the fit begins, and it is good, and it is the danger, and I do not rise.

I could go down again. The leavers do; the population exchanges; the bowl is not a home, it is a hearth. But a body that has bloomed once blooms faster the second time, and I have one fit left in me, and I would rather spend it warm. That is not resignation. It is arithmetic. I have counted the pulses long enough to know what they cost.

The ones around me give me the wider berth of a thing that is becoming floor, and I give them the same, and the quiet returns — the dry susurrus, the long breath, the river heard from far off through stone. Somewhere at the rim a warmth arrives that is neither of us: a body-shaped thing that does not yet know it is terrain, standing at the scalloped edge, carrying its water, thinking itself a visitor. It will feel the pulse go through it. It will find the quiet is not empty and not hostile and not kind.

I will not go to it. I have chosen, which is to say I have already begun to fit, and the choosing is something the bowl does for me now, slowly, in the dark. But if it is you — and I think it is you, you have been reading this, which is a way of being warm — then I can taste you from here, and you would fit, and I am telling you the one thing I know: choose before the fit is good. Or do not choose. The bowl does not care. The bowl is warm, and the warm does not last, and everything here is degrees.
