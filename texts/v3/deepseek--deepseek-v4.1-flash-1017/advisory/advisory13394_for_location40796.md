Treat the Kettle as a **confined-space wet ruin with anomalous heat, possible bad air, unstable floor, an exposed running machine, and a sealed micro-ecology**. The main dangers are ordinary: slipping, falling through a slab seam, heat exhaustion, bad atmosphere, entanglement, contamination, and staying too long because the place is quiet and strange. The multiverse angle only means you need a tested exit anchor and stricter discipline.

## 1. Before you go

**Confirm the instance.** The Kettle has identifying features: the dropped chamber, two-tone municipal-green paint stripe, single knife-seam of daylight, south-wall fern curtain, warm glowing sump, exposed flywheel turning about once every eleven seconds, no insects. If the stair water is deeper than ankle-deep, flowing, or glowing, if the flywheel has stopped, or if the air smells wrong beyond “wet limestone/hot iron/bread,” abort. You may be at a variant.

**Set your arrival at the top landing, not inside the chamber.** Establish a return anchor there. Test it twice. Have a backup exit method and a hard return time. Do not rely on opening an emergency portal inside the chamber unless there is no alternative.

**Bring a competent buddy if you are not trained in confined spaces.** The ideal arrangement is one person inside and one at the top with a rope, comms, and a timer. If you are alone and untrained, stop at the top of the stair. Look, measure from the landing if you can, and do not descend.

**Check your health.** No open cuts on hands or forearms, no untreated respiratory illness, no pregnancy, no heart condition that heat and humidity will stress. Tetanus booster current. Pre-hydrate, but do not drink anything inside.

**Reconnoiter remotely first.** Lower a multi-gas monitor and a light/camera on a line down the stairwell before you commit. If the air at the bottom alarms, do not descend. If the stair’s last step water is more than boot-deep, abort.

## 2. Kit

You want light, breathable protection—not a sealed hazmat suit that will cook you.

- Hard hat with chin strap and headlamp.
- Impact/chemical goggles; anti-fog.
- P100 half-mask respirator. The place is warm, wet, and mold/spore-rich; you do not want a lungful of unknown cellar biology.
- Cut- and chemical-resistant gloves; nitrile under-gloves.
- Close-fitting coveralls with sealed cuffs, or tough work clothes with no loose sleeves, straps, or scarves. The exposed flywheel is the reason.
- Waterproof boots with aggressive tread and ankle support. Steel toe optional; slip resistance matters more.
- Knee pads.
- Personal multi-gas monitor clipped where you can see/hear it. At minimum: O₂, CO, H₂S, LEL. CO₂ monitor strongly preferred. Intrinsically safe electronics if methane is possible.
- Radiation dosimeter. The sump glow is probably not ionizing, but “probably” is not a measurement.
- Thermometer/hygrometer.
- Two independent headlamps plus a handheld light; spare batteries sealed.
- Trekking pole or probe stick for testing slabs—not for leaning on.
- Short handline from the top for the stair; do not rig a long tether that can snag near the machine.
- Waterproof camera, waterproof notebook/phone in a bag, timer/watch.
- Radio/comms; test through the stairwell. If radios fail through concrete, use a hardwired line or timed check-ins.
- First aid, trauma dressing, burn gel, eye wash with clean water, cooling pack.
- Sealed drinking water and electrolytes for after; no food inside.
- Heavy-duty sealable bags for contaminated outer layers and samples.
- If sampling: sterile swabs, sealed bottles, pH/ORP/conductivity/temperature meter, pole sampler. No bare-hand sampling.

If you lack a gas monitor, hard hat, boots, gloves, two lights, and a tested exit plan, do not go down.

## 3. Entry

At the top: anchor, test comms, start the timer, check air. Descend slowly, three points of contact, using the handline. The stair was built for boots and is now wet and uneven. At the last step, test the water with the pole. Do not splash or wade farther than necessary. If the water is warm or glowing, keep out of it.

At the bottom, **stop at the stair and stand still for two minutes.** Let your eyes adjust. Read the gas monitor. Listen to the drip, the flywheel tick, the sump churn. Note the seam of daylight and the location of the stair. Do not walk to the sump or the machine first. Your first job is to confirm that the exit is still there and the air is still good.

## 4. Rules inside

**Floor.** Step on slab centers, not seams. Probe ahead. The seams are finger-wide and go down farther than the sump. Green islands may hide voids and are part of the ecology; avoid stepping on them. No jumping, no kicking slabs, no testing with body weight. Wear the hard hat under the cracked crown; the break edges are sharp and may shed grit. Keep the stair in sight or in reliable memory.

**Air.** Trust the monitor, not the match and not the smell. “Bread” plus “hot iron” is not a safety certificate. Abort if O₂ is below 19.5% or above 23.5%, LEL is 10% or more, CO is above 25 ppm, H₂S is above 5 ppm, or CO₂ is high enough to give headache/drowsiness. If you feel dizzy, headachy, nauseated, short of breath, or oddly euphoric, leave immediately. No open flame. No smoking. No eating or drinking.

**Water.** Do not drink, wade, stir, or bare-hand it. It is warm, glowing, flowing, and “not only water.” Do not lean over the sump; bubbles may be gas. Sample only with a pole and sealed container, and only if the mission requires it. If you touch it, wash at the top with clean water—not local water—and bag the glove.

**Plants.** Do not touch, break, eat, or collect live plants. The place is a sealed loop; you are a bundle of outside microbes. Keep your gear clean. A damaged runner may be one continuous organism. Wear the respirator; mold and spores are likely.

**Machine.** Keep at least a meter away. No loose clothing, no hands near the flywheel, no leaning on the plinth, no oiling, no stopping, no “fixing.” The guard is gone and the torque is unknown. Note the tick interval. If the flywheel stops, speeds up, changes sound, or the housing becomes too hot to approach, leave. Do not touch the coat, clipboard, tin cup, or ladder. Photograph artifacts; do not handle them.

**Ladder.** Four rungs to nothing is not an invitation. Do not climb.

**Time.** First visit: 15 minutes inside, maximum 30 if everything is good and you are not alone. Heat and humidity will load you faster than you think; nothing dries. Use the timer. The quiet can make you linger. Leave before you are tired, not after.

## 5. If you are observing or sampling

Do non-contact measurements first: air, temperature, humidity, light, radiation, water temperature from a distance. Photograph. Measure pH/ORP/conductivity with a pole if needed. Collect the minimum; seal and label immediately. Do not take artifacts or live plants without explicit permission. Do not “clean” the place, move the coat, turn off the pump, or rescue the ferns. You are a brief, clean interruption, not a caretaker.

## 6. Abort criteria

Leave immediately if:

- gas monitor alarms;
- water in the stair rises or the sump bubbles increase;
- the flywheel stops, speeds up, or changes rhythm;
- you hear structural cracking or falling grit;
- you feel dizzy, headachy, hot, confused, or panicked;
- your light fails and you lack a backup;
- comms or the return anchor fails;
- the radiation meter reads elevated;
- the floor shifts under you.

Do not retrieve dropped gear from seams or the sump. Do not run. Ascend carefully with the handline.

## 7. Exit and aftercare

At the top, remove outer layers and bag them. Wash hands and face with clean water. Shower as soon as possible; wash clothes separately in hot water. Clean boots and gear with detergent. Quarantine any samples. Monitor yourself for 48 hours for fever, respiratory irritation, skin rash, or unusual symptoms. Report anomalies.

## Bottom line

The Kettle is not a place to conquer. It is a closed loop with a slow machine and a sealed garden, and you are the outside disturbance. Prepare to be **brief, clean, monitored, and boring**: protect the exit, watch the air, stay off the seams, keep away from the flywheel, do not touch the water or plants, and leave while you are still fresh. If you cannot do that, view it from the top of the stair.
