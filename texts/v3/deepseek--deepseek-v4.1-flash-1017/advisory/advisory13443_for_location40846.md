Prepare as if you are entering a living glacier cave that is also a slow mind and a hive: not a cave with life in it, but a life with a cave in it. Your job is to be a brief, clean, quiet, retrievable visitor. The room will incorporate your warmth, your water, your shed cells, your breath, and your sound. Minimize all of that, and leave before you become part of the furniture.

**Before you decide to enter**
- Screen yourself. Do not enter if you have claustrophobia, panic attacks, vestibular problems, heart conditions, respiratory disease, immune compromise, open wounds, or a tendency to sensory overload. The chamber is small, stooped, wet, loud, dim, and humid; the low chord can cause nausea, headache, fatigue, and trance-like time distortion.
- Do not enter alone if you can avoid it. Best: one person inside, one outside at the slot. If two go in, stay in sight, use hand signals, and agree in advance: no touching, no sampling, no splitting up, no lingering.
- Set a hard time limit before entry. A few minutes is enough. Use a silent vibrating timer, not an audible alarm. The room’s rhythm will stretch your sense of duration; do not trust it.
- Treat the air as suspect. A biogenic mat in a closed chamber may mean high humidity, low oxygen, high CO2, or unknown fibers. If you carry a gas monitor, check at the slot and keep it on your body. If you do not, keep the visit short and leave at the first headache, breathlessness, dizziness, or confusion.
- Decide your ethics before you go. You are not owed access. The room is unfinished, alive, and indifferent. Do not try to “help” it finish, communicate with it, feed it, sample it, mark it, or perform for it.

**What to wear**
- Close-fitting, smooth, non-linting layers. Avoid cotton, down, loose fleece, dangling straps, jewelry, and anything that sheds. You want insulation that still works damp, and an outer layer that does not crinkle or snag.
- Soft-soled shoes or boots with shallow treads, cleaned before entry. No mud, no spikes, no deep lugs that carry outside biota in and chamber material out. Accept that you will step on growth; minimize it, do not drag your feet, do not kneel or sit.
- Thin intact gloves, long sleeves, covered hair, warm cap. Not because you will touch things, but because accidental contact is likely in a wet folded space. Cover any open wound waterproof.
- Avoid contacts; wear glasses if you need them. The air is saturated and full of drifting fibers. A simple filter mask is a judgment call: it may help with fibers, but it will wet out and should not be trusted. If you have respiratory issues, do not enter.
- No perfume, sunscreen, insect repellent, lotions, or scented anything. They are chemicals, and the room treats chemicals as food or insult. No loose objects in pockets. Everything zipped, sealed, and worn tight to the body.

**What to carry**
Carry as little as possible, and carry nothing you are not willing to have colonized or consumed if it is left behind. The room does not tolerate the made; it gathers around metal, rope, chalk, jars, drones, lights, and offerings, and in a year or ten they are feedstock.
- A dim red light, used sparingly. Let your eyes adapt to the blue. Do not use flash, floodlights, flares, chemical glow, or open flame. Unknown gases, wet surfaces, and a living chamber make fire a bad idea.
- A silent timer, a small sealed pouch, a mask if needed, hearing protection if you know low-frequency sound bothers you. Musicians’ plugs are better than full blocking; you still need to hear the room and your companion.
- No food, no drink opened inside, no samples, no markers, no chalk, no cairns, no rope left in place, no drone, no music, no recordings played aloud. If you must photograph, use available light or a very dim lamp, no flash, and keep the camera on your body.
- If you need a safety line, use it only if necessary, anchor it outside or to yourself, and retrieve it. Do not anchor to growth. Do not leave anything “for later.”

**At the slot**
- Pause. Listen. Feel the warm edge. Check footing, water level, and head clearance. A tall person will stoop. Wear a soft cap; watch the ice lid.
- Keep the exit unobstructed. Do not block the slot with your pack or your body. Know how you will turn around.
- Enter slowly. Short steps. Test the floor before weight. The mat is folded, wet, and slippery; water lenses hide holes. Do not lean on walls. Do not touch the ice. Do not touch the cold stone faces where water flash-freezes; frostbite is fast and the report is sharp.
- Do not push through hanging curtains of growth. Move around them or leave.

**Inside**
- Stay near the exit. The chamber is small, but it is densely full. Do not wander behind growth. Keep the slot at your back or in sight.
- Let your eyes adjust. The blue is barely light. Bright light will blind you and add a foreign pulse. Use the dim lamp only when you must.
- Watch the thermal gradient. The mat is body-warm; the walls and ice are cold; the water is wet and often below the temperature where it should freeze. Do not put your hands in the growth or the standing water. Do not kneel. Do not sit. Keep moving slowly, but do not work up a sweat; saturated air will not dry you.
- Breathe through your nose if you can. Keep your mouth closed. If you cough or sneeze, turn toward the exit and cover. Do not spit, urinate, defecate, eat, or drink inside. If you need to, leave.
- The note is loud. Raise your voice only if necessary; do not shout, sing, tap rhythms, or play recordings. The room sorts sound; your voice is noise and data. Keep it minimal.
- Do not stare at the slow pulse of light. It can pull you into a trance. If you feel euphoria, drowsiness, nausea, chest pressure, or time slipping, leave. The room is not hostile, but it is absorptive.
- Keep gear on your body. Do not set anything down. If you drop something, retrieve it only if it is safe and does not require digging into growth. If you cannot retrieve it, leave and report it. Do not dig.
- If you fall, do not thrash. Protect your head, get up slowly, check yourself, and leave if injured. Do not brush growth into your mouth or eyes.

**Exit and after**
- Leave at your timer, not when the room “finishes” or responds. It will not. It is unfinished and will remain so.
- Do not linger in the slot. Outside, check your clothing and gear for hitchhikers. Brush off away from the site. Change out of wet clothes, warm up, dry off, drink and eat outside. Watch for delayed dizziness, nausea, tinnitus, respiratory irritation, or cold injury.
- Do not carry out samples, water, growth, or “souvenirs.” Do not bring outside mud, seeds, pets, or canaries in. You are the contaminant; act like it.
- The sound will follow you. That is normal. If it persists as ringing or pain, get medical attention.

The short version: go in clean, quiet, warm, and brief; touch nothing; leave nothing; do not try to talk to it; do not wait for it to notice you; and accept that the room will close the water you tracked in and take the warmth of your standing there, and go on tuning itself in the dark without you.
