My intuition: the Kettle is not a wilderness or a ruin. It is a working town inside a fresh geological wound — a hot, dripping, sharp-edged machine for making heat and water meet. You are an ordinary soft human: a warm, wet, water-filled body that produces heat, sweat, waste, and mistakes. Prepare to be a guest in a cramped industrial basement where the ceiling sweats, the floor is warm, water is property, and the main dangers are cumulative rather than dramatic: heat, bad air, slips, cuts, dehydration, contaminated water, corrosion, and claustrophobia.

The Kettle will not kill you instantly if you are careful. It will wear you down if you are casual. Preparation should be about **layers, lines, limits, and local permission**.

## Before you go

1. **Get a local contact and permission first.**  
   The Kettle is a settlement, not an abandoned site. Ask before entering. Ask who controls the entry shaft/winch/ladder, whether there are fees or water rations, where visitors may sleep, and which galleries are closed. The town is organized around catchments, gutters, condensers, and sumps; touching the wrong pipe or standing in the wrong drip zone can be a property issue, not just a nuisance.

2. **Ask specific current-condition questions.**  
   - Is the entry winch/ladder sound? Has anything collapsed recently?  
   - Which cracks are active? Which catwalks are load-limited?  
   - When is the foundry running? Where does its vent discharge condense?  
   - Which water is potable, who owns it, and what does it cost?  
   - Where are the sumps and old foundation pits?  
   - What is the evacuation signal and muster point?  
   - Is there a medical person, and where?  
   - Are there rules about lights, photography, climbing, or touching machinery?

3. **Establish an exit plan and a timed check-in.**  
   The Kettle is a sinkhole. Exit may depend on one winch, one ladder, or one shaft. Confirm it works. Set a return time with someone outside. If you have a multiverse anchor or return method, set it at the **surface rim**, not inside the cavity, and test it. Do not rely on it as your only exit. Carry a whistle and agree on rope/whistle signals if radios fail — rock and metal will swallow radio.

4. **Do not go alone.**  
   Use a local guide and a buddy. The place is small enough to cross in minutes if you know the ways and confusing enough to get you lost vertically. A guide is also your social passport and water-rights interpreter.

5. **Screen yourself for claustrophobia and heat tolerance.**  
   There is no sky, perpetual dusk, low ceilings, dense sound, and hot humid lower air. If enclosed spaces or heat make you panic, do not go, or stay only in upper, well-ventilated areas with a guide. Panic in a vertical stack of catwalks and ladders is a real hazard.

6. **Medical prep.**  
   Tetanus up to date. First aid for cuts, burns, heat illness, and blisters. Personal medications in waterproof packaging. If you carry antibiotics, carry only what is prescribed for you. Do not plan to “tough out” heat illness or a bad cut in a damp salt environment.

## What to carry

Pack light but layered. Water is heavy; gear must be hands-free and narrow.

- **Clothing:** loose, breathable, quick-dry long sleeves and trousers for cuts and dust; a light rain shell or poncho for localized condensation drip; spare socks; a fire-resistant outer layer if you will be near the foundry; no loose straps or dangling jewelry. Avoid heavy waterproof clothing — you will cook.
- **Head and hands:** hard hat for low ceilings and rockfall; impact goggles; cut-resistant gloves; earplugs for machinery but keep ears open for alarms; a dust mask/respirator — at least N95/FFP2, better P100 if you will be in dusty or condensate-aerosol areas.
- **Feet:** sturdy boots with good grip and ankle support; heat-tolerant soles; spare socks and foot powder. Streets are gutters and puddles stand in sumps; expect slippery, salty, abrasive ground.
- **Light:** headlamp with red mode, spare batteries, backup handheld light, glow stick. The place is perpetual dusk; sodium lamps make dim islands. Do not blind people with white light.
- **Air and heat:** if possible, a calibrated multi-gas monitor for O2, CO, CO2, and combustibles. At minimum, ask locals where bad air pools and avoid sumps and low pits. Carry a small thermometer/hygrometer and a battery fan if you can. In hot humid lower galleries, sweat does not evaporate well; wet-bulb heat is the thing to respect.
- **Water:** your own bottles/bladder, a filter, purification tablets/UV, electrolyte powder. Treat all local water as suspect unless a trusted local says otherwise. Condensate on cold pipes may look clean but has touched metal, rock dust, and salt; sump water is worst.
- **Food:** high-calorie, low-bulk, familiar food. The Kettle has no animals and only sparse lichen/salt-moss; do not forage. Local food is likely imported and may be fine, but ask and eat cooked food.
- **Protection from corrosion and damp:** dry bags, silica gel, waterproof map/phone pouch, zip ties, a little sealant, a rag. Salt crusts bloom and eat metal. Rinse and dry gear later.
- **Navigation and communication:** paper map in plastic, watch, whistle, notebook, local currency/trade goods. A rope and harness only if you are trained and the locals approve; do not improvise vertical travel.
- **Health and comfort:** first aid, antiseptic, blister kit, barrier cream, eye mask, earplugs, spare batteries, high-energy snacks, water. A small towel and dry socks are luxuries.
- **Trade goods:** water-filter cartridges, good sealant, rope, lamp batteries, durable socks, small tools. Offer, do not impose. Do not bring drones, open-flame stoves, pets, or conspicuous weapons unless explicitly required.

## Arrival and orientation

Arrive at the surface rim, not directly inside if you can choose. The dry plain above is a different hazard: sun, exposure, and a fresh sinkhole edge. Use an established path and rope near the rim; fresh basalt is sharp and may still be settling.

Descend only by the established route — winch, stairs, or fixed ladder — with a guide. Check air at each level. If your gas monitor alarms, or you feel dizzy, headachey, confused, or short of breath, go **up and out**, not down. CO2 can pool in low sumps; foundry exhaust can add CO; the lower galleries are hotter and thicker.

On arrival, orient before you explore:

- Locate the exit, the muster point, the medical point, the latrines, and a cooler/ventilated rest spot.
- Note where condensation rain is heaviest: under cold pipes, condensers, and the foundry vent path. Do not stand under dripping machinery without permission; it may be someone’s catchment or a contamination source.
- Identify sumps and foundation pits and stay out of them.
- Ask where you may sleep. Prefer upper, ventilated, drier rooms away from pump noise and foundry fumes.
- Learn the local water rules before touching a roof, gutter, pipe, or puddle.

## Moving through the Kettle

- **Keep hands free and gear tight.** The place is vertical and crowded: catwalks bridge gaps, ladders drop through floors, terraces are narrow. Use three points of contact. One person at a time on ladders and suspect catwalks. Do not jump. Do not trust an old railing.
- **Wear your helmet.** The rock is young, sharp, and still settling; lintels crack and the town repairs itself in patches. Watch overhead and underfoot.
- **Do not touch pipes, valves, gauge glass, wiring, or machinery** unless invited and instructed. Pumps thud, heat exchangers tick, condensers drip; they are working infrastructure, not scenery.
- **Do not block gutters or catchment roofs.** Streets are gutters; roofs are catchments. Water is the town’s organizing principle and likely its politics. Ask before climbing on a roof, standing under a drip, or filling a bottle.
- **Avoid open flame.** Use battery lights. Do not cook with a stove in enclosed galleries. Combustion in a closed sinkhole is a bad idea.
- **Near the foundry:** wear eye protection and fire-resistant outer layers, use ear protection, stay out of fume paths, and know the uphill/outward route. The foundry mouth is red and dry; the chimney vents into the rock ceiling, and vapors can condense and rain down elsewhere. Do not linger under that return path.
- **Use light discipline.** Headlamp on low or red in shared spaces. Keep a backup light on your body.
- **Listen.** The sound is muffled and close; pumps cycle, valves sigh, water drips. Do not rely on animals for warning — there are none. Use your instruments, your guide, and your whistle.

## Heat, air, water, food, health

- **Heat:** the floor is warm and lower galleries hold thick air. Work in short stretches, rest often, drink before thirst, and take electrolytes. In humid heat, sweat does not cool you well; a fan and rest in a ventilated upper area matter. If you stop sweating, feel confused, or get a headache, treat it as heat illness and get to cooler, moving air.
- **Air:** avoid low sumps and pits. Use a gas monitor if you have one. If you feel dizzy or short of breath, move upward immediately. Do not lie down in a low place.
- **Water:** treat all water. Filter plus chemical/UV, or boil if safe and permitted. Do not drink from puddles, sumps, or random drips. Carry your own water and pay for safe refills. Do not waste water; it is heavy, scarce, and owned.
- **Food:** eat familiar, high-calorie food; wash hands; keep food sealed. Do not forage lichen or salt-moss.
- **Cuts and skin:** wash cuts immediately with clean water, antiseptic, and cover. Damp salt environments invite infection. Dry your feet, change socks, use powder. Watch for heat rash and fungal irritation. Rinse salt and dust off skin when you can.
- **Sleep:** there is no sky and no normal day. Use a watch, set a sleep schedule, use an eye mask and earplugs. Avoid heavy alcohol and sedatives; they dehydrate and impair judgment. Sleep in the coolest safe, ventilated place you are offered.

## Social rules

Be a quiet guest. The Kettle is small, dense, and sounds carry. Ask before photographing people or machinery. Do not wander into cellars, repairs, or foundry floors. Do not complain loudly about heat or water; locals live with it. If offered water or food, accept graciously or decline politely. Pay for water, guides, lodging, and refills. Offer useful trade goods rather than charity. The safest posture is: **curious, slow, clean, and permission-seeking.**

## Emergency rules

- **If lost:** stop. Do not descend hoping to find a way out. Use your whistle, conserve light and water, and stay where a searcher can find you. If you must move, move toward known upper/ventilated space, not into sumps or unknown lower galleries.
- **If air feels bad:** move up and out. Do not investigate.
- **If fire or foundry accident:** evacuate upward/outward along the known route. Do not go down.
- **If rockfall or collapse:** helmet on, get clear, do not re-enter. The Kettle is young and still settling; fresh cracks are not decorative.
- **If the exit is blocked:** go to the local muster point and follow local instructions. Have a secondary plan only if locals confirm it is safe.

## After you leave

Rinse and dry gear; salt and damp corrode everything. Clean boots and clothing. Monitor yourself for cuts, rashes, respiratory irritation, and heat exhaustion. If your multiverse travel protocols require quarantine or decontamination, do it — the Kettle is a human settlement, not a sterile site. Write down what you learned: which routes were safe, which water was potable, when the foundry ran, where the drips fell, who to ask.

The short version: **arrive at the rim with a guide, a helmet, a respirator, a headlamp, your own water, and an exit plan; stay out of sumps, foundry fumes, and other people’s catchments; move slowly; treat every drip as suspect; and remember that in the Kettle, heat rises, water condenses, and you are the soft, sweaty thing in the middle of the machine.**
