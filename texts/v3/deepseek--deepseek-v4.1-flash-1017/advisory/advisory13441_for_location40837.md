**How to prepare for Vaeless, for an ordinary human multiverse traveler**

Prepare to become **quieter, drier, slower, and less certain**. Vaeless is not hostile, but it is not built for you. It is a young, wet, dim, half-empty city that keeps itself and is larger inside than its own skin. The right preparation is less about survival gear than about **low-impact comportment**: soft voice, waterproof layers, patient feet, dim light, and a mind that does not try to solve the place out loud.

### 1. Pack for wet dimness, not wilderness
- **Clothing:** breathable quick-dry layers, light long sleeves and trousers for brushing through crowded green, a hooded rain shell you can put on in seconds, spare socks, foot powder, antifungal cream. Avoid cotton. The air beads on your skin; every surface sweats.
- **Footwear:** closed-toe, grippy, already broken in. The streets are warm-grey and drink rain fast, but the edges, moss, and well-courts are slick. You will walk wide empty distances between encounters.
- **Bags:** rolltop waterproof pack, dry bags inside for electronics and sleep gear. Keep gear quiet — no clanking carabiners, no rattling tins.
- **Light:** low-lumen amber or red headlamp and a small backup. The city glows pale at night and is dim by day; white floodlights are rude and blinding. Bring a sleep mask — there is no true dark.
- **Health:** anti-nausea tablets or ginger for the “bigger inside than out” interiors; electrolyte tabs; vitamin D if you stay long; first aid; any personal meds. The humidity is the main ordinary-human nuisance: chafing, foot rot, moldy gear. A small towel and dry socks matter more than a knife.
- **Water/food:** carry a filter or purification tablets and basic rations. The well-court water is described as warm, clean, mineral — but you are foreign biology. Ask, filter, or both. Do not pollute cisterns; do not wash clothes in them.
- **No drones, no pets, no seeds, no loud speakers, no repair kit for the city’s conduits.** Clean your boots and gear before arrival; do not carry mud or seed into a place that is still becoming.

### 2. Arrival: stand still before you walk
If you can choose, arrive in the morning before the first rain pass. Find a low rise — but not a sharp fresh ridge — and just stand. Let your eyes adapt to the filtered, underwater noon. Look for the well-courts: they are the bright coins of the city and your best orientation nodes. Then go sit at the edge of one. Do not wade. Children wade; adults sit. Use the water as the locals do: somewhere to rest your eyes and let conversation pause.

In your first hour, ask quietly — low voice — about rain timing, drinking water, where a traveler may rest, which edges of the floodplain are soft, and whether the horned grazer is to be given a wide berth. Do not ask why corridors are longer than buildings. Do not measure. Do not announce surprise. The city’s spatial patience is not a spectacle; it is a fitted window.

### 3. Walk as a guest, not a surveyor
- **Stay on the warm-grey surfaces and walkways.** The low ridges are sharp, unweathered, and still collapsing softly. The floodplain verges drop “like a coin in a well” and no one looks up — that does not mean you should stand there.
- **Follow the detours.** The conduits, gutters, and transit rails bend around trees, ponds, single old figs. Do not cut through, prune, or force a straighter line. The green was let back; respect the courtesy.
- **Do not touch the pale translucent walls or conduits.** They carry light, they are never mended, and they never fail. That is the city’s contract with itself. Do not test it, lean on it, attach things to it, or try to repair anything that looks broken. If something seems wrong, leave it and tell a local quietly.
- **Keep groups together in interiors.** Doors open onto rooms larger than the building allows. Do not split up. Note the door you entered by. Do not count paces aloud. If a corridor runs long, keep walking calmly; if you feel dizzy, sit, look at water or a pale wall, breathe, and exit the way you came. Ordinary humans get motion-sick from impossible geometry; treat it like a boat, not a puzzle.
- **Do not mark walls or leave breadcrumbs.** Use a small waterproof notebook and landmarks: well-courts, the low rise, the floodplain edge, the pale glow of particular walls. If lost, return to the nearest well-court and wait or ask quietly.

### 4. Voice and social manner
The city is sparse — designed for three or four times its population — and the emptiness has presence. It is not lonely; it is generous. Do not mistake quiet for hostility, and do not overfill it. Speak low. Sound carries farther than it should through wide pale streets, so no speakerphone, no music, no calling out. When conversation runs out, let it. Sit by the water. Give people room. Ask permission before entering interiors or photographing. Do not stare at the few children. Do not treat the spatial oddities as a show.

The people walk unhurried because the indefinite tech does most of the daily work. They speak as though the buildings were asleep. Match that. Be polite to the city the way they are — a courtesy toward an old clock that has never needed winding.

### 5. Daily rhythm and body care
The day is long, dim, and punctuated by three or four short soft rain passes. Carry rain gear always; the clouds never quite commit, then they do. The rain is the loudest thing in Vaeless — drumming on cistern courtyards and flat pale roofs — so plan walks between passes and use the rain as a cue to sit under cover or at a well-court edge. Keep a normal sleep schedule with an alarm, sleep mask, and maybe earplugs for the rain; the city never goes truly dark. Use dim red/amber light at night. Eat and hydrate on schedule; the light will not tell your body what time it is.

Avoid the floodplain edge after rain, do not approach the horned calm grazer — it is avoided by custom, not fear — and do not feed the birds. The insects are busy but do not bite; the green is thick but not hostile. Your main risks are slick footing, soft ground, spatial disorientation, and your own loudness.

### 6. The mindset to carry
Vaeless is mid-sentence. The land is new, the ridges still sharp, the city holding rain it has not yet learned to let go. You are a guest in a place that maintains itself and does not explain. The correct preparation is not to understand it but to **move through it without adding noise**: low voice, dry feet, dim light, patient steps, no repairs, no measurements, no spectacle. Let the well-courts be your anchors. Let the rain set your pauses. Treat the emptiness as room, not absence. Leave no trace but quiet thanks.
