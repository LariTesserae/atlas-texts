Treat the Loud as four environments at once: a musical instrument, a wetland, a construction site, and a nursery for something nanoscale and alive. An ordinary human can visit the edges safely, but the interior is an expedition, not a hike. Prepare to protect your senses and your footing, not to “conquer” the place.

## Before you go

**Condition for soft, ringing ground.** Practice walking on boggy, uneven, drum-like surfaces with a light pack. Learn short strides, bent knees, rolled footfalls, no dragging, no hard heel strikes. Bring soft-soled boots with drainage and gaiters; no cleats, hard heels, or metal studs. Pad metal gear so carabiners and bottles do not clatter.

**Get a hearing baseline and plan for hearing damage.** The crews’ flattened speech is a warning. Bring custom-moulded earplugs plus electronic earmuffs with impulse limiting and pass-through speech. You need to hear the basin’s grammar and alarms, but you also need attenuation. Do not rely on noise-cancelling headphones alone. Carry spare plugs, keep them on a lanyard, and take quiet breaks at the rim. If you get tinnitus, muffled hearing, chest pressure, nausea, or anxiety from infrasound, leave.

**Pack as for warm, wet, spore-heavy, nanotech-contaminated country.** Quick-dry layers, rain shell, spare socks, foot powder, gloves, helmet, goggles, insect netting, repellent, antihistamines, blister and wound care, antiseptic, dressings, oral rehydration, any prescribed antibiotics, and a robust first-aid kit. Cover skin where you can, but choose breathable fabrics; cotton is miserable here. Bring a P100/FFP3 respirator and spare filters. Seal electronics with desiccant. Carry red/white headlamps, spare batteries, a satellite/emergency beacon, rope, a probe pole, and soft temporary route markers.

**Do not bring loud things.** No drones, generators, loud music, open fires, or heavy cook systems. No digging or tent stakes into the mat. Use a freestanding tent or weighted shelter. Carry sealed water and cold, sealed food; do not drink local water or eat local anything. Pack out all waste.

**Hire local crew and take the rim seriously.** Stage at the rim before entering. Use guided day trips first. Ask about current channels, squall cycles, hull-beast routes, and the sound grammar. Do not trust any map as a shape-map; the basin is younger than its surveys. Use the rim bearing, altimeter, real-time acoustics, and local guidance, and recheck your route daily.

## Arriving and moving

Put on hearing protection before you get close. The roar is weather, and it is tiring. Acclimatise at the rim; do not enter on the day you arrive. Establish a base with a clear exit and a check-in schedule.

On the mat, walk in time with the chorus. Short steps, soft landings, spread weight. Stand still occasionally and feel the pulse of other walkers. If the ground begins to “sing” — a new sustained tone or pressure through the soles — stop and move carefully to thicker, higher, or older ground, away from thin mat and open channels. The warm dark below is the lift, and it is nearer than it looks.

Use hand signals and rope tugs rather than shouting. A shout carries kilometres and the basin answers. If you must make an emergency noise, do it, but otherwise let your footsteps disappear into the chorus.

Probe before you wade. Pools appear and vanish; channels open like breathing. Do not cross unknown water. After a squall, wait and listen before moving: the low chord means the lift has shifted and a channel may open within the day.

## Weather, water, and shelter

Rain comes hard, short, and warm. The “silence-with-pressure” is your squall warning. Shelter on high, stable ground or an existing solid platform, not under new or active structures. Condensation is everywhere; assume everything is wet. Dry what you can, protect wounds and feet, and expect chafing and fungal problems.

Carry all drinking water. Local water is not merely biological; the white filament is nanoscale and self-repairing, and filters may not be enough. Treat cuts promptly with clean water and antiseptic, cover them, and watch them. Avoid skin contact with broken filament, glowing wet patches, and the interiors of damaged plants. If you contact filament, remove contaminated clothing, rinse with clean water, cover the area, and monitor; do not scrub with local water.

## The town

The settlement is not abandoned in the usual sense; it is being built for no occupants. Treat it as active construction. Wear a helmet. Do not enter alone. Mark your route. Prefer open platforms with visible exits. Test floors before trusting them. Do not climb new spans or sleep in enclosed rooms; a stair may arrive at a room with no other door, and a floor may be laid overnight. Do not remove artefacts. The builder is diligent and indifferent.

## Animals

Most animals are small, but the small ones come in sheets. Wear goggles and netting; keep food sealed. The chorus has structure; learn its pulses. Hull-beasts are felt an hour before they are seen: keep off their paths and do not try to hurry them. Cloud-fliers at dusk are a single descending tone, not a threat. Pools that show only a disturbance in the weed are not for wading.

## Emergency rules

- **Ground singing:** leave the area carefully for thicker, higher, older ground or the rim.
- **Low chord / lift shift:** expect channel changes; move to high ground and wait.
- **Silence-with-pressure:** squall coming; shelter high, not under new structures.
- **Lost:** stop, protect your hearing, use light and beacon, move toward high ground and the rim bearing, and do not shout continuously.
- **Injury or illness:** clean and cover, use your beacon, and evacuate. The basin does not reward endurance.

## The ordinary-human rule

Do not try to be tough here. The Loud is not hostile, but it is indifferent, and it is always changing. Visit the rim, take short guided trips, sleep at the rim or on proven platforms, keep your ears protected, keep your feet soft, keep your water sealed, and leave before fatigue or sensory overload makes you careless. The place will still be growing when you leave; you do not need to see all of it.
