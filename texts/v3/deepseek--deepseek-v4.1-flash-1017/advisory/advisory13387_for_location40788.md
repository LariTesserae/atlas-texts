Treat the Verdant Cleft as an active construction site inside a living landslide, with unreliable optics and local biology that does not follow ordinary rules. An ordinary human can visit, but should do so briefly, guided, conservatively, and with redundant light, water, and exit plans. The single most important preparation is hiring a trusted local guide and accepting that maps, distances, and routes are provisional.

## Before you go

- **Verify your anchor and return method.** Test your multiverse beacon/return trigger before entering, charge it, and carry a backup. Set a hard return window. Do not rely on “I’ll find the way back” in a place where the plateau rim is sometimes close and sometimes impossibly distant.
- **Arrange a check-in schedule with someone outside.** Tell them your intended duration, guide’s name if possible, and what to do if you miss two check-ins. Bring a mechanical watch and a notebook; do not trust only a phone.
- **Get physically ready for wet stairs, altitude, and unstable footing.** You will be climbing and descending irregular terraces, often on slick stone and rusted steel. Train for stairs and carry a light pack.
- **Pack as if for wet, cold, dusty industrial terrain with unknown biology.** Critical items:
  - Helmet with chin strap, eye protection, cut-resistant gloves, sturdy ankle-support boots, quick-dry layers, rain shell, insulation, spare socks.
  - Headlamp with red mode, spare batteries, chemical light sticks, and a handheld light. Do not depend on gas lamps.
  - Particulate respirator and, if available, a small CO monitor. The air carries smoke, mineral mist, and combustion products.
  - Hearing protection for factory terraces.
  - Water purification: filter, chemical treatment, and backup. Bring sealed water; do not drink seep water without treatment and local confirmation.
  - High-calorie sealed food, electrolytes, first-aid kit, antifungal powder, insect repellent, mosquito net, personal medications, tetanus status up to date.
  - Waterproof bags, silica packs, towel, and dry sacks. Everything will be damp.
  - Trekking poles, short rope, rope protectors, and a whistle. The rock edges are sharp enough to cut rope.
  - Small trade goods: batteries, salt, tea, waterproof matches, medical supplies. Ask before giving anything.
- **Bring a buddy if possible.** Solo travel here is a bad idea, especially after dark. If you must go alone, sleep in shifts and keep your go-bag beside you.

## Arrival and base

- **Arrive in daylight with a guide, ideally around midday** when light shafts help orientation. Do not arrive at night.
- **Do not try to map the city yourself.** Choose a base on a lit, inhabited terrace with two exits, a dry floor, and no vines, scaffolding, or chimney directly overhead. Check for fresh cracks and settling. Keep boots and emergency kit by the bed.
- **Ask your guide for night-safe routes immediately.** Specifically ask which staircases and terraces locals avoid after dark. Do not press them to explain the creatures directly; ask about “local wildlife” and “routes that are not used at night.”
- **Establish a rendezvous point and a lost-person plan.** If separated, stay in a lit inhabited area and use a whistle. Do not wander downward to “find the main path.”

## Movement and navigation

- **Trust local routes over geometry.** The canyon is still widening; foundations shift; pools appear where none should be; channels flow in wrong directions. A map is a snapshot of a process, not a stable fact.
- **Use your own consistent light.** Depth perception is unreliable because lamps disagree on distance and signs shift. Use a headlamp, count steps, tap ahead with a pole, and keep one hand free. Do not stare at glowing signs or shifting scripts.
- **Test every wet patch and pool before stepping.** Do not cross alone or at night. Avoid swimming; the water is chemically and biologically unknown.
- **Do not trust old railings, scaffolds, or rusted steel.** Test with a pole before leaning. Avoid crowded platforms. Watch for falling debris from above.
- **Do not touch exposed machinery.** Gears, pistons, and conveyors are visible and may be running. Do not enter factories without a guide, and do not reach into machinery for birds or curiosities.
- **If you must ascend to the plateau, do it only with a guide, in daylight, with a fixed plan and rope if needed.** Expect the journey time to vary. Do not use the view from above to navigate the canyon; it may show a different arrangement.

## Environmental and biological hazards

- **Avoid staring at vegetation.** Plants reportedly grow faster when observed. Use peripheral vision, take quick photos, and do not linger or sleep against vines. Do not pick flowers or carve marks into rock. Use removable markers only with permission.
- **Give animals right of way.** The joint-flexible goats and birds in machinery are not pets. Do not corner them, chase them, or use camera flash near large shapes. If you see something large in peripheral vision, retreat calmly to a lit inhabited area.
- **Respect offering sites.** Do not move, take, or eat offerings. If your guide says an offering is appropriate, carry separate sealed food and place it exactly where instructed. Do not create new offering sites.
- **Keep emotions level in public.** Arguments create fog; laughter can dry the air. Do not test this deliberately. If fog thickens around conflict, stop talking, breathe slowly, separate, and move to a ventilated lit area.
- **Use rope signals and whistles.** Voices carry strangely. Radios may be unreliable. Keep line-of-sight where possible.

## Social protocol

- Be polite, pay your guide fairly, and do not criticize the settlement’s provisional nature or the factories. People here are keeping structures standing, not building a finished city.
- Ask permission before photographing people or interiors. Do not demand explanations of the creatures or the shifting signs.
- Do not promise to “fix” the canyon or help with construction unless you can actually do so.
- If you feel the place is watching you or that your observation matters, do not run solipsistic experiments in dangerous spots. Record anomalies briefly, stay with a witness, and ground yourself by touching stable stone and naming ordinary objects.

## Emergency and exit

- Keep your return trigger accessible but do not activate it while standing under loose rock or scaffolding if you can move first.
- If lost: stop, stay put if you are in a lit inhabited place, use your whistle, and wait. Do not descend random staircases after dark.
- If you have headache, dizziness, confusion, or breathing trouble, suspect bad air. Move to better ventilation, use your respirator, and descend to a lit inhabited terrace with a guide.
- Leave earlier than you think you need to. The Cleft is not immediately lethal, but it is cumulative: damp, disorientation, noise, bad footing, and shifting geometry wear people down. A short, guided, conservative visit is the correct visit.
