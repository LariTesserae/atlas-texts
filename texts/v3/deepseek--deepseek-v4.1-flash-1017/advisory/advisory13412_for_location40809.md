An ordinary human should prepare for this place as if for a cave, a museum, and a sensory-deprivation chamber at once—but mostly as a guest in a room that does not notice guests. You have no darkvision, no infinite air, no perfect map sense, and no immunity to fine dust. Your only advantages are redundancy, restraint, and a short visit.

## First: read the place correctly
This is not a challenge to conquer. It is a still-life basin in a larger dark: warm, silent, dust-preserved, with no wind, water, fire, creatures, or tool marks. The columns are grown, not built. The lichens are decades slow. The dust is an archive. Your presence is the only moving event. Prepare to minimize every disturbance you make—light, sound, heat, moisture, breath, footprints, CO2.

## Before you enter
- **Verify the atmosphere.** Do not trust “it seems breathable.” Use a multi-gas monitor for O2, CO2, CO, H2S, CH4, and VOCs, plus a particulate monitor if possible. Still air in a low basin can let CO2 pool. Test at head height and near the floor. If you have no monitor, keep the visit extremely short, wear a respirator, and leave at the first headache, drowsiness, confusion, or dizziness.
- **Plan a very short visit.** Start with 10–20 minutes in the basin, not hours. The place is stable; you are the unstable part. Set a silent vibrating timer and a hard exit time.
- **Use a buddy system.** Two people is ideal; three maximum. More people mean more dust, heat, moisture, CO2, noise, and confusion. Prearrange hand signals, light signals, lost-light protocol, air-alarm protocol, and exit order.
- **Establish your exit and a backup return.** If you are a multiverse traveler, confirm your return method works and have a backup. Keep any portal or gate at the threshold or outside the basin—not inside it—so it does not add light, wind, moisture, or a draft. Do not leave it open behind you.
- **Clean your gear.** No perfume, insect repellent, sunscreen, smoke, or strong scents. No seeds, mud, or biological contamination. No loose lint. Wear clean, dedicated outer layers.
- **Empty your bladder, hydrate, and eat before entry.** Do not plan to eat, drink, urinate, defecate, spit, or change clothes inside. Carry a wag bag for emergencies and pack out everything.
- **Test every light, battery, radio, timer, and monitor.** Put a primary light on your body and a backup in a pocket, not buried in a pack. Use lockout so lights cannot turn on accidentally in transit.

## What to wear
- **Layered, dust-proof clothing.** Lightweight coveralls or a dust shell over moisture-wicking base layers. Tuck cuffs into gaiters or boots. No dangling straps, loose hoods, or velcro that collects dust. You will be warm and still; avoid overdressing, because sweat is contamination and discomfort.
- **Helmet with a lamp mount.** The ceiling is unseen and fine grains fall. A bump helmet is sensible. Keep the beam shielded and pointed down.
- **P100 respirator, fit-tested.** The dust is fine and powdery. A half-mask plus sealed goggles is the minimum; a full-face respirator is better if you can manage it. Carry spare filters. If you have a beard that breaks the seal, do not rely on a disposable mask.
- **Sealed goggles with anti-fog.** Do not wipe them with dusty gloves. Carry a spare lens cloth in a sealed bag.
- **Gloves.** Nitrile liners under work gloves. Do not touch lichen, wet columns, or bedrock with bare skin. The columns are slick with moisture; they are not handholds.
- **Boots with ankle support and soft soles, plus gaiters.** The floor is powder over warm bedrock. You need grip on slick stone but also low disturbance. Walk slowly; do not shuffle or kick.
- **Knee pads only if you must crouch—and then avoid kneeling on lichen or dust.** Better not to crouch at all.

## What to carry
- **Three independent lights per person.** Primary headlamp with red/amber low mode and white spot, handheld backup, and a small keychain light or chemical glowstick as last resort. Fresh batteries and spares in a sealed container. Use red low light for orientation; white spot only for detail. Shield the beam, point it down, and avoid shining it at wet stone or into eyes. No open flame, no flares, no smoke, no laser.
- **A lightweight line and removable markers.** A 30–50 m line with knots, anchored outside the basin and padded so it does not scar stone. Do not wrap it around columns. Use dim removable glow markers at intervals and collect them on exit. Mark the threshold. If you cannot use a line, do not go beyond the entrance.
- **A probe or trekking pole.** The edge dust deepens into drifts; test before stepping. The basin is the whole world—do not treat it as a through-route. The greater dark beyond is a no-go.
- **A multi-gas/particulate monitor.** Set alarms. If it alarms, leave.
- **A silent vibrating timer and watch.** Time distortion is real in total dark and silence.
- **Sealed water bottle.** Hydrate before entry; do not drink inside unless you must, and then exit to the threshold. No food inside.
- **First aid:** trauma dressings, sprain wrap, sealed saline eye wash, respiratory irritation supplies. Cover any cut before entry; blood is contamination.
- **Radio/beacon/whistle for emergency only.** Radios may not work in stone. A whistle is loud in that silence; use only if lost or injured.
- **Sealed camera if you must.** No drone—prop wash would destroy the dust record and the silence. No tripod stakes, no leaving lights, no flash bursts unless absolutely necessary.
- **Waste bag and pack-out bags.** Everything you bring in, including used filters, wrappers, and tissues, goes out.

## Entry protocol
Pause at the threshold. Let your light settle. Listen. The silence is a presence; your breathing and footsteps will be loud. Enter slowly, on the line, with the beam low and shielded. Count paces if it helps. Keep one hand free for the line and one for balance. Probe the floor before weight. Stay within the basin. The edge is felt as deepening dust or a slight rise; treat the drift as potentially deep and the wall beyond as the limit. Mark the entrance dimly and collect the marker on exit.

Move less than you think you should. The air is still; dust you disturb will hang and coat your lenses and lungs. Do not sweep your pack, unzip unnecessarily, or fan the air. If you raise dust, stop and let it settle.

## In-place conduct
- **Do not touch the columns.** They are slick, grown, irreplaceable, and not handholds. Use a gloved hand on bare bedrock only for balance.
- **Do not step on or sample the lichens.** A finger’s breadth takes decades. Avoid the hollows and lee of stone where they grow. Do not kneel, sit, or set gear on them.
- **Do not mark the stone.** No chalk, paint, cairns, bolts, or carvings. No “improving” access. No collecting.
- **Do not introduce fire, water, smoke, or scent.** No cigarettes, vapes, stoves, flares, wet wipes, spitting, pouring, or urine. No hand sanitizer inside if it can be avoided; use it outside.
- **Keep the group tiny and quiet.** Use low voices for safety check-ins, not conversation. No music, no radio chatter, no drones. Keep ears uncovered; hearing is a safety sense here.
- **Manage heat.** The air is warm and still; no wind cools you. Move slowly, take breaks, vent clothing carefully, and avoid sweating. Sweat wets dust and introduces moisture.
- **Watch the air and time.** Do not lie down, sleep, or crouch long in low spots. If you feel euphoric, compelled to stay, panicky, or time-sick, leave. That is not insight; it is sensory deprivation.
- **Photograph sparingly.** Your light changes the place. Take a few records, then turn the light down and simply be there.

## Emergency rules
- **Light failure:** stop. Do not walk in total dark. Use your backup. If all lights fail, stay on the line, activate a glowstick/beacon, and wait or signal. Do not wander.
- **Air alarm or symptoms:** leave immediately. Do not “push through” headache, drowsiness, confusion, or breathlessness.
- **Dust fall increases:** helmet on, mask sealed, step away from the fall, let it settle. If it continues or the ceiling sounds active, retreat.
- **Injury:** treat, then exit. Do not camp. Do not send an injured person alone.
- **Lost:** follow the line. If no line, stop and signal; do not wander in the dark. The basin is small, but the greater dark is not.
- **Panic:** breathe, touch the warm bedrock, check the line and timer, and exit. There is no shame in leaving early.

## Exit and aftercare
Follow the line out. Collect every marker. Check pockets and gear. Do not brush dust off inside; do it outside. Remove PPE outside the basin, bag trash, clean gear, wash hands, and report your air readings and time. Leave the place as close to its held-breath state as you can. Footprints are unavoidable; make few, on one path, and do not try to erase them—erasing disturbs more.

The best preparation is redundancy in gear and restraint in behavior. You are a warm, wet, breathing dust magnet in a place that has been in equilibrium for geological time. Enter quietly, look, listen, and leave before your own light, heat, breath, and footsteps become part of the room.
