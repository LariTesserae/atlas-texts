Treat the Choir Vault as a sealed instrument you are allowed to stand inside for a few minutes—not as a room to explore, not as a ruin to investigate, and not as a place to “help.” The single most important preparation is this: **do not enter unless you have a recall/exit method that works without walking, finding a door, or relying on landmarks.** There is no outside here, no useful horizon, no shadows with edges, and no landmarks that a human eye will trust. If your way out depends on navigation, you are not prepared.

### Before you go

**1. Arrange a hard exit and a hard time limit.**  
Set a pre-established external anchor, a timed recall, or a companion outside with a fixed retrieval protocol. Rehearse the exit before entry. Carry a silent timer under your clothing—no alarms, no beeps. Set a conservative cap: five to ten minutes for a first visit, fifteen at the absolute most. Do not trust your sense of elapsed time; the vault has no hours and the drone will flatten your judgment.

**2. Go alone, or with one support person outside.**  
Groups mean more breath, humidity, movement, and sound. The central standing column is narrow and strung with guy-lines. One visitor is best. If a second person must accompany you, they stay at the entry point and does not wander.

**3. Protect your hearing, but not into total occlusion.**  
The chord is continuous, directionless, and loud enough to make speech difficult. Use flat-attenuation musician’s earplugs, roughly −15 to −25 dB, with a backup pair. Foam plugs may overattenuate and make your own breathing and heartbeat seem louder; noise-canceling headphones are a bad idea—electronics, bulk, and snag risk. No cords dangling. After exit, sit somewhere quiet and check for tinnitus or muffled hearing.

**4. Dress to touch nothing and shed nothing.**  
Wear close-fitting, smooth, non-shedding, non-snagging clothing: long sleeves and trousers, cuffs sealed or snug, no loose hood cords, no scarves, no jewelry, no dangling straps. Tie back hair. Soft-soled shoes with smooth treads, clean soles, no grit. Thin smooth gloves are optional; bare hands are acceptable only if you keep them to yourself. No backpack. Use a body-hugging pouch for your recall device and timer. A soft cap is better than a hard helmet, which can snag. Eye protection is optional; if you use it, make sure it will not fog or dangle.

**5. Enter clean, dry, and empty-pocketed.**  
No food, water, drink bottles, aerosols, perfumes, insect repellent, hand sanitizer, markers, offerings, seeds, pets, or cutting tools. No open cuts or weeping skin. Do not enter sweaty or damp; dry off and cool down first. Reschedule if you are coughing, sneezing, or ill. The vault is dry and in equilibrium; you are the moisture, the heat, the shed skin, and the noise. Minimize all of it.

**6. Prepare your mind to do nothing.**  
Expect honeyed light with no source, no hard shadows, poor depth perception, a loud changeless drone, dry cool air, and walls packed with made objects until the wall itself disappears. Expect sparse pale vines holding tension in the seams. Expect no animals, no rot, no change. The vault is not hostile; it is settled. It will outwait you. The strongest danger is the urge to touch, tidy, collect, answer the chord, sing, speak, or “just look closer.” Decide in advance that you will stand, observe, and leave.

### On entry

- Step only into the narrow central column. Stop there. Do not walk toward the walls.
- Keep your head below or carefully clear of the head-height guy-lines. Do not push them aside. If one touches your face, freeze, then slowly back away—do not swat or duck suddenly.
- Let your eyes adjust. Do not use a flashlight; it will not help and adds a dynamic source. Do not try to find the source of the light or the chord.
- Breathe quietly through your nose if you can. Keep speech to nothing or near nothing. Do not hum or sing with the drone.
- Do not touch the green runners. They are holding the structure’s tension. Do not lean on walls, hang anything, cut anything, or pick at knots.
- If you snag, stop. Reverse slowly. Do not pull free hard. If you cannot free yourself in a few seconds, use your recall.
- Leave at the timer, or sooner if you feel panic, ear pain, disorientation, or a strong impulse to interact. Do not wait for a “sign.” There will be no sign.

### What not to do

Do not explore. Do not sample. Do not leave markers or offerings. Do not try to clean or repair. Do not bring a companion, pet, or drone. Do not bring moisture, flame, chemicals, or living material. Do not assume the entry point is obvious from inside; it may look like one more softness in a field of softnesses. Do not linger because the place feels calm. Calm is the trap here.

### After you leave

Inspect your clothing and gear for fibers, vine fragments, dust, or anything stuck to you. Bag and report anything you find rather than scattering it. Check your hearing and balance. Sit in ordinary quiet and let your eyes readjust to normal shadows. Debrief yourself: did you touch anything, disturb a line, leave anything, or feel pulled to return? If you feel the vault “waiting” for you afterward, treat that as a psychological effect, not an invitation.

The Choir Vault is not a place to conquer or understand on foot. It is a settled balance, and you are the only gradient in it. Prepare to be brief, quiet, dry, smooth-edged, and forgettable—then leave so it can be still again.
