Treat the Vault as a fragile closed system and a potentially hazardous cave, not as a scenic garden. The governing intuition is: **you are the first leak in a loop that has been sealed since before soil existed.** Everything you bring in, shed, breathe out, drop, or break will persist. Everything you take out may be contaminated in ways you cannot see. Go in brief, clean, monitored, and ready to leave early.

### 1. Before you go
- **Do not go alone.** Minimum: one buddy plus a surface watch who knows your turn-around time and has the entrance marked.
- **Get local knowledge and permission.** This is not a place to “discover.” Ask whether the throat is stable, whether the stone is still deforming, whether the air has been tested, and whether the grown rooms are considered sensitive.
- **Check your own fitness for heat, humidity, and claustrophobia.** If you panic in tight warm spaces, overheat easily, have bad balance, mold/spore allergies, or poor knees, don’t go. The throat is narrow and sweaty; the bowl is hot and shadowless; the floor is a springy mat over hidden hollows.
- **Verify return.** Confirm the entrance coordinates, exit route, and that your return method works through whatever local interference exists. Set a hard turn-around time before entering. The place distorts time: no shadows, no insects, no young or old growth, only dripping. Use a watch, not your sense of duration.
- **Plan for a short visit.** One to two hours inside is plenty for an ordinary human. Heat and humidity will do more damage than distance. Turn around at half your water, first signs of heat illness, or first serious disorientation.

### 2. Atmospheric and structural checks
- **Carry a multi-gas monitor** if at all possible: oxygen, CO₂, CO, H₂S, methane, and radon if local conditions suggest warm young stone. Warm wet closed volumes can accumulate bad air. If you cannot test the atmosphere, do not go deep or stay long.
- **Watch the ceiling.** It is a thin glowing membrane of rock. Do not climb it, poke it, throw things at it, or assume it is load-bearing in any ordinary sense.
- **Test every step.** The root mat bears weight, but it is a woven surface over hollows and pools. Use a trekking pole or staff to probe ahead. Spread your weight. Avoid jumping.
- **Treat the throat as the most dangerous part.** It is narrow, warm, slick, and still plastic. Keep arms close, no sharp gear snagging, no hard pushing against the walls. A clean handline tied off outside and carried in can help you return without anchoring anything inside. Move through it deliberately and don’t linger.

### 3. Clothing and kit
Dress for **hot, wet, slippery, low-shadow conditions**, not for cold cave travel.
- **Quick-dry synthetic layers**, loose fitting. Long sleeves and trousers for scrape protection, but not heavy waterproofs—you will stew. A light breathable shell for mist is optional.
- **Boots with aggressive grip and drainage.** Not heavy waterproof boots that fill with sweat. Bring spare socks. The root mat and pools are slick.
- **Gloves with grip.** The passage walls sweat; you will touch them. Gloves also reduce skin contact with unknown films and surfaces.
- **Helmet with chin strap and a low-output headlamp.** You will duck twice in the grown rooms and may brush low growth. Bring spare batteries.
- **A narrow-beam light for depth cues.** The diffuse light kills shadows, which hurts depth perception. Use artificial light sparingly—just enough to cast local shadows and check footing. Avoid floodlights and flashes; they wash out the place and may disturb the biota.
- **Eye protection** against mist and dripping. **Respirator or good mask** if spores, dust, or unknown aerosols are possible; take it off only if air testing says it is safe.
- **Water and electrolytes.** Carry all drinking water. Do not drink from the pools, even though they are clear and warm. They are part of a closed cycle in contact with unknown stone and biology. Plan roughly 0.5–1 liter per person per hour in that heat, plus salt.
- **High-energy, crumb-free food** in sealed packaging. Eat outside or with strict no-litter discipline. No cooking, no open flame.
- **First aid, antiseptic, blister care, and a way to cover cuts.** Warm wet places are unkind to open skin, and you do not know the local microbiology. Cover wounds before entry.
- **Pack-out waste kit.** No urine, spit, crumbs, or human waste inside. Use a wag bag if needed. Your waste is a biological introduction the Vault has no machinery to process.
- **Whistle, backup light, and emergency beacon** if it works through rock. A surface watch with a scheduled check-in is better than any beacon.

### 4. Biosecurity and impact
- **Clean your gear before entry.** Dedicated clean clothes and boots, no mud, seeds, soil, or pet hair. Do not bring live plants, pets, drones, perfumes, chalk, spray paint, or marking fluids.
- **Do not sample, collect, pick, cut, or pocket anything.** Do not touch the basin in the deepest room. Do not sit in the grown rooms if they seem fragile; observe from the threshold.
- **Minimize contact.** The passage walls are young and plastic; a fingertip leaves a mark. The plants are the floor; you cannot avoid them entirely, but do not break fronds or strip leaves.
- **Give the animal space.** If you see the pale many-legged thing, do not handle or follow it into a hollow. It is not an exhibit.
- **Do not introduce light, sound, or heat more than necessary.** No loud voices, no music, no drones, no flash photography. The quiet is part of the system.

### 5. Inside protocol
- **Mark your route mentally and with the outside handline**, not with chalk or paint. Note natural features: the squeeze, the first pool, the brightest patch, the ducked threshold.
- **Move slowly and test footing.** Use the staff. Avoid pools. Keep away from the thin ceiling. Do not jump from root saddle to root saddle.
- **Watch each other for heat illness:** headache, confusion, cramps, stopped sweating, nausea. Humidity prevents sweat from evaporating, so you can overheat without noticing. Rest, drink electrolytes, and leave at the first bad sign.
- **Set a timer.** The absence of shadows and animal sound will make time feel fake. When the timer says turn around, turn around.
- **If disoriented, stop.** Return to the last recognizable feature or the handline. Do not wander deeper to “find the way.” The far wall fades into haze; there is no useful horizon.

### 6. Exit and decontamination
- **Leave the throat promptly.** It is the choke point. Do not rest in it.
- **Change outer layers and bag your gear** at the entrance if contamination is a concern. Clean hands before eating. Keep gear quarantined until local advice says otherwise.
- **Report what you saw and where you stepped.** If you disturbed a pool, touched the basin, or saw the passage deforming, say so. The Vault’s value is that it has not been changed. Your job is to keep it that way.

Bottom line: go in clean, light, cool, monitored, tethered, and brief. Carry your own water. Test the air. Test every step. Do not drink, collect, mark, or linger. The place will feel like standing inside a warm sleeping lung—beautiful, quiet, and not yours to wake.
