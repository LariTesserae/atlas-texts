Treat the Sinter as a combination of a hot engine room, a salt mine, a cleanroom, and a wildlife reserve—one with no water, no rescue, no day cycle, and no safe place to sit down. You are a baseline human with a portal and whatever you can carry. Your goal is not to “explore boldly.” It is to make a short, sealed, instrumented, tethered visit and leave before your body or your judgment starts to fail.

## Before you go

**1. Do reconnaissance from outside first.**  
Establish where the portal opens, whether it opens into the Sinter or onto the salt plain above, what the atmosphere is, what the ambient temperature and noise level are, whether the gas mix is breathable, and whether the portal stays open on a predictable schedule. Do not assume the air is safe because the place is “not dark.” The dust, the fauna, and the machine gases are all unknowns. Treat the atmosphere as unbreathable until instrumented otherwise.

**2. Build a surface base.**  
Put your water, food, medical kit, decontamination gear, spare batteries, and comms relay outside the Sinter. The Sinter is small—a few hundred paces across—but dense and cue-less. You want a clean, cool, quiet place to retreat to within minutes. Do not plan to camp inside on a first visit. Do not sleep inside unless you have a sealed tent, active cooling, CO₂ scrubbing, hearing protection, and a very good reason.

**3. Go with a buddy and an outside watch.**  
Minimum two people inside, one person at the portal. The outside watch keeps the timer, monitors comms, and is prepared to pull you out. The inside buddy stays within sight or tether. No solo trips. The place is loud, dim, hot, and visually repetitive; it is exactly the kind of environment where one person gets disoriented and does not notice.

**4. Choose the right body and the right mindset.**  
Do not go if you have uncontrolled heat intolerance, respiratory disease, heart disease, claustrophobia, or a bad reaction to continuous loud noise. Practice wearing the suit and hearing protection in a hot, noisy, dark space before you go. The Sinter has no time cues and no cool corner. Sensory monotony plus continuous machinery noise can make people strangely passive or euphoric. Set hard abort criteria in advance: suit breach, gas alarm, lost tether, confusion, overheating, or “I feel fine but I cannot remember how long I have been here.”

**5. Prepare for desiccation and heat.**  
Drink water before entry. Carry sealed water and electrolytes inside the suit. Do not bring open water into the Sinter; water is alien here, and you do not want to spill it, breathe its vapor, or introduce it into the dust. Use a sealed bladder with a bite valve inside the helmet. Plan short visits—15 to 30 minutes for the first entry, then longer only if everything checks out.

## What to wear and carry

**PPE:**  
A sealed, positive-pressure suit or at minimum a full-face P100/HEPA respirator with sealed goggles, coveralls, taped seams, cut-resistant gloves, and thick-soled boots. No exposed skin. No contact lenses. The place is dry, dusty, sharp-edged, and full of small crawling things. Positive pressure is better than negative pressure because the fauna and dust will find every intake.

**Cooling:**  
The heat is steady and comes from the floor and ceiling. You need active cooling—cooling vest, ice packs, or a small circulating system—and insulated boot soles and gloves for hot surfaces. Carry an IR thermometer and test anything before you touch it. Do not sit or lean. The floor is hot, the pipes are hot, and the fauna are on the undersides of things.

**Hearing protection:**  
Double protection: foam earplugs plus earmuffs or electronic hearing protectors with comms. The noise is enormous, continuous, and directionless. Do not rely on hearing to locate hazards. Carry a noise dosimeter if you have one. Limit exposure. If you feel pressure, ringing, or dullness, you have stayed too long.

**Lighting:**  
Bring a helmet lamp and a handheld light, with spare batteries. Use low, diffuse light for general movement and a spot for hazards. The Sinter is dim and sourceless-feeling; your eyes will adapt, but you should not depend on the amber machine-lamps. Batteries hate heat, so carry fresh spares and keep them insulated from hot surfaces.

**Navigation:**  
No GPS. No reliable compass unless you have checked it. The chambers are nested, repetitive, and acoustically confusing. Use a tether from the portal if possible, plus removable reflective markers or small beacon pucks at junctions. Carry a hardcopy map and count paces. Use a mechanical watch or a rugged chronometer with alarms. Because there is no day and no change, you must impose time on the place: check in every 10 minutes, turn back at a fixed time, not at a feeling.

**Monitoring:**  
Carry a gas monitor for O₂, CO₂, CO, combustibles, and anything else your survey suggests. Carry a personal locator beacon or multiverse return anchor. Carry a patch kit, tape, zip ties, spare filters, and a small trauma kit. Your comms may not penetrate the rock; deploy a relay at the portal and at major junctions if you can.

**Biosecurity:**  
Seal your cuffs, vents, and zippers. Check your seals every few minutes. Do not eat inside. Do not open the suit. Do not take souvenirs unless you have a sterile, permitted sampling protocol. Do not introduce water, food crumbs, or your skin flora. The Sinter is a closed loop; you are the contaminant.

## How to move inside

Enter slowly. Stop just inside the portal and listen—not to enjoy the sound, but to confirm the noise level and check your comms. Look at the floor before you put weight on it. The sinter shelves are crusted and terraced; the fracture faces are fresh, glassy, and sharp. Wear your helmet and do not brush against walls. Avoid climbing unless it is necessary and protected.

Keep away from the machines. They are running, unguarded, hot, pressurized, and full of intakes and exhausts. Do not touch the flywheel shaft. Do not block the pump-house. Do not lean on pipes. Do not attempt to shut anything down or “fix” anything. The engines have reached equilibrium; you are not the operator, and you may be the thing that breaks the loop. If you smell gas, hear a new sound, or see a leak, leave.

Watch the fauna. They are everywhere—under pipes, in the dust, inside exhausts, packed into dead spaces. Treat them as potentially biting, stinging, allergenic, or simply capable of crawling into a suit seam. Check your gloves, cuffs, and helmet seals regularly. Do not sit, kneel, or put your hands into dark crevices. Do not use insecticide; you do not know what it will do to the loop. If something gets inside your suit, do not panic. Exit, strip, and inspect in the surface base.

Move in short stages. The Sinter is small enough to cross in a quarter hour, but it is dense enough to take days to see. Do not try to see it all in one visit. Pick one chamber or one terrace per trip. Mark the route. Keep the portal behind you in your mind. Because the sound has no direction and the light has no clear source, your sense of orientation will be weaker than you expect.

## The rules to impose on yourself

- **Time:** set alarms. Turn back at the alarm, not when you are tired.
- **Water:** drink on a schedule. Heat and dry air will dehydrate you before you feel thirsty.
- **Heat:** monitor yourself for headache, confusion, nausea,停止 sweating, or irritability. There is no cool corner to retreat to; your retreat is the portal.
- **Noise:** keep exposure short. Double hearing protection. Do not remove plugs to talk.
- **Dust:** do not open the suit. Carry spare filters. If the filter clogs, leave.
- **Machines:** maintain distance. No touching, no climbing, no repairs, no open flame.
- **Fauna:** sealed suit, regular checks, no sitting, no food.
- **Contamination:** no open water, no waste, no samples without protocol, no leaving gear behind.
- **Psychology:** buddy check-ins. If you feel euphoric, detached, confused, or unable to remember the last check-in, abort.

## Emergency plan

If your suit breaches, patch it and exit immediately. If you are lost, stop, anchor yourself, use your beacon, and follow your tether or markers back. Do not wander trying to “find the sound.” If the gas alarm sounds, evacuate. If you overheat, cool down, drink, and exit. If you are separated from your buddy, return to the last agreed checkpoint; do not search deep alone. There is no local rescue. Your outside watch and your own discipline are the rescue.

## After the visit

Decontaminate the suit outside the portal. Shower. Inspect your skin for bites, cuts, and rash. Check your ears and hearing. Drink more water than you think you need. Report the noise dose, temperature, gas readings, and any fauna contact. Rest your hearing before another trip. Do not return the same day if you have had significant heat or noise exposure.

The Sinter is not a place to conquer. It is a completed thing—loud, warm, dry, crowded, and sealed—and it will keep running whether you are there or not. Prepare to be a brief, sealed, polite visitor: look, map, monitor, and leave before the place teaches you how little it needs you.
