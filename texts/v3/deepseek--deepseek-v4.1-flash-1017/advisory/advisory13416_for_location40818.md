Treat The Felt as a combination of desert, concert hall, living archive, and unmarked minefield. An ordinary human can visit, but not casually. The correct posture is **guided, sealed, quiet, hydrated, fireless, and humble**. If you can avoid going alone, do. If you can avoid going at all, that is also a defensible choice.

## Before you go

**1. Get a local guide and permission.**  
Do not arrive at the edge of the plateau and start walking. Ambersill’s spacing, paths, acoustic etiquette, and taboo boundaries are not obvious. You need someone who knows which looms may be approached, which are instruments, which are weapons, and which are simply old enough that nobody feeds them anymore. Arrange permits, lodging, and an emergency plan before transit.

**2. Get a medical and sensory baseline.**  
Have your hearing tested and recorded. Have a respiratory check. Do not go if you have asthma or other respiratory disease, significant hearing loss or sound sensitivity, open wounds, immune compromise, or pregnancy unless a local medic clears you. The place is dry, dusty, loud, and biologically closed; you are the alien contamination risk.

**3. Bring your own water—and treat it as a dangerous alien fluid in public.**  
There is no local water. Bring enough for the entire stay plus a 48-hour reserve, in rigid, silent, drip-proof containers. No leaking hydration bladders, no sloshing canteens, no exposed tubes. Electrolyte tablets, sealed. Do not drink local “bright” under any circumstances. Do not offer water casually as a gift; the local word for water is a warning about longing. Ask your guide whether drinking in public is rude. Keep your water private, sealed, and silent.

**4. Bring food that does not crumble, click, or smell.**  
Sealed, high-calorie, ready-to-eat, low-crumb food. No cooking. No open flames. No loud packaging. No strong scents. No alcohol—it dehydrates and impairs. Pack out every scrap; crumbs attract mites and other small hard animals.

**5. Build a dust-and-spore kit.**  
Fitted P100 respirator with spare filters; sealed goggles rather than contact lenses; dust hood; anti-static coveralls; gloves; boot covers; tape for cuffs. The drift is mineral flour and spores, abrasive and persistent. Keep cameras, electronics, and water connectors sealed. Bring lens cloths and spare filters.

**6. Build a hearing-protection kit.**  
The quietest hour there is louder than a city’s noon. Bring custom musician’s earplugs with filters, electronic earmuffs with pass-through so you can hear speech, spare foam plugs, and a baseline audiogram. Learn hand signals. Keep your voice low. Silence every device: no beeps, alarms, shutters, drones, speakers, musical instruments, or rhythmic tapping. A camera with a silent shutter only, and ask before recording.

**7. Dress for dry cold, dry heat, and static.**  
Layers, windproof shell, soft-soled boots, padded straps, no loose jewelry, no jangling gear. Anti-static clothing and grounding strap. The air is dry; sweat vanishes and you dehydrate without noticing. Carry lip balm, nasal gel, barrier cream, and sealed sterile saline for eye flush—not local water.

**8. Carry no fire and no sparks.**  
The felt is dry fibre; the air smells of ozone and solvent. No smoking, no lighters, no open flames, no sparking tools, no hot work. Use sealed, low-lumen amber or red headlamps and intrinsically safe electronics. Carry a small CO2 extinguisher. Static discharge is a real risk; ground yourself before touching metal.

**9. Bring sanitation that packs out.**  
No burying. No soap into the mat. No local water for washing. Sealed waste bags, gloves, dry wipes, non-flammable sanitizer used carefully. Human waste and grey water are contamination in a closed ecology; pack everything out or use whatever sealed system your guide specifies.

**10. Bring navigation redundancy, but no drones.**  
Local guide, map, compass, GPS or inertial nav if it works, radio with earpiece, emergency beacon, spare batteries. Drones are loud, alien, and will disturb the mat and fauna. The plateau is flat but visually ambiguous: soft shadows, grey-gold drift, sparse looms. Do not rely on the horizon. If lost, stop, radio, and stay on the path; do not shout unless your guide has given you a distress protocol.

**11. Bring quiet gifts, if any.**  
Ask first. Do not bring seeds, animals, water, bright-coloured imitations of bright, loud devices, or biological samples. In a closed ecology, “souvenir” and “invasive species” are the same word.

## On arrival

Keep your respirator on before you step onto the felt. Let your guide choose the entry point; do not step off the pad onto virgin mat. Greet people softly. Let them fit you with ear-baffles or show you how to wear your plugs. Ask three questions immediately: **What is the distress signal? Where do I put waste? What should I not touch or say?**

Check your water containers for drips. Cover any cut before you go deeper. Silence your devices. Walk single file on established paths, with soft steps. Do not tap the rock—it rings—and do not tap, pluck, hum at, or lean on looms without invitation. Rope bridges are slung low and swinging; use the local harness, one at a time, and do not bounce.

## Living there

**Acoustic etiquette is survival etiquette.** The mat is phonotropic and records sound. Speak low. Avoid repetitive noises in the same spot. Do not sing to nodes, tune looms, enter amplifier galleries, or “see what happens” with a resonant chamber. Some looms are instruments, some are treaties, some are weapons still being fed because nobody dares stop. Do not feed or tune anything you have not been told to feed or tune. Assume every sound you make is permanent and may be read centuries later. Do not say secrets you would not want archived.

**Do not touch bright.** It is the local lifeblood, not water, not coolant, not lamp oil. It is dense, oily, solvent-smelling, and does not evaporate. Gloves on. If it contacts skin or gear, dry-wipe, seal the contaminated material, and tell your guide. For eye splash, use sealed sterile saline, not local water, and seek local medical help.

**Watch for wet clicks.** The long grey predators hunt tap-beetles by listening for the wet click of a wound. Cover every cut immediately. Keep water containers silent and drip-free. Do not handle tap-beetles. Do not feed gliders. Keep food sealed against dust-mites. Do not swat or chase fauna; you are the loud, wet, alien thing.

**Respect the looms.** They are grown, lived in, and grown over. Do not cut, tie to, climb, or lean on walls without permission. Do not enter the oldest buried chambers or amplifier galleries alone. If invited to listen to a stratum, follow instructions exactly; some recordings are psychologically disturbing, and some arguments are better left buried.

**Hydrate on a schedule.** Dry air hides fluid loss. Drink before thirst. Watch for headache, dark urine, cracked lips, nosebleeds. Eat sealed food away from looms if possible. Sleep with earplugs and eye mask in whatever chamber your guide assigns; do not camp on virgin felt. Use existing platforms or hammocks with padded straps and permission.

**Plan for sensory fatigue.** Constant murmur, dim ambiguous light, no hard shadows, no acoustic privacy. Take breaks. Use your filtered plugs. Tell your guide if you feel overwhelmed, irritable, or disoriented. Short visits are better than heroic ones. Maintain a buddy system. Do not go wandering at night because the plain glows; the glow is beautiful and misleading.

## Emergency rules

- **Fire:** worst-case. Dry felt, dust, ozone. No open flame. If fire starts, alert locals, use CO2 extinguisher, evacuate along the guide’s route. Do not improvise with water.
- **Cut or bleeding:** cover immediately, pressure bandage, leave the area, tell guide. Wet click attracts attention.
- **Bright contact:** dry-wipe, seal contaminated clothing, report. Do not rinse with local water.
- **Lost:** stop, radio, stay put, conserve water, use the agreed signal. Do not shout randomly into the mat.
- **Loom damage or collapse:** do not climb; clear the area; alert locals.
- **Hearing pain or ringing:** increase protection, leave loud areas, seek local medical.

## Leaving

Decontaminate and seal gear. Do not carry away mat fragments, bright, soil, plants, animals, or unapproved samples. Get a hearing and respiratory check. Report any bright or wound exposure. Leave quietly. Your sounds remain in the mat; make sure they are sounds you can live with being preserved.
