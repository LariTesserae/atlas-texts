The place is called the Gearsheath. It is not a city in the traditional sense, but a sealed, industrial cavity knotted into the roots of a planetary core, humming with a dense, static pressure. The parameters are a puzzle, and the landscape resolves it by turning inward.

**Terrain and Materials:**
The geography is one of verticality and compression. It is a single, immense shaft, a geodesic tube drilled through the crust, but its walls are not rock. They are a lattice of fused slag, crystalline vaulting, and poured ferrocrete, forming a cylindrical world-enclosure three kilometers in diameter. The "elevation" of 3.0 is not a mountain to climb, but a depth to sink into; the floor is a metallic plain of heat-sinks and intake vents, and the "sky" is a ceiling of interlocking plates, a domed infrastructure that peaks kilometers above. The structure is young (erosion 1.3)—the seams between plates are still sharp, the welds gleam with unweathered alloy, and the air hangs thick with the scent of freshly milled dust and ozone.

There is no water here (0.2). The moisture has been scrubbed, condensed, and recycled for centuries. Instead, the chemistry is dry and mineral. The air is a filtered vapor of industrial gases, tasting of chlorine and cold iron. The ground is a mosaic of sintered ceramic tiles, geometric and precise, laid over a bed of sound-dampening foam. In places, the tiles are cracked, revealing not soil, but a pale, granular salt-like sediment—a mineral wash from the world's dry, internal chemistry.

**Atmosphere and Light:**
The light is dim (1.4), coming not from a sun, but from the infrastructural glow of the place itself. It is a twilight of meters and indicators. Pale green and amber LEDs stripe the walls, casting long, soft shadows. High above, vast arclights flicker on in slow, cyclical patterns, bathing the upper strata in a brief, sterile white light that dies down to a dusky blue. The air is cool (0.6), maintained by the ceaseless hum of the great heat-sinks that line the lower walls, a steady 16-degree Celsius chill that sinks into the bones.

**Ecology and Fauna (Unrecognized Biology):**
This is a post-life or non-recognized biology zone (vegetation 0.5). There are no plants, no fungi in the organic sense. Yet it is alive with a dense, industrial ecology. The "vegetation" is a growth of crystalline fungi and piezoelectric lichen that thrives on vibration and minor electrical differentials. They form in fractal clusters on the walls, harvesting static from the air. They are slow, silent, and luminous, pulsing with a faint, stored light.

The fauna is abundant (2.1), but it is mechanized or post-biological. The primary inhabitants are the Scrappers: multi-legged, insectoid robots the size of a dog, with articulated limbs and delicate manipulators. They are everywhere, crawling on the walls, the ceiling, the machinery, in near-touching density (1.9). They are not conscious in a human sense; they are autonomous maintenance units, following inherited protocols, endlessly repairing and harvesting. Their skittering and clicking is the primary sound, a soft, metallic patter that forms a baseline for the mostly quiet (1.3) atmosphere. They interact with the crystalline "flora" in a symbiotic cycle—the lichen harvests energy, the Scrappers harvest the lichen's crystalline growths for raw material.

**Built Infrastructure and Technology:**
The urban density (1.8) is extreme and layered. There are no open spaces; every surface is functional. Catwalks crisscross the void, connected by humming elevators and pneumatic tubes. The "city" is a hive of small workshops, communal silos, and habitation pods bolted to the walls. The technology is industrial (2.4)—but it is old, reused, and adapted. Generators throb with a deep, resonant bass. Conveyor belts run along the floors, carrying mineral grit from unseen vents to crushing mills. The air smells of lubricant and hot metal, a dry, non-organic scent.

The structure is a single, contained pocket-world (scale 0.3). It is a micro-environment, sealed from the planetary exterior. This is not a cavern; it is a built habitat, a lifeboat sunk into the geology. The boundaries are absolute—the walls are the end of the known world.

**Rules of the World and Weirdness (0.6):**
The Gearsheath operates on a simple, harsh rule: **Nothing is lost, everything is recycled.** This is the law of the dry chemistry. The air, the metals, the minerals, even the heat—all are captured, filtered, and reused. The weirdness (0.06) is subtle: the place has a memory. The walls hum with a faint, sub-audible resonance, a ghost of every sound ever made here, replaying in faint, distorted waves. The Scrappers, in their mindless loops, sometimes trace patterns that mirror the walls' memory, creating intricate, meaningless designs in dust and oxide.

**Dynamic State:**
This is a turbulent (2.7) system. The Gearsheath is in a state of hyperactive stability. Constant micro-earthquakes shift the plates, requiring the Scrappers to run their repair protocols endlessly. The air pressure fluctuates, triggering warning klaxons that echo softly before being absorbed by the sound-dampening walls. There are no seasons, no day or night, only the slow, rhythmic pulse of the machinery and the sudden, chaotic bursts of activity when a heat-sink overloads or a conveyor jams. The energy is anthill-like—a million small movements creating a vast, emergent purpose: the survival of the sealed system.

**Emergent Details:**
As you walk the catwalks, you notice that the Scrappers have, over generations, developed a crude form of collective behavior. They cluster around certain "memory nodes" in the walls, where the resonance is strongest. They don't create art, but their path-wear creates patterns—smooth, polished grooves in the ceramic tiles that spiral toward the central silos. The "quiet" is not silence; it is the absence of non-mechanical sound. The only other noise is the occasional, deep *groan* of the structure itself, settling into the planetary crust.

The atmosphere is one of serene, industrial claustrophobia. It is a world without weather, without open sky, without water or plants. Yet it is teeming with a life of metal and crystal, of protocol and resonance. It is a cathedral of efficiency, humming with the cold, cool breath of its own endless machine.
