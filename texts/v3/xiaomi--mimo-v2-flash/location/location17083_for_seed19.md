The place is called the Thinned Wold, and it is a high plateau of impossible age. The ground is not soil but a fine, granular dust the colour of old bone, ground down from mountains that must have existed eons ago but now exist only as a memory in the rock strata. This dust is not silicate; it is the powder of a mineral world, a dry mineral called parawax, which clings to everything with a faint, greasy static. The air holds no water—humidity is a concept that simply does not apply here. What little atmosphere there is is thin and perfectly still, a permanent, cool exhalation from the planet itself.

The scale is vast. The plateau extends in all directions, a flat, featureless plain that seems to curve slightly at the horizon, not with gravity, but with the sheer age of the world. The highest elevations are not mountains, but the plateau itself, a mesa carved by primordial erosion into a state of near-perfect equilibrium. There are no sharp edges, no fresh scars; every contour is rounded, settled, and ancient. The erosion is so complete that the landscape is less a surface and more a final, stable form.

Yet, despite the void, the place is dense with presence. The dust is not merely dust. It is layered, one microscopic stratum upon another, each layer a record of a different geological epoch that has since been ground into a single, homogeneous powder. Embedded within it are crystals—not quartz, but faceted lattices of a dark, non-reflective material that absorbs light. They jut from the ground at random angles, forming a dense forest of sharp, silent spires. Some are as thin as a finger; others are the size of a house, but all are angular, precise, and utterly still.

The built elements are scattered and minimal. There are structures here, but they are not buildings. They are stone cairns, arranged in patterns that defy any logical purpose. A circle of flat stones, each one carved with a symbol that is not a writing system but a topological map of a sensation. A wall that is only three stones high and stretches for a hundred meters before stopping, as if the idea of a wall was more important than its completion. The tools are primitive: stone axes with handles of petrified root, knotted ropes of a fibrous, wax-like plant that grows in patches, and fire-pits that have never been used. The only light is normal, a pale, steady silver-grey that seems to emanate from the sky itself, with no discernible sun.

The ecology is a paradox. Fauna is overwhelming, yet the place feels like a void. The creatures here are not animals in any biological sense. They are mobile ecosystems, mineralogical entities that move with the slow, grinding purpose of tectonic plates. One such entity is the “shard-drifter,” a being composed of a thousand crystal shards held together by a silent, magnetic will. It flows across the plateau, its components clinking with a sound that is so soft it is felt in the bones rather than heard. Another is the “dust-jelly,” a translucent, pulsing sac of parawax that drifts on the minimal air currents, absorbing dust and light and emitting a faint, cool phosphorescence. They are everywhere, filling the dense space, each one a complete, self-contained world of silent, non-aqueous chemistry.

The weirdness is a gentle breakdown of reality. The crystals do not refract light; they *compute* it. A shard-drifter passing near a crystal will cause the crystal to project a hologram of a future erosion pattern onto the ground, a ghost image of itself decaying over millennia. Time does not flow linearly here; it pools and eddies in certain areas. A walk of a hundred meters might take you across a hundred different geological ages. The silence is profound, broken only by the faint, granular sound of dust settling on stone, a sound that has been ongoing for so long it has become the baseline of existence.

The world is in a frozen equilibrium. Nothing changes. The dust does not blow away; it simply settles back into the same patterns. The crystals do not grow or erode; they are in their final, stable state. The creatures do not feed or reproduce; they simply *are*. The cool temperature is constant, the light never wavers, and the silence is eternal. This is not a world at the end of its life, but a world that has achieved a perfect, static state of being. It is a landscape that has been waiting, not for anything to happen, but for everything to have already happened, and now simply persists in the quiet, dense, and overwhelming memory of itself.
