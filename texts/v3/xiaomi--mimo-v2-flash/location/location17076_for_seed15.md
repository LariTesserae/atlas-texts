I am suspended in a shaft of warm, bright water.

The light is not the diffused gloom of the deep ocean; it is a sharp, horizontal column, a shaft of gold and blinding white that cuts through the liquid. It comes from a surface I cannot see, perhaps an opening far above, or perhaps the water itself is the light source, charged with energy. The water is warm, almost body-temperature, and perfectly clear, without murk or particulate. It holds the light like a solid crystal.

Beneath my feet—though "feet" feels inadequate here—is a floor. It is not rock or sediment. It is a mosaic of geometric tiles, each about a meter square, made of a smooth, non-porous material that resembles polished obsidian but is warm to the touch. The tiles are arranged in a precise grid, and the gaps between them are hairline fractures, through which a faint, cooler luminescence pulses, a slow, rhythmic heartbeat of blue-green light. The floor is not level; it slopes gently in one direction, and in the low point, water does not pool—it is simply more water, the same temperature, the same clarity. The floor *is* the substrate; there is no soil, no sand, no natural ground here. This is a made place, fully constructed, and the construction is total.

The scale is intimate, pocket-sized. I can see the walls of this chamber. They are not cavernous, but rather the dimensions of a large room, perhaps ten meters across. The walls are vertical, seamless, and made of the same dark, warm material as the floor, rising into the bright shaft of light above. There are no doors, no openings, save for the light shaft itself. The space is contained, a single, perfect cell within a larger, unseen structure.

Vegetation is sparse, but present. It grows from the fractures between the tiles. These are not plants as I know them. They are crystalline filaments, branching like frost on a windowpane, but warm and alive. They glow with the same soft blue-green luminescence as the light from the fractures. They are slow-moving, almost imperceptibly so, extending a millimeter over the course of a day. They have no leaves, no roots in soil; they are anchored directly to the tile, drawing energy from the light and the faint currents in the water. There are only a few dozen in this entire chamber, scattered, each one a unique, intricate sculpture of light and form.

There is no fauna. Not a single fish, not a microbe that I can see. The water is sterile in a way that feels intentional, not barren. The silence of life is profound, but it is not empty. The place is *active*. The water itself moves in slow, deliberate currents, a gentle circulation that is not wind-driven but seems to come from within the walls, a breath of the structure itself. The blue-green light in the floor fractures pulses in a slow, steady rhythm, like a sleeping giant's heartbeat. A fine, warm mist rises from the surface of the water in the shaft of light, condensing on the upper walls and dripping back down in a slow, constant cycle. This is the "weather": a microclimate of condensation and evaporation, contained entirely within this bright, warm room.

The sound is loud, but not chaotic. It is a deep, resonant hum, the sound of the water itself moving, of the structure vibrating at a frequency just at the edge of hearing. It is the sound of pressure, of a vast system operating. It is the sound of the light. It is a constant, soothing drone that fills the space completely, making the silence of animal life feel not like absence, but like a chosen state.

The rules of this world are simple. This is a place of pure geometry and light. The technology is primitive in its execution—the tiles are hand-set, the filaments are cultivated, not engineered—but the principle is advanced. It is a machine for holding light and water in a perfect, eternal balance. The erosion is young; the edges of the tiles are sharp, the fractures between them clean and new. There is no wear, no decay, only the slow, intentional growth of the crystalline filaments and the endless, gentle circulation of water and light.

If I reach out and touch the wall, it is warm and smooth. If I listen closely to the hum, I can hear the faint, higher-pitched chime of the water droplets hitting the pool below. The light does not blind; it illuminates everything with perfect clarity. There are no shadows here, only gradients of brightness.

This is not a place to live, but a place to *be*. A pocket of perfect, made order, submerged in warm, bright water, humming with its own quiet, active life. A sanctuary of geometry and light, waiting for nothing, complete in itself.
