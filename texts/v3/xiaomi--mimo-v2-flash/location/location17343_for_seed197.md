The place is a hollow. Not a hollow in the sense of a valley or a depression, though the ground is low and flat, but a hollow as in a space that has been scooped out and left with the echo of a shape inside it. The air is thick, not with moisture exactly, but with a kind of preserved dampness, a condensation that has settled into the very architecture of the scene. The water here is not a river but a memory of one; it seeps from the seams of things, gathers in shallow, lens-like pools on the broad, warm leaves of the ground, and falls in a silent, persistent mist that never quite stops, never quite begins.

The vegetation is total. It is not a forest, not a jungle, but a single, continuous organism that has chosen to occupy this space. The ground is a mat of living fiber, a dense, springy substrate that breathes in slow, rhythmic waves. From this mat rise structures: pillars of woven vine, arches of interlaced leaf-ribs, domes of translucent petal-flesh that filter the normal, golden light into a perpetual amber. There are no trees as separate entities; there are only extensions of the floor, growing vertically for a time before curving back into themselves, forming rooms, corridors, and vaults. The architecture is organic but deliberate, as if the plant had a memory of human settlement and decided to mimic it without understanding the purpose. A street, perhaps, but one that is soft underfoot and alive.

The scale is small. You can see from one end of the hollow to the other, a few hundred meters across, bounded by a sheer, living wall of the same vegetation that forms the interior. It is a cathedral-sized room, a single, enclosed ecosystem. The density, however, is near-void. Despite the total presence of plant matter, the spaces between the structures are vast. You can walk for ten paces and encounter nothing but the soft, breathing mat of the floor and the slow, vertical drift of mist. The emptiness is not empty; it is charged with the potential for presence. The fauna is abundant, but it is shy, silent, and perfectly adapted to the hollow. Small, multi-limbed creatures with phosphorescent markings scuttle through the hollows of the plant-architecture. A bird-like thing with six wings and no eyes hops from one broad leaf to another, its calls absorbed by the damp air before they can reach you. There are no predators here, or perhaps the entire ecosystem is a predator, and its prey is the light, the water, the very stability of the ground.

The world here has broken down. Not violently, but gently, as if a rule has been forgotten. The sound is the proof. It is deafening, but not with noise. It is a silence so profound it has weight, a pressure in the ears that makes the mist feel like a solid thing. The plants themselves emit a low, sub-audible hum, a vibration that you feel in your bones rather than hear. The fauna contributes clicks, whistles, and chitters that do not travel; they are born and die in the same instant, localized to the creature that makes them. The result is a cacophony of near-silence, a wall of sound that is the absence of its own echo.

The dynamics are active. The plant-architecture is in constant, slow motion. New shoots emerge from the mat, curling into place over days, forming new arches or sealing old ones. The mist cycles, thickening and thinning in a rhythm that has nothing to do with day or night. The fauna moves in synchronized pulses, a wave of movement that passes through the hollow every few hours, as if the entire community is breathing in unison. The erosion is established; the edges of the hollow are smooth, worn by centuries of this gentle, perpetual motion. There is no technology here, no tool, no fire. The only construction is the plant's own, and it is built to last, to breathe, to listen.

You stand on the soft, living floor, and the world holds you. The hollow is a place that has been waiting, not for you, but for itself to finish becoming. It is a cathedral of silence, a city of plants, a memory of water, and a reality that has decided to dream its own way forward.
