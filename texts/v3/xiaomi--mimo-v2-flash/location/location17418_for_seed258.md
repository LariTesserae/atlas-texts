The place is a high marsh, a wetland suspended in the sky.

It is built. Everything is made. The ground underfoot is not soil but a mat of woven roots, dense and springy, a living fabric stretched across a framework of ancient, hollow architecture. There is no raw geology here; the landscape is a construction, a vast and intricate scaffold. The elevation is high—a plateau, a plateau’s plateau, a shelf of land held aloft by what might have been columns in a forgotten age, now overgrown and submerged in living matter.

Water is constant. It is not a river or a lake, but a slow, pervasive exhalation. The air itself is wet, heavy with condensation that drips from every leaf, every strand of moss, every bulbous growth. The rocks—if they can be called rocks—are weeping stones, porous and soft, seeping a mineral-rich fluid that feeds the plant-life. The ground is a sponge; press down and water wells up, not in pools but in a uniform dampness that makes the entire landscape seem to breathe.

Vegetation is total. There is no bare earth, no open water, no sky unbroken by a canopy or a stem. The plants are the architecture. Great, hollow-trunked trees, more like fungal columns, rise in scattered ranks, their sides furred with phosphorescent moss. Between them, a secondary layer of broad, waxy leaves forms a continuous ceiling, catching the moisture and funneling it downward in a constant, soft patter. Vines, thick as a man’s arm, weave themselves into living bridges and arches, their surfaces slick with algae. The space is filled, but not crowded; the density is sparse. There is a great deal of space between the major presences—the massive tree-columns and the open glades where the leaf-ceiling thins—but the space is not empty. It is layered with ferns, with creeping plants, with fungi that glow with a faint, internal light. The balance is with emptiness, but the emptiness is a volume of air and light, not of ground.

The temperature is warm, a constant, humid warmth that never breaks into heat. The light is normal, filtered through the leaf-canopy into a diffuse, greenish gold that shifts only slightly with an unseen sun.

The fauna is rare. You might see a glimmering insect, a thing of iridescent carapace that navigates by the light of the fungi. Once, a slow, shapeless thing of gelatinous flesh moved through a glade, leaving a trail of damp earth where it passed, but it made no sound and left no lasting impression. There are no birds, no mammals, no calls in the night. The ecosystem is plant, fungus, and water, with the occasional, fleeting visitor.

The sound is loud, but it is a single, unchanging note. It is the sound of water: the drip from leaf to leaf, the seep from stone, the soft rush of air moving through the hollow trunks, a constant, low hum that fills the ears. There is no wind, no weather, no change in the soundscape. It is a steady-state symphony of moisture.

The world is in equilibrium. Nothing grows new; nothing decays. The plants are in a state of frozen growth, their leaves perpetually damp but never wilting, their roots perpetually extending but never seeking new ground. The erosion is emerging, but it is a construction, not a destruction. The landscape is under construction, but the construction is complete and static. New roots are weaving themselves into the mat, but they are not replacing anything; they are adding to a pattern that was set at the beginning. The embryonic state is one of perpetual, unchanging beginning.

The weirdness is in the details. The roots of the trees are not in the ground; they are the ground. The water does not flow; it exists. The plants do not compete; they collaborate in a silent, ancient agreement. The architecture is not a ruin; it is a living machine, a biological engine that has reached its final, perfect form and stopped.

You walk on a path that is not a path, but a slight depression in the root-mat, a channel worn by the slow passage of something that is no longer there. The air is thick with the smell of wet stone and chlorophyll, a scent that is both clean and ancient. You are alone, but the place is not empty. It is full of itself, in a state of perfect, wet, warm, high, sparse, loud, and eternal being.
