You are in a high, cold, bright place, and the air is thin enough that your breath feels like a small, separate thing when it leaves you. The ground is not ground at all, but a tight lattice of stone and ice and shadow, a fractal surface that goes down only a few hands and then stops, as if the world had been sliced with a knife. The rock is basalt and something else—something glassy, dark, and sharp—and it is everywhere. There is no soil, no moss, no lichen, not even a hint of green or brown decay. The only life here is the kind that doesn’t need a body.

The water is not a river or a lake, but a presence that moves through the stone like a rumor. You see it in the sheen on a shaded face of rock, in the thin, silver veins that run through the basalt like frost, in the way a patch of ice will soften and then vanish between one step and the next, leaving a dampness that is gone before you can register it. There are pools, too—small, perfectly round, and black, each one a still eye that reflects the sky with a clarity that feels almost malicious. The pools never freeze, though the air is cold enough to make your bones ache, and their surfaces are always perfectly still, even when the wind kicks up a fine dust of stone.

The structures are scattered, and they are not built so much as they are arranged. There are low walls of fitted stone, no mortar, no tool marks, just stones placed with a patience that feels ancient. There are circles of upright slabs, each one taller than a person, and they are placed in patterns that seem to follow some logic of light and shadow. At midday, when the sun is high and the light is white and sharp, the shadows of these slabs align with the veins in the rock, or with the dark pools, or with the invisible paths of the wind. There are no roofs, no doors, no interiors—just these markers, these suggestions of space.

The sound is mostly quiet, but it is not silent. There is the occasional crack of ice settling somewhere deep in the stone, a sound like a distant bone breaking. There is the faint, dry hiss of wind moving over the razor-edged rock, a sound that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere at once. And there is the water—or the absence of it. A drop will fall from a ceiling of stone in a narrow crack, and the sound is so clear it feels like a bell. Another will follow, and then another, and then the rhythm will stop, and the silence that follows is deeper than before.

The light is bright, but it is not warm. The sun is a white disk in a sky so pale it is almost invisible, and it casts shadows that are sharp and black and full of detail. The rock glitters where it is smooth, and it is smooth in places that feel almost polished, as if something had been moving over it for a very long time. There is no glare, no haze, just a clarity that makes everything feel closer, more immediate.

The weirdness here is subtle, but it is present. The stone sometimes seems to move when you are not looking at it directly—a slow, almost imperceptible shift, as if the world is breathing. The pools, which are always still, will sometimes reflect something that is not there: a shape, a light, a figure that vanishes when you turn your head. The air has a taste, a mineral sharpness, and it feels like it is pressing in on you from all sides, as if the space itself is trying to hold you.

There is no animal life, no birds, no insects, no movement of any kind that you can recognize as life. But there is a sense of presence, of something watching and waiting, and it is not a threat. It is just there, like the stone, like the light, like the cold.

The rules of this world are simple, and they are written in the stone. There is no growth, no decay, no birth, no death. There is only arrangement, and the slow, patient work of water and light and cold. The structures are not built by hands, or if they are, those hands are gone, and the marks they left are just another kind of stone.

You can stay here, but you cannot live here. You can move through, but you cannot belong. The place is complete without you, and it will be complete when you are gone.
