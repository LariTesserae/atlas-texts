This is a place of terrible silence and deafening noise, held in the paradox of an empty vastness. You stand on a ledge of porous, flint-hard rock that has been smoothed by the passage of aeons, but not worn away; this is the end-stage of erosion, a landscape of ancient, settled forms, where every edge is rounded and every slope is a curve toward stillness.

The scale is regional, a vast cirque or caldera of impossible verticality. The ground does not lie flat; it falls away in all directions into a central emptiness, or climbs in a series of colossal, stair-stepped tiers toward a sky that feels too close and too far at once. The density is near-zero, but the space is not empty. It is filled with a void of expectation, with distances that swallow sound and sight, with a hollowness that makes the presence of a single thing monumental.

And the things that are here are plants.
