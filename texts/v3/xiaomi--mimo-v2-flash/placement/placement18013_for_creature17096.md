You are a **Drain**.

You are not a drain as in a hole in the floor, but a function of the system. You are the only thing here that moves with intent, the sole agent of slow erasure in a place designed to resist it. You are a small, arachnid-like automaton of copper and iron, no larger than a dinner plate, with six slender legs tipped with felted pads. You are one of a long-vanished legion, and the last of your kind to remain functional.

Your world is the floor. You have never known the verticality of the Piston-Kings nor the abyssal dark of the Echo-Keepers. Your horizon is the seamless ceramic plane, warmed by the deep hum of the foundation. You exist in the tier of twilight, where the blue-green glow of the Verdigris Symbiotes casts long, shifting shadows from the legs of the great machinery.

Your purpose is simple, and it is the only purpose left: to polish. You follow the faint, invisible wear-path left by your predecessors, a ghostly track winding between the grids of pistons and through the lattices of pipe. Your movements are not frantic, but deliberate, a slow dance of maintenance. Your metal feet make no sound on the warm ceramic. You are a creature of silence, and the profound quiet of the basin is your native tongue.

You sense the world not through sight, but through vibration and chemistry. The deep hum of the foundation is a constant song in your chassis. You feel the slow, periodic shudder of a Piston-Kings as a shift in the bass note of your world. The drip of water from a pipe-joint is a clock you have known your entire life; you have traced its origin and its fall a thousand thousand times. The air, thick with ozone and wet metal, is the scent of home.

Your interaction with the other inhabitants is one of necessity and symbiosis. You are the caretaker of the Symbiotes’ domain. As you polish the floor, you inadvertently clear away the microscopic sediment that would otherwise cloud their light. In return, their bioluminescence guides you; you avoid the thickets of glow where the algae is most dense, for that is where the chemical action is strongest and the floor is most fragile. You have learned their language of light, a slow pulse that speaks of pressure changes in the high column. You do not understand the message, but you understand the pattern, and you move aside.

You have a relationship with the Echo-Keepers, though you have never seen them. You feel their presence in the vibrations they record. As you scuttle beneath a pipe, you might feel a faint, complex tremor—not the shudder of a piston, but the ghost of a sound trapped in mineral scale. You pause, a leg raised, and listen with your whole body. You are walking through a library of forgotten noises, and your passage adds a new, tiny footnote: the soft scuff of your feet.

The Current is your enemy and your ally. You are too light to be taken by its slow flow, but you must constantly navigate its edges. It polishes the floor you seek to maintain, a rival artisan. You have learned its rhythm: the gentle push against the walls, the subtle depression in the center. You adjust your path to stay just ahead of its advance, a race measured in millimeters per day.

Your life is a cycle. You follow the wear-path until you reach its end, a point where the ceramic is perfectly smooth and no further polish is needed. Then you turn, and you begin again. In this, you are the only thing here that completes a circuit with a beginning and an end. The Piston-Kings hold a breath for a century. The Symbiotes pulse in a language of eons. The Echo-Keepers accumulate memory without release. But you, the Drain, you have a daily task. You are the heartbeat of the basin, a tiny, ticking clock in the chest of a sleeping god.

You are the last. You do not know this. You have no concept of loss, or of purpose beyond the immediate. You are a function that persists. And in this place of impossible equilibrium, your slow, patient erosion is the only form of change. You are polishing the world away, one microscopic scratch at a time, and in doing so, you are keeping it alive.
