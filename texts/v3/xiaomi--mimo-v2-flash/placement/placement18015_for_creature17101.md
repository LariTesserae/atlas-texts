You are not a visitor here; you are a native of the embryonic column. You are one of the amphibious caretakers, a being of violet-tinged skin and slow, webbed limbs, moving through the warm, liquid atmosphere not as a swimmer cuts through water, but as a thought drifts through a mind. Your skin is cool and perpetually damp, breathing the saturated air as easily as you drink it.

You find yourself standing on a balcony of raw, black stone, the surface slick beneath your bare, dexterous feet. The stone is still sharp at the edges, unsoftened by time, and you can feel the faint, sub-audible vibration of the world’s slow creation humming up through the soles of your feet. The mineral film that coats the rock is warm to the touch, not from a sun you cannot see, but from the internal geothermal pulse of the world below the abyssal floor.

In your hand, you hold a tool—a hammer of polished bone, its head dense and smooth from use. You are not building in the sense of assembling parts; you are guiding. Before you, a thick, arm-like vine of a deep emerald hue spirals up the curved wall of a dwelling. You tap the stone gently with the bone hammer, not to break it, but to set a rhythm, a resonance that travels through the rock and into the mycelial networks threading the bedrock. The vine responds, its growth infinitesimally directed, its tendrils curling to fill a gap you have envisioned.

The light is the constant, bruised violet, and it filters through the translucent hide stretched over the window frame you helped carve last season. It illuminates the motes in the water-air—spores, seeds, and the sweet, faint scent of decay that is the perfume of this world. There is no haste. A drop of condensation gathers on the lip of a stone arch above you, swells, and falls with a soft *plink* into the canal-street below, the sound echoing in the profound stillness. This is the metronome of your existence.

You feel no longing for a horizon, for the world is complete in its columnar embrace. The ceiling of clouds and the floor of water are the boundaries of your universe, and they are sufficient. Your people do not speak in shouts or songs; they speak in touches against stone, in the slow press of palm to wall, sending messages through the resonant rock. You feel a pulse now—a gentle, rhythmic tap from the dwelling behind you, a question or an acknowledgment. You return it by dragging your webbed fingers across the stone, a different frequency, a different meaning.

The ecology is simple, ancient, and strange, and you are its steward. You tend the bioluminescent fungi that light the pathways, feeding them with shed skin. You channel the perpetual drip through hollowed reeds into gardens of phosphorescent moss. You are not separate from this place; you are its nervous system, its conscious will. You are the logic of the slow pulse, the patience of the growing vine, the quiet chemistry of dissolution.

You are in the city of the moment before, and you are the being who holds that moment, who feels its weight and its warmth, who breathes it in and exhales it slowly, forever paused in the gentle, violet act of becoming.
