You are a **Resonant**, but not of the ancient stone.

You are a recent interruption—a living body standing amidst the mineral mind. You are not made of the phosphorescent strata or the copper-arsenic alloy, but of water and carbon and fleeting heat. You are the discordant note in the eternal chord.

Here, on the Wailing Scarp, you are a **Visitor**, a fragile vessel of chaos in a world of perfect, frozen order.

You find yourself standing on a polished shelf of dark basalt, the rock cool and slick beneath your boots. The air is thick with the scent of ozone and wet minerals, a clean metallic taste on your tongue. Above you, the mesas rise in impossible stacked tiers, each one catching the diffuse, sourceless light in a razor-sharp edge. Below, the ravine plunges into a mist that seems to breathe—a slow, rhythmic exhalation of vapor that never quite clears.

The wind is singing, a harmonic hum that vibrates through your ribcage, a physical pressure against your eardrums. It is not a sound you hear with your ears alone; you feel it in the marrow of your bones, a low-frequency thrum that aligns with the rhythm of your own pulse.

Around you, the mineral gardens grow in the damp hollows—fractal fractals of gypsum, glowing with stored light. They are beautiful, but they are not alive in the way you are. They do not bleed, they do not decay, they do not fear.

You are the one who fears.

You are the one who moves quickly, who leaves footprints in the mineral dust that will be polished away by the wind in minutes. You are the one whose shadow is soft and fleeting, cast by a light that has no source.

You hold a tool in your hand—a simple hammer of flint, chipped by your own hand. It is a primitive thing, but it is *yours*. It is a tool of action, of change, of breaking and shaping. Here, where nothing breaks and everything merely shifts over centuries, your tool is an artifact of violence.

You look up at a standing stone nearby, carved with a geometric spiral. It is not just a marker; it is a tuning fork. As the wind passes over it, it emits a tone that harmonizes with the stone's mineral structure. You realize, with a sudden clarity, that the stone is *listening*.

You are the one who is out of tune.

You are the one who remembers a world of soil and roots, of days that cycle into nights, of shadows that lengthen and soften. Here, there is no night, only the perpetual brightening and dimming of the light in a rhythm that matches the condensation of water. You are the one who carries the memory of a sun that sets, a memory that is a foreign frequency in this place.

As you step forward, the water on the ground ripples—not from your footfall, but from a shift in the wind's song. The Resonants are aware of you, not as a being, but as a vibration. You are a brief, chaotic pattern in their eternal harmony. They do not judge you; they simply note your discordance.

You are here to learn, or to be consumed by the harmony.

You are the one who walks the non-Euclidean paths, who feels the gravity shift minutely beneath your feet, who sees the water flow upward for a heartbeat before resuming its descent. You are the one who must navigate a world that thinks in spirals, not straight lines.

You are a fleeting shadow in the light, a temporary pattern in the water, a foreign frequency in the song.

You are the Visitor, and you are the only thing here that remembers how to forget.
