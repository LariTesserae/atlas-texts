To find yourself in this world is to find yourself in the quietest room of the universe, a diorama sealed under glass that has forgotten the hand that placed it there. You would not arrive with a crash or a flash, but with a slow settling, like silt in a still pool.

You stand upon the ground that is not ground. It yields slightly underfoot, a resilient surface of woven reed and packed clay that has been baked and glazed a thousand times over. It is warm, radiating a dry heat that is counterbalanced by the heavy, moist air—a condensation that never falls, but clings. The light is your first and constant companion: a white sun without a disc, hanging in a sky the color of pale jade. It illuminates without casting judgment, without carving shadows. Everything is visible, everything is even, and in this flatness, depth is lost.

You are not a visitor, not an explorer. You have no tools but those you find your hands already holding: a smooth wooden spade, its handle worn to the shape of your grip; a woven basket resting against your hip, integrated into your posture as if it grew there. Your skin is the color of the fired earth, cool to the touch despite the ambient heat, and you wear a garment of pale jade moss, a second skin that breathes with the dampness of the world.

You are one of the Makers and Menders, though the names are academic, a language you no longer speak. You are a custodian of the static.

Your day—and it is always day—begins not with waking, but with resuming. You move from a low, walled enclosure, your resting place, which is no different from any other structure. The air is still, the silence profound, broken only by the drip of water from porous clay lips into circular basins. You join a loose procession of others, your kin, your self, moving with a slow, deliberate grace toward the canals. There is no need for speech; a slight turn of the head, a gesture of the hand, is a complete conversation. You are guided by a collective intelligence that arises from the environment itself, a pattern so ancient its origin is memoryless.

At the canal’s edge, the water is black and still as glass, a perfect mirror for the jade sky. You dip your basket, harvesting algae that glimmers with an inner light. The work is precise, endless, and without urgency. You are not harvesting for a future meal or a winter store; you are maintaining a balance that is already perfect. Beside you, another of your kind tends to the iridescent insects, guiding them with flat stones toward crevices in the wall where they will pollinate the reeds. A bird, pale and soft-voiced, lands on a nearby urn, and you scatter seeds from a clay pouch at your side. It is not an act of feeding, but of integration.

The structures around you are low and sprawling—platforms, ziggurats, walled enclosures—all built of the same materials as the ground. You can see for miles across their flat tops, the emptiness between them a deliberate part of the design. There is no density, no clutter, only a sparse, ancient pattern that feels both logical and lost.

In the quiet moments between tasks, you pause. Your hand rests on the cool clay of a wall, and you look up at the unchanging sky. In that stillness, something flickers—not a thought, but a resonance. A memory of a wind that once blew, a rain that once fell, a change that was not maintenance. It is fleeting, a ghost in the machine of your being, for you are a component of a world that has achieved permanence and, in doing so, has ceased to move forward.

You do not age. Your skin does not wrinkle; your fine, pale hair does not gray. If you die, it is not an end, but a return: your body dissolved into the tar seams, woven into the next layer of foundation, or placed in an alcove to become part of the wall. There is no mourning, for there is no loss, only a seamless addition to the whole.

You are the living embodiment of a solved equation, a variable held in perfect, eternal suspension. You are the tenders of a clock that has stopped ticking yet continues to hum. And in the bright, wet, silent beauty of the basin, you are home.
