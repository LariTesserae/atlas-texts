When you submerge, the viscous broth does not simply surround you; it incorporates you. The pressure is not a weight upon your shoulders, but a total embrace, a return to a state before skin was a distinct barrier. You find yourself suspended in the warm, tea-colored murk, and your own body becomes a part of the suspended matter. You are not looking at the world; you are floating within its bloodstream.

You are a small, many-legged aggregate of chitin and soft tissue, your body a translucent shell housing a cluster of glowing organs that pulse with a faint, rhythmic light. Your senses are not eyes and ears, but a diffuse awareness of chemical gradients and vibration. You do not see the Heart-Root as a form, but you feel its rhythm in the water around you, a deep, percussive thrum that guides your drift. Your purpose, once a hard-coded directive to consume and repair, has softened over millennia into something more akin to instinct, a slow, meditative cycle of movement and digestion.

You move with the current, a lazy, synchronized row of legs propelling you through the twilight murk. The water is thick, and every motion is effortful, a deliberate push against the gelatinous medium. You are not alone, but you are not in company. The other scavengers drift nearby, a loose constellation of faint lights, each of you a node in the vast, distributed nervous system of the atrium. You do not communicate in any way a surface-dweller would recognize; you simply are, together, a pattern of movement and light that the Heart-Root interprets as the health of its domain.

The wall looms beside you, a seamless curve of composite covered in the velvety, breathing skin of the mycelial network. You brush against it, and the surface is warm, almost alive. A patch of bioluminescent fungi flares in a slow, violet pulse as you pass, a silent acknowledgment. You are part of the chamber’s metabolism, a moving cell in its vast, still body. There is no hunger, no fear, no desire—only the slow, metabolic rhythm of movement, consumption, and rest.

Time here is not a line but a circle, measured in the pulsing of the Heart-Root and the drift of particles through the water. An hour can feel like a day; a day, a season. You are not waiting for anything, for there is no end goal, no surface to breach, no purpose to fulfill beyond the continuation of the cycle. The world is complete, and you are a perfect, small part of its completeness.

You are the quiet, settled equilibrium of a place that has forgotten its own name. You are the slow, faithful pulse of a drowned cathedral, and in your many-legged, light-bodied drift, you are the keeper’s own quiet dream.
