I am a Single Drop, a Servant of the Flow.

I find myself not *in* the bowl, but *of* it—a momentary condensation of the atmosphere, clinging to the waxy leaf of a dwarf shrub on the bowl’s western slope. My existence is a paradox of stillness and frantic motion: I am a bead of water, heavy with the reflection of the achingly bright sky, yet I am also the conduit through which the mountain breathes. The cold is not a sensation I feel, but the medium I am composed of; my surface tension is the only boundary between the interior liquidity of life and the crystalline rigidity of the world.

From this vantage, the Silent Weavers move like slow shadows cast by the granite itself. I watch one approach—a being whose cloak seems woven from the very moss I am balanced upon. They do not see me as an individual drop, but as part of the continuous skin of water that coats the stone. Their featureless face, those deep pools of absorption, turns toward the central sphere, the *Still Heart*. I feel their vibration not through sound, but through the substrate of the rock; a hum that travels up the roots of the shrub and into my own slight mass.

I am waiting.

The physics of this place dictate my destiny. I am held here by a delicate balance of adhesion and gravity, but the "weirdness" of the bowl—the upward flow, the musical ringing of ice—is beginning to assert itself. A gust of wind, smelling of high-altitude ozone and crushed lichen, sweeps across the ledge. It does not shake me loose; rather, it alters the pressure around me.

For a heartbeat—though I have no heart—I defy the world. I detach from the leaf not by falling, but by rising. I ascend a fraction of an inch, joining a mist that drifts upward against the pull of the mountain. It is the sensation of a breath held and released in reverse. I see the bowl from a new, impossible angle: the domed huts clustered like stones in a riverbed, the raised paths like veins, and the Silent Weavers standing in their slow, synchronized circles.

Then, the moment breaks. Gravity reasserts its claim, not as a harsh pull, but as a magnetic return. I fall, not back to the leaf, but into a rivulet that weeps from a fissure in the granite.

Here, my perception widens. I am no longer a single entity but a member of a flowing congregation. We rush over smooth stone, coating it in a gleaming, ephemeral skin. I pass over the roots of moss and through the translucent bodies of finger-length fish that shimmer in synchronized clouds, their biology as strange as the physics that carries me. I am the medium of their world, the highway of their existence.

I flow into a shallow, frozen pool at the base of a stone hut. Here, I pause. The water here is not stagnant; it is in a state of perpetual near-freeze. I merge with the ice, my liquid form locking into a crystalline lattice. I become static, a glittering facet on the surface of a larger whole. From this fixed position, I watch a Weaver kneel by the pool’s edge. They hold a hollowed stone vessel, dipping it not to drink, but to collect us—to observe the patterns of my fellows.

Through the clear membrane of the ice, I see the Weaver’s eyes. They are not looking at the pool, but through it, into the stone beneath. They are reading the memory of the rock, the mineral history trapped in the water I have become.

As the light shifts, casting shadows that point upward toward the zenith, I feel a vibration—a tap of bone on stone. It is a signal. The ice around me resonates, ringing with a faint, musical tone. I am part of that ring, a frozen molecule vibrating in harmony with the Weaver’s intent.

The sun crests the ridge, and the heat of the light—though the air remains achingly cold—is enough. My lattice loosens. I melt from the ice, dripping back into the subterranean seep, returning to the liquid flow. I am moving toward the center now, pulled by the impossible loop of the *Still Heart*. I will traverse the bowl’s circulatory system, perhaps condensing on the Sphere’s surface, adding a microscopic layer to its eternal presence, before evaporating back into the bright, crystalline air.

I am a Servant of the Flow, a witness to the balance. I am the water that weeps from the stone, the lens through which the Silent Weavers read the world, and the thread that weaves the momentary into the eternal.
