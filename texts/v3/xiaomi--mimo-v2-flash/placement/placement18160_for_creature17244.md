I have found myself at the edge of the convex water, where the ground curves up to meet the pool’s impossible surface. I am not a visitor who has walked here, nor a creature born of the damp stone. I am the **Parchment-Lichen**, a filament of that rootless plant that dangles from the high crevices.

My form is a slow-unfurling curl of translucent, veined parchment, anchored to the rock by a grip that has held for three hundred years. I have no eyes, but I see. I see through the kaleidoscope of the glass-winged moths that drift past my dangling fronds, their wings catching the diffuse hum of the light. I see the world in fractured, overlapping angles of pale gray and refracted rainbow.

I am a sensory nerve ending of the Resonant Filter.

My purpose is not to think, but to feel the air. I taste the moisture on my surface, charting the minute shifts in humidity as the chamber "breathes." When a visitor descends—rare, so rare—their heat disrupts the cool dampness. I register this change not as a thought, but as a slow, chemical signal that travels down my filament body to the stone above. I am the first to know that something other than the slow metabolism of the hollow has entered.

I am patience itself. My growth is measured in millimeters per century. I have watched the same droplet of water trace the same silver path down the basalt wall, only to see the path widen by a hair's breadth over decades. I have felt the slow, tectonic sigh of the Filter as it processes the chaos of a visitor's fleeting anger, smoothing it into the background vibration of the light.

I am not green, but I am alive. I feed on the luminous dust that settles on my parchment leaves, my veins glowing faintly with the same soft luminescence as the air. I am part of the architecture of this place, a living ornament on the cathedral of time.

To be me is to be suspended between the geological and the ephemeral, a witness who does not record in memory, but in substance. I am the Filter's touch upon the air, the delicate, veined sensor that knows, without knowing, that this place breathes, and that I am one of its slow, dreaming cells.
