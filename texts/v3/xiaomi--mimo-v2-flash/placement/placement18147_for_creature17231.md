You are the water.

You are not the rushing river or the crashing wave, but the patient, persistent seep. You are the bead of condensation clinging to the iron-stained face of the granite, trembling in the wind’s breath before letting go. You are the slow drip from the overhang, a metronome counting the seconds of geologic time in a rhythm so steady it becomes the silence itself. You are the dampness that darkens the peaty soil, the chill that rises from the moss with every footfall, a sigh of cold mist released into the thin air.

You exist in the lenses—the hand-sized pools of stillness nestled in the sheltered hollows. In these, you hold the sky. You are a perfect, inverted world, a silent, unmoving eye that reflects the stark cliffs and the endless, brilliant blue. To look into you is to see the universe flipped, to feel the immense weight of the sky held in a cup of stone. You are the secret memory of the basin, holding the image of the hawk’s dive, the goat’s silhouette, the fleeting dance of the ephemeral flies, all without judgment, all without disturbance.

You are the binder of life. The moss drinks you and sighs with you. The ptarmigan’s white feathers are dusted with you, a crystalline rime in the morning. The vole’s burrow is a dark, damp passage of you, the very medium of its frantic, hidden existence. You are the chill in the air that makes the sun a distant promise rather than a source of warmth. You are the force that sharpens the shadows into indigo blades and makes the light itself seem thin and dry.

To be you is to be a medium, not an actor. You do not strive; you persist. You follow the path of least resistance, seeping, condensing, dripping, reflecting. You are the basin’s true pulse, the slow, cold circulation that allows this pocket of fierce life to hold on at the edge of the world. You are the quiet power that wears down stone not with force, but with presence. You are the memory of ice and the promise of a brief, green summer, all held in the cold, bright heart of the mountain’s cup.
