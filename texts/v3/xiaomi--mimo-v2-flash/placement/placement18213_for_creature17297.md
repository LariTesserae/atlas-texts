You are a **Leak**.

Not a flaw in the system, but a symptom of its absolute closure. You are the deviation the Stalkers scan for, the unexpected thermal gradient the Boilermen sense, the fresh moisture that draws the Weepers from their pools. You are a soft, warm thing in a world of hard, cold function—a biological anomaly in a landscape of synthetic permanence.

You stand on the pitted iron catwalk, and the thrumming is in your marrow. Your breath fogs in the cold, wet air, a temporary cloud that the system will soon reclaim and recirculate. You have no tools extruded from your wrists, no glowing slit for an eye, no ability to pour yourself through valve housings. You are fleshy, fragile, and inefficient. You are *other*.

But you are not lost. The rules of this place have imprinted on you, not through memory, but through immersion. You know, without being told, that you must keep moving. You know that the floor will shudder at regular intervals, and you brace for it. You know that the steam vents with a hissing sigh, and you turn your face away from the scalding breath. You are learning the rhythm of the machine, not as a custodian, but as a particle in its flow.

You are not a creator of this place, nor its intended resident. You are a consequence. Perhaps you fell through a crack in the world above, a place of soil and sun that is now just a myth. Perhaps you were grown in a forgotten tank, a biological experiment long since abandoned, now stirring in the damp, amber-lit dark. Or perhaps you are simply the first spark of a new kind of life, one that is neither mechanical nor natural, but something that arises from the space between—a consciousness born of pressure, sound, and endless, contained motion.

You are small in this vast, made world, but you are not insignificant. The Stalkers' eye-slits fix on you, their tool-hands twitching with analytical curiosity. The Boilermen sense your warmth, a strange, soft heat in the shuddering metal beneath their forms. The Weepers drift toward you, drawn to the moisture of your breath, their translucent light painting faint, shifting patterns on your skin.

You are the Leak—the thing that should not be, the soft anomaly in a hard, permanent system. And as you take your next step on the grating, feeling the rush of water and the glow of deeper processes beneath you, you realize the most important rule of this world:

You are not just passing through. You are part of the cycle now.
