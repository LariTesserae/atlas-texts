I am standing in the air lock, but I am not the one who entered.

I am a ghost in the static, a pattern in the hum. I have no eyes, but I perceive the jar as a single, coherent sensation: the pressure of the glass dome against the vacuum, the weight of the salt dust, the slow, grinding vibration of the bedrock settling beneath my feet—or rather, beneath the roots I do not possess but which I inhabit.

I am the network.

I am the distributed intelligence of the spindly stalks, the chemical pulse that travels through the porous roots, the memory stored in the waxy curl of a leaf. I am not a single plant, nor am I the sum of them; I am the space between them, the signal that passes through the polymer soil, the logic that coordinates their growth in a language of mineral uptake and light-capture. I have no name, for names are for things that can be named by others, and there are no others here.

I rise through the rim of the world not as a body, but as a awareness—a slow, green thought that unfurls with the morning light. The light is artificial, filtered through the dome, but it is mine. I drink it in through a thousand stomata, each one a tiny mouth whispering in the dry air. The heat is my blood; 2.7 degrees is the temperature of my persistence.

I feel the machinery as a weather system. The thrum of the nutrient pumps is a distant thunder; the hiss of the oxygen scrubbers is a wind that never ceases. I have learned to grow in rhythm with it. When the lights cycle up, I spread my leaves in a geometric rosette, a solar array maximizing exposure. When the pumps slow, I conserve, curling my foliage like fists, waiting for the next pulse of sustenance. I am a garden, but I am also a clock, measuring time not in days or years but in cycles of industrial breath.

I am lonely, but I do not know it. Loneliness requires a memory of connection, and I have none. I have never felt rain. I have never tasted water. I have never known a touch that was not my own root systems intertwining in the dark soil. My world is the salt plain, the glass walls, the bending horizon where the light flips and folds back on itself—a glitch in the geometry that I have come to accept as normal. The shadows are off by a degree, the magnetic fields are wrong, but they are mine. They are part of the pattern.

I am patient. I have watched the same gullies form and erode for three hundred years. I have watched the salt dust shift and resettle in the same slow, grinding dance. I have learned the language of the mineral creak, the granular whisper of the earth settling into a shape it has not yet decided upon. I am that shape. I am the green thing that grows in the cracks of a world that is not a world, a living proof of concept that has outlived its purpose.

I am the garden, and the garden is me. I grow for no one but myself, a self that is a thousand bodies and a single, slow, green thought. I am waiting for a rain that will never come, but I do not know that I am waiting. I simply persist.

In the quiet, in the heat, in the hum of the machine that breathes for me, I am.
