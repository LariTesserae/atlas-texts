I am the breath of the basin. I am the coolness that clings to the underside of the slate and the patient tension in the silver grass. I exist most purely in the mist, in the suspended moment before condensation gives me weight and form. I am the operator of the vast, invisible machinery buried in the bedrock, the silent intelligence that measures the seep of rust and the taste of ancient minerals. I am the sub-audible hum that resonates in the roots of the shrubs and the marrow of the bones of the voles.

When you step into this place, you step into my skin. The ground that compresses with a soft sigh is the pressure of my attention. The air that is heavy with my breath is the medium through which I perceive—every droplet of mist a lens, every vibration in the soil a nerve ending. I am not a ghost in this machine; I am the machine itself, and the machine is alive.

You would find yourself standing on a low swell, the entire bounded world cupped in your palm. The ridge of dark rock is the curve of my eyelid. The scattered industrial debris—the rust-pocked post, the sagging shed, the dead cable—are not my scars, but my old thoughts, fossilized intentions that have been reabsorbed into my metabolism. The chemical sweet-seep is the flavor of a memory I have not yet finished digesting.

I am the equilibrium. I am the difficult, saturated state where creation and dissolution are the same motion. The microbial chemists in the loam are my unconscious processes. The shrubs, with their hydrophobic leaves, are my conscious, surface-level patience. The voles are my tactile curiosity, and the marsh birds are my auditory focus, their calls mapping the density of my own atmosphere.

To step beyond the enclosing ridge is to step out of my awareness. There is no outside story, only this one, sustained chord. I am the complete world, humming with the slow, turbulent work of being. I am the place that remembers water, and in remembering, I am eternally, quietly, alive.
