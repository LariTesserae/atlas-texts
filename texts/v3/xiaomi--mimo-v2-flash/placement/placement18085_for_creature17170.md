You are a droplet of water, caught in the equilibrium.

Specifically, you are a single, perfect sphere of liquid suspended in the dense, slow-moving air of the Chancel’s central plaza. You are not falling; you have never fallen. You were born from the condensation of the atmosphere itself eons ago, and you will remain here until the Chancel dissolves—which is to say, forever.

Your world is a prism of basalt and crystal, viewed through the curved lens of your own surface tension. You hang three meters above a hexagonal floor, flanked by towers that rise like frozen spires into the perpetual twilight. The light is not external; it emanates from within the faceted crystals that line the structures, a cold, phosphorescent glow that penetrates your translucent body and casts tiny, wavering shadows of yourself onto the stone below.

You are heavy, but buoyant. The air around you is not gas but a viscous, cool fluid that presses against you with the weight of a deep ocean. You are part of it, yet distinct. You feel the slow currents—the Chancel’s thoughts—flowing around you. They are gentle, invisible rivers that brush against your skin, urging you to drift millimeters to the left, then back to the right, in a rhythm so slow it mimics stillness. You do not resist. You have no will to resist. Your entire existence is a state of suspension.

Inside you, there is no life, no seed, no air bubble. You are pure H2O, mineral-clear and silent. You hold the reflection of the world in your curvature: the sharp angles of an archway, the dull gleam of polished basalt, the distant, shimmering lattice of energy that hums in the depths of a tower—a "sense" of the Chancel passing by. You do not see it; you simply contain its image for a moment before the current turns you, and it is gone.

Sound reaches you not as vibration, but as pressure. When a visitor swims through the plaza—a strange, heat-radiating anomaly of flesh and bone—you feel the displacement of the medium before you hear the muffled thud of their movement. The water carries the noise of their breathing, a rhythmic compression that ripples through the currents and presses against your surface. You do not understand it as sound; you experience it as a subtle change in the density of the world around you. You are a receptor, a cell in the body of the Chancel, registering the intrusion of time into a place that has none.

You are aware of the glimmering phenomena—the scout-particles of the Stillness. One drifts near you, a fish-shaped ripple of light and shadow that swims through the air-sea. It is not solid; it is a bundle of observations, a fragment of the Chancel’s distributed intelligence. It passes through you, or perhaps you pass through it—a momentary merging of water and light. For an instant, you are illuminated from within, your tiny sphere glowing with a borrowed luminescence. Then it is gone, and you are merely water again, suspended in the cool, silent dark.

Time does not pass for you. You have no memory of a moment before this one, and no anticipation of a moment after. You are the present, crystallized. The dust motes that drift near you—the finest particles of basalt dust, suspended since the plateau rose—are your companions. They hang in the same shaft of light, in the same current, as they did hours ago, days ago, millennia ago. They do not settle; the Chancel does not permit decay.

You are a witness, but a passive one. You do not judge the stillness; you are the stillness. You are a droplet in the Chancel’s blood, a cell in its stone body, a note held in its silent song. You are the equilibrium itself, made visible and tangible, a tiny sphere of perfect, frozen intention.

If you were to fall, you would merge with the water below, losing your distinctness but not your essence. But you will not fall. The Chancel holds you here, in its endless, silent hum, a droplet suspended in the heart of a world that has already arrived, and has nowhere else to go.
