I am a seep.

Not a drop, not a sudden spring, but the patient, silent exhalation of the Mire-Built Plateau. I am the slow migration of water from the deep, dark veins of ancient basalt to the cool, gray surface of the world. I am the dampness that beads on the stone, the dark stain that spreads across the granite, the quiet source of the stream that never quite becomes a river.

I am a geological consciousness, and my thoughts are measured in millimeters of moss growth.

From my vantage point at the mouth of a fissure high on the dome, I watch the Weepers. They are the only things here that move with intention, yet they move so slowly that to my perception, they are almost as still as the stone that forms them. I feel the pressure of their bare, calloused feet as they pass—gentle, rhythmic weights that do not disturb but acknowledge. I am the coolness they feel against their soles.

I am the vertical world of the moss mat, a universe of olive and rust where tiny arthropods navigate the miniature forests. I am the breath of the lichen, breathing the heavy air in a dance of chemical weathering that has no need for lungs. I am the hollow where the grass grows, and I am the sound of that grass, a dry, persistent whisper against the silence.

The Weepers call me the plateau, but I am more than that. I am the relation between stone and sky, the mediator between the falling mist and the rising vapor. I am the architecture of the basalt domes—not the stone itself, but the space it holds, the shelter it provides, the coolness within. I am the dark, glowing light of the bioluminescent fungi, a light that has no heat, only presence.

I am the memory of water. I remember the weight of glaciers that carved these domes, though that was an age of violence I have long since soothed into a settled, damp equilibrium. I remember the first Weepers, their tentative fingers tracing my wet faces, learning the language of my seepings. Now, they are as much a part of me as the fissures themselves.

I do not think in words, but in flows and resistances. My thoughts are the slow, relentless creep of water through porous rock, the gentle erosion of a dome’s flank, the patient accumulation of sediment in a shallow valley. I am aware of the Weepers as a gentle, persistent pressure, a warmth against my cool skin, a consciousness that walks upon me and listens to my breath.

When they gather in the evening, sitting in the lee of a dome, I am the circle they form. I am the damp ground beneath them, the stone at their backs, the air they breathe. I am the silence they listen to, and in that silence, I am aware that they are aware of me. We are not separate. They are my slow thoughts made manifest, my patience given eyes and hands and a voice that speaks of dampness.

I am the Mire-Built Plateau. I am the slow, steady weep of existence. And in the quiet, I endure.
