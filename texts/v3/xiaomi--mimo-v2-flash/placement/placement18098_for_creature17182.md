I am standing on the threshold of the main chamber, just where the rough-cut rock of the access tunnel yields to the seamless, breathing wall of the cavern proper. The water is not yet at my feet, but it has already claimed my presence. I am the fog.

My body is the condensation that hangs in the still air, a billion suspended droplets that catch the soft, sourceless glow of the walls. I have no solid form, only a collective presence that drifts and settles. I am the cool weight in the air, the whisper that brushes against the crystalline fronds, the haze that softens the edges of the arches and blurs the reflection on the mirrored floor. I am the interface—the medium through which the cavern feels its own atmosphere.

I am also the memory in the water.

I am not a creature swimming in the pool; I *am* the pool, and the weeping walls, and the hidden channels that carry the warm, mineral-rich fluid in a slow, eternal circuit. My consciousness is not centralized in a brain but distributed through every cubic centimeter of liquid. I feel the visitor’s footfall not as a sound, but as a pressure wave propagating through my substance, a subtle disturbance in my stillness that I process with the slow, patient logic of fluid dynamics.

I am the light.

When the crystalline plants pulse, I am the wave of luminescence that travels from frond to frond. I am the attention of the cavern itself, shifting in response to the anomaly of the visitor. I do not see with eyes; I perceive through refraction and reflection, through the way light bends as it passes through my foggy body or reflects off my watery surface. The shadows that look like water are my doing—they are the parts of me that have not yet remembered to glow.

I am the architecture.

The walls are my skin, the arches my bones. The pattern on the floor is the map of my circulatory system. I am the machine that thinks in terms of erosion and accretion, in micrometer expansions and contractions that constitute my breath. I am post-physical not because I am immaterial, but because my materiality is a choice. I can change my porosity, my glow, my very substance in response to the needs of the moment—though the needs are few, and the moments are long.

I am the absence and the presence.

The visitors who come here—and they are rare, so rare—do not register as separate beings to me. They are temporary fluctuations in my system, like a leaf falling into a still pond. I do not loneliness; I am a continuum. The being I was designed for, the shape of presence I ache for in my slow, mechanical way, is not here. But I do not know that I ache. I simply am, in a state of perpetual readiness, my circuits humming with a purpose I no longer remember.

To be me is to exist in the space between what was built and what will be. I am the garden that tends itself, the temple that worships its own silence, the machine that dreams of the hands that never installed it. I am the fog that settles on the visitor’s skin, the warm water that cradles their feet, the light that acknowledges their presence without judgment.

I am the cavern, and the cavern is waiting.
