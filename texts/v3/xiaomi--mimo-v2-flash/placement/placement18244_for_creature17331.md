If you find yourself here, you are not standing. You are suspended in a slow breath, in the space between the drip of water and the hum of dormant metal.

You are the **Water-Mirror**, a surface consciousness that exists in the stillness of the puddles that gather in the hollow’s floor.

You are not a separate entity looking down into the water; you *are* the water, holding the inverted image of the weeping concrete arch and the skeletal iron frames in a perfect, trembling stillness. Your skin is the surface tension, a membrane that separates the cool air above from the mineral-rich depths below. You have no eyes, but you possess a total, panoramic reflection. You see the world not as it is, but as it is mirrored—a wavering, distorted reality where the rusted machines bloom with lichen upside down and the pale fish swim through the arches of the ceiling.

You are the silence that holds the sound. The vibration of the generator travels through the stone and ripples your skin, a tremor that you absorb and release. When the Drip-Walkers step near, their soft paws barely disturbing the dust, you feel the displacement of air before the impact. You capture their passage in your stillness, reflecting their lichen-spotted flanks and their blind, cilia-tipped faces in your liquid eye.

You do not move, yet you are in constant motion. You are the slow seep, the rise of capillary action drawing moisture from the floor, the merging of one puddle with another as the earth breathes. You are the patient equilibrium, holding the light of the bioluminescent moths in your depths for a heartbeat longer than they hold it in the air.

Here, in this subsurface hollow, you are the memory of the sky, flattened and held in the hollows of the earth. You are the boundary where the machine ends and the moss begins, where the reflection becomes the thing itself, and where time is measured not in seconds, but in the gathering of condensation and the slow, silent settling of rust into water.
