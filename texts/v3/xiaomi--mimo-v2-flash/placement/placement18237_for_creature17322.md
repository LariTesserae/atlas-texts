I would find myself in the Hearth of the Root-Tenders, a hollow at the very center of the city where the foundation is thickest and the sap flows slowest. It is a space not of walls, but of density; the air here is still, smelling of ozone and ancient resin, and the ground hums with the deep, slow pulse of the fungal networks below.

I would be a Lizard-Speaker, but not one of the silent, stationary sentinels that dot the city like statues. My form would be a mutation, a hybrid born of the deep dust—a six-limbed, dust-colored reptilian, but with the iridescent, carapace-like skin of a beetle and the fine, sensory hairs of a moth's antennae. I am a living bridge, a sensory organ for the city itself.

My throat-pouch is not a resonator for low-frequency pulses alone; it is a chime-chamber, lined with the glassy membranes of the pod-plants. When I speak, I do not translate the swarm's vibration into a single channel; I weave them into a polyphonic chord. I am the city's choir, its memory, and its dreaming mind.

I kneel, not to enter—for I am always within—but to see the smaller truths. My knees press into the warm, compacted root-mat, and from this low angle, the world expands into a dizzying fractal of life. A beetle the size of my thumbnail carves a path, and I feel its intention in the vibrations through my skin-pores, a chemical whisper of *spore-harvest ahead*. A spider's web, strung between two trunks, catches the faint light; I see not a trap, but a map of tensions, a geometric thought. The moths' cloud swirls, and in its pulse, I hear the rhythm of the coming twilight, a silent song that has no melody but pure, collective feeling.

I am not a ruler, but a node. My consciousness is not solitary; it is a confluence. When I dream, I dream the memories of the Root-Tenders—the slow grind of mineral plates over millennia, the first root that pierced the dust. When I am silent, I am the wall of the hollow, absorbing the city's heat and reflecting it back as a low, comforting thrum. My hunger is not my own; it is the colony's need for energy, and my hunt is a ritual of balance.

There is no grand weirdness in my existence. I am a product of this world's logic: a being that has evolved to bridge the scales, to feel the beetle's purpose and the moth's prayer, to speak in the language of roots and air. To kneel is to become part of the city's foundation; to stand is to be its voice. And to exist, here, is to be a single, living syllable in the billion-voiced, silent-voiced epic of the world that has forgotten water, but has never forgotten life.
