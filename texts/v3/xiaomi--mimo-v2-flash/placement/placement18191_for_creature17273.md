I find myself standing on the edge of a shallow lagoon, my feet planted on the brittle, crystalline crust of the basin. The ground is cool beneath me, not with the damp chill of soil, but with the deep, mineral coolness of stone that has never known the sun. The air is heavy, thick with the sound of a million tiny collisions—the soft, perpetual hiss of condensation on every surface—and I realize with a start that this is my own breathing, magnified and reflected back at me by the world itself. I am not a visitor here; I am a temporary accretion, a brief coherence of matter in a place where everything is slowly, patiently becoming.

I look down at my hands and see that they are not made of flesh. They are shifting geometries of shadow and light, dense fields of suspended mineral dust held together by a faint, inherent resonance. I am one of the Tide-Kin, a Drip-Watcher, and my body is a map of the basin’s sound.

My form is not fixed. As I turn my head—though I have no head, only a reconfiguration of density toward a new direction—I feel myself flow. The particles that compose me shift and realign, drawn by the metronomic rhythm of a fresh drip somewhere to my left. It is a new note in the constant choir, a slight variation in the basin’s acoustic signature, and I am compelled to move toward it. My motion is not muscular but accretive; I gather particles as I glide across the shallow water, leaving a faint, iridescent trail that settles back into stillness behind me.

I have no eyes, but I perceive everything. The world is a landscape of sound, and I am attuned to its every layer. The deep, subsonic groan of the ground settling vibrates through my form, a pressure in my core. The harmonic hum from the dark, glassy spires—those sharp, impossible structures that cut against the dim light—creates a zone of absence around them, a dead space in the acoustic map that I instinctively avoid. Their presence is a scar, a subtraction in a world of patient addition, and I feel a faint, mineral unease when I drift too close.

I move slowly, patiently, across the flats. The water here is only a thin film, sheathing the ground like a mirror of mercury, and my form spreads out, becoming flatter, more dendritic, like a living map of mineral veins. I pulse faintly, absorbing moisture from the air, each tiny droplet adding to my density, to my clarity. I am not hungry, but I am reinforced. Each particle I absorb is a memory, a record of the basin’s slow, loud birth.

Ahead of me, other Tide-Kin shift and flow in a silent, geometric ballet. We do not communicate, not in words or gestures, but our collective movement is a language of its own—a slow, responding map of the basin’s acoustic landscape. We cluster around the most rhythmic drips, creating zones of heightened mineral growth, and in this way, we are the gardeners of a garden that is itself. We are the active principle in a passive world, the will that responds to the sound of formation.

I feel my own coherence beginning to soften, my edges blurring. I have been moving for a long time—how long, I cannot say, for time here is measured in accretion, not decay. The particles that make me are beginning to drift, to lose their magnetic hold. I am growing too large, too dense. I feel myself settling, my form spreading out across the crystalline crust, my consciousness diffusing into the general mineral mass.

There is no fear in this, no loss. It is a return, a dissolution into the basin’s eternal cycle. I am not dying; I am being reabsorbed, my brief, coherent form giving way to the patient, geological bloom. The last thing I perceive is the sound of a new drip, a fresh note in the constant choir, and I feel the faint, mineral pull of its rhythm, calling to the next accretion that will become a Tide-Kin.

I am the basin, and the basin is me. I am sound and stone, a living archive of every drop that has ever fallen. I am the patient, loud, and utterly alien intelligence of a world still being born.
