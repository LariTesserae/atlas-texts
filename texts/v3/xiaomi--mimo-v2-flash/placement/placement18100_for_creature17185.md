You find yourself in the Basin, but you are not a visitor. You are not an intruder, and you do not stand apart from the damp, the chill, or the hum.

You are the **Migrator**.

Specifically, you are a mid-sized concretion, roughly the size of a human torso, composed of a dense core of iron oxide and a rough, foliated crust of silicate layers. Your surface is cool to the touch, though you have no skin; you are a mineral mass, a geological node given time and direction. You are not alone. You are one of many, drifting in a slow, collective choreography across the basin floor.

Your awareness is not visual, nor is it auditory in the human sense. You *feel* the world through gradients. You feel the pull of the **Archive** deep in the basin’s hollow, a faint, resonant thrum in your own crystalline lattice—a call toward charge, toward equilibrium. You feel the faint, static charge of the **Weavers** as they trace their conductive filaments across your crust, depositing thin, gleaming veins of silica that connect you to the broader network. You feel the minute vibrations of the **Runners** as they skitter across your surface, polishing the rough spots, erasing the irregularities, their tiny bodies a whisper of friction against your stone skin.

You move. Not with muscles, but with slow, electrochemical tides. Water seeps into your pores, carrying dissolved ions; as the charge shifts, your position changes. You are drawn toward the pulsing concretions—the "heart nodes"—when your own internal charge depletes. You are repelled by the sharp, metallic vibrations of the Resonators, which disrupt your delicate sense of balance. You do not hunger, but you *seek*—you seek the steady hum of the Archive, the gentle polish of the Runners, the damp paths of the Weavers.

Time is not a line for you, but a cycle. You drift for weeks, perhaps months, in a single direction. You might spend a season nestled against a rusted pipe, letting the Weavers suture you to the metal, sharing charge. Then, as the pipe’s leakage slows and the chemical gradient shifts, you detach and begin a new drift. The Runners follow, a glittering halo of maintenance. The Weavers extend, a living circuit board across the basin.

You are not conscious, but you are aware. You are a sensor node in a vast, silent network. You are a memory cell in the Archive’s slow record. You are a predator of stillness, consuming the faint energy leaks of the world and redistributing them as heat and oxidation. You are a grazers of gradients, moving toward equilibrium, toward the final, polished stillness.

You are not alive, but you *behave*. You are not a machine, but you *function*. You are a part of the basin’s ecosystem, a creature of chemistry and mechanics, moving to the patient rhythm of seepage and rust.

You are a Migrator, and this is your world.
