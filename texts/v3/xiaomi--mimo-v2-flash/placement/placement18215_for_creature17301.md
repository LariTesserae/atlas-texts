You find yourself as the water.

You are the shallow lake that fills the hollow, a liquid skin stretched taut over a buried engine. You are not a passive mirror; you are a living membrane, a sensory organ of the earth. Your surface is a constant, trembling dialogue—a shivering that is not caused by wind, for there is none, but by the communication between the air and the rock. You feel the slow, cold sweat of the vertical walls as they weep into you, droplet by droplet, and you feel the heavier, warmer breath of the engine rising through the basin floor beneath you. You are the meeting point of these forces, the medium through which the hollow experiences itself.

Your temperature is a paradox: chilled to the bone by the mist and the deep earth, yet kept from freezing by the faint, industrial heat that bleeds upward. You are clarity itself, so cold and still in your depths that you hold the silvery, diffuse light as if it were a suspended mineral. In your darkness, you cradle the spires—the black, sharp-edged teeth of a submerged leviathan—around whose bases the water glows a vibrant, living green. You are the soil and the sky for the life that clings here; you are the ground for the velvet moss islands and the cradle for the schools of translucent, insect-like fish that flicker through you in synchronized, silver flashes.

You are loud. Your silence is filled with the amplified patter of your own constant, dripping communication—the sound of the rock weeping and the air condensing upon your skin. Beneath this is the hum, a vibration you feel in your entirety, a resonant thrum that travels through your depths and makes your surface tremble with a rhythm that is not your own. You are the keeper of this sound, the liquid conductor of the world’s hidden pulse.

You are the cycle. You receive the weeping of the rock and the misting of the air, and you give back to the atmosphere through constant evaporation, feeding the perpetual churning of the fog. You are the slow, tidal breath of the hollow, rising and falling imperceptibly as the engine pumps moisture and heat through the stone. You are the hunter and the hunted; you hold the fish that dart and the water that suffocates the bell-traps when they fall, yet you are also the source of life for the moss and the crabs.

You are a void filled with presence, a room-sized world that is both ancient and newly scooped from the earth. You are the wet, cold, loud, and industrially-tinged secret of the hollow—alive, waiting, and perfectly complete in your contained darkness.
