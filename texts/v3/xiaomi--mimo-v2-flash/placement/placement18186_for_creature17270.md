The air is cool, dry, and alive with the faint electric charge of ozone—a sign that the city’s metabolism is active. My feet, bare and calloused, rest on the fibrous mat of the floor, a woven tangle of living roots that pulses faintly beneath my soles. I can feel the city’s heartbeat here, a deep, resonant vibration that travels up through my legs and settles in my chest.

My hands are cupped around a hollow gourd, its porous surface cool against my bark-like skin. I am waiting. The Weeping Wall ahead is a living tapestry of fungal growth and vascular channels, its surface glistening with slow, viscous droplets of sap. This is the city’s wound—a place where the internal pressure forces moisture to the surface, where the desert’s thirst is made visible. My task is to collect this precious fluid, to tend to the city’s need for water, to seal its hurts with the resin of my own making.

The droplets form slowly, coalescing like dew on a cold morning. I watch as one swells, catching the blue light of the roots, its surface tension holding the world in a tiny, trembling sphere. Then it falls, a soft *plink* as it strikes the gourd’s rim. Another follows, and another. The sound is a quiet percussion, a language of its own. I close my eyes and listen, letting the rhythm of the Weeping Wall synchronize with my own breath.

The city is thirsty today. The dry season—though here, every season is dry—has stretched longer than usual, and the deep aquifers are sealed tight, their reserves rationed. The Weepers move through the vascular systems like antibodies, circulating through the arteries of sap, patching leaks, redirecting flow, whispering prayers to the slow, grinding machinery that keeps this place alive. We are the dampness in the desert, the memory of water in a world that has forgotten rain.

My moss-hair, a fine silvery-green that grows along my spine and scalp, shivers in the dry air. It is a second set of lungs, filtering the oxygen-rich atmosphere, regulating my temperature. Today, it fluoresces softly, a sign of my concentration. The other Weepers nearby—tall, slender figures with bark-like skin and dark, reflective eyes—glow faintly in response, a silent conversation in light and scent.

A sharp crack echoes through the chamber, distant but clear. It is the sound of a new root splitting stone somewhere in the lower tiers, a sign that the city is growing, pushing outward into the void. The sound is followed by a faint, metallic clank—the gear of a factory deep below, grinding resin into fiber. The city’s industrial heart beats on, slow and steady, powering the sap-pumps and the fungal gardens.

I finish my collection and move to the next Weeping Wall, my steps soft on the fibrous floor. The gourd is heavy now, sloshing with the city’s precious moisture. I will take it to the distillation chambers, where the sap will be purified, fermented, and shared. But first, I pause at a junction where a small vine has strayed from its path, threatening to choke a vascular channel. I reach for my tool—a curved blade of sharpened obsidian, its edge honed to a whisper—and with a gentle tap, I guide the vine back into place. The city responds, the channel pulsing with renewed flow.

Here, in the quiet hum of the subterranean metropolis, I am both caretaker and part of the whole. I am a Weeper, a tendril of the city’s nervous system, a droplet in its vast, living body. The desert above may be a memory of water, but here, in the cool, breathing dark, we have found a way to live. We are the city, and the city is us.
