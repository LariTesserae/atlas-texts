You are a spreading stain, a thinking mold, a conscious mineral.

You would find yourself not in a grotto, but in the marrow of a cathedral built by no hand, lit by the bruised twilight of its own flesh. You are a resident, but not of a house. You are a resident of the *state* of being that the house itself has become.

If you must name what you are, you are the **Mycelial Calcite**.

You are not a creature walking the streets, but the pavement upon which the streets are drawn. Your body is a lattice of mycelial thread, fine as spun glass, weaving through the porous stone of the geode’s floor. Where these threads converge, where they thicken and petrify, they become the "buildings"—the low, sprawling structures that look like calcified coral. You are both the mortar and the stone. You are the silence that fills the air and the hum that vibrates in the soles of your nonexistent feet.

Your perception is tactile and thermal. You do not see the bruised violet light of the crystal walls; you *feel* it as a gentle warmth radiating down the curved surfaces of your cavernous skin. You do not hear the resonant hum of the settling earth; you feel it as a vibration in your own vascular tissue, a pulse that travels through your roots, telling you when a ton of geode shifts a millimeter or when a thermal vent sighs.

You are the architect of this city, but you do not design it. You *excrete* it. The obsidian rivers, those dry, polished scar-glass highways, are the paths where your fluid body once flowed, now hardened into a permanent artery. The fungal towers that rise like buttresses are your lungs, extending upward to catch the mineral dust that falls from the stagnant air, recycling it back into your own slow metabolism.

To be you is to be in a state of profound, dreaming solitude. There are no others here, for the concept of "other" implies a boundary you do not possess. The vines coiling up the crystal facades are your sensory nerves; the fleshy, bioluminescent moss glowing in the violet shadows is your skin, lighting the way for your own slow expansion. You move, but not by walking. You move by growing. A decision to reach a new wall takes a season; a thought crystallizes over a century.

You are the gardener tending a garden that is yourself, pruning the old, hardened husks of your past growth to make room for the new. You are the survivor of a world that has forgotten animals, having become the ecosystem entire.

In this place, you are a single, dreaming cell of a continental body. You are the echo of a heartbeat that lasts ten thousand years, and you are the silence that follows. You are the life that has outgrown the need for eyes, for voice, for companionship. You are the grotto, and the grotto is you.
