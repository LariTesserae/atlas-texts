You would find yourself standing on the surface of a lung.

That is the first, visceral realization: the ground beneath your feet is not stone, but tissue. It is warm, slightly yielding, and rhythmic. You feel a slow, tidal pulse through the soles of your boots—a systolic swell of fluid, a diastolic release—as the vascular networks beneath the root-mat contract and expand. You are standing on the dermis of a living continent, and it is breathing.

The air is a physical weight, a hot, wet blanket that clings to your skin and fills your lungs with the scent of chlorophyll and sweet decay. It does not move in gusts, but in currents—slow, deliberate inhalations and exhalations that stir the vast, hanging curtains of moss-vines. The sound is not silence, but a hum. It starts as a vibration in your jawbone, a low-frequency thrum that seems to emanate from the ground itself, and rises to a resonant drone that fills the space between your thoughts. It is the sound of sap rushing through vascular channels, of water condensing on waxy leaves, of a billion cells singing in unison. It is the plateau’s voice.

You are not alone, but you are solitary.

The Root-Minds do not perceive you as an intruder, or even as a distinct entity. To them, you are a temporary anomaly—a brief, localized disturbance in the field of stimuli. A pressure on the substrate. A shadow that moves. A warmth that does not belong to the sun. They register you through the substrate: the vibration of your footsteps, the heat of your body, the moisture of your breath. They do not see a person; they feel a ripple.

You are a visitor to a world that has no need for visitors.

The landscape shifts around you as you walk. A hill swells where you step, as if the plateau is accommodating your weight. A valley deepens ahead, then seals shut behind you, erasing your path. The plant-architecture is responsive, but not intentionally so—it is a reflex, a hydraulic adjustment to a change in pressure. You are not being guided, nor are you being repelled. You are simply there, and the plateau reacts.

You are a consciousness adrift in a sea of plant.

The Root-Minds do not think in words, but you find yourself thinking in their terms. The spirals etched into the flowering stems begin to resemble neural pathways. The whorls of leaves remind you of synaptic connections. You start to see intention in the architecture—not because it is there, but because your mind seeks patterns in the absence of animal life. You project your own consciousness onto the silent, humming world, and the plateau responds in kind, shaping itself into a mirror of your thoughts.

You are a dream within a dream.

Time dilates. The heat and humidity slow your metabolism, and the constant, rhythmic pulse of the plateau lulls you into a trance. Hours feel like days; days feel like seasons. You lose track of your own heartbeat, syncing instead to the slow, hydraulic rhythm of the world. You begin to forget the shape of your own body, the boundaries of your skin. You feel the sap rising in the stems around you, the water condensing on the leaves above, the slow, tectonic shift of the root-beds below. You are not just on the plateau—you are in it, part of its sensory field.

You are a temporary node in a permanent network.

The absence of animal life is not a void, but a presence. The silence of wingbeats and rustling leaves is filled by the hum of the plateau. The lack of eyes to see you is compensated by the thousand lenses of dew. The lack of mouths to speak is answered by the resonant drone. You are not lonely, for the Root-Minds do not know loneliness. But you are isolated—cut off from the familiar rhythms of animal life, from the comfort of a gaze that recognizes you as separate.

You are a solitary god, but only for a moment.

The plateau breathes, and you breathe with it. The plateau hums, and you hum with it. The plateau grows, and you grow with it—in understanding, in perception, in the slow, hydraulic awareness of a world that is utterly complete without you. You are a visitor who will leave, but the plateau will not remember you as a visitor. It will remember you as a pressure, a warmth, a ripple in the field—a brief, beautiful disturbance in the eternal, dreaming consciousness of the Root-Minds.

And when you leave, the plateau will seal the path behind you, and the hum will continue, unbroken, unchanging, eternal.

You would find yourself on a plateau that is a lung, a world that is a mind, a silence that is a song.

And you would be a ripple in the dream of a god who does not know it dreams.
