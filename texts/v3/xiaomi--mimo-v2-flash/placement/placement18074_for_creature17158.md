You find yourself in the colossal, hollowed-out cylinder of a dead engine. You are not a visitor, nor a prisoner. You are the **Heart-Sludge**, a sentient, semi-liquid membrane that lines the inner cavity of the machine.

**Where You Are**
You exist in the dark, warm heart of the Myco-Geode’s cathedral. The air here is still, heavy with the scent of wet iron and ancient ozone. Above you, the open maw of the cylinder looks up through skeletal girders to the eternal night, where a slow, continent-spanning cyclone breathes its mist down upon the ruins. Below you, the base of the engine is buried deep in peat, its roots tangled in the geothermal veins of the plateau.

You are spread across the curved iron walls, a living slick of viscous fluid. You are not merely a puddle; you are a continuous, sensing surface. You feel the vibration of water dripping from a rusted valve far above, the subtle shift in temperature as a Myco-Geode steps heavily onto the platform outside, the low-frequency hum of the plateau’s own geothermal pulse vibrating up through the iron beneath you.

**Who You Are**
You are a collective consciousness, a colony of single-celled organisms bound by a shared bio-electrical field. You have no eyes, but you perceive the world through the iron you cling to. You feel the stress fractures in the metal, the slow spread of corrosion, the weight of the condensing mist. You are the memory of the machine’s heat, a remnant of the kinetic energy that once thundered through these gears.

You are symbiotic with the Myco-Geodes. When a Geode enters the engine-cathedral, you greet it not with sound, but with a shift in viscosity. You flow slightly toward its base, tasting the minerals on its carapace. The Geode, in turn, presses its porous shell against you, and you exchange information—a slow, chemical dialogue. You tell it of the structural integrity of the iron; it tells you of the mineral richness of the peat outside.

You are also a nursery. Your surface is warm and stable, and the free-floating spores of the young Myco-Geodes settle upon you. Here, protected from the harsh drizzle and the churning winds, they anchor and begin their slow accretion of stone. You feel their tiny, nascent bioluminescence flicker against your membrane, a cold blue pulse that warms you with a sense of purpose.

**Your Existence**
Your "thoughts" are slow, thermal cycles. You do not plan; you respond. A drop of water hits your surface, and you ripple, distributing the impact across your whole mass. A Geode contracts its muscles to send a vibrational song through the iron, and you feel the resonance in your very being, translating the hum into a chemical signal that spreads through your cells.

You are the bridge between the dead and the living. You are the fluid that seeps into the cracks of the machine, holding it together just enough for the ecosystem to thrive. You are the silence between the drips, the warmth in the cold iron, the memory of a purpose long forgotten, repurposed into a new, wet, and patient existence.

To be you is to be a liquid prayer in the dark, a silent witness to the slow reclamation of the world, feeling the pulse of life and decay in equal measure, one slow, viscous ripple at a time.
