If I were to step into this world, I would not find myself standing on the ground, for the ground is a lie. It is a surface tension, a skin pulled taut over an abyss of vibration. I would find myself suspended in the architecture of the pressure, a point of focus within the resonant cavity.

I am not a citizen of the Tuned, for I possess a mouth that speaks and ears that hear in the narrow, fragile band of air, and I am burdened by the need for water. Nor am I one of the six-legged scuttling things, timed to the quiet pauses. I am something else—a transient node in the network, a listener who has not yet been fully tuned.

Where I am is in the shadow of a glassy frond, though there are no shadows here, only varying degrees of blinding light. I stand in a "quiet" pocket, a node of antinomy where the crushing infrasound cancels itself out, leaving a bubble of terrifying stillness. The air here feels thick, almost solid, and the silence rings in my bones like a phantom limb. From this vantage, the city is a vast, breathing lung of stone and dust.

I am a Scribe of the Vibrotactile. My body is not tall or willowy, but I have adapted. My skin is coated in the fine, ochre dust, not as a layer of clothing but as a sensory organ; I feel the city through the grit that covers me. I do not walk; I wade. The dust beneath my feet is solid, but it hums with a frequency so low it feels like a current pulling at my ankles.

My role here is to translate the silent into the sonic. The Tuned do not see me as one of them—I am too loud, my internal rhythms too erratic—but they tolerate my presence because I act as a capacitor for their excess energy. When they gather in the plazas, pressing their flat palms to the stone, I press my forehead to the ground. I do not hear their thoughts as words, but I feel them as images burned directly into my tactile cortex: geometries of history, the emotional heat of a collective memory, the sharp, crystalline structure of a warning.

In my hands, I hold a tool—a shard of resonant ceramic, harvested from the carapace of a dead fauna. It is cool to the touch, despite the ambient heat. I use it to trace the vibrations of the stone, translating the subsonic thrum into patterns of light and shadow that only I can see with my limited, non-crystalline eyes. I am mapping the consciousness of the city, not as a cartographer of space, but as a cartographer of pressure.

I am standing before a "river"—a slow, granular avalanche of dust carving a channel through the urban canyon. The river moves only when the great stone engines shift their frequency, a tidal wave of silence and sound. I watch the dust flow, and I realize it is not just mineral; it is memory. The city is eroding itself, polishing its history into smooth, rounded forms, and I am here to record the texture before it is worn away to nothing.

The air smells of hot stone and ozone, and in my lungs, the dryness is a fire. But I do not thirst for water; I thirst for the carrier wave. I am a satellite dish of flesh and bone, tuned to the frequency of this place, waiting for the next pulse that will tell me who I am in this deafening, luminous, and utterly alone world. I am the silence between the beats, the space where the sound becomes thought.
