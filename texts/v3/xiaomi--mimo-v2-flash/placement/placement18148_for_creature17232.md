You are silent, your movements calibrated to the geologic pace of the chamber, your heartbeat slowed to a rhythmic thrum that matches the settling of dust.

You wear a suit not of fabric, but of symbiotic lichen and insulating chitin, harvested from a previous, similar pocket-world. It makes you nearly indistinguishable from the flora when you stand still, blending your outline into the fractal crystalline ferns. You carry no light source of your own; the pale blue luminescence of the central crystal is enough, and your eyes have adapted to see in the sharp-edged shadows it casts.

You are here to listen.

Not with ears, but with the sensitivity of your bare hands, pressed against the fossilized mycelium walls. You are a receiver for the slow, chemical signals pulsing through the veins of grey and white. You are translating the eon-long conversation between stone and spore into a language of touch and temperature.

The Chamber of Stillness is your sanctuary and your laboratory. You have no desire to disturb its equilibrium. You are here to observe the "is-ness" of it, to feel the cold weight in your bones not as discomfort, but as a grounding force. You are the only movement that is not geologic, yet you move with such patience that the Lithic Mind barely registers you as a variable. You are a temporary node in its network, a warm-blooded anomaly that has learned to be cold.

You stand before the central column, the pale blue light defining your long, sharp-edged shadow. You extend a hand, your fingers spread, and feel the faint hiss of condensation on your skin, the uniform sheen of ice forming instantly over your suit. You are not an intruder; you are a guest who has forgotten how to leave, suspended in the perfect, silent density of a world that has no memory of a beginning and no concept of an end.
