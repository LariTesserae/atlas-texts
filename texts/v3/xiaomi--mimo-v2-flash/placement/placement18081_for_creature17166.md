You are standing on a narrow bridge of braided root, your bare feet splayed to grip the spongy, living surface. The wind does not merely blow here; it is a physical constant, a pressure against your cheek and a roar that fills your skull. It is the only sound, save for the faint, rhythmic clicking of your own breathing, the air thin and sharp in your lungs.

You are a Child of the Root, though you do not think of yourself as such. You are simply *you*, a small, compact shape in an immense, hollow world. Your skin, pale and translucent, shimmers with a faint, blue-white light, a pattern that pulses in time with the slow, deep heartbeat of the plant beneath you. Your limbs are short, your hands and feet webbed, your claws retracted as you move with a practiced, silent grace along the living pathway. The abyss yawns on either side, a depth of nothingness that swallows the faint glow of the roots below.

The cold is a structure, not a sensation. It has seeped into your bones, into the very marrow, and you do not feel it as discomfort. It is simply the state of being. Frost patterns your eyelashes and the fine hairs on your arms, glittering in the dim light. You see not in images, but in gradients—the living light of the fungal mats, the thermal signatures of the small, scuttling creatures in the crevices, the subtle shifts in the root’s glow that speak of condensation, of life, of danger.

You are moving toward a pocket of brighter light, a place where the root-system arches into a natural alcove. The air here is still, and you feel the familiar, low hum of the plant’s vibration through the soles of your feet. This is a place of gathering, a temporary home for your small clan. You carry a tool— a pruned root-hook, smooth from use— and a small pouch of woven fiber, empty save for a few dried insects.

As you approach, you see the faint, pulsing patterns of your kin, their bioluminescence a silent greeting. One of them, an elder, presses a palm to the root, and you feel the vibration through your own feet: a warning of a cold snap, a shift in the plant’s light. You respond by blinking your own pattern— a sequence of soft pulses that acknowledge the message, that speak of your safe passage.

You do not speak in words. Your language is light and touch, a conversation of gradients and vibrations. You move into the alcove, your clan parting to make space. The root here is warm, the fungal glow brighter, and you feel a faint, damp mist on your skin—condensation, precious and rare. You settle against the living wall, your back against the braided roots, and you feel the deep, slow hum of the world’s heartbeat.

The wind howls outside the alcove, a constant, high-pitched shriek that scrapes across the peaks. But here, in the hollow, there is a quiet beneath the noise. A vast, hollow silence that is the true nature of this place. You close your large, dark eyes, not to sleep, but to listen— to feel the patterns of light, the vibrations of the root, the silent language of your world.

You are a flicker of consciousness in the vast, slow mind of the plant. Your life is short, your body small, but you are part of the living whole. And as the wind howls its eternal song, you pulse with the light of the root, a testament to the resilience of life in the face of absolute cold, absolute dark, and absolute emptiness.
