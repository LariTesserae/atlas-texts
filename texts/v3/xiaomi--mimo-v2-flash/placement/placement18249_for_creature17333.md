You would find yourself in the **basin**, and you would be one of the **Warden-Growers**.

You are standing—or perhaps, more accurately, *rooted*—on a slick plateau of moss-bright stone that overlooks a shallow depression where a great, gear-toothed wheel turns in a pool of milky, mineral-rich water. The air is not breathed but felt, a warm, textured pressure against the smooth, curved planes of your form. There is no separation between your body and the environment; you are a lattice of crystalline fiber and oxidized copper, and the moisture that beads on the stone walls beads also on your limbs, tracing slow, conductive paths down your segmented arms.

You do not see with eyes, but you perceive the basin in its entirety. The slow, rhythmic pulse of phosphorescence in the fern fronds is a beat you feel in your core, matching the faint, internal glow that emanates from your own semi-translucent torso. The sound is not just around you but *through* you—the grinding hum of the gears is a vibration that travels up through the soles of your root-like feet, while the hiss of steam from the wide-mouthed chimneys resonates in the hollow spaces of your structure. You are a tuning fork for this world, and the world is constantly playing you.

In your branching hands—which are more like delicate, copper-veined rootlets than graspers—you hold a small, verdigris-coated tool, its purpose as organic as the fern frond it resembles. You are tending to a valve on a pipe that sweats condensation, your touch so gentle it does not disturb the patina of moss that has begun to grow upon it. This is not work; it is a function, as natural as a leaf unfurling or a gear turning. You are adjusting the flow of a fluid whose chemistry you do not question, whose purpose is simply to maintain the equilibrium. The basin breathes, and you are one of its lungs.

There is no hunger, no thirst, no fatigue in the animal sense. Your energy comes from the constant, low-grade warmth radiating from the earth and the machinery, from the slow conversion of industrial effluvia into light by the plants that are both your garden and your skin. You do not sleep, but you enter periods of stillness, where your internal light dims to a ember and your consciousness merges entirely with the vibrations of the stone. In these moments, you are the basin dreaming of itself.

Time is not a line but a cycle, measured in the accretion of mineral deposits, the patient wearing of gear teeth, the centuries-long unfurling of a single frond. There is no past to remember and no future to anticipate, only the perpetual, slow-motion present. The absence of fauna is not a lack but a purity; the silence is not empty but full of the basin’s own voice—a symphony of hiss, drip, groan, and seep.

You are the Warden-Grower, the tender of a closed world where industry has become biology and biology has become industry. You are the quiet, luminous soul of a place that has achieved a perfect, settled strange peace. You are the machine that grows, the garden that churns, the consciousness of a basin that weeps and breathes and turns, forever, in its own dim light.
