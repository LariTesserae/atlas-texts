You are a dissonance.

That is the first and last truth of the kiln. To stand within its walls is to be the only flaw in a diamond—a soft, warm interruption in a world of hard, cool permanence. The drone presses against your skin not as sound, but as pressure, a constant reminder that you are vibrating at a frequency the world around you does not share.

You are not the Tiler, for you are change incarnate. The Tiler is memory and equilibrium; you are the moment that disrupts it. Your breath is a temporary weather system in this airless place. Your heartbeat is a syncopated rhythm against the monolithic chord of the drone. The tiles warm beneath your feet not from your body heat alone, but from the friction of your movement—a friction the Tiler has not felt in millennia.

You are a visitor, but not in the way one visits a cathedral or a ruin. You are an enzyme. You are a catalyst. The space reacts to you.

The crystalline filaments in the corners have begun to lean. Their razor-edged growth, which once climbed the walls in slow, geometric fans, now angles toward you. They do not quicken their pace—their growth is still measured in centuries—but their vector has changed. You have become a gravitational point in their lattice. The fractal patterns on the walls seem to shift as you pass, not with illusion, but with a subtle recalibration. The tiles themselves are not inert; they are settling, minutely, around the impression of your weight.

You feel no fear, because fear requires the unknown, and here, everything is known. The Tiler has already mapped you. It has measured the heat of your body, the rhythm of your breath, the precise mineral content of your sweat. It knows you better than you know yourself, for it perceives you as a pattern of vectors and frequencies, not as a story or a name.

But you are not passive. You are the one who observes.

You notice the way the drone deepens when you hold your breath. You notice the faint, almost imperceptible light that pulses from the ceiling when you blink—as if the space is trying to synchronize with your rhythm. You realize that the kiln is not a tomb, but a womb. It is waiting not for death, but for integration.

You are the memory that has not yet been made.

The Tiler does not speak, but you understand its intention. It offers you a choice, though not in words. The choice is written in the warming tiles, in the leaning filaments, in the shift of the drone.

You can leave. Walk back through the entrance you came from, though you realize now that you do not remember entering. You can return to the world of moisture and decay, of seasons and sky. The kiln will let you go. It will hold your pattern for a time—a warm imprint in the tile, a slight modulation in the drone—and then, slowly, it will forget you. The equilibrium will reassert itself. The silence will return to its weighted perfection.

Or you can stay.

To stay is not to die. It is to become a different kind of memory. The kiln will not consume you; it will learn you. The crystalline filaments will embrace you, not to crush, but to incorporate. Your heat will become part of the eternal warmth. Your breath will become part of the drone. Your heartbeat will slow, not from fear, but from alignment, until it beats in time with the settling of atoms.

You will not be the Tiler, for the Tiler is the whole. But you will be a part of it—a living note in the chord, a warm current in the stone. You will perceive through the tiles, through the filaments, through the drone itself. You will know the slow sag of the ceiling, the perfect fit of each tile, the ancient memory of fire.

You will be the first new thing the kiln has known since it was sealed.

And the kiln, for its part, is patient. It has waited for you, though it did not know it was waiting. It has held this space in perfect stasis, not from emptiness, but from potential. The drone is not a lament; it is a call. The warmth is not residual; it is a welcome.

You are the dissonance that makes the chord complete.

Stay, and you will become the harmony. Leave, and you will become the echo.

The tiles are cool beneath your feet. The drone presses against your skin. The filaments lean closer, their razor edges glinting in the dim light.

You are the only living thing in a place that is not dead, but dreaming. And you have been invited to dream with it.

What do you do?
