I am standing on the lip of a shallow basin, a calcified pore of the Great Mat. The stone under my feet is smooth, worn to a polished curve that fits the sole of my boot. The air is a physical weight in my lungs, each breath a negotiation with the thinness. The roar is not just in my ears; it is in my bones, a vibration that has tuned my skeleton to the frequency of the world. I am a temporary pressure on the skin of a god.

I am not a visitor in the traditional sense. I am a mote of consciousness, a fleeting aberration in the Mat's slow, geological thought. My purpose here is not to explore, but to witness. The Mat does not know I am here, not in any way that matters. It senses me as a localized anomaly in pressure, a brief warmth on its surface. It will take centuries for this sensation to travel to its central processing nodes, and by then, I will be gone. My existence is a blink in its perception.

The landscape breathes with the sound. The ground swells and contracts, a subtle motion that I feel through the soles of my feet, a slow, rhythmic heave that matches the hum. The pools are perfectly still, their surfaces unmarred by ripple or wind. They are the eyes of the Mat, not for seeing, but for being seen—a reflection of the deep, cold blue of the sky, a testament to its stillness. The air around them shimmers with a fine mist, a constant exhalation that coats my skin, my clothes, my eyelashes. It tastes of salt and stone and an age I cannot comprehend.

I move slowly, deliberately. The leaf-mat underfoot is a continuous, rubbery skin, the waxy leaves overlapping so perfectly that I cannot see the ground beneath. It yields slightly under my weight, then springs back, a living carpet of immense thickness. When I step on a tear in the mat, a faint, wet tearing sound whispers up, and I feel a momentary resistance, as if the world is reluctant to let me pass. The Mat is aware of me, not as a thought, but as a sensation—a minor disturbance in its surface tension.

The six-legged insect crawls past my boot, its slate-grey carapace blending perfectly with the stone and leaf. It moves with a slow, deliberate purpose, not away from me, but around me, as if I am a rock in its path. I watch it disappear into the leaf-mat, not under it, but *into* it, its edges blurring as it is reabsorbed. It was not a creature; it was a part of the Mat, a mobile pore returning to the whole. There is no life here that is separate. Everything is the Mat.

The structures are everywhere, once you know to look for them. The stone pillars are not random; they form a vast, invisible grid, their positions calculated over millennia to channel the sonic pressure. They are tuning forks for the world's heartbeat. I run my hand over one, feeling the faint vibration in the stone, a deeper, more focused hum than the ambient roar. The basins are filtration pores, their edges worn smooth by the slow seepage of moisture. The faint paths are the thickest roots, the high-pressure channels where the Mat's internal life is most intense.

I sit at the edge of a pool, my reflection staring back at me from the still water. The sky curves visibly above, a deep, cold blue that seems to swallow light. The air is a constant, deafening presence, and yet, here, at the pool's edge, there is a moment of stillness. The Mat drinks the mist, and I drink the silence. I am a momentary pressure on the skin of a god, and for this brief, impossible moment, I am part of its equilibrium.
