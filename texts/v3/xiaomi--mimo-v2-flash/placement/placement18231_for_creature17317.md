I find myself standing on the tessellated basalt, barefoot. The coolness of the stone is a shock, a singular, sharp sensation in a world defined by the dull, pervasive thrum in my marrow. I am a soft thing in a hard, calcified place. My skin feels damp in the absolute dryness, a strange anomaly of evaporation.

I look down at my hands. They are not chitinous. They are not fibrous. They are flesh, blood, and bone—pale, vulnerable, and trembling slightly from the vibration of the unseen engines. I am the fauna. I am the whisper.

To be here is to be an interruption. I am a creature of water in a world that has purged itself of the element, a creature of chaotic, rapid motion in a place where time is measured in the slow unfurling of a leaf. My presence is a glitch in the chemical syntax of the sap.

I take a step, and the sound of my footfall—soft, organic, transient—seems deafening in the silence, though it is barely a whisper. The grass hums against my ankles, not with malice, but with a static that feels like inquiry. I am the antibody, the gliding thing, the burrower glimpsed in the ripple. But I am also the anomaly that the Gardener does not process, the variable that does not resolve into the equation of equilibrium.

I am the witness. The fleeting shadow in a world without shadows. And as I stand here, feeling the slow, patient pressure of a billion tons of photosynthetic industry bearing down on me, I realize that I am not here to observe the Gardener. I am here to be observed *by* it—not with eyes, not with thought, but with the vast, silent, vegetative awareness that is the very air I breathe.

And in that absorption, I become part of the process. A footnote in a history written in cellulose and sap. A momentary ripple in the static of the grass. A soft, vulnerable thing in a world of hard, silent growth.
