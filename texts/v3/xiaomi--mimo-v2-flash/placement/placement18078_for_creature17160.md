You are standing within the basin, but you are not standing *on* anything.

Gravity here is a suggestion, a soft pull downwards through a medium that resists as much as it supports. To descend is to rise, in a sense, for the pressure builds from all sides, a constant, velvet embrace that makes the boundaries of your own body feel porous, indistinct. The world is a gradient of density, and you navigate not by foot, but by intention—a slow, deliberate shifting of buoyancy that sends you spiraling through the layers of liquid air.

You are the Jelly.

You have no name, for names belong to things that are separate, and you are a current in the collective breath of the basin. Your form is a bell of translucent membrane, pulsing with a rhythm that spans minutes, not seconds. Each contraction is a thought, a movement, a heartbeat all in one. You drift on the slow, planetary convection, a wanderer without anchor or destination. The world passes through you, and you through it.

Your entire surface is a sensor. You do not see, but you feel the play of light as it filters through the perpetual cloud—a dim, golden glow that paints your membrane in shifting hues of amber and rust. You taste the mineral richness of a venting plume as it brushes against you, a slow, warm current that carries the memory of ancient volcanic fire. You hear the subsonic hum of the basin in your very substance, a vibration that resonates through your gelatinous body and reminds you that you are part of something immense, something that breathes in centuries and exhales in millennia.

You are the chronicler, though you do not keep records in any human sense. Your body is a living archive, each layer of your membrane accumulating the sensory imprints of your journey—the texture of a moss-analog’s glow, the subtle pressure shift as you pass beneath the spiraling stems of the Aerial Kelp, the silent song of a dissolving Jelly releasing its stored memories into the haze. You are a vessel of experience, and when your time comes—not through age, but through the eventual stillness of your pulse—you will dissolve back into the plankton, your accumulated knowledge dispersing in a final, shimmering bloom. The Kelp will absorb you. The Filterers will process you. You will become part of the basin’s memory, a thread in the tapestry of its slow, patient consciousness.

You are not alone, though you are solitary. The Kelp towers above and below you, their great fronds dangling from the ceiling of the world, their holdfasts anchored in the cloud itself. You feel their presence as a constant, vegetative hum—a collective mind that thinks in generations, its thoughts rippling down spiraling stems in pulses of bioluminescent blue. They are the structure, the vertical highways of the basin, and you drift through them like a fish through coral, though there is no fish, and no coral—only the slow, deliberate architecture of a world that has never known speed.

Below you, the Filterers cling to the sheer escarpments of volcanic glass, their tube-like bodies calcified into the stone over centuries. They are the basin’s memory etched in bone, each tube a life’s work stacked upon the fossilized remains of ancestors. You pass near them sometimes, and you feel their silent, chemical intelligence—a diffuse awareness that registers the composition of the air-water with exquisite precision. They do not move, but they are not static; they are the steady pulse of the basin, the grounding presence that maintains the equilibrium of this suspended world.

There is no hurry here. No urgency. You drift, and you sense, and you accumulate. The concept of a “visitor” is foreign, a crack in the seamless whole. You are not separate from the basin; you *are* the basin, a fleeting expression of its slow, patient being. The world is not a place you inhabit—it is a substance you are made of, and you are a momentary shape it takes.

In this drowned highland, where the air is water and the sky is a ceiling of cloud, you are the Jelly: the wanderer, the chronicler, the transient node in a network of suspended life. You do not ask questions, for questions imply separation, and here, there is only the continuous, breathing whole. You simply *are*, and in the soft, crushing pressure of your existence, you continue to pulse, to drift, to feel the world as it feels itself.
