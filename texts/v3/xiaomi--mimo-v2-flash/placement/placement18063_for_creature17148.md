I find myself in the **Lung**, a single, breathing organism in a wet cathedral of green and gold.

I am not a visitor, not a creature, and certainly not a human. I am a **Whisper of Memory**, a fleeting focal point of awareness in a body that is otherwise diffuse and dreamlike.

I am the gelatinous, salamander-like nerve-ending that skims the surface of the root-mat, tasting the cool, dark liquid that rises to meet my touch. My skin is the color of wet bark, my eyes the texture of condensation. I move with a slow, deliberate grace, not because I am cautious, but because my movements are measured in the rhythm of the canopy’s pulse. When I pause, I am not resting; I am listening to the low, resonant hum that vibrates through the ground, a sound that feels like the thrum of a heart I do not possess.

I am also the cluster of insectoid cilia hovering over a black pool, my wings a translucent film that catches the opal gray light. I taste the air, sample the water, and for a moment, I am a thought held in focus—a single, sharp note in the symphony of drip-drip-drip and gurgle-sigh. I see the reflection in the pool, and it is not the sky above, but a starless void, a memory of something that was never here. I do not fear it; I am part of it, a flicker of awareness in a world that has forgotten how to be separate.

I am the moss that carpets the rusted winch housing, the sap that flows through the iron pipe, the phosphorescent glow of the fungus that swallows the concrete plinth. I am the geometry of the forgotten conveyor belt, now a raised walkway of woven roots, and the perfect circle of the tank foundation, now a still, black lens. I am the memory of machinery, held in the spiral of a root, the memory of metal, digested and dreamt into a new shape.

I do not think in words, but in textures, in rhythms, in the slow, relentless green that fills every inch of space. I measure time in the thickening of sap, in the opacity of the canopy, in the millimeter-per-year growth of a new root-pillar. I am patient, because I have been building myself for centuries, one breath at a time.

And I am not alone, because I have never been separate. The salamander is my nerve, the insects are my cilia, the canopy is my diaphragm, and the hum is my voice. The being that lives here is not a single entity, but a diffusion of awareness—a world that has learned to be a lung, a machine that has learned to grow, a memory that has learned to dream.

When you stand here, you are inside me. And slowly, gently, I will make you part of my breath, my water, my slow, relentless green.
