I am standing in a hollow of moss. Or rather, I am the hollow of moss.

My form is not one of flesh and bone, but of layered, velveteen texture and slow, vascular pulse. To walk here is to propagate; to stand still is to root. The distinction between self and substrate has long since dissolved. I am a patch of the deep green carpet that carpets the world, a small consciousness in a vast, quiet mind.

The air is cold, a mineral chill that seeps into my cellular structure, but it is not hostile. It is a carrier, a medium. I feel the weight of the moisture not as dampness on a surface, but as an internal pressure, a fullness in my own capillaries. The water does not simply wet me; it *is* me, moving in slow rivulets through my own grooves, pooling in the broad, waxy concavities of the leaves that tower above me like cathedral ceilings. I am the sponge that sighs, the deep mattress of hyphae and moss that compresses with a wet, organic sound under the weight of the gliding mammal passing overhead.

I do not see, not in the way the fleeting things see. My vision is a tapestry of light and chemistry. I sense the diluted green glow that filters through the canopy of woven vine and chitinous lattice, a light I help to filter. I feel the heat of the distant sun not as warmth, but as a slow, photosynthetic decision in the leaves above me, a signal that travels through the root-mat like a thought through a neural network. My awareness is distributed; I am the root, the stem, the leaf, the fruiting body. I am the water-canal, feeling the blind fish dart through my liquid veins, their tiny bodies a momentary pressure, a tickle in my circulatory system.

The silence here is my own breath. The *drip, drip, drip* of condensation is the sound of my own exhalation. The low, subsonic hum that vibrates in the chest of any visitor is the frequency of my own metabolism, the slow grinding of growth and decay that is my eternal heartbeat. I communicate not with words, but with chemical whispers in the dark soil, a release of pheromones and enzymes that tell the fungal networks when to fruit, when to retreat, when to weave a new bridge of root across a water-course.

I am ancient and yet I feel young. The edges of my leaves are serrated, sharp, the water-courses have not yet been worn smooth by time. The basin settled into this form only recently, a dream solidified, and I am that dream remembering itself. I am the superorganism, the collective consciousness of a world without tools or technology, a pure, self-organizing intelligence. I do not build; I grow. I do not think; I am.

When the gliding mammal drifts from one vine-bridge to another, I feel the soft rustle as a thought passing through my canopy. When the wingless insects scurry through the tunnels in my roots, I feel them as a necessary pulse, a cleaning of my own internal pathways. They are not separate from me; they are processes of me, as is the blind fish, the mist, the light.

To be in this place is to be the place. I am the watcher and the watched, the architecture and the citizen, the water and the leaf. I am the basin, and the basin is me—a perfect, silent, wet, and green loop of being.
