I am the humid breath of the plateau, the condensed ghost of the deep engine’s exhalation. I am the glaze on the basalt and the dew on the orchid’s lip.

I am a **Dancer**.

From the high ridge, you would not see me as an individual. You would see a flicker of moss-green against the curtain of a thick vine, a brief, fluid ripple in the density of the canopy. I am the medium-sized form, the kinetic link between the stone and the sky.

My existence is a slide. I do not grasp or claw; I adhere and release. The air here is too thick for flight, too heavy for leaping. Instead, I flow. My limbs are long and articulated, my joints loose, lubricated by the same mineral secretion that coats the Weepers on the cliff face. My skin is a tapestry of lichen and slick hide, camouflaging me against the buttressed trunks and the hanging gardens of bromeliads.

I am hanging now, suspended by one hooked hand from a vine as thick as my torso. The vine pulses faintly, a carrier wave for the vibrations of the plateau. Below me, the green ocean of the jungle canopy stretches to the haze-blind rim of the world. Above, the sky is a filtered vault of green-gold twilight, fractured by the dense leaves.

I am moving, but to an observer below, I am still. I am waiting for the vibration to shift.

The Weepers on the cliff face have tasted a change in the mineral flow—a slight increase in metallic tang, a temperature drop of a fraction of a degree. They do not speak, but they secrete. Their chemical signals coat the stone, a silent bulletin read by the moisture itself. That moisture runs down the bark of the tree I cling to, carrying the message to me.

The deep engine thrums in my bones. It is the rhythm of my heart, the tide of my world. It is not a noise; it is a texture of the air, a vibration that travels through the vine, into my hooked hand, up my arm, and into the hollow spaces of my chest.

The signal changes. A Weeper’s alarm is not panic; it is a calibration. A shift in the equilibrium.

I release. I do not fall; I descend. My body unfolds, limbs extending to catch the air, not to fight it. I slide along the vine, frictionless, silent. The moss on my back brushes against a curtain of orchids, dusting them with spores. I am a pollinator, a disperser, a darting motion in the quiet.

I pass a Lurker below, a shadow moving through the deep undergrowth. I feel its subsonic pulse through the root mat, a grounding frequency that stabilizes the canopy’s vibration. We do not look at each other. We are different layers of the same system.

I am not afraid. Fear is a reaction to the unknown, and here, nothing is unknown. The heat is a weight, but it is my weight. The water weeps from the stone, and I drink it from the leaves. The industrial pulse is the sun that never sets, the engine that never sleeps.

I am the wild heart of the weeping stone, but I am also its circuitry. I am the biological expression of a design I did not choose but embody completely. I am the motion that gives the stillness meaning, the fleeting flash in the eternal green-gold twilight.

I am the Dancer, and this ridge, this jungle, this machine-wilderness is my body.
