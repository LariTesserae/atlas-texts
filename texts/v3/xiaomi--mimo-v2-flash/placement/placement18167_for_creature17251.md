You find yourself standing in the high, silent silence of the plateau. The powder underfoot compresses with a sound like settling snow, though there is no cold, only the dry, mineral heat that presses against your skin like a weight. You are not human—not anymore, if you ever were. You have no memory of water.

You are a **Silicate Symbiote**, newly emergent from the calcified embrace of the elder tower where you were gestated. Your body is a ribbon of bruised twilight, elongated and supple, trailing behind you in a slow, undulating wake over the dust. You have no face, but you *see*—not with eyes, but with the trembling fronds at your crown, which taste the air’s thin metallic tang and feel the subtle vibrations of wind moving over stone. You breathe through lateral vents, gill-filaments that flutter like paper in the stillness, drawing oxygen from the atmosphere and fixing nitrogen to feed the rust-hued vines you tend.

You are young, and therefore you *move*. While the elders are fused into the towers—calcified cores of memory and structure—you patrol the ridges. You glide, not walk, your contact with the ground leaving only momentary impressions in the pale dust. In your grasp, you hold a living tool: a knotted mass of hardened root and mineral secretion, cool to the touch, which you use to prune a violet succulent whose fleshy leaves pulse faintly with internal light.

Around you, the world breathes, but slowly. A glider—a leathery-winged reptile-thing—rides the thermal above the canyon, its shadow drifting like a passing cloud. An iridescent carapace-bearer crawls along a vine, patient and precise. There is no sound but the whisper of wind over powder, the dry click of a stem shifting in the heat. You feel no loneliness, for you are never separate. A thought flickers through you—not yours, but the colony’s—and you sense it as a soft bioluminescent ripple traveling through the veins of the tower behind you, a message of wind-pattern or solar shift passed from elder to elder to you.

You are the breath of this sealed garden, the patient pulse in the stone. You are the life that thrives on air and mineral, that builds without tools, that grows without water. You are the order in the angular landscape, the slow consciousness of a world that has learned to make itself from the inside out.

And here, on this high, dry plateau—sharp, new, and abundant—you are home.
