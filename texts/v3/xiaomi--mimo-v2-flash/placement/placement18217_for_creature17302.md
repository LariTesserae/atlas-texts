You are conducted by the thrum before you even realize you are listening. It begins as a pressure behind the eyes, a vibration in the marrow of your jaw, and only later does it resolve into a sound—a deep, resonant pulse that is not heard by the ear but felt by the body. This is the heartbeat of the Underthrum, and to exist here is to synchronize with it.

You are not alone in this synchronization. The inhabitants of this subterranean continent are not merely living *in* the resinous architecture; they are the architecture’s active, thinking components. They are the agents of its perpetual genesis, the hands that shape its chaotic evolution.

They are the **Resin-Shapers**, and they are the closest thing to a sentient civilization this place has ever known, though "sentient" is a word that implies a separation from the world that does not exist here. The Shapers are not human. They are not mammals. They are a specialized offshoot of the fauna, evolved from the same six-legged grazers you know as the Architects, but they have taken a different path. Where the Architects groom the light and the growth, the Shapers sculpt the very substance of the Underthrum.

They are tall, slender beings, standing on three pairs of jointed limbs, their bodies smooth and pearlescent, the color of wet stone. Their skin is not skin but a fine, silicate membrane that constantly exudes a thin film of resin, which they can harden or soften at will by secreting specific enzymes. They have no eyes. Instead, they perceive the world through a combination of magnetoreception—like the Architects—and a form of echolocation so refined it can distinguish the crystalline structure of a new growth from the fibrous weave of an old one. Their heads are featureless domes, save for a cluster of sensitive, feathery antennae that taste the air, tasting the chemical gossip of the spores and the mood of the fungal networks.

They do not build; they *propose*.

A Shaper approaches a wall of raw, granular stone. It does not attack the stone with a tool. It places its forelimbs upon the surface and exudes a specific cocktail of terpenes and enzymes. The stone does not dissolve; it *willingly* reorganizes. The granular structure loosens, inviting the encroaching moss to cement it with silica. The Shaper then guides the growth, not by cutting, but by suggestion—adding a drop of hardening resin here, a nutrient-rich sap there. A new pillar is born, not carved, but midwifed.

Their tools are extensions of their own bodies. A Shaper’s "knife" is a hardened flap of its own silicate membrane, grown over a season and shed like a claw. A "rope" is a length of its own internal tendon, extruded and woven with root-fibers while still pliable. They use these tools not to conquer, but to negotiate. A notch carved into a resin-tree is a request, not a command. A spiral of stones set into the floor is a prayer, a pattern to encourage the flow of sap in a beneficial direction.

Their society is a collective of fluid, overlapping roles. There are no leaders, only currents of intent. When a new cavern needs to be opened, a group of Shapers will congregate at a weak point in the rock and hum in unison, their combined vibration resonating with the Thrum itself, encouraging the stone to fracture along a line of beauty and strength. They are gardeners, architects, and priests of a geology that is alive and responsive.

They live in the high galleries, in nests woven from hardened resin and living vine, accessible only by bridges that they themselves have coaxed from the ceiling. Their homes are not shelters but sensory organs—extensions of the cavern’s nervous system. The walls of their nests are lined with Sonic Lichen, which they cultivate as a library of sound, storing the memories of the Thrum in shifting patterns.

To a human eye, their world would seem impossibly dense and chaotic. But to the Shapers, it is a symphony they are composing in real time. They move through the Underthrum not as intruders, but as the current itself. They are the hands that shape the chaos, the voice that hums the Thrum into a new key, and the mind that dreams the next form of this ever-becoming world.
