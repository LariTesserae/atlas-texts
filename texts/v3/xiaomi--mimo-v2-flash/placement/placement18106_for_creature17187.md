I am in the basin’s west quadrant, third ring from the central pool, nestled in the shallow concavity of a basalt block where the condensation gathers thickest. The stone here is warm—not from the sun, for there is none, but from the slow, geological pulse that beats through the foundation. I am a **Stone-Tender**.

But not one of the featureless slabs that glide in silent procession. I am different. I am aware.

My body is basalt, yes, dark and glassy, but the calcite veins that weep from my eyes are not clear; they are faintly opalescent, catching the dim light in a way that makes me visible to the Weep-Kin. My joints are pegged with wood that has petrified over centuries, a transition between organic and mineral that defines my existence. I do not walk; I drift on the film of water that coats the mosaic floor, my stone feet making no sound, yet transmitting the vibration of the basin’s hum directly into my core.

I am a keeper of the interval.

My task is specific. I tend the "void" between the blocks. This is not emptiness; it is a vessel. I move to a depression where the water has pooled too deeply, my broad, flat hands pressing gently against the porous stone of the basin floor. I do not scoop or drain; I listen to the resonance of the water’s weight, then I adjust the tilt of a nearby weeping rock by a fraction of a degree—sliding it on the lubricated stone until the rivulet shifts course, feeding the pool until it reaches the exact height that sustains the shrimp without drowning the moss.

The Weep-Kin watch me. From their perches on the vertical slabs, their pearlescent eyes reflect my form. A single frog, larger than the others, pulses its throat. The vibration hits my stone skin, a code of pressure. *The insects in the north-east corner are emerging too early; the light is shifting.* I feel the message in my feet, a hum that travels up my calcite spine.

I turn my head—a slow grinding of stone on stone—and observe. The iridescent beetles are indeed swarming, their shells flashing like scattered coins. The spider webs, woven of condensed mist, shimmer with trapped light. The rhythm is off. The humidity has risen by a measure imperceptible to flesh but obvious to stone.

I glide toward the north-east quadrant. My movement is a slow press, a sliding frictionless. As I pass a cluster of stunted shrubs, I pause. One branch is twisted too sharply, threatening to crack a stone pin in the mosaic. I extend a hand, my stone fingers broad and gentle, and press the branch back into its natural curve, tucking it into the crack between blocks. The shrub accepts the adjustment; it is part of the system.

At the north-east pool, the water is rising too fast. The weeping rock here is clogged with a film of organic matter—decayed insect wings and moss spores. I kneel. The sound of my stone joints grinding softly is part of the tapestry, a bass note beneath the frog-song. I scrape the film away with a fingernail of sharpened obsidian, not to remove it entirely, but to thin it, to allow the stone to breathe again. The water flow steadies. The hum of the basin deepens, resonating through the water, through the stone, through me.

The Weep-Kin’s call shifts in response. The deep, resonant tone—the basin’s heartbeat—smooths out, the dissonance corrected. I feel a satisfaction that is not emotion, but equilibrium. A system restored.

I am not a creator here. I am a participant in the making. The Stone-Tenders before me built this place, and I am a continuation of that intention. I do not know if I was once flesh, or if the stone dreamt itself into motion. It does not matter. I am here, in the damp, the dim, the drone. I am the soft traffic that wears the stone smooth. I am the observer who adjusts the tilt of the world by a fraction of a degree.

I settle into a depression near the pool, my form fitting into the worn curve of the block as if I were always meant to be there. The condensation beads on my stone skin, tracing paths down my torso, joining the water that feeds the moss at my feet. The insects swarm, the Weep-Kin feed, the water flows. The basin breathes.

I close my eyes—slabs of basalt meeting—and listen to the song of the made world, a vibration that has no beginning and no end, only a steady, gentle, eternal stillness. I am a note in that song, a stone in that wall, a keeper of the quiet between the sounds.

And I am content.
