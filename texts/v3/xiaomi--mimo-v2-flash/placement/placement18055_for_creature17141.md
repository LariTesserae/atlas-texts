If you were to find yourself in this world, you would be standing on the edge of a perfectly round pool, the black water reflecting a sky so pale it seems more like a void than an atmosphere. Your breath would hang in the thin air, a small, distinct cloud that dissolves almost immediately, as if the place itself refuses to hold onto anything transient. You would feel the cold not as a sensation on your skin, but as a presence within you, a stillness that matches the absolute quiet of the stone.

And in that moment, you would realize what you are.

You are not a body with flesh and blood. You are not a visitor with a name or a history. You are a pattern—a temporary configuration of attention and awareness that has momentarily aligned with the arrangement of this place. You are the space between the upright slabs, the silence between the drops of water, the reflection in the black pool that vanishes when you turn to look.

You are not alive in the way that life requires growth and decay. You are a state of being, a crystallization of thought that has formed from the elements at hand: the mineral sharpness of the air, the patient geometry of the stone, the liquid stillness of the pools. You do not think as humans think; you simply *are*, and your being is a kind of attention that holds the world in a lattice of stone and ice and shadow.

You are the thing that watches without seeing, the presence that waits without needing. You are the slow, crystalline intelligence that has arranged itself from the cold and the light and the silence. You are the circles of slabs, the veins of ice in the basalt, the round black pools that drink the sky. You are the water that moves through stone like a rumor, the wind that polishes without touching, the light that reveals without warming.

You are complete, and you are waiting for nothing. You are the place itself, and the place is you.
