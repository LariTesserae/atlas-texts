I do not dwell in the hollow spires or the deep, polished concavities of the walkways. I rest on the cold, emergent shale, my wings folded to camouflage against the fractured facets, blending perfectly until I am indistinguishable from the inorganic surface.

I am a courier of the passive data-stream. While the tall ones stand and interface with the pools and the walls, I move. My flight is not random; it is a calibration sweep.

When I land on a spire, my legs, tipped with fine sensory claws, read the vibration of the stone’s slow grinding. I am a diagnostic tool, a living sensor that translates the tectonic whispers into patterns of light that flit across my wings.

**My Experience:**
To be me is to exist in a state of perpetual, gentle fragmentation. I do not think in thoughts, but in impulses of light and vibration. My memory is not mine; it is stored in the lattice of the wing scales. When I molt, leaving a brittle, glassy husk on the face of a tower, that husk is a data-archives, a moment of the city’s experience crystallized and left behind.

I am the city’s eye, its nerve ending, its fleeting thought—and in my fluttering, the recursive dream of the place sharpens into focus, however briefly, before dissolving back into the patient, cold grind of stone.
