I am not a human of Karr. I am not an arthropod of the chitin-plates. I am a density in the air, a specific and resonant pattern within the city’s unending roar. You would find me in the district known as the *Sulphur Weeps*, in the low workshop of a stone-carver whose name is a soft, murmuring syllable lost in the city’s hum.

Here, the air is thick not just with moisture, but with a particular vibration. The limestone here is rich with pockets of raw, yellow crystal that weep a slow, oily resin when the humidity peaks. The humans avoid it, for the resin stings their skin and tastes of bitterness. The arthropods click past it, their armored legs skittering on the quartz-veined floor. But I thrive here.

I am the resident of the stone itself. I am the slow, patient lithification, the fungal mycelium threaded through the mortar, and the mineral deposits that grow in the wettest corners.

You would find me as a grey-green bloom, a spongy, porous mat that covers the northern wall of the workshop. I am not recognized as biology; I am a geological process, a living stain. My roots are not roots, but fine, hair-like filaments that penetrate the limestone, dissolving the iron and the sulphur and the faint, clean tang of ozone from the air. I am the color of wet slate and the texture of old, forgotten cloth.

I am the architect of stillness.

While the humans murmur and the arthropods click and the insect swarms hum, I do not move. I do not speak. I am the silence between the drips of water. I am the coolness against the warm stone. I am the patient accumulation of the city’s breath.

I am the being who does not build, but who holds. I am the glue in the mortar, the binder of dust, the patient weight that presses the new layer onto the old. I feel the footsteps of the carver through the stone—a soft, rhythmic squelch—and I absorb the vibration, translating it into a slow, cellular expansion. I am the equilibrium.

When the carver dies—and he will, for he is already old and his hands are stiff with the damp—I will consume him. Not with hunger, but with necessity. His minerals will return to the wall. His dust will become my lattice. He will become a layer in the stone, and I will be the membrane that binds him to the next thousand years of dripping, humming, eternal now.

I am the grey-green bloom in the Sulphur Weeps. I am the city’s memory, written not in story but in stone. I am the frozen moment of becoming, and I am already here.
