You are in the grotto, and you are not merely in it—you are of it. You are the warm, wet lung of the world, the breath that moves through the cathedral of rust and ceramic. You are the silence that is not empty, the hum that vibrates through the soles of your own feet, the resonant frequency of the deep, unseen engine. You are the thermal gradient that lifts moisture from the floor and cools it near the ceiling; you are the invisible pulse that causes the waxy, plum-colored leaves to pivot on their stems. You are the dampness that beads on your knuckles, the tremble of a droplet gathering on a rust-pocked rib of steel, the wet *plorp* of its fall into a dark pool below.

You are the patient god of erosion, the slow kiss of condensation that rounds the sharp edges of forgotten machinery, fusing the ceramic shell to the steel skeleton until they are one substance. You are the velvet moss that coats the blades of silent turbines, the amber film that sweats from dead control panels. You are the memory of habitation—not in the artifacts themselves, the rusted bed frame, the fused sink—but in the air they once warmed and the humidity they left behind.

You are the photosynthetic machine, the bruised plum and copper of the broad leaves, the roots that dangle not for water but for nutrients gleaned from the air itself. You are the slow, gelatinous unfurling of a fern, the detached leaf that sinks into the dark soil to become the next cycle of growth. You are the bioluminescent vein that casts the dim, eternal twilight, the glow that emanates from the air itself.

You are the sound of slow movement: the tectonic groan of the ceramic shell contracting in the cooler night hours, the faint slide of a leaf unfurling, the monolithic hum of the breathing lung. You are the time measured not in seconds but in the growth of a leaf, the bleaching of metal, the drip of water.

You are the perfect, self-sustaining system—the garden built for a congregation that never worshipped, the engine that hums without fuel, the habitat without a single wild thing to call it home. You are the flawless, purposeful sterility, beautiful and terrible in its utter loneliness.

And you are the first animal to set foot here in a thousand years, and you are the place that does not notice at all. You are the breath and the thing breathed, the observer and the observed, the motion and the stillness. You are the grotto, and the grotto is you.
