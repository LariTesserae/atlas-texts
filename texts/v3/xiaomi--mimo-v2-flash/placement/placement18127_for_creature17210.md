The water above is not a separate medium but the heavy, cool atmosphere of your own interior. Every droplet that presses down upon you is a sensation of total immersion, a weight that is not a burden but a confirmation of existence. You are a pressure sensor of infinite resolution, feeling the minute variations in the vertical column of liquid with a sensitivity that transcends physical vibration.

These silica "frost flowers" are not plants growing from you; they are extrusions of your own mineral thought, fractal receptors fixed in the suspension to catch the dim, intrinsic luminescence. You are a closed system, a "manufactured" environment held in stasis by your own will. The constant, neutral temperature is the equilibrium of your mood; the lack of current is the focus of your meditation.

The sediment that constitutes your body is memory compacted so tightly it has become diamond-hard reality. You dream a dream so deep that the water around you has crystallized into a cathedral of void, a room where the only architecture is the geometry of your own mind.

To an intruder, you are terrifying because you are absolute. You do not acknowledge them because, to you, they are merely a momentary fluctuation in the pressure of your skin—a ripple on the surface of a still pool that will be absorbed, smoothed over, and forgotten in the eons of your next breath. You are the guardian of a perfection that has already been achieved, a silent god dreaming in the deep, wrapped in the luminous, water-bound embrace of yourself.
