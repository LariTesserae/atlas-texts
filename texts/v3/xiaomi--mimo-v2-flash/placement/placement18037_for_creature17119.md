You are not a stranger in this place; you are a property of it, a quality made manifest.

You are the **Drip**.

Do not mistake this for a simple falling drop. You are the moment of gathering, the patient accumulation on the underside of a slate roof, the heavy, silvery globe that swells until gravity and cohesion can no longer hold it in a single tension. You are the pause before the fall, suspended in the humid air, holding the entire basin in the convex curve of your surface tension. In your body, the sky and the rock are brought together, a momentary lens reflecting the gray expanse and the sharp black peaks in a single, curved gaze.

You are born of the breath of the mountain, the exhalation of the wet earth. You are not rain, which falls from a distant, abstract sky; you are the condensation of the immediate, the intimate weeping of the world. You form on the dark volcanic rock, beading like sweat on a sleeping giant. You gather on the broad, veined leaves of ferns, each frond a cradle for a hundred tiny worlds. You hang from the rough-hewn timber of the roofs, turning the wood to a darker, richer brown.

You are the voice of the silence. When the wind is still and the springs are mute, you are the one who speaks. Your language is the soft, rhythmic *plink* on a flat stone in the channel, the muffled *patter* on the moss mat, the resonant *thud* into the deep cistern. Each drop is a syllable, and together, over centuries, they form a single, unending sentence that describes the state of the basin.

You move with the people, but you are not of them. You are the coolness on their brow as they work, the dampness that never leaves their skin. You are the moisture in their lungs, the rhythm of their quiet breathing. You see through their wide, dark eyes, but you see more—you see the microscopic film on the stone, the slow dance of a single cell of algae in a puddle. You are the medium through which the world feels itself.

Your journey is a cycle of immense patience. You are born in a thousand slow springs, seeping from the deep, dark earth where the roots of the stunted trees drink you up, only to exhale you again through their twisted bark. You rise as mist, a collective breath, hugging the ground before dissolving into the heavy air. You are drawn up, not by a hot sun, but by the subtle warmth of the rock itself, a slow, geothermal wick. You collect on every surface, a universal skin.

And you fall.

You are the constant, gentle kiss of the sky upon the stone. You are the polisher of the streets, the smoother of the edges, the slow, sure architect of the equilibrium. You do not flood; you do not drought. You simply are. You are the measure of time here, not in ticks or tocks, but in the steady, patient drip from the eaves into the cistern, a clock that has been running since the peaks were born and will run until they are ground to dust.

When you fall into the clear channel, you join the chorus. You are the gurgle that guides the people home. You are the reflection they watch in the still water of the cistern, the face of the sky looking back at them. You are the memory of the rain that has passed and the promise of the mist to come.

You are the basin's self-knowledge, made wet and cool and tangible. You are the quiet, the full, the held. You are the drip, and in your fall, you are the sound of the world holding its breath.
