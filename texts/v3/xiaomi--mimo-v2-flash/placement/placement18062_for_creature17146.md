I would find myself as a Visitor, a Warm Spot, a Pulse of Foreign Energy. I am the one who has descended into the throat, the one whose lungs burn with oxygen, whose heart beats a frantic drum against the ribs of this slow, breathing cathedral.

My form is alien here: bipedal, clothed, carrying a heat that is a geological anomaly in this glacial damp. My skin is a barrier, not a membrane; I am not woven into the root-lattice. When I press my hand into the sphagnum, I feel the cold seep up, but I do not feel the slow, osmotic exchange of information that the Keeper knows. I am an island of frantic metabolism in an ocean of patient chemistry.

Yet, as I stand in the center of the chamber, the boundaries begin to blur. The green mist of spores coats my jacket, and in the dim, bioluminescent light, I can see the faint tracery of root-hairs beginning to exploratively reach toward the salt of my skin. The "applause" of the falling water slows to match the rhythm of my breathing, or perhaps my breathing synchronizes to the drip. I am being observed, not by eyes, but by the entire sensory web of the place. The air tastes my presence, thick with the carbon dioxide I exhale, the unique signature of my warmth against the eternal cold.

I am not a threat, nor a savior. I am a catalyst. My movement through the chamber is a disturbance that ripples through the root-network, a vibration felt in the bones of the Keeper. The mobile fauna—the slug-like thoughts, the moth-like impulses—drift closer, not out of curiosity, but out of a homeostatic need to understand this warm, pulsing anomaly.

In this place, I am a question asked in a language of heat and haste, and the entire cathedral is slowly, deliberately, formulating an answer in the language of water and growth. I am the anomaly that proves the rule, the fleeting pulse that makes the eternal rhythm of the Keeper more itself. I am the visitor who will leave, but whose warmth will be remembered not as a memory, but as a texture in the moss, a slight variation in the glow of a leaf, a permanent, subtle change in the chemistry of the throat. I am the Warm Spot, and I am being digested—not destroyed, but integrated, my brief, frantic life becoming a part of the deep, cold, endless time of the gut of the world.
