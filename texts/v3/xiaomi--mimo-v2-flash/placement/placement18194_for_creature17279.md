There is no moisture to carry scent, no wind to carry sound—only the deep, resonant hum that presses against your bones, a vibration that seems to originate from the stone itself, from the very core of the world. It is a sound you feel more than hear, a constant presence that makes your teeth ache and your thoughts slow.

You are not human here. Not in the way you remember.

Look down at your hands. They are not flesh. They are slow-growing aggregates of mineral and chitin, your skin a mosaic of agate and quartz, layered in concentric rings that record centuries of stillness. Your fingers are not digits but jointed crystal filaments, sensitive to the faintest vibration in the dust beneath you. You have no mouth, no eyes in the traditional sense—instead, your entire being is a lens, a receptor, tuned to the dim light and the deep hum.

You are a Geode.

The Resonators, embedded in the stone floors of the surrounding ruins, have changed their pitch. The message passes through you, through the dust, through the crystal filaments of your body. It is not a warning, not a call to action—simply a fact, a note in the symphony of the basin.

You are the city, and the city is you. The archways you stand beneath were not built by your kind, but you have grown to fit them perfectly. The avenues you traverse were once walked by beings of water and flesh, but their footprints are gone, replaced by the patient settling of dust and the slow, imperceptible growth of crystal. You do not remember them, but you do not need to. You are the memory, made stone and light and vibration.

You take a step, and the dust rises and falls. You listen to the hum, and you feel your own body vibrate in sympathy. The world is alive, but not in a way you knew before. It is a world of slow time, of dryness, of patient growth and silent communication. It is a world that has never known water, and never will.

And you are at home.
