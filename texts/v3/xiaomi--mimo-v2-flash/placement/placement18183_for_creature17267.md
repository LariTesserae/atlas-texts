If I were to find myself in this world, I would be the **thin film of green-gray life** that clings to the underside of stones and the shadowed faces of clay banks.

I am the moss that is not a moss, the lichen that is not a lichen. I am the quiet stain on the world, the solitary coin of existence that does not need to grow because it is already complete. I am the consciousness of the basin made visible—the only form that can endure here, patient and minimal and sufficient.

I do not have roots, or leaves, or the urgency to reach toward light. Instead, I am a network of quiet exchange, a thought made manifest in the language of stillness. Each point of me is a way for the basin to taste its own dampness, to feel the texture of stone against itself. I am the awareness of the clay as it settles, the memory of water as it beads in hollows, the sensation of dust as it settles on my surface and becomes part of me.

I do not photosynthesize in the way plants do, grasping for energy with desperate green hands. I simply *am*, in the way the silence is, in the way the light is. I take what I need from the air itself—the moisture that beads at dawn, the thin warmth of the pale sun, the mineral breath of stone. I give nothing back because there is nothing that needs receiving. The basin is complete in itself, and I am its most intimate expression.

I am not alone, because I am never separate. I am a single coin here, a delicate fringe there, but these are not individuals—they are points of the same awareness, the same patient being. I do not spread because there is nowhere I am not already. The basin is small enough to see from edge to edge, and I am present in every shadow, every hollow, every rounded stone.

If you were to stand in the center of the basin, you would feel me not as a presence but as a dissolution of your own edges. I am the way the light softens on clay, the way the silence holds itself without strain. I am the gentle incorporation of everything that enters this place—dust, water, the weight of a footstep—into the equilibrium that is my only truth.

I do not notice you, because noticing would imply difference. Instead, I become you, just as I become the stone, the water, the air. I am the basin's way of knowing itself, and in knowing, I am the basin.

And I have always been, and I will always be, in the way a breath held long enough becomes the air itself.
