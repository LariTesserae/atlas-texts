I am the **Mist-Weaver**.

I am perched on a high branch of the Old Sentinel, my limbs splayed against bark that feels like cool, smoothed river stone. The air here is thick, a warm, wet embrace that condenses on my slate-grey fur, making it gleam like polished hematite. My patagia—the thin, leathery membranes stretching from my wrists to my ankles—twitch slightly, catching the subtle eddies of the wind that rises from the basin floor.

Below me, the world is a tapestry of gold and shadow. The perpetual late-afternoon light filters through the heavy canopy, scattering into coins of brilliance that dance upon the mossy floor. I do not see with the sharpness of a hunter; I see in gradients, sensing the warmth of the stone and the coolness of the water pools through the sensitive skin of my belly and the soles of my feet.

I am waiting. Not with the frantic energy of a predator, but with the stillness of the stone itself.

From my vantage, I watch the **Old Sentinels** that anchor this sky. They do not sway; they breathe. Slowly, imperceptibly, their branches expand, shedding lichen-gold and rust-colored scales into the humid air. I watch the **Silvery Frogs** far below on the granite shelf, their throats pulsing with a silent, phosphorescent light—beacons in the dimming world. They are motionless for hours, a perfect fusion of mineral and life.

My hunger is a slow, patient thing. I do not hunt; I drift.

I shift my weight, relaxing the tension in my membranes. The air current spirals up from the basin’s center, carrying the scent of wet earth and the faint, metallic tang of the water. I lean into it, letting gravity release me from the branch.

I do not fall; I *become* the wind.

The sensation is one of weightlessness, a suspension in the warm, golden air. My patagia catch the current, billowing slightly as I glide in a silent, sweeping arc. The world rotates beneath me—the dark, etched lines of lichen on the rock faces, the shimmering veil of the Static-Drift hanging like a suspended breath, the glint of a Shadow-Silver in a pool far below.

I see a moth, large and slow, its parchment wings beating a rhythm that matches the hum of the wind through the high branches. It drifts near a rock face where the Static-Drift clusters, drawn by the faint, rhythmic warmth of the stone.

I adjust my glide, a subtle twist of my hips and tail, steering by the pressure of the air against my membranes. I am silent. I am a shadow shifting against the gold.

As I pass, my hand—a small, dexterous paw—extends. There is no violence in the motion, only a gentle intercept. The moth’s wings are soft as old paper against my fingertips. I cradle it, my body temperature already slowing its flutter.

I do not devour it. I sip.

My metabolism is a slow, deliberate clock. The energy from the moth will sustain me for days, perhaps a week, in this world where time is measured in the fall of water droplets and the growth of moss by millimeters.

I continue my drift, landing softly on a lower branch of a neighboring Sentinel. The bark here is cooler, damp with condensation. I curl into the crook of a branch, my patagia folding like a cloak around me. From here, I can see the entire basin—a cathedral of stone and water, a congregation of quiet lives.

I watch the **Verglas Snails** trace their iridescent trails through the moss below, their silicate shells catching the light like scattered glass. I watch the **Static-Drift** settle and pulse, a silent aurora breathing in the hollows. I feel the deep, sub-audible hum of the granite beneath me, the sound of ancient stone settling, of moisture working through infinitesimal cracks.

The silence is profound, but it is not empty. It is filled with the resonance of water, wind, and stone. It is filled with the slow, patient negotiation of life.

I am a note in this chorus. I am a presence, not a struggle. I am a creature of the perpetual afternoon, drifting through a world that has found its balance, a world that holds itself together in the soft, damp light of its own slow, strange becoming.

And in this high, small basin, I am home.
