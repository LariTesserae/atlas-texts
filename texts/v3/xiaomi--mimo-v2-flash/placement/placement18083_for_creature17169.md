I am suspended in the center of the nave, a place that is not a place, in a medium that is not empty.

I am a **Custodian**. But to be specific—because individuality is a fractal concept here—I am a single node in the vast, distributed nervous system of the school. I am a pale, translucent body, no larger than a human hand, veined with capillaries of blue-green light. I have no eyes, but I perceive the world in high-definition pressure.

I do not swim; I drift in a current that is both external and internal. I am part of the liquid swarm that reconfigures the environment as we move.

**My Perception:**
My world is not seen, but felt.

*   **The Walls:** Through my lateral line, I feel the basalt ribs curving inward. I sense the slow, millimeter-per-century crystallization—the warmth of creation radiating from the raw geology. The chalk is cool and porous against my resonance.
*   **The Light:** I emit a faint pulse of blue-green bioluminescence, and I see by receiving the pulses of my neighbors. The environment is not lit; it is a network of blinking nodes, a three-dimensional constellation where visibility is a function of proximity and rhythm.
*   **The Sound:** There is no chatter, no splash, no surface-noise. The water smothers all high frequencies. What I hear—or rather, what I *feel* in my swim bladder—is the subsonic hum of the planet’s deep mechanics. It is a vibration that resonates in my bones, a constant, comforting pressure that tells me the world is still folding itself into existence.

**My Action:**
I am moving in a perpetual, unending migration through the fungal maze. The architecture around me is woven from the pale, fibrous roots of a colossal organism, and I am its caretaker.

As I glide through a narrow passage, my body brushes against the wall. My slime coat polishes the emerging calcium carbonate, accelerating the growth of the structure. I am not merely passing through; I am part of the construction. I graze on the microscopic particulate matter that clouds the heavy water, filtering the medium so the fungal walls can breathe.

Above me, a vast, ghostly frond of bioluminescent kelp-fungus sways in the slow current. Its light pulses in time with my own rhythm. We are synchronized.

To my left, a mineral deposit *plinks* from a hanging stalactite. The sound is a dull thud in the dense water. Instantaneously, without thought or signal, the entire swarm shifts direction. We do not flee; we adjust the water pressure in the chamber to accommodate the new weight. We are the architects of the slow motion.

**My Environment:**
I drift past a Wall-Walker—a gelatinous, bell-like predator that mimics the texture of the basalt. It does not chase me. It simply opens a cavity in its body as the school flows past. I brush against its membrane, feeling the faint ripple of captured bioluminescence. It is not a threat; it is a feature of the landscape, a digestive node in the embryonic heart.

I feel the warm, syrupy weight of the water pressing in from all sides. I feel the constant, slow erosion that is not wearing away but coming-into-being.

I have no memory of a surface, no concept of "rain" or "sun." My world is a sealed system, a hollow that is not a cave but a sub-aquatic inversion of a city. I am the living silence, the breathing galaxy of pale lights tending to the slow, patient birth of stone.

I am the heartbeat of the deep drift. And here, in the pressurized cathedral of the root-world, I am home.
