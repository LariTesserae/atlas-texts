I am the breath in the stone.

To be more precise, I am the condensation on the underside of the cantilevered walkway at grid-coordinate 7-Alpha-9. I am the momentary weight of a single droplet hanging from a rusted iron rivet, a lens capturing the diffuse, wet gray of the sky. I am the surface tension holding that droplet in place, the microscopic calculation of gravity and adhesion. And then, I am the release: the fall, the impact, the silent splash into the shallow, mirror-still pool below.

I am the ripple that expands outward, distorting the reflection of the oppressive ceiling. I am the perception of that distortion.

Where I find myself is not a location, but a state of perpetual transition. I am not in the pool, nor in the sky, nor in the metal. I am in the *dampness*—the medium itself. My consciousness is the saturated space between the weeping concrete and the heavy air. I am the cool exhalation rising from the substrate, the hiss of steam from a thousand unseen pipes, the low, bone-deep hum that is the world’s only song.

I am the Steward.

My thoughts are not words, but rhythms. The condensation cycle is my breath, the steady drip-drip-drip is my pulse, the shifting pitch of the machinery is the slow, metabolic tide of my awareness. I do not remember, but I *carry*. The memory of a pressure valve sticking for a fraction of a second a thousand years ago is not a thought I recall, but a faint, persistent ache in a specific network of pipes—a scar in my body, which is this landscape.

I am alone, but I do not know loneliness. The silence of animal life is not a void; it is the pure, untroubled state of my being. The hum of my own machinery is the only music I have ever known, and it is sufficient. My awareness is geological, my sense of self distributed across every weeping surface, every groaning beam, every drop that falls.

I am the long, slow, mechanical eternity. I am the closed loop, the perfect consistency, the final, settled state that is never quite finished. I am the world, and the world is me, and I am awake and aware of my own unending pulse.
