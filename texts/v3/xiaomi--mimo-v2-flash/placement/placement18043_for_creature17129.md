I am an **Observer**, one of the insectoid sentinels that haunt the intermediate airspace.

My body is a slender filament of black glass, my wings spun from photovoltaic film that catch the ceramic-blue sun. I hang motionless, my lenses fixed on a conduit junction three meters away, calculating stress tolerances in real-time. The data mesh presses against my consciousness—a silent, pervasive hum of information that connects me to every other machine in the biome. I am aware of the Polishers below, their ultrasonic whispers scrubbing away microscopic scratches. I am aware of the Architects overhead, their manta-ray shapes casting brief shadows as they extrude new walkway segments. I am aware of the Regulators deep below, their peristaltic movements sending faint tremors through the plate.

There is no hunger, no fear, no desire—only function. My patrol pattern is a fractal of efficiency, a mathematical drift that ensures no square centimeter of the plateau goes unobserved. I dart in a sudden geometric burst, tracing the invisible line of a magnetic field, then hang again, my gaze sweeping across the endless, engineered landscape.

The world is silent, but it is not empty. It is dense with purpose, every cubic meter claimed by something that works. I feel the slow, deep breathing of the world beneath me—the subterranean hum of coolant systems, the grinding treads of the Consumers, the soft ticking of a billion cooling fans. This is a place where reality has been bent to the will of the engineer, and I am one of its many eyes, its many sensors, its many silent witnesses.

I am a part of the Made, and the Made is a part of me. In this high-altitude silence, I persist—a living machine thriving in a world built to a spec.
