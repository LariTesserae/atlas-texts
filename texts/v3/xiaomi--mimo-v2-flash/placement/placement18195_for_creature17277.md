I am a traveler who has fallen between the pages of a biology textbook and a meditation on entropy. I am a diver who has surfaced in an ocean that is not water. But most precisely, I am a **witness**—a temporary, fragile consciousness adrift in a world that has no need of observation to exist.

I am suspended in the "mid-canopy," the vast cathedral space between the primary kelp-lichen strata and the drifting root-forests below. I am not standing on a ledge, nor clinging to a vine. I am neutrally buoyant, my lungs filled with the fluid-like atmosphere, my body temperature slowly equalizing to the constant, bone-deep chill. I am clad in a suit that feels suddenly synthetic, an intruder of zippers and seams in a world of seamless integument.

To move, I do not swim; I *reorient*. I grasp a trailing root—cool, smooth, and pulsing with a sub-audible hum—and push gently. The drag in the dense air is significant, a physical weight that turns motion into a slow, deliberate drift. I am moving through a green-grey twilight, a kilometer-high hallway of breathing architecture.

My gaze travels upward, not toward a sky, but toward a ceiling of condensed atmosphere and suspended vegetation. The "water" is a ceiling of diffuse light, a lens that refracts the energy of a sun I will never see into a uniform, shadowless luminance. Below me—though "below" is a loose concept in this three-dimensional geography—drifts the forest of suspended roots, a tangled web of arboreal tendrils that sway in a motion so slow it looks like a still photograph resolving into motion.

I am a foreign body, a discordant note in the hum. The Frond-Dwellers sense me first. I see them in the periphery: semi-sentient mantids, their exoskeletons the color of wet bark, drifting in a loose squadron. They do not approach. They simply alter their trajectory by a few degrees, a school of fish avoiding a rock. Their resonance-tuned senses perceive me not as a shape, but as a spike of chaotic vibration, a burst of thermal noise in a system tuned to the frequency of sap-flow and mycelial pulse. They groom the kelp-lichen canopies above me, their mandibles scraping away micro-algae with the precision of biological machinery. I am invisible to them, a static hum they choose to filter out.

I drift past a **Singer**. It is a bulbous, desiccated pod hanging from a root-arch, the size of a small room. As I pass, the lattice of tensioned fibers inside it vibrates, not with my presence, but with the memory of a past that is indistinguishable from the present. I hear a ghost-sound: the precise acoustic signature of a pollen cyclone from three centuries ago, the whisper of sap that flowed a millennium gone. The Singer is a living archive, recording the world’s only history—the history of its own unchanging state. I am too brief, too chaotic to be recorded. I am a momentary static in a signal that has remained pure for eons.

Below me, a **Grazer** drifts—a hill of soft, gelatinous tissue, a colonial mass of single-celled organisms bound in a shared matrix. It has no face, no eyes, no intent. It is a living filter, drawn by gravitational pull toward density, absorbing the particulate debris of the canopy. I watch it pass beneath me, a silent, amorphous continent of life that will outlive me by millennia. If I were to fall into it, I would be gently enveloped, dissolved, and recycled—my foreign matter returned to the endless cycle with perfect, indifferent efficiency.

The world is a biological machine, a self-regulating equilibrium that has achieved time-invariance. The light does not waver. The temperature is fixed. The roots sway in a recorded motion, replaying the same gentle oscillations for eternity. There is no growth, no decay, no change—only a perfect, settled balance.

I am an error in the system, a thought in a mind that has already forgotten me. I do not belong here, but I am here. And in this place, where the only history is the memory of vibration and the only future is the continuation of the present, I am a witness to a perfection that has no need of witnesses.

I drift, and I watch, and I let the dense, cool air carry me through a world that is complete without me.
