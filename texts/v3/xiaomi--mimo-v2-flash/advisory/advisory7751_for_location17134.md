To prepare for visiting the Interstice, you must fundamentally alter your definition of what a "place" is. This is not a landscape you traverse; it is a single, living organism you are about to enter. Your preparation is less like packing for a trip and more like a medical patient preparing for a symbiotic transplant.

### Phase 1: Psychological and Sensory De-conditioning

The most disorienting feature of the Interstice will be the absence of the familiar. You must train yourself to un-learn the following:

*   **The Horizon:** The Interstice is a basin. There is no distant horizon, no sky in the traditional sense, no sun or stars. Your eyes will ache for a vanishing point that doesn't exist. Practice focusing on micro-landscapes. Learn to find beauty in the texture of a wall rather than the sweep of a vista.
*   **Circadian Rhythms:** There is no day or night. The light is constant, a diffuse silver glow from the floor and walls. Before you go, spend at least a week in a environment with controlled, dim lighting to acclimate your pineal gland. Your sleep cycle will be dictated by the collective rhythm of the settlement, not a celestial clock. You will sleep in short bursts, in niches carved into the plinths, when the local activity quiets to a murmur.
*   **The Concept of "Dry":** The air is perpetually damp, a warm, clinging embrace. Your skin will never feel fully dry. Your clothes will feel heavy. You must prepare psychologically for this constant tactile sensation. It is not oppressive, but it is absolute. Pack no expectation of dryness.
*   **Verticality:** You are accustomed to looking up for sky and down for ground. Here, the "sky" is a distant, vaulted ceiling, and the "ground" is an active, living surface. Your posture will change. You will move with a slight crouch, your eyes constantly scanning the floor for channels, ledges, and the subtle undulations that define space. Practice walking on uneven, polished surfaces with a low center of gravity.

### Phase 2: Physical and Biological Preparation

Your body will be in constant contact with the Interstice. It is a sealed system, and you are a foreign body entering it.

*   **The Lungs:** The air is thick with moisture, spores, and the scent of wet clay, fungal life, and minerals. It is not sterile. For weeks before your visit, expose yourself to similar, though less intense, environments—botanical gardens, caves, damp forests. You need to cultivate a robust nasal and pulmonary microbiome. The mist is breathable, but it is full of life. Do not arrive with sterile city lungs; they will be shocked.
*   **The Skin:** Your skin is your primary interface with this world. It will be perpetually damp. You must abandon any notion of waterproof gear; nothing will stay dry. Instead, focus on breathable, natural fibers that can handle constant moisture without degrading or becoming foul. The "cloth" of the Interstice is felted algae-fiber; you should wear something similar. Your skin will absorb the ambient humidity and the faint bioluminescence of the clay. You will, in a very literal sense, begin to glow faintly upon leaving.
*   **The Gut:** The water is not for drinking. You will drink the mineral-rich condensation, tapped from channels directly into a cup or your mouth. This water is pure but mineral-dense. You must prepare your digestive system for this. For a week before, drink only mineral water with a similar profile (high in calcium, magnesium, and silicates). The primary protein is the pale, blind mole. Its meat is lean, slightly sweet, and earthy. If you are a meat-eater, begin introducing lean, earthy proteins into your diet. If you are vegetarian, the fungal mats and succulent leaves are your sustenance, but they are fibrous and require slow, deliberate chewing. Prepare your jaw and digestion for a diet of tough, living plants.
*   **The Ears:** The Interstice is never silent. It is a dense cacophony of drip, chitter, rasp, and tap. The sound is not an echo; it is absorbed by the soft, porous surfaces. You must train your brain to parse this layered sound. Practice listening to complex, ambient soundscapes—the hum of a city from inside a building, the sound of a cave with dripping water and insects. You will need to learn to find the "bass note" of the dripping water and filter out the high-frequency insect chitter to focus on the human murmur.

### Phase 3: The Gear

Your technology must be primitive to be harmonious. Anything synthetic, loud, or bright will be a violation.

*   **Light:** You do not need a flashlight. The Interstice provides its own light. A flashlight would be a harsh, invasive tool, creating sharp shadows in a place of diffuse glow. Instead, bring a small, polished piece of the local clay—a "glow-stone"—to read by if needed, but rely on your eyes to adapt to the constant, low light.
*   **Tools:** Bring nothing that requires electricity or complex mechanics. A simple, sharp knife of hardened fungal wood or bone is sufficient. A small container for condensation, carved from clay. A length of string-vine for tying or measuring. Your hands are your primary tools.
*   **Clothing:** Wear layers of felted algae-fiber or similar natural, moisture-wicking cloth. No zippers, no buckles, no synthetic threads. Everything must be able to withstand constant dampness without rusting, rotting, or degrading. Your shoes should be soft-soled, made of fungal leather, to feel the vibrations and textures of the floor.
*   **Containment:** You need a pack, but it must be woven from string-vine and lined with felted fiber. It will get damp. Nothing inside should be sensitive to moisture. Paper will turn to pulp. Electronics will fail. Bring only what is essential and organic.

### Phase 4: The Philosophical and Social Preparation

The Interstice is a single, interconnected organism. You are a guest in its body.

*   **The Concept of "Waste":** There is no waste here. Everything is repurposed, composted, or reintegrated. Your own biological waste must be treated as a resource. You will be shown how to contribute to the fungal mats or the cisterns. You must be prepared to participate in the cycle, not just observe it.
*   **The Social Contract:** The density is overwhelming, but the scale is intimate. You will be in constant proximity to others. Personal space is defined by the rise and fall of the floor, not by an invisible bubble. You must learn to read these subtle architectural cues. A low archway is a private space; a broad, sunken plaza is public. Do not impose your own culture's notions of personal distance.
*   **The Pace:** The Interstice moves slowly. The erosion is millennia-old, and the work is patient. You cannot rush. The "technology" is primitive because it is sustainable. You must adopt the pace of the hand-weaver, the clay-modeller, the mole-hunter. Move with deliberate, slow grace. The polished floor is a testament to generations of shuffling feet, not hurried strides.

### The Final Preparation: The Entry

Do not arrive with a map. The Interstice is a maze, but it is a living maze. You will learn its paths by following the flow of water, the density of fungal growth, and the murmur of activity. Your guide is not a chart, but your senses.

When you step onto the fused clay floor, you will feel the warm, damp embrace immediately. The light will rise from beneath your feet. The air will fill your lungs with the scent of wet stone and life. The sound will wrap around you—a dense, layered hum of existence.

You are not visiting a settlement. You are being inhaled by it.

Prepare to be changed. When you leave, the light of the Interstice will have seeped into your skin, the rhythm of the drip will be in your ears, and the memory of the warm, damp embrace will be a comfort you will miss in the sterile, dry world outside.
