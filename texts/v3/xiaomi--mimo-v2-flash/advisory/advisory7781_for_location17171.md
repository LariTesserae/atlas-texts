To prepare for a journey into this wet lung, you must fundamentally recalibrate your understanding of what it means to visit a place. You are not going to a location; you are entering a state of being. The air here is not an atmosphere you pass through, but a fluid you must learn to breathe differently.

**Physical Preparation: The Skin as a Membrane**

Your first consideration is your own integument. The air is described as a "saturated blanket," meaning humidity is absolute. Standard waterproof gear will fail not by leaking, but by trapping condensation against your skin until you are boiling in your own sweat. The key is breathability on a level beyond modern fabrics.

*   **Clothing:** Wear loose-fitting, natural fibers. Think raw silk or fine, unbleached linen. These materials can hold a layer of moisture against your skin without feeling clammy, acting as a second, more adaptable epidermis. Avoid anything synthetic; the static and the clinging will be psychologically torturous. The color should be a non-reflective gray or green, to avoid disturbing the "opal gray" light.
*   **Footwear:** The ground is a "mat of roots and moss" that yields and releases liquid. Waterproof boots will become suction-cup prisons. Instead, opt for open-weave sandals or shoes made of porous, pliable leather that can drain and dry instantly, even while you walk. The sensation of cool liquid with every step is not a hazard to be avoided, but a sensory reality to be accepted. Your feet must be able to feel the texture of the world.
*   **Respiration:** The air "catches in the throat." This suggests a high particulate count of spores, pollen, or ozone. A simple cloth mask might filter larger particles but would become sodden and useless. A better solution is a simple respiratory aid, perhaps a ceramic or porous stone filter held loosely in the mouth, designed to humidify the air you draw in rather than fight it. You are not excluding the environment; you are pre-filtering it.

**Sensory Preparation: Calibrating to the Slow Dynamic**

Your senses will be overwhelmed not by intensity, but by saturation.

*   **Sight:** The light is diffuse, without sharp shadows. Your eyes, accustomed to contrast and focal points, will struggle. Before you go, spend time in a fog, or in a room lit only by north-facing windows. Train your gaze to find meaning in texture and subtle shifts of tone, not in hard edges. The "green-gold patches" of light are your only clocks; learn to read them. Do not stare at the pools for long; the description warns of a reflection of a "starless void." This is a perceptual trap. Keep your gaze moving, soft, and unfocused.
*   **Hearing:** The soundscape is a symphony of drips, gurgles, and a sub-bass hum. This is not silence, but it is also not the chaotic noise of a city. Prepare for this by listening to recordings of deep caves or the inside of a termite mound. Learn to distinguish the "wet sigh" of the architecture from the drip of water. The low hum is a constant; you must learn to feel it in your sternum and accept it as the baseline of existence here. It is the sound of immense, slow processes—the "drip, drip, drip" of geologic time.
*   **Smell and Touch:** The air smells of "wet earth, crushed herbs, and ozone." This is the scent of life and decay happening simultaneously. Before you go, acclimate yourself to the smell of a greenhouse after watering, of a forest floor after rain. Your sense of smell will be your most honest guide here. Touch every surface with the back of your hand first. The "fibrous, rubbery plant-matter" will feel alien. The "creeping vine" might be warm to the touch. The "bulbous, fungal growth" might pulse. Accept this.

**Psychological Preparation: Dissolving the Ego of the Visitor**

This is the most crucial preparation. The wet lung is a single organism, and you are a foreign microbe within it.

*   **Abandon the Concept of Ruins:** You will see industrial artifacts—a winch, a pipe, a concrete plinth. Your mind will label them "ruins," "relics," "abandoned." This is a human-centric illusion. Here, they are "integrated," like bones in a body. They are not waiting for repair; they are being remembered, digested, and given new purpose. To prepare, practice seeing the world this way. Look at a tree growing over a fence and see it not as "nature reclaiming civilization" but as a single, new organism.
*   **Surrender Linearity:** The rules here are written in slowness. Your human impulse will be to measure progress, to seek a destination, to map the space. But the "architecture is grown, not built." There are no straight lines that remain straight. Your map will be obsolete by the time you finish drawing it. Instead, prepare for a state of receptive wandering. Your goal is not to reach the other side, but to understand the rhythm of the breathing.
*   **Accept the Intelligence:** The place is described as a "living machine" that has "forgotten its original design." This implies a latent, non-human intelligence. It is not hostile, but it is utterly indifferent to your comfort. The geometric patterns in the moss, the sap in the iron pipe, the waiting quality of the machinery—these are expressions of its thought process. Prepare to be observed, not by eyes, but by the structure itself. You are a data point in its slow, vascular consciousness.

**Final Provisions: What to Carry and What to Leave Behind**

*   **Carry:**
    *   A simple, waterproof notebook and graphite pencil (ink may bleed, pens may clog with spores). Record the patterns you see, the sounds, the feel of the air.
    *   A small, smooth stone or a piece of glass. Something with a simple, predictable geometry to hold onto when the architecture feels too overwhelming.
    *   A vessel for water, but be prepared to find it already full. The world is water.
    *   A willingness to be patient. Measure time not in minutes, but in the "millimeter-per-year growth of a new root-pillar."

*   **Leave Behind:**
    *   **The Clock:** Time here is not linear. It is measured in condensation, in growth, in the slow pulse of the canopy.
    *   **The Mirror:** The self you know is a creature of dry air and sharp light. In the wet lung, you will be softened, blurred, and integrated. To hold onto a rigid self-concept is to invite psychological distress.
    *   **The Map:** The only map is your own body’s adaptation to the humidity, your own senses learning the language of the drip and the hum.
    *   **The Need for an Exit:** You do not visit a lung to leave it quickly. You enter to understand the breath. The exit will present itself not as a door, but as a change in the rhythm of the breathing.

To prepare is to prepare for a dissolution. You are not a traveler visiting a place. You are a temporary cell entering a larger, slower, greener body. Your only preparation is to become as permeable, patient, and receptive as the world itself.
