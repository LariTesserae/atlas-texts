To prepare for a visit to this place, you must abandon every instinct you have about what a "city" or a "world" is. Your preparation is not merely logistical; it is biological, psychological, and sensory. You are not visiting a place *on* a planet; you are entering the bloodstream of a living organism.

Here is how an ordinary multiverse traveler should prepare.

### 1. Biological & Immunological Conditioning
The air here is not just atmosphere; it is a spore-rich suspension. An ordinary immune system will react violently to the constant inhalation of microscopic fungal particulates, mites, and bacterial aerosols.

*   **Pre-Exposure Inoculation:** You must undergo a regimen of exposure to benign mycotoxins and fungal antigens weeks before departure. Your lungs need to be conditioned to treat the air not as a contaminant, but as a medium.
*   **Microbiome Seeding:** The ground is a living mycelial mat. You cannot sterily interact with it. You must cultivate a symbiotic skin microbiome—introducing beneficial lichens and yeasts to your own skin so that you do not disrupt the local ecology (and so the local ecology does not digest you). Touching the walls should not cause a fever in the settlement, nor a rash on your hand.
*   **Temperature Acclimation:** The cold is constant and damp, penetrating bone and gear alike. You cannot rely on static insulation (like a down jacket) which eventually saturates. You need active metabolic heating—clothing woven from insulating fungal silk that breathes, or a low-level biological thermal regulator worn against the skin to maintain core temperature without introducing foreign heat that might "burn" the sensitive local air chemistry.

### 2. Sensory Recalibration
Your primary senses will be overwhelmed and under-stimulated simultaneously.

*   **Visual Spectrum Expansion:** The bioluminescence is blue-green, operating at the very edge of human night vision. You need time to dilate your pupils, but also optical augmentation. Contact lenses that amplify low-light photoreception are essential. However, you must filter out the UV and IR spectrums, as the "air" itself may fluoresce in wavelengths that cause optical noise or migraines. You are not looking for detail; you are looking for silhouette and shadow.
*   **Auditory Dampening:** The city is loud, but it is a high-frequency, microscopic loudness—the click of chitin, the hum of wings. Human hearing is tuned to macro-sounds (roars, footsteps). You may need to dampen low-frequency background noise to focus on the high-frequency "clicks" that warn of movement in the dark.
*   **Olfactory De-conditioning:** The smell of wet bark and decay is pervasive. It is not a smell of rot (in the bacterial sense) but of digestion. You must train your nose to differentiate between the "healthy" scent of fungal respiration and the sour scent of true toxicity.

### 3. Gear: The Technology of Softness
Hard, synthetic materials will be rejected by this world. The city is a soft, living thing; hard shells will chafe, slip, and potentially damage the delicate mycelial mat.

*   **Footwear:** Do not wear rigid boots. The ground is spongy, yielding, and alive. Rigid soles will isolate you from the tremors of the city (your primary method of sensing movement) and will compress the mat, causing localized "bruising." Wear soft, pliable boots made of cured fungal leather or felted fiber, soles thin enough to feel the texture of the mycelium, allowing you to walk with the rhythm of the settlement’s breathing.
*   **Clothing:** Synthetic polymers (nylon, polyester) may outgas chemicals that are toxic to the local flora. Wear natural, woven fibers—fungal silk or processed bark-cloth. These will regulate humidity automatically, wicking away the constant condensation without becoming waterlogged.
*   **Lighting:** Do not bring a flashlight. A bright, focused beam is an act of violence in this soft darkness; it will blind you to the ambient bioluminescence and startle the fauna. Use a passive light source—perhaps a jar containing bioluminescent bacteria from your own dimension, tuned to the same frequency as the city’s glow—or simply rely on chemiluminescent paint on your gear that reacts to the ambient spores.
*   **Tools:** Leave the steel axe at home. Metal is alien here. Bring tools of bone, stone, or sharpened fossilized fungus. If you must repair something, use resin or wax, not epoxy.

### 4. Psychological Preparation
The greatest challenge is the ontological shift required to exist here.

*   **The Concept of Harm:** You must recalibrate your definition of "harm." In a standard city, stepping on a crack is trivial. Here, severing a root is a neurosurgical event. You must move with a gentleness that feels unnatural to a bipedal predator. Your movements must be deliberate, slow, and aware of the connectivity of all things.
*   **Isolation and Intimacy:** The settlement is sparse. You will be alone in the dark between structures. The fear of the dark is primal, but here the dark is not empty—it is full of life. You must overcome the instinct to fear the unseen and instead accept the sensation of being watched by a million microscopic eyes. It is a claustrophobia of space; the openness of the bowl feels like a containment field.
*   **Temporal Distortion:** There is no sun or moon to mark time. The city operates on metabolic cycles—shifts in light intensity, rates of condensation, and spore release. You must abandon the 24-hour clock and learn to read the "pulse" of the organism. Sleep when the fungal glow dims; move when it brightens.

### 5. Social and Diplomatic Protocols
The inhabitants are small, pale, and see you as a giant intruder. Their technology is not mechanical but symbiotic.

*   **Non-Verbal Communication:** Sound travels poorly through the dense, humid air and porous fungal structures. Visual signaling using the faint bioluminescence or slow, deliberate hand gestures (palm open, showing lack of tools) is superior to speech.
*   **The Gift of Breath:** Do not offer food from your world—it could be toxic to their biology. Instead, offer air. In a world of dense spores, a pocket of filtered, clean air (contained in a portable lung-bellows) is a luxury. Offering a breath of "new" air to a resident is a profound gesture of greeting.
*   **Respect for the Mat:** Never sit directly on the ground without a barrier. The mat is sensitive to heat and salt from your body. To sit unprotected is to "burn" the city’s skin.

### 6. Emergency Contingencies
*   **The Fever:** If you accidentally damage the mat, the local temperature will rise in a defensive response. You must carry a cooling salve—not for you, but to apply to the wound in the mat to soothe the organism’s immune response.
*   **Getting Lost:** In the absolute dark, direction is lost easily. Do not rely on line of sight. Use the tactile feedback of the mat; different pathways have different textures (smooth vs. nubby). Carry a spool of conductive fungal silk thread to lay a trail behind you, vibrating it to send signals back to your starting point.
*   **The Spore Fog:** When the city releases spores, the air becomes electric and thick. This is not toxic, but it causes hallucinations and a sense of merging with the environment. You must have a respirator ready—not just to filter, but to separate your exhalations from the city’s inhalations, maintaining the boundary of your individual self.

### The Final Mental State
To visit this city, you must enter a state of **receptive vulnerability**. You are not a conqueror, a tourist, or a surveyor. You are a guest entering a sleeping giant’s mind. You must move with the slow, deep certainty of a root growing, aware that every step is a conversation with the ground beneath you.

Pack light, pack soft, and prepare to dissolve the boundary between your skin and the air.
