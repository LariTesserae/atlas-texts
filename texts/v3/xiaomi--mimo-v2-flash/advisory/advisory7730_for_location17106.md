To prepare for visiting this place—an antechamber to a mineral afterlife, a geode-cathedral of built stillness—you must treat the journey not as exploration, but as pilgrimage into a state of being that is profoundly alien to human biology. This is not a hostile environment, but an indifferent one, and indifference is often more dangerous than malice. Your preparation must be physical, psychological, and metaphysical.

### Phase 1: Physiological and Atmospheric Acclimatization

**1. Hydration and Moisture Lock:**
The air is 0.4/3 dry—a vacuum-thirst that will strip moisture from your lungs, skin, and eyes with ruthless efficiency. You cannot rely on the atmosphere to hold any humidity. You must arrive fully sealed.
-   **Wear a full-body, sealed environment suit.** This is not for pressure, but for moisture retention. The suit should be a personal micro-atmosphere, with a closed-loop breathing system that recycles your exhaled moisture. A standard spacesuit will fail; the air is not toxic, just arid. You need a suit designed for Martian or lunar regolith—something that prioritizes humidity capture over filtration.
-   **Pre-hydrate aggressively.** For 48 hours before transit, drink electrolyte-rich fluids until your cells are saturated. Your body will lose water the moment you enter; you must start with a surplus.
-   **Eye protection is non-negotiable.** The dryness will cause instantaneous, painful cracking of the tear film. Wear sealed goggles with anti-fog internal coatings. Blinking will be a luxury you cannot afford.
-   **Lip and nasal mucosa:** Coat all exposed membranes with medical-grade lanolin or a specialized barrier gel. Your nostrils will bleed within minutes if left unprotected.

**2. Thermal Regulation:**
The floor is a persistent 1.0/3 cool—not freezing, but a deep, unwavering cold that will leach heat through the soles of your boots over time.
-   **Insulated boots with thermal barriers.** The floor is tiled with salt-crystal; its coolness is conductive. You need multiple layers: an inner moisture-wicking sock, an aerogel insulation layer, and a tough outer sole. Do not let your feet go numb.
-   **Layered suit with active temperature control.** Your body's core temperature must remain stable. The ambient air is cool but still; without metabolic heat generation, you will slowly cool. The suit should have minimal active heating—just enough to counteract the floor's drain.

**3. Respiratory Protection:**
The air is a "mineral breath"—dry, particulate, and possibly reactive with human tissue.
-   **Particulate filtration.** Even if the air is not toxic, mineral dust can be abrasive. Your breathing system must filter out any suspended particles. The "hum" you will hear is vibrational, but fine dust could be present.
-   **CO₂ Scrubbing.** In a sealed chamber with no atmospheric exchange, your exhaled CO₂ will build up. Your suit must have a chemical scrubber (like lithium hydroxide) to keep the air breathable.

### Phase 2: Psychological and Sensory Preparation

**1. Sensory Deprivation and Overload:**
This place is a paradox: visually flat and bright, yet acoustically overwhelming with a single, bone-deep tone.
-   **Ear protection is essential, but selective.** The hum (2.5/3 loudness) is a structural resonance. It is not noise; it is the sound of the place *being*. It may cause disorientation, nausea, or even vibrational tinnitus. Wear high-fidelity earplugs that dampen the tone but allow you to hear your own breathing and suit systems. You do not want to be cut off from all sound, but you must protect your inner ear from the constant vibration.
-   **Visual adaptation.** The light is sourceless, shadowless, and flat (2.8/3). This will erode your depth perception. The walls will appear closer than they are; the ceiling will be an infinite haze. Your suit's visor should have a slight tint to reduce glare, and you should practice navigating in evenly lit, shadowless environments beforehand (like a photography light tent).
-   **The Hum.** Before you go, listen to recordings of infrasound or Tibetan singing bowls at low frequencies. Acclimate your mind to sustained tones. The hum is not a threat, but it will become the only thing you perceive. You must learn to hear *through* it, to let it become background, or it will consume your sanity.

**2. Psychological Conditioning for Stasis:**
This place is in "frozen equilibrium." It does not change. For a human, whose brain is wired for change, this is profoundly unsettling.
-   **Practice stillness.** Meditate in a completely static environment—no windows, no moving air, no variation in light. Learn to sit with the sensation that time has stopped. Your mind will scream for novelty; you must train it to accept permanence.
-   **Abandon the search for meaning.** The structure feels "designed, but without a designer." It is grown, not built. Do not look for tools, for signs of intelligence, for purpose. It is a *fact*, not a message. Your preparation must include releasing the need to interpret.

### Phase 3: Equipment and Tools

**1. The Suit as a Interface:**
Your suit is your life, but also your limitation. It must be minimalist, silent, and reliable.
-   **No external tools.** The tech level is 0.5/3—no devices, no mechanics. Do not bring scanners, lights, or communicators. They will feel alien and intrusive. The walls emit light; the air has no data to give. Bring only what sustains life: air, moisture, temperature.
-   **Navigation aids.** The scale is hidden, and the space is vast but uniform. You will lose your sense of direction. Use a simple, inert compass (if it works here) and a tether line. Do not rely on GPS—it will not function here. The tether is your anchor to the entry point; the walls are too uniform to mark.
-   **Recording devices.** If you must document, use a passive, non-electronic method. A graphite pencil and waterproof paper. The environment may interfere with digital storage. The act of writing will also ground you in the present.

**2. Footwear and Traction:**
The floor tiles are mineral deposits, smooth and cool. They may be slippery.
-   **Sole material:** Rubber will be too soft; it may react with the salt-crystal. Use a hard, non-reactive composite sole with fine grooves for traction. Test it beforehand on salt flats or mineral deposits.
-   **Gait:** Walk slowly, deliberately. The hum will vibrate through your feet; you will feel the structure's resonance. Let your steps be part of the hum, not a disruption.

### Phase 4: Metaphysical and Conceptual Preparation

**1. The Concept of "Builtness":**
The walls are not stone; they are "builtness itself." This is a philosophical challenge.
-   **Reframe your perception.** You are not entering a cave or a tunnel. You are entering a *manifestation of form*. The walls are a physical idea. Do not try to categorize them as biological, mineral, or technological. They are simply *built*. Accept this, or you will spend your time trying to classify the unclassifiable.

**2. The Silence Within the Hum:**
The place is silent except for the tone. This silence is not empty; it is full.
-   **Embrace the monotony.** The stillness (dynamic 0.2/3) is not emptiness; it is presence. You will feel the weight of eternity. Prepare by contemplating the concept of timelessness—what it means for something to exist without change. This is a mental exercise that will prevent panic when you realize nothing moves.

**3. The Exit Strategy:**
This is a sealed pocket-world. There is no guarantee of return, or of a stable exit point.
-   **Mark your entry.** The tiles are uniform; you will not find your way back by sight. Use a non-destructive marker—a small pile of inert, foreign material (like a sealed container of sand from your origin world) placed at the entry tile. Do not scratch the tiles; they are perfect and may be reactive.
-   **Transit timing.** Do not stay longer than 2-3 hours. The psychological effects of stasis and sensory monotony compound over time. Your body and mind are built for change; this place is not.

### Final Checklist Before Transit:

1.  **Suit Integrity:** Sealed, moisture-cycled, temperature-regulated.
2.  **Hydration:** Pre-loaded, cells saturated.
3.  **Eyes & Mucosa:** Protected with barriers.
4.  **Ears:** Fitted with selective dampeners.
5.  **Mind:** Meditated, conditioned for stillness and the hum.
6.  **Tools:** Tether line, graphite/paper, inert marker.
7.  **Footwear:** Hard-soled, tested for traction.
8.  **Exit Mark:** Sealed, foreign material prepared.
9.  **Philosophy:** Accept the builtness. Accept the stillness. Accept the hum.

You are not entering a room. You are entering a state of being. The place will not harm you, but it will unmake your assumptions. Prepare to be still, to be dry, to be resonant, and to walk in a light that has no source. Walk slowly. Listen to the hum. And remember: the perfection you see is not designed; it is simply *what is*.
