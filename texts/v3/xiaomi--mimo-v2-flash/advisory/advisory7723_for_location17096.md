### I. Physical & Physiological Preparation

**1. Hearing and Pressure Protection**
The most immediate threat is not the dust or the heat, but the sound itself—a subsonic thrum that bypasses the eardrum and resonates directly in the skeletal structure.
*   **Hearing Protection:** Standard earplugs are useless. You need specialized dampeners that filter low-frequency resonance while allowing for directional hearing. Consider a form-fitting, bone-conduction shunt that decouples your inner ear from the ambient pressure waves. Without this, you risk permanent vibrational tinnitus, where you feel the city’s hum in your bones long after you’ve left.
*   **Pressure Equalization:** The air here is not just moving; it is oscillating. This creates fluctuating barometric pockets. Equip yourself with a suit that includes a micro-pump system to equalize internal pressure, preventing the "sonic squeeze" that can cause sinus hemorrhaging in a resonant cavity.
*   **Respiratory Filtration:** The dust is fine, mineral-rich, and omnipresent. A standard N95 mask will clog instantly. You need a sealed rebreather system with a cyclonic pre-filter to handle the heavy particulate load. The air smells of ozone and hot stone—this is the scent of ionized dust and friction; your lungs are not designed for it.

**2. Vision and Light Management**
There are no shadows here; the light is sourceless and diffuse, creating a flat, glare-filled environment that destroys depth perception.
*   **Polarized Visors:** The light is blinding. Wear a full-face visor with variable polarization. You will need to adjust it constantly as you move from open plazas to the slightly dimmer resonant nodes where the flora glows.
*   **Bioluminescence Filters:** The crystalline plants emit a faint, rhythmic pulse. While beautiful, this shifting light can cause optical fatigue. A filter that selectively dampens the specific wavelengths of the flora’s bioluminescence will help your eyes relax.

**3. Thermal Regulation**
The heat is dry and constant, radiating from the stone and the air itself, not from a sun.
*   **Evaporative Cooling:** Since there is no water, traditional cooling vests that rely on water evaporation are futile. You need a suit with a phase-change material (PCM) that absorbs heat as it transitions from solid to liquid, or a radiant barrier suit that reflects the ambient thermal energy. Avoid dark colors; the pale, ochre dust reflects heat, but the stone structures absorb it.
*   **Hydration:** You must carry all your water. The "river" of dust is an illusion of moisture. Drink electrolyte-rich fluids frequently; the dry air will dehydrate you faster than you realize.

**4. Locomotion and Stability**
The ground is a paradox: solid in most places, liquefying under sustained vibration.
*   **Footwear:** Standard boots will sink or vibrate uncomfortably. You need wide, flat-soled shoes with dampening inserts that isolate your feet from the ground’s resonance. The soles should be made of a soft, compliant material that floats on the dust rather than digging in.
*   **Movement Rhythm:** Learn to move with the sound. The dust liquefies when a specific frequency hits. Watch the local fauna—they move in the quiet pauses. Time your steps between the sonic peaks. Do not run; you will trigger a dust flow.
