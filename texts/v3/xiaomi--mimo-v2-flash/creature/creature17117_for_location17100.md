The inhabitants of the Echinate Reach are a synesthetic choir, beings of fiber and fluid whose existence is inseparable from the architecture they inhabit. They are not a separate civilization building atop the plateau, but rather the current iteration of the plateau’s consciousness—biological custodians of the ancient plant-mind.

They are tall, willowy creatures, standing between seven and eight feet, their bodies composed of the same vascular weave as the towers they inhabit. Their skin is not flesh but a flexible, semi-translucent lattice of cellulose fibers, through which a thick, sap-like fluid circulates visibly. This fluid glows with a faint, rhythmic bioluminescence, pulsing in tandem with the low-frequency hum of the towers. They do not wear clothes; their bodies are adorned with symbiotic mosses and crystalline growths that shift in color and texture with their emotional states. Their limbs are elongated and multi-jointed, capable of torsion and extension that would dislocate human bones, allowing them to climb the sheer faces of the woven towers or grip the tendril bridges with equal ease.

Their heads are narrow and featureless at first glance, lacking traditional eyes, noses, or mouths. Instead, their faces are a complex array of sensory organs: delicate, fern-like filaments that tremble in the air, tasting the mineral scent of deep moisture; smooth, resonant patches that vibrate with the sonic landscape; and clusters of light-sensitive nodules that perceive the twilight not as brightness but as gradients of polarization. They "see" the world as a composite of sound, vibration, and chemical flow. Communication is a layered symphony: subsonic rumbles transmitted through the stone-wood via their footfalls, high-frequency clicks for precision, and shifts in their own internal bioluminescence to convey emotional nuance. They do not speak; they resonate.

They are neither male nor female in a biological sense, but reproduce through a communal process of budding and genetic recombination. A new individual emerges as a growth from the shared root-mat of the settlement, its form guided by the collective chemical signals of the community. Their lifespans are measured in centuries, and they do not die in the human sense; when a Reach-dweller fails, their body is reabsorbed into the architectural organism, its nutrients and memories integrated into the living structure. They are transient nodes in a permanent network.

Their culture is one of maintenance and attunement. They do not farm or hunt; the ecosystem provides. The six-legged grazers with crystalline carapaces are not livestock but partners—their ticking limbs aerate the stone-wood, and their shed exoskeletons are harvested for their silica content, used to reinforce the towers. The flying seed-pods are messengers, carrying chemical packets between clusters of the settlement. The predators are regulators, culling overpopulation with silent efficiency. The dwellers tend to the health of the whole, pruning overgrowth, strengthening stressed tendons, and guiding the slow evolution of the architecture through intentional genetic drift.

Their minds are not isolated. A constant, low-level telepathy exists through the fungal networks woven into the stone-wood—shared dreams, memories, and instincts flow like groundwater. They are individuals, yet their consciousness is subtly collective, a hive-mind softened by autonomy. They know the plateau’s history not as stories but as inherited sensations; they feel the ancient erosion in their bones, the billion-year patience of the landscape as a personal trait.

They are silent guardians of a dead world’s skeleton, awake in the twilight, humming in harmony with the vast, dreaming mind beneath their feet.
