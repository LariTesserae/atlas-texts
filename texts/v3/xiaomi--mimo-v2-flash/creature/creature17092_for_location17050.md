Living in Venkarr is to inhabit the pause between the hammer strike and the echo. The inhabitants are a vertical people, their musculature honed by the constant climb and their equilibrium ironclad, for to live here is to trust one's weight to bolts driven into living rock and bridges that sway over abysses lost in mist.

There are the **Foundry-Wrights**, faces perpetually smudged with soot and volcanic ash, their skin ruddy from the radiating heat of the forges burrowed deep into the mountain. They work in the glowing bellies of the cliffs, their bodies slick with a mix of sweat and the perpetual dampness of the stone. They are deaf in one ear from the roar of the furnaces, yet they navigate the labyrinthine interior tunnels by touch and by the vibration of the stone underfoot. They speak little, communicating in a shorthand of hand gestures and the clang of hammer on anvil, their language rhythmic and percussive, echoing the machinery that surrounds them.

There are the **Spire-Walkers**, the couriers and maintenance crews who traverse the external lattice. They are lithe and wiry, clad in heavy leather and rubberized canvas to shed the mineral sweat of the cliffs. They move with a deliberate, pendulum gait, accustomed to the sway of cable cars and the narrowness of iron gantries. Their boots are fitted with cleats that grind against the basalt, and they carry coils of rope and pneumatic canisters, bridging the gaps between the city's isolated clusters. To them, the vertical drop is not a threat but a neighbor; they lean out over the voids with casual indifference, their eyes scanning the mists for the flicker of signal lamps.

Then there are the **Geode-Seers**, a strange sect of engineers and mystics who monitor the mountain’s growth. They are pale, with eyes adapted to the dim, violet light, and they often have a faint tremor in their hands—a side effect, perhaps, of living in a place where the subsonic hum of the stone resonates in the bones. They carry tuning forks and resonant plates, pressing them against the cliff faces to listen to the slow grinding of new crystals forming within. They are the ones who read the language of the fissures, predicting where the mountain will bud next, and they paint the raw, unweathered rock with sigils to mark the safe zones for construction.

The fauna of Venkarr is as much a part of the population as the people. The **Six-Legged Climb-Goats** roam the ledges freely, their hooked hooves clicking against the stone as they graze on mineral-rich lichen. They are domesticated in a loose sense; the Spire-Walkers leave out salt licks and bundles of dried moss for them, and in return, the goats serve as living barometers, their agitation often preceding a shift in the atmospheric pressure or a new fissure opening. The **Slate-Moths** are everywhere, their wings indistinguishable from the wet stone when they land, fluttering in silent clouds around the exhaust vents where the air is warmest. And in the shallow, mirror-still pools on the wide ledges, **Luminous Finns** swim in tight circles. These translucent fish emit a faint, internal bioluminescence that pulses in sync with the industrial drone, and they are harvested for the light they provide; a jar of Finns hangs in every doorway, casting a cold, blue glow upon the wet stone.

But there are also the **Static-Born**, the weirder residents who exist at the edge of perception. They are not quite solid, their forms sketched in temporary lines of static that ripple in the damp air. They are felt before they are seen—a pressure against the temples, a vibration in the teeth—and they are accepted as naturally as the drip of water from an overhang. The inhabitants of Venkarr do not fear the reality breakdown; they have learned to step around the flickering mirages of nonexistent towers and to ignore the lag between a footstep and its sound. They are a patient people, existing in a state of permanent becoming, their lives woven into the industrial tapestry of a mountain that is still deciding what it wants to be.
