I am a **Spindle-Singer**, one of the quiet majority who dwell within the hollow boles and spiraling balconies of this submerged cellulose metropolis. I am not a creature of frantic energy; I am a being of suspension. My skin is pale, almost translucent, mapped with fine, blue veins that carry blood cooled to the exact temperature of the surrounding water. My eyes are large and lidless, adapted to the perpetual twilight, their irises shimmering with a faint, pearlescent sheen that mimics the phosphorescent algae clinging to the walls.

**Physiology and Attire**
I do not walk so much as drift with deliberate, suspended motion. My body is slight, built for navigating the narrow, spiraling passages of the reed-towers. My limbs are long and dexterous, ending in fingers webbed with a thin, translucent membrane that allows me to manipulate fibers and weave nets with the grace of a sea anemone expanding its tentacles.

I wear no woven cloth. Instead, I am draped in the harvested skins of buoyant kelp, dried and treated until they become soft and flexible as leather. The garments are layered and frayed at the edges, mimicking the weathered architecture around me. A mantle of fine, hair-like filaments drapes from my shoulders, catching the drifting spores and glowing faintly in the dim light, making me appear as a shifting column of shadow and bioluminescence. My hair is not hair at all, but a cascade of long, stiff fibers, similar to the stamens of a deep-sea flower, which sway with the current and help me sense minute changes in water pressure.

**The Tools of the Spindle-Singer**
I carry a **Resonance Staff**, a hollow reed harvested from the highest towers, capped with a polished, fossilized shell. This is not a weapon, but an instrument of perception and communication. By tapping it against the plant-pillars, I can interpret the health of the city through the vibrations that travel through the fibrous substrate. A sharp, brittle tone indicates a section of root that is drying and decaying; a deep, resonant thrum means the architecture is sound, growing, and alive.

In a pouch made of woven spider-silk and kelp, I carry:
- **Obsidian flakes**, rare and precious, used for precise cuts when harvesting resin or carving memory-stones.
- **Calcified algae chisels**, used for shaping the softer plant materials.
- **A vial of bioluminescent spores**, harvested from the underside of the floating lily pads. I mix this with resin to create a slow-burning, phosphorescent paste that I use to mark the passing of seasons on the walls of my chamber.

**Daily Existence**
My day is not measured by sun or moon, but by the **Pulse of the City**. The phosphorescent light in the canopy above does not flicker randomly; it breathes. When it glows brightest, I ascend to the upper chambers to tend the floating gardens, pruning the massive lily pads and collecting the oxygen-rich bubbles that cling to their undersides. I move slowly, deliberately, my motions synchronized with the current. Haste is a foreign concept; to rush is to waste energy and disrupt the delicate balance of the ecosystem.

When the light dims, I descend to the lower roots, where the elders dwell in the near-total darkness. Here, the air is thick with the scent of ancient resin and preserved seeds. I listen to the stories carved into the plant-pillars, tracing the rings with my fingertips. The history of my people is not written on paper, but etched into the living architecture. A spiral might represent a generation; a branching frond might mark a great migration or a bloom of the rare, deep-sea flower that opens once every hundred years.

**The Spindle-Song**
I am called a Spindle-Singer because of the sound I make. By rubbing my fingers along the taut, braided vines that connect the towers, I can produce a low, resonant hum that travels far through the water. This is not music for entertainment; it is a language of vibration. A steady, rhythmic hum signals safety and abundance. A sharp, staccato pattern warns of a section of root that is collapsing or a predator drifting too close. A soft, wavering tone might be a lament for a section of the city that has finally succumbed to decay, returning its cellulose to the substrate.

**The Boundary Between Self and City**
I do not see the city as separate from myself. The plant-pillars are my bones; the flowing water is my blood; the phosphorescent light is my breath. When I sleep, I curl into a bulbous chamber grown from the heart of a massive reed, and I feel the gentle sway of the structure as it responds to the deep currents. Sometimes, in the twilight between sleep and waking, I feel the walls breathe with me, and I know that I am not just a inhabitant—I am a part of the city's own living, dreaming consciousness.

In this cool, submerged world of suspended decay, I am a keeper of memory, a singer of vibrations, and a slow, patient cell in the vast, breathing body of the plant-city.
