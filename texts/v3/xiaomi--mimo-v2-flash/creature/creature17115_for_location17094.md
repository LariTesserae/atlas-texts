The things that live here are not alive in the way we know it. They are not fueled by the sun, nor by the rot of organic matter. They are alive in the way a glacier is alive: slow, inevitable, and grinding.

The basin is a kingdom of the **Rimewalkers**. They are the tide of this world. Thousands of them, moving in unison across the pocked, fused floor. They look like fragments of a shattered cathedral—hexagonal plates of polished obsidian and dark iron, interlocking to form a low, domed carapace. Underneath, a hundred tiny legs of crystalline silica click and scrape, a sound like wind through a frozen reed bed. They do not look up; they do not look down. They graze the surface, their bodies absorbing the microscopic film of condensation and the electric charge that hums in the fungal veins beneath the stone. They are the grazers of the static charge, the eaters of the mist. When the Shriekers cry out, the Rimewalkers freeze, their plates vibrating in resonance, becoming one with the stone, before they surge forward again in the falling silence.

Larger and more terrible are the **Lithovores**, the anchored giants. They are not mobile; they are geological accidents that have woken up. They resemble boulders of granite, but their surfaces are porous, riddled with holes that look like the open mouths of caves. From these holes, and from the rough skin of their "bodies," whip-thin filaments of metallic hyphae extend—muscles of crystal. They do not chase prey; they create a current. The filaments thrash slowly, agitating the damp air, pulling mist and drifting spores into their central cavities. They are the basins’ lungs. Smaller creatures—rust-colored mites of raw magnetite and fleets of glass-shelled amoebas—get caught in this draft and are drawn into the dark, dissolving mouths. The Lithovores are sessile predators, stationary whirlpools in a world of slow motion.

Then there are the **Shriekers**, the tyrants of the acoustic space. They are not creatures of flesh or even fully of mineral, but of resonant geometry. They appear as clusters of elongated, needle-like crystals, growing in jagged formations from the basin floor. They look like black, splintered trees. They are the heart of the ecosystem, the metronome. They vibrate with the internal heat of the planet, emitting a sound that starts as a subsonic thrum you feel in your marrow, escalates to a deafening, glass-shattering frequency that ripples the surface of the pooled water, and then fades again. The Rimewalkers time their migrations by this scream; the Lithovores filter the particulate matter shaken loose by the vibration. The Shriekers are not sentient, yet they command the behavior of every other entity in the basin.

Scattered among the giants are the **Drip-Feeders**. These are delicate, chandelier-like structures of silica gel and branching crystal that hang from the sagging underbellies of the overhangs. They are stationary, dangling like frozen stalactites, but they are alive. Their tips are sticky with a viscous, non-evaporating fluid that catches the metronomic drops of condensation from the stone above. As the droplet hits, the crystalline lattice contracts, pulling the water inward, filtering minerals from it before expelling a purified vapor back into the mist. They are the kidneys of the cavern, processing the endless dampness.

Finally, there is the **Grazer-Mist**, the lowest order of life. It is not a single animal, but a suspension—millions of microscopic, airborne spores and glass-winged motes that drift in the stagnant air. They are visible only as a faint, shifting haze, catching the dim, internal luminescence of the fungal veins. They feed on the ozone released by the Shriekers and the heat radiating from the Lithovores. They are the atmosphere made animate, a living fog that coats the Rimewalkers and settles in the hollows of the basin.

There is no hierarchy here in the biological sense, only a circuit. The Shriekers provide the rhythm and the kinetic energy; the Lithovores filter the particulate matter and harbor the smaller life; the Rimewalkers scour the surface and stir the dust; the Drip-Feeders regulate the moisture; the Mist floats between them all, the medium of exchange.

They do not fight. They do not flee. They simply *are*, a dense, deafening, damp ecology of stone and vibration, existing in a state of perfect, heavy equilibrium.
