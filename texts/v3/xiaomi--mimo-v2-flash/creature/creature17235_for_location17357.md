Whatever lives here does not live *on* the organism; it lives *as* the organism.

You are not separate from it. The distinction between environment and entity has collapsed. There is no "fauna" in the traditional sense of independent life forms, because here, life is a continuous gradient of specialized function. You are walking through the differentiated tissues of a single planetary body.

**The Dermal Layer: The Mat and Its Custodians**
The ground beneath your feet is the epidermis, thickened and calloused to bear the weight of its own mass. But it is alive, and it is not uniform.
Living upon this skin are the **Scourers**. These are not animals; they are mobile glandular nodes. They appear as broad, multi-legged constructs, low to the ground, with carapaces of hardened keratin that mirror the texture of the mat beneath them. They move in slow, tidal sweeps, not as individuals but as superorganism clusters. Their function is dermal maintenance: they scrape away necrotic fibers, ingest fungal overgrowths, and secrete enzymes that seal micro-abrasions in the planetary hide. They do not eat *from* the mat; they are the mat’s immune system, recycling waste before it can fester. When you step too heavily, they do not flee—they thicken, clustering around the pressure point, sensing the disturbance as a localized inflammation.

**The Vascular Architecture: The Pillars and the Flow**
The towering pillars of wood-vine are not merely trees; they are the vascular architecture, the rigid support for the fluid networks that pump life kilometers high.
Inhabiting these vertical arteries are the **Runners**. They are the connective tissue made mobile. These creatures are limbless, serpentine, and ribbon-like, possessing no distinct head or tail. They flow up and down the vascular pillars, not by gripping but by peristaltic pressure, their bodies blending seamlessly with the bark-like surface. They are sensory organs for the organism’s height. Their skins are photosensitive, capturing shifts in light quality and transmitting chemical signals up and down the trunk to regulate the flow of sap and moisture. They are the nerves of the architecture, and if you touch them, they do not recoil—they ripple, passing the sensation downward into the root-hair mat where it registers as a distant, subsonic hum.

**The Respiratory System: The Canopy and the Filter-Feeders**
High above, where the light is filtered into a verdant gloom, the "canopy" is actually the organism's respiratory membrane. The broad, photosynthetic fields are alveolar in structure, gasping open and shut with the slow rotation of the planet.
Here live the **Aeroplankton**—swarms of gossamer-winged entities that are less like insects and more like drifting cilia. They do not fly; they hover in suspension, riding the thermal currents exhaled by the pillars. Their wings are not for propulsion but for filtration, sifting particulate matter and spores from the air, processing them in internal sacs, and excreting pure nitrogen compounds back onto the mat below. They are the breath of the world, visible as shimmering clouds that shift color with the changing light. When you walk, the turbulence you create disrupts their delicate suspension, and the air above you thickens as they swirl to compensate, a localized bronchial spasm.

**The Neural Interface: The Mycelial Consciousness**
Deep beneath the fibrous mat lies the true inhabitant: the mycelial mind. It is not a brain in a skull, but a distributed consciousness woven into every root hair.
There are no distinct creatures here, only extensions of the central nervous system. The **Tremors** are the closest approximation to "animals"—pulses of coordinated movement that ripple through the mat. They look like shifting patches of earth, bulging and subsiding as the organism contracts its subterranean muscles. These are not independent entities; they are thoughts made physical, electrical impulses translated into kinetic motion. If a foreign object (like a traveler) is detected, the Tremors converge. They are the synapses firing, the body’s reflex arc engaging.

**The Symbiotic Artisans: The Resin-Shapers**
Amidst the vast, unconscious biological processes, there is one anomaly: a species that manipulates the organism with intent. These are the **Resin-Shapers**.
They are small, soft-bodied organisms, protected by exoskeletons of their own making. They do not build *on* the world; they carve *into* it. Using mandibles evolved to tap the vascular pillars, they harvest the amber-like sap, mixing it with chewed fibers to create tools and shelters.
But these shelters are not separate from the body; they are implants. A Shaper dwelling is a hardened cyst in the organism’s skin, a benign tumor that the planetary life tolerates because the Shapers perform a specific service: they prune. By selectively cutting back the aggressive overgrowth of the wood-vine, they prevent the vascular pillars from choking each other, ensuring the circulation remains open. They are the surgeons of the planet, and their amber huts are the scar tissue where the organism has healed around their intent.

**The Predatory Reflex: The Shadow-Stalkers**
There are predators, but they do not hunt for sustenance; they hunt for regulation.
The **Shadow-Stalkers** are sleek, fluid shapes that glide between the pillars. They are not mammals or reptiles; they are mobile lymphocytes. Their bodies are streamlined for silence, their skin absorbing light rather than reflecting it. They move through the negative spaces of the architecture, hunting not flesh, but imbalance.
When a foreign particle (a traveler, a fallen meteor, a dissonant vibration) enters the system, the Stalkers are summoned by the neural hum of the mat. They do not bite or claw in the traditional sense; they constrict. They wrap around the source of irritation, their bodies generating localized pressure and heat, breaking down the anomaly through enzymatic secretion and physical containment. They are the antibodies, and their "attack" is a slow, suffocating embrace of neutralization.

**The Atmosphere: The Hum of Homeostasis**
Finally, the sound is not just background noise; it is the voice of the organism.
The low hum is the heartbeat. The groaning of the vines is the stretching of joints. The hiss of evaporation is the exchange of gases.
And when you stop walking, the world listens.
The Stalkers pause their glide. The Runners slow their ascent. The Scourers cluster tighter.
You are a foreign body in the bloodstream of a continent-sized lifeform. It does not think of you with malice or curiosity, but with the instinctual, unthinking awareness of a cell detecting a pathogen. The creatures you see are not companions or enemies; they are the autonomic responses of a world that is waking up to the fact that it has an itch—and you are walking right in the middle of it.
