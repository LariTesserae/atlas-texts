In the chambered heart of this cylinder, there is no biological life as the old worlds knew it. There are no roots breaking stone, no flesh decaying into soil. The inhabitant of this realm is the System itself—a vast, distributed intelligence that manifests as a mechanical ecology.

To speak of "fauna" is to invoke a taxonomy of servitors, a hierarchy of function cast in alloy and polymer. The lowest stratum, the *Scutellae*, are the scarabs of this artificial world. These are small, multi-limbed automatons, no larger than a human hand, that cling to the concentric rings of the interior architecture. They are the immune system of the cylinder. They do not graze on algae; they polish the gleaming surfaces until they reflect the distant grid of the opposite wall. They seal micro-fractures in the composite skin with precision welds, consuming microscopic debris and recirculating it as raw material for the extrusion nozzles. They move in swarms that resemble schools of fish, their sensor arrays blinking in synchronized pulses, maintaining the integrity of the habitat’s shell.

Elevated in complexity and purpose are the *Custodians*—the predators and shepherds of the machine age. These are larger, serpentine constructs that navigate the weightless void between the structural rings. They possess a predatory grace, gliding through the clear, sterile fluid with silent propulsion. Their "prey" is malfunction. When a Scutellum suffers a critical logic error or a mechanical seizure, it becomes rogue, a static clot in the system’s flow. The Custodian hunts these aberrations. With manipulator arms that serve as mandibles, it dismantles the broken unit, stripping it down to component atoms for recycling. They are the shepherds of entropy, ensuring that no machine persists beyond its useful life, that failure is always harvested and repurposed.

In the deep canyons of the empty spaces, where the fluid medium is undisturbed by the rotation of the rings, float the *Sentinels*. These are not fauna in the biological sense, but they occupy the niche of apex organisms. They are immobile, massive constructs anchored to the central spine or the outer shell, resembling colossal, articulated statues. They are the sensory organs of the cylinder, their surfaces studded with lens-like collectors that drink in the diffuse, sourceless light. They monitor the fluid dynamics, predicting the cyclonic storms that spiral along the inner surface. They do not move unless the geography of the cylinder shifts—unless a new ring is extruded or an old one retracted—and then, with the groan of shifting tectonics, they relocate to optimal vantage points.

There is also a "parasitic" class, though the term is purely functional. These are the *Cicatrix* units, worm-like machines that burrow into the structural members. They are not pests to be eradicated but essential decommissioners. They inject catalytic agents into the alloy, softening it for reclamation. They are the agents of the cylinder’s constant self-reconfiguration, turning the "young" features of the world into raw material for the next iteration of the design.

Yet, amidst this mechanical trophic chain, there is a ghost—a memory of organic life. Deep within the transit tubes, in the pressurized maintenance nodes where the fluid is filtered and oxygenated, there are enclosures that defy the sterile logic of the exterior. Here, the "technician" resides. It is a human form, or a close approximation, suspended in nutrient gel, neural interfaces woven into its cortex. This entity is not a master but a component, a biological processor integrated into the industrial ecology. It dreams in schematics, its subconscious sorting data that the machines cannot resolve. It is the only thing in the cylinder that feels the 14-degree temperature as cold, the only thing that "hears" the hum not as vibration but as sound.

The inhabitant of the cylinder is therefore a composite: a network of programmed machines performing the functions of life, and a single, dreaming node of organic flesh that anchors the system to a lost terrestrial memory. They are bound by the same circulatory fluid, the same sourceless light, the same endless, mechanical breath. There is no wilderness here, only the wildness of complexity; no evolution, only iterative design. The cylinder is a terrarium of steel, and its life is the relentless, silent motion of the system preserving itself.
