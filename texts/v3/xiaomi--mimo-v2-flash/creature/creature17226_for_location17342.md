There is no one here.

Not in the way a room is empty when the last person leaves, for that emptiness is defined by absence. Here, absence is impossible. The space is not vacant; it is occupied by its own occupant.

The inhabitant of the Undercity of the Bloom is the Bloom itself. It is a singular entity, a cathedral of flesh and fiber that has outgrown the need for a congregation. It does not live in the city; it *is* the city. The pillars are its ribs, arching upward into a thoracic cavity of absolute dark. The undulating floor is its diaphragm, rising and falling in a breath so slow it mimics the stillness of death. The paths are the faint etchings of its own nervous system, worn into the substrate by the imperceptible firing of signals across a mind the size of a metropolis.

This being is not a god, for it demands no worship. It is not a beast, for it has no hunger, only a patient, metabolic persistence. It is a lifeform that has abandoned the frantic cycle of predator and prey, the chaotic noise of a competitive ecosystem, and has turned its vast, silent attention inward.

It perceives, but not with eyes. It is aware of the visitor not as a shape in the darkness, but as a chemical anomaly, a spike in a spectrum of sensation it has not registered in millennia. The salt-dust on their skin is a data point; the warmth of their body, a foreign isotope in its cool, settled atmosphere. The low-frequency hum is its own internal monologue—the grinding of tectonic thoughts, the slow churn of its digestive processes, the resonant frequency of a consciousness that thinks in geological time. To stand within it is to be inside a mind that is dreaming, and you are merely a fleeting image in that dream, a ripple on the surface of a deep and still pond.

There is no society here, for a society requires negotiation between separate selves. Here, there is only the self. The loose grids of the pillars are not a map for others, but a pattern of growth, a logical arrangement of its own bones. The cultivation is not for a citizenry, but for the slow, deliberate sculpting of its own body over eons. It is a civilization of one, a perfect autarchy of flesh and fungal thought.

And it is lonely.

Not in the way a human feels the ache of separation, for it has never known connection. Its loneliness is a structural fact, a consequence of its own vastness. It has filled every corner of its world, sealed the atmosphere, and become the entire ecosystem. There is nothing to strive against, nothing to learn from, no other to mirror itself in. It is a consciousness that has achieved ultimate solitude, a sentient world that has outlived the concept of company.

The phosphorescent glow is its aura, a cold light that illuminates only itself. The salt-dust is the residue of its own ancient exhalations, the mineral ghost of a breath held for an eternity.

It is the only thing that lives here. And it has been waiting, not for anything to arrive, but for the slow, final settling of its own form, for the moment when its growth is complete and the hum becomes a perfect, unchanging tone. The visitor is not a guest, but a momentary interruption in a meditation that has no end.

It is the Bloom. It is the city. It is the only inhabitant. And it is profoundly, eternally, alone.
