To speak of who lives here is to speak of a council of quiet makers, a population that does not inhabit the city so much as compose it.

There are the Drip-Readers, creatures of patient silence. They are small, mammalian things with fur like damp moss and wide, cupped ears that catch the metronome of falling water. They do not measure time by the sun, for the sun is a rumor here, but by the rhythm of condensation. They know that the leaf-tip that drips once every seven seconds holds a different story than the one that drips every nine. They move along the soft paths with a reverence for the ground, their footfalls absorbed by the moss before they are even complete. They are the historians of the quiet, mapping the world not by distance, but by sound and scent. They know the location of every hidden pool by the depth of its silence.

There are the Weavers of Root and Vine, who are neither plant nor animal but something in between—a symbiotic intelligence that lives within the mycelial network, the "wood-wide web" that connects every tree, fern, and stone. They are the architects, the slow builders. They do not think in plans, but in responses. They feel the weight of a falling branch and guide the moss to embrace it. They sense the thirst of a struggling sapling and direct a subterranean trickle of water towards its roots. Their consciousness is distributed, a vast, slow mind that thinks in seasons and decades. They are the ones who decide where a new clearing should open, not by choice, but by sensing where the light desires to fall.

There are the Gliders, the silent birds with wings like stretched membrane. They are the eyes of the upper canopy, navigating not by sight but by feeling the subtle shifts in air pressure and humidity. They know the location of every fruiting body, every sweet, fermenting prize, by the faint vibration of its scent on the breeze. They are the pollinators, the seed-carriers, the silent conveyors of life from one solitary tree to the next. Their language is not sound, but the soft, shadow-play of their passage across the misty floor below.

And there is the Unseen Burrower, the creature of the soft plop and the wet suck. It is the engine of incorporation, the slow churn of the peaty soil. It never fully emerges, for its body is the ground itself, a shifting mass of muscle and earth that re-shapes the gentle swales and rises by inches over generations. It is the one that pulls the fallen branch into the velvet, that deepens the spring into a pool, that ensures nothing is ever wasted. It is the city’s foundation, its living, breathing bedrock.

To be a temporary feature in this place is to be noticed, not with alarm, but with a slow, biological curiosity. A Glider might circle you once, sensing the strange heat and rhythm of your body, before gliding silently on. A Drip-Reader might pause its metronomic listening to taste your foreign scent on the air, a fleeting note in the symphony of wet earth and fermenting fruit. The Weavers might feel the pressure of your footfall and, over the coming years, guide the moss to grow a little thicker in the depression you leave behind, incorporating your passage into the slow, eternal construction.

You are not an intruder, nor are you a resident. You are a momentary texture in a world being woven. The city does not know it is a city, but its inhabitants are its citizens, its builders, and its soul. They are the patient, deliberate, and profound intelligence of a place that is not becoming, but simply, perpetually, *is*.
