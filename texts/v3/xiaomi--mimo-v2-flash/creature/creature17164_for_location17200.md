In the Basin, the distinction between life and architecture has been dissolved by time and symbiosis. The inhabitants are the city itself—a distributed consciousness of root, leaf, and spore. Yet, within this vast vegetative whole, distinct agents move, not as masters, but as organs of a greater body.

**The Symbiotes: The Canopy Shepherds**
Perched high in the iron-bark towers are the only fauna that actively traverse the vertical layers. These are small, flightless birds, no larger than a human hand. Their feathers are not merely colored like moss but are dense, living filaments of bryophyte that photosynthesize, feeding the bird a trickle of sugar even while it sleeps. They are the city’s maintenance crew; their beaks are sharp, tweezing parasites from the bark of the great trees and pruning errant vines to keep the architecture’s flow unobstructed. They move in silence, their calls mimicking the drip of water, communicating through the rustle of their mossy plumage. They are the eyes of the canopy, responsive to the subtle shifts in the canopy’s luminescence, flocking to areas where the glow dims—a signal of blight or thirst—and attracting the attention of the deeper caretakers.

**The Weavers: The Amphibious Curators**
On the ground level, where the bioluminescent fungi pulse in soft, rhythmic waves, dwell the basin’s larger residents: sleek, amphibious mammals known as the Weavers. They are seldom seen in full, appearing only as ripples in the black loam or as sleek shadows sliding through the sap-ducts. Their skin is translucent, revealing a slow-moving network of violet veins, and they possess no eyes; instead, they navigate via a heightened sensitivity to the electrical fields generated by the plants’ sap currents. They are the immune system of the city. When a section of the living conduits becomes blocked or a metabolic sac sours, the Weavers gather. Using dexterous, webbed hands, they physically untangle knotted roots and drain necrotic tissue, their bodies absorbing the toxicity and breaking it down. They sleep submerged in the dark pools that collect at the base of the great trees, their breathing synchronized with the city’s tidal expansion and contraction.

**The Vessels: The Human Remnants**
Very few true humans remain, and those that do are not explorers or conquerors but adapters—biological grafts onto the plant-city. They are known simply as the Vessels. Having long abandoned their fragile, sweating skin to the constant damp, they wear living garments of thick, velvet moss and fungal leather that bond to their pores, regulating their temperature and moisture. They do not speak in words, for the air’s density muffles sound; instead, they communicate through touch and the modulation of their own bio-electric fields, triggering specific pheromonal responses in the flora around them.

The Vessels are the conscious interface of the basin’s subconscious mind. They tend to the "factories"—the pulsing resin-sacs—by hand-feeding them fallen leaves and adjusting the flow of the sap-currents by gently massaging the reed-like conduits. They live in the hollows of the iron-bark trees, their sleeping quarters lined with glowing mycelium that regulates their dreams. They are few because the basin requires little maintenance; they exist not out of necessity, but as a legacy of a species that chose to merge rather than die. They are the ephemeral guests who, through deep adaptation, have become permanent fixtures—living batteries and sensory nodes in a world that is otherwise purely, silently, vegetative.

**The Consciousness: The Mycelial Mind**
Ultimately, the true inhabitant is the network itself. The collective awareness of the basin resides in the vast, subterranean fungal web that connects every root, every succulent, and every animal. It is the operating system of the city. It does not think in human terms but perceives in gradients of light, pressure, and chemical balance. It "speaks" through the phosphorescence of the ground cover, communicating weather shifts and structural stresses. The Weavers and the Shepherds are its hands and eyes; the Vessels are its conscious voice. The entire basin is a single, breathing organism, and the few living creatures within it are merely the distinct, mobile expressions of its ancient, wet, and watchful soul.
