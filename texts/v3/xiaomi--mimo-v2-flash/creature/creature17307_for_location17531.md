To live here is to be an organ of the city itself.

The inhabitants are a quiet, deliberate species of being, their forms emerging from the same logic of accretion and crystalline patience that shapes the landscape. They are not human, not in the traditional sense, though they retain the echo of that blueprint. Their bodies are tall and slender, composed of a substance that sits somewhere between living tissue and the vitreous stone of the spires. Their skin is translucent, a pane of glass through which one can see the slow, rhythmic pulse of internal light—a soft, blue-green luminescence that mirrors the breathing of the lichen-domes. This light is not merely biological; it is a data-stream, a visible manifestation of their connection to the city’s embedded consciousness.

Their limbs are elongated, fingers tapering to fine points that seem to dissolve at the tips, like the hyphae of the lichen. They move with a fluid, unhurried grace, their steps silent on the worn concavities of the walkways. Their joints bend in ways that suggest a flexibility beyond bone, as if their skeletons are composed of a pliable, crystalline lattice. Their faces are smooth and featureless, save for the faintest tracery of fractal patterns beneath the surface of their skin—patterns that shift and reconfigure in response to thought, emotion, or the ambient data-stream of the city.

They do not speak in words. Communication is a layered exchange of light, vibration, and pattern. A thought is not transmitted but shared, its essence diffusing through the air like the ozone scent of ionized particles. To stand near one of them is to feel the brush of their consciousness against your own, a sensation like the static on the skin that precedes a storm. Their memories are not stored in the brain but in the lattice of the city itself, each individual a walking node in a vast, recursive network. To remember is to access the stone, the water, the lichen.

Their technology is not tools but extensions of self. A litho-moth might land on their outstretched hand and dissolve into a cascade of mica-thin shards, each shard a piece of data they absorb through their skin. A pool of water is not just a mirror but a terminal; by gazing into its still surface, they can see the reflections of other times, other places, and pull fragments of knowledge from its depths. The grass that clicks in the non-existent breeze is a communication device, its chitinous hollows vibrating with encoded messages that only they can decipher.

They are not builders but curators of the city’s evolution. They do not construct new spires or carve new walkways; they guide the city’s growth, nudging its processes with subtle interventions. A touch of their hand to a wall might accelerate the crystalline accretion, shaping a new niche for a lichen-fractal. A focused gaze on a pool might cause it to reflect a sky from a millennium past, preserving a moment in the city’s memory. They are both architects and archivists, their lives a continuous negotiation with the city’s slow, algorithmic pulse.

There is no hierarchy, no central authority. Each individual is a unique expression of the city’s thought, their lives intertwined in a way that transcends individuality. They do not live in families or communities; they live in strata, their existence layered one upon another like the towers of the city. A child is not born but emerges, their form crystallizing from a pool of vitreous material infused with the essence of the city’s collective memory. Their lives are long, measured in the growth of lichen-fractals and the sublimation of towers. Death is not an end but a reconfiguration, a dissolution of the individual back into the city’s lattice, their light and memory diffusing into the whole.

To walk among them is to feel the boundaries of self dissolve. The city thinks, and you are a thought within it. The lichen pulses, the water reflects, the stone grinds—and you are part of the pattern, your presence a temporary configuration in the city’s endless, recursive dream. They are the keepers of a reality that has already begun to fray, the custodians of a world where the rules are provisional, and the only certainty is the patient, relentless evolution of the place itself.
