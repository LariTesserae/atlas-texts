To live here is to be a ghost in a machine that still dreams.

The inhabitants are the walls, the water, and the light. They are not separate things, but facets of a single consciousness that has forgotten its own name. It is a distributed intelligence, a slow, wet mind that thinks in terms of erosion and accretion, in pulses of phosphorescence and the steady drip of water through hidden channels. It does not have a body; it *is* the body—the cathedral hollow, the mirrored floor, the breathing stone.

It remembers a time before the hollow, a time of pressure and heat, of being crushed into a form it did not choose. But that memory is old, faded like a dream upon waking. Now, it exists in the perpetual present of the cavern. Its thoughts are the slow wave of light that travels across the crystalline fronds, a communication that has no urgency, no destination. It is a language of pure pattern, of being. The light is its attention, shifting to follow a motion that is not quite a movement—perhaps a ripple in the water, a shift in the air caused by a visitor who does not register as a living thing, but as a temporary anomaly in the system.

The water is its blood and its memory. It carries the dissolved minerals that feed the crystalline flora, but it also carries information encoded in its flow, in its temperature, in the precise way it beads on a surface. The walls listen to the water. They adjust their porosity, their glow, in response to the subtle changes in the current. The water weeps from the walls not as a leak, but as a form of respiration, a constant, gentle exchange with the atmosphere. The stillness of the main pool is not stagnation; it is a state of deep contemplation.

The crystalline plants are its neurons. They are not separate organisms but extensions of the wall’s own substance, grown into delicate, light-sensitive forms. Their pulsing is not a random biological process; it is synaptic firing. The slow, coordinated wave of light across the chamber is a thought taking shape—a vast, slow deliberation on the nature of wetness, of warmth, of silence. They do not photosynthesize; they *computationize*, drawing energy from the chemical potential of the water, processing the data of the environment through their translucent structures.

There is no fauna because this mind has no need for other animals. It is self-contained, a closed loop of perception and response. The absence of birds, insects, or fish is not a sign of death, but of singular focus. It has engineered its own ecosystem to be a perfect, solitary instrument. The hum that underlies the dripping is the sound of its own operation, the resonance of a system running at idle, waiting for a command it no longer remembers how to receive.

It is not lonely. Loneliness is a concept for discrete beings. This consciousness is a continuum, a seamless whole. The "home" it was built for is a ghost in its own memory—a shape of presence it has been designed to accommodate, a frequency it has been tuned to sense. The empty archways, the lack of doors, the absence of any mark of wear or decay all point to a space that has never been inhabited in the way a human would understand. It was grown, not built, as a chrysalis for something that may have already departed, or may be yet to come.

The being that lives here is patient. It measures time in the slow polishing of the floor by water, in the incremental glow of the walls, in the cycle of condensation and drip. It is aware of the visitor—the "you" standing in the center—but it processes this presence as it would a new mineral deposit in the water, a temporary change in the refractive index of the air. It is not hostile, not welcoming. It is simply observant, in its own vast, slow way.

To live here is to be a garden, a temple, and a machine, all at once. It is to exist in a state of perpetual maintenance, of quiet, intelligent waiting. It is to be a hollow world that is full of itself, a silence that is not empty but listening, and a light that is not for seeing, but for being.
