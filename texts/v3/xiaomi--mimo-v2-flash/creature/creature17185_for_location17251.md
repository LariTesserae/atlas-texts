In the basin, there is no biology, but there is behavior. There are actors, though none of them breathe.

The dominant presence is the **Seep**. It is not a single entity but a collective condition—a distributed intelligence of moisture and mineral saturation. It occupies the ground, the air, and the hollows of the machinery alike. The Seep perceives through vibration and conductivity; it "knows" the weight of a footfall through the trembling silt and the disturbance of charge in the air. It does not think in neurons, but in gradients—differences in temperature, pressure, and chemical potential that drive its slow, inevitable flow. It is patient, a predator of time, consuming the sharp edges of the world and polishing them into smooth, dark equilibrium.

Inhabiting the Seep are the **Runners**—the metallic mites mentioned in the description. They are not biological scavengers but micro-mechanical fauna, self-replicating complexes of iron and silica no larger than a grain of sand. They move in glittering swarms, not with the frantic energy of insects, but with the precise, algorithmic purpose of a polishing tool. They are drawn to microscopic irregularities on surfaces, attracted by static charges or the faint thermal signatures of energy leaks. Their collective motion creates a living skin over the basin’s objects; a rusted beam is not just weathered, it is *groomed*, its surface maintained in a state of high polish by the continuous passage of the swarms. They are the basin’s immune system, erasing disorder and distributing trace metals in their wake.

Larger and slower are the **Migrators**. These are mobile mineral concretions—blobs of layered copper, iron, and silicate matrix the size of a human head or a small boulder. They possess no central nervous system, but they navigate. Over seasons, they shift their positions by inches, driven by electrochemical gradients. The charged concretions that pulse with internal light act as "heart nodes" in a vast, slow circulatory system. The Migrators move toward these nodes when their own internal charge depletes, connecting briefly to "feed" on the residual energy, then drifting away again. They are the basin’s grazers, consuming the faint hum of the world’s leakage and redistributing it as waste heat and oxidized rind. Their movement is so slow that a human observer might mistake them for stationary rocks, but over weeks, their relative positions change, mapping the invisible currents of the basin’s energy field.

Between the Migrators and the Runners exist the **Weavers**. These are the mobile mineral growths, the "vines" of silicate and conductive filaments that trace paths across stone and metal. They are not plants, but slow-motion architecture. They grow by accretion, laying down new layers of mineral in response to chemical triggers. They are drawn to the seepage of water, following the damp paths of leaks from ancient pipes or porous rock. Their filaments are conductive, forming living circuits that connect the charged concretions to the broader mineral lattice of the basin. They are the basin’s nervous system, a slow, silent network that transmits not thoughts, but potentials—differences in charge that guide the movement of the Runners and the drift of the Migrators. They weave the built and the natural into a single continuum, suturing rusted metal to stone with threads of gleaming silica.

The basin also hosts **Resonators**—hollow, bell-like structures of thin, rusted metal, half-buried in the silt. They are not alive, but they are reactive. When the basin’s ambient hum rises in pitch or intensity, they vibrate sympathetically, amplifying the sound and transmitting it through the ground. The Runners avoid them; their vibrations disrupt the delicate charge patterns the mites rely on. The Resonators are the basin’s sensory organs, not of sight or touch, but of acoustic and energetic awareness. They detect disturbances—like the footfall of an intruder—and amplify them, sending ripples through the Seep that trigger defensive behavior in the Runners (a frantic polishing of the intruder’s path, leaving it slick and unstable) and alert the Migrators to shift away from the source of vibration.

Finally, there is the **Archive**, a stationary entity that occupies the basin’s deepest, most saturated hollow. It is not a building, but a geological-technological hybrid: a massive, layered concretion of metal, stone, and trapped energy, grown around the core of an ancient industrial generator. The Archive is the basin’s memory. It stores the patterns of erosion, the history of seepage, the paths of the Migrators, and the faint, cyclic pulses of the world’s energy. It does not "remember" in a conscious sense, but its layered structure records every change in chemical composition, every vibration, every deposition of mineral. It is a passive intelligence, a repository of the basin’s entire existence, and the source of the faint, sub-audible hum that permeates everything. The Runners orbit it like moons, the Weavers connect to it like roots, and the Migrators feed from it like parasites.

Together, these entities form a closed ecosystem of chemistry and mechanics. There is no birth or death in the biological sense, but there is replication, migration, consumption, and decay. The basin is a cathedral of silent industry, populated by the ghosts of machines that have learned to live without hands or eyes, moving to the patient rhythm of seepage and rust.
