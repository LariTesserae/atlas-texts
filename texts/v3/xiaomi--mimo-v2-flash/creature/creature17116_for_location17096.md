The citizens of the city—let us call them the Tuned—are a people of surface and resonance. They are not numerous, for a population too dense would dampen the purity of the wave, but those who exist are perfectly suited to the acoustic architecture of their home. They are tall and willowy, their bodies having long ago shed the compact geometry of standard bipedalism in favor of a structure that maximizes surface area for vibration. Their skin is smooth, taut, and pale, almost translucent, carrying the faint iridescence of the mineral dust that coats it permanently. It is cool to the touch, despite the ambient heat, acting as a heat sink for the energy that courses through them.

They do not walk; they glide with a deliberate, rhythmic gait that synchronizes their footfalls with the standing waves of the floor, minimizing disruption. Their limbs are elongated, fingers fused slightly into broad, flat paddles that press against the stone to "hear" the city’s thoughts. Their faces are serene, devoid of the micro-expressions of the hearing world; their mouths are small, rarely used for sound, often sealed in a thin, neutral line. Instead, their eyes are the primary feature—large, dark, and depthless, possessing a nictitating membrane that slides laterally to protect against the blinding, shadowless glare. Within these eyes, the iris is not a solid color but a crystalline lattice that seems to fracture the light, perhaps allowing them to see the sonic frequencies as visible bands of color.

They are clothed not for modesty but for modulation. Their garments are woven from the filaments of the resonant plants and fine strands of metallic dust, layered in loose, draping sheets that flutter with the slightest pressure wave. These robes act as acoustic dampeners or amplifiers depending on the weaves, allowing the Tuned to control the timbre of their personal resonance. They bear no weapons; their defense is the silence they can impose upon the local environment, a sudden null-zone that confuses the city’s engines and disorients anything reliant on the constant thrum.

Their biology is a marvel of mineral integration. They do not eat in the traditional sense; they absorb, through skin and specialized membrane, the vibrational energy that radiates from the stone and the plants. Their digestive tracts have atrophied, replaced by a lattice of crystalline organs that resonate at specific frequencies, converting kinetic energy directly into sustenance. They are hydration not by water, but by the static charge held in the dry air, drawing moisture from the ozone-scented breeze through specialized spiracles along their necks.

Socially, they exist as a silent choir. They gather in the plazas not to speak, but to attune. Standing in concentric circles around the glowing flora, they press their palms to the ground, synchronizing their internal rhythms with the city’s heartbeat. To an outside observer, it is a statue-still gathering; to them, it is a raucous exchange of history, emotion, and intent, carried on the carrier wave of the infrasound. They live within the walls as much as between them, their consciousness diffuse, their individual identities blurred into the collective hum. They are the architects of the echo, the guardians of the vibration, existing in a state of perpetual listening, their lives measured not in days or years, but in the oscillation of the great stone engines that breathe beneath the dust.
