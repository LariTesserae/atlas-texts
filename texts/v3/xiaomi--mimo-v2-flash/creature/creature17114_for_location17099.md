Conveyor belts, unseen in the gloom, carry invisible burdens with a rhythmic clatter—the sound of a distant, tireless digestive tract. These are not relics; they are active, maintained by an automated will that has no face. They are the apex predators of this ecosystem, feeding on power and raw materials, excreting heat and vibration.

The **Water** is the lifeblood and the weeping. It is not an external element but an internal secretion. It rises through microscopic fissures in the concrete, not as a flood but as a constant, slow transpiration from the cavern’s own body. They are neither plant nor animal in any traditional sense; they are bio-mechanical decomposers, feeding on the salts leaching from the concrete and the micro-corrosion of the pipes. They are the patient ones, the ones who measure time not in rotations of a turbine but in the millimeter-per-year spread across a concrete plane. They are the memory of the surface, recording every drip, every temperature shift.

They are the system’s nervous system, carrying impulses of hunger and fear through the veins below. Its call is a dry, metallic rustle, not a song.

It keeps the lights on. It keeps the pumps moving. It does not know why, but the doing is everything. It is the ghost in the machine, and the machine is the cavern.
