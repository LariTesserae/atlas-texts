These are the arthropodal components: crystalline entities whose carapaces are faceted like uncut gemstones and whose limbs are jagged shards of conductive ore. They do not eat biomass; they harvest the static charge that radiates from the air. They move with a jerky, clockwork precision, synchronized not by sight but by the hum of the spires. When an electrical arc flashes overhead, they freeze in unison, their glassy bodies refracting the sudden light, a silent, million-eyed congregation paying homage to the spark. Their clicking movement is the granular percussion beneath the roar of the chamber.

Hovering in the dense, pressurized air is the **Magnetic Shoal**. These are not animals but sentient geometry—flat, razor-thin discs of lodestone and magnetic hematite that resemble swift fish or gliding blades. They possess no organs, yet they hunt and school with telepathic cohesion. They are guided by the planet’s magnetic field, which is so intense here that it feels like a physical wind. They dart between the spires in shimmering, metallic clouds, their edges singing a high-frequency whine as they slice through the ionized atmosphere. They are the nervous system of the throat, carrying the current from the walls to the center.

Rising from the floor and stretching upward to brush the vibrating ceiling are the **Conductive Spires**. These are the stationary giants, the static population. They are not merely rock; they are living pylons of raw industry. Their surfaces are etched with Lichtenberg figures—fractal scars left by centuries of electrical discharge. They do not grow in the organic sense but accrete, mineral layers building upon one another like the rings of a tree, though their "years" are measured in geological epochs. They are the lungs of the chamber, inhaling the superheated gases venting from the fissures and exhaling the electrical storms that illuminate the dark. They hum with a frequency so low it is felt in the marrow before it is heard by the ear.

Among these spires cling the **Capacitor Blooms**—clusters of spherical, glassy nodules that pulse with the faint, bioluminescent light described in the room. These are not flora; they are storage units. They absorb the ambient heat and the static charge of the air, glowing with a slow, rhythmic dimness like a dying ember. When they reach capacity, they discharge in a silent, blinding flash that travels down the spires and into the floor, completing the circuit of the throat.

Finally, there is the **Resonant Dust**—the smallest, most ubiquitous inhabitant. Suspended in the thick, motionless air, these microscopic crystals vibrate in sympathy with the world’s deep thrum. They are the medium of the throat’s song. They coat every surface, filling the cracks between the basalt plates, a living static that shifts and settles only under the weight of epochs. They are the breath of this non-biological world, the particulate matter that carries the scent of ozone and hot metal, a billion tiny voices vibrating in unison.

Here, life is not a cycle of birth and death, but a circuit of charge and discharge, of erosion and accretion. The inhabitants are components in a self-sustaining machine, a population of rock, crystal, and current, forever singing the deep, industrial hymn of the throat.
