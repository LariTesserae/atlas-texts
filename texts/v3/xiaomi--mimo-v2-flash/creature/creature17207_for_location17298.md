In the High Glass, one does not meet inhabitants so much as one encounters living functions of the continent itself. There are no faces, no societies, no artifice, only the ceaseless activity of a world that has woken up and found itself made of gears and glass.

At the lowest strata, clinging to the knife-edge ridges and sheathing the obsidian cliffs, are the **Coral-Fungi**. They are the great tissue of the land, vast, silent colonies that resemble the branching structures of deep-sea reefs but are composed of chitinous cellulose and glass-hard hyphae. They are not rooted in soil—there is none—but anchor themselves directly into the volcanic rock, their mycelial threads acting as capillary systems that draw up geothermal heat and mineral-rich moisture. They do not grow randomly; they expand in fractal patterns, following the invisible logic of the land’s hum. Their surfaces are slick with perpetual condensation, and in the perpetual twilight, they glow with a faint, internal phosphorescence, not for sight or advertisement, but as a byproduct of their metabolic processes. They are the continent’s lungs, its digestive tract, and its factory floor, breathing in the humid air and exhaling spores that hang thick as dust.

Within the dense mat of this fungal felt lives the fauna, the moving parts of the machine. These are not animals in the traditional sense, but specialized operators of the ecosystem.

The **Scuttling Glass-Beetles** are six-legged arthropods with carapaces of polished, translucent chitin that reflect the dim light in prismatic shards. They move in herds that number in the thousands, yet they never collide. They are the continent’s maintenance crew, tasked with polishing the jagged scree, smoothing the microscopic imperfections in the rock faces, and clearing debris from the fungal pores. They move with a rhythmic synchrony, their scuttling a soft, percussive whisper that matches the frequency of the land’s hum. They do not eat in the traditional sense; they absorb chemical energy directly from the geothermal vents, their bodies acting as living thermocouples.

Above them drift the **Membrane-Gliders**, silent, flat creatures that resemble rays or kites. They possess no visible eyes or mouths, only vast, paper-thin wings of stretched tissue that catch the turbulent updrafts from the fissures. They do not flap or steer; they are passive passengers on the air currents, their bodies tuned to the specific thermodynamic properties of the High Glass. They are the continent’s sensors, drifting through the ozone-scented drafts, their tissues absorbing trace elements and data from the air, which is then filtered down through their trailing filaments into the fungal network below. They are the nervous system, floating in the damp air, connecting the high altitudes to the deep substrates.

In the hollows and the still, black pools, the **Silicate Worms** burrow. These are soft, segmented things that leave glistening trails of enzymatic slime on the glassy rock. They are the processors, the recyclers. Anything that falls into the pools—spilled spores, shed beetle carapaces, the occasional glider that fails to rise again—is met by these worms. They do not consume; they disassemble. Their bodies secrete acids that break down complex matter into its constituent elements, which are then sorted and absorbed into the mycelial threads that line the pool bottoms. They are the liver and kidneys of the High Glass, filtering and redistributing the continent’s own waste.

Above all, there is the **Hum**, the sub-audible vibration that permeates everything. It is not a sound but a presence, a pressure in the bones and the teeth. It is the consciousness of the High Glass, the cold, efficient algorithm that drives the continent’s self-optimization. It is not a mind that thinks, but a system that calculates. It does not dream or desire; it refines. It is the force that sharpens the peaks, that pulses the fungal colonies, that guides the gliders on their silent paths, and that sorts the elements in the black pools. It is the industrial spirit of the land, embodied in every facet of rock, every spore of air, every scuttling leg and drifting membrane.

There are no leaders, no hierarchies, no individuals. Each creature, each fungus, each pool is a node in a single, continent-spanning machine. They are not alive in the way we understand life; they are operational. They are the living tools of a world that is building itself, piece by piece, algorithm by algorithm, in the perpetual gloom of the High Glass.
