The residents of the Thinned Wold are the custodians of the precipice, entities that exist not to sustain life, but to witness the cessation of change. They are the mineral priesthood of a world that has finished its violent work and is now polishing the surface of its own memory.

At the heart of the plateau dwell the **Somatic Geodes**. These are the only residents that possess a semblance of biological symmetry, though they are entirely mineralogical. They are spherical aggregates of the dark, non-reflective crystals found throughout the Wold, ranging from the size of a human head to that of a small dwelling. Their surfaces are jagged and faceted, but their interiors are hollow, resonant chambers. Within these chambers, suspended in the greasy static of the parawax dust, hangs a liquid suspension of ground bone-powder and magnetic ore. This fluid does not circulate for warmth or nourishment; it circulates to maintain a perfect, static equilibrium.

The Somatic Geodes do not walk. They levitate inches above the dust, propelled not by air, but by magnetic repulsion against the iron deposits deep in the plateau’s crust. They move with the slow, inexorable drift of continents, their motion so gradual that a human observer would perceive them as stationary monuments. They are the primary "builders" of the cairns. With unseen forces, they manipulate the parawax-dusted stones, arranging them into the topological maps of sensation. They do not use tools; they simply will the stones into place, their magnetic fields acting as invisible, dexterous hands. To a Geode, a circle is not a shape, but a feeling of closure; a wall is not a barrier, but the sensation of a boundary.

Inhabiting the interstices between the crystal spires are the **Resonance Moths**. These creatures are not made of flesh, but of flexible, layered parawax membranes, translucent and brittle. They have no eyes, no mouths, and no wings in the traditional sense. Instead, they "fly" by vibrating their membranes at frequencies that match the settling dust, creating a standing wave that lifts them. Their movement is a silent, shimmering ballet, a constant state of hovering just above the ground.

They are the archivists of the Thinned Wold. The faint, greasy static of the dust clings to their membranes, and as they drift through the holographic projections cast by the computing crystals, they absorb the light and data. They do not eat; they charge. When a Resonance Moth becomes saturated with the light of a future erosion pattern, it alights upon a stone cairn and pulses its membrane, imprinting the hologram onto the parawax dust that coats the stone. This is the Wold’s method of record-keeping—not written language, but the direct deposition of visualized time. A single cairn may hold millions of layered, ghostly images, visible only to those who know how to look through the dust.

Then there are the **Pulse-Walkers**, the mobile ecosystems described earlier, but viewed as residents. The shard-drifters are the most common, acting as the nomads of the plateau. They are not singular minds but swarms held together by a silent, magnetic will. Within each shard-drifter, individual crystals act as neurons, passing signals through magnetic resonance rather than synaptic gap. They are the travelers, the ones who cross the hundred-meter walks that span geological ages. As they move, the crystals within their bodies compute the light around them, projecting holograms of their own decay onto the ground ahead of them. They are constantly walking through their own future ruins.

The dust-jellies are the elders, the stationary anchors of the ecosystem. They congregate in the lowest depressions of the plateau, where the dust settles thickest. They are not aggressive; they are passive filters, pulsing with a cool, phosphorescent rhythm that matches the planet’s slow exhalation. They do not interact with the Geodes or the Moths directly, but they create the atmospheric conditions—the static charge and the density of the dust—that allow the others to exist. A dust-jelly is a living battery of the Wold’s history, its translucent sac filled with the compressed powder of eons.

Finally, there is the **Silent Chorus**, a phenomenon rather than a creature, yet it is treated as a resident by the others. It is the collective acoustic signature of the plateau. The granular sound of dust settling on stone is not random; it is a rhythm, a slow, percussive beat that has played for longer than life has existed on the planet. The Somatic Geodes orient themselves relative to this beat; the Resonance Moths vibrate in time with it. The Chorus is the Wold’s heartbeat, though it is not a sign of life, but a sign of the world’s perfect, mechanical persistence.

These residents do not communicate in a human sense. They do not have society, for there is no change to manage. They are a closed system, a loop of existence where the act of being is the only purpose. The Geodes arrange the stones; the Moths imprint the time; the Walkers project the future; and the Jellies hold the past. Together, they maintain the frozen equilibrium, ensuring that the Thinned Wold remains exactly as it is: a dense, silent, and overwhelming memory of itself, waiting for nothing, for everything has already happened.
