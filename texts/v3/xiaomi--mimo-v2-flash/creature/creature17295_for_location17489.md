In the Grotto of the Ironroots, one does not merely live; one *is* lived in. The city is a tenant of a colossal, porous organism, a symbiotic lodger in the great, breathing fungal bulk that swallows ferrous veins and exhales warm, metallic air. To be here is to exist in a state of perpetual, humid twilight, lit by the slow-drifting snow of bioluminescent spores and the occasional, brutalist flash of an industrial arc-lamp.

The inhabitants are as integrated into this ecology as the mycelial threads that form the sky. The primary citizens are the **Myco-Ferric Guilders**, a people whose biology has subtly adapted to the environment. Their skin has a faint, resilient sheen, like the velvet moss that lines the streets, and their lungs are efficient at filtering the heavy, ozone-laced air. They are industrious and patient, their movements deliberate, matching the slow, rhythmic pulse of the weeping water and the deep thrum of the geothermal heart below. They do not build with stone or wood, but cultivate the living fungal mass. Their homes are not erected, but *carved* and *sealed*, the edges hardened with a translucent resin that glows with a soft, internal light. They are architects of growth, guiding the expansion of the brackets and terraces with a knowledge passed down through generations, a blend of mycology and engineering.

Their technology is a marriage of the organic and the mechanical. They wear garments woven from fungal fiber, tough and pliable, often stained with the coppery tang of the city's veins. Their tools are brass and iron, but the handles are grown, not attached, seamlessly emerging from the tools themselves as if the metal were a natural outgrowth of the fungus. The Guilders navigate their tiered city on gear-driven lifts that ascend the leather-brown terraces, powered by the ceaseless geothermal engine. Their work is the city's metabolism: tending to the conveyor belts of woven fiber that transport raw ore, maintaining the web of brass pipes and copper wires that cling like vines to the walls, channeling steam, water, and current.

Living alongside them, and inextricably woven into their society, is the overwhelming fauna. The Guilders have a complex, utilitarian relationship with the smaller creatures. The six-legged insectoids with iridescent carapaces are not pests, but a source of chitin and pigment. Their carapaces are harvested for the lustrous dyes that adorn the Grotto's public amphitheaters and the ceremonial gear of the Guilders. The slug-like creatures with their glowing patterns are a source of light; their phosphorescent trails are carefully cultivated in residential passages, creating living, moving lanterns that wax and wane with the creatures' slow glides.

The bat-like aerial creatures are a more contentious element of the ecosystem. They are the sky's workforce and its chaos. Flocks of them, with their leathery wings and multiple eyes, flit between the mycelial cables, their shrieks a constant part of the soundscape. They are prey for larger, nest-building avians, but they are also scavengers, and the Guilders have learned to use them as such. Designated "sky-scour" zones exist where the remains of industry and the occasional natural death are left for the flocks, keeping the lower terraces clean. Their droppings, rich in minerals, are a prized fertilizer for the cultivated fungal walls.

Above them all, in the vaulted canopy, are the **Spore-Singers**. This is a reclusive, quasi-religious order of Guilders who live in the highest, most precarious dwellings, carved directly into the mycelial cables. They are attuned to the city's atmospheric symphony, interpreting the patterns of the drifting spores and the migratory paths of the aerial fauna as messages from the fungal organism itself. They wear masks of hardened resin and chitin, filtered to breathe the densest concentrations of spores, and their role is to predict the city's health—foretelling the ebb and flow of steam vents, the stability of the terraces, and the timing of the great spore-falls that can clog the machinery.

Beneath the terraces, in the deeper, warmer channels where the ferrous veins are thickest, are the **Core-Tenders**. These are the Guilders who interface most directly with the geothermal heart. Their skin is often stained with soot and mineral deposits, and their hearing is dulled from the constant, overwhelming thrum. They are the keepers of the engine, the regulators of the steam, the guardians of the city's lifeblood. They are fewer in number, their society insular and steeped in the ritual of maintenance. They alone know the full layout of the central caverns, a labyrinth of heat and pressure where the line between the city's machinery and the planet's own biology is most blurred.

The city is alive with a hierarchy that is not one of power, but of function. The Spore-Singers watch the sky, the Core-Tenders serve the heart, and the Guilders of the mid-terraces tend to the body. It is a society as integrated as the ecosystem it inhabits, a dynamic equilibrium of biology and industry, of reverence and utility, all existing within the warm, damp, and loud embrace of the Grotto of the Ironroots.
