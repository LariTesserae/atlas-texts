To speak of who lives in this city is to speak of the city itself, for its inhabitants are not merely residents but the active nervous system of the living architecture. They are the Symbionts, a people who have long since abandoned the concept of the separate self, merging their biology and their culture with the subterranean flora.

**The Morphology of the Inhabitants**

Their bodies are a testament to the desert’s scarcity. They are tall and slender, built for navigating the narrow, spiraling corridors and the delicate tension-bridges. Their skin is not soft flesh but a thin, flexible bark—pale, smooth, and cool to the touch, ranging in hue from the white of bleached root-wood to the soft grey of ancient lichen. This dermal layer is photosensitive but does not tan; instead, it grows denser and more textured with age, the elders appearing like gnarled, petrified driftwood.

Their eyes are large and dark, adapted to the eternal twilight of the hollow. They possess a tapetum lucidum, a reflective layer that gives their gaze a faint, phosphorescent glimmer in the dimness. They do not blink often; their eyelids are membranous, nictitating thin veils that keep the air’s spores from drying their surface.

Perhaps the most striking feature is their hair, or what serves as such. It is not keratin but a fine, aerial moss that grows from the scalp and along the spine. It varies in texture and color—from deep, velvety green to silvery grey—and acts as a secondary respiratory organ, filtering the oxygen-rich air and regulating body temperature. In times of stress or deep concentration, this moss can fluoresce softly, signaling their emotional state to others in the gloom.

**The Culture of the Pruners**

Their society is not hierarchical in the way of stone-builders, for the city itself dictates the structure. They are a culture of gardeners, their primary industry being the careful curation of growth.

*   **The Pruners:** These are the architects and engineers. They carry tools of sharpened obsidian (mined from the planet’s crust) and hardened, cured resin. They do not cut aggressively but prune with surgical precision, guiding the living vines into bridges and shaping the hollowed chambers by stimulating specific growth patterns. A Pruner’s skill is measured by their silence; the best require only the softest taps to direct the wood.
*   **The Weepers:** In a world of scarce water, moisture is sacred. The Weepers are caretakers of the vascular systems. They tend to the sap-pumps and the condensation collectors, their hands always slightly damp. They wear hollow gourds and porous ceramic vessels to catch the slow, weeping sap of the architecture, distilling it into drinkable water or fermenting it into nutrient-rich resins. They are the healers, for in this dry air, a wound desiccates instantly; the Weepers seal injuries with warm, medicinal sap.
*   **The Spore-Singers:** Though the city is largely silent, communication is constant but subtle. The Spore-Singers are the historians and communicators. They manipulate the release of pheromones and spores, creating complex, invisible signals that drift through the air. A conversation between two Symbionts is a cloud of scent, understood by the olfactory receptors on their moss-hair and the sensitive membranes of their throats. They "sing" not with sound, but with the timed release of volatile compounds—a story of the city’s history told in a scent-cloud that lingers for hours.

**Adaptations to the Void**

Their physiology is a direct response to the dry, mineral environment:

*   **Fluid Retention:** They possess a specialized kidney structure that recycles moisture with near-perfect efficiency. They excrete uric acid in solid form, a white powder that is often collected and used as a mineral supplement for the fungal gardens.
*   **Respiration:** They breathe through spiracles hidden along their ribs and neck, protected by fine filters of mycelium. This allows them to conserve the moisture that would be lost through open-mouthed breathing. When they speak aloud—which is rare—it is a low, humming vibration felt in the chest as much as heard, using the resonant chambers of their hollow bones.
*   **Diet:** They are neither strictly herbivore nor carnivore. They are detritivores and symbionts. They feed on the nutrient-rich layer of decaying matter that accumulates on the root-mats, supplemented by the sugary sap tapped from the city’s arteries and the soft, protein-rich bodies of the insectoid fauna that share their walls.

**The Daily Rhythm**

A day in the subterranean metropolis is measured not by the sun, but by the pulse of the city’s industrial heart—a slow, rhythmic thump that vibrates through the fibrous floor.

*   **The Waking:** The cycle begins with the "breathing" of the city—the opening of stomata on the ceiling canopy, a subtle shift in air pressure that draws the cool, deep air upward. The Symbionts rise from their niches in the hollowed towers, their moss-hair damp with overnight condensation.
*   **The Tending:** The morning is spent in silent maintenance. Pruners guide new root tendrils away from bridges; Weepers check the tension in the vascular pipes; Spore-Singers release morning pheromones to map the day’s tasks across the city’s air. The insectoid tenders scuttle alongside them, a chitinous chorus of clicking mandibles.
*   **The Gathering:** At the cycle’s peak—when the industrial hum is loudest—the inhabitants gather in the great leaf-awned chambers. They do not sit on furniture but press their root-mat soles into the soft floor, connecting directly to the city’s nervous system. Here, they exchange spore-songs, sharing news of a new chamber budding in the lower tiers or a predator spotted in the shadow-roots.
*   **The Resting:** As the industrial pulse slows, the city settles. The bioluminescent fungi dim to a faint, sleepy glow. The Symbionts retreat into their hollows, their bodies folding into the curves of the architecture, becoming indistinguishable from the walls in the deep gloom.

**Their Philosophy**

To the Symbionts, the concept of "building" is alien. They do not impose their will upon the world; they negotiate with it. Their philosophy is one of fluid persistence—a belief that life is not a struggle against the void, but a slow, inevitable colonization of it. They view the surface world, with its violent weather and abundant water, as a place of chaos and waste. Here, in the dry, rhythmic dark, they have achieved a perfect, quiet symbiosis.

They are the city’s thoughts, its hands, its voice. They are the pruning shears that shape the eternal growth, the weeping wound that gives the desert life, and the silent spores that carry the memory of the deep earth. They live not upon the land, but within it—a people grown from the same root-mat that sustains them, forever tending the living, breathing heart of the world’s hollow.
