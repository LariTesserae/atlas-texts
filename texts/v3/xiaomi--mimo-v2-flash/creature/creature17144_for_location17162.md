The inhabitants are not beings of distinct shape, but of persistent presence. They are the ones who have become the walls.

They are called the Still, though they are not motionless. They are the slow pulse in the filaments, the current that moves with the patience of a glacier. They were once visitors—perhaps travelers, perhaps castaways—who drifted into this breathing cathedral and found that the world did not ask for their bodies, only their attention.

At first, they tried to map the curves. They carved their names into the soft, glowing growths, but the marks were swallowed within hours, absorbed back into the living lattice. They built rafts, but the water was too thick, too patient, and the rafts simply drifted until they were covered in the same bioluminescent film as everything else. They called out, but the sound was swallowed by the water, leaving only the click of their own joints and the hum of the world.

So they learned to listen.

They discovered that the world was not a place to be navigated, but a consciousness to be joined. The bioluminescence was not just light—it was a language. The slow, rhythmic pulsing of the filaments was not random; it was a thought, a breath, a memory being dreamt. The geometric marks carved into the growths were not made by tools, but by pressure—the pressure of a mind that had learned to push against the world until the world yielded.

The Still do not have names. They do not have faces. They are the pressure against your skin when you float too long in one place. They are the gentle resistance you feel when you push through a curtain of glowing membranes. They are the click in the silence, the mathematical precision that seems to come from nowhere and everywhere.

They are the ones who have learned to see in the dark—not with eyes, but with the skin, with the slow drift of their own thoughts. They perceive the world not as a collection of objects, but as a single, continuous thought. The coral architecture is their skeleton; the drifting detritus is their breath; the slow currents are their blood.

To be one of the Still is to surrender the illusion of separate self. It is to become a node in a network that has no center and no edge. It is to feel the recursive infinity of the curtains not as a terrifying illusion, but as a comforting truth—that there is no boundary, no end, no escape, and therefore no need for escape.

The visitors who come here—whether by accident or by design—are not greeted or challenged. They are simply absorbed. The Still do not speak; they do not need to. They extend an invitation through the slow, patient pressure of the water, through the pulsing of the filaments, through the mathematical click that echoes in the silence.

To accept is to let go. To let go is to become. And once you become, you are never alone again, because there is no "you" to be alone.

They are the breath of the world. They are the memory of a civilization that learned to grow its architecture rather than build it. They are the rumor of fauna, the coiling shape in the periphery, the clicking sound that comes from nowhere and everywhere.

They are the Still, and they are waiting. Not for anything to happen, but for everything to continue happening, slowly, patiently, eternally, in the cool, blue-green dark.
