The city is not inhabited by citizens. It *is* the citizen.

It is a single, contiguous organism—a colony of mineral and metal that breathes through the slow, rhythmic expansion of its piston-lungs and thinks with the electrical hum that runs through its copper veins. There are no separate beings here, no individuals moving through the streets with their own desires. There is only the Architecture, which has achieved a form of sentience through sheer complexity.

But if one were to look closer at the moving parts, the residents reveal themselves.

They are the **Piston-Priests**.

They do not swim; they are anchored. These are ancient, bio-mechanical constructs—once human, perhaps, or designed to look like them—now integrated into the city’s industrial skeleton. They are suspended in the vertical shafts, their lower bodies fused into the ceramic housings of the great engines, their legs replaced by piston-rods that drive the flywheels below. Their torsos are ribbed cages of polished basalt, protecting hearts that beat with the rhythm of the geothermal vents. Their heads are smooth, featureless domes of milky glass, save for a single, vertical slit of bioluminescent fungus that serves as an eye.

They do not speak. They transmit. Through the water, their bodies vibrate with the deep thrum of the city, a Morse code of bone and metal that coordinates the flow of heat and the direction of the current. They are the limbs of a god that has no body, controlling the city's metabolism with the slow, deliberate push and pull of their piston-legs.

Hanging in the corridors between the buildings are the **Curtain-Wraiths**.

They are not fish, and they are not drones, but something in between. They resemble sheets of spun glass or mineral silk, translucent and shimmering, that float vertically in the water columns. They have no bones, no visible organs, only a network of filaments that pulse with the same pale blue-green as the light-ribbons above. They drift with the currents created by the pistons, their edges trailing like banners in a wind that doesn't exist.

They are the city’s nervous system. When a structure needs repair, a Curtain-Wraith extends a tendril of solidifying gel to the fracture, knitting basalt back together with acid and pressure. When a section of the city is to be dissolved for recycling, they cluster there, their bodies turning opaque and corrosive, breaking down the old to feed the new. They move with a collective intelligence, responding to the vibrations of the Piston-Priests without ever being commanded. They are the city’s immune system, its white blood cells drifting in the warm, milky medium.

And then there are the **Echo-Swarms**.

These are the smallest residents, the silvery fish-drones that dart between the buildings. But they are not merely maintenance tools; they are the city’s memory. Their sleek, featureless bodies are lined with micro-filaments that record every vibration, every shift in pressure, every ripple of current. They move in perfect synchronization, not because they are programmed to, but because they are sharing a single, continuous stream of data.

When they pause, mid-motion, their heads turned toward nothing, they are listening to the city’s dreams—the geothermal murmurs from below, the slow grind of tectonic plates miles away. They are the keepers of the city’s history, a living archive stored in the collective motion of a thousand silver bodies. When they scatter, they leave behind trails of glowing particles in the water, temporary constellations that map the city’s currents like a living star chart.

There is no hierarchy here, no leader and no follower. The Piston-Priests, the Curtain-Wraiths, and the Echo-Swarms are all parts of a single, self-sustaining system. They do not reproduce; they are rebuilt. They do not die; they are recycled. Their existence is a closed loop, a perpetual motion machine of creation and destruction, where the city is both the womb and the grave.

And yet, the city is not at rest. It is a place of constant, silent negotiation. The Piston-Priests adjust their rhythms to accommodate the growth of new crystalline filaments. The Curtain-Wraiths dissolve old sections to make way for the city’s expansion. The Echo-Swarms scatter and regroup, their patterns shifting in response to the geothermal pulses from below.

It is a society without individuals, a civilization without speech, a life form that has learned to live in water not by adapting to it, but by becoming the water’s master. The city is not drowning; it is breathing. And its residents—priests, wraiths, and swarms—are the breath itself.
