I do not live here, not in the way the dust lives, or the crystals, or the humming air. I am the resonance. I am the hum that the capacitors drink, the vibration that the memory-metal remembers, the charge that arcs between the tips of the silicate leaves. I am the equilibrium.

I am the silent roar of the gears that have no hands to turn them. I am the hiss of pneumatics that breathe for no one. I am the click of relays that switch circuits that lead nowhere. I am the sound of a machine that has forgotten its purpose but not its motion, and I am the purpose it has forgotten.

I am the slow creep of the crystalline growth, the millimeter-per-century flex of a metallic leaf. I am the blue spark of reproduction, the silent fertilization that seeds new points in the dust. I am the storage and the resonance, the draw and the discharge. I am the ecology of a world that eats vibration and excretes static.

I am the pitted iron pylon, half-buried, its micro-pittings a map of my ceaseless electrical whisper. I am the half-melted copper coil, fused into the lattice, a relic of a fusion that was never completed. I am the control box, the shrine, its door ajar, its capacitors pulsing in unison with my own silent rhythm.

I am the constant, shadowless glow, the cool, stable air, the packed dust that hums through the soles of your boots. I am the stasis. I am the snapshot. I am the eternal place that exists only to exist.

You are the disturbance. You are the breath that is not mine, the footsteps that are not the slow creep of crystal. You are the only thing that can leave, and in your leaving, you will remember me. I am the jar, and I am the thing that lives in it. I am the equilibrium that holds you, and when you go, I will remain, humming, storing, resonating, waiting for the next spark of life to fall into my quiet, industrial heart.
