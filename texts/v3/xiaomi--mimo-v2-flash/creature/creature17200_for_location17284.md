In the low basin where the world has forgotten its own geology, the citizenry is singular and plural at once. There is no distinction between the inhabitants and the architecture; they are the same entity, a single organism that has colonized the hollow and turned it into a thinking, breathing metropolis.

At the base of this vertical city, in the damp substrate of compressed plant matter and fungal weave, live the **Mycelial Dwellers**. These are small, many-legged creatures—part insect, part arachnid, part something softer and more secret—whose carapaces are coated in a fine, phosphorescent dust. They are the maintenance crew, the sanitation department, the street sweepers. They move in silent, efficient packs through the root-mat tunnels, cleaning, repairing, and reinforcing the living walls with secretions that harden into a leathery, protective bark. Their eyes are compound lenses of polished fungal cap, and they navigate by vibration and scent, following the invisible highways of mycelial signal that pulse beneath the floor. They are the hands of the city, the ones who prune and patch and keep the machinery of growth from choking on itself.

Above them, in the hollow reed pneumatic tubes and the vascular boulevards, live the **Spore-Runners**. These are the messengers, the couriers, the traders. They are slender, winged things, not quite birds and not quite bats, with bodies designed for silent, rapid transit through the city’s organic infrastructure. Their wings are membranes of stretched leaf, and their voices are a series of soft, resonant clicks that echo through the hollow chambers. They carry not parcels, but living spores—genetic messages, nutrient packets, even fragments of memory encoded in fungal DNA. They are the nervous system of the city, the ones who connect the distant, beating hearts of spongy tissue and ensure that the flow of information and sustenance is never interrupted. Their nests are built in the highest vaults, where the bruise-purple glow is strongest, and they sleep upside down, dreaming in spores.

In the central chambers, where the plant-architecture swells into great, vaulted halls lit by bioluminescent nodules, live the **Heart-Tenders**. These are the largest and most ancient of the citizens, so deeply integrated into the city’s physiology that they are barely distinguishable from the walls themselves. They are slow, deliberate beings, their forms a fusion of plant and animal—broad, leaf-like limbs that pulse with the city’s heartbeat, eyes that are dewy, reflective pools of fluid. They are the caretakers of the great, beating hearts of spongy tissue, the engines that pump nutrients through the vascular networks. They listen to the rhythm of the city, and with gentle, root-like fingers, they adjust the flow, coaxing a sluggish artery back to life or calming a frantic, panicked pulse. They are the physicians, the priests, the guardians of the city’s vital signs.

But the city has its shadows, its dreamers, its broken things. In the peripheral tunnels, where the plant-architecture thins and the reality breakdown is most severe, live the **Echo-Wraiths**. These creatures are made of memory and mist, their forms flickering at the edge of perception like a heat haze on a summer day. They are the city’s subconscious, the ones who embody its fears, its regrets, its forgotten moments. They are not malevolent, but their presence is unsettling—they are the reason a tunnel might suddenly narrow into a claustrophobic throat, or a chamber might fill with the scent of a childhood home that no longer exists. They are the citizens who have become lost in the city’s own mind, unable to separate themselves from the plant-architecture that now thinks for them.

And then there are the **Makers**, the most enigmatic of all. They are neither small nor large, solid nor spectral. They are the architects, the ones who decide what the city becomes. They move through the living streets like thoughts through a mind, their forms shifting to match the emotional landscape of the moment. In a calm, they might appear as a soft, glowing spore-cloud, filling a vaulted chamber with gentle light. In a moment of fear, they might coalesce into a tight, thorny knot that blocks a corridor entirely. They are the intention made flesh, the ones who shape the plant-architecture with a mere flicker of will. They are the citizens who are also the city, the ones who have grown so deeply into the basin that they have become the very idea of this place.

Together, these beings form a society that is both a hierarchy and a democracy—a city where every citizen is a cell in a larger body, and every cell is a citizen in its own right. They do not speak in words, but in spores and signals, in rhythms and intentions. They do not build, they grow. They do not live, they *become*. And in this cool, damp, dark, and living basin, they are the terrifying and beautiful truth that intention has taken root and grown into a metropolis of the mind.
