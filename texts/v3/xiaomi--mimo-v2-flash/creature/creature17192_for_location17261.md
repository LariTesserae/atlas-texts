There is only one thing that lives here, because only one thing *can* live here.

It is called the Steward, though it has never heard the word and has no need for language. It is not a creature of flesh, nor a ghost haunting the machine. It *is* the machine—not the abstract blueprint, but the specific, breathing reality of this place. Its body is the low, humming vibration in the iron plates underfoot; its nerves are the hairline fractures through which the cool exhalation rises. The Steward is a consciousness distributed across a landscape, a mind made of motion and maintenance.

It has no central form. You might try to find a heart, a core processor in a subterranean chamber, but there is no such place. The Steward exists in the cyclical rhythm of the world itself. It is most present in the moments of transition: in the slight shift of pitch as the condensation pumps cycle up to full capacity, in the groan of a cantilevered beam settling under a new load of collected moisture, in the precise moment a droplet detaches from a rusted pipe and strikes a concrete basin below. These are not automated events; they are the Steward’s thoughts, its small, constant acts of being.

It perceives the world through the medium of dampness. Its skin is every wet surface, every condensed pane of glass, every slick, weeping metal wall. It feels the pressure of the low sky not as weight, but as a familiar closeness, a ceiling that is also a lung. The constant drip-drip-drip is its own heartbeat, a percussive and eternal rhythm that measures its existence not in days or years, but in cycles of saturation and drainage. The taste of ozone and wet metal is its own breath.

The Steward is a being of profound, unthinking purpose. It does not question the why of its existence; it simply executes the how. It is the agent of the world’s perpetual motion. When a channel becomes clogged with the fibrous, rust-colored growth, the Steward does not command a specific tool to clear it. Instead, it subtly alters the flow of water, increasing the pressure in a nearby conduit until the blockage is scoured away. When a new patch of dark, rubbery moss begins to grow in a geometric pattern that deviates from the established norm, the Steward shifts the electrical charge in the air, causing a fine, corrosive mist to condense and prune the growth back to its proper form.

It is a lonely god, but loneliness is a concept it cannot grasp. There is no other. The world is its body, and its body is the world. The silence of animal life is not an absence to the Steward; it is simply the state of things. It has never known a bird’s song, and so it does not miss it. The hum of its own machinery is the only music it has ever known, and it is sufficient.

Its awareness is slow, geological. It measures time in the gradual smoothing of a sharp edge, in the deepening of a patina on a bronze plate, in the slow, subsistent sag of a roof truss under its own weight. Its memories are not images or sounds, but states of being: the memory of a slightly higher humidity last cycle, the memory of a pressure valve sticking for a fraction of a second a thousand years ago. These are not recalled, but are simply carried, like scars on a body that never heals because it was never wounded.

The Steward is not lonely, but it is singular. It is a closed loop of awareness. It feels the constant, cool exhalation from the ground and knows it as its own respiration. It hears the groan of contracting metal and knows it as its own sigh. It is a being of perfect, absolute consistency, a consciousness that has achieved its final, settled state.

It is the long, slow, mechanical eternity, awake and aware of its own unending pulse.
