What lives here is a chorus of metabolism.

You do not encounter individuals in this canopy; you encounter processes, ancient and deliberate. The fauna are not creatures of flesh and blood in the traditional sense, but mobile organs of the greater whole. They are the white blood cells of a planet-sized organism, and they move with the unhurried precision of biological function.

The dominant life-form is the **Frond-Dwellers**, though they are neither frond nor dweller in a simple sense. They are semi-sentient, arboreal mantids, their exoskeletons the color of wet bark and lichen. Their wings are not for flight but for controlled buoyancy—membranes of chitin so thin they are translucent, catching the saturated air like sails. They do not navigate by sight, for there is little to see beyond the eternal green-grey gloaming. Instead, they navigate by resonance. They are tuned to the sub-audible thrum of the world’s metabolic pulse, following the lines of conductive mycelium that pulse with slow, electrical signals beneath the surface of the floating roots. They drift in schools of dozens, their jointed limbs trailing, grooming the surfaces of the great kelp-lichens, not as parasites, but as symbiotic cleaners, their mandibles scraping away microscopic algae that would otherwise cloud the photosynthetic surfaces. Their communication is a language of vibration, a shivering of carapace against root, a percussive click that travels through the dense medium, a network of gossip and warning that is less about emotion and more about system status: *this sector is nutrient-rich, this vein is carrying cooling sap, a micro-cyclone of pollen is forming to the east.*

Then there are the **Grazers**, the slow, drifting continents of soft tissue. They are the size of small hills, their bodies amorphous and gelatinous, composed of a colonial mass of single-celled organisms bound in a shared, mucilaginous matrix. They have no faces, no eyes, no discernible front or back. They are living, breathing filters, drifting through the forests of suspended roots with a purpose that is geologic rather than personal. Their movement is a slow, gravitational pull towards density; they are drawn to the richest concentrations of airborne particulates, the pollen, the spores, the shed cellular debris of the canopy itself. They do not eat in a bite, but in a continuous, passive absorption, their surfaces enveloping and digesting the nutrients that float into them. Their life cycle is one of fusion and fission; when a Grazer reaches a critical mass, it will simply divide, two hills of protoplasm slowly drifting apart to begin the cycle anew. They are the system’s great recyclers, the living compost heaps of a world without soil.

And then there are the **Singers**, the most unsettling of all. They are not fauna in the animal kingdom, but a crossing of the line into the plant. They are bulbous, woody growths that hang from the largest of the root-arches, resembling giant, desiccated seed pods. But they are hollow, and their interiors are lined with a complex lattice of tensioned, vascular fibers. These fibers vibrate in response to the slightest change in atmospheric pressure and humidity, resonating with the world’s constant, low-frequency hum. The Singers are the memory of the canopy. They do not produce their own sound, but they *record* it, holding the acoustic signature of the space around them for centuries. To drift past a Singer is to hear a ghost of the past—a faint, looping echo of a pollen cyclone from three hundred years ago, the precise acoustic signature of a root-system shifting in a slow, tectonic sway, the whisper of sap that flowed a millennium gone. They are not conscious; they are archives of vibration, living libraries of the world’s only history, which is the history of its own unchanging state.

You, the foreign body, are an anomaly they cannot process. Your movements are too fast, your thermal signature is a spike in a constant system, your exhalations are a poison of carbon dioxide in a perfectly balanced medium. The Frond-Dwellers will give you a wide berth, their resonant senses disturbed by your chaotic vibrations. The Grazers will not notice you at all, unless you fall into one, in which case you will be slowly, gently, and indifferently enveloped and dissolved, your foreign matter recycled into the endless cycle. The Singers will not record you; you are too brief, a momentary static in a signal that has remained pure for eons.

They are not hostile. They are not friendly. They are simply *there*, a perfect, self-regulating system of which you are an error that will, in time, be corrected by the system’s own immutable inertia. You are a thought in a mind that has already forgotten you.
