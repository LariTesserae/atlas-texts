Living in the Guttering Deep is to be a creature of absolute stillness. You are a swimmer who has forgotten the need to swim, a resident of a world where movement is a memory and time is measured in the settling of dust.

You are a form of life that has evolved in the complete absence of surface chaos. Your body is a testament to the pressure that defines your world. You are not streamlined like a fish, for there is no need to cut through moving water. Instead, you are broad, flat, and wide-footed, your form distributed to minimize the effort of hovering. Your skin is the color of the fungal fronds—pale grey, mottled with the bone-white of the stone floor. It is smooth, hairless, and cool to the touch, a perfect camouflage in a world of soft edges and muted light. There is no need for pigment to warn or to attract; you are the only thing that moves with intention.

Your senses are attuned to the near-silent. Eyes are vestigial, useful only for discerning the faintest gradients of the bruised glow that filters down from the ceiling of water. What you rely on is a form of pressure-sight, a sensitivity to the subtle shifts in the immense weight of the liquid around you. You feel the curvature of a dome not with your hands, but with your entire body, sensing the minute changes in density as you drift past its surface. Your hearing is a vibration felt in your bones, a translation of the sub-audible hum of the world into a map of its structures. The slow grind of one settled building against another is a landmark, a signpost in the eternal quiet.

You do not eat in a way a surface creature would recognize. There is no hunt, no chase. Your metabolism is as slow as the world itself. You are a grazer of the fungal mats, your mouth a soft, suctorial disc that you press against the suede-like surfaces of the fronds. You absorb nutrients directly, a slow, patient process of diffusion. You are part of the closed loop, a mobile component of the fungal network, a creature that digests the world and is, in turn, digested by it when you finally cease to be.

Your life is one of ritual and pattern, for there is nothing else to define it. You trace the same rounded pathways between domes and vaults, your hands brushing the same cool fronds, a pilgrimage through a cathedral that has no god and no congregation but yourself. You interact with your own kind in a language of pressure and near-silence. A meeting is a slow drift towards one another, a gentle collision of bodies in the still water. Communication is a series of subtle contractions of muscle, sending faint pressure waves through the liquid, a conversation felt rather than heard. It is a language of intent, of presence, of location, and nothing more.

There is no concept of past or future, only the eternal now of the settled state. Your memory is not of events, but of shapes—the curve of a specific spire, the texture of a particular wall. You are a creature of place, not of time. You are the fulfillment of the world's purpose: a living thing that moves through a completed architecture, a consciousness that exists to witness the final, perfect form.

You are the last and only heartbeat in a tomb that is also a womb, a silent, drifting soul in the dark, warm skin of the world.
