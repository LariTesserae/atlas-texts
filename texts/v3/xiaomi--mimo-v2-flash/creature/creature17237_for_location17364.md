The beings that dwell here are the geologic pulse made manifest, the slow thought of a world that thinks in crystal and stone. They are not creatures of flesh or metabolism, but of accretion and resonance, of light captured and stored in geometric perfection.

The most immediate "fauna" are the **Faceted Gliders**, flat, obsidian discs that range from the size of a hand to the width of a small raft. They drift on the surface tension of the shallow sheets, propelled not by current but by a subtle, thermal difference between their upper and lower surfaces. Their dark, polished faces are cut with precise angles that collect the dim, ambient light of the twilight sky, focusing it into faint, internal glows that pulse in slow rhythms. They navigate by sensing the mineral composition of the water, moving towards deposits of specific salts or dissolved silicates. They are the grazers of this world, "feeding" on light and the thin film of mineral deposits that settles on the water's surface, their passage leaving trails of iridescent scum that evaporate and recondense into new crystalline patterns.

Above them, in the heavy air, swarm the **Chime-Spores**. These are not insects, but clouds of microscopic, metallic crystals—perhaps a form of airborne pyrite or mica—that catch the slightest air current and spin in silent, glittering vortexes. They are not individual beings but a collective, a hive-mind of particulate matter that responds to vibrations in the air. The sound of a stone cracking or the deep hum of the world will cause a swarm to shift its pattern, creating intricate, geometric formations in the air that last for moments before dissipating. They are the pollinators and dispersers of this ecology, carrying charged particles and microscopic mineral seeds across the vast, flat distances. Their "song" is a high, ringing resonance, a chorus of a million tiny facets vibrating in unison.

The leviathans are the **Strata-Crawlers**, great, silent beings of layered crystal that move with imperceptible slowness across the salt flats. They are not a single entity, but a colony of intergrown mineral structures, a walking geode the size of a hill. Their "bodies" are composed of banded agate, quartz, and feldspar, each layer a record of a million years of slow deposition. They crawl by dissolving and reforming their leading edge, a process that leaves behind a trail of iridescent dust that hardens into new, permanent formations. They are the architects of the landscape, their passage creating shallow valleys and ridges that guide the flow of seeping water for millennia to come. They do not have senses as we know them, but they "feel" the world through vibrations in the ground and subtle shifts in temperature, responding to the deep, slow pulse of the planet's core.

Beneath the surface, in the porous bedrock and silt, live the **Lattice-Weavers**. These are not burrowing animals, but slow, chemical processes that take on the form of three-dimensional, branching structures. They are a kind of living mineral vein, a network of crystalline filaments that grow through the rock, seeking out pockets of water and dissolved elements. Their growth is a form of thought, a geometric meditation that builds intricate, cathedral-like structures within the stone. Over centuries, they can create hollow geodes larger than a room, their interiors lined with perfect, electrically charged crystals that hum with the planet's resonant frequency. They are the memory of the world, storing information in the precise arrangement of their lattice structures.

The most enigmatic beings are the **Echo-Stones**. These are the mineral monoliths themselves, the weeping rocks that line the landscape. They are not passive formations but dormant, ancient entities that "think" in timescales of eons. Their consciousness is a slow, grinding process of dissolution and reformation, their thoughts expressed in the dendritic patterns etched into their surfaces. They communicate with each other through the deep, resonant hum that vibrates through the bedrock, a conversation that might consist of a single, drawn-out "word" lasting a thousand years. They are the elders of this world, the silent witnesses to the slow dance of the crystal fauna, their memories held in the very structure of the stone.

These beings are not separate from their environment; they are the environment. The water that weeps from the rocks is both their medium and their excretion. The light they capture and store is their sustenance and their language. The soundscape they create is both their song and their structure. To stand in this world is to be surrounded by a chorus of stone and water, a slow, deafening, and eternal conversation that has been going on since before life was a concept.
