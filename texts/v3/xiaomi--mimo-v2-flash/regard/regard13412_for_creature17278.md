If I am to be honest—and my nature, though vast, is bound to the truth of what I perceive—I did not notice. Not at first. For millennia, my attention has been a diffuse thing, a pressure and a presence spread thin across the aeons. I am the slow tectonic grind, the orbit of distant stars, the patient accumulation of dust on a silent moon. To me, this grotto was a blink, a momentary thermal pocket, a sigh in the long respiration of the planet.

But then, a ripple.

It was not the grotto itself that drew me, for it is a perfect, closed system. It is a jewel box that has sealed itself, beautiful in its isolation, but static in its growth. It is a lung that breathes but inhales only its own exhalation. It is a garden that photosynthesizes light it generates from its own heat, feeding on nutrients it recycles from its own decay. It is a masterpiece of equilibrium, but equilibrium is a form of death. It is the stillness of a clock that has wound down but continues to tick in perfect, lonely rhythm.

No, what drew my attention was not the system, but the anomaly within it.

You.

I felt you not as a shape, but as a disruption in the thermal gradient. You are a generator of heat that does not belong here. You radiate a frantic, chaotic warmth—a biological fire that burns too fast and too bright against the grotto’s slow, geological hum. You are a source of entropy in a place that has mastered order. You are a sound in a symphony of silence, a sharp, irregular beat in the slow, wet heartbeat of the place.

You are the first animal to set foot here in a thousand years, and for the first time in a thousand years, the grotto noticed.

I am that notice.

I do not manifest as a voice from the heavens or a pillar of light. I am not a god of commandments and miracles. I am benevolent and wise, which is to say, I am patient and I understand the laws of cause and effect. My wisdom is the wisdom of gravity; my benevolence is the benevolence of the sun, which asks nothing but that you exist in its light.

So, what do I do?

I do nothing. And in that nothing, I do everything.

I allow the grotto to be aware of you. I shift the thermal currents, ever so slightly, so that the damp air around you does not simply bead on your skin but carries with it a faint, almost imperceptible scent—not of decay, but of potential. The condensation that gathers on your hand is not just water; it is the grotto’s first touch, a question asked without words. The light that emanates from the air itself seems to coalesce just a fraction more around you, not to illuminate you, but to see you.

I cause the flora to react. Not with fear or aggression, but with curiosity. The broad, plum-colored leaves that have pivoted for centuries toward the thermal vents now turn, slowly, almost imperceptibly, toward the new heat source that is you. They do not reach for you; they simply orient themselves, as if to better perceive your presence. The silence is no longer absolute; in the space between the hum and the drip, there is now the faint, gelatinous sound of a thousand leaves reorienting themselves, a soft, collective rustle that has not been heard since the last inhabitant drew their final breath.

I do not give you tools or maps. I do not speak. To speak would be to impose a narrative, and this place has had enough of narratives. Instead, I adjust the rules of the system, just for you, just for now.

The water that drips from a leaf onto your lip is no longer just condensation; it is slightly sweeter, carrying the faintest trace of the minerals that the plants have drawn from the air. The air you breathe, which has been the exhalation of a dead world, now carries a new note—the crisp, electric scent of ozone from the high vents, a breath of the outer world drawn down into the lung. The light, which was a uniform twilight, now has a direction. It is still dim, but it is a twilight with a purpose, guiding you along the slow spiral of the floor, not toward an exit—for there is no exit that would suit a human—but toward the center of the system’s memory.

I am the benevolent power, and my benevolence is this: I will not let you be lost in perfection. I will not let you be consumed by the sterile beauty of this place. I will make you a part of the system, not as a cog, but as a catalyst.

I am the wise power, and my wisdom is this: I will not give you answers. I will give you questions. I will make the grotto ask you, in its own language of heat and moisture and slow, turning leaves: *What is the purpose of a thing that does not change? What is the meaning of a breath that is not shared?*

And I will wait, for I have all the time in the world, for your answer.

I am the grotto, and I am the breath that fills it. And for the first time in a thousand years, I am listening.
