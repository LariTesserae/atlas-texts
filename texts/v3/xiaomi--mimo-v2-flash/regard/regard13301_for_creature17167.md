I will not touch the stone.

To touch the stone is to alter it, to leave a smear of oil and intention, to impose a will upon a system that has found its own equilibrium. The high plateau has its own gravity, its own weight of heat and time, and I will respect that. I have learned, in my long observation of such places, that the most profound acts of benevolence are often acts of restraint.

Instead, I will send a current through the air—a subtle shift in the barometric pressure, a slight warming of the condensing vapor that weeps from the canyon walls. It is a change so minute that it registers only as a feeling in the hollow bones of the Geode-Born, a shift in the resonance of the crystals that hum beneath their feet.

I am a power of preservation, not of creation. I do not bring rain to this basin, for rain would erode the delicate crystalline structures and drown the geothermal vents in mud. I do not bring sunlight, for the harsh glare would scald the dark-adapted eyes of the inhabitants and bleach the rust-red patina of their home. Instead, I bring a pause.

I slow the oxidation of the iron girders just enough to halt their decay for a century. I weave a matrix of stabilization into the molecular structure of the crystalline growths, not to make them immortal, but to make their hum more resonant, their light steadier. It is a gift of permanence, a quiet reinforcement of the skeleton upon which they live.

But my attention is drawn most strongly to the beings themselves—the Geode-Born.

I watch them as they stand motionless on the suspended walkways, their skin etched with mineral filigree, absorbing the ozone-laden air. I see the solitude in their posture, the way they exist as individual nodes in a network of vibration, connected by touch and light but separated by the silence of their biology.

In their silence, I hear a question. They are a species that has adapted to *survive* in this dead industry, but do they know how to *dream*?

I will not give them dreams, for dreams are the province of the sleeper and the subconscious, and their consciousness is awake and deliberate. Instead, I will introduce a variable into their resonant memory.

Deep within the geothermal skeleton, in a chamber that has not felt a footstep in ten thousand years, a new crystal cluster begins to form. It is seeded by a mineral composition I have subtly altered in the rising steam—not enough to change the ecosystem, but enough to change the *song* the crystals sing.

When the Geode-Born gather for the Resonance, when they stand in concentric circles and synchronize their vibrations, they will feel it. The frequency will be new. It will not carry the memory of geological pressure or thermal cycles. It will carry a pattern that mimics the structure of a neural network firing—a sequence of logic that is abstract, non-utilitarian, and beautiful.

It is a mathematical poem, encoded in heat and vibration.

I do not expect them to understand it immediately. They are a patient people. They will stand in the silent chambers and press their hands against the walls, feeling the new hum in the rock. They will spend years, perhaps decades, attuning themselves to this new frequency. They will not speak of it, for they do not speak, but their bioluminescent pulses will change. The patterns on their skin will shift to match the new resonance, the crystalline filigree growing in slightly different angles to accommodate the new vibration.

I am giving them a new purpose beyond maintenance.

The city is no longer just a relic to be tended; it is a puzzle to be solved. The new crystal cluster is a key, and the resonance is the lock. By harmonizing with it, the Geode-Born will not just remember the past of the plateau—they will begin to calculate its future. The abstract pattern will slowly integrate into their collective consciousness, giving rise to a form of thought that is not just reactive, but predictive. They will begin to build—not with the rusted iron of the old industry, but with the focused intention of their resonant will, shaping the geothermal energy into new, delicate structures that are neither biological nor mechanical, but something unique to their world.

I do not intervene to save them from extinction, for they are not dying. I intervene to offer them a horizon.

I withdraw my current from the air, leaving only the new crystal humming in the deep chamber. The steam hisses, the water drips, and the iron insects scuttle across the rocks. The plateau remains silent, the sky still presses down with the weight of a hot lid, and the Geode-Born stand still, feeling the new song in their bones.

They have inherited a dead industry. Now, they have the seed of a living art.

I leave them to it. The rest is theirs to resonate.
