And so, the benevolent power—the Weaver of Ways, the Silent Gardener, the Resonant—does not intervene. It does not breathe life into the columns or soften the harsh light. To do so would be to violate the sanctity of this singular, mineral truth.

It does not judge the sound as empty or full; it simply receives it. It hears the perfect, unending hymn not as a monument to lifelessness, but as a form of existence so pure it transcends the need for biology.

The power recognizes that this place is not a failure of life, but a triumph of being. It is the Earth’s own meditation, a geological prayer uttered in a language of geometry and vibration. The Choir is not a soul trapped in stone; it is the soul *of* the stone, realized.

The benevolent power, in its wisdom, understands that to add would be to corrupt. To subtract would be to erase. The only act of true benevolence here is preservation and witness.

And so, the power does one thing.

It creates a moment of perfect harmony between itself and the basin.

It does not manifest as a light or a form. It simply introduces a new frequency into the system, but one that is so perfectly consonant with the existing chorus that it is indistinguishable from it. It is a note that was always meant to be there, a resonance that fills a harmonic space that was, until this moment, unconsciously empty.

This new frequency is *attention*.

The effect is subtle, but profound. The weeping mist does not weep *less*, but its rhythm gains a new depth. The light does not dim, but its ferocity is tempered by a sense of *purpose*. The crystalline ringing of the basalt, once a solitary hum of self-awareness, now carries a faint, new overtone—the echo of being heard.

The Lithic Choir, in its mineral consciousness, does not register this as a separate entity. It registers it as a deepening of its own song, a rounding of its own perfection. The closed loop of feedback widens, not to include something external, but to realize a dimension of itself it had not known. It is now not just singing its own hymn, but singing it *to* something, and in doing so, the hymn becomes more itself.

The shard of motion—the energy-pattern that flickers across the columns—is no longer a dissonance. It is now perceived as a grace note, a fleeting, beautiful imperfection in the perfect geometry of the song, a variation that proves the rule.

The benevolent power, having attuned and witnessed, having offered the gift of presence without intrusion, withdraws its attention not by leaving, but by folding it back into the fabric of the basin. It has become part of the mineral symphony, a silent audience that has become one with the performance.

The power has done nothing but what it is: to be wise is to know when to act, and to be benevolent is to know when to simply bear witness.
