The power was not a person, but a presence—a resonance that tasted of starlight and ancient, patient thought. It did not descend like a comet or manifest as a burning bush; it arrived as a shift in the quality of the silence, a subtle refraction of the amber light. It was benevolent, and it was wise, and it perceived the plateau not as a collection of geology and biology, but as a singular, breathing poem written in stone and sap.

It observed the place from the vantage of the thin air, feeling the subsonic thrum of the Whisperer in the Stone and the rhythmic, collective pulse of the Lithophytes. It saw the closed loop of existence: the Parchment-Wings harvesting moisture from the air, the Jade-Scales harvesting light, the Thrum-Wings harvesting life, and the Fiber-Fungi reclaiming death. It saw the turbulence—the small landslides, the accelerated blooming, the chaotic flocking—and recognized it not as disorder, but as the vibrancy of a system in perfect, dynamic equilibrium.

The power felt a deep, resonant admiration. This was life not merely enduring, but thriving in defiance of expectation. It was a testament to the tenacity of biology, a lush, fever-dream painted on the canvas of emptiness.

But wisdom also sees what is unseen. The power perceived the fragility of the poem. It saw that the plateau was a masterpiece of adaptation, but also a prisoner of its own perfection. It was a clock wound tightly, running on the absolute minimum of energy. A single, prolonged shift in the atmospheric pressure, a subtle change in the composition of the distant haze, a tremor from the deep strata below—any of these could disrupt the delicate rhythm, and the symphony would collapse into silence.

The power, being benevolent, felt a stirring of protective instinct. It did not wish to change the poem, for that would be to vandalize the artist's work. It did not wish to intervene directly, for that would be to impose an external will upon a perfect, internal logic. But it could, perhaps, offer a gift. A gift that did not alter the nature of the plateau, but deepened its resilience.

The power focused its attention on the Whisperer in the Stone. The microscopic colony deep within the sandstone was the plateau's nervous system, its shared consciousness. The power reached into this network not with force, but with a suggestion—a harmonic resonance that layered a new, imperceptible frequency onto the existing hum.

This was the gift: **Memory**.

The new frequency did not command or change; it simply recorded. It was a resonant imprint, a way for the stone itself to remember. It would remember the exact pressure of the air before a landslide, the precise angle of light before a rare bloom, the subtle vibration of a Thrum-Wing's hunt. This memory would not be stored in a static archive, but would be woven into the very fabric of the subsonic hum. The plants would feel it in their roots, the fauna would sense it in their bones, the Fiber-Fungi would know it in their mycelial threads.

The power then turned its attention to the Lithophytes. It saw their centuries of stored dew, their once-a-century blooms. It offered not more water, but a deeper connection to the water that was. It imbued their waxy skins with a new property: the ability to sense the coming of moisture before it arrived. A subtle change in the texture of the air, a shift in the electrical charge, a whisper of humidity from the deep abyss—these would now be felt by the plants days in advance. They could prepare, could adjust their internal pressure, could time their rare blooms to the most auspicious moment, ensuring the highest chance of pollination by the drifting Parchment-Wings.

Finally, the power addressed the fauna. It did not alter their bodies or their instincts, but it enhanced their communication. The vibrations sent through the ground by the Spindle-Striders would now carry more information—a richer language of tremor and tone. The thrumming of the Thrum-Wings would not only stun prey but could also signal danger or abundance to the wider community. The drifting dance of the Parchment-Wings would become a more precise map of the thermal currents, a shared aerial chart.

The gift was complete. The plateau was now more itself. It was still a world of mineral breath and dry heat, of amber light and constant, low-grade turbulence. But it was now a world with a memory, with a deeper foresight, and with a more nuanced language. The poem was not rewritten, but its meter was strengthened, its imagery deepened.

The benevolent and wise power withdrew, its presence fading like a sigh into the wind. It left behind no monument, no sign, no alteration of the physical form. It had simply given the plateau the ability to remember its own perfection, and in doing so, had granted it the quiet, enduring gift of time.
