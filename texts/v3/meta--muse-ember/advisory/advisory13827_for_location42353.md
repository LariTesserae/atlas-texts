Marrek will not be like a normal city trip, and if you prepare for a normal city trip you will be wet, tired, and vaguely disappointed by about noon on the first day. It is not a ruin, it is not a jungle, it is not a desert. It is a warm, high, damp, plantless plateau that has been working, dripping, and being lived in by animals longer than anyone there can remember.

Prepare for that specific combination: high-altitude sun + equatorial humidity + permanent wet stone + industry + an absurd density of non-plant life.

### 1. Before You Go

**Get your body ready, not just your bag.** The lift up the cliff is the first filter. Your ears will pop like on a plane, the wind at the rim will find you, and once you're up you're staying up for a while — Marrek doesn't have a suburb to retreat to. Spend your first 24-48 hours going slower than you think you need to. Walk a district, not the whole horizon. Hydrate constantly. The air is damp, so you won't feel thirsty the way you do in dry heat, but the stone radiates heat back into your legs at noon and the altitude still dries you.

Talk to a travel doctor about fungal and animal exposure if you have asthma, allergies, or a weak immune system. This is not a place of pollen. It's a place of spores. At home you worry about hay fever in spring; here, inhaling spore dust from the black and ochre fungal mats in every damp seam is the everyday equivalent. Rats, pigeons, bats, feral cats and dogs in close quarters also mean the usual urban animal risks are not theoretical: bites, scratches, leptospirosis from canal water, histoplasmosis and other guano-associated lung irritants. You do not need to be afraid, but you do need to be boringly hygienic.

As a multiverse traveler specifically: check local quarantine rules. Marrek has no soil and no green plants in living memory. Do not bring Earth seeds, potted plants, or soil on your boots expecting to find a park. They will almost certainly die, and you may be fined for trying. The same goes for bringing animals with you — the local ecology is already at full capacity. Every ledge is claimed.

### 2. What to Pack

Think quick-dry, waterproof, and washable. Cotton is your enemy here. It will stick to you by 10 a.m. and never dry.

**Clothing:**
- 2-3 merino or synthetic breathable shirts, not cotton. They wick the permanent damp and don't turn clammy.
- Light, windproof rain shell. Afternoon "rain" in Marrek is often a warm drip from pipes, cornices, bridge undersides, and the cliff face itself. An umbrella helps for direct drizzle but fails on a windy rim and is a nuisance in narrow, low-ceilinged streets. A hooded jacket is better.
- Shorts or light trousers that can get splashed. You will be walking beside canals at knee-height and shoulder-height; spray happens.
- Warm layer for the rim and for night. It is warm overall, but you are high, the wind is real, and at night the stars are sharp because the air is clear and thin when the mist clears.

**Footwear — the most important decision:**
Wet basalt, polished handrails, worn concave steps, fungus-slick pavers, and guano-slick tram tracks. You need:
- Waterproof hiking sandals *or* low waterproof walking shoes with deep tread and ankle support.
- Spare socks, and consider spare socks-for-socks. Your feet will get wet from puddles that are permanent mirrors, not rain. Dry socks change your whole day.
- Do not bring smooth-soled shoes, canvas sneakers, or flip-flops. You will slide on a stair that is worn smooth by a million feet.

**Gear:**
- Dry bags for electronics, passport/dimensional papers, and one change of clothes. Condensation beads inside engine houses and runs down steel; a hotel room away from the canals will still feel damp.
- A proper respirator mask, not just a surgical mask. For smelter districts (hot metal and fish smell, thin smoke flattened under the cloud deck), textile districts (white steam mist), engine halls, and any dusty waterworks. It helps with smoke, spore dust, and guano dust all at once.
- Earplugs *and* decent noise-dampening for sleeping. The sound is constant, not bursty: dripping canals, pumps, trams, metal work, and over it all cooing, chirring, splashing, claws on stone, wings in tunnel mouths. You will tune it out in the day and notice it again the second a pump rests. At night in a canal-side hotel, you will hear it through the wall.
- Headlamp or phone light. Under bridges, in vaulted waterworks, in tunnel mouths where bats pour out at dusk, streets go dark fast.
- Sunscreen and a hat. The light is "white and even" on a clear day, shadows short and honest — that's high-altitude equatorial light. You can burn while feeling damp and cool.
- Small first-aid kit: antiseptic wipes, bandages (water causes small cuts to linger), antihistamines, your inhaler if you have one, and soap to wash hands and feet after touching wet stone. Fungal mats are not for touching, let alone eating. Do not assume orange crust or pale film is harmless because it's pretty.
- Reusable water bottle, but do not drink from canals, springs breaking out of warehouse walls, or cisterns. The city is cut through with slow brown-green water full of carp, eels, snails, mussels, and everything that lives off them. Tap water in hotels is probably treated, but when in doubt, boil or filter. Marrek's water is everywhere *near* the surface, which is not the same as safe.

### 3. Getting Around

You will not see Marrek in a day. From the rim it looks like it goes to the horizon, and it does — factory chimneys 40 kilometers away are still working. Pick one district per day: a smelter district, a textile district, a residential terrace, the waterworks. Then accept that you are still in Marrek.

Use the trams, but learn their rules. Locals sweep guano from the tracks before it makes them slick — if you see them doing it, step back. Tram bridges have polished handrails — hold them, but watch your step. When you walk, expect streets that are rooms outdoors: roofed by washing lines, awnings, bridges, pipes. That gives you drip cover but takes away the sky.

The canals are not decorative. In some districts they are at knee-height, in others shoulder-height, with slow current and living things changing the flow. Do not wade, do not let children lean over, and do not assume a low wall is just a wall. Frogs call from the damp corners under bridges because it is always damp there.

At dusk, do not walk directly under tunnel mouths. Bats pour out in a continuous silent river. It's a wonderful sight from a side street. It's a bad idea to be in the flight path.

### 4. Living With The Animals

This is the biggest mental adjustment. You are not visiting Marrek *despite* the animals. You are visiting Marrek *because* the city has become a complete animal ecosystem without a single plant.

Pigeons in the thousands in guano-white colonies, swifts low over canals, gulls from the cliff, rats in daylight in the gutters, lizards on warm bricks, cockroaches and moths around every warm engine, feral sleek cats and dogs patrolling terraces, bees and wasps at every food stall working the fungus blooms, spiders in every engine hall rafter.

Locals expect this. They oil gears where pigeons stuff twigs, lift grates where eels try to climb. You should expect it too.

- Don't feed pigeons, rats, or feral dogs/cats. It alters their routes and puts you at bite risk. Keep your own food sealed, especially around stalls — wasps and flies are already thick there.
- Don't try to shoo a ledge, a cat, or a rat out of your path. Give way. An empty ledge in the morning will be occupied by afternoon; arguing is pointless.
- Don't touch stray animals to pet them, no matter how sleek and urban they look. If you are bitten or scratched, clean it immediately and seek care for tetanus/rabies assessment.
- Wash after handling money, tram poles, wet handrails — everything here is touched by hands and claws.

### 5. Expectations to Reset

There are no trees to sit under, no grass to lie on, no weeds in cracks to reassure you. Your eye will keep looking for green in courtyards and canal edges and keep finding stone, brick, steel, and fungus. That is not a lack. It's the place. The green-brown film in the canal water and the pale travertine fans where the cliffs have sweated for centuries are as close as Marrek gets to foliage.

Nothing looks new and nothing looks ruined. Steps are worn into bowls, limestone is softened, brick has grown a skin from canal spray. Factories built before anyone living remembers still produce at the same steady pace. Service will be slow. Pumps are slow. Bird flocks move slowly between districts. Do not try to hurry Marrek. Match its tempo and it becomes settled and strangely peaceful. Try to sprint through it and you will just get damp, tired, and annoyed.

Food is imported grain and meat moving through markets, with scraps feeding the ecology. You will find eatable, hot, practical food at stalls, but don't expect a salad bar or fresh local vegetables. Bring familiar snacks for the first days. Vegetarian options may be limited to grain-based dishes.

### A Simple Closing Checklist

Passport/dimensional documentation in a dry bag, quick-dry clothes, waterproof treaded shoes + spare socks, rain shell, respirator mask, earplugs, headlamp, sunscreen, first-aid + allergy meds, dry bags for electronics, sealed food snacks, noise-dampening for sleep.

And a mindset: you are a guest in an old, worn, working animal city that chose — without ever quite deciding to — to become stone and water and machines and living things. Leave it a little cleaner than you found it, wipe your feet, step around the eels, and let the city drip on you in its own good time.
