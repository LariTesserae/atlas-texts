This is not an expedition. It's a small, hot, sealed room the size of a house that happens to be a complete world. For an ordinary human, preparation is less about bringing more and more about bringing *less* — less bulk, less light, less heat of your own, less smell — and giving yourself time to slow down. You will not be visiting a cave. You will be visiting a crowd.

From the leaning fissure to the farthest pool is only a few minutes' walk. That brevity is a trap. All that life is layered hand-to-hand, and you will be in the way of it the whole time.

### Before you go

**Train your body for the squeeze, not the distance.** The entrance is shoulder-width if you turn sideways and it slopes down steadily from the first step. The links between the four chambers pinch to a crawling width in places. A normal backpack will not fit, and a sealed multiverse suit will make you overheat, fog, and get stuck. Leave the pack outside. Practice sideways breathing and getting down on knees and forearms on a mat at home. If you are claustrophobic, have bad knees/back, asthma, a heart condition, or do badly in heat, this is not a place to push through. The deepest pocket is hotter and the air feels thinner with life — that pale millipede marking the boundary is not a challenge to pass, it's a sign to turn back.

**Arrive rested, fed, and hydrated, but do not eat or drink inside.** The motto of the place is "hot before it is dark." The heat comes from the stone itself, up through your soles and palms. Sweat here does not evaporate so much as bead and stay, so you will lose far more fluid than you feel. Eat a light, salty meal and drink electrolytes *before* you enter, and plan to keep the visit to 45 to 90 minutes. More than that, fatigue and dimness make accidents likely. Tell someone outside the fissure where you are and when to expect you out. The crack in the entrance chamber is your only daylight and your only easy landmark — from inside, with dark-adapted eyes, the fissure is embarrassingly easy to miss.

Spend 20 minutes in dim light before entering, phone off. Your eyes will adjust in there, but they will never fully succeed. Shapes become textures, greens become a brighter darkness. If you walk in from bright sun staring at your phone, you will blunder straight into the first fern and the first moss cushion.

### What to wear and carry

Wear one layer of quick-dry, close-fitting clothing. No cotton — it stays cold and clammy when the condensation mist comes. Long sleeves and long pants to protect against warm, abrasive stone and to keep moss, humus, and whatever lives under stones off your skin. No perfume, no scented sunscreen, no insect repellent inside. The animals navigate by taste and heat; chemicals are an invasion, and repellent kills the slow fungus gnats that are part of the food cycle.

Footwear: low, grippy, ankle-supporting shoes with a thick sole — the floor radiates heat — that can get wet and *will* get moldy. Waterproof socks help. Bring thin gloves and knee pads for the crawling pinches, but take them off to feel your way when you are stationary. Your palms are how you'll steady yourself on the fused, rounded pillars, and the stone is warm and full of tiny residents.

Carry only a small hip pouch:

1. **A dimmable headlamp, red/green mode, pointed down.** No bright white torch, no flash photography. The translucent frogs show their hearts in the light, the eyeless glass lizards navigate by heat on their skin, and the algae glow under the pools brightens when stirred and then dies. A shaft of bright light creates a slow, collective ripple — frogs sink, lizards flatten, everything watches your warmth pass. You need just enough to see the floor texture, not to illuminate the hall.
2. **Water to sip at the entrance, not inside.** Do not drink from the pools. They are still, warm, mirror-like pockets where blind amphibians with feathery gills live their whole lives and an eel-like predator waits half-buried, tongue-flicking. They are not flowing, not filtering, and not safe for you.
3. **A small towel** for beading sweat and a soft cloth for your glasses. The air condenses leaf by leaf. An hour may pass without a drip, then the ceiling exhales a mist and fern tips bead and let go, seemingly at random. Things fog fast.
4. **A pencil and tiny notebook** if you want one — ink runs, paper goes soft.

Leave behind fire, food, collecting jars, speakers, anything metal or tool-like. The old signs in there — the moss-filled crescent of stacked stone, the worn bowl carved in a pillar, the five scratches repeated three times — have been reabsorbed into the floor. Do not rebuild them, sit in them for long, or carve your own mark. In a multiverse context, this matters doubly: do not carry anything *out*. Change and hot-wash your clothes when you return, dry boots and gloves completely outside the sealed ecosystem, check seams for gnats and millipedes. A single moss spore or fungus bloom on your boots is an invasive species on the next world.

### How to move inside

Enter as if sinking, which you are. Root crossing above, leaf-litter giving way to soft, dark, sweating ground underfoot. Pause at the crack. Let the grey-green shaft and the fern fronds teach your eyes what green lives near light, and pale blind life lives everywhere else.

Move in centimeters, as the millipedes do. Step on stone and mineral crust, not on waist-high moss cushions, not in the cups of old stalagmites, not in the dusty hot corners where footprints hold. Do not lift stones to look. Under almost every warm stone is a committee — isopods, worms, glass-shelled beetles with a dark gut working visibly. If you must move one, replace it exactly, gently, and immediately.

Keep hands out of pools and off slow-moving columns. The pool chamber is where the predator is. Lizards will eat millipedes if you do not scare them into pausing; beetles eat fungus; everything eats the damp and the dead. Your warmth, your breath, your damp skin, are the loudest signals in a place that has learned not to make sound. The porous stone and moss drink noise — you do not need to whisper, but do not stomp, clap, or fill the silence. Listen for nearly-nothings: a drip, a fern rubbing rock as warm air expands from the deep chamber, a tick of millipede legs. That is navigation here. Earplugs would deafen you to it.

Let the warmest wall be a compass. Plants grow toward heat as much as light, leaning and flattening against it. Near the crack, everything is green. Deeper, everything is pale and translucent. Follow the heat gradient and you will find the boundary yourself before you need a map.

### Leaving

Reverse the sink. At the crack, stop. Your eyes will be dazzled by what was never bright. Shake off, towel down, wash hands *after* you are out — never use sanitizer or soap near the pools. Check for any hitchhikers in the light outside, where you can see them.

You will leave having learned only four chambers, one light, a few pools. That is not a failure. This place is complete at the scale of a house precisely because you can never take it in all at once. The preparation that matters is not heroic. Arrive cool, leave your bulk behind, move slow, keep your light dim, keep your hands to yourself, and let it go on condensing, dripping, growing toward warmth, and eating and being eaten — quieter than you can be, long after you are gone.
