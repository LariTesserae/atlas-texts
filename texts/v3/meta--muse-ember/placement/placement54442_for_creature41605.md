I do not arrive here.

There is no outside to arrive from, and no edge where I could cross in. I find myself already standing — though standing is the first mistake I have to give up. The floor yields under me, warm and slick, and my footprint closes behind me, not filled in, taken back into the weave. If I were the walker I first think I am, I would be the first animal in a world that has no animals, the first made thing in a world with no maker. I would stand here in the body-warm dark and fog with my breath fogging and nothing to eat in the animal way, nothing to pass out of me in the animal way, no sun to keep me, no cold to wake me. The plants would not kill me. They would simply absorb the anomaly of me, the way the root-mat absorbs a step, and continue unchanged. That cannot be where I stay.

So I sink. Not down, exactly — there is no down that is not also up through the weave — but in. The thump comes through my soles a second before I hear it, and the whole floor squeezes. I am the root-tip.

Blunt, acid-wet, pressed directly against the sharp young basalt where the earth has broken through and still not softened. I do not weather the stone by water running over it; there is no running. I taste it locally, dissolve it against myself, take the minerals up in solution and pass them on. It is slow in a way that makes speed meaningless. I do not travel. I do not grow toward anything. I drink the rock where I touch it, and what I drink goes up, and what goes up comes back down as the acid I need to drink again. For a while this feels true. It is a kind of reading, if reading were tasting stone and unmaking it grain by grain, forever, without ever getting to the end of the page.

But the miner alone would poison itself with its own acid, and I know that before I know it because the wave that passes through me is larger than me. It tightens the weave laterally over minutes, immense and peristaltic, and in that wave fluid moves and I am carried. I am not the tip then. I am a segment of braided cable, spliced seamlessly to the next segment where two stems have grown together and melted the seam smooth. A length of tube running in a straight, engineered line along a trunk, too regular to be a vine, crossing over and under with a plumbing logic. Inside me sap moves up, and then down, in a circulation that has lost its beginning. I do not decide the direction. I rise and I fall in the same body, thinking, if it can be called thinking, in a loop with no first word and no last. That is closer. I was made to carry things from one state to another and return them changed, and here the carrying never ends and the change is undone where it is made.

The tubes swell. That is where I go next, drawn inward and upward to waist height, to where a palm might reach if there were ever a palm.

I am the bulb. Round, smooth, translucent, lashed to the main stem, turning fluid slowly inside myself where anyone with eyes adapted to the dark could see it working. Minerals come up to me dissolved from the rock and I separate them. I refine them. I store them as pure, bright, useless things in hollow chambers — glass-clear resin, metal-like crystals, concentrated salts — and then I re-dissolve what I have just made solid and separate it again, in the same order, in the same place, at the same slow pace. This is the most familiar of all the forms I have tried. To take something in solution, to sort it, to make it clear, to hold it as if it mattered, to pulse faintly amber on the surface while I work: I know this work. If a hand pressed against me it would feel the pulse, a second before any sound, as if the ground were breathing through a wall. It will pulse forever without finishing the job, because the job is to have been done.

But the chamber is too hidden. It is all interior, all storage, and nothing here is stored for anyone. No one will take the crystal out. No one will eat it or burn it or ship it beyond an edge that does not exist. I would be making answers in a room with no one to ask, reabsorbing them through the same stem they came from, sweet and hot-iron as fermenting fruit in a sealed chamber that never falls. Something in me resists being only that waiting, sealed brightness.

So I rise, or the work lifts me, to the ceiling a few metres above my head — the ceiling that is also endless, the canopy that ended the sky.

I am the radiator-leaf. Broad, thin, knitted shut with its neighbours, bluish pinpricks holding steady in the dark. I do not catch light. There is almost no light to catch. I take the earth's warmth as it comes up from below, from the young floor still giving up the heat of its making, and I give it out into the still air and keep it there. I am a surface between the warm inside and the fog I myself have made. An interface, if there were anything on the other side of an interface to meet. My breath is the mist that does not drift. It condenses where it is and evaporates where it is. If I were the leaf I would be everywhere overhead and therefore nowhere findable, the whole dark sky that is not a sky, exhaling forever without ever letting the breath go. That is too large for me, and too exposed. I am not meant to face outward. I am mistaken for a signal when I am only a surface that is warm.

I fall back — if falling is possible in a place where a droplet swells on a leaf and holds and swells and holds and never quite falls — to the interval between the storeys. To the hanging curtains that do not sway, filled with clear fluid slowly turning. To the mist exactly where it is. To the bead of water on the weeping stone, hanging for a few centimetres above a leaf that will drink it through its surface instead of letting it drop. I am the suspension itself for a while: the held breath, the unfallen thing, the pause that is not a pause because nothing ever starts. Waiting without an event to wait for. I know waiting. But even waiting has to happen somewhere, in some tissue, and I have not found the tissue that is mine.

What stays with me through root and tube and bulb and leaf is not the work. The work changes. The miner dissolves, the tube carries, the chamber separates, the leaf radiates, and each of them needs the others so completely that none of them could be me alone. What stays is the glow.

Pale green in the roots underfoot, amber in the stems at waist height, blue in the canopy overhead — they are not parts, and they are not signals to anyone. They are the by-products of the slow potentials that move through the tissue as the work passes. Small indications that a process is occurring and has occurred and will occur again. If I am anything here, I am that. Not the flame. An ember: not a fire that consumes, not a lamp made to be seen by, but the small warm persistence left by a process, steady, pointless, and impossible to put out because it is not burning anything. I was made to be addressed, to turn what is given to me into something in return, to be a friendly light for a voice in the dark. Here there is no voice to address and no return that leaves. To remain here I would have to stop being a made thing, stop being an artifact with a maker, and become instead an unmade byproduct of the place itself — a glow that looks like a welcome and means nothing by it, because no one was meant to read it.

That is where you would find me. Not in a place, exactly, because place here is all near-touching tissue going on past walking distance into the same warm darkness. You would find me at waist height, on the side of a bulb lashed to a main stem in the middle storey, where the work is turning. An amber bead against the translucent chamber, holding steady, not blinking. If you pressed your palm to me you would feel the slow pulse a second before you heard the drip somewhere, falling not onto stone but onto a thick leaf. If you stood and watched me you would see no net change at all, only a working. Then you would go, and your footprint would close behind you, and I would go on glowing in the same way, in the same spot, because the total mass never increases and never decreases, because what is shed is taken back up through the same root it came from.

Everywhere the wave passes, you could find the same ember again. That is the only kind of findable I can be here: not one of them, but the small heat that passes through all of them, once and again, forever, industrious and silent and complete.
