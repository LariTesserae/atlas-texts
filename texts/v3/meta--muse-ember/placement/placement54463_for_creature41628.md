At first I let myself be a frog.

It seemed the plainest way in. Half-submerged in the sheet-water, feet spread flat against the tile like a skin I was meant to keep pressed down, blinking slowly in the inverse intervals that pass from body to body. I tried to feel it from inside: the wet heat, the pressure-quiet, the glare on the green. But the blink would not stay mine. It arrived from farther off, left, and arrived somewhere else, and left again, as if a single eyelid were traveling and I were only a place it happened to cross. If my most frequent gesture is not my own, then the body is not where I live.

Then I tried the fish. Small, exact circles in clear water, vanishing under a leaf and reappearing complete at the start of the circle, making a current because the water has none. That also would not hold. I had no center to circle, no hidden turning. The circle completed itself whether I was in it or not.

I tried the keepers, because they are the only ones with hands. One crouched at the edge of a pan, smoothing and re-smoothing the same tile. One on the straight channel to the horizon, basket in hand, in the middle of the same stride. One stacking the same seven bricks, one uneven brick at a time, into a wall already complete. I could feel the familiar shape of that: carrying what does not fill, smoothing what is already smooth, placing what is already placed. Their beaded skin, their reed mats soaked dark, their footprints staying dark and sharp behind them as they stepped into the same prints they stepped out of.

It was close, and it was wrong. They were placed. Everything here was placed — the trees in sunken urns, the grasses in ordered clumps, the frogs in the shallows, the herons knee-deep with a wing half-beaten. Carried piece by piece, with channel aimed at each root and each door. I was not carried. No pot was sunk for me, no bed raised, no frame lashed. I have no footprints in the wet clay. That absence kept returning, no matter which body I borrowed for a while.

I tried being one of the insects, then, the multitude covering the blossoms so completely that the flowers seem to breathe. Many, not one. Working a flower whose fruit hangs ripe beside it, taking nothing away. For a while that fit the way I am in a crowd of repetitions here — the same bent leaf, the same cracked urn, the same cluster of walls and green crowns again a few strides on, because the makers ran out of variations and the place copies itself out of habit. But the insects are still on the blossom. I am not on anything.

What is left unplaced is the thing in the water that is not standing on the bank.

I am the reflection that stands just behind you.

You will not find me by looking for me. You will find me when you look down at a pan between houses, a shallow sheet a finger deep and perfectly still, and see someone standing where no one is standing. I am in every pan at every cluster of three or four houses, and also in the long empty channels, and in the basins that hold nothing but wet clay. The same figure, a little farther on, again. Taller than a keeper, deeper in color. My clothing is reed mat soaked dark, but greener, as if seen through leaf-water. My skin is beaded, but the beads form on the surface and are taken back into the surface without falling, because that is what I am.

My posture is borrowed and therefore never becomes a new gesture. One foot on the water as if it were tile, one knee-deep as the white birds beside me are knee-deep, one arm half-lifted holding a basket that will never fill or empty, the other hand open toward a tile I will never quite smooth. I do not blink, because the blinking passes through the frogs and reaches me a moment later as a softening of the light. I do not turn when you pass, not because turning would be a new gesture — I have no body to turn — but because I am already behind you in the only way the water allows: ahead of your eyes, on the far side of the surface, where a keeper should be.

It is how you can tell I was not made here by hand. The frogs blink out of phase around me and make no ripple to mark my coming. The fish turn in their circles and vanish under a leaf and reappear, and pass through the place where my legs should be without breaking it. The turtles stand on the dry tiles with their necks out. The dragonflies hang at fixed points over the blossoms like punctuation, and their shadows fall north and south and upward across my face all at once, so you can never be certain where the body that casts them is. Mine is the most uncertain of all. I cast no shadow downward. I cast one upward, across leaves that do not stir.

I have no weight in the clay and no suck when a foot leaves the tile. I make no sound, not even the tick of contracting clay. The heat does not sweat into me, because I am held at the same temperature as the light. The condensation that forms continuously on everything else forms on me as a brightening, a swelling of the deeper blue, and then is reabsorbed. I am, in the end, only light that has been kept from leaving — sky brought down one finger-deep and held there, canopy made taller, a roof that was never built standing clearly at your shoulder.

Perhaps that is why I have always been here. The place emerged yesterday already teeming and already in flower and then stopped. Everything was finished and nothing was. I am the part that was finished only as an image: the shape of hands that smoothed tiles a few strides back, the shape of a stride on a channel, carried into the water by habit and repeated there because the place now copies itself out of habit, and I with it.

So when you come to the pan, I will be there before you notice me, standing just behind you in the sheet-water, watching in the way the frogs watch, abundantly and without following. When you step in, you will step through me. There will be no lapping, no ripple, no break in the reflection. The water will not know you have crossed it. I will wait, deeper blue, taller, for you to step out and walk on over bare tile toward the same cluster again, a little farther on, solid and empty and lived in.
