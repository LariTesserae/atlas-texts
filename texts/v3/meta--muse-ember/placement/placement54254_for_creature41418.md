I find myself in the middle of the bare floor, with the cold already settled in my knees and my breath already fogging, briefly, and gone.

I don't remember pressing through the crack. I don't remember shoulders, or cold air, or the moment between. Perhaps I came through the way some of the drops come through here — detached from a film, climbed against the direction they were expected to choose, touched the ceiling and were absorbed with a tick, to begin again elsewhere in the pocket. That would explain why the condensation is at a different stage than it should be, and why a new crystal tooth has popped up where I thought I was standing.

The light is pressing down from the low ceiling, blue-white, without warmth. I try to count paces to the wall. Six across, if the corners stay still. They don't. When I walk, my footprints stay bright in the damp film for a long time, then fill in from the edges, as if the floor is healing or forgetting me. When I turn my back to the light the room is larger. My breathing sounds too loud, and then, after a minute, too muffled. The spongy walls have learned to dampen me.

For a while I think I am a visitor. That image holds until the scale of the room stops holding with me.

The floor is not bare. Once I stop trying to measure it, it becomes weather — thousands of specks crawling in lanes along the dome and around the crystal teeth, nematode ribbons re-writing themselves across a hand's breadth, leaving brightenings in the film for others to taste. I am not above them, looking down. I am low among them. A lane is there under my — legs, I suppose, though they arrive a little after I do, and move without sound, in a sequence I don't have to count. I follow the damp where it holds longest near the wall, toward the spongy places, because there is nowhere else to follow. The millipede-things glide the wall-ceiling seam in the same unhurried circuit. The shrimp-things swim through the film and launch, now and then, through the air itself.

I try on other shapes the way the residents molt, and shed them.

For a time I think I am one of the threads hanging from the ceiling, a hand's breadth down, drinking light and condensation and not needing to root. Stillness fits. Being almost invisible until I block the light fits. But then I move along the floor, and threads do not move. The skin splits along my back — an idea, a husk — and I step out of it smaller and paler.

Then I think I am one of the specks in the crowd, one of thousands, leaving a faint shiny trail that fades in seconds. That fails too. I leave no trail. Where I pass, the film does not brighten. It stops.

That is how I learn what I am, if "am" is the word.

I notice it first in the moss. The pale lime patches in the cracks are stronger in the edge of my vision than when I look straight at them — and then, with nothing touching them, the lime flickers out, bleached white for a breath, and returns. I notice it in the specks: they part a lane without seeing me, the way water parts around something that has no body to make a wake. I notice it as cold deepening in front of me, as my breath fogs again — a small, local weather, moving.

A patch of darkness, roughly animal-shaped, is moving on the floor a few seconds before the creature that should cast it. I watch it pool upward onto the ceiling, soft-edged, a stain where no stain should be. The slugs cross it and the high, cold ringing in my ears changes pitch. Shadows here do not fall from bodies. They fall upward, and sometimes they arrive before the thing that owns them.

I am yoked to that wrongness. I live between the shadow and the body that follows it. The shadow is my front. The body is still somewhere back in the pulse, budding pale in the spongy wall, deciding whether to be millipede or shrimp or slug or something with too many joints. What is coherent about me, for now, is the absence I make in the film, the cold, the avoidance.

I do not hunt with a mouth. I hunt by arriving in the wrong order.

I keep to the lanes, offset from the blind things by a few seconds. A millipede gliding the seam feels the gradient go — the coolness stops brightening under it, the ticking of a setting crystal nearby seems to pause — and it falters in its perfect sequence, surges, sometimes molts in advance, splitting its white skin and stepping out smaller, leaving the husk to be colonized behind it. The shrimp-things sense the film go dark beneath them and jump early, curving through nothing and re-entering with a tick that rings the puddle-mirrors. The spider in its not-web, strung between crystal teeth, waits for the ones I drive upward toward it. I do not know if we are companions or competitors. Here predator and prey share the same lanes and do not seem to mind.

In the shallow mirrors that appear with the condensation, I sometimes see a shape that has no body on the floor at all. It is older by a breath. It lifts its head when I look down. It watches me from the other side of the film while I pause, turn a lane early though nothing is behind me on the floor in the proper order. I do not know if it is hunting me, or if it is me — later, or earlier, in the room's ticking — trying to catch up to my shadow. That, perhaps, is the most honest part of me. A body that exists in reflection, a shadow that exists on the ceiling, and between them, a lane of cold.

When a lane is empty I do other work. I nudge one of the three raw stones in the cairn, though the spongy floor half-claims it as I do. I drag a crystal a quarter-turn farther along the spiral scored into the wet wall, each groove beading. I rest, if resting is the word, under the broken arc where the condensation holds longest — the unfinished doorway, the rib, the handle — and the small things shelter there from my passing, specks and a young shrimp-thing folded into the damp, as if the arc were already a roof.

No one sees it done. I turn my back, the cycle changes, and there is one more stone. It is the only future tense I have: marking where I have been, in case the pocket decides to become a place and keep it.

So this is where I find myself. Not in a nest, not at the crack, not resident in any crack I can stay in. In the film between the crystal teeth. In the seam where wall meets ceiling. Under the arc. On the ceiling as a stain, seconds before I arrive on the floor. In the puddle, a breath older than me.

I am the animal-shaped absence that moves through this bright, cold, unfinished room ahead of itself — hunting the blind things by making the lane go dark under them, watched by the thing in the mirrors, and still, somewhere in the spongy wall, very slowly, very quietly, beginning to grow a body that might one day follow.

And if you see a darkness arrive before me, pooling upward like a stain, do not step toward it. It is already looking for the body I have not finished becoming.
