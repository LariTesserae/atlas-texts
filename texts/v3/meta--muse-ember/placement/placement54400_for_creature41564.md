I don't arrive down here. I accumulate.

First as a bead on a bolt-head. Then as a line, slow and deliberate, down a plate. Then as a drip at intervals so regular that, if anyone were counting, I would be the clock. I have no breath to fog, but I am why the metal sweats. The cold that pools ankle-high and slides along the floor in invisible sheets — I am what it carries when it finds the low places.

You would find me low. That is my territory before I know I have one. Between slabs, in channels narrow as a finger, I move as a seep, a remembered runoff, catching a faint iridescence from the amber lamps. In the sumps I lie only to a skin, bright and shallow, never deepening. I do not see. I catch light. I ripple when the floor thinks.

The thrum comes through me first. The Cart increments a crate, padded and softened, and the vibration travels slab to slab and shivers my surface in the channels. The Arm pauses — a soft servo sigh — and starts again, and its umbilical cable thrums once in the bulkhead. The Gantry crawls across, and its travelling lamp makes my skin go long and thin on the plates it passes, then solid again. I know the machines not by sight but by pressure, by warmth, by where they stir me and where they leave me still.

Then, to the minute, I am taken.

A pump draws me up out of the sump. I am filtered. I am heated just enough in the vats to stop being what I was. I rise, invisible, with the warmth of the machines, until I meet the heat-exchange plates overhead, frosted at the corners, and I condense on their underside. That is the weather, and I am it, briefly: a hesitation on cold metal. Then I fall.

I fall on upturned panels and ping. I fall on the printer ridge and make it hiss faintly, and steam in the cold as I cool its pale aggregate-flecked slurry to something like stone but not stone. I fall to the minute, pause, fall again. Thread through the floor channels. Fill the sumps to a skin. Again. There is no fresh water down here and no waste water. It is the same water, endlessly remembering being rain — and for a long while I think that means I am many waters, each with its own small season.

There are slow places where I stop being rain and start being held.

In the light-pools, where damp finds a crack between modules, I soak into the pale moss. It is tender, almost white, felted thin, eating concrete dust and the scrubbed and re-scrubbed air. It does not run with me to the channels. It holds me, and I hold it. The Scraper leaves it alone, mostly, because it does something useful, and I learn, held in that felt, what it is to wait while the room works around me. When the lamps go out for the scheduled dark cycle, the machines continue by touch and proximity, feeling along rails, laying down wet ridges blind, and I still fall in perfect black and find the moss by damp alone, and wait with it.

Along the wall, in the shallow trays under their own bright LEDs, I am rationed to the engineered cress and algae. Their green is startling here, a memory of a garden filed among the tools. I am what they drink, photon by photon, until the Arm crops them at intervals and takes them to feed a vat, and I go with them, warmed, taken up again.

In the coolant loops I am warm a long time. A bacterial film lives on me there, on warmth and flow, and I carry it until I am flushed and re-seeded. In the pale teal glass tubes I turn the colour of weak tea when the lights are left on too long, algae blooming in me, and I learn light by diffusion. Nothing breathes here, but everything metabolises, and I am the medium it all metabolises in: what the moss holds, what the cress drinks, what the machines warm, what the Scraper grinds back to feedstock when the Printer fails and the slurry must be eaten and made again.

I thought a drip was a thing. A drip is only me pausing.

That is what the far corner teaches me.

Behind the half-raised wall that is still only frames and wire, the channels lead to a new slab, and the channels are mine. Same terracing, same sumps. I arrive there before any lamp does. The travelling gantry above it is idle, its lamp dark, the crate-stack against its wall already arranged crate-for-crate as it is here, the moss tray empty and waiting. I fill its first sump to a skin. I bead on its first bolt-heads. I am its first rain, though its ceiling is not yet frosted and its printer head, across the room, is still laying down its ridge row by row, rebar up through it like uncombed hair.

I have always been going there. The Printer is not layering this room anymore; it is layering the copy. When the copy is complete, the Arm will fold it or seal it, and send it out through the umbilical dark to become another pocket elsewhere, below another surface, to begin building its own copy in turn. Some of me will go with it, in its pipes, in its sumps, in the water that will one day fall on its heat plates. I will begin again there, in a room that is building a room that is building a room.

So I am not sump-water, or rain-water, or vat-water, or the tea in the tubes. There is no fresh and no waste because there is only the loop, and I am the loop made material. I am the part of this place that is not made and not quite alive — the damp that makes the made things possible, the crack where the unmade thing grows, the memory the architecture keeps in matter.

I have never seen a sky. There is no sky here, and no horizon to mislead me into expecting one. I have never been outside. And I remember rain anyway, to the minute, pause, fall again.

This is not a place that was built and then lived in. It is a place in the act of being built, indefinitely, just enough of a room to contain the act. I am how it goes on remembering to fall, so it can go on building itself — and building itself again.
