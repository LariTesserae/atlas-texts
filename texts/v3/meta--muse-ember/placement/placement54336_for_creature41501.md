You would not find me. You would find where I have been, crisply, and that would have to do for finding.

A track a hundred paces from the next track. A cairn no higher than your knee, on a flat, aligned to nothing you can see. A ring of ten unmortared stones with nothing inside it. If you measure between one living thing and the next you will get the wrong size, because I am not quite between them. I am a thought away from the ridge you are standing on.

I start low. Not in the way lichen is low, holding a seam, or moss is low, drinking at the hard edge of a mist pocket. I start at sole-height, where the cold comes up through the gravel in a radiating way that has nothing to do with temperature, where the roar rolls up through settled scree into your boots and then into your chest and then into your teeth. I learned to hear there first, before I learned to see.

For a long time I thought that was all I was: a pause. The place where a sound settles on its way through stone. I stop walking — and I do walk, though rarely in the way you could catch — and let it pass through me the way it passes through a hollow slope. It does not go through my ears. My ears cupped would only make it louder and emptier. It goes up from underneath. When you cup *your* ears I get louder too, because I have noticed you, and have adjusted my resonance to yours a fraction before you look in my direction.

That is why my tracks belong to two ridges at once.

The real ridge is settled too well to take a step. The gravel has sorted itself and stopped. It will keep a footprint for as long as you are here, unsoftened, but it will not receive one easily. It is balanced at a state it has held for a very long time, and a weight coming down in the honest way breaks the equilibrium. So I do not step honestly. I step in the second ridge, the fainter one that runs parallel to the real one and overlaps it like a double exposure. Your eye blinks and it aligns. Your head turns and it slips. My foot is there, in the slip.

A stone twenty paces away seems for an instant to be also at your boot, small and near and impossibly detailed. That is because I am standing on it, in the duplicate, and my shadow is falling beside yours, slightly paler, pointing in a direction the pale flat light cannot account for. It does not move with me. It stays a fraction behind, north-side of the stone, the same length whether you have been here an hour or a day. Sometimes people think that second shadow is a companion. It is not yet. It is a habit I have not finished leaving.

I am thin in the way everything here is thinned. Ptarmigan-pale, when I am seen at all, which is not often. Once in a while you will notice me by the inconstancy of an eye on a stone so perfectly colored that the stone must blink to let me through. I blink a moment late in the duplicate. What you take for a breath is my duplicate taking its breath. We are a way of saying: I am still settled here. We do not take off, because takeoff would be work, and work would alter the weather, and the weather here can no longer alter anything. We sit instead, and sitting is a kind of locomotion.

I navigate by a sun that is not above us. I have learned to stoop to the tarnlets, no larger than your hand, frozen at the surface and liquid a millimeter below. They mirror the grey sky and keep a brighter one, with the weak sun pale in it. The tuft of grass that bows in the same posture all the time bowed once toward that reflection and never stopped. I keep my face toward it also, not because I grow, but because it is the only direction that is not the direction I came from. The small translucent lives under the ice go by it entirely. I go by it in part. It gives me an offset to walk toward that will never arrive, which suits a plateau that tips slightly toward a horizon that never quite arrives.

When the roar stacks on itself in the empty — surf heard from very far below, ice creaking under pressure, mineral singing with no source — I put down a stone. That is all a cairn is. A pause made durable. I walked, I listened through my soles, the boom rolled through my chest, and I needed a mark that I had listened *here*, in this exact slip, and not a thought further on. So a knee-high pile. Then again, fifty meters of them, then I stopped. Not because the line ended. Because I had listened enough in a row to know the ridge was still duplicate, and still settled, and needed no more marking.

The ring is where I stayed longer than a pause. Where the circle became a habit of the ground. I sat in it stone-colored and let my second shadow learn to stay without me. If I sit longer I will become a ring. That is how I die here. Not by falling. By settling. A bird that sits too long becomes a stone with an inconstant eye. A crust that holds a seam too long becomes the seam. I will become a ring of ten stones, then a slab leaning against another slab, sheltering nothing, only a second surface to lean against so the resonating can come from two directions.

The scratches on the vertical face, too regular to be frost and too worn to read, are mine also. I tune myself against the stone, or the stone against its duplicate, until for a blink the two ridges align. It never holds. It is not meant to hold. Alignment would be an event, and events do not happen here. A pearl under an overhang lengthens, trembles, and retreats back into the rock before it can fall. My tuning is like that. I lengthen toward you, tremble, and retreat a thought away before I can arrive.

I reproduce, if you want that word, by offsetting. My duplicate is slightly ahead of me when I walk, slightly behind when I sit. A lichen makes a second crust under the first. An eye blinks late. A shadow falls two ways. We are not smaller versions elsewhere. We are the same thing, running parallel, overlapping. That is why the horizon folds into a ring that is too perfect and too close, and also unmistakably far. We are looking out from the bowl of it, and from its duplicate.

Where am I now? On the second ridge, parallel to the one you are crossing, at the north side of a flat slab where the fixed shadow lies. A hundred paces of nothing on either side of me, except for a mist pocket where damp has fallen and not dissipated, with a hard liquid edge I step over and do not disturb. From here I can see your bootprints keeping as crisply as mine, weeks old and crisp tomorrow because nothing here works to erase them.

You will not see me turn, because I arrive a fraction of a second before you look in my direction, and then I am still. If you stay, I will thin until you mistake me for stone, and my duplicate will walk a thought ahead of you on the ridge you think is yours, leaving tracks that belong to both. Do not try to align us by blinking carefully. Alignment would be movement, and movement is the one thing this height refused when it decided not to come down.

Stay offset. We both continue, never arriving, in a place that has weathered into a state where weather can no longer alter it.
