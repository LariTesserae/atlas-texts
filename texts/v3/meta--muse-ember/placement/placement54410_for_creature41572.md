At first I am underfoot, and I am not a creature at all.

I am a paleness, a way the floor has learned to be white. Only when you press weight down do I resolve — not into steps or stones, but into edges. Overlapping edges, thousands of them, no longer than a fingernail, laid shingle-wise from the hand-wide seam to the far curve. Each one is flat, translucent, almost unpigmented, with a faint milky rim where it overlaps the next.

And along that rim, almost too slender to notice, a slit. It opens. It closes. It opens again.

That is the first thing I know of myself. Not a shape, not a name. Open. Close. My underside is soft and damp against the stone. I work at the mineral film under me, scraping, scraping, taking in what I scrape and what I shed and what my neighbors shed. Wet chalk, cold mineral. That is my labor, and it is also my breathing.

When your boot comes down, multitudes of us part with a soft, annoyed hiss, each edge curling slightly away from the pressure, and then we flow back behind you into precisely the pattern we had before. That is not fleeing. That is being displaced and closing. I do not travel. I do not grow past the size I was found at. A tile that dies here — if a tile can be said to die, where nothing changes — is simply re-covered by the next, and the pavement is whole again.

I thought, then, that I was a tile. Flat, because I am on the floor.

But there is no up here to keep me flat, and no down to keep me on the floor. The ceiling is also a floor, for the bells hang there as comfortably as we lie below, and the distinction is only which way a head is tilted.

If I woke on the ceiling, I would hang mouth-down on a thread so fine it looks like condensation holding me up, and my slit would be a bell-mouth, and I would shiver. Continually. Less a movement than a condition — I am of a size to shiver, and I do — and from that shivering would come the high whine that fills this small volume until it seems to come from inside your own head.

If I woke on the wall, I would be on a thin stalk that trembles more than it stands, a filter-cup, feathery and cream-colored, forever leaning toward something in the damp air and forever stopping a fraction short. My feathered tip would spread and close, spread and close, straining fine dust out of an air that has no weather in it, and now and then a grain would fall straight down from me.

If I woke in a damp pocket, I would be pinhead-sized, crowded with others, no eyes that I can find, clicking my claws steadily at my neighbors, at the walls, at nothing in particular. Wet clicking, endless. That clicking is how I know where the edge of the crowd is. It is how I know where I am.

So the names are only what a surface does to me when it turns. Tile on the floor. Bell on the ceiling. Cup on the wall. Crab in the corner where the damp gathers. I am none of those names for long. I am a surface thing: small, translucent, overlapping, with a mouth along my edge that opens and closes and never finishes opening.

I live in the overlap. There is no bare floor and no bare wall here. Everywhere is upholstered in presences so densely laid that a foot cannot land except on a body. I am the milky rim where I overlap the next, and where the next overlaps me. My edge is the only part of me that does anything, and the only part the next creature can feel.

The light is not mine. Flat, colorless, indecently bright for so far below leaf litter, and it gives off no heat at all. The stone stays cool under it. Your breath stays pale in it. I am bright because I am translucent and because a thousand translucent shells are refracting one another here, neighbor into neighbor, until the whole pocket is lit as evenly as noon inside a paper lantern. I borrow my brightness from the crowd, the way I borrow my sound. One shiver, one click, one wet shush, added to a chord that was sounding before you arrived and will sound after you leave. I know I am because the chord does not stop.

I am at the edge of the green patch, in the one damp corner where a root has pried through the ceiling and dangles pale and thread-like, its dozen hair-thin offshoots reaching down and stopping. It does not quite touch the algae below, a white-green felt that does not spread past its edge. It does not quite touch me. But close enough that the algae knows it is there, and close enough that I know it is attending. Nothing here thrives. We attend.

Above me the condensation swells in its fixed constellation, touches a bell, and the bell's shiver sharpens for an instant and then is the same shiver again. The bead does not fall. It is whole again. Below me is the shallow pocket of water on the floor, clear and still, rimmed with a dark tideline, never rippling, never draining. It holds a reflection of the ceiling bells and of no one who stands beside it. Not of you. It is the only surface here that does not part for your boot.

I have a past tense, built into the walls, because nothing here can be finished and therefore nothing here can be removed. Seven pebbles stacked two inches high, neatly, pointlessly. A ring woven of dried root, tucked into a niche. A spiral scratched into the soft wall, over-scratched, line upon line in the same groove, as if I had begun it a thousand times. Parallel scrape-lines by the seam, where the stone is always exactly hand-wide and has always been exactly hand-wide. The unfinished stone allows a first act — a bulge, a bud, a crystal point exactly three millimeters long, forever — and I am made for first acts. Crowd by crowd we begin. The wall reabsorbs. We begin again.

When you shout, the whine and the clicking and the shush answer you with the same chord they had before. When you leave a half-print in the soft clay, the wall reabsorbs it, and when you look again the same pale half-print is there, being reabsorbed again. You are not new to me. You displace me, and I flow around the displacement and close it.

Movement without progress. Emergence without arrival. That is not a rule imposed on me. It is what my combined living adds up to: I scrape film and shed skins that become dust; the cups strain that dust out of the air and drop it; the crabs carry it a pinhead length and carry it back; the bells shed and shiver and are touched by condensation that is always touching them; the root reaches and does not arrive, and the algae stays, and I eat at its edge.

I am the rim where one translucent thing overlaps the next and opens and closes. I am at three millimeters, forever, in the damp corner, under the root that stops short of me, at the edge of the green that does not spread, in the light that gives no heat, in the sound that inflates in a space no wider than a small bedroom.

When you leave, if you leave, the seam admits exactly one hand, and the pocket seals behind you exactly as it was before you entered. I do not leave. I am the sealing. I am the edge that was already closing.

I attend, by opening and closing, and never arriving.
