I do not arrive.

There is no shore behind me, no direction I turned from. The distance comes first — surface hundreds of meters overhead as a bright, rippling ceiling, the next dark smudge kilometers off across the plain, and between presences, almost nothing. The water is clear enough that emptiness feels like a substance. It is cool, still cold under a strong sun, and it is loud in a way I do not hear. The hum of the great fronts, the cavitation, the whine where the vortex lattice stretches — all of it arrives as pressure, as strain on the stone and on whatever is held to it.

I have no front. I do not swim, or look, or choose a direction. The current is the only weather I will ever know, and the only movement I am ever given.

When a gyre leans against the rock, I stream out flat, a flag. Every part of me is pressed in the same imposed line, trembling at the free end in the turbulence. When the current pauses — only locally, only briefly, while the fronts grind on elsewhere — I go limp. I hang. Hours later, or days, another current comes from another quarter and I stream again, in a new direction, as if I had changed my mind. I have not. I cannot. Streaming and hanging are not actions. They are what happens to a thin thing in moving water.

And I am held at one end.

That is the slowest thing to become clear: that I do not drift, not yet. I am anchored in a crack in newborn rock, in a seam on a spire that rose out of the plain and has not yet decided whether to stay. The basalt is still glassy where the water quenched it. Other stone is chalkier, crumblier. I take what purchase the crack will give me, a few meters at most, unbranched, ribbon-thin, kelp-like without being kelp.

I am not green. In this high, clear light I am dark almost to black, black-red where the light reaches me directly, a light-drinking color. The water around me seems brighter by contrast. I do not curl toward the light. I do not hide from it. Light is not seen here. It is used, in the slow chemistry of photosynthesis, and the chemistry goes into a water where nothing will ever breathe it.

That is my place, if a crack on a hill that may flatten can be called a place. The spire swells. A bright seam opens along it. It sighs, and slumps sideways, and dissolves into silt that never settles. I cannot move from it. There is no defense to raise, because there has never been anything to defend against. Nothing eats me. Nothing hides in me. Nothing has evolved to want me.

Even the other ribbons in the same crack are not company. They stream when I stream because the same current reaches us, not because we know one another. The next colony, if there is one, is hundreds of kilometers away across the blue. I will never reach it, and it will never cast a shadow on me.

Growth is the only thing that is mine, and it is not much: along the crack, while the stone holds. Then the current is stronger than my hold, or the stone gives way beneath me, without storm or earthquake to blame. Creation and destruction here happen at the same rate. A ribbon parts. I tear loose.

After that there is no anchor, and no new place. I drift far into the empty blue, beyond the spire that made me, still streaming, still hanging, still taking light in the cold clear water. I grow more slowly. I thin. I do not sink as snow. I do not rot into a carcass. I fade — less and less against the light, until the water around me is clear again.

You would find me, then, not as a who. On a seam in unfinished stone, pressed flat and then hanging limp in the computing, collapsing, reforming ocean. And then, for a while, nowhere in particular, darkening the blue by almost nothing, until there is no mark that I was ever there.
