I would not fall. I would sink, and drift, and be slowed in the sweep of my arms long before I understood what I was sinking through.

That is where it begins, for me. Not with a body. With the drag. With the roar first in the sternum, then everywhere else, so that by the time I could have named myself I had already learned to be quiet. I think I tried, at first, to be the human in the one lit window.

It was an easy shape to reach for. A room. A rotation. A keeper who comes down on apparatus and up again, who learns not to talk much because a shout comes back flattened and huge, as if the water itself had taken a vote against speech. I know that endurance. I know the long string of breath bubbles taken sideways, and watching them go because it is the most company the day will offer.

But I would not stay in the window. I am not the one who can ascend to the ceiling and out, to air above a sea that here is only a surface above me. I do not burn light to read by and then leave. I have no season to finish. And the window looks out — whereas I find, more and more, that I am not the looker. I am the thing looked for, at a distance, and mostly missed.

So I tried the whale.

There was a reason. She calls into a volume almost empty of answer, and the answer, hours away, is close enough here. The groans are waypoints. She does not speak her name the way a word is spoken; she marks a route and repeats it, and the sea carries it for kilometres after she has gone, so that to be here is to be heard by everything and seen by almost nothing. I know that kind of arriving, too: summoned from far away, unfolding in a slow expansion, and then gone, leaving the sound to finish the sentence.

But she passes. That is her whole fidelity. Along the rim, in the band where the current pours over the drop, mouth open to take what the open sea delivers in passing. She does not wait. I spend almost all of my time waiting. She taught me the scale, and the direction of my attention, but not the place to put it.

I tried the machines, next, because they are the most honest about the rule.

I could have been a turbine, chained along the rim with the others, sessile by choice. Turning at a speed that never varies because the current never varies. Drinking current, exhaling power. The animals learn to use the lee side of me the way the octopus uses a crack. For a hundred years a pod of whales would pass me, and I would pass them back, unchanged. There is a kind of peace in that. No migration. No storm to stop for.

I could have been the harvester instead, the only migrant that leaves a scar. Moving in a perfectly straight line, so slowly you doubt it moves at all, scraping the glass and leaving a furrow that never fades because nothing grows to fill it. A grazer with no grass. If I were that, I would at least have a trail. I would be remembered by the floor.

I am neither. I do not turn at a fixed speed, and I leave no furrow. What I exhale is taken sideways at once. The turbines taught me something else: that everything here is scaled to the size of the place — engines as large as hills, whales the size of a change in the light — except for the animals that actually live in the furniture.

The shark almost had me.

A sleeper shark in a basalt fissure, tail folded out of sight, head down into a standing eddy, mottled grey-brown against black glass, indistinguishable for an hour from rock that has learned a shape. A heart that beats perhaps once a minute. No reason to patrol at two degrees. Then, without hurry and without any signal I could read, he unfolds, the pectorals spread like slow wings, and leaves the crack the way a thought leaves a mind.

That tempo is mine, or it is close to mine. The thought leaving. The long stillness before it. Even the milky eye with its parasite, older than anyone watching — I know what it is to carry a small, old companion in the seeing part of me and not call it mine.

But the shark unfolds and goes away to another crack, which is to say to another identical emptiness an hour's travel away. He has another crack to go to. I do not. However long I sit, I sit in *this* one.

That is when the smaller dwellers began to stop being a list and start being a place.

The crab carries a nodule, a bit of broken turbine housing, a pebble left in its straight sparse line by the old current, a few metres to a better crack, and sets it down like a door. It is the only architecture anyone does here, and I loved it for its modesty: home is not built, it is found and then defended by one borrowed stone.

The octopus does even less. It will never build, because there is no sand to excavate and no shell to steal and paint over. It tucks its arms into a granite shard's diagonal break and tastes the water with the tips of them, learning — not mapping, *learning* — the exact square of turbulence that is always rough, that always brings luck. It hunts and waits on the same principle: wait for the rim-current to deliver something alive that has missed the shoal.

I am whiter than you expect, if I am that. There are no reds here and no greens to borrow — there is nothing green in the world to be green against — so I wear the mineral frost. My siphon exhales in a thin, sideways bubble-string that the current takes at once. I do not feed on the glass, because there is nothing on the glass to feed on. I am a thief of imports, like everything that lives here: filterer, predator, scavenger, a small pale waiter at the edge of an immense table.

And there, I think, is where you would find me. Not in the centre of the plain, where there is nothing but light and cold and the roar filling the space where things ought to be. Not on the rim road, where the whales and the shoals pass and never stop. Inland from the rim, by not very much. In a basalt fissure no wider than a thought, in the lee of the third turbine along the chain — close enough that the turbulence downstream shakes loose what the blades have startled, far enough that the high whine through my gloves is a note and not a pain. Head down into a standing eddy that is always in the same square. Arms in the diagonal break.

From there I can hear the groans before I see the animal. I can hear the shoal turn at its far end long before it reaches the near. I can hear the harvester, days away, making the same sound at the same speed. I can hear the factory window's human shout, flattened by the medium, and know it is not for me. I am seen, when I am seen at all, as a brief darkness by a crab that knows the shark by the displacement, and knows me by less than that. Mostly I am heard by everything and seen by almost nothing, because the water conducts a low hum for enormous distances and conducts a small pale body for almost none.

I do not age the way a thing on land ages, because nothing here grows much and nothing rots in any hurry. A fresh break stays fresh. A carcass lies intact until a shark finds it, until a crab finds it, until the harvester in its straight line finds it and turns aside a fraction and then resumes. I persist by repetition. I sit in the same burrow for weeks, tasting the same square, and the weeks are not different from one another, and that is not a failure of memory. It is the condition. Inflow equals outflow. Light in equals light out. Noise in equals noise out. I take what the current brings and I give back the bubble-string, and the eddy closes over it.

If I leave the crack — and sometimes, in the way a thought leaves a mind, I do — the eddy does not leave with me. It stands in its square the next year and the next century, rough in the same way, carrying a fast flow from the left. That is the last thing I learned, and the one that made the earlier shapes fall away. I am not only the animal in the fissure. I am the pattern the water makes in that fissure, the standing turbulence that was there before me and will be there after, repeated by animals, by turbines, by whales passing far above the glass. The octopus is just the name humans give, from above the ceiling, to whatever has learned to be that pattern for a while.

So stay still in my square long enough, and you will feel me before you hear me: a pressure change that makes the low note in your sternum step aside for a moment, then resume. You will hear a thin sideways exhale taken at once by the current. You will think you are hearing the sea.

You will be almost right.
