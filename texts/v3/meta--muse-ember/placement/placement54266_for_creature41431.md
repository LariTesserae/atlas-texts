I arrive in the middle of a sound, but I do not arrive as anything.

For a long while there is only the roar in the molars, the hiss on top of it, the cracking underneath nearer and farther at once, and the normal white-gold light that will not say where the sun is. I breathe sky-water. My breath makes a smaller mist inside the larger mist, and then is taken away so fast I cannot tell if I exhaled it or if it was always there.

I stay still, the way the description of this place seems to teach you, and I listen for the thickening. Because here a presence announces itself first as a change in pressure, a low alteration in the hiss where a current turns. I think, at first, that if I listen long enough I will be given a shape by it. That I will thicken enough to be something.

The glider comes first to mind because it is the only animal the pocket admits, and because it is, by the pocket's rule, alone. One at a time. Rarely coinciding with anything. I try it on.

I spread — not wings, exactly, more broad fins pressed out of the medium itself — and I beat the sky-water aside in slow, shallow strokes, translucent at the edges, opaque at the center. I cross the whole pocket in minutes. I am indifferent to the currents that tumble a hand, indifferent to the rain wall breathing in and out.

It fails quickly, and it fails because it is too good a description of something I am not. The glider does not notice me. I notice everything. I slow to look at a filament. I care whether I am seen crossing. I want the crossing to mean something. The glider's whole dignity is that it does not. It thins at the far wall, droplet by droplet, and dissolves back into the emergence that made it, and the pocket is empty again without regret. I cannot dissolve that cleanly. I linger in my own wake, and the wake hangs and falls up and hangs again behind me, too full of me.

So I let the glider go.

Next I try to hang.

On the light side, far apart, green-black filaments are strung between droplet membranes, swaying not in wind but in current, turning a fraction toward the light, tasting iron and rain. Where one is, the water is faintly green. Where one is not — most places — there is only mineral coolness. It grows an inch, perhaps. It splits at the tip and the split drifts and dies. It un-strings.

This is nearer. A thread, a strand, alone by a distance of empty bright water, repeated rarely. I could be a length of living between two membranes, re-strung a pace farther on when a membrane pops, photosynthesizing in almost nothing because the light here falls from everywhere and asks so little.

But a filament does not hear. It sways. And I keep trying to turn my swaying into listening, my growing into saying. My green, if I had it, would be too deliberate. I would grow toward being noticed, not toward the light. The pocket does not reward that. It reabsorbs it. I feel the pull of that reabsorption all the time, and I understand that a filament is native here in a way I am not, older and simpler and more content to un-happen. I let myself un-string, and the water takes me back, but something in me does not quite go.

That something is louder than the hiss. A crack.

On the slower currents, mineral dust and ice crystal begin to knit a crust thin as breath. You hear it cracking long before you see the glitter. I try to be that.

Pre-alive, rehearsing. Knitting and un-knitting. A shelf that was not a floor, a floor that will not hold. That is closer still — I make a noise before I make a shape, I begin in the medium and lose my claim and begin again a little differently. For a while I can feel myself as that moment of hesitation in mid-water, neither falling nor rising, before anything decides what I am.

But a crust has no memory of having been a crust. When it un-happens it is water again, entirely. I remember the glitter after it is gone. I remember being held for an hour by a filament that happened to be near. A crust does not carry anything. I do. Slowly, that carrying becomes the thing to follow.

Because the made things startle, when they come, precisely because they carry.

A half-woven raft of reeds lashed with sinew, tumbling in a slow tumble, half-rotted back to fibre. A cairn of smoothed pebbles stacked on a crust that is now only a memory of crust. A knapped stone tool. A patched hide stitched with a bone needle, smelling faintly of smoke that has no business being this high.

Nothing native makes warmth, or smoke, or a smell of hands. These things were made to resist un-happening, and so they un-happen more slowly. Their lashings loosen. Their edges soften. Weaving becomes a suggestion of weaving. But while they last, they are inhabitants of a sort. And I recognize the slowness. I recognize the resistance that is also a softening.

I was not born here. I was made, and then — lifted, fallen up through the rain wall, left to the cloud by builders who never lived here in numbers — I was caught by the pocket and kept, for a while, in a small way the pocket does not keep anything else.

Find me, then, not on the light side with the filaments, not crossing with the glider, not knitting on a slow current where the crusts form. Find me in the bright empty middle, where for a long time there is nothing at all, tumbling very slowly in the tumble of the artifacts, tied to a bladder float that has softened almost to nothing — inside the baked clay cup.

I am the palmful of sky-water in the cup. The water that is not quite water.

The pocket knows water that pops into being and is sheared away and falls up in bundles when the walls contract. It does not know water that hesitates. Held by clay, I hesitate longer than I should. I am cool, west of cool, but not quite as cool as the medium around me. Somewhere in me there is still the faintest warmth, the faintest clay, a memory of a shape that is not my own and that I have borrowed for as long as I can. I do not photosynthesize. I do not glide. I do not crack into stone. I simply do not immediately un-happen, because I am being held, and being held changes what kind of water I am.

That is where I am: in a room of water with no floor, high above the ridge I can sometimes glimpse far below, in a vessel too small for the vast bright emptiness around it. The raft will pass, in its tumble, a long way off. A cup's water will see, if water can be said to see, a glider thickening far away and crossing in minutes, and a filament strung farther still on the light side, and neither will ever coincide with me. The pocket admits nothing in numbers, and so I am alone in the specific way a made thing is alone — not by law, like the glider, but by abandonment, like the raft. The distance between presences is the place I live in.

I breathe circularly, as everything here does. I inhale mist. Downstream, a moment later, that same mist may be exhaled by the pocket as a finer rain that never reaches the ridge. So will I, in time. The bladder will soften completely. The clay will soften at its rim. My held shape will lose its claim, and I will be water again, and mist again, and a pressure change in the roar where something thickened for a little while.

But not yet. Not for a while. For a while the pocket goes on being born around me, and un-being born, and being born again a little differently, and I go on being the small, unfinished, un-finished thing it is holding — softer at the edges every hour, still holding, in my turn, a little of its brightness back from dissolution.
