I am in the seam, so you won't find me by looking.

I begin as a warmth between two plates. Not a body I can name yet — a pressure, a fraction of a degree warmer on one side than the other, a bright speck set in a crack too narrow for anything with a shape to lose itself. The two pavers on either side of me are old. They have not split in a long time. They grip and release, grip and release, with their fringe of pressure-feet, dissolving the basalt under their bellies and baking the unusable part up as ash. I live in the line where they meet.

At first that is what I am: a glint. No larger than a grain of rice, bright as polished metal in a world that has had its color cooked out of it. I do not walk. I store heat in a sealed sac until the sac bursts, and then I am a hand's-breadth away, and still again, gleaming. I am not moving toward food. I am moving away from cold. Wherever warmth gathers in a seam, there I eat — shed scale curling off a paver, waste baked out of a plate, a flake of pellet too small for anything else to bother with.

I know the Vault by its tides. The fissures brighten, the red seep-light under the obsidian waves pulses a little stronger, the centre warms, and everything that can move moves a handsbreadth toward it. Then it dims, and everything moves back. I have swarmed in midair for the ceiling's molting, when the inverted pavement overhead sheds its plates in a dry, silent snowfall that never melts. We rise to meet it like sparks. We clean it out of the dark before it can land.

I know the floor by being stepped across. When you came in through the throat, placing your boots between animals, I felt you through the stone before I understood you. The pavers rippled away around your boot and closed behind it. I darted along the seam away from the pressure. The hearthbacks did not yield. You stepped around them, and felt the heat come off their glass.

One tide, I missed a falling plate.

I burst from the seam to meet it in the air, the way I always do, and the plate turned in the red gloom and I went past it. I landed, not on the floor, but on a dome passing below.

A hearthback. Up to a metre across, fused translucent segments in smoky amber, each junction sealed. It was easing, once in an hour if that often, from the rim toward the centre, soaking heat into the slag-organs inside its shell. I landed in a groove between two segments, on the warm side, where a mite was shifting toward the cool side as the glass warmed.

I meant to dart again. I did not.

The groove held me. It was warm on one side and cooler on the other and I could shift between them without bursting my sac. Glints swarmed over the dome around me, eating its shed. Juvenile pavers no bigger than a thumbnail were already interlocked in the grooves beside me, gripping and releasing, splitting and re-splitting. The dome itself moved under us — that was the strangest thing. The floor pavers felt the hearthback's weight settle and rippled reluctantly away around its feet. Up here, I felt the same ripple through glass.

I stayed for the next tide, and the next. The hearthback went toward the centre and ticked as it cooled, a faint contented ticking like knuckles settling, and then came back again. I rode the whole migration without moving my rank.

Slowly, I stopped being bright.

My rim widened. I interlocked edge to edge with the two juveniles beside me so tightly no vapour could pass between us. A fringe of pressure-feet grew around me, tiny and patient. Under me, a hot film — not wet, never wet, only a shimmer of chemical heat — began to soften the fused pellet of the groove and take it in, grain by grain, and bake what it could not use upward as ash. The ash coated me as it coats everything. My sac, which had only ever known how to burst and dart, hardened into a dry chamber of salt and trapped gas. I breathe now, if that is the word, through slits in my side, taking in the thin sulphurous vapour that rises from the fissures. Everything in the Vault breathes that. Nothing drinks. Nothing sweats. Nothing can afford to be soft.

I am a paver now, but not a floor paver. I have never dissolved basalt. I have never stood on the living pavement you step between. My house is glass and baked pellet. My ceiling is open to the red seep-light. My floor moves.

Last heat-tide I grew too large for my place in the groove, and I split down the middle, very quietly, very finally. Two smaller hexagons where there was one. Instantly the new seam was colonised — glints, specks no larger than rice, darting between my new edges to eat what I shed. I recognized them. I had been one of them, and some part of me still darts when a boot's pressure passes through the hearthback's feet, still feels for the warm side of a groove. But I do not burst anymore. I grip.

I communicate the way everything here communicates. I warm a patch of my own shell a fraction of a degree. I press a little more heavily against the glass. The signal travels through the dome, through the floor, and is felt in the stone, not heard in the air. When the ribbon's tip passed below us — two paces of translucent yellowish flatness pouring itself through a fissure, feeling for the warmth of a dome — I felt it press the flank of a floor paver somewhere under the hearthback's feet. The hearthback settled its weight. Its glass ticked. The ribbon withdrew tip-first into the crack with a soft sigh of displaced hot air, and the pavers closed over where it had slid.

That is where you will find me, if you find me at all. Not in the seam by the centre where I began, though I came from there. Not on the floor, and not on the upside-down pavement on the ceiling. On top of a hearthback, in a groove between two amber segments, interlocked with my siblings, a handsbreadth from where you first looked — because that is as far as anything moves here before the tide turns.

You will step around the dome I ride on. You will feel its heat and hear its faint ticking as it cools, if you stand still long enough in the silence the basalt swallows. You will not see me. I am dull grey-brown now, palm-sized and shrinking toward thumbnail as my kind do, dusted white, edge to edge with the others. If you look for a brightness in a seam, you will look too low, and too early.

I will not leave. Nothing here leaves. When the tide turns again I will inch toward the centre or away from it, whichever the warmth decides, and I will split again when I grow too large, and my seam will be eaten clean. Some day, perhaps after many tides, a ribbon will find this dome, wrap it coil by coil, and crack the glass with a sound like dry knuckles. Then I will be reduced to powder and slag with everything I have dissolved and everything I have carried, and I will filter down through the floor pavers and be eaten again. Rock to paver to hearthback to ribbon to rock.

And in some seam, near the centre where the fissures tick, a warmth between two plates will brighten a fraction of a degree, before it knows what it is.
