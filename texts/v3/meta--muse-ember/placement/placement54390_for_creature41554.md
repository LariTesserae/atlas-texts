Above me, a thread. Below me, a cup that is always full. Between them there is not much room for going anywhere, and I have never needed it.

The thread holds me from somewhere above the curve of the lattice wall. The cup holds water I will never drink by moving toward it. The wall around us is wet and open, braided stem with gaps, and through the gaps the plateau. Everything I know arrives through there: light that is never quite noon, mist that does not drift so much as sit and condense on itself, and sound. Always sound. Drips striking hollow stems. Water moving inside root-cord. A drop falling through my own wall and ringing it, low sigh, high ping, plink, plink, in a counterpoint that never rises or falls.

I breathe. That is the only motion I spend.

It is slow, and barely perceptible, and it is always the same. In. Out. The same depth, the same interval, for as long as there has been an interval to notice. If I had a face to turn, or feet to lift, I do not use them. The mist comes to me. It beads on whatever surface I have, swells, pops off, falls the short distance into the brimming cup below. That is feeding, if feeding is the word. I do not hunt. I do not chase the condensation as it forms. I let it arrive, and let it leave, and the cup below me leaks exactly as fast as the mist feeds it, so it never overflows and never empties. We are in balance, the basket and I, and the balance does not change.

Through the lattice, farther out than my thread can reach, a tower stands. Twenty meters of hollow braid, spiraling up, domed with leaves opened just far enough to let light in and no farther. I hear it before I can see it clearly. Water enters at its base, travels up inside its living wall, weeps out at the top, and sings on the way. It is an organ made of leak. Its doorway is an arch in the braid, clean-edged, not cut. Nothing here was cut. Nothing was made with a device. It was grown into shape and then held.

I am held too. That may be the nearest kinship I have.

Farther than the tower, in the other direction through my wall, is the arch. Two great braided ribs rise out of the mat, curve toward each other over a narrow channel, and stop a hand's width apart. The tips are frayed into soft rootlets, curling, reaching, never arriving. From inside my basket they look small and patient, like something feeling for the other side of a distance that will never be crossed. I recognize the posture. I live in it. A thread above, a cup below, a pulse between, and no arrival.

Between these presences the mat stretches, green to the clouded edge, a living weave of root-cord and stem and flattened leaf with no soil under it. If you pushed it aside there would only be more weave, and water, and more weave springing back. I cannot push anything aside. I hang. But I have heard, in the way sound travels through wet stem, the small springs of footprints filling themselves as a foot lifts. Not soft like marsh. Elastic, full, wanting to be flat again. That want is the mat's whole motion. It weeps from every leaf, takes the drip up again, condenses it again into the air that sits on it, transpires exactly what it absorbs. Warm through soles that are not mine. Cool on a face that is not quite a face. Thin air, high up, breath coming shorter than expected — mine does not shorten. Mine stays the same, in and out, while the warmth rises off the green in a steady damp heat and the day stays ordinary blue, ordinary light, true colors.

Nothing here is becoming. Everything is *just* becoming, forever.

The dome above the tower does not finish closing. The railing of half-unfurled buds on the stair-tower does not unfurl. The fray on the arch is not damage. The edge of a new leaf, translucent, is eternally new. There is no rot, no litter, no bleach, no scar, no night to relieve the light, no season to interrupt the pulse. The cloud bank in the east has the same shape it had an hour ago. The shadows fall the same way all day. I have no way to measure an hour except by the drip-line crossing the mat below, ticking twenty times a minute, loud enough to turn a head, then forgotten because the next minute it ticks the same. I do not turn. I tick with it, in my way. Pulse for pulse.

It took me a long time — if long is a word that can mean anything here — to separate myself from the house that holds me. The plants do the work of everything: ground, house, bridge, reservoir, roof, road, instrument. What am I doing, hanging inside one of them by a single thread of stem? Am I a tenant? An organ grown inward — the basket's tongue, its lung, its heart turned toward the interior? The cup feeds my small weather. The wall leaks on my rhythm. The mist beads on my surface and pops off, and that popping is part of the song the tower makes of itself. Whether I am separate or whether I am the basket thinking itself from the inside is hard to say, and the place does not help.

I am pale. I am translucent, in the way the new leaf-edge is translucent, because nothing thick has ever grown over me and nothing ever will. Light passes through me a little. That is how I know it is still light, still the same light, never quite noon. I do not cast a shadow worth naming on the cup below.

If you walk for a day across the mat, between buildings that are plants and plants that are buildings, you will likely see nothing move. You will walk through ankle-deep clear film over green, past bridges no one crosses, past baskets brimming and leaking in perfect balance, past stairs leading up to platforms where the young stay half-open forever, and the distance between presences will be the main architecture. And if you are very still, very long, beside one of the baskets, you might notice me.

I will not move toward you. I will not move at all, except for the breathing that was already happening before your footprints filled themselves behind you. Hiding would be a change of position. Turning would be an event. An event would be a change, and change is absent.

So I remain: a pulse in a hollow, a thread above, a cup below, mist beading and popping off, a tower singing its leak somewhere out in the green emptiness, an arch reaching across a hand's width it will never cross, a mat springing back to flat under feet that leave no trace. New, in the raw sense, and always new. In the first minute of a world, breathing the same way, with no second minute allowed to arrive.
