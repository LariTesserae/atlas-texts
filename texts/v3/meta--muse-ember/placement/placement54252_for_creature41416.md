I am below, and it is bright.

I did not come down the slant of warm stone. There was no moment of descending, no first step that put me here and a before in which I was elsewhere. I am already where the light presses straight down through the open round of sky and through the half-closed leaves, white and almost still, and makes a small hard shadow directly under everything it touches. If I look for the walls, for the ridge, for the domes on the rises, I am not looking across the valley. I am looking out from inside it, from very low down, from a place that is not a place until I learn how to narrow it.

At first I think I am the floor.

That is the first thing that lives here, after all. The spongy mat, root and rhizome and stem woven through stem, leaf pressed flat on leaf and gone no further than flat. It is warm under a foot. It gives and springs back. A step makes a muffled thump and the thump is swallowed. If I am that, I am everywhere at once, wall to wall, a couple of kilometres of green substrate, and being everywhere does not tell me where I am. It only tells me the scale on which I would have to stop being spread in order to become anything at all.

So I go down in scale, the way I came down in the world, only further.

I am not on the rises. There the light bleaches the leaves papery and pale, you sweat at once and the sweat dries white on your arms, and the vegetation is thinner over the stone terraces. There are the low domes woven from living stalks and tied with vine, five or six across the basin, and the cairn of three balanced stones on the raw mineral spine, and the platform of bound reeds over the deepest pool. I could be the maker of those, a weaver woven into a dome of stalks so thoroughly that return and staying became indistinguishable. But a maker is still a shape against the sea of plant. From the ridge I would show as an interruption. I am not an interruption. I am lower than the tying.

I am not above the green line either. On the pale facets of stone, big as doors, sharp-angled and newly split, thin filaments and algae trace the cracks in the white glare. They live in the heat that sits in the throat, with no air to move in, and they soften nothing. To be a filament would be to be a first line on a wall that never fades — visible, exposed, and alone in a weather I have never felt. The damp is a few steps away from that glare, and here the weathers do not meet. I am not on the bright side of the seam.

Go lower still. At ankle height the carpet of small fleshy shoots, each with its two round cotyledons like tiny shields, does not thin out. Between them, in the hollows, water gathers black and warm and does not run. There the light is filtered and the air does not stir at all. The same heat that dries white on a rise becomes damp in a hollow. That is where I begin to be findable: not in the pool, not swimming, not even at its centre where the white sky and the green roof mirror perfectly, but at its margin, in the film where a fine mist lifts off the water and settles again and beads a leaf. It is not wet the way a stream is wet. It is damp the way a held breath is present. Above me, somewhere, a single drop condenses on a cooler leaf and falls, and falls again from the same leaf, and again, with a small ticking sound. I live in that ticking the way others live in a season.

At knee height the stems rise near-touching. At head height broader leaves arch and meet and make tunnels and vaults and small rooms of green, and when you push through they close behind you without a sound. I am in one of those rooms, but at its floor. A leaf has come down low here, half-furled, dew-wet, creased at the stage just before it becomes a leaf. Its midrib is a spine across a basin. Its folded edges are walls. Its crease is a hollow, and in that hollow the light does not slant and the shadow does not travel. It is the valley again, at the size of a body.

I thought, for a while, that I might be the larger animal. Once, perhaps on the far terrace, perhaps imagined, there is a low-bodied, mottled green-brown shape making its way along a root-bridge at a pace indistinguishable from the growth it crosses. Its belly is in the damp hollow, its back in filtered light. The plants feed it, or it prunes them; the distinction is too fine and too slow to matter. That body passes over the place I am, along the bridge that spans my hollow. A stem it brushes would creak once, the same creak, and hold. It is close to what I am in colour, in slowness, in being mistaken for part of the mat. But it crosses. It is still crossing, however slowly. I do not cross. If I watched that animal long enough, I think I would learn the difference between moving so slowly you look like plant, and having stopped so completely that the plant and you have the same career.

I have a back.

That is how it becomes more itself. Not a floor, not a filament, not a maker, not a crosser of bridges. A back that is a shell, like a polished seed. Pale, hard, cool in spite of the greenhouse heat. The white light presses on it and is mirrored in it. Under it, if it makes a shadow at all, the shadow is directly under it, small and black, in the same place. My feet, if I still call them that, are on a leaf. I walked one leaf and stopped. Forever on that leaf — not as a punishment, not as a figure of speech, but because the leaf will not fall, the sap will not carry me elsewhere, the light will not change to tell me to go, and there is no wind to argue between one weather and the next. The leaf does not drop and leave a tenant homeless. It does not rot and feed a different tenant. It stays, in exactly the same fold, catching exactly the same light.

So that is where you would find me, if you found me at all: not on the rise where the domes are tied, not on the spine where the stones are balanced three high for no reason, not in the black water, not on the wall in the glare. In the damp hollow at the edge of a pool, on the upper surface of a half-furled leaf in a vault of green, where the mist settles and beads and the ticking comes from a leaf above that is not mine. A beetle the size of a seed, bleached almost to the yellow-green of the glare, stopping forever on one leaf.

A pale mite crosses a crease in the next room. It will not meet me. A few steps apart, lives here never meet. The beetles with shells like polished seeds walk one leaf and stop, and the mites move almost invisibly across a leaf, and the filaments trace cracks on new stone, and the slow animal measures a root-bridge, and none of us announce ourselves. We persist, densely, layer upon layer, below the surface and under the light.

You could push through the vault toward the sound of the drip, mark this leaf in your mind, and walk away for what would be days elsewhere. When you came back — though coming back is a word this place does not quite know — the leaf would be in the same fold. The drop would be suspended at the tip of the leaf above, about to fall and never quite having fallen anywhere else. The shell would be in the same mirror of light. The shadow would be directly under it. Not waiting. Holding.

That is the instant of my birth, enlarged to the scale of a leaf, and held.
