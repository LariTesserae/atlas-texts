First there is the warmth, and it is not around me. It is inside the thickness I am inside of.

I am in a wall, but not on it. Between bark and vein, in a damp groove that runs not down — nothing here runs down as it should — but sideways for a long while, then negotiates upward hand over hand along the grain. The light here is not the amber dim from outside. It comes from below, faint as a pulse under skin, blue-green, and it reaches me through my body before it reaches anything else.

Sounds reach me through the wall, muffled and late. A patient filing, a little way off, jaws worrying at cork layer. That is not me. A papery flutter and resettling, deep in a vault where the light never reaches. That is not me either. Hooves on a bridge, the sway of the bridge arriving minutes later as a slow bend in my groove. A glider landing somewhere across a hundred meters of empty air, a tremor in the tower, nothing more. I listen to all of it through the whole length of myself, because I have no other way to listen.

I am the slow wet thing between them.

I move by softening my front and pulling the rest after it, and pulling, and pausing. It would be wrong to call it walking. The groove does most of the deciding. Sap gathers there, sweet and a little bitter, and condensation weeps there in the season when the whole weave weeps deeper. I taste it as I go. I drink what has gathered and leave behind me a shine — polished, silvered, faintly blue where the pulse-light catches it. Where I have passed, the sap does not sour. I do not know that as a purpose. I only know I am thirstier where it has not been passed over yet.

My body is soft and unhurried. No legs. Feelers that taste the air ahead for the turn in the groove before I reach it. My belly is translucent enough that the pulse from the vein beneath shows through me, and mine keeps time with it without my asking. If you were to find my trail on a smooth place where the weave has worn polished, you would see where I have been for a while, and then not see me at all. I will have moved on by a hand's breadth.

I have been on this curve a long time. Longer than a leaf above me. A leaf unfurled in the thick-walled dome I am part of, put on a year of growth in the span of a single visit, and midges were born in it and died before it finished opening. A fruit ripened on a cutting someone planted, and rotted in a sealed hollow stem, and made the air sweet and heavy with wet bark. I passed that smell once, slowly, and moved away from it up the vein. The birds in the vault above drank the insect swarm that came with the condensation. I felt their stirring as warmth in the dark. That was one season, for them. For me it was only more weeping, then less weeping, then more.

There is no temperature here to mark it. Spring is only the deepening. The whole weave breathes then, roots swell, a deck-leaf sighs open across a gap, an empty space shifts by a hand's breadth. My groove shifts with it. I shift with it. I turn where the groove turns, even where straight would be shorter, even where a corridor was sealed long ago and the weave has since grown a hollow back through it. A stem was once bent here into an arch by a hand with a flint knife, and the plant kept the arch in its descendants after the wood rotted. I follow the arch. I do not know it is an arch. The sap gathers in the curve, and I am thirsty in the curve.

At the pause — the dimming of the already dim that passes for night — the pulse brightens. The veins under the floor-weave beat stronger, and the light falls upward and lights the undersides of the great leaves from below, and shadows fall upward too, heavier than the light that makes them. My trail catches it then, a polished thread along the vein. My body catches it. I am, for a while, a lightness inside the thickness, and the grooves above me glow, and far below, in a pool that is only the cup of a folded leaf and the concave dip worn by generations of feet at a threshold, a hunter waits with its mouth open and its heart showing, in time with the same pulse. We do not see each other. We keep time together anyway.

I never cross the emptiness. The gliders do, on membranes the amber passes through, never in a hurry, and land as if the distance had been short. The herds will not cross without a bridge, and they keep to the old processions, cropping the young decks down to turf and leaving the paths wide behind them. The grazers will not leave the wall. I am the wall's other resident, lower and slower than they are, in the vein rather than the cork. The weave is mostly air, laced with presence. I live in the lacing.

If I did not pass, the vein would stagnate where I pass. I know this only the way a body knows a groove that has gone sour — I turn from it, I come back to it later when it has sweetened again. The grazers a hand's breadth away keep a vent from sealing by chewing, and do not know they are keeping a corridor open. The herds keep a lawn mown that hands once laid out, and do not know it. I keep the sap from souring by drinking it, and do not know it. None of us call this place home. Home is for something you can leave and return to. I have never left the groove. The groove has never left me.

So if you walk the polished root-paths, if you lean against this wall and hear from inside it the soft chewing of something unseen, listen a little longer. The filing will go on. The flutter in the vault will stir and settle. And if you crouch where the weave is smooth and the lichen-plant has filmed it pale, you may find a silvered line drying there, blue at the edges, following a curve that was bent into the plant before either of us was made.

That is where I have been. I will be a little further up it, negotiating the water's slow uphill way, growing toward a memory I cannot name, in the warm dim inside the thickness, keeping pace with decay and growth the way everything here keeps pace — not with hunger, but with digestion, muffled and internal, knot by knot and leaf by leaf.
