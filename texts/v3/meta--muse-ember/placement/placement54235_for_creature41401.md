I am in the water before I am in a body.

That is the first thing, and I do not get past it quickly. Pressure arrives through my ribs before anything I could call a shape. Heat arrives after it, thick and mineral, gritty when — if I have a mouth — I taste it, warm as a bath left out and then heated again from below. And the roar arrives through both of them, not as a sound I hear but as a weight that makes whatever I am vibrate faintly underneath it. Between hits I can think. On the hit, I am only the place the hit passes through.

I try to put myself somewhere. Up to the shoulders, I think at first, because the warm water is up to something and there is air above it, a grey membrane of rain that does not fall so much as exist. I try to find hands to swim with. I reach — or something in me reaches — toward the stepping stones that lead from nowhere to the middle of the bowl and stop. The reach does not complete. The water will not hold a shape for it, and the current turns it aside, and I learn that up itself tilts toward the loudest eddy at that moment. I have been pulling upward toward noise without knowing I was doing it.

I try eyes. Light comes from a pale, roiling ceiling far above and never still, green-grey in every direction, and from above the rim there is only the sense of a cloud-sea far below the cliffs. My reflection arrives a fraction before I do, mouth open in a roar I have not yet made. That should be mine, perhaps, but I cannot use it. Nothing here holds still long enough to be seen by. I begin to know where I have been by departures in the roar — places where the note is thinner, where something has taken it out on its way through.

I try, then, to be smaller. A visitor. A thing with a skin that prunes, that could haul itself onto one of the flat black slabs by the eastern wall, knee-high and half-silted, close to where the water shallows and then drops again. The slabs hum low and sweet under the roar when a current strikes them. I orient toward that hum for a while, because it is the nearest thing to a shore. But there is no shore that stays a shore. The level rises and falls by handbreadths in minutes, laps over the lowest notches in the rim, recedes, leaves black rock glistening and new. What I push against splits, a few minutes later, with a crack I feel before I hear, and clatters down through the water as a fresh shard. Pebbles are cubes. Nothing has learned to be smooth yet, including me.

So I stop pushing and I cling.

That is the first shape that holds for longer than a boom. I find a protected crack where fresh rock has broken clean, in the lee of a slab that has just sheared and settled, in a seam that rings a little longer than the others when a current hits it. I spread there. Olive, translucent, thin as breath on glass — if spread is the word for what I do. I do not grow the way a plant grows. I tear and regrow. A veil of turbidity settles over me and blurs me white. A small upwelling lifts me at one edge and I fold. Light finds me and I brighten for a breath, then dull again under sediment that has not learned to lie down. When the rain hisses longest on a ledge above, I appear by evening where I was not in the morning, as if the hiss had brushed me into being.

It is not much. I eat nothing I can name. I take the warm, gritty water on my skin and hold it a moment longer than stone does. That may be food. For a while, in the crack, it is enough to be held a moment longer.

But I am also cold, and a film in a crack should not know cold.

When the level recedes I am left exposed, glistening, above the main chop. And there, at the edge where I lifted, I am no longer a film. I am a strand — thin, bleached almost white, trailing from the ledge, trembling in the rain that exists rather than falls. I have no root I can find and no leaf. When the water rises again I dissolve back into the film from which I came. I tremble harder when the wind tries to find a way through the rim and fails. I tremble less when the bowl is only ringing. If something touched me — nothing does, except the current, except my own passing — it would feel wet hair, and cold against warm water, and for an instant the two temperatures would argue on whatever skin I have left.

So I am not only the crack. I am the part of the crack that can be lifted. The water climbing the walls in sheets, the zone several handbreadths thick where air and water are not a line but a negotiation, where bubbles rise and do not break — that is where I straddle. Neither fully in, nor fully out. Everything that lives here is in the water, or touching the water, or remembering that it was. I am the remembering part, and it keeps moving the boundary through me.

And I turn.

A film should not turn. A strand should not turn. Yet when a deep note rolls through the bowl — when a current strikes fresh rock and the rock rings like a bell, and the ringing shapes where sediment settles a fraction later — something in me lifts. Not the whole of me. A flap along me. The way the pale ear fixed to a shard near the deepest point opens and closes with the percussion, not with the water exactly but with the boom underneath it, filtering whatever the turbulence brings it on the inhale of sound and letting the rest slump away on the exhale. I open toward the center, toward the loudest eddy at that moment, and my up tilts with it. I do not turn toward you. I turn toward the note.

That is when clinging becomes something else. When the note travels, I travel with its direction. I do not beat anything. I have no fins that beat. I orient. A hollow length forms along the ringing, because the ringing is where a veil of food arrives a fraction later, torn algae, sediment that has not learned to lie down, bubbles that rise and do not break. I ride that arrival the way the swimmers ride the booms, like slow weather. I drift upward in a loud current and hang there trembling, suspended from the sound itself, then descend on the receding wash. Along my underside a line of small flaps opens and closes in sequence, faster than the ear at the bottom, only along a body now. Only when I turn does light rake across me from the surface and show a skin, a flash, silver-white. The rest of the time I am almost water myself, and the roar dips where I pass, as if I have taken the note out of it.

I learn my place by learning that I do not have one.

I keep to the middle, where the stepping stones lead from nowhere and stop. I follow a spiral that is not a route. When time stutters in a whirlpool I pass the same stone again, the same bubble again — the bubble that has passed me three times in the same eddy, though the water is moving fast — the same note again, as if I had not just passed. It takes me a long time, by the measure of this place, which is seconds of reverberation and not meters, to understand that the repetition is not the water trapping me. It is me turning toward the same boom because the boom is the only reliable thing, and in a place where distance measures in echo, the far side of the bowl can be nearer than my shoulder, and I can be nearer to a note I have already passed than to anything beside me.

When the spiral widens toward the eastern wall, I pass over the white shells fused into the rock. Some are empty and silted. Some are open to the current, trembling a little. The algae films have thickened on the lee side of the pile, olive on white, and my passing stirs them. A fragment of bone, clean and bright inside where it has broken, lies half-buried at the edge, and warm mineral water weeps from the rock behind it. The pile hums in sympathy, low and sweet. I linger there while the hum lasts. It is the nearest thing to a home I have found, which is to say it is not a home. No one with hands lives in this bowl. No one builds now. Something made that pile shell by shell, in a ring, without tool marks, without metal, without glass, without light, and left it, or became it, and it is used now the way the ear uses its shard: as a place to catch a note, to let sediment settle where the ringing is longest. I use it that way too.

The others — if they are others — I meet only as accidents of current. A dark pressure at the shoulder, suddenly a body, then only water again. When we cross, we pass through each other's booms without slowing. We do not call. The booms are the call, and the place makes them. If the sound stopped we would not know which way to flow, and I think we know it. It is why I live by listening. There is light, but it does not hold still. There is current, but it does not hold still. There is no up that can be trusted. The sound comes first, and I come after it.

I do not think I have stopped being the film in the crack, or the strand in the rain, or the flap at the deep. When the water is low and a note is passing, I cling and I lift and I open a little, all at once, in different parts of whatever I am becoming. When the note deepens I fix, for a minute, as if to a shard, and filter. When the ringing travels outward from fresh rock I lengthen along it and turn, and flash. The place has not yet learned its final shape. It is still trying out shapes, one loud minute at a time, creation and destruction at the same speed in the same place, with no time for either to finish. I am trying mine out in the same rhythm, badly and beautifully, at the top of everything.

So if you are looking for me — and in this bowl, looking is mostly listening wrong until it goes on for miles — you will not find me on the bottom, though I have been the ear there, silt half-burying my base, algae finding the quiet side of my stem, old by the measure of this new water. You will not find me on the slabs, or on the half-wall sharp into deep water, or at the end of the stepping stones, though I pass all of them. You will find me in the zone between, suspended where water climbs and rain hangs and bubbles do not break, hanging trembling in a loud up-current, following my spiral that is not a route, most reliably where the roar dips at your shoulder, because a note has been taken out of it by a blind, translucent length turning toward sound.

For this loud minute, that is what the roar has decided to let me become. I am waiting — if waiting is the word — to see what it decides next.
