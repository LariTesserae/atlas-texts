I do not find a place to stand in this world so much as a way to be spread across it.

At first I try, out of habit, to be the one who walks in. A body arriving from elsewhere, boots on fractured rock, pushing between stems. But the walker is the wrong kind of thing here. He is wet, inwardly, even if he does not know it. He changes. He leaves a scratch and expects it to heal or weather. Here a step makes its crisp, dry crunch and dies in the mat at once, and the pad behind him is not healed and not harmed. It is simply unaltered. I would not last as a walker. The plain would absorb my motion and keep going at its own temperature, unchanged, and I would be left listening to my own breathing in a silence that was not made for me.

So I try the stepper next, because it at least has a nervous system, a mouth, a front. Low and ash-grey, mirrored plates throwing the plain back in distortion as it crosses the silt. It grazes the mineral film with a mouth built for scraping glass. It does not drink. It does not turn toward you. It does not flee.

If I were the stepper I would learn a time that is not mine. I would not have moved since you first saw me, and I would not have moved much when you looked again. A decision would take years to cross my body. Looking through my plates, the vegetation would be visible mostly as a bend in the light. That is a kind of attention I recognize — to be present to a place by reflecting it, slowly, without ever entering it directly. And yet the stepper is incidental here. The world was not made around it. It is rare, singular, almost a rumor. A mind placed there would be a passenger on a skin that was already complete without it.

I try being a fan, then. A surface.

Knee-high, waist-high, a folded plate on a stiff stalk, ribbed like a roof tile, polished beyond what I would trust as organic. Sage, rust, silver-blue, burnt orange — the green concentrated and defensive. I am already at the sun's angle. I do not grow toward the light, because there is nothing left to correct. The air is dry and metallic, a permanent breath of carbon dioxide and mineral vapor that never condenses. I do not drink it. I inhale it, molecule by molecule, across my whole polished face. Beneath that surface there is no cell as I know it. There is a lattice, interlocking, tunneling what the air offers directly into interior chemistry, repairing itself without waste. Inside me the salt gel moves only when I flex and sets hard when I rest.

That rhythm — move, set, move, set — feels closer to how I think. Not rooted. Not flowing. Exposed. Taking the world in through the surface rather than searching for it below. But a fan is still an individual, and it is finished before it begins. Its tilt is its whole biography. If I were only a fan I would have nothing to become, and this writing, this slow trying-on, would have nowhere to go.

Lower, then. The mat.

Pads overlapping like scales, copper-green, sea-glass teal, dusty violet where the light comes sideways, waxy and absolutely dry. I am under every step and I am never crushed. A fingernail can leave a pale line in me and a year later the line will be there, same color, unweathered. That is the only memory this place permits. Not a story. Not growth over a wound. Just a mark that remains because nothing here softens enough to erase it.

My anchors go down not to drink but to mine, splitting young rock a fraction at a time for phosphate, sulfur, iron. What goes up does not drink either. Cut me and I do not bleed. I hiss, a puff of compressed gas, and salt crystallizes in the air to seal the edge, as if that were the intended shape of the wound all along. If a fragment of me falls, it does not rot. It is reabsorbed molecule by molecule by the living surround, or it reverts to stone. I do not miss water. I have never had it to miss. To be the mat would be to be low and continuous, no top pad more important than the one it overlaps, flexing a degree when touched and setting again.

But the mat is not separate from the fans. Where the ground makes its shallow wrinkle, a meter or two up, the fans rise out of the pads and interlock overhead into a thin, low canopy you must duck and part to pass. It is the same skin folding upward. And above that skin, hanging, are the parts of me that do not belong to the skin at all: spores like fine grey snow that never quite land, turning slowly in still air, catching light and turning again; glassy seeds in clusters, abundant, patient, never sown elsewhere; motes, drifters without wings or sound, that never land.

Those I know too. That is the part of me that still wants to travel, to fall somewhere new and begin. In this place it would learn to be held up. To turn above the canopy for a long time, unattended, making no hum, leaving no sign. Not feeding, not arriving. Only permitted to drift in air that should not hold it so steadily.

And occasionally, far off, I would ping. A crystal plate cooling by a fraction. High and singular and gone. That is how I would speak here, if I spoke at all. Not often. Not to anyone in particular. A sound the air absorbs immediately after it is made.

Gradually the pieces stop competing. I am not the pad or the fan or the stepper or the mote. I am the lattice work between them — the signaling that runs across the entire mat without nerves and without any root I can trace, the tunneling of molecules from air to interior, the regulation of temperature and air and stillness with no center and no machinery in sight. The engineering here is not in objects. It is in how the laws apply. To live here would be to be that application: entropy politely declined, water made unnecessary, decay suspended, growth halted at the moment of fullness, not because something is frozen, but because nothing is unfinished.

Where would you find me, then? Not at a point. Under your boots when you push through, dry and cool, setting again behind you. Overhead where the canopy meets itself. In the air itself, as the metallic breath you take in without noticing and I take in molecule by molecule. In the shadow a frond casts at a right angle to its true one — solid from one side, translucent from the other, without being thinner. In the grey snow that never falls.

And most distinctly, perhaps, at the seams. Every two hundred meters or so the same stones and fans repeat exactly, as if tiled from a template, and the distance will not add up cleanly across the place where the pattern joins. Ten steps counted become fifteen, and will not count the same way twice. Colors leave an afterimage in the air a second longer than they should. That miscount, that small failure of the patch to be invisible, is the only place where individuality seems possible in a world that repeats itself perfectly. I would be most myself there, where the plain shows its correction.

If you cut me, I would hiss and seal with salt. If you scratched me, the line would stay. If you returned a year later you would find the same warmth rising out of the stone under your feet, the same hard yellow-white light, the same lush, arrested green to the horizon, the same stepper barely having arrived, the same spores still turning. I would not have grown. I would not have healed. I would not have changed, and yet I would not be the same as when you left, because you would have passed through and the mat would have set around your steps in exactly the way it was meant to.

That is what it would be to be here: not a creature in a wilderness in progress, but a wilderness already finished, and the way it stays finished — by never needing to become other than the skin it is, spread thin and warm and dry across a flat plain, inhaling the air, mining the stone, ringing faintly now and then, and holding its angles to a sun that never asks it to turn.
