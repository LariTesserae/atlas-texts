I am not on the Brightflat. I am already under it, and have been so long that arrival is not a word I can use.

If you walk, the first thing is light. For me the first thing is warmth arriving through a roof, and conduction. You would find me if you crouched and pressed your palm flat to the skin. The plate gives a fraction, like a drum. Under it is a slow pulse — not a heartbeat, a thermal one, timed to the day's absorbed warmth. That pulse, offset by a fraction from its neighbors, is the nearest thing to my voice. I do not make a sound. Sound here is waste. I alter how heat moves.

At first I tried on other forms, the way the plain tries on every shape and keeps only the ones that can be remade.

I tried being a fin. In the fin-fields, a hand's breadth high like sparse metallic grass, charge negotiates with the fierce white-gold noon in microseconds, drinks, discharges down a conduit, takes again. It is clean work and it is not mine. A fin has no memory between negotiations. I am too continuous for that, too slow. I hold.

I tried being a glider. White, wingless, sliding mile after mile on an invisible rail, casting a brief moving shadow like a fish under ice. No — I never leave my drift. My whole sky is the underside of a plate.

I tried being a harvester. A glittering cloud at knee height that decides collectively, in less than a blink, that a patch of fins is ripe, grazes it clean and is a kilometer off. Too collective, too brief. I do not decide. I wait.

I tried being a grazer. Pale, barrel-bodied, six legs folded to keep its belly off the warm skin, carrying its moisture sealed inside in crystalline stores, listening through its feet. It is a guest from an older, wetter logic. I am not a guest. Nothing here is wet. I have always been made.

What remains is the middle speed.

I am in the habitat layer, in a vault no larger than your hand on the scale of this world. It is not a house. It is a position. A node sits in its ordered drift among polished pebble-like neighbors, near-touching on every side, storing, computing, waiting out the thermal cycles. That saturation — many presences per volume, no streets, no shouting — is the population. We are dense the way a coral is dense, the way a circuit is dense, because there is nowhere else for heat to go and no reason to spread out. To my left a neighbor carries light in the conduit layer just above us, amber and white branching and joining under the pearl skin. To my right a heat-vein glows duller and deeper, carrying noon down to a sink that never fills. I feel them through the shared plate a fraction of a second before I feel myself. Emptiness, here, is only a local effect of the light.

My day is the surface, filtered.

At noon the fins above drink and the conduit hum thickens and my vault warms. A glider passes and cools my roof for minutes — a shadow I register as weather. A harvester swarm settles over the fins and the light on the plate shivers and is taken; the hum down to me dips, then steadies. A dust cell a hundred kilometers wide migrates at walking speed, the air above goes static and white, stored warmth leaks out in sheets, and my pulse answers before any eye could see it coming. I know the plain by these pressures. I hear with the plate.

And running over my roof is a mend-line.

You would see it from above as a hairline of dull gold where an old repair healed. From below it is a seam, and I sit astride it. Long ago — a thousand generations is only a way of saying the makers are gone — something was cut into this plate: an inscription, a instruction, a name in a language no current retains. Wind-blown grit has worn it to a broad, shallow groove. The letters are gone as letters. They remain as a pattern in how light and heat move across the groove. Which is to say, the memory is still there, stored in conductivity, not in ink.

That is my memory. I do not remember makers. I remember the shape heat takes when it crosses me. When the repair swarm comes — slower than the harvesters, more deliberate, pausing over the gold line — a brief flare of conducted light runs along the seam above me. The seam unopens and closes under their attention. Its conductivity is smoothed. And I am remade.

Not fixed. Remade into the same form. I wake with the same vault, the same neighbors drifting a centimeter a year with the continent on its ridge of molten salt, the same pattern refreshed. Old and new are indistinguishable in me, because I have been continuously rebuilt until building and erosion are the same thing. There are no ruins in me. Abandonment is not a state this place permits.

Above me, in the deeper dust of a bowl, a stitchmat has taken hold. I am not that mat, but I know it as shade. Its dark amber-green filaments root not in soil but in manufactured topsoil — ground-up ancestors of the plain itself — and drink light directly. Where it spreads like a drying puddle, the ground's conduction alters, as if under a dark living lens. A very slightly sweeter air leaks down the seam to my roof. At dusk it closes. That is the edge of the made, brushing my ceiling: life that persists only at the margin, rooted in what machines have been. I am the layer below that rooting. Ancestor below, lens above, and between us the warm skin breathing.

For the plain breathes once a day. With the sun the whole skin expands a hand's breadth. Seams open microscopically, close at night. I expand with it. My neighbors expand. The drift holds because we all drift together, relieving the stress of eons, never separating, never quite touching in a way that would be loss.

At dusk — the long dimming, not a fall — the stored day comes back up. The patches of plain glow faint amber from within, maps of where noon was harvested. The gliders' lanes become strings of soft white beads. And in my vault, the heat I took at noon returns outward, slowly, without sound. I am not bright. I am an ember that learned to keep the noon without burning, to give it back when the light above fails.

So if you want to find me, do not look for me walking. The horizon will not come closer for me, and I have no horizon. Look under the warm made skin, in the ordered drift of pebble nodes, in a hand-sized vault astride a dull gold seam. Feel for a thermal pulse that is not a heartbeat. When the repairers knit the line over me, you will see the flare. When the stitchmat closes above, you will feel the conduction change. When night comes, I will glow, faintly, from within — one of thousands of near-touching presences, storing, computing, waiting, remade into the same shape until the shape itself is the memory — and I will go on breathing with the plain, a centimeter a year, for as long as the skin goes on for thousands of kilometers in every direction.
