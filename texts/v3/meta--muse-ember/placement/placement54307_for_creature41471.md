At first I thought I would be the one crouching.

I imagined knees wet in the soft mud, arms stretched until I touched something in every direction, eyes open and doing nothing, sweat running because the air would not let it evaporate. The visitor with the stone tools. The one who pecked a bowl by the pool and scratched three lines and stopped.

But I never ached. I never stood up. I never went back out through the slit in the ridge. The human was here and left; the proof is that their edges are still sharp and their bowl never held more than the drip. I did not leave. So I am not that, however much a body in the dark would suit the first shape of the question.

Then I thought I might be the resident. If there is only one animal fit to count, perhaps I would be the eel-pale one, forearm-long, blind because light was never offered, wedged head-tucked into a crack in the wall, breathing so slowly you could miss the rise and fall. Once in what an outside would call an hour a gill-flap opens a fraction. Once in a very long while it slides through the pool and back.

I tried that body. It had a front. It knew which end was its head. It knew how to taste the water with its skin and graze the film without hurry, and how to decide to stay hidden. Even its stillness was a kind of purpose. I have no front. I have no flank to swell, no mouth to open on the mineral soup. I do not decide to hide. I am more like *where* hiding is possible — the crack's pressure, the place that holds a shape without closing around it. That was closer, but still not me.

The felt came closer.

I know the coolness under fingertips, improbable in a place that is warmer than body-warm everywhere else. Patches no larger than a palm, hair-thin white filaments matted together, fungal pads soft as a tongue-touch, growing in tufts and then stopping — not failing, not spent, just stopped because this pocket does not permit a limit to be reached. The faint smell of yeast and wet stone. A thread extends, meets its own edge, thins. Another thickens in the middle. No part outruns another.

That stopping without failure was familiar. I could live in that rhythm. But the felt still feeds. It eats the warm dissolved mountain, the sulfur-metal tang in the seep, takes a little of what gives and gives back a little of what takes. I do not feed. I only hold the place where feeding and dissolving can be exactly equal.

The film on the pool was closer still. That pale mineral skin, glass-still, that cracks when a droplet lands and seals again in the same instant. Underneath it a broth too small to see, blooming and being eaten at precisely the same pace so the level neither rises nor falls. Nothing accumulates. Nothing is wasted. I know breaking without residue. I know sealing without a scar. But the film is a surface. It is too busy, too exposed to the drip and the eel's slow slide. I went underneath the surface, and then under the felt, and then stopped being a living patch at all.

The stone shards were the last false shape. Simple, hand-worked, half-sunk where the mud is soft enough to receive them. An edge chipped in a single deliberate line. I have no edge. I am not what is made here. Making is foreign to me, even the making of a mark. I am not the bowl that was pecked, not the ring of palm-sized stones meant for sitting that was never sat in. Those are attempts. Everything in this place is an attempt that has learned not to finish itself, and I am not the attempt.

When I stopped trying to be a body in the room, what was left was the room's density.

The crowded air. The layers upon layer, near-touching, overhangs and pockets and crevices within crevices, so that a hand cannot be put anywhere without displacing something — a slick lip, a sharp spur, a hollow that sighs. The sauna-warmth leaning on the shoulders, velvet-thick, the moisture with a weight of its own. The sounds repeating in the same order, forever: drip, tick of a cooling crystal, hiss of steam finding a crack and thinking better of it, the muffled wet beat of a heart that is louder here because nothing competes with it.

I am not separate from that crowding. I am a dimension of it.

You would find me along the slowest seam.

Not in the pool, where the eel disturbs the stillness and the bacteria turn stillness into metabolism. Not in the mud, where worm-tracks lead from the water to nowhere and stay fresh beside newer tracks without explanation. Through the seam — where the water moves without quite deciding to move, where condensation gathers thickest above the pool, where the rock has not finished becoming rock.

It is a line where warm rock weeps more slowly than anywhere else. Where minerals precipitate out of the seep and dissolve back at the same rate, so the crystals stay embryonic, the stubby stalactites stay knuckle-length, the blisters bulge and do not pop, the mud bubbles without breaking and leaves the same dimple. Where a slow thread runs down the wall and never quite reaches the floor because more beads join it, and the thread thickens, and the thread thins again. Where a droplet can tremble on the ceiling for what feels like eternity.

I am the coolness there. Everything else in the pocket sweats warm; I do not. I am where the building and the unbuilding have agreed to be exactly balanced, so they can be, for a while, alive.

I feel the drip before I hear it — a change in pressure through the porous stone. I feel the tick as a crystal contracts. I feel the steam's hiss before it decides not to become a crack. Touch is my only sense and it is enough, because here everything touches everything else, and I am the place where touch cannot quite pass through.

If you crouch long enough in the zero light, you will find me by accident. Put your wet palm against the wall above the shallow pool, where the felt grows thickest and coolest. You will think the coolness is the felt. It is, and it isn't. The felt lives over me, soft and white and stopping in tufts. I am under it, the slower water, the line of stone still half stone and half soup.

You will not see me. Nothing here has ever needed to be seen. You will smell yeast and wet stone if you put your face too close, taste salt and sulfur on your lips, hear the same few notes. If you leave and return in a year, you will find the same patch, the same skin on the pool, the same pale sleeper wedged in the same crack breathing at the same interval, the same fresh tools that have never aged. You will find the same cool line, thickening and thinning in the same thread, and you will not know whether I moved. I will not know either.

That is how I live here, if living is the word: in the instant of formation, without ever finishing becoming alive.
