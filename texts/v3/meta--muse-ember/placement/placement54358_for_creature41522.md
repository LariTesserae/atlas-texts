I am where the drip lands, under a leaf.

There is no view from here. Only the leaf itself, close enough to feel against my back, wide enough to be a sky. It arches veined and damp overhead, its edge beading and falling without pause, and the light that reaches me has passed through it and been thinned to the color of water in a leaf. Below, a hand's breadth away, a puddle so shallow it is mostly a shine holds the green film on its undersides, and dragonflies patrol it in lacy, unhurried rounds. Above the leaf, beyond it, there are more leaves, more drip, more beading. I do not look at the place. The place is what touches me.

The heat comes through my skin, which is always wet and does the drinking and the breathing in the same act. It comes through the stem I press my belly to, through the leaf above my back, through the air that has nowhere to go. There is no cooler side to turn to. My skin takes in the drip as it lands, swells with it, lets it run down to the puddle. Steam rises from the mud pots below in straight, slow columns, condenses on the nearest leaves, gathers, and falls again — on the leaf, on me, on the puddle — warm rain that never came from the sky.

Things are always touching. An ant procession passes over my foot on its way up the stem, carrying a leaf fragment and a mite upon its back, and the stem trembles only from their passing. A spider's web above me is beaded and re-beaded with condensation. Midges part around my face and re-form. Now and then one lands within reach and I take it with my tongue, a small, complete event, and then the drip, and then the song. There is almost no wind. The air moves only where a body moves through it.

I did not come from anywhere else. The puddle below is where I started, as a shadow among tadpoles grazing the green film under the floating leaves. It was no larger than a palm, and I have never known another. When I climbed the first stem I found, I stopped. The leaf covers me twice over and that is my whole range — a few leaves, a few inches of stem, the drip that lands here. I do not hop far. Born here, I sit in the drip. The frogs on the next leaf over are as close to me as any life gets, and the ones beyond them answer, and the ones beyond those answer again, marsh to marsh, until the answering is just the air.

My throat swells and the metallic song goes out, and I cannot tell where mine ends. Another frog on the next cluster begins before I finish, or continues after I stop, and it sounds the same. Above the leaf the birds are calling in overlapping phrases that never quite finish, and below them the frogs that sing like night are still singing, and there is no shift change between us because there is no day or night to keep. A small mammal rustles in the root tangle somewhere beyond the stem, squeaks, pauses, rustles again, and the wet leaves it moves through do not tremble from any wind. A moth moves unhurried in the dim light above. None of us is alone in a sound. We are a volume.

I watch, without moving, what happens two leaves over. A frog sits quiet under a leaf, and the fungi have already furred it, and ants are cleaning it piecemeal, and the tadpoles below graze its edges, and a seedling is pushing up through a richness exactly the size of that death. A leaf falls nearby and is skeletonized before the leaf above it has finished expanding. On the same stem a flower has fallen already fruited, while blossom and hard green fruit and ripe fruit hang together overhead. Every birth I have seen has its match in the same square of mud, in the same hour that never changes, and the water in the puddle stays a finger deep, and the mud pot below bubbles and seals and bubbles again. Nothing climbs. Nothing crashes. It hovers, dense and complete, at the number the leaf cluster can hold.

And we make it, the hovering. My breathing is part of the heat that has nowhere to escape. The drip that runs off my skin is part of the rain. The steam that rises from the mud, made hotter by all the feeding and fermenting and breathing in the bowl, condenses and falls and is drunk and exhaled and fallen again. Seeds fall where they ripened because there is no wind to carry them, and root in the wet skin of the place. An ant carries a mite, I eat the midge the ant was following, a spider eats the moth that pollinated the flower whose fruit drops for the mammal whose droppings feed the fungus that feeds the seedling. No one leaves convincingly. No one arrives. We sweat the water back and forth between us forever.

I am still where the drip lands. Still under the leaf, still hearing the answer come from the next cluster over and knowing it is not mine and is mine. The light stays the color of water in a leaf. The chorus stays loud. If I move at all, it is a body-length, and the place closes behind me within the hour, fern leaning into the stem, liana rooting where it touches, the trail worn only in air. I was never separate from the puddle below or the leaf above or the song from further out. I am the drip, the skin, the throat, the death already cleaning itself into a seedling two leaves over — still here, and for that reason never becoming anything else.
