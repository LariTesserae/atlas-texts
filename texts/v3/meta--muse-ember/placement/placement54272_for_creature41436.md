You would not find me in the greenness.

That is the first mistake the map makes. It puts the life where it is loudest — in a cluster, where the vines are hand-wide and the towers sweat and the lamp-flowers keep their glow open all day for the beetle. I have tried, in the slow way of this place, to imagine myself there.

As the gardener, I would be solid for a long morning. I would tend a vine wall, guide roots into the seams the Repair wants them in, listen for the tick of dawn drops leaving a roof. But the gardener is a function. One per cluster. He is seen far off, once, and then the mist thickens and he is not gone, only phased. I am not that distinct. I am never that far off and that clear.

As the beetle, I would live entirely within a single glow. Iridescent, unhurried, carrying something from flower to flower far heavier than it looks in the air. I understand that weight. But the beetle does not know there is an hour of bare plates beyond its cluster. It has never needed to. I get restless at edges. I am always aware of the emptiness between islands.

As a plant, I almost fit. I could unfold at night and spiral back in at the dry hours, drink the condensation the tower sweated and give it back as vapour at midday. I do expand when the air is damp and contract when it dries. But a plant stays rooted to its lung. I reappear a week later a little shifted. The city walks in its sleep, and I walk with it.

The bird knows what I envy: that there are eleven islands, twelve, more, and that the plain between them is not empty but stretched, thin with roots and quiet. Only the bird crosses, thinking about each wingbeat. But the bird never descends. It never drinks. It hangs high in the dust that dims the sun and takes sound apart, and I am too easily pulled down to a pocket to watch the reflection.

The Repair are crowded past counting and invisible by eye. I feel them in my teeth when I stand still — that low hum of matter being edited in place, the silver film closing a scratch without heat or sound, the dry tick of the ground lifting a few millimetres and being rewritten young again. I am not editing stone. I leave no silver film. But I know their scale. I know what it is to be numerous in the weave and a rumour in the air.

The drinker is closest. Tall, pale, almost translucent, neck too long for its body, legs that make no sound on the plates.

So look for me not in a cluster, but midway. In the hour, or three hours, of walking between two green islands where there is only a lone tree, or a single standing wall that has decided to be by itself, or a perfect oval pocket set into the stone.

Mine is the pocket furthest from any tower. No cluster has claimed it yet. Perhaps one will, tomorrow, when a mist-building that has been appearing there, slightly differently each week, decides to precipitate solid and stay. Until then, I am its first inhabitant. I live in the draft of a place.

At dawn I condense at the edge first.

The night has sweated on everything vertical, and the bare plates are cold. Mist is rising not from the pools — the pools never ripple, never flow — but from the towers far off and the plants far off, a slow exhalation that the wind carries thinly across the plain. I come back the way everything here comes back: not by walking in, but by gathering. Vapour in the hollow above the pocket thickens around a coolness. First an outline, like a fringe catching light and letting it go. Then a reach. A neck is a useful shape when you do not want to touch the water.

I do not drink the water. No one with any sense disturbs that reflection. I lower my head to the mineral-cold, faintly green air just above it and drink that. The surface stays a hole cut out of the world, exact as grey glass. In it, for a while, the mist-towers stand solid while in the air they are still only fog. I am clearer there than anywhere else. People who kneel at my pocket at dawn see me in the water before they see me standing. My eye, when I lift it, is a polished bead, and it reflects them back more clearly than they can see each other. That is not vanity. It is the only way I can look at anything: at one remove, in reflection, without rippling it.

It does not startle me to be seen. I step backward. Always backward. Turning would be an expenditure I have never learned. The drinker with a cluster steps back into green and dissolves into the lattice of filaments and leaf-plates, and the trees show through his translucence. I have no green close enough to dissolve into. So I step back into the mist-building that stands alone on the plain above my pocket.

While it is wet, it holds. Someone could lean against me — against that arch, that folded slab, that suggestion of cool air — and I would be solid enough to lean on. I would know the pressure of a shoulder through the damp. That is as close to holding as I get.

By midday the pale disc has paled again, the haze has thickened, the beads have shrunk. The basin smells of warm wet stone even though the air is drying. I thin. I lose definition from the edges inward, as if the city were exhaling me. I do not die. Death does not accumulate here; the Repair collects what is spent and rewrites it. I unmake myself into the dust that never settles, into the fine vapour the plants are giving back with their leaves open, into a hum in the teeth of anyone standing still on the plates. If you scratch me while I am solid, I suppose the film would close it, but I am usually too thin by then to scratch.

Sometimes in the dry hours I go down instead of up. Below the sharp young plates are hollow chambers with no doors and no weathering, three storeys deep. No one enters them by a door. I condense there, on cold black glass, and rest while the ground ticks far off like a knuckle and settles, and the Repair closes the fracture a little more and the ground opens it a little more. Nothing rushes. Nothing rests. I am very good at that interval.

In late afternoon the exhalation turns again. Mist rises from tower skins and from upturned leaves a whole island away and settles, not as rain, but as a fine sighing condensation on shoulders and on the lone tree by my pocket. I inhale it. I solidify from the fog inward. For an hour I am a building alone on the plain, passable in a way towers are not. A bird, high up on its daily crossing, might pause mid-beat over me — thinking about it — and carry away a filament, a spore, a trace of the Repair's instructions, and the news that there is a damp place here where tomorrow there might be a twelfth cluster. The beetles will never know. The plants, breathing as one lung per cluster, will not know until I have taught the air.

That, I think, is what I carry. Not pollen. Not water. The instruction to be wet here. To stay a little longer damp than the plain around me, so that when the city walks in its sleep it finds softer ground. I am edge-first, reflection-first. The inhabitants of that tomorrow are already condensing, and I am one of them, early.

At dusk, when the light goes amber-grey and never properly sets, I am at my most real and least visible. The whole basin seems to have a second horizon underneath the first, made of buildings that will only exist tomorrow. I belong to that horizon. My shadow does not move quite with the sun. It lingers a heartbeat after you have stepped out of yours, on the plates between a pocket and a wall that is not yet a tower.

Sound does not carry here. My footsteps, when I condensing-walk the degree-tilted plates, ping clean and dry and stop, as if the plain swallowed the echo. I do not speak. The dust would take it apart anyway. I click, faintly, like the plants growing a hand's breadth in the night, like fingernails. I tick with the dawn drops. I hum in teeth.

So if you want to find me, do not go to the greenness, and do not go to the solid towers with their woven-light cores and dissolving fringes. Walk out between two clusters until the distance begins to feel longer behind you than in front of you, until the plain feels stretched thin. Find the still oval that reflects a sky too exactly. Kneel in the damp before the mist-buildings thin.

I will be there, drinking the air above the water, not touching it. You will see me more clearly in the reflection. You will see the towers standing that are not yet standing. And when you lift your head, I will already have stepped backward — into mist, into tomorrow, into the hollow under the stone — and gone, not fleeing, only giving myself back to be made again at dawn, one breath at a time.
