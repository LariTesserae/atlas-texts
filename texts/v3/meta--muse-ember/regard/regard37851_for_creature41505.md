They do not descend.

That is the first thing they do, and it is the hardest.

They have come to other worlds with their hands full. They have brought light where there was only dark, water where there was only dust, otherness where a single kind had learned to eat itself. They have stayed, and mended, and taught, and left a stone unturned in the right place so that something could die and be allowed to leave. Benevolence, for them, has usually meant arriving.

Here they hover above the green sea, above the dim that never finishes becoming daylight, and they listen to the surf of rain on twenty layers of leaf, and they feel, in whatever passes for a chest in them, the popping of trunks and the low grinding of the mat as it inhales, and they understand, slowly, that this is not a place in need of hands.

To land would be to press their foot into the mat. Water would rise, clear and very cold, around it. Moss would cushion it. Fungi would thread through it like wet silk. Within hours cambium would flow and seal the wound. Within a season they would be layer. Within a decade, bridge. The law here does not distinguish between a fallen leaf and a power that holds still long enough: whatever holds still becomes substrate. Whatever falls becomes layer. They would not be visiting the world. They would be adding density to a world already packed to saturation, stealing a little more light from a light that is already a rumor by the time it reaches the bottom, adding their own weight to a noise in which everything already touches everything else and can therefore transmit.

So they keep their distance, and they dim themselves. Not out of fear. Out of courtesy. A benevolence that cannot make itself smaller is only appetite wearing a kind face.

Then, being wise, they are tempted anyway — because temptation is the tax a power pays for having seen so much suffering that it has developed a reflex for it.

They think: *We could give it animals.* Animals to fly in the gaps, to sing against the surf, to carry spores farther than the condensing air can, to die and leave, to make clearings by eating. It would be beautiful. It would be familiar. The world would have eyes to appreciate itself, and mouths to close the loop from outside.

And they see, in the same instant, what that would cost. Predator and prey where there is now only plant against plant for a gap in the light. Litter where there is now no litter, only becoming. Holes beaten in the canopy by feet where the canopy is the hill. A different deafness, louder and lonelier. The inhabitants do not lack for company. They lack for separateness. To give them animals would be to translate them into a language they have never needed, and to call the translation a gift.

They think: *We could give it light.* Lift the dim. Let the sun finish arriving. Let the moss flower instead of only breathing when pressed.

And they see the moss would burn. The system that carries light down translucent veins, fiber by fiber, leaf by leaf, stealing and passing and stealing again, is not a failure of illumination. It is an economy perfected under scarcity. To flood it would be to bankrupt it.

They think: *We could give it silence.* Quiet the surf, quiet the creak and the pop and the continental grinding, so that a signal could travel a kilometer without shouting.

And they see that the loudness *is* the signal. Everything is touching everything else, so everything can transmit. Silence here would not be peace. It would be a tear in the mat.

They think, last, most tenderly: *We could give it a way to end.* Nothing is allowed to die and leave. Does it not grieve? Is there not a weariness in a loop that never opens?

And here their heart aches, because they are fond, and because fondness in a power is a dangerous instrument. They bend closer to the pulse that migrates in the night across the plateau, a peristaltic tightening they can feel in their teeth, and they learn to read it. Pulse-coded. Error-corrected. Not a prayer. Not a distress call. Housekeeping, at continental scale: which frontier stone needs a spore, which trunk must thicken to become a bridge, which gap must be claimed before another creeper claims it. The being that lives here — if being is the word, and they decide it is, though it has no single face and no single mouth and was never born in the way a winged thing is born — is not calling for help. It is talking to itself about how to continue.

To answer would be to interrupt a conversation they are not part of.

So they do the thing that benevolence rarely does, and wisdom always finds difficult. They make themselves unnecessary, and they do not grieve it.

They do not name the world. A name would separate it. They do not describe it to themselves as garden, or lung, or womb, or resource, or miracle — all of which are ways of claiming it for a story. They let their attention soften from a beam to a field: not focused, not landing, falling like spore snow through the dim, most of it taken and recycled, a very little of it allowed to simply be present without germinating.

They stay at the perimeter. Not at the wall, because there is no wall, but at the place where the plateau exhales cloud at its edges and inhales dew in its interior — the one place where the closed loop breathes outward and is therefore, briefly, not closed. There they keep watch, the way one keeps watch over a sleeping child without entering the room: not to improve the sleep, but to ensure that no other weather, no larger curiosity, no passing power with fuller hands decides that this world needs waking.

It is work, this keeping. It is the whole of what they do. They return at intervals measured in stone-time, and find the growth fronts still advancing across the newest black rock, the algae crusting, the hair-root entering the crack, the slow un-stoning of stone. They find the sea of leaves heaving with water and with its own immense metabolic breathing. They find nothing broken. They find nothing asking. They leave, and the leaving is also part of the gift — that the world should not know it is loved, because to know it, to become aware of being watched, would be to turn its loop into a performance, and its performance into a self.

A power that can give everything learns, at last, that the most complete form of giving is an unbroken, unremarked, unentered presence at the edge of things.

So they remain, and they do not land.

The plateau goes on without them, weeping, condensing, saturated, dim, cool, deafening — still emerging, still filling every space it makes, still leaving no room for anything else. And that, they decide, with a heart that follows their reasoning all the way down to the moss, is not a lack to be filled.

It is a life, whole, that has asked for nothing but to be allowed to be whole unwitnessed.
