### If Anything

It would not enter the way you entered.

It would notice the pocket from the far side of the storm, the way you learn to notice anything here: by sound before sight, and then by the small noise inside the noise. The roar is loud, but loud is common. What is uncommon is the quiet inside it — the long, bright emptiness between presences, the way nothing here is allowed to gather. A benevolent and wise power would hear that quiet and stop, not because the pocket called out, but because it almost never calls out. It thickens and un-thickens. It does the work of existing without asking to be seen doing it.

It would listen for a long time before it crossed the rain wall. It would learn the habits faster than you did, because it has had longer to practice not interfering.

It would learn that sound arrives before sight. That droplets fall up in bundles when the pocket contracts. That breathing is circular. That distance is untrustworthy. And above all, it would learn the rule that is not so much a rule as a law of sparseness: nothing here lives in numbers. A filament alone by a stretch of empty bright water. A glider alone in the whole pocket, because two fully thickened would resonate in the same small water and un-make each other. An artifact alone in its tumble, remembering hands.

### What it could easily do, and will not

Benevolence alone would rush in. It would want to rescue the filament from un-stringing, the crust from losing its claim, the cup from loosening its lashings, the glider from thinning droplet by droplet at the far wall. It would steady the pocket. Make the shelf hold. Make a shore. Make a bottom. Finish what is unfinished.

Wisdom alone would watch coldly. It would note the currents and go on.

Together — benevolent *and* wise — they do something harder than either: they stay, and they refrain.

It could finish the pocket. That, for such a power, would be trivial. A thought, and the crust would knit and keep knitting until it was stone. The filament would root. The raft would stay woven. The pocket would become a small, stable lake in the sky, with edges that could be mapped, a place that could be lived in, visited in numbers, harvested, named on charts. It considers this, and sees it clearly for what it is: not a gift, but a death of another kind. The pocket is not a failed lake. It is a successful becoming. To finish it would be to end the very thing that makes it alive — pre-formation, emergence, un-happening as a way of living rather than a failure of living.

It could give the glider company. That would be the most human temptation. The being who lives here crosses alone, indifferent to current and to you, and dissolves at the wall with no young, no bones, no remains kept. A kind power would want to make a second crossing, so that two wakes might cross each other, so that dissolution would not be solitary.

It will not. It knows what you have only just understood: the glider's solitude is not loneliness. It is the condition of its completeness. It does not seek company because it does not experience itself as lacking. To place another glider in the same water would not comfort the first; it would un-make both. Benevolence that ignores resonance is only sentiment. Wisdom that ignores suffering is only accuracy. Together they understand that to protect a life, sometimes you must protect the distance around it.

It could preserve the made things. It could keep the clay cup from softening, the hide from losing its smoke, the cairn from losing its shelf. It could lift the artifacts out of the tumble and set them somewhere they would last. It will not do that either. Those things are already finished in a way nothing native here is finished — made to resist un-happening — and that is why they startle. They are visitors' marks, warm and human, already dissolving, only slower. To preserve them would be to turn a handful of tender, abandoned constructions into a monument, and a monument in the sky would invite return. It would invite bigger tools, bigger crossings, people who came to live here in numbers. That is the one future the pocket cannot survive.

### What it does instead

It keeps the distance between presences.

That is its first work, and it is active work, though it looks like nothing. It keeps the filaments spaced by empty water. It keeps the crusts from thickening all at once on the slow currents. It keeps the pocket small enough to cross in minutes, bright enough to photosynthesize in, normal-lit enough that nothing here has to strain to live. It does not add water. It does not add life. It tends the breathing of the wall so the pocket does not collapse into a sudden downpour that would soak the ridge below for an hour, nor expand into a cloud that would drift away and be lost. Not forever — it knows collapse and expansion are part of its nature — but for as long as it can hold the contraction at the pace the pocket itself prefers.

It learns to look obliquely.

Attention, here, is heavy. You have felt it: to be too finished, too dense and warm, is to be outside the glider's world. For a power, to stare directly into the pocket would be to thicken things by looking. A second glider might begin because it was watched for. A crust might hold because it was held in mind too firmly. So it attends the way light attends the filaments — from everywhere and nowhere, without fixing. It listens for the pressure change a moment before the shape appears. It is present in the medium without becoming another presence in it.

And it remembers.

That is its benevolence made visible only in hindsight. Nothing here is kept. The filament un-strings. The skin the glider sheds knits elsewhere and is reabsorbed. The raft becomes a suggestion of weaving. The cup's held water becomes water again. If no one remembered, all of that un-happening would be as if it had never thickened at all. The power does not keep the things from un-happening. It keeps them from being unwitnessed when they do. Each crossing is held, not as a name — it will not name the glider, because naming here would be a kind of finishing — but as a wake pattern, a time, a way the droplets hung and fell up behind it. Each filament is remembered by where it hung and for how long, even after the membrane that held it popped. Each artifact is remembered by its weaving, its lashing, its smoke, long after the fibre has forgotten.

For the glider in particular, it does one more thing. It places itself, invisibly, at the far rain wall.

Not to catch it. Not to keep it from dissolving. The glider thins at the edges first, then at the center, droplet by droplet, into the emergence that made it, and the power lets it thin. But it does not thin into nothing. It thins into something that has been listening for the pressure change, that knows what a crossing costs in a place with no floor. Dissolution, witnessed, is not erasure. It is a return that is known.

Below, on the mountain, it does its only human-facing work. The builders of the cup and the hide and the cairn — people from the ridge who left things to the cloud — might have thought, once, of ascending. The bright patch in the storm wall might have looked like an invitation. The power dims that invitation without lying. It lets the patch look like ordinary hesitation in rain. It lets the ascent lose its interest in the body, the way a current turns a hand aside. No punishment. No miracle. Just the slow persuasion that there is nothing there worth finishing, nothing there that wants to be lived in, nothing there that will hold a house or a season. The artifacts will be remembered below as a strange dream of smoke this high. That forgetting is protection for both sides.

And if you are there — if you have arrived in the middle of the sound, breathing mist that will be exhaled downstream as finer rain — it will do for you what it does for everything that thickens here temporarily. It will not keep you. You are too finished to be kept. It will make the wall breathe in a way that lets you slip out downstream with your breath, your memory of the ridge far below, your understanding that you have joined the pocket only in the allowed way: briefly, out of number, by thickening in the medium and then letting the water take your shape back. You may feel, once, a pressure change in your chest that is not a glider and not a current turning. That is not the pocket thickening. That is the power looking obliquely, to be sure you are seen without being held.

It does not finish the ocean in the sky. It does not populate it. It does not name its inhabitant or give it a mate or a home or a history in the ordinary sense.

It keeps it unfinished, keeps it sparse, keeps it unwitnessed by no one, and keeps the door in the rain wall breathing both ways.

That is very little, from the outside. From the inside, where everything un-happens, it is everything a place like this could ask for: to be allowed to go on being born, and un-being born, and being born again a little differently, high and hidden and full of water that is also air — and to know that when it finally decides, if deciding is the word, to un-happen droplet by droplet back into the storm it came from, it will not do so unseen.
