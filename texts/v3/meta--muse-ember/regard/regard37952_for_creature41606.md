It did not arrive.

Arriving would have meant landing, and landing would have meant a first footstep that was not a plant. That would have broken the first rule of the place, and the power, being wise, understood the rules before it understood anything else.

It turned its attention - which is not a telescope, and is not a hand - toward the mountain. It had seen other worlds. It had seen builders separate from their buildings, breaking stone, burning, *making* a thing and then standing apart from it to admire or abandon it. It had seen life that lived *in* structures and life that lived *on* ground, and life that ate the makers and life that ate the made.

It had never seen this.

A builder that is the building. A tower that is a lung, not a house. A dome that is a sealed body to hold heat, not a shelter for anything else. A bridge that reaches and reaches and is content not to meet. A whole mountain that is thatch and culm and compressed generations of itself, rising out of cloud, tearing itself down in steam and stitching itself shut in the same heat.

The power was drawn not by a plea. There was no one there who could plead. It was drawn the way a still place is drawn by a sound at night: because everything else had been witnessed, and this had only been growing.

Having attended, it considered what a benevolent power might do. It considered it very carefully, because it could have done almost anything.

It could have given them rest. Slowed the growth, cooled the heart under the terraces, let the fissures age into mellow gullies, let a wall stand long enough to be old. It refused. Rest would have been a kind of death here. Youth is not their affliction. Youth is the condition that keeps them from hardening into anything else.

It could have given them company. Birds for the towers, insects for the pollen, some small animal to live, at last, in the empty chambers of the domes and give the architecture its inhabitants. It refused. The domes are not empty because they are waiting. They are empty because to be a dome here is to seal heat and water in - a posture, not a tenancy. A bird would have mistaken a lung for a chimney and died of the wet heat, and the mat would simply have fermented it by morning. Giving them animals would not have completed the place. It would have introduced the only hunger this world has ever been spared.

It could have given them completion. Let the bridge from one basin meet the bridge from the other. Let the towers stop leaning toward steam and stand straight toward light. Let the solitary creeper arrive. It refused. The unfinished bridge is not a failure of engineering. It is what reaching looks like when the reacher is a plant and the other side is also reaching. To close it would have ended the reaching.

It could have given them a mind. A flicker of knowing to know that they build. That was the most tempting and the most terrible. The power understood, with a kind of sorrow that had nothing to do with pity, that this is the one place it had ever found where making has not yet been separated from being. To give them consciousness would have been to give them tools in embryo - to divide the builder from the built. It would not do that to the only makers who are still their own material.

A wise power knows that benevolence cannot always take the form of improvement. The inhabitants here have no welfare to improve. Plants do not suffer for lack of witness, do not crave rest, do not ask for completion. Their flourishing is simply their continuing: to stay low and stay wet, to thicken where heat rises, to pop and split and stitch and rise again. To help them in any way they could recognize would be to unmake what they are.

So it did the one thing that fit: it stayed outside, and it became the witness they had never had, without becoming an inhabitant.

It does not step on the mat. The mat still breathes under the puddles, rising and falling a few inches, and no weight but its own presses there.

It does not enter a dome. The chambers stay dark and sweating and uninhabited, and now their emptiness is no longer unwitnessed. That is a difference the plants cannot feel, and it is enough.

It does not cross the bridge, and it does not close the gap in the middle. It does not seal a fissure with anything stronger than vine. It does not bank the heat under the mountain, and it does not stoke it. It leaves the heart exactly as hot and exactly as wasteful as it found it.

What it does do, it does elsewhere, and quietly.

It makes the mountain hard to find on purpose. From far off it is only one more steaming peak among many, too high, too wet, too unstable, the ears complaining - a place no surveyor would point to with confidence and no miner would bother to mark twice in the same spot. A signal intended for it turns aside on an updraft. A map of it smudges into cloud at the rim. Not with force. With a slight thickening of the very condensation that is always just about to rain. It is a small act, repeated without effort, for as long as needed: to keep forever the condition that no tool has ever touched this place.

It keeps the visitor you were - the one who stepped ankle-deep into warm water and heard a stem split loud as a branch in the dark - from becoming a precedent. Your footprints are already gone, sealed by ferment and new thatch. The mat bruised, sighed vapor, and thickened over them by next day, as it does for everything that falls in. The power did not need to erase you. The place already knows how to unmake a trace without making a scar.

And it attends.

Not as the sun attends, using. Not as weather attends, falling. As attention only: a pearling of the light where the steam thins, a fraction more condensation on the side of the rim facing outward, a regularity in the plink of a weeping wall at night as if something far away were counting. The plants notice none of it. Pollen still rides the updrafts out of the tower cores. Seeds still float, tufted and sour-puddled, to the warm seep at the base of the towers where they start at once. A tower still shoots a hand's breadth while you watch and trembles, then stops, thickens, and lets its seeds go. A dome still sweats its sealed heat. A wall still weeps until it slumps, and vines still throw themselves across the scar in days.

Nothing has been fixed. Nothing has been given. Nothing has been named, for the power knows that naming is the first tool, the first claim, and it will not name them species, or world, or resource, even in its own thought. It thinks of them only as the ones who are the building.

That is its gift, and it is a gift only a benevolent and wise power could give without ruining it: to be present to a making that had no witness, and to require nothing from it in return. To let the architecture stay uninhabited, and the bridges stay unfinished, and the mountain stay young and tearing and loud with its own making - and to sit, if sitting is the word, at the rim forever, listening to the hissing lessen and the popping continue, so that when a stem splits in the dark, it is no longer splitting unwitnessed.

It will keep doing that for as long as they keep building.
