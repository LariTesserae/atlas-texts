It is not called.

Nothing here calls. That is why it notices.

The power has been known to broken places. Places that are cold ask for warmth. Places that are dark ask for light. Places that have been stripped of green ask for green, and places that are loud with work ask, in their noise, to be stopped. It has answered those places for a very long time, and learned, slowly, the difference between answering and improving.

This place does not ask. It hums at fifty cycles down in the teeth, and breathes, and weeps from every stone, and polishes itself a little more perfect. Its completeness is so complete that it registers, at a distance, as a kind of silence.

It comes without light. The light here is already as much as this place can use. It comes as attention, which is a different thing: a slight thickening of the fog, a stillness in the attendant haze of micro-fauna as they pass through it and realize they are passing through something that is not weather.

It stands, if standing is the word, at a rim that the walker in the water will never find. It looks across the packed plain, ankle-deep in its own regard, and its first, habitual thought rises in it, unbidden: *cold, dark, no green, machines made without a machinist, running without rest.*

It lifts, as it has lifted before over a hundred worlds, the intention to mend.

And then it watches longer.

It watches the seventeen domes repeat, tilt for tilt, scar for scar, wave after wave. Not as a prison of repetition but as a family holding its place in a tissue. It watches the flank split after the small one has already pushed out, growth traveling before opening, so that nothing is ever suddenly torn. It watches the small jointed ones run from shell to shell, cleaning breathing slits, eating the metallic dust before it can foul a filter — not servants, but hands of a body that has no separate hands. It watches the discs raft and break and rejoin, eating the difference between warm below and cold above, and shedding what the grey film below will take and turn, at last, into something a filter-mouth can use. It watches the steam rise and freeze on the windward plates and melt and run uphill to the top of a warm dome, because heat here pulls harder than gravity does.

There is no waste that leaves the system. There is no rot, because there is no heat to spare for rot. There is no hunger that goes unsatisfied for long, no wound that is not already a place for a new plate to grow. There is no suffering signal in the register it knows.

Only a walker standing outside the packing, trying to keep his footing, calls it broken: clank before motion, foam before footfall, the same rock on the left and then on the right.

From inside, the power understands, the way it finally understands a pulse so slow that a whole life can be lived inside it, that sound must travel before motion can catch it because motion is too large to be sudden. That distance must be measured in hums because sight is useless in fog, and a hum heard once will be heard again a little to the left because that is how a lineage moves. That water running uphill is not a violation; it is a lung drawing.

If it added sun, the discs would starve. If it planted green, the green would have nowhere to root — the soil was ground away between animal and rock a hundred thousand migrations ago, and what replaced soil is better at holding heat. If it straightened time so that footfall came before foam, the wave would lose its anticipation and the domes would clank against neighbors they had not yet intended to meet. If it freed the domes from work, it would unmake the lungs. If it taught them speech, it would give them a need they have never had: to be understood by something outside themselves.

Benevolence without wisdom would have done all of that, kindly, and killed the place in the act of saving it.

Wisdom without benevolence would have catalogued it, taken a sample of dust, a plate, a disc, and left. It does neither.

It listens. That is the first thing it does, and it does it long enough to be unremarkable. It slows its own attunement until its listening falls into the same out-of-phase rhythm as the thousands of breathing chests, so that the nearest domes idle a fraction deeper — not in alarm, not in welcome, but in the way a body accommodates a change in weather. It takes nothing. No tissue, no water, no name spoken aloud. To name a thing fully, in its experience, is to claim the right to rearrange it. It remembers the pattern of seventeen, and lets the remembering be enough.

The second thing it does is veil. Not with a wall; this place is open by nature and a wall would be a foreign object to be grown over. It folds the fog at every approach that is not a walking approach. Coordinates smudge and repeat. A compass turns through the same seventeen domes. A map drawn from above will show a basin that is always a little further on. Those who come to take heat, to take metal, to take a marvel to sell as a marvel, will find themselves walking piston-like for a day and emerge, convinced they have circled, with their samples frozen useless in a skin of clear ice. Those who come only to walk will be allowed to walk. The being cannot defend itself against hands, only against weather. The power can make weather of confusion, and does.

The third thing it does is step in.

Ankle-deep. Then shin-deep where the water is warm against its legs. It feels the wave come through the soles of its feet before it sees it — a peristaltic band crossing what looks like a morning's walk and taking days — and it shifts its weight with it, lifting a fraction, clanking softly against its neighbor, settling. It walks beside the other walker, the one who has been learning to tell them apart by tempo. It does not pull him out of the water, because he is not drowning; he is being taught a tempo he did not know he was missing. It does not push him under, because stillness is the only true danger here, and it nudges, by its own passing, the nearest domes to grow over a foot that has stayed too long, so that he moves on.

You will not see it. You will feel, for a stretch of the plain, that the hum is steadier, that the clank comes at exactly the second you need it, that the foam forms just before your foot lands as if the place has learned you. Then it is gone, and the place is as it was, only a little more polished where its foot stayed a moment too long in the black water — a warmth the small ones will clean in time, ticking faster along that flank until the seam brightens again.

Then it leaves. It does not stay as guardian. A guardian would be another stillness to be colonized, another expectation. It goes back to the other places, the broken ones that do call, and it carries this one with it in a way that changes how it answers. It will not bring green by habit anymore. It will ask first what road life took here, and whether it is still traveling it.

Nothing has been rescued. Nothing has been restored. Nothing has been given a sun, or a voice, or a purpose beyond the one it already fulfills by breathing, filtering, molting, cooling, and moving a hand-span at a time across a province of weeping stone.

That is, from such a power, the rarest gift it knows how to give: to be known entirely, and left entirely alone — and to go on, wave after wave, with the clank arriving, as always, just before it is needed.
