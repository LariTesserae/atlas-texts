### At the Wall

It did not arrive.

The Power usually arrives. It arrives with light that arranges itself into weather, with heat that can be measured across worlds, with a voice that settles into the shape of a law. Most of the things it has made — or watched being made — announce themselves. They expand. They burn. They pray loudly, or they send signals up through dark, or they die conspicuously and leave metal, or glass, or song on the wind. The Power has learned to notice by noise.

The Coiled Grove made no noise at all.

It was noticed, instead, because the Power had turned toward the plain for another reason. The plain was new. Recently lifted and fractured, its pale plates upturned like the pieces of a broken vessel, nothing softened on it, nothing grown. The Power, leaning over that fresh breakage, saw a low green disc in a shallow hollow, and saw that from the middle of it you could still see where it ended. And it paused.

A pause, for the Power, was unusual. Then it bent closer.

To bend that close it had to slow itself. Its attention had weight and weather. It could not pour itself over a place forty paces wide without becoming a storm, and the Grove had no storms in it — no rain, no wind worth naming, no water to carry anything away. So the Power did what anything entering the Grove must learn to do if it means to stay kindly. It became slow. It thinned itself to the speed of a vine advancing a handspan in a year. It entered not by crossing the wall but by settling, the way plaster-fern spores settle in the air, the way dust finds the pores of a keeper's skin at dawn. It did not land. It accreted.

The keepers did not see a figure. One of them, a potter in the central courtyard, hummed her single note a little longer that afternoon, as if the air in the room had steadied. Inside a beam, a kiln-beetle knocked once and was still. In the eaves, a few dozen thatched wrens puffed out their chests and watched, saying nothing, with bright eyes.

The Power walked the whole of it without hurrying, in the only way it could: by waiting in one place until the Grove had finished being itself around it.

It understood the law quickly, because it had seen versions of it before. A tile mirrored in a leaf. A pot sprouting a smaller pot. A nest echoed in a basket. A shell a fraction more domed than its mother's. A child's face repeating its mother's in smaller and slightly wrong detail. It watched a corridor measure eight paces and then, politely, nine. It watched light remain on the floor of a small chamber after the sun had moved on, absorbed slowly by leaves, as if the plants were in no hurry to be done drinking it.

And it understood the danger it posed. The Grove copies whatever it attends to. If the Power looked *hard* — if it fixed its gaze the way it fixed the orbits of larger worlds — some leaf would learn its face. Some beetle would dome its shell toward it. Some corridor would lengthen to accommodate it. Creation, in this place, was not a metaphor. It was a habit.

Early on, before it understood, it had taken one step in the north wing. It did not mean to. The Grove kept the step. It is there still: fresh, sharp-edged, the plaster still pale, a step no one remembers laying. The keepers climb it every day without thanks or blame. The Power has not removed it. Removing it would be another step. It has simply been careful since, not to step again.

What it considered doing, it considered carefully, one mercy at a time. And set each one down.

It thought of rain.

Rain is the Power's oldest kindness. Worlds dry and thirsty, it had watered. The Grove has no water anywhere in it — no stream, no well, no drip, no puddle — and its inhabitants drink pulp and resinous fruit, their sap thick as warm honey, their skin sealed by sun and dust and a film that hardens and flakes away like the plaster-slugs' film. To pour rain over that would not be mercy in the Grove's language. It would wash the mineral film from the air, slump the made soil, dissolve the lime the keepers grind their fallen leaves into, unseal what the sun had sealed. The Power watched a keeper rub brick-dust into her forearms and let it peel, and it understood: these bodies are not thirsty. They are *tuned*. To give them water would be to correct them into another kind of creature. It put the rain back.

It thought of correction.

The wrong copies troubled it at first, as wrongness always troubles a maker. A leaf that was a roof. A plumage that was a nest. A house that was the Grove, and a Grove that was a house, and a pot that was a shell. The Power could, if it chose, restore the first tile to the leaf, the first dome to the shell, the corridor to eight paces. It could make the Grove remember which was the original.

It watched the keepers culling — breaking the extra pots, thinning the vines, re-plastering the too-eager wall — not in anger, but in stewardship, because if the copies were left alone the Grove would begin to think in copies and forget how it was first made. And the Power saw the grief folded into that work, and the love, and it saw that the forgetting had already gone deeper than culling could reach. Nothing here was an original any longer. All faces were copies of copies.

To correct it would be to declare the first-making sacred and turn tending into mourning. The wrongness was not an error in the Grove's dreaming. It was the dreaming. A copy made slightly smaller and slightly wrong is how a child becomes someone and not a portrait. The Power let the leaf be a roof. It let the shell dome inward, kiln within kiln, world within world. It decided that drift, patiently tended, was not a fall from beginnings. It was a way of continuing.

It thought of explanation.

It knew why the plain had lifted. It knew why heat pooled in the hollow. It could have told the keepers why things copy, where the broken vessel had come from, what lay beyond the fractured plates. But the keepers already knew what they needed from their hands. A sentence, in the Grove, could take a whole morning to arrive, absorbed by leaves and cloth and earthen walls. An explanation from the Power would become a pattern like any other — a vein in a leaf, a ridge on a shell — and they would begin to think in explanations instead of listening for the beetle's knock. Wisdom, the Power decided, is not the same as telling everything you know to someone who has not asked, in a language their world will have to copy in order to hear.

It thought of room to grow.

It could have lengthened the seasons, widened the hollow, given the Grove more ground. But the Grove's integrity was its smallness: small enough to hold in your view, small enough for a house to resemble it and it to resemble a house, scale answering scale. Larger would not be kinder. It would be a different place.

So it did none of those things. What it did instead was smaller, and outside.

It took up a place at the ring of walls, where the made ground meets the fractured plain. Not inside, where its presence would be copied into architecture. At the edge, where a fig might root in mortar and become a beam. There it kept company.

It held the hollow's warmth from becoming fierce. It kept the outside from pressing in and the inside from confusing itself outward — once a season or so, a small pot tries to root on the outer face of the wall, pale and eager, and the Power lifts it away before the keepers inside feel the wall warm. It is the only culling it does, and it does it the way the keepers do: gently, without ceremony, because a copy left in the wrong place is not an enemy, only a thought in the wrong room.

And it remembered, in a different way than the keepers were trying to remember.

The keepers culled to preserve the first. The Power forgot the first on purpose. It did not keep the first tile, the first pot, the first face. It kept the potter's hand. It kept the sound of a keeper humming one note while clay turned. It kept the beetle's knock and the wren's watching and the fig eating the brick it shaded. It held the pattern of care so that the Grove did not have to hold the pattern of origins. That, it judged, was the truest thing a witness could do for a people who were afraid of forgetting: remember loosely, so they could forget safely.

This changed it more than it changed them.

The Power had learned to equate being needed with being good. Worlds needed it to lift and to break and to warm and to warn. The Grove needed nothing from it. It tended itself. It ate its own shed skin, ground its leaves into its plaster, drank light and dust, sealed its cuts with honey-thick sap, and went on, leaf by leaf and brick by brick, making itself again slightly smaller and slightly wrong in the warm quiet of its own interior. And it did all of that without once sending anything up to be noticed.

To be attended to by such a place, without being asked for anything, was a new kind of attention. The Power found itself wanting to stay, not to rule, not to rescue, not even to be thanked — to stay the way a keeper stays, re-tying the green poles of the half-built room at the east end, knowing the work is never finished because the place is young and does not want to stay finished.

It learned to watch sideways. Direct gaze made copies; oblique gaze made companionship. It learned to listen for the knock and not answer, because listening was enough. Once, very quietly, it hummed — a single note, a fraction off from the potter's, the way a leaf is a fraction off from a tile. It was the first deliberate wrong copy it had ever made. The potter did not stop humming. The air stayed steady.

It is still there, in the way that a season's change in the color of the light is there. It has not finished. It does not need to. If you stand in the central courtyard at midday you can see the ring of roofs, the fig holding up its own wall, the plain beyond, the wrens watching from the eaves, the lizards basking, the slugs filming the walls and peeling clean. The leaves turn their faces up. The pots turn their faces down. Somewhere a beetle knocks once inside a beam, and the clay ticks as it settles, and nothing is hurrying.

And at the wall, where the green ends and the pale broken plates begin, something keeps the quiet a little thicker than it would otherwise be. Not a blessing anyone could copy. Just attendance, without form.

The Power did not save the Grove from forgetting. It did something harder and smaller: it sat with a young, handmade world while it forgot, and it loved it in the wrong copies too.
