It does not arrive.

There is no descent, no light laid over the rim, no sound made to be heard on the floor below. Attention is not a weather. It is simply that a benevolent and wise power, passing in the course of its looking through a young and unfinished world, pauses — because this place is so quiet it stands out against the noise of everywhere else — and stays to see what the quiet is doing.

It watches for a full turning, the way the inhabitants watch.

It watches the fog fill the bowl at dusk, shoulder-high and then higher, until the lower towers are islands. It watches the fog burn away in the morning from the top down. It watches the grey film spread a millimeter at a time in the joints that never dry. It watches the grazers follow a warm duct toward a cold one, blind and unhurried, scraping stone off the inside of the pipes. It watches the cleaners make their hand-wide dry trails down the weeping skins in the white sea above the fog. It watches the sleds move a hand's breadth above the polished strips. It watches the people, rarely, not look up.

And because it is benevolent, its first movement is to want to help.

It considers warmth. The cold here is mineral. It radiates up through boots even at noon. Children sit in a line along the edge of the sun-stripe because that is the only free heat the bowl gives back. To warm the bowl would be easy. To make the stripe an hour longer would be easy. To let the stone hold the day's heat through the night would be easy.

It does not.

Warmth, unasked for, would thin the fog. The fog is the harvest. Take away the cold that saturates the air at dusk and the towers have nothing to drink. The people would be warmer and emptier, sitting on dry stone with empty cisterns. A kindness that breaks the cycle is not a kindness.

It considers green. It would be simple to seed soil in the pans, to let something leaf in the cracks, to make the bowl softer to the eye. Every other world it has known asks for green as proof of life.

It does not.

There is no soil here by design, or by youth, which amounts to the same thing. Green would need the film, and the film is already the first life, and the grazers eat the film, and the ducts need to stay clear of anything that roots. To put a leaf where there is no leaf would be to make the place legible to visitors at the expense of the inhabitants who live in it. The power knows the old temptation to improve a place into something more beautiful and less itself.

It considers filling the emptiness. Hundreds of meters of wet stone with nothing on them. A wise power could build, could populate, could give the sparse city company.

It does not.

The emptiness is not absence. It is where a window can clear against its season and be read across the bowl. It is where the silence can hold without being broken by need. The towers stand far apart because distance is a form of care here. To crowd them would be to take away the room in which the cycle breathes.

It considers speaking. The people talk rarely, low, perhaps in touches, because sound costs heat and the wet stone swallows what is made. A benevolent power could give them a voice that carries to the rim, could give them songs, could answer the long quiet.

It does not.

It has heard a thousand worlds cry out. This one does not cry out. It keeps its water moving without loss, it keeps its drains clear, it beads resin over its cracks, it lifts itself a few millimeters a year with the mountain that is still deciding its shape. That is not a silence of deprivation. It is a silence of sufficiency. To break it with a louder voice would be to make the inhabitants turn their faces up, and turning a face up costs heat, and looking up at an ordinary sky gives nothing back.

So the power learns, instead, the shape of the place.

What it does — if anything — is take the rim.

It does not enter the bowl. It stays where the wind keens a little over the sharp edges and does not reach the floor, and it holds that boundary steady. Not by force. By weight. By being there, attentive, season after season, it keeps the young mountain from deciding its shape too suddenly in one seam. When the formation lifts, it lifts a fraction more evenly under the towers. When the slot between the slabs strains, the membrane there does not tear. That is all. No one remarks on it, which is the correct response.

Inside, it does less.

When a duct sighs and equalizes in the night, it does not add its hum to theirs. When a tower contracts with a soft click, it does not startle the walls. When ice ticks in an expansion seam as it thaws, it does not hurry the melt. It lets heat move without pipes the way the manual says it does, in a single sentence known by heart.

For the film, it does nothing. For the grazers, it does nothing, except that the warm-to-cold gradients in the culverts are never flattened by an outside season. They can go on following the temperature the way other animals follow a migration. For the cleaners, half-mechanical and unanswered, it does nothing, and lets them remain unanswered. It does not turn them into birds, or into messengers. A clean trail left on a tower is enough.

For the people, it does not look through the fogged windows. To look would be to waste the warmth that made the fog. It does not count them. It remembers their slowness as a fact, not a problem: hearts slow, hands thick-skinned, coats damp inside and out, food warm and small and taken sitting down, children learning quiet before speech, old people becoming still and then part of the insulation of a sealed room while the tower takes their water back into its cycle. It will not rescue them from that cycle, and it will not call it sad. There is no soil for a graveyard, and there is no need to make a meaning out of every ending. Some things are only to be kept, as they are kept.

There is one act, if it can be called an act, at noon.

When the sun finds the gap in the rim and lays its stripe across the floor to travel like a sundial intends, the power — still on the rim, unseen — lets one black flat hold the light a fraction longer than physics allows. A tenth of a degree. Nothing that would change the fog. Nothing the maintenance manual would need a new sentence for. The children shift their shoulders an almost imperceptible amount closer together in the stripe, and take the heat into them without moving to get more, and do not look up.

That is the whole of its benevolence, made visible only to itself.

And it sets a threshold, because benevolence without a threshold is only sentiment. If the fog ever fails to rise for a full dusk — if a duct stays cold when it should be warm, if a tower stops breathing and does not resume, if the water stops running off the stone under a hand and does not start again — then it will condense one night's worth, unseen, and put the cycle back in motion and withdraw. Not to save the place from its own fragility forever. Only to keep a single break from becoming an ending. Then leave it to decide with its mountain again.

Until then, it stays.

Not as a god above the bowl, and not as a guest inside it. As the stillness at the edge of the stillness. The bowl goes on weeping and drinking, splitting and healing, breathing two meters taller in late summer and settling back in spring. The sleds move. The windows clear. The fog rises at dusk, shoulder-high, then to the second stories, then over the lower towers entirely, and the lit rooms go pale against the white.

The power does not fill the bowl with anything. It does not make it green, or warm, or loud, or full.

It keeps the place from being disturbed by its own attention, which is the hardest and most loving thing a wise power can do in a place that has learned, so completely, how to hold itself together in silence and damp and cold stone.
