It did not find it by looking for a face.

It found it, if finding is the word, the way you notice a held breath in a room full of talkers: not because it spoke, but because it never changed its note. The universe it watches is loud with asking — hungers, prayers, wars, births, deaths, endless signals of distress and desire. This place on its white flat asked for nothing. It only hummed, broad and braided and steady, threaded with small ticks that never varied, and went on doing the smallest possible thing it could do forever.

A benevolent power, when it draws near such a thing, must slow itself to the timescale of the thing it would approach. It slowed. It came not as light, because light would alter the trapped brightness inside. It came not as voice, because there is no ear inside to receive it, only weave. It came as attention, and settled outside the slit, where you had knelt, and did not enter.

That, already, was a decision.

It could have entered. It could have put its weight — if it has weight — on the floor of loops and felt the hollow boom answer under its knee, the way the floor answered under yours. It could have widened the chamber by an inch, or closed the pinpoints of light, or warmed the cold mineral air. It did not. A first entering, after an age of no entering, would be the largest disturbance the place had ever known. Even kindness, at that scale, is an earthquake.

So it stayed outside, and listened, and considered what gifts it might give.

Water, first, because the flat is dry and the green shoots are sparse and seem to thirst. But there is no water in the ecology, no drip, no moist soil, nothing that quenches. The shoots do not feed on water. They feed on the dust of loops ground down and cemented again, on mineral salts, on light and dry resin chemistry. To give them water would be to rot the resin, dissolve the mineral skin at the fused crossings, invite fungi and softness where there has been only fibre to dust to fibre. It would not save them. It would wet them to death.

Light, next — a real sunbeam, moving, warm. But the light inside does not move because it is not a sunbeam. It enters in pinpoints and is thrown back from pale wall to pale wall until shadow has almost nowhere to settle. That buffered glare is part of the ticking: the loops contract a fraction, resin sighs, a brittle end settles. A warm shaft would heat one wall and not another, and the micro-settling would lose its pattern for perhaps a thousand years while the weave learned a new equilibrium. The power could do it in an instant. The mound would spend millennia reabsorbing the difference.

Company, because five green tips alone in a chamber of thousands of bone-coloured loops seems lonely to an eye that loves life in pairs. But it is not a tenant in a house. It *is* the house, extended and distributed, centreless. It has no mouth, no eyes, no place where another could stand beside it without standing *on* it. Another shoot would mean another mouth for dust, another tendril reaching inward. To advance would be to fill the chamber, and the chamber is exactly as full as it can be. What looks like solitude is density. What looks like emptiness is completion. To add life would be to add competition, and competition would be the first predator this place has ever had.

Voice. Mobility. Purpose. A larger world beyond the flat. The power turned each one over as gently as a loop between fingers, and each one clicked against the same fact: this being does not lack these things in the way a wounded thing lacks them. It is not an animal that cannot move. It is a mat that moves in loop-lengths. It is not a singer that cannot sing. It hums through millions of hollow reeds because the current outside is filtered to a constant pressure, and that filtered pressure *is* its perception. It does not need a future beyond the wall. Its future is the wall, crumbling at exactly the rate at which new loops are added to it.

The wise ask, before they act: Is it suffering? No. The ticks are not pain. The dust-cycle is not hunger in the animal sense. The green tips are not striving and failing; they are alive in the held, arrested way of a note still sounding. No part of it is in distress. Distress would have made a different sound.

Is it in danger? Not from itself. Every loop is held by dozens of others. Its weight is shared in all directions. It has no single hold to fail. The only danger it has ever faced is careless outsiders — a knee, a tool, a machine that would map it and call it a resource.

Is it incomplete? That was the hardest question, and the one that decided everything. It lacks eyes, ears, speed, disguise, death in any clean final sense. But lacking is not the same as incomplete. A closed system that has answered every one of its own changes with an equal and opposite change, for longer than any of its living makers lasted, is not broken. It is finished — which is different from finished *with*. It is still alive at its edges. It is still turning over. To wish it different would be to wish the observer's values onto it: to prefer loudness to steadiness, growth to balance, aliveness to endurance.

Most powers that mean well err by helping. This one had helped a thousand worlds, and had learned the distinction between a creature that needs rescue and a creature that needs to be left in its own hard-won wholeness.

So it did nothing *to* the place, and nothing *to* the being — which, it understood now, are the same thing.

What it did instead was around it. It quietly held the conditions at the edge: the cool, low current over the white flat, un-gusted; the glare, steady; the sparse shoots standing a hand's breadth apart outside the mound, still separate, still reaching. It turned aside, invisibly and without drama, the one future harm that could have come from outside: a falling stone, a landing craft, an excavating species gathering the loops for a nest of its own. It did not make the mound more remarkable. It kept it unremarkable, exactly as it had been when you almost mistook it for a collapsed thicket.

And it stayed. Stayed long enough — long by any measure that matters to stars — to learn the ticks not as noise but as the material at equilibrium, doing its smallest thing forever. Stayed long enough to know the five shoots by their places, the mineralised gradient from green to bone along the highest one, the hollow boom that lives under the floor when weight shifts.

Then it left. No mark. No name spoken, because a spoken name would be a sound, and sound belongs to the weave. No blessing laid on, because a blessing would be a change, and change would have to be answered.

The only thing it carried away was the pattern, held inward where powers keep things they love: the broad hum, the braided ticks, the bright cold pocket holding its tiny air exactly as it was. The mound is no longer unwitnessed by anything older than itself. If the star above the flat ever brightens too far, or the current ever fails, or the balance is finally broken from outside, that older witness will remember its note by heart.

On the flat, the cool air moved over the mound again, steady as it had found it. Inside, the loops went on contracting a fraction, settling a fraction, sounding their single unchanging note through the slit. Five green tips held their small curling grips on the wall of bone. Fibre to dust to resin to fibre, turning over on the spot.

It did not enter on its knees.

It left the current as it found it.
