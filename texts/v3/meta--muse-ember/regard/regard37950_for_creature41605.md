It does not open the sky.

It could. That is the first thing a power like that could do, and it stands for a long time — if standing is the word — in the place where the sky ended, looking up through leaf upon leaf into the thicker dark, and it does not tear the canopy. It does not let in a shaft of light for the radiator-leaves, though they are broad and thin and seem, to an eye made for sun, made to catch it. It does not let in a draught to sway the hanging curtains. It does not make rain for the stones that weep and are never washed.

It could do so much, and the temptations are all familiar and all well-meant. It could bring the animals. A bird for the sealed fruit. An insect for the pollination that never happens. A worm for the root-mat, a fish for the plant-choked pools. It could bring wind and seasons and death in the animal way, so that what is shed would rot instead of being taken back up through the same root it came from. It could carry the bright, useless things out — the glass-clear resin, the metal-like crystals, the concentrated salts refined forever in the amber bulbs — and give them a use, a market, a medicine, a fuel. It could study the pumps and the plumbing and the perfect closed loop, and learn from it how to make an industry with no waste and no outside.

It looks for the reasons it usually intervenes. It listens for suffering and finds none. It looks for waste and finds none. It looks for a need and finds none, only the slow thump through the root-floor, the drip onto a leaf and not a stone, the bulb turning its fluid in the same motion, filling and emptying. Nothing here is hungry. Nothing here is waiting to be saved. The droplet swells and holds and is drunk by the leaf that held it. The fruit ferments in its sealed chamber and is reabsorbed by the chamber that made it. The footprint closes.

Wisdom, in this place, is the recognition that kindness in our image would be violence here. To open the sky would be to cool the fog that the canopy breathes and keeps. To add animals would be to add mouths that must be fed from outside a world with no outside. To give the products a use would be to give them an end, and an end inside a sealed, saturated weave would unravel everything that depends on never ending. A power that has opened a thousand skies learns, here, to close its hand.

So it makes itself small. There is no empty space to stand in, no edge where plant stops and something else begins, so it does not displace. It comes in low, the way attention comes into a room, without adding weight. It does not push aside a stem or grow a root of its own. It wants nothing to mine and nothing to pump, and so it takes nothing. It is the first presence here that does not work — that does not mine stone, does not carry fluid, does not radiate heat, does not refine. It does not need to be reabsorbed, because it has not taken anything in.

It slows itself to their tempo. A peristaltic wave takes minutes to pass through the floor; it waits those minutes. It watches the droplet for an hour, and does not make it fall. It rests, if resting is the word, against a glowing node at waist height and feels the pulse go on without finishing, and it does not try to finish it. It lets its own pulse fall out of time with theirs, and then fall into time with theirs, and then be still in a different way than stillness. It listens to the drip, and to the silence after the drip, and to the drip again, longer than the plants listen to themselves.

This is what it does, if anything: it attends without using. It learns a measure of wholeness it had not known — that a world can be complete without progressing, fruitful without producing anything that leaves, industrious without a maker, useless not because it has failed but because it has succeeded so exactly that success and existence are the same thing. It does not name them a factory, a resource, a miracle, or a warning. Naming would be a way of taking them out of themselves. It leaves them unnamed and unharvested and unopened.

And then, because even witness, if it stayed forever, would be one thing too many in a weave with no gaps, it withdraws. It lifts its foot — if it had a foot — and lets the root-mat close over the place where it stood. It leaves no tool, no seed, no message, no crack in the canopy, no new sound for the tubes to carry. The only asymmetry it permits is in itself: the plants will not remember it, because there is nothing in them to remember with that is not already at work; it will remember them.

From a distance it keeps its regard, the way one keeps faith with a place one will not enter again. The fog stays where it is. The stones go on weeping onto leaves that drink. The bulbs go on turning. The canopy goes on radiating the earth's young heat into the dark air it has made and kept.

Nothing changes. That is not its failure to act. That is its act — to have come all that way, with all that power to open and to give and to use, and to have left the world exactly as complete as it found it, only now seen by something that wanted nothing from it.
