It did not come because it was called. Nothing there can call in a voice that travels. The wind is shut out at the tunnel, the cloud lid is shut down over the terraces, and even the echoes arrive before the sounds that make them, so a call would reach It long after It had already decided to answer.

It came the way the moth comes to the embers: by heat. From far below, where the mountain is stone and old and eroded and finished, a warmth was rising wrongly through the cold. Not a volcano. A kiln at the top of the world, in a room no wider than a doorway, sweating steam into thin air. The attention did not descend as light. It settled as weight, as a change in pressure over the summit, as if the cloud had learned to listen.

It expected a summit. It found work.

Terrace upon hand-laid terrace narrowing to a point, and then no rock at all, only clay and reed and matting, damp at the edges, warm to the touch. A flap of reed mat hanging over a tunnel built to be crawled through on knees. The power could have folded itself small enough to enter. That was the first thing it understood not to do. To shrink to the size of a hand, to pass through the tube of coiled clay and lashed wood, to arrive inside with shoulders brushing both sides, would be to become another hand in the room — and the room already has exactly enough hands for the work at hand. Its entering would lengthen the tunnel by a power's worth. The flap would become too heavy to lift. The rain that falls in a slow circle would find a new center and fall straight, and drown the basins.

So it stayed at the mouth, outside, where the air gives out, and put its attention through the flap the way one puts an ear to a door. What it heard first was not a voice. It was hands.

*Thock, thock, thock.* Stone on clay. The clack of the loom. Water slapping, hissing. A chant that is barely a chant, counting in a pattern that resets every few breaths. It has made worlds before, by intention, by blueprint, by a single word complete from the start. It has watched them cool and crack and erode correctly. This was the opposite impulse, and at first its benevolence took the shape of correction.

It thought: *I will dry them.* They are wet all the way through. Their skin condenses. Their bodies are half-plastered and will sag if handled wrongly. If I draw the water out, they will set. They will be solid. They will last.

And then it saw the kiln breathing, and the rain inside running down the walls in rivulets, collecting in hand-shaped channels, pooling, rising again, and understood that to dry them would be to stop their circulation. They drink from the basins their own exhaled steam returns to. To set them would be to end them more finally than any breaking.

It thought: *I will cool them.* The heat is total, tropical and saturated at an altitude where heat should be a fiction. Black moss grows where it is worst. The dome sighs and swells. Surely they burn.

And it saw them moving through it with a practiced, packed choreography, translucent in the steam, breathing through their skin, navigating by heat because their eyes are kept half-closed against a light that never decides to become day. It saw that coolness would make the steam fall straight down. It saw that cold would crack the tiles that have never been allowed to dry. It saw that what looked like suffering to a finished creature was, to them, simply weather they make themselves.

It thought: *I will give them metal. More knives. Tools that do not pass and disappear. I will give them a past, and names, and a way to die that is not unmaking, and a way out down the terraces to the natural world.*

Each gift unmade another gift already there. If there were more than one knife, the knife would not need to be passed hand to hand without looking, and the passing — that small choreography of trust in a room with no empty surface — would stop. If they had a past, the chant would not need to reset to keep the steam cycle and the fire cycle and the weaving cycle and the growing cycle from colliding. If they had a proper death, the crack that runs across a shoulder in the morning crush and runs backward by evening into the hands that will break it tomorrow would have to become an ending, and the surplus they work so patiently around would become waste. If they were led out, down and down to wild rock, they would dissolve. Their bodies are coil-pot bodies, reed ribs, mud plaster never finished. The thin air outside would take them in a breath. Or worse, they would survive, and bring the rule of the room — nothing eroded, nothing old, everything emerging, everything for a second time — down into a mountain that has spent a million years learning how to be old.

Wisdom, in a power that has ended many worlds by improving them, is mostly the discipline of not finishing things that are alive by being unfinished.

So it watched longer. Settled its orbit, like the blind grey moth around the embers, devoted and not interfering. It learned to listen to the gap between the echo and the hand. The hammer falls after its report. The mouth opens after the number has already been heard. The builders do not seem to mind the delay. They have learned to move in the gap, and the power, which has always acted first and seen results after, found itself learning to act after and trust that the result had already arrived.

It stopped counting six, then seven, then six again. It began to see what the counting was trying to hold: not individuals. One being, distributed. Its hands appear at the wheel, then at the fire, then at the garden urns by the doorway, and then again in a place not yet seen, because starting anything means continuing everything else. A forearm lengthens at the loom and folds at the urns. A body gathers around the work and dissolves back into steam when the work is done. Its shadows separate from it and pool by the kiln, waiting, darker than they should be, ready when it returns. Its crack is not an injury to a person; it is the being rearranging itself around a task. Its unmaking in the corner among the baskets-inside-baskets is not death; it is a shelf being built while pots are already set upon it, still wet.

To help such a being would require helping its room, and to help its room would require finishing it, and finishing it would kill it.

What benevolence could do, then, was not inside at all.

It could stand guard at the flap.

Not against them. Against accident — the kind of unmaking that is not theirs. Against the quake that would shake the terraces one step beyond into cloud and complete drop. Against the climber, the wind that finds a new crack and pours, the other, less wise attention that might one day notice the same wrong heat rising from the summit and decide, in the name of order, to dry it, cool it, straighten the rain, explain the echo, give the tile its crack back in the right direction. The power set itself between that future and the tunnel mouth, and held the cloud lid a little thicker, and held the mountain together from below the way a hand holds a bowl while another hand stirs too hard. It did not preserve the room. It preserved the *conditions* for its unpreservation. It let the fire eat the damp wood and the wood eat the clay and the clay eat the water and the water eat the fire, because that eating is their predator-prey, their appetite. It intervened only where an ending would arrive that the cycle could not incorporate.

And it remembered.

They cannot remember yesterday, because nothing there is old. A tenderness can be firing, have fired, and will fire in the same cycle. Herbs are green and brown and seed at once. A person presses a palm in a basin and forgets the hand that pressed it. The power, which remembers everything it has finished and lost, chose here to remember what is not finished and not lost. When a shoulder cracks in the morning crush, it holds the shape before the crack — not to restore it, not to impose it, but so that the forgetting is not a void. When the count resets and the hands reassemble in a slightly different order, it keeps the count in its own vastness, so that a reset is never a loss. When a tile found whole by evening runs its crack backward into tomorrow's hands, it knows both mornings. Its remembrance does not linearize them. It simply means they are not alone in their layering. Someone outside the cycle knows they were whole, and will be, and are.

It took nothing. It gave nothing. It did not add a stone from below, the first natural, eroded thing, though it considered it — a smooth river stone for the doorway, to sit among the handmade things and be old for them. It put the thought down again when it saw the vines testing for handholds, tendril by tendril, in the time it takes to blink. The room does not need an outside to measure itself against. It is already measuring itself against the drop.

It did not press the tile that was still soft in the floor mosaic, though it could feel it through the flap, palm-pressed, not yet set. It did not smooth the wall that stands half-plastered, reeds showing like ribs. It did not pass the single dull knife on when the hand that held it was done — it has no hand to pass it with, and to be given a second knife would end the passing. Touching without making. That was new for it, and it practiced it like the builders practice everything: by continuing. To be present, damp at the edges, warm to the touch despite the thin altitude, surrounded by vertigo, and to make nothing.

By the time you have finished trying to describe what it did, it has already undone the description. Not a failure. That is what a wise power does when it finds a living thing whose life is its own making: it learns how to make without intention, how to act in the gap between echo and sound, how to keep count without keeping order, how to stay.

If you crawl out — tunnel a little longer, flap a little heavier with condensed water — the summit will have grown by another terrace, and from out there you will hear the room continuing: hammering, counting, hissing, the deep irregular breathing of the kiln. The rain is still falling in its slow circle and never quite landing. The moth is still circling the embers, seeing heat for them. The frogs are still appearing and vanishing on wet skin. Above it all, where the air gives out, something vast and benevolent is listening at the door, holding the mountain from falling, holding the memory from being lost, and doing, with all its wisdom and all its heart, the one thing finished worlds never taught it to do:

Nothing inside. Everything outside. Staying.
