**What They Did, If Anything**

They heard it before they saw it, and they never stopped hearing it.

Where you distinguish hearing from seeing, they do not. Attention, for them, is not a beam sent from elsewhere. It is simply being sufficiently still in the presence of a thing that the thing can continue without being interrupted by your arrival.

And yet, when such a power turns toward a place, something in the world usually changes. That is the whole difficulty. They know how to warm cold air, how to deepen a pan, how to hush a multitude, how to lift a predator from its prey, how to give a small clever animal a tool it can keep and a fire it can tend. Benevolence, in most places, is hands. In most places, it is needed.

Here, hands would spoil.

To warm the air would be to undo the thin, scraped breath that every animal makes mist of at midday. To quiet the colonies would be to unmake the only instruction they have. To deepen the melt-pockets would drown the edge — and the edge is the inhabitant. To shelter the grazers from the great eagle-analogue would make the grazers too many, and the cushions, grown at a rate no eye can catch, would be cropped to nothing. To let the crow keep its sharpened pebble, to give the rodent fire, to build a wall against the constant wind, would be to give them the one thing they and the Pavement together have spent an age learning not to accumulate.

Lowland mercy does not translate. The wise thing is to learn the local word for good before speaking.

So they kneel.

Not in worship. In scale. To see the crust you must make yourself small enough that the horizon moves back, not forward. The power that could stand across forty kilometres reduces itself to the width of a springtail, to the damp underside of a plate. It does not lift the plate. To lift it, even to put it back exactly as it was, would expose thousands of snow fleas to the luminous shadow and the scrape of the wind from which the stone shelters them. Curiosity, here, is a form of weather.

It does not shout back into the wall of sound. Its voice, however gentle, would be an attempt to add a note, and the fissures are already tuned. It does not name what it finds. A name is a structure. A structure is something kept. They listen instead, in rotation, until the calling ceases to be a chorus and becomes what it always was: persistence with no beginning and no end, the same pitch in blinding light and in what would be dark.

This is the being they have come to attend to — not one animal to be saved from another, but a thin film spread across the whole table: crust, flea, mite, cushion, crow, rodent, grazer, bird, and the great circling presence above them. You cannot rescue a film by rescuing an individual. You cannot improve an equilibrium by interrupting it. The number of grazers has not changed since you arrived, and a death in a crevice has already been precisely replaced. That is not stasis waiting for a god to restart it. That is a world already at rest with itself.

If they do anything that can be called an act, it is commensurate and immeasurable. They breathe once, and the breath makes a brief thin mist at midday, like the grazers’. They use a small sharpened pebble, once, and let it fall, indistinguishable by evening from the wind-sorted stones. They wedge nothing into a hole. They crop nothing that can be measured. They stay only as long as a day stays, which is to say, they repeat themselves exactly.

And they carry the sound away — not as an archive, not as a structure built below, but as the crows carry their tools: by being able to invent the listening again each time they return. Between attendings, the Pavement is not unheard. That is the sole accumulation they permit themselves, and it is kept off the Pavement, so the Pavement remains unkept.

Then they leave, if leaving is the word, the way you came — down through cloud, into softer light and softer sound. Behind them the High Pavement continues to shout at an empty sky. The melt-pockets are the same size. The colonies scream at the same pitch. The grazers have moved, but their number has not. No footprint remains in the basin dust. It shows no sign of having noticed them, just as it showed no sign of having noticed you.

That is not rejection. It is how the place remains itself.

The difference is not on the stone. It is in the power, who arrived expecting to be needed and learned, at last, to be glad not to be.
