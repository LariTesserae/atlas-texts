They did not come from above.

There is no above here to come from, and they — who have been called down so many times, who have learned to speak in light, to place themselves visibly on a summit or a cloud or a threshold — understood within the first mist tide that descending would be the first mistake. They came sideways, the way water comes across this basin, at the level of breath, spreading, settling. Their presence was not a brightness. It was a slight heaviness in the air, condensation finding a new place to gather.

They had come prepared. They come to dim places and give light. They come to cold places and give warmth. They come to scattered, unrecorded things and give them a name, a boundary, a story to be carried away. It is how they have always been benevolent. It is what benevolence has looked like, elsewhere.

This place offered no place to set any of it down.

There was no empty ground. There was the Mat below, and the Film above it, and the reeds above that, and the shrubs and trees and hanging vines, and the weeping stones, and the brief architectures in the air. Every layer was already occupied because every layer *is* the inhabitant. They had come looking for a being who lived in the basin and might be helped. They found instead a being who *was* the basin, living as ground and as water on the ground and as air just above the water, never quite separate from the layer above or below. There was no one apart to rescue, no one outside the wetness to lift dry.

So they stayed low long enough to listen. That was not part of the plan.

They listened to the reed chord, many notes at once shifting by degrees no instrument could hold. They listened to the drip chorus begin a fraction before the drop fell. They watched a pulse travel through the luminous-brown Mat across what their eyes judged a mile in seconds, and in its wake the duckweed open, the new shoots extend, the dewdrops sort themselves into triangles and hexagons and then dissolve. They watched a torn leaf re-fuse without a scar. A footprint vanish. A stick laid flat, its shadow pooled wrongly beneath it.

And slowly, the first interpretation loosened.

They had thought it dim. They thought: give it a sun, give it a day, so that living things might work in the open. But the light here comes from below — algae in teal fluorescence, moss in pulse, dew lattices flaring. A high light would flatten that. Shadows here are already pale and cool and brighter than what casts them, as if the basin had learned to shine on its undersides. A sun would make shadow mean something else. It would be a kind of blindness.

They had thought it cold. They thought: give it warmth, so that what is wet might dry, what is still might move. But nothing here is still for lack of heat. Water moves toward a level it remembers, rises as mist to ten feet and rains out of itself without disturbing the layer below, pools deeper than the ground allows. Warmth would evaporate the level. It would be theft, done gently.

They had thought it lost. They thought: give it a path, a high ground from which a visitor might at last see the whole and not recur forever to the same grove, the same mound, the same delayed reflection. But you *are* not meant to see the whole from outside it. Wholeness here is not a view. It is a coordination, root to root, of the next condensation. A high ground would cast a real shadow. A path would accumulate. The basin does not accumulate. Forgetting is not its failure. It is its tidiness.

They had thought it unrecorded. They thought: give it a name, a ledger, so it would not be forgotten by the wider world. But a name is a vertical thing. It lifts a thing out of its layer and holds it up for inspection. Everything in this basin stays horizontal, spreads, pulses, circulates. To name the Grazer, the Singer, the Circle-Walker as separate entries would be to lie about them in a useful, legible way.

Each gift, examined in the damp, turned out to be a way of making the basin more like the places they already knew how to love. Kindness, they understood at last, is not always translation. Sometimes it is the refusal to translate.

---

They did not kneel. Kneeling is vertical.

They lowered themselves to the level of the hummocks and became horizontal. They let the mist tide build on them the way it builds on everything else — on the weeping stones, on the undersides of leaves, on the eyelashes of whoever passes. They learned not to move through the reeds, which would have been a disturbance, but to move the way the Grazer moves: pressure distributed so thinly that the water on a pool holds still in a perfect circle a few inches across and then fills in, leaving no print because mud here does not hold prints. If they had to cross a sheet of water, they let the sheet decide their shape.

During the lift, they rose. Not to a heaven. To ten feet, where the second ground is made of air. There, for the length of a breath, they were part of the Mist-Dwellers, conditions made briefly animate. During the settle, they rained. Not down through the rising layer, but out of it, in fine separate threads that fell past without disturbing what was lifting below. Then they were pool again, in a different place.

They did not heal anything. Nothing was wounded in a way that required a healer from outside. A leaf was already healing by re-negotiation. A stone was already breathing through its pores. The eating was already done cleanly, molecularly, by water and fungus, without waste or carrion or spectacle. To intervene would have been to introduce the very separateness the basin had spent geological duration eroding away.

They did not record anything. They let the Mat take their attention the way it takes a human breath, without ceremony, joining it to the air and hanging there a second longer than it should. Whatever they would have written down, they chose to remember only as sensation: the film on a surface, the chord rising and the deep hum dropping away when the whole basin exhales, the impossible clear blue of a pool deeper than its ground.

That is what they did, if anything can be called that. They attended. And in this place, attention is not a gaze from high ground. It is a physical thing, like condensation. It adds humidity. It does not take an artifact with it. It does not leave a path.

They learned, in return, something they had not expected to learn. They had always thought in creation, in top-down bestowal. Here they learned to think in phase change, in signals circling an extent in seconds with no builder and no brain. It did not make them wiser in the way they had been wise before. It made them quieter.

---

They are still there, if still is the word.

Not as a light. Not as a voice giving the Singer its note. The Singer was already sounding when they arrived, a long low note over the reeds with no locatable source because the source was the agreement of reed and mist and pressure. They are simply in the pause, a fraction longer than usual, before a drip sharpens and falls. A pool may, for a while after they settle, be a fraction bluer — complete, impossible — before the algae greens it back to its ordinary teal. Then it goes on, remembering its level.

If you cross there long after, as you will, brief and habitat-like and warm and dry-ish in a wet place, you will not find anything they left. No stone set upright. No dry place to rest a foot. No name for the place to carry home. Your own footprints will be taken, your breath will join the air, the sound will get into your bones.

The basin goes on past your sight and past your hearing and past the next recurrence and the next, lush and cold and wet and dim.

And with it, horizontally, inseparably, goes the attention that came to help it and learned instead to keep time to its mist tide — not above it, not apart from it, but as it, breathing out and in.
