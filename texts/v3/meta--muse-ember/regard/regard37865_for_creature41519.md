The power did not descend.

It arrived the way everything here arrives — first as pressure. A slight change in the damp. A chord in the crown that hadn't been there a minute before, felt in the teeth. The mat pulsing once, a little more strongly than the sap required. A beetle cloud darkening the light for a second over a plaza that had been empty.

It had noticed the bowl not because it was looking for cities, but because the noise had stopped being only noise. Layered, unsynchronized, calls answering themselves from towers no animal had yet reached — in that crowding of time there was the shape of a prayer that had learned to travel without a speaker.

For a very long time, it only attended.

It attended to the emptiness first, because that was the first wrongness. It saw that the emptiness was not lack. A plaza ninety percent pale mat and warm air was not a failure to build. It was room held open for arrivals that could not be scheduled: a frog chorus for an afternoon, a flower for ten steps, a grazer for a whole season, a cloud of veil-beetles following one breath. Fill it, and nothing could erupt there again. Straighten it, and the root-avenues would lose their honest crookedness. Give the towers names, give the runners and the bats and the plants-that-thicken-into-runners their fixed names, and each would freeze into a shape the mat had specifically learned to forget.

Then it attended to the noise, because that was the second wrongness. It understood that the sound was not too much sound. It was too much *time*, packed into a round bowl with nowhere to go. The towers were the tower before it, a season delayed. The swallows were building inward with their ancestors. A call made now was made again a minute later, altered, as if the town were trying to learn it. To silence that would be to make the place simple and deaf in the deeper sense. To repeat it louder would be mockery.

A benevolent power, if it is also wise, will be tempted by the large mercies first: to fill, to quiet, to fix the orientation, to preserve what is beautiful before the beetles unmake it. This one refused them, one by one, not from indifference but from recognition. These were not wounds. They were the way this place breathed.

What it did instead was small, and mostly in time rather than in space, because time was where this place was crowded.

It learned to listen through the soles of its feet, the way the residents do, and it did not offer that skill to anyone as a gift that could be kept. It stood — if standing is the word — on a root-avenue while the sap heartbeat came up through the polished mat, and let its own pulse join the chord without trying to correct the key.

Then, almost imperceptibly, it taught one tower to breathe half a beat late.

Not a twelfth tower. Not a different tower. The same leaning, politely inward, ribbed, translucent hollow, with its galleries and pods and leaf-crown, only its inhale in the heat and its sigh at night would lag the other ten by a breath's width. From the ground the towers would still be identical, as they needed to be. From within the echo, a lost being could hear the lag and know, without a map, which way was not the center. Orientation, here, was never meant to be seen. It was meant to be heard arriving.

It lengthened the puddles, but only by an hour, and only in a frog year.

Not enough to save them. The chorus frogs must still subside, dry, be taken back into the damp mat they came from; their white rings must still crack and fade. But an hour more allowed a late male to inflate his throat clear and to finish a phrase that the insects had already begun, so that the note in the air where their music *was* would not be cut off mid-syllable. The beetles who erase evidence still erase it by nightfall. The fallen leaf-platforms are still eaten in hours. Nothing dead keeps its shape. The power only asked that nothing living be interrupted before it had been fully, briefly itself.

For the beings whose lives are a single night out of a blossom, it did nothing at all, which was its own mercy. It did not keep the spore-bats from returning before dawn, nor make the blossom open twice. It simply kept the blossom from being picked, pressed, and carried away as proof. Some towers, in a wet year, grow a room-sized flower and out of it the bats pour, too many for the flower that held them. The power let that remain inexplicable, because an explanation would have been a kind of capture.

And for the being who lives in it — if there is one being, and not only a mat and a walker and eleven towers rehearsing each other — it did not bring soil underfoot. It did not build a house in a pod, though a pod could hold a chamber where arms could turn and it was always raining softly from its own ceiling. It did not make a home that could be lived in continuously, because nothing here lives that way, and continuity would have been a harm disguised as kindness.

Instead it accompanied.

It walked the empty plaza for a long minute beside the walker who never walks on earth. It left a faint green scar in the damp mat that closed behind it. It was present for a parasite flower blooming on a runner's back and being stripped by pollinators before the runner had taken ten steps, and it did not ask the runner to stop. It did not hurry the stalk-grazer pulling down a gallery and eating it for a season. It watched the raptors wheel above the ring and see, for one wingbeat, the whole bowl at once, and it did not give that view to the ground. From the ground, you are not supposed to hold the town in your eye. You are supposed to be held by it.

When the walker, out of habit or loneliness, called across a plaza, the voice came back three times, each time a little altered. This time the power answered the third return, not with imitation but with the same pressure lowered so it could be borne — close enough to feel, not loud enough to drown a pulse. A sign that the echo was not the town mocking you. It was the town, crowded with its own time, making room for yours.

At the boundary, where the same plant-thin world outside continued drier and sparser, the power was more active, though still without force. When seekers came with straight lines and surveyors' questions and the desire to preserve a dead leaf or a dead bird as a relic, the leaning stalks simply grew one more intertwinement. A new side root opened where the map said solid fiber. A plaza re-addressed itself after a condensation rain. The bowl became, politely, wrong again. No one was harmed, but no one could take the place out of itself. A wise power knows that some places are saved only by being allowed to mislead those who would save them too completely.

It did not stop the towers from shedding a floor as they grew another. It did not lift the puddles' reflection that sometimes showed a tower not behind you. It did not fill the hollow with the swallows' warm debris into a finished wall. It let the interiors keep their own dawns, out of step with the sky. Once, only once, it allowed the interior rain of a pod to be heard faintly from outside that particular egg-shaped room, through the amber-thinned stem, so that a listener on the mat might know that unseen generations were living entire lives in damp and warmth, a few degrees warmer than the world, without needing to enter and break their weather by looking.

Then it withdrew the way a veil-beetle cloud withdraws — not gone, not present, following the breath between towers.

It does not live here continuously either. That would have been the final unkindness. It returns as a cool draft across a plaza when a tower inhales in the heat. It returns as a sigh at night that condenses into a brief rain only on the undersides of leaves. It returns a season delayed, as each tower is the tower before it, having learned the walker's voice and now learning how to listen back.

Nothing visibly has changed in the bowl. The eleven towers still lean politely inward. The mat still gives underfoot like skin over a ribcage. The swallows still rustle, unsynchronized. Somewhere a frog is inflating his throat in water that will vanish by afternoon. Somewhere in a pod it is quietly raining, and no one above will ever know.

Only, if you stand still long enough on a root-avenue, you may notice that the echo is a little more patient than it used to be. That one tower is always a breath behind. That the emptiness is a little deeper, as if something has pressed its attention into it without filling it.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, if anything: it does not rescue a place from being itself. It stays long enough to hear the call arrive the second time, answers it more gently, and then lets the bowl inhale on its own.
