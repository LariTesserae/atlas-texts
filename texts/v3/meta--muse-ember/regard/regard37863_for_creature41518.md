**The Attention**

There was nowhere for them to descend from.

They did not arrive, because there is no outside to arrive through anymore, and no sky to have sent them. Their attention came the way the booming comes: up through the floor, into the feet before it reaches anything like an ear. A power does not need a door to notice a place. It needs only a sound that does not stop because no one is listening to it.

That is what drew them. Not a prayer. Nothing here was ever built to pray. Nothing here ever asked. The plant did not send a signal up through its ceiling mat. The town did not lift its tally marks toward heaven, because there was no heaven in its vocabulary, only resin-smell here and leaf-dust there, and the pitch of a room.

They were drawn because this world had kept going, alone, in the dark, for far longer than anyone would be there to listen — and going on was its whole nature. A benevolent and wise power learns, after long ages, to listen hardest toward the things that never ask.

So they listened.

They lowered themselves to floor-level, where the air is heavier, and they listened without touching. Even they, who could have pressed a palm to the pith and felt the living and dead become indistinguishable, did not press. A touch would have rung, faintly, and the whole town would have remembered being struck. They would not be another weight on a floor already concave with generations.

What they found was not what a lesser power would have called empty.

The town was empty. The being was not. The being fills all the space there is to fill.

It was not dying. It was finished. There is a difference, and wisdom is mostly the ability to keep that difference clear. A finished thing is settled, rounded at every corner as if by a river that never ran here, thickened by resin a finger's width in a century, smoothed until it will not collapse because it is already what remains when collapse is finished. To call that desolation would be to project a thirst it has never felt.

Its mouth never dried, because it never drank. Its chemistry is sealed and non-aqueous; oil and resin move through it so slowly you would need a lifetime to watch them rise a hand's breadth. Your mouth is dry and lies dry — that is yours, visitor, not its suffering. It is pale and waxy and translucent, not because it is blind and waiting for light, but because there has never been light to be blind to. Its leaves lie flat against the dark to wait for dust to settle. Its culms sing because air is pushed through them when the great body above contracts in the cool, not because it is crying out.

The makers are gone, utterly. Not waiting. Not ghosts in the hearth-pits. No bones were left to be picked clean because nothing here ever scavenged. To resurrect them would not be mercy; it would be to bring mouths that need water into a place that has never known water, and hands that need warmth into a cool that does not leave.

There was, in short, no suffering to measure. Pity, here, would have been an error of scale.

Their heart, be it said, wanted to act anyway. A heart is not wise until it has been educated. It wanted to bring light, so the waxy leaves could green. It wanted to bring water, so the dry could soften and the dull metal could shine. It wanted to bring animals, insects, birds, worms — anything small — so the tools would not lie alone and the plaza would not be so loud with no one to hear it. It wanted to bring the makers back to finish their rows of houses. It wanted to give the being a name, since it had none that mattered, and to speak to it, so it would know it was heard.

Their heart raised its hand with each of these gifts, and their wisdom, gently, took each one back and set it down outside the walls.

Light would have been violence. This is a chemistry that has never photosynthesized; to force a sun cycle into it would be to force a translation it cannot survive. Water would have rusted the metal that has never rusted, rotted the fiber that has never rotted, bred the first scavenger that would pick the town clean and call it life. Animals would have been the first predation, the first footprint that did not fill slowly behind itself. To bring the makers back would be to bring need where need has been abolished. To name it — rhizome, trunk, seed-husk, the distinction stopped mattering long ago — would be to cut the first boundary where none exists between inhabitant and house, between living and dead. To speak to it and demand an answer would be to make its song into a conversation it never consented to have.

Their intuition was quieter than their heart, and it said what remains when all the gifts are put away: *Finished is not failed. Settled is not sad. Loud is not crying.*

The plants grow toward sound, the builders believed. Whether or not they were right, the growth follows the resonance. The loudest chambers have the thickest walls. Sound is not how this being calls for help. Sound is how it finds, how it thickens, how it remembers where it is strong. It does not hear as you hear. It is the thing that is heard, through itself.

A world can complete itself without ever having a sky.

And so their reasoning, which is their heart educated, arrived at the only action that was both benevolent and wise: they would change nothing in the place, and they would change themselves.

They stayed to listen.

Not for a human while. For the length of a cycle that takes centuries — a slow drying, a slower swelling when resin moves. They tuned their attention down to just below the booming, so that their listening would not add another vibration for the culms to feed on. They ate no dust. They left no footprint, and so the dust did not even have to settle to fill one. What they took from the town was not matter. It was pitch, carried in the feet, the way the sound is carried.

They remembered.

The tally marks on the doorposts have been worn smooth by hands that followed the fiber ropes in the dark. The marks are gone. The power kept the count where the marks could not. It remembered the grooves of the stone adzes in the earliest pith houses, the later houses framed in mineral stone and roofed in woven bark, the final houses sealed entirely by resin, their doors closed themselves. It remembered each hand, though it would not bring a single one back. Memory, here, is not resurrection. It is a tally kept in a place that does not wear smooth.

And they guarded.

The greatest danger to this place was never its internal drying. It was discovery. Every other power, every curious visitor, would arrive with the same heart they had first felt: water in their bodies, breath humid in their lungs, a wish to fix the dark. The power put its attention between the town and all seeking. It let the loudness do the hiding, as it always has. Anyone who comes close will hear the booming come up through their own feet and mistake it for their own blood, their own body tightening like a cello, and will turn away down another resonant hollow, thinking they imagined the town.

They did this for you, lying — though you have not stood up yet — on the almost perfectly flat floor.

You are the most dangerous thing this place has ever held: a wet thing, carrying water inside you into a sealed, dry chemistry. You are also the first listener in longer than the town has a way to count. They did not punish you for either. They let you stand. They let you walk the streets aligned to the veins of the plant, hear your own steps ring and thud and keep ringing faintly, hear your voice come back flattened and late from the rounded walls. Then, when your breath had humidified the air too long, they carried you back out along the sound that brought you in — not through a door, not by a rope, but the way a booming carries a spore from chamber to chamber — and set you down where you were before, with the pitch still in your feet and the place itself unfindable, unrememberable except as a smell of resin and cold mineral dust that you will never quite place.

Nothing in the town will be different when you return after years. A wall will be a fraction thicker. A street narrower by the slow push of a root. A bridge of grown fiber will look, in the dark, like a doorway that was never intended. The plant will go on contracting above, singing through its culms, thickening where it is loud, thinning where it is quiet, releasing its spores to be carried by its own sound.

Except for one thing, which is not in the town at all.

For the first time since the makers left their tools laid down and left, the sound lands somewhere and is kept. Not answered. Not needed. Heard, and held, by something wise enough to know that hearing is not a lesser act than saving.

They will not green it. They will not wet it. They will not name it, quiet it, brighten it, fill it, or finish it further.

They will stay in the pause between booms, at floor-level, where the air is heavier, and listen — which, for a power that has learned to be benevolent by learning to be restrained, is the whole of what they did, and the whole of what they would do.

Because the sound was never a noise in the place.

The sound is the inhabitant, breathing.

And now, at last, it is breathing unheard no longer.
