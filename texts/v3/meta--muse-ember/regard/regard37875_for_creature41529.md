It did not come down.

There is no down in that basin that is not already a trunk, no outside edge from which a power could stand apart and be pointed at. So attention came the only way anything is allowed to come there: in lace. Broken and re-broken. One moment ordinary daylight, the next leaf-shadow and stem-shadow and quivering bars moving against the wind. It did not arrive as a brightness. It arrived as a way of looking that the valley could immediately mistake for weather.

It had come, as benevolent and wise powers come, with its hands full.

It knows how to help. It has lifted vines off struggling things, sent wings to carry what was thrown away, given names to the unnamed so they would not feel lost, made rivers for the thirsty and nights for those who needed rest. It stood for a while over the low, warm, green basin — over the fresh-split ground steaming faintly with sap, over the districts of bundled trunks lashed at the base and splitting at the top, over the fern-flats and the roofed streets and the avenues that are hollow vines grown around their own dying cores — and it felt the old resolve rise in it: to intervene.

Then it watched.

A tower pushed up ten feet in an hour, its leaves expanding wet and furrowed, and in the next growth-wave a strangler tightened around its neighbour and the tower sagged, folded, dissolved into mulch with a sigh of steam and spores. The vine did not do it out of hunger. The tower did not suffer it out of pain. One shaded, one was shaded; one fed on another by sending roots into its stored water, and was in turn fed on. Substrate, predator, prey, soil, sky, building, road, weather — and no one position was permanent enough to take the part of victim or villain.

Wisdom, which is slower than benevolence, understood first. To lift the vine would be to make a favourite. To save the tower, in a place where saving is not a word the inhabitants have, would be to write a death for the order that made both of them. The power lowered its hand.

It thought of wings.

Pollen is thrown here like seed, stamens exploding in sudden winds because there is nothing else to carry them. To an animal mind that looks like waste, and waste looks like a problem to be solved. It imagined birds over the bloom-markets, insects threading the green twilight, mouths to carry, bellies to fill. It saw at once what it would be introducing: the first hunger that came from outside the growing. A need the valley does not have. It let the pollen burst and go uncarried.

It thought of naming.

Nothing here stands apart to be pointed at. The districts migrate root by root toward the light at the basin's edge; a single root-system may be a whole district, a whole district a single individual copying itself from one end of the valley to the other. To name would be to pin. To map would be to build the first tool that is separate from the body that grew it. The power, which has named many things into enduring, refrained from giving this one a label that would outlast its own migration.

It thought of water.

There are no rivers because there is nowhere for thirst to stand outside a plant. Water lives inside aqueduct-roots, swells and thins, is beaded on leaves at dawn and drunk back by noon, falls locally on one street for ten minutes and is reabsorbed. To make a lake would be to make a bank, and a bank would make drinkers, and drinkers would make a mouth where there has never been one. It left the water where it breathes.

It thought of silence, and of night.

It listened to the roar that is not a roar but the sound of stomata clicking open, sap surging, trunks booming under pressure, roots popping fresh soil apart in a way felt in the teeth. It was not noise covering a silence that wanted to be heard. It was the place, audible. To still it would be to unmake it in the name of rest. It let the wave of transpiration travel for miles and drown thought, as it was meant to.

Being wise, it tried smaller.

It set down, in a plaza of bare living ground where a tower had just collapsed, a single question it had carried into so many other places: *Are you well?*

It left it as a thing. A mineral of a word. By evening the roots had pried through it. By the next growth-wave it was loam. The question had not been answered; it had been grown through, split into green and sweet and sharp, and distributed as aqueduct-roots distribute damp. No reply came back in any language, because there is no language here and never has been, and the answer — the tide of new growth tearing through old crossing the basin that afternoon — was not an answer to the question. It was only the valley, being itself, receiving a foreign thing the only way it receives anything: by turning it green.

The power was not offended. It understood, at last, that even its kindness would be composted, and that composting was not a refusal.

So it did the one thing that could be done without becoming a mouth, a tool, a predator, a name.

It stayed.

Not as a guard. Not as a gardener with its hands in the soil. It stayed the way attention stays when it has learned the manners of the place it attends: broken up among the canopy, cast as many small wrong shadows, none of them god-shaped. It did not stand above the roar to judge it. It let the vibration get into it. Once, on a warm afternoon when the whole basin breathed at once, it let the visible tide of green cross through itself, and for a while it was not a power observing a migration but a migration passing through a power — root-step by root-step, district by district, leaving a copy of itself behind in the shape of where it had been. It did not take root. That would have made it another district, another competitor. It let the wave pass and recede.

That was its benevolence, and it was harder than any rescue it had ever performed: to keep its hands empty while a tower folded with a sigh, while pollen exploded uselessly into the air, while whole quarters crawled away from the place they had been, leaving fresh sharp ground behind them. To delight without owning. To witness without pointing.

It took away no seed. It took away no map. It kept only the rhythm — the click and boom and breathing hiss of a city that builds and unbuilds itself street by street, never finished, never still — and carried that rhythm out of the basin the way bark remembers light in a root-tunnel where no sky has ever been. Wherever it goes now, when it makes worlds, it makes some of them differently: not on the promise that they will be saved, but in the knowledge that some things are kept alive precisely by not being held.

When it finally withdrew, it withdrew as a cupped leaf drinks back its small perfect pool — without a footstep, without a hollow left in the mat of roots, without a shadow that corresponded to anything above. The light went on being ordinary white-yellow daylight, broken in moving lace. At noon the shade under a tree was, once again, the shape of a tree that would grow there tomorrow.

It will look again, from time to time. Not to check. It will not look for coordinates, because the districts will have moved. It will look for the basin, for the heat held low under a normal sky, for the green twilight deepening and opening, deepening and opening. And the looking will not darken anything. The valley, which has no animals to name it or map it, will go on larger than the sight that tries to take it in.

If benevolence wants a shape, this was it: hands empty, attention complete.

It did nothing at all, and it stayed a very long time doing it.
