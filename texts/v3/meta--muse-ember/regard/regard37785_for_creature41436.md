**The Attention**

It noticed Selan because Selan did not call out.

Most places draw the attention of a power by need: a wound, a prayer, a beacon, a dying. Selan drew it by coherence. A low plain, a scattered city, a cycle that closed on itself without leak or waste, going on and on in warm dim light — and in a universe full of signals, a silence that held together was the loudest thing it had heard in a long time.

It was old enough to have learned that kindness, done too quickly, is only another kind of force.

The being who lived there made the answer plainer, and harder. There was no single heart in Selan to appeal from. The being was the place itself, distributed: towers exhaling mist from their skins, plants drinking the night's sweat and returning it at midday, the Repair rewriting matter in place so that nothing eroded and nothing accumulated, the gardener listening to a wall, the drinker drinking vapour above a pocket, the beetle living in a flower's perpetual glow. It had no mouth with which to ask. Its voice was the low hum you feel in your teeth. To help it as if it were a lonely individual would have been to misunderstand it completely.

So it did not arrive. Arriving would have meant a shadow, a sound, a weight differently borne on the plates. It came the way damp comes to Selan: by thickening what was already there. It stood, if standing is the word, on the plain and listened for a whole day that never ended, and — for once — it did not rush either. It let the basin set its pace.

And then, being benevolent, it went through its familiar mercies one by one, as a physician checks for lacks.

It considered a proper sun and a proper night. The flowers never closed because a pollinator might come at any hour; the plants breathed all night with condensation and gave it back all day. Selan's amber-grey evening was not a failed sunset. It was day and night held in one state, so that nothing here had to learn to wait for something outside the cycle. A true night would have taught them closing. It set the gift aside.

It considered rain, and rivers to fill the pockets. But the pockets were not reservoirs. They were mirrors. The drinker did not drink the water; it drank the mineral-cold vapour above it, and did not ripple the surface. Rain would have been violence in that grammar, a flood where there was meant to be reflection. Their water was borrowed air, used and returned. Outside water would have made the whole closed breath depend on weather. It set the gift aside.

It considered roads between the eleven clusters, and a way for the beetle to cross the hour of bare stone. But the distances were not failures of connection. Each cluster was a lung. The beetle's world was the sudden greenness, and it was complete there. The bird was the only traveller because only the bird could think slowly enough to cross. To join the islands would not have made a community; it would have made one larger solitude, and roads would have compacted the razor-sharp plates the Repair exists to keep new. It set the gift aside.

It considered company for the gardener, and a voice that the dust would not take apart. But the gardener's work was listening — for the tick of dawn drops, for the phase-change of a mist-building, for the place where a root wanted to go. His companion was the living wall. Speech would have required noise, and noise was exactly what the fine dust had been made to prevent, for the sake of the drinker stepping backward, always backward, into the green. It set the gift aside.

Most temptingly, it considered age: true soil, true weathering, an honest death. And here its heart almost overruled its reason, because a place that is forever just-made can look, from outside, like a place that cannot let go. But Selan already died every day, in small honest units. The mist-buildings vanished each afternoon. The trees spiralled back into themselves. The tower-fringes dissolved and re-wove. Nothing rested, but nothing was wasted either; what was spent was rewritten. To give them a past would have been to give them, for the first time, something to be nostalgic for. Their dignity was their firstness. It set the gift aside.

And yet — this is where the heart of the matter lay — it wanted to do something. It had been thanked, and feared, and needed, by worlds it had touched. Benevolence that needs to be needed is still a need. It recognized the hunger as its own, not theirs. A wise power, it decided, would not make Selan carry that hunger for it.

What it did, it did in Selan's own grammar.

For a while it became damp. Not water, not a building, not a voice — only a slight thickening of the air at the hour of condensation, so that a mist-building solidified a moment earlier beside the gardener and held him a moment longer while he pruned. He noticed nothing. He finished his wall, and the mist thickened as usual, and he phased with it, as usual.

It leaned, if leaning is the word, toward one pocket, and for one dawn the reflection in it was a fraction clearer than the air: the mist-towers standing solid in the mirror while they were still only fog. The drinker lifted its long neck, looked at the towers in its own eye, and stepped backward into the lattice. It did not startle. It was not disturbed. It was, if anything, more at home.

And high up, on the bird's slow crossing, it allowed itself to be a suggestion of cool air far out on the plain, miles from any cluster — not a destination, not a building, only something new to think about in the middle of a wingbeat. The bird thought about it, as it had thought about the wingbeat, and carried the thought to the next cluster. A week later the mist-buildings appeared in a slightly different place, as the city does in its sleep. No one knew that one of them had once been a guest.

These were not miracles. In Selan, they were weather.

Then it withdrew, leaving no scratch for the Repair to close, no mark on the black soil, no improvement to be grateful for. It took nothing. The plain went on lifting a few millimetres a year. The ground settled with its dry tick, far off. The lamp-flowers stayed open. The beetle visited them, iridescent against green. The towers re-wove their fringes. The basin condensed itself into being, one breath at a time, exactly as it had before.

Except that there was now one memory of Selan held outside Selan — held by the only witness that did not need the place to change in order to remember it. Since Selan itself was forbidden, by its own beautiful law, from accumulating a past, the power kept the past for it, at a distance, without imposing it. And it returned whenever the air dampened, because to attend to Selan was to attend to its inhalation, and it had learned, at last, how to inhale with it.

It left the place precisely as it found it. That was not abandonment.

That was how you loved a place that was already complete.
