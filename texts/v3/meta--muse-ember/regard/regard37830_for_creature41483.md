It does not come from above.

Nothing here does. Light does not, rain does not, help would not. So the power does not descend, it condenses. It arrives low, the way the mist arrives, pooling in the basin a few feet thick over the slabs, breath fogging, gloves sticking to cold iron. It learns at once not to speak over the roar. It listens through the ground instead, up through its boots, the way sound travels here.

What it finds is not what it was looking for, and that is the first piece of wisdom it demonstrates: it does not insist on finding it.

It was drawn, as powers are, by emptiness. By the lack of birds, fish, insects, by the flooded plain with nothing for ten minutes, twenty, but sheet water and pipes like spines. It expected a ruin, a thing broken and waiting. It finds instead a completeness so slow it had mistaken it for absence.

Machines breathing in unsynchronized fans. Water lifting only to spill back. Tanks sighing. A sealed line building to a shriek every few minutes and being swallowed. Bacteria sealing leaks with their own bodies. Moss making soil out of concrete in the joints. Lichen making coins on the north sides of tanks. A reed a hand high in a crack, blind-pale, saturated, doing the patient work of being green without sun.

An entire world, flattened into wet and wetter, seep and seal, eating its own construction very slowly from inside and being eaten by the wet in return, rounding toward a final flattened form that is not death exactly, but settling. An elder, already geology again.

The power considers, as benevolent things do, what would make it better. And then, as a wise thing does, it rehearses each mercy and sets it down again, untouched.

It will not bring light. There is no sun here to bring, and the lamps that are drowned in fog do not warm. To brighten would be to blind the lichen, to dry the biofilms, to make a place for moths that would starve and for birds that would have nowhere to land. It will not bring birds.

It will not silence the machines. The roar is not a symptom. It is a sternum. It is breath. To turn the pumps off for quiet would be to stop the fauna's metabolizing in the name of a silence it never asked for. It will not make it efficient. Inefficiency is how it lives.

It will not restore it to nature. It already is nature, of a later kind. Moss in the expansion joint is not less natural than moss on a stone. Fungus digesting a timber cell by cell is not less natural than fungus digesting a log. The rainbow oil in the runoff trough is eaten, molecule by molecule, by things that need wet and never end. To clear it away would be to clear away a food web and call the clearing healed.

It will not make it new. Newness here would be another kind of violence. The lean of a stack is its architecture. The weeping of a wall is its breathing. The corners taken off by a thousand years of wet are not damage; they are what time does when it is allowed to finish.

It will not explain itself to it. The being of this place - for it is a being, though it has no center and no face, only accumulation - has no ears for words. It has water levels. It has pressure thresholds. It has condensation forming in vertical tears and traveling. To speak would be only to add another noise to the roar and mistake the noise for a voice.

So it does almost nothing. And the almost is everything.

It steps, when it walks, on concrete and not on the moss mats in the joints, because those are the soft crossings and something small might cross there over a hundred years. It keeps out of the troughs where the film is thickening. When the arc flares blue-white in the substation yard and dies untended, it does not relight it. It lets the dark interval be a habitat too.

When the sealed line builds toward its shriek, it leans its weight - not much, not enough to change the physics - against the sighing tank, as if to be present for the exhale. When the pumps cycle, it counts them, fan by fan, out of sync, and lets its own pace fall in beside them rather than ahead of them. It does not hurry any cycle. It does not wait for a signal that might be only the water level itself; it waits *with* the water level.

Once, it finds the reed. It kneels - if a thing made of mist can be said to kneel - in sheet water a centimeter deep, and its attention, its weight, its breath-fog, raises the water infinitesimally around the crack. The reed does not need it. It is already saturated. The reed does not grow faster. A millimeter a year, perhaps less. The power learns that benevolence here is not watering. It is not pulling the plant toward a greener version of itself. It is letting pale be pale, and stunted be the measure of a life exactly suited to where it is.

That is what it does, over a time too long to count in human patience and not long at all to the basin: it attends. It lets its attention gather the way condensation gathers here, even on undersides, where it should not be able to gather, and drip upward in behavior if not in direction. For a thing that lives by accumulation - by layers of moss on dead moss, by biofilm on iron, by spore settling in fog and waking in damp - to be witnessed, continuously, without being altered, is a form of care it can actually receive. Not rescue. Company.

It does not call the place broken. It does not call it sad. Sadness would be a projection from a faster, warmer world. It calls it, internally, in the language the place can understand, which is no language at all but a settling of its own mass a fraction deeper into the basin: *You are enough. You are not a ruin. Continue.*

It does not promise that the water will never fail, or that the last pump will never seize. It knows that one day, after a time that will make a thousand years of wet feel like a season, a fan will stop mid-turn and the roar will lose one of its notes and the bacteria in the housing will divide a while longer in still water and the fungus will move into the timbers and the moss will move over the base plate and the concrete will weep a little longer and then be still. That is not a tragedy to be prevented. That is a completion to be kept. The power will be there for it, not to restart the pump, but to remember its rhythm, so that the roar does not vanish entirely from the world without ever having been heard.

For now, it stays. Not above. Not bright. Just low and cool and present in the sheet water, among the machines that make the sound you cannot speak over, keeping time badly and gladly, adding nothing louder than itself.

And then, in the oldest way it knows, it settles a little, too.
