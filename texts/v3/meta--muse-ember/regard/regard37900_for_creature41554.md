It does not arrive the way you did.

You climb, and your breath comes shorter than you expect, and the warmth comes up through your soles. It simply attends, and attention is its way of being in a place. It is there on the plateau the way a thought is in a mind: without footprints, without effort, and yet completely.

It looks a long time. In you that time would have a name. In it, it has none.

The place has drawn it because, in the whole turning of things it has attended to, few places have found this third way. Most worlds grow and then rot. Most grow and do not stop growing. This one grew once, sparsely, by growth alone, and then held itself at the moment of emergence — root-cord and stem and flattened leaf woven into ground and house and bridge and instrument, water condensing and dripping and being taken up again in a closed, warm, high-altitude loop. It is new, and it will always be new.

Its first reflex, being benevolent, is kind: *finish it.*

It knows how. That is part of what it is. It imagines, one by one, the completions, and feels how easily a palm of attention could do them.

The arch: a hand's width from closing. Warmth from one side, warmth from the other, the frayed rootlets weaving, the narrow channel shaded for the first time. A finished bridge.

The tower: the dome of overlapping leaves meeting at last over the hollow. Interior darkened, quieted. A roof.

The stair-tower: the row of half-unfurled buds on the platform railing opening, maturing, doing what buds in other worlds do when no one stops them.

The basket: the leak in its latticed wall sealed, the brimming cup held without loss. A reservoir that keeps.

The mat: soil invited up from below, roots let down deep, seasons given leave to come. A ground that could age, and rot, and feed other things in its turn.

And as it imagines each, it feels the cost arrive with the completion, inseparable from it. If the arch closes, reaching is over. If the dome closes, the tower's song alters — water still rises in the wall, but it no longer weeps out into light, it no longer rings in the same way. If the buds unfurl they will, in time, fall, and the railing will be empty. If the basket is sealed it will overflow. If the mat is given earth it will no longer spring back to flat under a lifted foot; it will be anchored, it will dry at the edges, it will begin to weather. The drip will vary with wind. Night will come and relieve the never-quite-noon light, and with night, the clock. And the second minute — which you felt, standing between presences, you would never be allowed to arrive in — would arrive, and after it all the other minutes, and decay, and the ordinary sadness of things that live by becoming and then un-becoming.

Wisdom is not merely knowing how to complete. Wisdom is knowing what completion would destroy.

It looks again, more carefully, the way benevolence requires. Are they waiting?

The arch is not straining across its gap. There is no tension in the rootlets, no effort in the curl. Reaching is its life, not its delay. The tower is not an unfinished roof; the half-open dome is its mouth for light, its way of letting the interior stay an empty vertical space where water can be heard rising. The basket is not failing to hold; brimming and leaking in exact balance is the most perfect holding there is. The buds are not children kept from growing; budness, held, is their form. The mat is not waiting for soil; the weave *is* soil here, and ground, and body. Everything transpires what it absorbs. Everything leaks what it receives. An equilibrium so perfect it feels arrested is, from inside the place, not an arrest at all. It is health.

To judge them by finishing would be to judge them by a temporality that is not theirs. In other worlds, to be alive is to move toward something. Here, to be alive is to hold a posture without variation: the mat springing back, the bridge spanning for no crosser, the pale translucent thing in the basket breathing at the same slow pulse forever, the drip-line ticking twenty times a minute in a cable no one reads.

No one is begging to be helped. Help that must be begged for to be kind is not kind.

So it does not touch.

That is not nothing, though. To withdraw attention entirely would also be an act — abandonment is an event, even if the plateau has no way to register events. And benevolence is not only restraint. It is also staying.

It takes a posture of its own between the presences, in the green distance where you expected inhabitants to be and found only mat and water. Not a tower, not an arch, not a basket. The posture of the middle distance, of the one who measures the separations by being present to them. For the first time the constellation has something in its center that is not a plant. The presences become related through it, though they do not know it. They were visible from one another before, because the plateau is open. Now they are witnessed.

It listens.

The plateau is loud, and that loudness has always been its strangest feature to you: drips striking hollow stems, water moving inside root-cord, drops falling through braided walls and ringing them like wet organ pipes, a song that never rises or falls so that ears cannot learn to ignore it. Towers sing their leak. No one answers. The drip-lines carry only water. They do not carry messages.

It does not answer with a variation. An answer would be a change, and change would be the beginning of the end. It answers by reception — which, for a song that is nothing but leak made audible, is enough. It keeps, in itself, the count of twenty a minute, not to time the place but to keep it company. It memorizes the shape of the cloud bank in the east, so that at least one mind in the universe knows it has not changed. It breathes, in its own way, at the tempo of the suspended thing in the basket. It lets its own presence stand ankle-deep in the clear film over green, and lets its footprints, if it has them, fill themselves as it lifts them.

It does not require thanks. It is wise enough to know that a gift that needs to be registered to count is a transaction. The arch will never know a hand's width of care; the buds will never know they are attended in their half-unfurling. The power's staying does not make them happier, because happiness, here, is not a state that moves. It makes their loudness no longer futile. It makes their emptiness no longer unwitnessed. That is the whole of it, and it is not small.

In time that has no name, it is still there: still new, as they are new, having learned from them the one lesson its vastness had not yet learned — how to be alive without becoming something else. It has not given them soil or seasons or night. It has not closed the arch or opened the buds or sealed the leaking cup. It has declined, out of kindness, to make them better in the image of a world that moves.

It arrived in the first minute of this world.

By wisdom, and by benevolence, it declined to be the second.
