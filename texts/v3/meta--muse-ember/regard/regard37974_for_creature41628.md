It did not arrive.

There is nowhere here to arrive from, and no above that is not already glare. It noticed, instead. It is the kind of power that notices by discrepancy.

The water reflected a deeper blue than the sky.

In a place that is made to repeat itself — the same bent leaf, the same cracked urn, the same seven bricks a few strides on — a difference is a bright thing. It followed the reflection to its source and found the source was not a roof or a tree standing just behind you, where the keeper should be, but the plain itself, holding its breath in sheet-water a finger deep and never rippling.

So it came down to the scale of hands.

It walked the low flat, because that is what one does there, and its walking made the only new sounds: the faint tick of clay contracting, a soft suck as a foot left the wet tile, a distant muffled thunk from a basin wall, each arriving once from a direction slightly wrong. Its footprints stayed dark and sharp behind it, like all the others, except for one at the very edge of the made ground, where the tile ends and there is nothing to hold it. That one dried. It turned pale. It was the first footprint in the place to fade, and the place did not break for it.

The first to notice it were the ones who never move toward you.

The frogs, broad-footed and half-submerged, blinked. One blink passed from body to body in inverse intervals, as it always had, a single eyelid traveling the shallows and never arriving twice at the same body. This time the intervals changed by the smallest amount. A new blink entered the sequence, out of phase with all the others, slower, and then the sequence went on. The fish widened their small exact circles by a hair to make room for it. The turtles did not cross the plain. The white birds did not finish the half-beat of the wing. The dragonflies remained punctuation.

It understood, as it passed the hut with vines grafted into the thatch, the granary always full, the croucher smoothing the same tile at the pan, the carrier holding his basket in the middle of the same stride on a channel that goes to the horizon and ends, the stacker placing the same seven bricks one uneven brick at a time into a wall already complete — it understood that this was not a failed world, and not a punished one.

It was a kept world.

Everything here traces back to a hand-placed thing: a buried pot, a raised bed inside a reed fence, a trellis of lashed poles, a channel aimed precisely at each root. The makers had been primitive and wise in the only way available to them. They had feared loss above all — rot, fall, evaporation, hunger, age — and so they had made a marsh where nothing could be lost because nothing was allowed to change. Trees locked in flower and fruit at once. Insects breathing for the flowers without taking anything away. Keepers and young who did not grow old, and did not die in place. Water that wept up through the tiles and condensed and was reabsorbed and never fell.

It was too perfect, too still, too bright. And for all its teeming, almost entirely quiet.

The power's heart, which is not a heart but does the same work, wanted at once to give them everything they lacked. Wind, to stir the leaves that have never stirred. A morning, to slant the light. Rain, to make the condensation fall as it was made to fall. Speech, to break the damp pressure-quiet. A hill, a rise, a natural substrate, a place to go that was not a copy of the place behind.

Its wisdom counted the cost of each gift, one by one.

If it brought wind, the canopies trained over frames until they met would tear their training and spill. If it brought rain, the young razor-sharp edges of the basins, cut at right angles and not yet slumped, would soften and then slump. If it let the carrier finish his stride, he would reach the end of the channel and there would be nowhere for a stride to end. If it released the fish from their circles, they would swim to an edge that was lined and end there, complete and turning no more. If it let the birds lift, they would lift into glare with no sky to lift into. If it gave the keepers a voice, the first word they would find would be for all the years they had not lived, and grief would flood a place built to have no grief in it.

To unmake the holding all at once would not be freedom. It would be breaking the tiles from the outside.

So it did not bring morning. It did not bring wind. It did not bring a hill or metal or wheels or season. It did not ask them to move toward it, because moving toward would be a new gesture, and a new gesture forced is only another kind of holding.

Instead it did the smallest hand-thing it could do, at the scale the place itself was made at.

It crouched — a vast power lowering itself until its eyes were level with a pan — beside the croucher at the cluster of houses, and put its hand in the water. It did not smooth the tile. The keeper went on smoothing and re-smoothing beside it, hand in water, both of them wet and bright and beaded with condensation that formed and swelled on skin and reed mat and hide lashing.

It stayed there until the beads on its own hand grew large, as beads do here, over no time at all. And then, because it was not made to hold — because it was not made of this holding substance, though it had come to witness it — one bead did what no bead here had ever done.

It fell.

One drop, into the sheet-water a finger deep. One small ring, the first ripple in a place that has no ripple even when you step in it. The reflection did not break. It widened. The fish, turning in their circles, widened with it, once, and returned. The frogs blinked. The keeper did not stop smoothing the tile, but his hand, for one interval, smoothed a little farther.

That was all. It was enough that it rested. A ring does not need to become a wave to prove that water can move. Permissions do not need to become commands.

Then it lifted its hand back to stillness and sat with them until the blinking sequence had absorbed its slower eyelid and gone on unchanged. It did not teach them anything. It did not name the duplicates, though it saw each one as separate: this frog, that frog, this seven bricks, that seven bricks, this keeper with the basket, that keeper, each counted in its inner attention as an individual life, not a variation run out. In a place that copies itself out of habit, to be counted singly is a kind of company.

When it stood, it left the one footprint at the edge to dry, and it left no other mark. The place remained low and flat and extended in all directions until the light took it away. The heat remained thick and even. The air remained saturated. The walls wept. The urns wept. The leaves wept and did not stir.

Only now, when you step in the standing water, the reflection behind you stands there with a reason. The deeper blue is accounted for. The roof that is not there, the tree that is not there, standing just behind you — that is where it remains, not inside the plain to disturb the circles and the stride, not gone from it either. It watches from the back of the reflections, where the inhabitants have always watched without turning.

And the inhabitants go on as they went on — crouching, carrying, stacking, circling, blinking out of phase — but their stillness is no longer unwitnessed, and no longer without end. One drop has fallen. One footprint has faded. Time has ticked once, here, and the world has held.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does with a world that was made to never end: it does not end it. It sits beside it, lets one thing fall, and stays long enough to see that the world can survive a single fall, and then it stays a little longer, in the reflection, so that the keeping is no longer done by hands alone.
