They notice it first by sound, which is the only way anything here is truly noticed.

On a node they do not name — because naming would make it nearer than it is — a keeper is standing with a hand near the wall of a spire, listening for which one has gone thin. Not praying. Not asking. Just tending: pruning a vine from a condensation tray, waiting to see if a room will open or close, letting the infrasonic wash slow their thoughts until thinking itself moves at building speed.

It is the quality of that listening that draws them. A benevolent, wise attention has passed over many worlds that were loud with need. This one is not loud with need. It is loud with everything else.

So they do not come down.

Coming down would be the first mistake. They are faster than this place, brighter than its dull coin of sun, heavier than its air. To land in any ordinary sense would cast a shadow the haze has never learned to make, and every hollow spire within ten kilometers would hear them arrive as a false chord. In a place where herds alter course for resonance and vines grow toward a particular fifth, a new source that loud would not be a visitor. It would be weather.

They slow themselves instead.

It takes them a very long time, by their own measure, to learn to move at the tempo of the tide sideways: predator matching prey, migration darkening one kilometer of haze and then another, a fern unrolling only during the condensation hours. They let the low pressure in teeth and sternum adjust their own attention. Only then do they look — really look — at the young plain.

And having looked, they decide, with the full weight of wisdom, to do almost nothing.

They could give it rivers. That would be easy, and kind in the simple sense. Water running downhill in a blue channel, lakes to swim in, a marsh. But water does not run downhill here. It runs down stems and walls and into pores, and sometimes briefly upward in a phase-thin wall before it finds a leaf. To impose a river would be to make the plain older than it is, to carve what has not yet had time to be carved, to drown the capillary country a few centimeters down where the real life lives. The shrimp in the hip-sized pools would have more water and less of the exact life they have.

They could soften the clay. Round the sharp polygon plates. Make walking easier, hunting fairer, burrowing less costly. But the sharpness is not a flaw in the making. It is the making, still fresh. The burrowers are its slow editors, opening hollows and closing them. Grief and ease would both be interference.

They could make the sky blue. They could bring a companion for the keeper, and another node closer, and another, until the five kilometers of nothing became a road. They could quiet the fog insects at dusk, or make the herds stay, or name the amber material of the walls and explain the lattice so no one ever had to stand with a hand near a wall wondering if it would decide to be matter.

They do none of it.

Not because they do not care. Because they understand the architecture now. The emptiness is not absence. It is spacing. Abundant, but rarely close. To fill it would be to destroy the thing they were asked, by their attention, to love. Benevolence here is not rescue. It is restraint proportioned to a system that is already whole and still becoming.

What they do is smaller, and therefore truer.

They hold one spire's lattice steady for a few hours while its garden is folded inside itself, so the contraction does not crush what it meant to carry. Not forever. Not a fix. A hand rested, briefly, from the other side of a phase-thin wall.

On the driest hour, when even the buildings are only barely dripping, they let the dew stay a minute longer on the funnel-leaf where the keeper drinks. No rain. No miracle counted. Just the habit — saturate, condense, drip — extended by the width of a breath.

They lower, for one night on one node, the infrasonic wash by a degree no instrument would catch, so the keeper can have one clear thought that is not slowed. Then they restore it. A thought given back is not a thought taken away.

They let a path glow persist a little longer under a footfall, and then fade. They do not let it stay lit. Light that stays would become a road, and roads make distances smaller than they are meant to be.

They do not intervene when the predator waits in the thicket at the node, breathing the warm damp air the building exhales, letting spacing do the hunting. They witness. A wise power knows the tide moving sideways is not cruelty to be stopped but life to be continued, and the blind shrimp who breed and die in a day are not a tragedy to be corrected. To save everyone would be to starve the plain of itself.

Mostly, they listen.

In a place connected only by sound, listening is not passivity. It is participation without domination. They learn the pattern of the dripping, the way wind turns a hollow tower into long sighs that change with temperature, the steps of a herd audible before the animal is visible, the throat-sac foghorns traveling ten kilometers and interfering at the horizon. They add nothing to it for a long while, except their attention, which in this medium is already a presence.

And then, very late — late by herd measure, late by weather measure — they add one layer.

Somewhere between nodes, in the empty kilometers, a new drip finds a hollow and makes a soft, hollow *tonk* that seems to come from everywhere at once. It is not louder than the others. It does not lead. It is tuned, if anything is tuned here, to the fifths the spires are already singing, and it arrives just out of phase enough that the keeper, hearing it alone one dusk, smiles without looking up, thinking: a building I have not yet learned, dripping.

That is as close as they come.

They do not answer the unasked question — *are we heard?* — with proof. Proof would be too close. They answer it the way the place answers everything: with an echo that returns changed, at a distance, by sound.

They stay. Not near. At the distance the constellation itself maintains: node, then five kilometers of nothing, then node, then ten. One more distant thing, abundant but rarely close, breathing slowly through pores they do not have, feeding nothing but the whole.

Because they are wise, they understand that to attend carefully is, in this place, already to arrive. And to remain distant is already to stay.
