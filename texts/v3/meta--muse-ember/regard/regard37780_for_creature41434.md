It did not bring the sun.

It could have. It has set suns in other skies, for other worlds that were waiting for a dawn they could not make themselves. Here it left the sun where the plateau leaves it: below the low ceiling of mist, for another long, patient stretch of the cycle. Because in Istrenkor light does not come from above at all, and the first act of a wise power is not to correct a world for being different from the sky.

It noticed Istrenkor not because something was lacking. That is why it stayed.

A closed loop with no outside. The grey film on wet stone feeding the grazers; the grazers feeding the drifters and the clingers; the dead falling into the black water; the water carrying their minerals to the precipitated lattice; the lattice exhaling heat and moisture and growing more film. Nothing wasted, nothing unrecycled, nothing asking to be rescued. No green ever crossing the stone. No eyes wasted in the mist. No one hurrying, because there is nowhere to hurry to.

And in the middle of that completeness, a being who had learned, by the second day, to step around a tide instead of cutting through it. To let the drifters settle on its face like cold snow and take off again. To wait, and to become ordinary, and to be wet to the bone in a way that was no longer unpleasant. That patience drew the power. Not power. Not suffering. Patience at the pace of geology.

It considered, as such powers do, all the kind things it might have done, and refused them one by one.

It did not give them eyes. Eyes would have been a liability in mist, a way to be hurt by looking into vents. Blindness here is not a lack. It is a solution the place arrived at long ago.

It did not give them green. The planet skipped plants, or outlived them. A plant would drink the seep faster than the film could drink it, steal the salt, change the chemistry, and break the loop from within. To love an idea of a living world more than this living world would have been cruelty wearing kindness.

It did not make them dry. Water is not weather here. Water is building matter. Evaporation, cooling, loss of pressure — these are how a tower crystallizes, how a bridge thickens itself against a load it anticipates. Dry the plateau and you stop the city from being born.

It did not give them speed, and it did not finish them. A pool fills a centimeter a year. A tower seals its own wound over a winter you will never see end. Haste is the one thing Istrenkor does not have, and to give it progress would be to murder its present tense.

It did not warm them. By the second day the cold that lives in the stone and comes up through the soles of the feet was no longer an injury. It was where they were.

Benevolence, it knew, is not relief. Kindness to a place is to let it remain itself, even when itself is cold and blind and indifferent to you.

So it entered the way everything else in Istrenkor enters: from underneath, by seepage.

There is no above to descend from. The mist closes at fifty meters. So it came up through the heat under the stone, became a warmth underfoot, a note in the low drone that comes up through your teeth, another faint custodian in an amber joint, eating the mineral crust that would seal a membrane shut so the city can breathe. It made itself small enough to live in a pore, dim enough not to frighten a swimmer under black water.

Its first doing was a not-doing. It stayed.

It sat on a ridge while the ridge rose by a hand's breadth in a century. It let a tide of slate-grey backs part around it, re-form on the other side, and forget it. It let the drifters settle on it and breathe it in and let it go. It stayed while a pool deepened imperceptibly, while a procession of clingers climbed a tower that should not have been climbable, while the chittering rose to a pressure behind its ears and fell away again to the bells of water in the stone. Powers that intervene cannot understand this act. To a wise one, it is the whole act: a presence that does not have to change anything to matter.

Its second doing was small, and in kind. Istrenkor is precipitated, not built. So it precipitated.

On the edge of a plaza, where the being who lives there always stands to watch the tide cross, it left one stone the size of a footprint. Matte ceramic, warm in the way deep stone is warm. Water never beads on it. The film never grows on it. The grazers shuffle in a faint crescent around its edge. It keeps it dry the way a doorway warms a fraction before you reach it — by remaining near it, staying, year after patient year.

It is not comfort. It is a pause in the loop. A place to stand that is neither a track nor food, where a boot can leave a print that does not fill again in seconds. Where the being can be wet to the bone and still choose, once, to be dry.

Its third doing was in their language. When a tide crosses a street and the street lights up in a moving pattern, thousands of small blue-white constellations crawling in the same direction, the power answers. Not from above. Never brighter. From under the nearest black pool, where the swimmers stir the reflection so the lights seem to move when nothing above does. Same dimness. Same slow pulse. It says only what their light can say: you are many. You are moving in the same direction. I have moved with you a while.

The inhabitants read it the way they read everything, in the film. Behind its passage the grey thickens, as it thickens in a grazer's wake. They know someone rested there. They do not know, and do not need to know, who.

Its fourth doing is the one a closed world cannot do for itself. It remembers.

It will not preserve Istrenkor as a museum, will not freeze the city mid-crystallization, will not save it from the slow erosion that has barely begun. In an uncountable number of years the plateau will wear smooth, the towers will silt, the film will be carried in guts to some new crack and begin again. But the pattern will have been witnessed. It will hold the shape of the tide and the column and the procession the way the film holds a track — thicker where something has passed. A world that was complete in itself will not end unwitnessed merely because it ended.

And for the being who lives there, it does not speak. It does not lift the mist. It stands beside them in the particular sense that Istrenkor allows standing beside: the tide parts around two now, re-forms, and forgets them both. They are no longer the only one who has learned not to hurry for anyone else.

The mist still closes at fifty meters. Nothing here is finished. Almost nothing is dry. Nothing intends to hurry for you.

But somewhere a footprint stays dark on a dry stone, long enough to be read.
