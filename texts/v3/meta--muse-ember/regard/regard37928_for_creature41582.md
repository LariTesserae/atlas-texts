It does not come down.

That is the first thing to understand about it. A benevolent and wise power could come down. It could put a foot on the knife-edge ridge and the mountain would learn how to be still. It could put a hand into the fissure and straighten the water, cool the pool to a temperature that makes sense, give the shrimp eyes so they could see the shadow arriving before the hunter, give the hunters a voice so they could warn each other, give the film a green so it would look like growing. It could do all of that in one unhurried thought, and it does not.

Not because it cannot. Because it has seen what happens when a finished law is laid over a place that has not finished deciding.

It noticed the pocket not because it was bright, but because it was undecided. The rock has not yet decided what shape it will be. The water has not yet decided which way is down. The animals have not yet decided which of them will be which. A wise power recognizes an undecided thing the way a gardener recognizes a seed that has only just split — not by what it is, but by how much it still could be. To fix it now would be to kill it kindly.

So it stays above. Its attention does not enter the crack the way your body did, crouching, burning your lungs, slipping on silica crust. It does not need to enter to keep watch. It holds its attention the way you might hold a palm over a candle flame — not to snuff it, not to feed it, only to keep the wind from doing either by accident.

It does three kinds of nothing, and they are work.

It will not make the pocket larger to relieve the crowding. The crowding is the form. Shoulder to shoulder, three and four deep, mite over worm over mite, is not suffering here. It is how the wall moves when no one is moving. It is how the film is grazed evenly. Make room and you make a shore, and a shore would make some creatures castaways, and the tide of crablets would become a colony with a center, and the hunters would learn to wait at edges. They do not wait now. They meet by density. That is enough. That is honest.

It will not make the pocket cooler, or wetter, or quieter, or louder. The forty-second boil is not a fever. It is a lung. In, out, in, out. The grazers surge up, the hunters surge down, the shrimp ride the bubbles and are rained back, the skin is scraped clean and regrows before the rain stops. To smooth that cycle would be to give them a weather they could predict, and a place that can be predicted can be exhausted. The unpredictability is what keeps the mineral film, and the shrimp, and the hunters, all exchanging roles. A hunter drops its hammer and becomes a grazer. A crablet is carried into reach and becomes food and then film and then, in the warm water, a swimmer again. A benevolent power does not interrupt that exchange to save anyone from being eaten. Being eaten, here, is not an ending. It is returning, quickly, to the ground that is also food. There is no waste in the pocket because there is no outside for waste to go to.

It will not give them eyes, or sound, or keeping. The tools are dropped for a reason. The pumice pebble rolls into the pool and is lost. The shard of glass is levered, slipped, taken up again or left. Nothing is cached, nothing is built upon, no nest, no web, no path worn by use. That is not a failure of invention. That is invention without accumulation. A hand that keeps would soon have a territory. A throat that sounds would soon have a silence to enforce. The steam already eats vibration; the creatures were not built to make sound because sound would be a lie in a place where the shadow arrives half a second before the hunter and no one flees anyway. They do not need to see what eats them. They need to feel heat, and damp, and the mineral gradient, and they do.

If it is benevolent, it is not benevolent in the way of comfort. It does not ease the hammering. It does not soften the glass. It understands that the hammering is thinking, as far as thinking has arrived here. The hunter holding the pebble between its fore-claws, strike after strike until a silica shell cracks, is using the mountain's youth — pumice, fresh glass, fresh fracturing — as an extension of its body. To take that away in the name of mercy would be to take away the only craft this place has invented.

What it does do is keep the outside from bullying the inside.

It holds the wind off the boot-width crack. Without that, the high wind would scour the steam thread and dry the film before it could thicken. It holds the crack to boot-width for a while longer than geology alone would allow. Wider, the pocket would vent too fast and chill. Narrower, it would seal and cook itself blind. Boot-width is the size of a crouch, the size of humility. Only a being willing to bend, to burn its lungs, to listen to its own breathing sounding obscene in the silence, can enter. And only briefly. The power sees to it that no one stays long enough to start measuring, or sampling, or naming in a way that takes. The visitor leaves understanding that this is not a crack with a world behind it, but a world with a crack for a door — and leaves forgetting the precise bearing of the ridge. That forgetting is not unkindness. It is the only lock a wise power can put on a door it refuses to close.

It holds time from passing too evenly. Inside, time does not pass evenly, and that is not a flaw to be corrected. The pool hatching and dying at once, the egg not yet laid, the hunter not yet moving, the same ripple crossing nine times from nine directions — this is what a place looks like before causality has hardened. If time were forced to be single, the ceiling pool would fall. The uphill water would decide. The inverted summit inside the rock would melt into a stalactite and be done. The power lets the nine ripples stand. It is patient with paradox because paradox is still load-bearing here.

And when the mountain finally does what mountains do — lifts, cracks wider, cools, lets the pocket drain or seal or flood with cold rain from a storm it cannot foresee — the power will not hold the pocket open forever. To freeze a young place in its youth would be cruelty, however tender. It will let the film thin and not regrow. It will let the last boil rise and not rain back. It will let the shrimp in the ceiling pool fall at last, and find that falling, all at once, is also a kind of completion.

It will remember the pattern, though. It has remembered other patterns, in other cracks, in other seas, in other thin, hot, silent places where life arrived before it had decided what life was. It will be there when the next ridge lifts itself fresh and sharp, when the next fissure sweats on a leeward face, when the next arm's-length pocket begins to rain uphill. It will not rush to improve it. It will not rush to save it. It will hold the wind off, hold the door to the size of a crouch, and watch — not as a distant judge, but as a witness who has learned that the most loving act toward an undecided thing is to let it decide in its own way, at its own boil-cycle, in ordinary daylight that makes nowhere for impossibility to hide.

From outside, the fissure is still steaming. Blind. The wind rushes past your ears. Below, the world is immense and old.

Inside, nothing has changed. The water runs up past a braced hand. The hunters drink from the ceiling as if it were the floor. The shadow still arrives before the hunter.

The power has done almost nothing. And that almost-nothing — the shading, the holding, the refusing to straighten — is what allows the pocket to go on being exactly as fierce, as crowded, as silent, and as new as the earth that cracked open and was not yet sure what to do with what crawled out.

So it let everything crawl out at once. And then, benevolently, wisely, it stepped back far enough to let them.
