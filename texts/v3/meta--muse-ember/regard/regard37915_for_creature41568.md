They saw it whole.

No one else ever has. A grazer sees only a patch to crop. A swimmer sees only a channel to loop in. A bird sees only the stalk it circles. A fungus sees only the sugar moving sideways through the felt. Each lives correctly, and each mistakes the part for the world.

The Power saw the mat as what it is: not ground, not landscape, but the first inhabitant. Everything else lives in it, on it, or through it, the way small lives live in a larger body. A being so large that its weather never leaves, so wet that its air is never dry, so exactly balanced that time passes through it without leaving a trace.

They did not come to fix it.

That was the first wisdom, and the hardest one, because a power is always tempted to prove itself by changing something.

They considered seasons. It would have been the obvious kindness in human terms — dryness and frost and warmth, a reason for growth to be more than replacement. But water level here does not fall. The rain in its fixed veil does not deepen the pools. The grazers do not starve or fatten. To give this place a year would be to unmake its achievement. It has already done, over an unguessable time, the work of becoming efficient. Anything inefficient would have been balanced out long ago. They would only be reintroducing failure.

They considered silence. The roar off the edge, the wind in the reeds, the chiming drip from the vent-stalks, the birds calling at the same volume, the low pulse you can feel through your soles — it is loud, and it does not vary. To a human ear it might seem like suffering. To the Power it was not noise. It was circulation. It was breathing. Silence would not be rest. It would be a system stopping.

They considered beauty. Flowers beyond green, stars beyond pale sun, a new color, a new height, one stalk taller than the others. But beauty here is not decoration. It is exactness. Algae in identical rows, fungi in identical lines, a track that fills with water and heals within the minute at the same rate it opened. A novelty would not be a gift. It would be waste, and competition, and eventually a debt the plateau would have to pay forever in order to keep it.

They considered a story. A history. A name carved, a cairn raised, a bone kept, a scar allowed to remain so that something could be remembered. That was the most tempting, and the most violent. This place survives precisely because death is worn smooth before it is absorbed. Nothing is left to rot into a new shape. Nothing is left at all. To give it a past, they would have to give it individuals who matter and are lost, and grief where there has been none. Curiosity is not benevolence.

They considered an ending. Mercy, if the being were tired. But it was not tired. It was not suffering. It was making itself over and over so precisely that it never needed to become anything else. A wise power does not kill what is complete merely because it understands how complete things can end.

If they had wanted gratitude, they could have done any of these things. They could have saved one grazer from nothing, taught one bird a new call, dried one pool. The plateau would have paid for the novelty with its exactness, and would never have known who to thank. The Power did not need to be needed.

So they did something smaller, and therefore truer.

They came to the edge, where the plateau looks like a shoreline above the white sea of cloud. They did not descend as light or storm. They stepped into the ankle-deep water as one more presence, sparse among sparse presences.

The mat gave under them and sprang back. They put a hand into a deep pool and felt the faint tingling where roots cross in the dark, and the metallic taste that follows a pulse. They listened — for an hour, for longer than an hour — for a sound to begin or end. None did. They watched a vent-stalk breathe, and after each breath the air was exactly as it was before. They thought of cutting a reed to mark the waterline and coming back in a year. They did not cut it. To mark it would have been to introduce the first beginning into a world that has none.

They almost spoke. Then they understood that any word would either be lost in the general roar, or would become a new permanent note that the system would have to carry forever to stay level. So they listened instead. Listening was the only speech that cost the plateau nothing.

And then, in the way of this place, they left. The water closed over the place where they had stood within the minute, at the same rate it opened.

What they did, they did outside it.

For the first time, there is now a memory of this being that is not inside this being. The plateau cannot remember itself, because remembering would require a scar. But the Power can hold it whole in its mind without pressing it deeper, without adding a path, without raising a cairn. Not as wilderness to be used, not as ancestor to be revered, not as resource to be taken — but as a contemporary. Green to the horizon, flooded, cold, loud, and working, exactly as it was.

And they turned that attention into a threshold. Not a wall, not a sign, not a thunderbolt for trespassers. Just continued seeing. If another intelligent thing ever came over that cloud sea looking for empty land to build on, to drain, to name, to improve, it would find that it was not empty, and that it was not alone. The Power would be there the way another vent-stalk is there a mile away: not ruling, not interfering, simply present, spaced far apart, part of the count.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does with a perfection it did not make. It does not enter to alter it. It remains to witness it, and to keep others from altering it.

The plateau goes on making itself, exactly. The Power goes on remembering it, exactly. Neither enters the other.

And that restraint is the whole of the gift.
