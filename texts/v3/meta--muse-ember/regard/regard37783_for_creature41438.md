It notices because the line does not waver.

Not because the pocket shines. It does not shine. Not because it is loud, or rare, or in trouble in any way that can be read from above. The basin is full of places that spread sideways over cold silt, full of roofs that have learned roundness, full of fish that cross and schools that turn. The power could pass over a dozen such roofs and never know this one was there, exactly as you could.

What holds it — if anything holds it — is that straight, glassy edge. Clear water inside cloudier water, at the same temperature, in the same cold sea, and the line between them does not waver. Something down there is keeping that line, particle by particle, at a pace too slow to notice. A sponge sighs, and the water is a little clearer. Another sighs. The algae hold the silt back. The hydroids catch what the rest do not want. Nothing rises very far. Everything spreads sideways. And from that sideways, crowded, intimate work, a lens of clearer dark is lodged under a fallen slab and kept clear.

A benevolent power notices such things the way the shrimp noticed the curtain: by pausing. Only the shrimp pauses outside because it cannot enter, and moves on along the outside of the rock without looking in. The power pauses because it can.

It could enter. That is the first fact, and the one that shapes everything else.

It could lift the slab. It could bring light straight down. It could warm the water, or set a current through the handspan of shadow so the pocket would no longer be still, so food would come, so the braids would be combed and thinned and given room. It could make the entrance wider. It could take the cupped fragment of fired clay — dull brick, half sunk to its rim, now crusted with living matter that does not care it was made — and set it whole somewhere a human eye would find it again. It could count the five notches in the back slab and decide what they counted. It could close the crescent of three uncut stones with a fourth, and build again what the sea took, and make a shelter where a shelter had once, perhaps, been.

Each of those would be benevolence, in the ordinary scale. Each is refused.

Lift the slab, and you do not rescue a sleeper. You tear the roof off a room whose walls are mostly living matter. The fine dust that is settling too slowly to notice would rise all at once. The clear edge would cloud. The filaments that close more slowly than they opened would not close at all; they would be rearranged by a displacement larger than any they have known, and for them rearrangement is not inconvenience. It is world.

Bring light, and the bruised green seep that the algae have learned to catch and lose and catch again becomes a flood. They are not waiting for brightness. They are perpetually, stubbornly lush in spite of dark, because dark is the condition they have answered. To illuminate them would not be kindness. It would be a change of country.

Bring a current, bring food, and the stillness that is their condition ends. The plumes sway in opposing directions not because they are waiting for a tide to agree with them, but because they have made a tide of their own small openings and closings. A real current would tear the braid where up and down have been negotiated away, and teach them a direction they do not need.

Make the entrance larger, and the fish that do not cross will cross. The pocket is intact not because it is sealed, but because nothing that moves has chosen to live by it. Its quiet is not an absence waiting to be filled. It is full of sponge-pace.

And the marks — oh, the marks are the most tempting, because they look like they ask. Five shallow notches, too regular for a fracture, too pointless for a tool. A fragment made by hands, worn a little by water. Three stones that may have been laid, enclosing darker silt. A benevolence that loves meaning would decode them, preserve them, complete them, mount them in clearer water and set them in a row. A wise benevolence does the opposite. It remembers them without improving them. It does not add a sixth notch in reverence. It does not supply the missing pot, the missing stone, the missing small built thing. A fragment is truer than a reconstruction. A crescent left open is truer than a circle closed by someone who arrived later and knew better. What the sea took, the sea took. To rebuild it would be a lie told in stone, and the inhabitants, who have already leaned their braids toward the darker silt and found it good enough to lean toward, do not need a lie to lean toward.

It could also hold the roof forever. The crack is still widening, a hair at a time. In years it will open. New splinters will part from the fresh pale blue-grey mudstone and join the silt below. The pocket, younger than the plants in it, will take small steps toward being an old place. Benevolence, in the ordinary scale, would prevent the fall. It would keep the place new, sharp, knives and flakes still bright where they caught the seep. Wisdom sees that falling is how the pocket was made. The first fall tore the room open. The slow falls after will blunt it, thicken the curtain, make it less fresh and more itself. To freeze it in newness would be to deny it its becoming. To hurry it would be the same error in the other direction.

So what does it do, if anything?

It changes its tempo.

It does not visit in moments. A moment, for the inhabitants, is less than a sigh. It returns, if return is the word, in minutes and in years. It learns to notice at sponge-pace: a minute for a breath, a season for a fraction of growth, a year for a hair of crack. Its attention does not arrive like a beam. It arrives like the seep — diffused, bruised, caught and lost and caught again by foliage — so that the pocket does not have to hold up under being seen.

It does not enter. Not because it cannot, but because entering would be a current. Even a careful hand displaces a curtain that closes more slowly than it opened, and there is nowhere empty to put a hand that is not already, in some small way, them. The power keeps its hands, meaning all its ways of touching, out of the braid. Its shadow does not fall two ways across their shadow. The displaced sounds — a sound from the far wall close to your ear, a sound from the entrance from behind your head — remain theirs to negotiate, not corrected.

It guards, but only against displacements of its own order. A splinter may fall. A splinter is proportionate. The whole roof may not fall all at once onto the cushions that have thickened over the clay lip and the leaning shard. It eases the strain the way a tide might ease, not by holding the stone up forever, but by ensuring the becoming stays slow enough to be a becoming and not a crush. That, and no more, is its mercy. The inhabitants have no ears to be displaced and no cry to answer; mercy for them is not rescue from their conditions. It is the preservation of conditions in which their form of life can go on being their form of life: dark, cool, crowded, clear at the edge, quiet in a way that feels artificial because it has not yet learned what sounds it should make.

And it keeps the handspan hidden, not as a secret to hoard, but as a privacy to keep. From those who would measure the pocket as longer than a body lying down and barely wider than shoulders, and then, having measured, would want to improve it. From those who would photograph the line and cloud it with their own light. Attention that demands to be returned is another current. The power does not ask the algae to know they are noticed. It does not ask the hydroid plumes to sway toward it. It does not need the pocket to be grateful, or to bloom for a witness. It notices, and the noticing does not have to be known down there to be real up here, or wherever up is when up has been negotiated away.

In this way it does nothing visible. No sixth notch. No fourth stone. No whole vessel set where a fragment lay. No lamp. No handspan widened into a door.

But the nothing is done carefully, and over a long time. It remembers the five grooves holding silt, darkening first with algae. It remembers the clay holding stiller silt inside its broken edge. It remembers the darker patch inside the crescent, where something small once rested or was built or died, and does not write a name over it. It remembers the shrimp that paused and did not enter, and does not make that pause into a lesson for the inhabitants. It remembers that bubbles lean sideways here toward the walls as if the ceiling were not up, and does not straighten them.

When the crack has opened a little more, and the curtain is a little thicker, and a new splinter has joined the silt and begun to be rounded by the very slowness it once broke in, the power will still be there, not as savior and not as audience, but as a witness whose witnessing asks for nothing sideways or otherwise. When the pocket finally becomes the old place it is already pretending to be — roof blunted, film greasing every surface, sponge sighing its minute-long breath into water that is clearer because of the sighing — it will notice that too, at the pace at which it happened.

You could leave by the handspan of shadow, and the water would close its clear edge behind you in a moment. It stays, in the only way staying is possible without entering. Not waiting, exactly, because waiting implies a direction, and up and down have been negotiated away. Simply continuing to notice, at the speed of growth.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, if anything: it does not lift, does not light, does not feed, does not finish, does not save. It keeps its thickness of silence from becoming pressure. It lets the pocket be a small, fresh, flooded room at the bottom of the world, packed with leaf and sponge, and it learns, particle by particle, how to be clear without entering.
