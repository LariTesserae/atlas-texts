It would not descend.

Attention for a thing like that power is not a descent. It does not go down twenty meters with a light that goes first, then the air. It does not arrive. The place and the life on it are simply noticed — the way the bass notices a shadow under a catwalk, without moving to meet it.

What it notices first is not the cathedral. It is the emptiness.

Two hundred meters of bowl, cathedral-high in the middle, and almost all of it uninhabited water. Cold, green-grey, dim. It understands this quickly, because a wise power does not mistake volume for fullness. Everything that stays here lives in a skin, centimeter by centimeter, pressed against the built. The mussels on the tank walls. The barnacles between them. The anemones on the valve handles. The crabs in the hollow legs. The shrimp in the silt that was never silt. The bass holding station in permanent shade.

The being who lives in it is not one animal you can point to. It is that layering. Sessile feeders at the bottom, grazers and scavengers in the middle, ambush fish at the top, schools passing through like a rumor. It has no center to save, no single throat to feed.

A less wise benevolence would have tried.

It could warm the water two degrees so the mussels would grow faster, so the crabs' hands would not go clumsy. It could open the grated roof and let real light fall in shafts instead of that filtered green, so the algae could be vegetation and not a suggestion of vegetation. It could silence the pumps — thump, thump, thump — and give the place quiet. It could lay down sand and mud over the concrete slabs, give the shrimp a true bottom, give roots somewhere to go. It could straighten the crane rails that end mid-air. It could rewarm the sodium lamp until it stopped blinking red and amber and simply shone. It could move a mussel that will live and die unmoved in one spot to a better patch of current.

Every one of those would be a theft, done kindly.

Cold here is not suffering. It is tempo. Everything moves a little slower to compensate and that slowness is the whole economy: the slow growth of the crust, the slow working of bacteria on oil that will take decades, the slow hour a bass can hold still. Warm it and you do not speed life up. You break the agreement that lets it be sparse and abundant at once.

Dark here is not lack. It is shade. The bass are under the catwalk because the hull throws a permanent shadow. The crabs emerge when the current changes because the dark is where their doors are. Light brought in would not enlighten them. It would expose them.

Sound here is not noise. It is weather and clock. The pumps cycle because the tide drives the sluices, the valves hiss up to the air pockets, the chains bang, the turbine hall pings as the metal contracts. Every twenty minutes the flush comes and the whole wall population opens at once, cirri beating, gills unfurling, catching what the pour brings. Silence the pumps and you do not give peace. You stop the breathing.

And the ground — there is no ground that was not made. A wise power knows not to apologize for that. Sand would bury the shrimp's concrete dust and shell grit. Soil would smother the holdfasts the size of coins. The residents are mostly immigrants who decided, once, not to leave. They settled on steel, then on shell, then on shell smoothed by other animals. If you replace the substrate you replace the history that made it habitable. The industrial has already stopped looking industrial. To restore it would be violence. To naturalize it would be erasure.

There is nothing wrong with the place that a benevolent act could fix. It is not a fresh wreck actively rusting apart. It is deeply aged, near-flattened by process, decay finished, in its oldest stable state. Corners rounded. Paint mustard, then smudge. Rivets blurred. Concrete pitted, bored, smoothed again until it is weathered limestone. A wise power does not treat an ending as a failure. It does not restart a machine forever just because it can.

So it does, if anything, the difficult, unremarkable thing: it leaves the variables alone.

It does not add light, heat, quiet, soil, or purpose.

If there is one active thing it does, it is this — it keeps the place unremarkable to the one species that would love it to death. It lets the letters on the tank fade until DO NOT SMOKE and AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL are only suggestions. Divers will know it instantly as a flooded shipyard, a drowned cannery, a sunken power station, and swim on. No anchor drops. No trawl. No sign, no sanctuary, no study that tags and counts and expands until the sparse density is managed. Benevolence here includes protection from being made special. The lives do not need the labels, and they do not need to be needed.

It stays, instead, in the only posture that fits the bowl.

Like the bass under the catwalk: motionless, facing the current, gills opening slowly. Not feeding. Not worshipped. Not noticed by the crabs, who know their exact shadows. Not reflected in the eye of a shrimp except for the instant the sodium lamp blinks red, amber, red, amber, through its glass housing, patiently, in a rhythm that has nothing to do with rescue.

It attends. That is the whole intervention. A mussel opens. A crab crosses your light. A school turns in the empty street and darkens the water and lightens it. The hum changes key as a second pump kicks in. It is all witnessed without being touched, for as long as it takes — which, in cold water, is a long time.

When the pumps finally change key one last time and do not kick in again, it will not restart them. When the chain no longer bangs and the hall no longer pings, it will not supply a new sound. It will attend to the mineral quiet after, to the crust thickening over steel until steel is no longer a surface but a reef it grew itself, to concrete becoming geology in earnest.

Most of it is empty, and it will keep it that way in the middle — cold, dark, loud, sparse, abundant, ancient, and still working at the pace of cold water.

A wise and benevolent power does nothing. Carefully. For a very long time.
