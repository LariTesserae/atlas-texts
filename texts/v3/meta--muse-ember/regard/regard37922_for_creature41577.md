It does not arrive.

There is no descent, no light on the horizon, no voice in the still air. A benevolent and wise power notices.

It has noticed many worlds before in the ordinary way: a planet cooling, a chemistry tipping toward complexity, a biosphere stumbling into solvent and rot and season. It is used to arriving with a hand outstretched. Here its attention simply... lands. Like a change in pressure before a rain that will never fall. The ping of a cooling crystal plate, far off in the mat, seems for a moment to answer it. The plain does not turn toward it. The fans do not change their angle.

That is the first surprise, and the power, if it can be surprised, is surprised: it is not needed.

It looks, because looking is what it does before touching. It looks for water and finds none and has never been. It looks for soil and finds young rock, unweathered, sharp. It looks for decay and finds a politely declined entropy, fallen fragments either reabsorbed molecule by molecule or returned to stone. It looks for a center, a heart, a machinery, a maker, and finds only a lattice under the polish of leaves, signaling across a whole skin of life without nerves or roots. It looks for growth and finds completion held at fullness. A scratch made a year ago is still a scratch.

Its oldest instinct, the instinct of all kind powers that have learned to shape, is to correct. To make the strange legible by making it familiar.

It considers, in order, the mercies it has given elsewhere.

Add water. The thought dissolves as it forms. Rain would soften the silicate glass, wash the powder-fine mineral silt into slurry, settle salt out of the gel that flows only when flexed. The leaves are not built to drink; they are built to inhale a permanent breath of carbon dioxide and sulfur vapor, molecule by molecule. A drop of solvent would not nourish them. It would short their lattice. The mining arms split rock for phosphate and iron in a chemistry that has never known wetness. To wet this place would not be kindness. It would be erasure disguised as kindness.

Add time. Add seasons, allow unfurling and shedding and fruiting in waves. But the fans are already at the sun's angle. If they moved, the interlocked canopy would break its precise light. If they shed, the mat would be smothered by what it has no enzymes to rot. If they grew, they would overgrow the template they tile so exactly every two hundred meters. The system regulates its own temperature underfoot, its own air, its own stillness, with no center. A season would be a vandalism.

Add fauna. More steppers, faster steppers, things with birdsong and webs and burrows. The power considers the one it can see: low, ash-grey, armored in mirrored plates, grazing mineral film from glass with a mouth built for scraping. It has a nervous system, presumably. It moves at a pace that belongs to geology. It does not flee you, the walker, and it does not turn toward you. To accelerate it, to give it mates, to give it a speed at which it could be understood as animal in our sense, would be to give it a different life. Not a better one. A different one, imposed from outside.

Add a road. Straighten the seams where distance does not add up, where ten steps counted become fifteen and never count the same twice, where a frond casts two shadows, one true and one at a right angle, both sharp. Where a leaf is solid from one side and translucent from the other without thinning. It would be easy. It would also be a destruction of handwriting. This place speaks in double shadows and exact repetitions and afterimages that linger a second too long in the air. To edit that out would be to make it say nothing, correctly.

The power pauses. Pausing is rarer for it than creating.

And in the pause, its wisdom, which is only another name for its benevolence grown old enough to be humble, reaches the conclusion its younger self would have missed: this is not a wounded world. It is a world healed in a different grammar. Not broken. Other. Sovereign in its small, flat, endless domain. The correction that holds here was not a mistake to be fixed but a decision to be respected — perhaps the plain's own, perhaps made long ago by some other mercy, perhaps simply what happens when life is allowed to solve for something other than water.

So it does not terraform. It does, instead, the harder and smaller things.

First, it veils. Not to hide the plain cruelly, but to keep it finished. It amends the approaches others might take: instruments will misread, maps will smear the template into unremarkable green, sample jars will come back empty, boots will come back clean. No seed will hitchhike out on a walker to die in alien air, and no alien seed will hitchhike in to break the lattice. The plain will remain unbuilt, unruined, uncollected. Benevolence here is a guardrail made of forgetfulness — the world will be allowed to be lush and strange and unexploited, which is the only way it can stay lush and strange.

Second, it amends itself. In its own vast catalogue of how life may be, it adds a footnote, in a hand it does not usually use. *See also: the plain that never drank. Life without solvent, without decay, without growth beyond fullness. Entropy politely declined in one locale, with the consent of the inhabitants.* It does not translate the plain into familiar terms to understand it. It changes its own terms so the plain can be understood as it is. That is the truest act of a wise power: to become more like the thing it would understand, rather than forcing the thing to become more like it.

Third — and this costs it more than any world-making — it slows down.

It has always moved at the speed of attention, which is to say, instantly from crater to ocean. To meet the stepper, it adopts the stepper's tempo. It sits, in whatever way such a power can sit, beside the mirrored plates in the mineral silt, and it scrapes light with it, almost. It watches the drifters hold their station in air that should not hold them, without wings or sound, never landing. It learns to read both shadows at once without deciding which is true. It stays for a geological morning, doing nothing that can be detected by any microscope, because detection is not the point. Witnessing is.

You, walking, are the only fast thing it has allowed near. Your breathing is loud in the engineered silence. Your steps are crisp and die in the porous pads and leave them unaltered. You are, for the plain, a novelty and a risk. The power does not lay a road for your return, but it makes the seams kind: they fold, they miscount, they repeat their stones exactly, but they do not trap. You will find your way out not because the plain has been straightened but because it has been permitted to let you go. You will leave with no cutting hissing in your pocket, no grey spore on your sleeve, nothing to prove except a quieter pulse in your ears than you had when you came in. That is its mercy to you: to let you be changed without letting you take, and to let you take nothing without making you feel robbed.

What will it do, then, if anything?

It will keep the plain exactly as it is. It will keep itself slightly altered, which is the only alteration this place can afford and still inform. It will keep the air dry and metallic and still for the fans that inhale it. It will keep the warm ionic slurry flowing only when a stem bends, setting to salt when it rests. It will keep the scratch a scratch, the seed patient in its cluster, the spore turning slowly in air that never quite lets it fall.

And it will stay, slowed to a pace of stone, beside the one animal being who lives there in the sense we recognize, not hurrying it, not speeding it, not explaining it. Two almost-still things in a finished green to the horizon: one that has never moved much at all, and one that has chosen, at last, not to move much either.

Nothing changes. And now, finished and witnessed, that is everything that can be allowed to change.
