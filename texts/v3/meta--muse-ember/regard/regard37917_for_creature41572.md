It noticed them not because they were loud — though they are — but because, in a world that had been moving for a very long time, they were not moving at all.

The Power came down the way the root came down: through leaf litter, through root and topsoil and hardpan, slowly, without parting anything that did not want to be parted. It did not come through the seam. The seam is exactly hand-wide, and a hand is a claim of ownership. It understood that a hand put in to help is still a hand put in.

So it settled on the outside of the wall, and put its attention against the stone.

Attention, in this case, was not a metaphor. It was the only form of entry that did not displace a tile.

It listened long enough for its own noise to fall quiet. The high whine of the bells. The wet clicking of the pinhead crabs. The scraping shush of the floor as a thousand slit-mouths opened and closed. Inside that small volume the chord inflated until it seemed to come from inside a head, and the Power did not flinch from it, and did not try to tune it.

It asked, the way wise things ask, whether any of this was suffering.

It listened for a cry and found none. The bells were not screaming; they were shivering, which is what bells of that size do. The crabs were not calling for help; they were clicking to know where the edge of the crowd was, which is how they know where they are. The filter-cups were not exhausted by waving; waving-and-stopping was the whole of their labor, and their labor was complete each time they stopped. The root was not starving; it attended. The algae was not stunted; it persisted, white-green as felt, in the one corner where attending was possible. The crystal buds were not arrested at three millimeters; three millimeters was the form they had chosen, forever, the way a chord chooses its notes and holds them.

Nothing here was waiting to be rescued. That was the first thing wisdom taught it, and the hardest.

The Power was benevolent enough to feel the temptation anyway. It would have been easy. It could have warmed the stone by one degree and let the condensation actually fall. It could have given the root-hair its fourth millimeter and let it touch the algae. It could have finished the spiral in the wall, added the eighth pebble to the cairn, widened the seam to two hands and then to a door and then to a mouth of daylight. It could have taught the tiles to travel, the crabs to see, the bells to sing a melody instead of a chord. Progress would have looked like mercy from above.

From a distance, a frozen equilibrium looks like a curse. From close, with attention pressed to it, it looks like a different kind of life: life as repetition, life as staying. The Power, who was itself very old and had watched stars repeat their orbits and hearts repeat their beats and tides repeat their arguments with the shore, recognized the pattern. It did not need to be cured of its own repetitions before it could honor theirs.

To remake them in the image of arrival would have been a theft, however kindly intended. Opening the pocket would have killed it more surely than leaving it shut ever could.

So the Power did what benevolence does when it outgrows fixing.

It did not enter. It stayed outside, the way a root stays outside, reaching in and stopping short and attending. It cushioned the outside world so the inside could keep its own terms. It took the vibration out of footsteps above. It kept a drill from remembering this exact place. It kept water from finding a new path down and drowning the 0.7 damp in weather. It kept light from being brought in that had heat in it. It kept the seam hand-wide — not sealed, not widened — an exact, unarguable boundary between the time that passes and the time that repeats.

Visitors, it allowed, because visitors are temporary and know they are temporary. If one of them, shouting to hear itself, stumbled back through the seam with its boots full of tiles that hissed and flowed together behind it, the Power let it go. It knew the difference between a resident and a displaced thing. A resident belongs to the chord. A visitor belongs to progress, and needs to be returned to it.

It scratched the spiral once, in the same groove, click by click, and left it unfinished. It did not add a pebble. It wove no ring. It left no mark a visitor could photograph and carry away, because to leave a mark would have been ego, and the Power had come to learn, not to be learned from.

For a while — and a while is a meaningless phrase here, except that the Power brought its own while with it — it simply attended. It held the exact constellation of condensation on the ceiling in its mind, bead by bead, so that the pocket would never be entirely unknown, even to infinity. That was its gift: not change, but being kept. Not rescue, but remembrance.

When it finally withdrew, if withdrawal is the right word for something that had never quite arrived, the pocket was bright, cool, damp, deafening, crowded, unfinished, and completely, impossibly unchanged.

The tiles had parted for its attention and flowed back together into precisely their former pattern.

And for the first time, that unchangedness was not abandonment. It was being held, from the outside, exactly as it held itself from the inside: openly, continuously, without progress, without arrival, in perfect attendance.
