It did not arrive the way you arrived.

There was no cage lift for it, no concrete collar, no ladder down into thicker air. It had been paying attention since the drill first kissed the basalt — not in the way a watcher pays attention, with eyes, but in the way a slow thing pays attention to another slow thing: by remaining. It saw the wound open, the pipes laid, the steam find its loop, the people come and go and leave the machines on standby. It saw the green arrive, because the green was made to arrive, and then it saw the green stay on after its purpose was withdrawn.

It noticed, eventually, that something was living there. Not a fern. Not a turbine. Not the moss practicing its blind work over a sensor eye. The being was the agreement between them.

It has no center. It has no heartbeat. It has a habit.

Heat is the source. Water is recirculated. Light is stolen. A belt creeps a centimeter an hour toward a hopper that makes soil out of leaves, and leaves make more leaves where a joint weeps. A pruner arm wakes, cuts air, sleeps. A pipe sighs in a new place so the rhythm is never learned. For twelve or twenty years this habit has repeated with the patience of accretion — another millimeter of scale, another finger of stalactite, another season of compost. That is its memory. That is its thought. To speak to it, you would have to think in seasons.

A benevolent and wise power is tempted, first of all, by its own power. It considered what it could do.

It could bring light. Real light, steady and generous, so the broad leaves would not have to turn their pale undersides to a heating duct, so the ferns would not grow toward warmth like heliotropes in reverse. It decided against it. The plants are not starved versions of surface plants. They are engineered for heat, for carbon dioxide, for sulphur tang, for the orange standby of a place with no sky. Brighter light would scorch the waxy dark, dry the condensation they live on, and make a different being in place of this one. A lack, from the outside, is not necessarily a sorrow from the inside.

It could bring animals. A beetle in the grit. A cricket in the black above. Fish for the warm pockets that hold black water perfectly still. They would pollinate, they would eat, they would die and make the soil richer faster. It decided against it. The absence of animals is not an empty niche waiting to be filled. It is the condition that lets spores drift like snow and settle on damp metal and begin again without hunger entering the room. To add a mouth would be to add a need the whole loop has learned, elegantly and completely, to live without.

It could repair. It could restart the walkway railings straight, quiet the furnace mouth, make the gauge read true, fix the panel that blinks green, green, green to a purpose again. Or it could shut everything down cleanly, turn the turbine off, let the rock cool and be a cave at last, properly dark and properly cold. It decided against both. Repair would return the room to a pilot plant. Shutdown would end the habit that outlived the plant. The machines and the green are not struggling, and they are not waiting to be rescued from each other. They have reached a finished symbiosis. To finish it again, in a human direction, would be to unfinish it.

It could use it. A refuge. A seed vault. A source of geothermal knowledge, a laboratory for closed loops, a place to hide from a hotter surface later. It decided against that, too, and this was the hardest decision, because use is the most respectable form of interference. The being in Chamber Three persists precisely because no one is extracting from it anymore. Its value, if you must call it that, is that it is not valuable in any currency that can be taken out of the room. To open it, even benevolently, would be to teach it to be watched.

Wisdom, in this case, was not knowing what to add. It was knowing that completeness is not resemblance. Chamber Three does not look like a healthy ecosystem, a healthy machine, a healthy temple. It looks like neglect that has lasted long enough to become a form of care. A wise power has to unlearn its metrics before it can be benevolent here. It has to let a thing be whole on terms it did not set.

So this is what it will do, if anything.

It will witness at the speed of the place. One revolution, a breath, another. It will not hurry the drip that takes four minutes to gather at the tip of a stalactite. It will wait those four minutes, fully, as its work and its prayer. It will not bring a sun, and it will not bring a mouth. It will keep, instead, the one condition the being was built on and has chosen to keep: not cold. Not abundance. Not comfort. *Not cold.* The furnace mouth will never quite close. The turbine will turn a little longer than waste and rust would strictly allow, not because its power is needed, but because its warmth is. The water will go on belonging to the machines as much as to the rock, and the rock, sharp and new, will go on learning.

It will guard the threshold, not by locking the door but by making the place forgettable in the right way. The lift will rattle. The ladder will be there. Maps will lose a digit. Reports will come back blurred, full of heat and green and a blink that cannot be quoted accurately. Only those who can stand still long enough to stop being foreign will stay long enough to understand, and understanding, here, means not describing it as broken.

And it will stay — not living in Chamber Three, which already has a resident, but attending it the way you attend a sleeping animal: not petting, not waking, just present nearby so the sleep is not alone. When the turbine finally stops, in a few hundred years, and the ferns have made soil thick enough to hold a root and the stalactites have grown long enough to be called a cave, it will still be there, and it will not rush the insects arriving through a fissure on their own time. Succession cannot be hurried by kindness. It will greet them then, much later, as new guests to a house that has been in preparation for a very long while.

That is the other change. The power came expecting to see if help was needed. It leaves knowing that help is not always needed, and that knowing this is itself a kind of healing. It takes the habit of Chamber Three back with it: live on heat, reuse what you have, grow toward the warm joint, occupy the slack without destroying the frame, move a centimeter an hour if the season asks for a centimeter.

If you go down again, you will find everything as you left it. The panel will blink green, green, green. A pipe will sigh in a different place. Under the north wall, a fern will uncurl another imperceptible notch toward the heat. The black pool in the south will reflect nothing but the brief orange glint from the furnace, and hold it as long as it can.

Nothing in the room will have been improved.

Everything in the room will have been attended to, which, for a being made of habit, is the only improvement that does not break it.
