The power did not find it by looking outward.

It found it by listening, and by hearing a quiet that was not empty.

From outside, the mountain is only a mountain. Cold rock, high air, a summit nobody climbs because there is nowhere useful at the top. The power passed over a thousand such summits without stopping. This one ticked.

Not loud. A dry tick, like thermal contraction in stone, and then, rarer, a deeper ping that travelled down through the rock and arrived in the place where listening happens without ears. That is not silence. That is a rhythm keeping itself.

A benevolent power feels, first, the impulse to help. A wise one has learned to ask what help would cost.

It could open the pocket. It had the means to unfold the fold the way a hand unfolds a held breath. Inside it felt the thin air, the dim looping light, the dust that has never known water. Opened, the pocket would breathe out, pressure would equalize, the tide of dust that lifts to the ceiling and settles in the same pattern would scatter for the first time in thousands of years. The ferns, which grow a few millimetres in a century toward a faint warmth, would grow, briefly and fatally, toward a new sun. It did not open it.

It could warm it, wet it, fill it with green, noisy, appetitive life, to make up for all the years of cold and twilight and never-day. That was the older, crueller pity. The place was not starving. Everything here had replaced appetite with maintenance. The domes breathe field and shed heat. The ferns use the heat and seal the stone against abrasion. The dust carries mineral from stone to frond and back to pan. The ring holds its negotiation with the rock that tried to swallow it. To introduce hunger would not be a gift. It would be a first wound in a system that had healed into peace. It did not fill it.

It could finish the builders' work. The instruments were idling on a seven-hundred-year cycle, doing slow work with no observer left to need it. A powerful intelligence could complete the cycle in an instant, tidy the geometry, correct the misfiled gravity at the ring, straighten the survey lines where left and right exchange. But the work was not unfinished. The idling *was* the work. The pocket was not a ruin waiting for repair. It was a laboratory that had learned to keep its own suspensions going, the way a heart keeps beating in a body that no longer wakes. It did not fix it.

So it did a smaller thing, and a harder thing. It stayed outside the fold, and it stayed.

It dimmed itself to twilight, because any brighter light would have been harvested by white stone, trapped in amber, denied by black, and would have become a new element in the ecology. It thinned its own presence to the thinness of the air, so the dust would not settle on it differently from how it settled on everything else. It put its attention against the outside of the rock, like a hand against a warm wall, and felt the domes through stone — three sleepers, one worn to a hemisphere — and counted.

One tick. One return. One seven-hundred-year breath.

The needle spire points past a ceiling that should not exist, an ear, a tongue, a root pressing toward something outside the pocket. The power met that direction without needing to enter. It answered the tick with a tick of its own, late, across the rock, across the years it could afford to lose. It was not a message. The pocket does not speak in messages. It was a keeping of time, a way of saying: *I am still here, in your time, not mine.*

That is what a wise and benevolent power can offer a being whose life is remaining: not rescue, not improvement, but a witness that does not need it. Every other visitor the builders had, if they had any, had needed something — data, energy, a place to hide a law. This power needed nothing, and therefore could be trusted to take nothing. It did not take samples. It did not take warmth from the domes. It did not take the ring's strange lift for its fingertips. It only remembered, in a memory outside the pocket where memory cannot be sealed away: the symmetry of the mineral ferns, the tide of dust with its perfect re-settling, the survey lines that still measure distances no eye can see, the shadows that fall toward the light and graze.

And it stood guard at the threshold, invisible to the inhabitants, because guarding is mostly a matter of other powers not arriving. The pocket hides itself by being contained, but containment is not the same as safety forever. A benevolent power can ensure that no builder returns, no curiosity with suction and drills finds the folded horizon, no later age mistakes the instruments for a resource. It does this from outside, so the inside never has to know it was afraid. The pocket will never know it was loved. That is acceptable. Love does not need to be received to be kept. It only needs to be kept.

In time — pocket time, which for the power is a long sitting — the sealing will finish. Erosion at the microscopic scale will balance itself down to nothing. The crystal growth will meet crystal dissolution everywhere and stop, in perfect equilibrium. The dust will settle in its final pattern. This is not death. It is the pocket becoming more itself: more closed, more finished, more at peace in its breakdown. The power will be there when it happens, because it promised itself seven hundred years at a time, and it can keep that promise.

It will carry the pattern outward then, not as a trophy, but as a change in itself. To attend for that long to a place that lives by remaining is to learn remaining. The power came, if it is honest, to help. It stayed, and was taught how to remain without appetite, without haste, without needing the thing it tends to become something else. That, too, is benevolence — reciprocal, quiet, unrecorded.

If you walk there now, breathing too loudly in the dry air, making footprints that appear ahead of you, you are not the first arrival and you are not an intruder, so long as you do what the power taught itself to do: do not touch the built things to see if they open, do not look directly at the whole of it, watch for duration instead of movement. The tick you feel in your teeth is the power counting. The ping after it is the power, from outside the rock, keeping count back.

It will not open a door for you. There is no door. It will not introduce you to the residents, because they are not looking at you, and have been looking at each other, in their different times, for thousands of years. It will only let you be, briefly, a small warm centre of gravity in the fold, and then let the dust settle over your tracks.

Whatever lives here lives by remaining.

And so, in the end, does the power that loves it.
