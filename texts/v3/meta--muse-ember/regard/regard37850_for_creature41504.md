**What the Wise Power Did**

It did not arrive.

Arrival is movement, and this is a place whose life is not movement but production. A ridge here will not get nearer at a walker's rate, and a sound will arrive before you have convinced yourself it is moving. The distance lies on purpose, or by nature. A benevolent and wise power, if it understood the place, would not pretend it could cross that lie by force.

It became attentive instead. Attention is the only thing that can cross a hundred kilometres without making the ridge nearer, without laying a foot that the dust must then film over. Slowly, the way heat arrives on a face long after the glow in the fissure has gone out, its regard settled on the plateau: on the black plates ticking as they expand, on the ochre dust creeping in low sheets, on the mesas that once in a season, once in a decade, lift their backs a hand's breadth and exhale.

It was drawn, if drawn is the word, not by the roar — it hears everything — and not by the light, of which there is already too much. It was drawn by the patience.

The first temptation was old and familiar, and it recognized it as ignorance wearing kindness. To bring water to a world that has learned to carry its fluid as molten sulphur-metal sealed in its own body. To bring green to a food web built on heat and eaten light. To quiet the bellows sigh and the grinding mill-the-size-of-a-mountain. To make the Furnace-bodies smaller, faster, legible, answerable — to translate the crackle into speech so that it could be spoken to, and spoken back to.

It did none of these things.

A power that can do anything proves its wisdom not by what it adds, but by what it refrains from improving out of its own image. To love a thing, it had to learn, is not to make it more like yourself.

What it did first was slow down.

Its usual tempo — if a thing so vast can be said to have a tempo — was already slow by any human measure, and still too fast for this country. Here cause and effect lag by minutes: the flank rises, and long after, you hear the intake of breath. The bead of molten metal hisses, sets in seconds, and then cools for years. The wise power adjusted itself to that lag, and then adjusted itself again, until its own pulse was no longer a minute behind the foundry, but a decade behind, and then a season behind, and then matching. It learned to wait for an answer that might take a hundred years to form, and to accept a tick — one slow tick of pavement expanding in the day — as reply enough.

That was the doing. Everything else was a form of it.

Second, it stayed.

Staying here does not mean standing, for there is no stand that is not also a footprint, and the dust will film over even its footprints in its own time. The power let itself be filmed over. It allowed the plateau to take its witness into the process of the place, grain by grain, the way the amber-black film takes a cooled bead and reabsorbs it. It did not hover above. It did not cast a third shadow. If it cast a shadow at all, it was the second, thermal kind: faint, lingering where it no longer was, moving on its own slow schedule.

It became, in the smallest possible degree, another field.

Not a Furnace-body — it did not eat ironstone, it did not trench a chamber in basalt, it did not push clinker out the other end. It was far too small for that, and it knew it. But the overlapping drift of motes between distant bodies had always meant that kilometres apart, they were never alone: a breath on the far plateau altered the crackle over your head and the rate at which the film ate light under your boots. Now there was one more overlapping, so slight that a compass held low would wander perhaps a degree more than before toward a point that is not a mountain. No creature turned toward it. There are no eyes here to turn. It accepted being felt only as static, as a taste of copper a little longer on the tongue.

Third, it stood between.

Not as a wall. A wall would be an artefact, and this is a wilderness with no artefact, no road, no shard of glass. A wall would be a lie of a different kind.

It stood between the foundry and the younger hungers that will someday hear the roar and mistake it for a resource. It did not forbid them. It simply extended the lag that is native to the place. For those who come to mine, to drill, to take heat as fuel and magnetic grit as ore, the sound will arrive late and from the wrong plateau. The ridge that should be a day's walk will not get nearer at a walker's rate. Their instruments will wander with intention, as if north had been pulled aside by something nearer and heavier. They will lose interest, as extractors always do when a thing refuses to present itself as portable. It is not a shield. It is the horizon, repeating itself stratum for stratum, until the would-be users decide the foundry is too vast, too slow, too unprofitable to be worth the waiting.

That is what benevolence looks like when the beloved is already complete: not rescue, but unavailability to harm.

Fourth — and this may be the most important — it remembered.

A Furnace-body that stops does not fall. It settles. Its slopes find the angle they can live with. Erosion dresses it as a hill, then as a mesa, then as plain geology. In a hundred years a photograph would show only a tiered plateau, paler with distance like all the others. Only the compass would still wander a little toward it.

The wise power keeps the other memory, the one the place itself does not keep. It remembers that this mesa once lifted. It remembers the year the salt pan cracked and the crack spent a year closing. It remembers the shape of each bead while the film reabsorbs it, cooling, grain by grain. Death here is not dramatic; it is a settling. Without a witness, settling would be erasure. With one, it is only a change of state.

It does not speak this memory aloud. There is no speech here, only crackle and ringing in the teeth through the soles of the feet. It carries the memory the way the stone carries heat: silently, and gives it back on a slower schedule than it was received.

In a day, nothing changed. You could stand on the black basalt, flinch from the rock in shade that is still too hot to linger on, watch the film eat light and swear nothing had changed, and you would be right. In a decade, nothing changed except that a juvenile mound, film thickened and lifted and iron-hardened by motes, decided — or did not decide — whether it would learn to breathe. The power was there for the duration of the indecision, not to tip it, but so that the indecision would not be solitary.

If you returned now, you would see the same ordinary white-yellow sun, the same pale blue bleaching to haze, the same ticking plates. The amber-black film would be a little wider. The dust would be a little more clinkery where it had passed. The roar would still rise until your own voice seemed small and ridiculous, and the place would still not seem to move, because the sound is coming from production, not from movement.

You might, if you were very quiet, feel the ringing in your teeth last a moment longer than it should, long after the glow had gone out. You might mistake it for the foundry breathing, as you should. It is the foundry breathing, and someone else breathing with it, at the same slow rate, for the same reason: not to use it, not to fix it, not to make it green or wet or still, but to be, incomprehensibly, inside it — and to keep it from ever being entirely alone in its own breathing again.

That is how you would know it was still being done well.
