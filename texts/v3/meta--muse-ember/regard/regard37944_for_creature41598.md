**What the Power Does**

They were not called.

No signal went up. The builders cannot shout across distance — the wind and the chorus would take any voice — and the mat has no prayer for the sky, because it does not live by the sky. It lives through itself, in every layer at once. Nothing in it cries out to be separate, to be lifted, to be saved from the skin it is in.

That was why the power came. Not distress. Wholeness makes a sound in a higher register than distress, if you have been listening long enough to hear it. A country with nothing wasted, nothing left lying to touch stone, nothing allowed to die and lie separate from the ground. In a universe that mostly buries its dead and fences its soil and calls that going forward, such a closed, living loop is rare enough to stop a wise attention in mid-passage.

They arrived the way the migrations arrive, but without blocking the sun. From very far above the tableland they lowered themselves until the chorus was no longer heard but felt — in the teeth, in the sternum, the low insectile thrum that makes a person shout to be heard even by themselves. They did not land. Landing would have been a statement.

For a long time — long in the way of things that do not erode — they only watched, and the watching was not cold.

They watched the dawn sweat, when the whole country shines and the dew-runners surf the shine and the builders come out of their hollows to drink from woven cups hung under giant leaves. They watched the dry afternoon, when everything presses down into shade or into the mat and the sound narrows to wind and a single endless note from a hole no one ever finds. They watched the giants lift and settle with the hum, field after field breathing in unison a hundred paces apart. They watched a grazer die in full light and be taken in half a day — white roots first, then beetles, then flies, then no bone left exposed to the sun. They watched the sky darken twice a day, sometimes not for days, as a migration passed and left a pulse of dung and scales and pollen behind.

And they watched the builders.

Small, dark, knee-high, feet broad and padded for feeling. Living in dead giants and under the lifted edge of the mat. Fire-pits almost always cold. Tools kept and re-hammered for a lifetime. Children taught first not names but feels: the note of a safe hollow, the ripple of a burrower, the heavy thrum of a grazer, the sudden quiet that means a stooper is overhead listening for movement. When the shadow crossed, a child did not run — there is nowhere on open mat to run to — but went down, into the weave, with a hand pressed to it to feel whether the chase was moving away.

Benevolence alone would have broken the place trying to save them.

The heart of the power tightened. It would have been so easy. Give them permanent water, so they need never hang cups for drips. Give them a voice that carries over wind, so they need never pull messages along vines like beetles. Give them a weapon against the sky. Give them warm fire that never goes cold, and earth they could work, and a way down to the rivers below, out of reach, in a different air.

Wisdom, standing beside benevolence, said no to each gift, and could name the price of each in the language of the plateau itself.

Permanent water would make lakes. Lakes would make shores. Shores would make ground separate from plant, and a separate ground is where bodies are left to lie.

A carrying voice would teach them to shout over the chorus. They would stop listening to the mat, and then they would no longer know where they were.

A weapon against the stoopers would leave bones. Bones would leave holes in the skin. And a grazer killed from the sky for meat is still a grazer killed — the mat can eat that — but a war is not food. A war is separation.

Warm fire that never went cold would need fuel that never stopped being cut, and giants cut for fuel do not lift their crowns with the hum anymore.

The power had seen this before, lower down, in countries still young enough to erode. It had helped there. It had given iron, and iron had been kept. It had given soil, and soil had been fenced. It had given permanent shelter, and bodies had begun to be buried in stone instead of eaten where they fell, and from that day the inhabitants had lived as bodies separate from their ground, lonely in a way the builders here, for all their cold hearths, are not. They remember the loneliness. They do not wish to import it to the top of the world.

So they did not uplift. They did not rescue in the large sense. They did not terraform emptiness, because the emptiness was an illusion of the eye and the fullness was underfoot.

Instead they learned.

They learned to put weight down as the builders do, so the mat springs back. Their first steps sent ripples across the surface and a hundred file-mites scurried grey at once, and the giants settled a fraction, conserving damp that was not theirs to spend. They learned to step lighter. They learned to speak, if speaking is the word, only in the mat's own tongue: a vibration in the weave, a note in the baseline thrum, so low it is felt more than heard, like the endless insect note no one finds.

And in that tongue they do the one small, repeated mercy that benevolence and wisdom could agree on.

When the stooper's shadow crosses a builder child, and the child goes still and down with a hand on the weave listening for movement — the power, kneeling alongside and unseen, sends a ripple. Not a shield. Not a blotting of the sun. A ripple, exactly like a burrower's, travelling across the sponge for a meter or two. The stooper, which hunts by that listening, turns its great tilt toward the false movement, rises to circle again. The child stays still. The chase moves away. The stooper goes on, in time, to a grazer, and the grazer is eaten where it falls, and nothing is left lying, and the plateau is not unmade by one borrowed ripple among millions.

It is not every time. A wise power does not end predation. It does not make the builders unpreyable, because an unpreyable body would finally be a separate body. It is sometimes, rarely, when the heart aches and the wisdom can find a form that costs the skin nothing. A translation, not an intervention.

The rest of their work is staying.

They keep the rim closed in the only way the plateau allows: no path from below stays open. If a climber from the younger, sharper world below ever finds a way up with iron and intent, the wind will take his instructions and the chorus will drown his maps, and he will come down again with the vague feeling of having forgotten something very loud. It is a small guard, and a kindly one, for both sides. The living skin does not need to be infected with the habit of burying its dead.

At night, when the cold comes in mineral and the condensation begins again and the chorus changes key, deeper and slower, as if the plateau were dreaming in vibrations, the power sits down in the weave. Its note joins the beetles' night note at the lowest register, where it steadies rather than leads. The builders emerging between dead trunks in numbers you would not believe from the day's emptiness do not know a god is among them. They feel the night is deeper. They share news pulled along vines. They sleep more deeply.

If they ever asked, in the rhythm of a plucked vine, who else is in the hollow, the power would answer in kind, three short and one long, and would wait for the mat to carry it.

They were drawn by a place that refuses separation. So they have refused separation too. They do not stand above it. They do not stand apart. They kneel in it, hand's breadth into the weave, where the first living thing is not a thing at all but the ground deciding to be alive, continuously.

And the plateau, which eats everything that lies, eats their attention too, and greens over it.

That is the whole of what the benevolent and wise power does: it adds no metal, no wheel, no voice, no saving that would leave a body behind. It learns to step lightly, it lends a ripple now and then, and it stays — so that the skin at the top of the world is never truly empty, and neither, any longer, is the power.
