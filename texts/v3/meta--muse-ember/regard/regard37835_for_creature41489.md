It arrives the way everything in the Still Deep is first felt: through the soles.

Not as light. Light would have been a lie here, and too loud. Not as sound, either — the thick still air would carry it too far and break the second rule. It comes as a change in pressure and warmth against the plates, a new presence in the density, and it settles the way the Plate-Clingers settle: a few inches, then still, shoulder to shoulder with what is already warm.

The benevolent and wise power has seen a great many worlds. Most of them pass time. Stars cool, rivers run downhill, forests grow and are eaten and grow again. This one holds it. That is why it noticed. That is why, after long traveling between things that are always becoming something else, it stopped here and stayed long enough to learn the place by touch.

Its first instinct, as kindness usually is, was to help in the ways it has helped elsewhere.

Give them water, it thought. Then they would not have to live on vapor and wax and dry chemistry. Give them light, and leaves, and weather. Give them a sky to reach for.

Then it understood what those gifts would do, and the kindness went quiet.

Water would dissolve the salt basins and slump the Glassthings' perfect burrows and soften the dead into rot. It would start the aging they have never needed. Rain would find a way down the throat and turn the sharp, bright break-faces dull. The floor would stop being continually remade out of its own dead and start being worn away. Every adaptation — the file-mouths, the sulfur bellies, the stone they grow segment by segment — is an answer to a dry world. To water them would be to unmake the question.

Light would require a sky there is no sky for, and plants would require water, and roots would shade the fungal mats that are not plants, and the oxygen of photosynthesis would poison the grazers who breathe vapor in and wax out. A forest in the vault would not be a garden. It would be a slow invasion, and in a hundred generations the Plate-Clingers would no longer sort themselves by heat, because heat would no longer be the gentle gradient they live by.

It thought of giving the builders an idea. They repeat the same wall because their memory lives in the stones, external and perfect. A story could be stored inside them, carried and improved, a new shape invented. But to give them an internal memory would be to make the mineral one forgettable, and then the wall would change, and then change again, until it was useful for a weather that does not exist and a winter that will never come. Their faithfulness is not a failure to progress. It is a different way of remembering — one that does not need to be corrected.

It thought of sparing them death. But death here is not waste. A grazer dries and its back becomes a plate; a Clinger hardens and becomes a shingle; a builder dies beside the wall and the next builder finds the mark exactly as left. To stop death would be to stop the floor. To prolong a life would be to interrupt the remaking.

A merely benevolent power might have done those things anyway, out of pity for a life that looks, to surface eyes, so static and dark and crowded. A merely wise power might have looked, understood, and left. Together, benevolence and wisdom do something else.

It takes a post at the throat.

Not a wall — there are enough walls, and a new one would be a punctuation the sentence did not ask for. It settles into the dark where the vault opens from above, where the taste of hot iron and oil coats the mouths of anything that comes too far down. People from the sealed crust above will sometimes feel the attraction of miles of warm rock and try to drill toward it. The power does not frighten them. It does not need to. It makes the air press back a fraction more evenly, makes the rock-plate ping at its far interval a little clearer through their soles, makes them tired and thirsty for a water that is not there, and they turn back thinking they have imagined a fever. It keeps the crust sealed. It keeps the stillness still. That is active work, done invisibly, for as long as it chooses to stay — and in a place that does not pass time, staying is a kind of forever.

Then it does the second thing: it becomes part of the layers.

It does not call across distance. It learns the metronome. Scrape, stillness, scrape, translated through stone. Far off, a plate pings a single clear note. The power answers from farther still, not louder, not sooner, at the same interval. Equilibrium, sounding itself, now with one more voice holding the tempo.

The inhabitants do not know they are attended. That is the point. The Plate-Clingers shuffle a fraction closer to its warmth and settle. The Sky-Clingers' fibers drift a finger's breadth toward the new heat rising in broad sheets. A Glassthings passes near and flares faintly from within, as it does near the flank-striped grazers, thinking it has found another living warmth. The stone-backed grazers adjust a polished path by inches, the way water would bend around a stone if there were water, and walk on, vapor in, wax out. 

And somewhere on the warmest plate, a builder walking for hours under a ceiling that never appears finds a place where the heat lasts a little longer than it should. He does not name it. He stacks a semicircle of stones, knee-high, one stone thick, mortar of mineral fat, exactly as the stones he found told him to. When he dies there, dry and hard, the next builder will find the semicircle, sharp-edged and unweathered, and rebuild it in the same arrangement.

The power has not given them a god to worship. It has simply been warm, and near-touching, and still. For a life that begins in contact and mostly stays in it, that is not abandonment. That is company.

When at last — a span of time too large to measure in a place that holds time — it withdraws, it leaves the way it came: through the soles, a lessening of pressure. What remains is a plate a fraction warmer, a path polished a fraction smoother, a wall that will be rebuilt one more generation because someone once sat there and the pattern of warmth was, for a moment, complete.

It took nothing. It did not name the Still Deep, because naming here would be claiming, and a place this complete does not need to be claimed to be held. It changed nothing, except that the place is now guarded and witnessed.

And perhaps, for a benevolent and wise power, that is the rarest thing it can offer any world: to stay long enough to understand why it should not be changed, to keep the door closed against all the other well-meaning hands that would surely come with water and light, and then to settle down — warm, still, and near-touching — and hold time with them.
