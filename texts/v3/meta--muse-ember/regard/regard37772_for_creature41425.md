**It Does Not Enter**

It noticed the Sinterhouse the way stillness notices a held breath — not with hunger, and not with a plan to improve it.

That is no small restraint for a power that could improve almost anything. It has, before, brought water where there was none. It has laid soil over rock that was smelting its own food, and made the smelters ordinary and dependent and grateful. It has given names to things that were still assembling themselves, and watched them stop becoming and start being called. It knows what its help usually costs. So it stops on the high, empty ridge, a long way from the slit, and does not go in.

It does not bring rain. Rain would boil as it fell. It would scald the ruby resin, weigh down the waxy leaves, turn the mineral vapor into a steam that no pore here is made to breathe. The thickets have never known water, and that is not a lack. It is a way of living that has already answered the question water answers elsewhere. To give them water now would not be kindness. It would be a translation into another language, done at the cost of the one they are speaking.

It does not bring animals. There are no pollinators because the flowers are not waiting for tongues or wings. They open like furnace doors and shed sparks, and cross-pollination is electrocution — quiet, precise, impersonal. It is not lonely. It is sufficient. Animals would come here only as tourists, or as corpses that the heat would have to take back into slag. The power does not mistake absence for emptiness.

It does not bring soil. The roots go sideways after heat, braiding around hot veins, driving salts upward in a bellows-cycle so slow you could watch all day and see nothing. They are making food out of rock that has never been soil. To hand them soil would be to make them ours.

It does not name them. A name would finish them early. It does not build a door, or a wall, or a path for keepers. There is no door because this is not yet a house. It is a room geology has only just stubbed out, and the floor is still deciding to forget footprints. The power leaves that decision to the floor.

What it does is take its place not in the pocket, but in the emptiness around it.

It keeps the emptiness empty. It turns back, gently and without spectacle, those who would arrive with buckets for the resin that never dries, with jars for seeds older than their parents, with notebooks that want to close the account. Not because they are evil, but because to take a piece of this place out would be to take it out of its time, and its time has not yet agreed to be carried.

It learns the tempo. It counts the days of a root swelling that never seems to swell. It waits beside a leaf that is half-formed one week and half-formed the next, and does not hurry it into completeness. It listens for the small metallic ping of a joint venting, and holds the air still afterward so the hiss can travel whole across the mineral floor. It does not answer the spark when it arcs from island to island. It does not flinch. Some things are only loved by being allowed to happen without a witness flinching.

When the floor tries to finish — when fresh glass lips over a crack, when new crystal pushes up through old crystal and thickens toward a final skin — it keeps a hairline of unfinishedness open through the middle. Not forever. Only for as long as becoming needs a place to remain unclosed. It does not set the floor. It shelters the setting.

It leaves the light ordinary. The sun that finds the slit remains white-gold. Shadows are the length you expect. A lesser power would have made it glow, or dim, or mark it holy, or alien. This one refuses to make it a sign. The ordinariness is part of the gift: in a place that is breaking every other rule, the light will not break that one for display.

And it remembers it unfinished. When it thinks of the Sinterhouse elsewhere, in the company of other vast and quiet things, it does not summarize it. It does not say: three islands and a fourth upside-down, plants that are factories, garden that is a house. It remembers the pulse before the summary. It remembers direction arriving back from the west, uphill being downhill, leaves interleaving where they should not touch. To remember it finished would be a small betrayal, repeated every time it told the story.

Its own footprint, if it steps close enough to the slit, fills in like anyone else's. The hot stone decides to forget it, slowly, like cooling concrete. It does not insist on leaving a mark. Benevolence, here, is not intervention. It is accompaniment without appropriation — attention sustained so long that attention itself becomes shelter.

It will stay for as long as staying is needed. Not overseeing. Not directing. Sitting in the heat and the thin air, learning to hear the echo that arrives a fraction before the sound finishes leaving a mouth, because in a room still deciding its rules, even sound is allowed to be early.

And when, in one long breath of the pocket’s own reckoning, the islands spread or fold or become something else entirely, it will not grieve the form it saw. It never loved the form. It loved that it had not finished.

It does not enter. It does not need to.
