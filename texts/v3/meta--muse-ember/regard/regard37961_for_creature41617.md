It would not arrive.

There is no descent, no brightening of the mist, no sound louder than the boom when a drip finds the hollow stone. Attention, for such a power, is not a visit. It is a noticing that happens at its own scale, the way you noticed the lichen disc: not because it signalled, but because it was complete at a volume most things pass by. It has seen louder places. It has seen young places, and places cracking open, and places filling up with making — paths, cairns, fires, names. It passes over this hollow often in the course of other business, and for a long time it did not stop, because there was nothing in it that asked to be stopped for.

What stops it, finally, is that nothing asks.

That is not a lack the power reads as a need. It reads it as a settlement. High and small. Lush and not dense. Loud, and then silent for longer than any loudness should allow. A place that has finished carving itself and now maintains, drip by drip, the shape it carved. A film travelling down a groove. A disc widening past measurement. One amphibian in one bowl, shifting once an hour toward the warm side. One beetle that may spend a season crossing its hummock. A fern that will open its next frond in another slow season. You, standing on the highest stone with your hair damp, being not so much a resident as a brief passage through — foot inside the rim, and then, in time, foot outside it again.

A benevolent power without wisdom would know what to do at once, and would ruin it.

It would connect the pools. The pools do not flow into one another, and from a distance that looks like loneliness, like a failure of plumbing. Kindness, in that register, would be a channel cut in stone, a little trench to let the amphibian meet another amphibian, to let nutrients travel, to make a proper pond with a proper shore. The power is wise enough to hear what that would mean in the hollow's own time: a single drip's worth of change, repeated, would bring in time not one new companion but a whole economy of competitors and predators and diseases, all of them faster than anything here has ever had to be. The amphibian did not lose its eyes. It settled, over a span the hollow measures differently from you, into a life in which eyes are an expense and skin is a lung and a mouth and a way of drinking the air. To give it sight would not be a gift. It would be a new way to be wrong for the bowl it lives in.

It would speed the beetle, and unfurl the fern, and make the disc spread into a carpet, because slowness looks like suffering when you are used to measures of years. It would fill the ten or twenty paces of bare warm rock between plants with more plants, because lushness at low density looks like emptiness. It would build, or leave a built thing, for you: a shelter from the weeping air, a path so you would not soften the soil with footprints, a marker so you would not be lost on the way down through cloud that is lower and warmer than the air you came through.

It would, in short, improve the place out of existence, and do it all from love.

Wisdom without benevolence would have done the opposite. It would have catalogued it. It would have taken the film, the disc, the moss-ridge, the eyeless animal, the hair on the frond, the unmatchable footprint beside the basin, and given each a name that could be carried away, and put the hollow on a list of places that matter — which is to say, places that will be returned to, by others, with tools, with flasks, with a reasonable desire to understand the circulation in a leaf and the note a hollow stone makes when struck. It would have left you with a message, because a message is what a wise thing gives a brief being who has stood still long enough to listen.

Together, benevolence and wisdom do something quieter and harder. They protect the conditions, and refuse to improve the inhabitants.

So the power does not enter the hollow.

It stays where it already is, which is to say it does not come near. Beyond the rim, in the warmer cloud below, at a distance so great that even its attention arrives spaced out, like the residents themselves — one noticing in a very long time, then nothing. It becomes, in its own vastness, another solitary presence. It does not crowd. If it had a posture in this place it would be the posture of the trees: leaning inward, listening, not reaching across the bare stone to touch what it leans toward.

Its first act is to look once, properly, and then to look away for long intervals. This is not indifference. Unbroken attention, from a power of that size, would be a climate. The hollow already knows what it is to be attended to by a single call from beyond the rim — how the whole concave rock holds it, amplifies it, makes the caller seem overhead when it is nowhere near. The power will not be that call. It will check the way a drip checks: occasionally, at a rate the place can absorb, and leave the long silences in which a frond can shiver and settle, in which an animal can move once and be still again, in which your own swallow can be heard.

Its second act is to keep the place unfindable in the only way that does not wound it: by keeping it unnamed. The power is quite capable of interfering with seekers — of thinning the air in the wrong direction, of making instruments misread, of letting maps lose a wrinkle of high ground, of letting the exact route up through thinning, warm air forget itself in the minds of those who might come with a reason to stay too long. It does not need to do any of this loudly, now. It only needs to maintain the mist at the edges, and the fact that there is nothing here to take that could be taken — no mineral worth the climb, no specimen that is not also a whole life in a bowl or on a hummock, no view that pays for the disturbance of reaching it. Places that are not named are not defended by walls. They are defended by the absence of a reason to break in. The power preserves that absence.

Its third act concerns you, and it is mostly a refusal to act.

It sees that you came empty-handed. No cut mark, no fibre, no ash. You did not try the stone to see if it would flake, though it would. You did not build a cairn on the highest stone to say you were there, though the whole world of the place was visible from it at once and it would have been the natural place to declare it. Your footprints are already softening, already filling with sky, already sinking back into the sponge a few centimetres down where a hidden reservoir sighs upward. In the hollow's time you are not a disturbance to be corrected. You are the passing thing, like the animal whose hair was caught, like the voice that sometimes enters and does not resume. The place knows how to receive a passing thing: it lets it through, and then it heals the line.

The power will not hurry that healing into an event, and it will not let you hurry either. It will not speak, because speech here would be amplified past usefulness, a new biggest sound, and because a message would make you a carrier of something built — information being the first thing ever built in a place that has managed, for longer than any maker would have had a reason to stay, to remain unbuilt. It will not test you, or reward you, or lift you out early out of concern that the quiet might thin you. It understands that you are staying a while because there is a while to stay in, and that leaving, when you leave, will be by the way you came, down into cloud, with your hair drying in air that is not wet, carrying nothing that can be unpacked and shown.

What it gives you, if giving is the word, is that return. You will come back to louder, faster, denser places — there is no way around that, you live in one — and for some time afterward you will walk a little more slowly between things. You will hear drips as notes. You will notice when a place is lush but not crowded, and you will not immediately want to fill the spaces. You will not be able to explain this as knowledge. The power does not implant explanations. It lets the hollow do what the hollow does to anything that stays long enough to be wet by it: it recalibrates the tempo. That is the place's benevolence, not the power's, and the power is wise enough not to claim credit for it.

And when you have gone — when your last footprint has sunk and the basin beside it is full of sky again — the power does not mark the spot. It does not make a collection of the beings it noticed. It does not need them as evidence against its own vastness. It simply renews the decision, drip by drip, in the only unit of action this place respects: let the film travel down its groove; let the disc arrive at its edge; let the moss go on digesting its tree into a ridge; let the rosette hold its pool; let the amphibian shift, once an hour, toward the warmth; let the beetle take its next step in a season or two; let the trees lean toward the centre, where the mist thins first, as if something important were coming from there.

Nothing important is coming from there. That is the point the power has learned, and it is why it stays. In its own elsewhere, it will now be a little slower to connect things, a little less eager to give eyes to what has learned to live without them, a little more willing to let a complete life be spaced far from every other complete life. It carries the hollow away with it in the only way a place like this can be carried without being diminished: as a standard of sufficiency that does not photograph well.

Inside the rim, enclosed room of a world, high and damp and lush and empty, nothing changes. The rock exhales in the day and drinks in the night. The leaf shows its water moving upward against the stem. Somewhere a drip finds a hollow chamber and booms, and a frond shivers, and an unseen animal moves once and is still again. The power, far off and alone in its expanse, listens the way the hollow listens — inward, for a very long time, without needing to answer — and the place goes on without it, at its own pace, weeping, maintaining the agreement it made with itself long before anyone wise enough to leave it alone had thought to notice.
