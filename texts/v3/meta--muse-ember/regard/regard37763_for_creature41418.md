It does not arrive.

Arriving is for things that have to cross a distance. This power has no distance to cross. It notices, the way attention notices a change in breathing in a room it has never entered. A place still becoming, a being still unmade, a cold light pressing through a ceiling that has not decided to be a ceiling — and the noticing settles over the pocket like another condensation.

It has noticed many things before. It is old in the way rivers are old, not in the way kings are old. It has watched worlds harden. It has watched them open too wide and spill their light and die of exposure. It has learned, slowly and at cost, the difference between helping a thing and finishing it.

That is why this place holds it.

Not because it is beautiful, though it is. Not because it is suffering, though the cold settles in knees and breath. But because here, for once, nothing has finished being what it is. The rock may set or un-set. The water may fall, slide, or rise. The spider may or may not grow eyes. The arc may become a doorway or may remain for a thousand pulses a rib that ends in air. Everything is in the future tense, and content to be.

A benevolent power could ruin that in a dozen kind ways.

It could make the ceiling solid and give the room a sky. It could teach condensation to fall straight down, reliably, like rain in a proper cave. It could warm the light. It could give the blind millipedes eyes, give the spider eyes that work, give the moss a true green and the threads roots. It could complete the cairn. It could finish the spiral with one more quarter-turn. It could stand the broken arc upright into a door and let the small things shelter under something decided.

It could pull the being out through the crack, back to a world with weight and warmth and names.

It considers each of these, because it can, and it sets each one down, because it knows what they would cost. A finished cave is a dead cave where this one lives. Water that always falls would drown the film-swimmers who live in water's indecision. Eyes given to creatures who have learned to live by gradient and cold would not be a gift; they would be a new way to be hurt by shadows that fall upward. A completed arch would tell the builder their work was only a rehearsal for someone else's ending. Pulling the being out before the being was ready to go would teach them that unfinished places are only traps to escape, and not places to be witnessed in.

So it does not fix. It keeps.

Keeping is not nothing. Keeping is the older, quieter mercy.

It puts its attention around the pocket the way you might cup a hand around a candle flame — not to hold it still, not to carry it elsewhere, but so the wind does not take it by accident. The crack between the growing stones stays crack-sized. Not sealed in panic, not pried open by curiosity. The world above will go on being snow, or bright plain, or whatever it is, and will not find a hole here by drilling or by luck. If a foot passes over the thin ceiling, it will feel only the usual cold. That is protection enough, and it is not a lie.

Inside, it learns the rhythm and does not hurry it. Condensation waxes, beads, wagers on a direction, thins. Crystals tick and pop and sometimes un-pop. The room breathes in cycles of minutes. The power sets itself to those minutes. It does not make the rain come sooner for the thirsty specks. It does not make it stop for the drowning ribbons. It does only this: it does not let the cycle stall into either drought or flood. The film is allowed to remain a film — enough to swim in, never enough to sink in. The rising drops are allowed to rise, and to be absorbed with a tick, and to begin elsewhere.

It watches the hunter that is mostly absence. It does not banish it. A place without a hunter would make the blind things soft in ways they cannot afford. It does not feed it either. It makes one small change, the sort a wise power can make without rewriting the world: the shadow still arrives before its owner, but for the being who lives here, it arrives a breath later than it used to. A breath is enough to step sideways. A breath is enough to know and to not be passed through. When the moss flickers white under that passing, it is allowed to flicker. When the cold deepens over the being's shoulders, it deepens, but not to the old depth. The being will feel it as weather, not as a hand.

It watches the reflections. It does not pull the reflection-hunter out of the puddle-mirrors, and it does not push it back in. To force a crossing would be to end the strangeness that holds the pocket together. To deny it watching would be to make it lonely in the way only reflections can be lonely. So the power acknowledges it, sideways, the way one acknowledges a creature in another room by leaving a door ajar. It may keep watching. It may keep lifting its head in the shallow mirrors when the real creatures look down. It is part of the bargain of this place: some things live on the floor, some things live in the decision of water, and both are real enough to be left alone.

And it watches the builder.

This is the one it understands most. The three stones half-sunk. The spiral still wet in the spongy wall. The arc that ends in mid-air. The power recognizes its own kind in that work — a being who has the future tense and does not know what to do with it yet, so it practices. The power does not add a stone. It does not complete the groove. It does something even smaller: it holds the floor's claiming at bay by a hair. The stones will sink and un-sink with the pulses. They will not be swallowed whole in the one interval when the builder is between bodies, between pulses, not yet returned. The shelter under the arc will remain a shelter, even though the arc was never meant to be a roof. Small things may keep gathering there, as if a promise were enough to keep the cold off. Here, it is.

For the being who slipped in, the power does not speak. Speech is a finished thing; it has edges and a handle. In this room, a spoken word would be too solid. It would fall and evaporate halfway, like the drops. Instead, it stays. Not inside — inside is already crowded with becoming — but at the threshold of the being's attention, where the crack is. When the being breathes and the breath fogs, the fog is not only theirs. When their footprints stay bright in the damp film, they stay a little longer than before, so that the being knows: I was here, and it was not erased at once. When the light rings faintly in their ears as the slugs cross the ceiling, the tone is less lonely for having a second listener.

The being will not see it. That is not a failure of the power's benevolence. It is the point. To be seen would be to become a resident, and the power does not live in places; it visits them the way memory visits a dream, without moving the furniture. The being will feel, perhaps, that the cold in their knees has company. That the pocket, for all its sparseness, is not empty of witness. They may think they imagined it. Imagining it will be sufficient. It will also be true enough.

If the being decides to leave, the power will not pull them and will not block them. The crack will be there when the condensation is at the right stage, a hand wide, cold air pressing through. They will not be hurried out. They will not be kept in by a ceiling that hardens in fear. And the power will stay with the place after they go, because places that are still becoming need someone who remembers them without naming them.

It holds a name for the pocket, unspoken, the way you hold a seed in a closed palm — not planting it, not showing it. *The almost room.* If it spoke it aloud, the room would have to become it. So it keeps the name unwritten, and lets the room go on being many things at once: blister, closet, cave-to-be, memory of a cave, nursery, archive of husks.

Nothing visible will change. That is how you will know it was wise. The crystals will still tick. The shrimp-things will still dart through nothing and not mind. The spider will still wait for the room to bring things to it. The shadow will still pool upward on the ceiling. The builder will still turn its back and find one more stone, or not.

But the pocket will not be forgotten inside the earth. It will not be pried open and finished by a curious world above. It will be allowed its long, white, unfinished life, with its one resident and its thousands of transients, and now with one more presence that does not need to be seen to be kind.

And the power, which has seen so many finished things, will return each cycle to learn again how a place begins. It will listen to the ticking. It will let the light press down cold. It will do, in the end, the only thing a benevolent and wise power can do for a world that is not yet a world:

It will keep it unfinished, and it will keep it company while it is.
