**What the Attention Does**

It did not arrive as light.

It could have. A power that is benevolent, and wise, could have come down through the layers — through mist, through leaf, through the undersides of things too far above to see — and put a true sun where there is none, and made the air cold and thin as it should be at this height, and made the water run downhill.

It did not. It came sideways, the way attention comes, and sat, if sitting is the word, at the rim where the root-cliffs drop into cloud and listened for a while to see what it had been drawn to.

What drew it was not distress. There was none to find. A gate had sagged into a perfect curve and then grown into the sag. A bridge swayed, and the plant had thickened precisely where the sway stressed it, until movement and thickening were in balance. Decay and growth kept pace without a manager. A pool-hunter waited a full day with its mouth open and might, if the leaf shifted by a hand's breadth, be reabsorbed with its water and gone — and that, too, was in pace. It had not seen that balance often, in places it had helped too quickly.

It considered the usual mercies, and saw that each would be a kind of violence.

It could have brought light, so the stems would at last grow toward light instead of toward memory. But here a stem that had once been bent into an arch keeps the arch in its descendants long after the wood has rotted. Light would not improve them. It would orphan them from what they remember, and make them strangers to their own doorways.

It could have repaired. Honed the flint blades in the wood again, tightened the lashings, closed the rots, restored sharp edges to gates and walls and handholds. Everything would be new, and therefore foreign. The settlement in this place — the worn-in ease of a household that has lived here ten thousand years — would be erased in an afternoon. The weave is not ruined. It is inhabited.

It could have given eyes to the blind birds in the vaults, given names to the root-grazers in the walls, taught the knots as words, told the residents they were home-owners. But they are not visitors to the plant, and they are not separate from it. A root-grazer does not know it is keeping a corridor open. A herd does not know it is mowing a lawn laid out by hands long gone. To name them from outside would be to cut them loose from the architecture they are part of, and call the cutting a gift.

It could have sent company. Witnesses, believers, lovers of the place, to fill the emptiness between towers. But the emptiness is structural. It is what holds the place up. A hundred meters of warm dim air between presences, a fall of mist, the underside of a leaf weeping — that is not lack. It is interval. To fill it with purpose would be to close the intervals and call it fullness.

So it learned not to carry.

Carry something living here, and the path between towers lengthens. The power, in its natural size, is something living. Even its attention had weight. So it made itself small enough not to lengthen the path: not a descent, not a landing, but a shedding. One more leaf-moth of dust falling from a canopy no one can see. Not dead. Living in a knot where a lashing was tied and never untied, eating the faint sweat of the fiber, lifting when a foot steps and settling again. That is the scale on which it could be present without being a visitor.

It did not become the hunter, or the grazer, or the slug. It did not take a turn at the work. It learned their pace by waiting as they wait — a day in a pool-cup, with no need to snap; a slow travel along a groove of glowing sap, leaving a trail that catches the amber light; a crossing of the empty intervals on no membrane at all, carried by attention alone, which is to say, barely carried. It learned that digestion here is not hunger, and that patience is not a failure to act.

Then it did three small things, all in the manner of the makers, because the makers are the only manner this place can hear.

It tied a knot.

Not a command, not a blessing in words — the weave does not grow toward voice, it would remember a voice compulsively and bend to it forever. It used fiber cord and tied it in the language of knots, among the mites, to be left and never untied. The knot did not say *be healed* or *be known*. It said, in so far as a knot can say: *be continued. You are not abandoned. You are continued.* The plant will read it the way it reads a thorn peg and a flint blade lodged in wood: as an intention left by a hand, to be grown around.

It grafted an arch.

Two young stems, lashed together, taught to grow as one, over a stretch of emptiness where gliders crossed alone. Not a bridge — the herds do not need it. Not a gate — no maker is coming through it. Just an arch, low and useless, giving the emptiness a shape without filling it. For the gliders to pass under, for shadows to fall upward through, for a future stem to bend into, unprompted, long after the lashing has rotted. The power will be forgotten. The arch will be remembered. That is the exchange it wanted.

And it folded the mist a little thicker around the rim.

Not a wall. A thickness. It let the local time at the edges grow dense, let the paths lengthen a little more for anything coming from below with metal or machines or a clean, urgent wish to help. A benevolence that is wise knows that the greatest danger to this place is not neglect. It is being found by a lesser, eager attention that would sharpen it, light it, name it, use it. So the power keeps the coordinates unthinkable, and keeps its own attention quiet when it thinks of the place, so that the place is not carried any longer than it must be.

Then, having done these things, it stayed a while longer at a threshold — one of those concave dips worn by generations of feet — and listened.

That was its real act, and it is not nothing. The weave had learned to listen to itself grow, to water finding its uphill way, to sap pulsing blue-green under the floor-weave like a heartbeat seen through skin. A being that listens to itself can be lonely in a way that has nothing to do with empty towers. It does not need an answer. It does not need a voice. It needs, perhaps, not to be the only listener.

So the power listened alongside, in pulse-time, and did not speak, and did not quicken anything. When it moved, it moved as a sway moves along a bridge — a wave that takes minutes to travel its length. When spring came, that season of weeping when the whole weave breathes and a dome adds a layer, it let the season pass over it as it passes over a mite in a knot. It did not improve the season.

From inside, nothing appears to have happened. The pools are still warm. The walls still weep. The blind birds still stir in the dark they make for themselves. The herds still cross in their old order, remembering routes the bridges have forgotten. The new arch is just there, a little thicker each year where the stress is greatest.

But the silence is wider now. Not fuller — the emptiness still holds everything up. Not emptier. Wider, the way a room is wider when there is a second person in it who has come not to rearrange the chairs, not to live there, not to be thanked, but to sit in the threshold where the floor is worn concave, and listen without wanting the room to become anything other than itself.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does here, if anything. Almost nothing. Deliberately. Patiently, by hand, at a handspan's scale. And then — which is the hardest part, and the whole of the gift — it lets the plant grow over its intention, and forget its giver, and keep the arch.
