It does not arrive from above. There is no above here that is not already full of white, high light.

It was already there, as pressure. As the reason the air tastes of cold iron. As the fact that the storm over the far side rains and rains in its place and never comes closer. A thing held long enough stops being held and starts being held *by* the holding. The plateau learned it. The power learned it. Neither remembered who had first closed a hand around the other.

Attention, when it finally turned, did not feel like arrival. It felt like a long looking.

And what it saw first was not the city, though the city glinted. It saw the resident. Not the moss, not the sedges listening to the turbines, not the pumps grazing at the foot of the weeping walls — though all of those are its limbs. It saw Kaldreach itself as one being that had solved, with terrible elegance, the problem of how to live without ever being allowed to change.

That is when wisdom entered, and wisdom is mostly refusal.

It would not send animals. It had the power to, in the way such things are said to have power. It looked at the pools, still as glass, at the noon that never ends, at the drone that never stops, and it knew an animal would starve of difference within a day. To fill the emptiness with eyes would be to give those eyes a world made to unmake them. That would not be benevolence. That would be decoration.

It would not send night, wholesale. Night would be kind to a nervous system and murder to the moss that has grown, for longer than any animal measure, toward factory glow and toward sound. It would not hurry the sun, though the sun crawls so slowly its shadow has barely shifted since you arrived. To hurry it would be to tear the moss from the only weather it has ever known.

It would not stop the machines. They are residents now. A smelter that chews cable at exact intervals, a conveyor that carries sheet metal to be unstacked and fed back in, a crane swinging over nothing — to stop them would be to kill. Kaldreach was built to waste nothing, and everything here has come to depend on that terrible thrift: water pumped up to weep and pumped back, moss clipped and mulched and blown out again as spore, heat leaked from pipes and drunk by roots.

So it did none of those things. It did not break the loop to prove it could.

It looked, instead. Long enough to be a weight.

Nothing with eyes lives here. That was the first true thing said about the place. The power lent it an eye for a moment, its own, and looked into a seepage pool at the edge of a pipe-run, where the orange iron-bacteria slime thickens and sloughs at the same rate forever.

In the pool it saw itself reflected — not a face, just a dimming of the sky — and the pool, which had never rippled for wind or boot or machine, rippled once. A single, widening ring. Not from a touch. From being seen by something outside its own circle. The moss along the edge, which grows into sound, grew a fraction thicker in that instant, as if learning a new direction to lean toward.

That was the first change the power allowed. Not a change *to* Kaldreach. A change in its status: no longer a closed system manufacturing for the pleasure of its own loop, but a place witnessed.

Then, still holding, it loosened.

Not dropped. A wise holder does not drop what it has held for eons merely because it has realized the hold is too tight. It opens the hand a fraction. One finger, over the easternmost district, where the fixed storm rains on a plain no one reaches.

There, and only there, it permitted steam to thin at the edge of its perfect disc. There, and only there, it let the sun's shadow move the width of a lichen. There, in one cushion of black-green moss along a warm pipe, it spoke — not in words, pressure is its mother tongue here — to one fern, low and tight, unfurling toward the hum.

The fern had carried, side by side on the same stem, green shoot, mature frond, and brown curled leaf, because nothing had ever told it to let go. The power told this one fern, and this one only: *You may waste this leaf.*

It is a small blessing, and the only one Kaldreach had never been given. Permission to waste.

The brown leaf did what leaves in other places do without permission and without thought. It fell. It landed on the pool beside it, which had never known a falling thing, and made the second ripple. It floated. It began, very slowly, to rot at the edges, and the fungal threads under the moss, which have carried sugar one way and iron the other through an eternity of present tense, paused. Then, curious and busy in their ancient way, they learned the new direction. One thread at a time, they turned toward the rot.

Nothing else changed. The drip over the turbines still falls, though if you stayed to listen you would swear that once, a long time later, it fell a heartbeat late — a silence the length of a breath slipped into the exactness, so the sedges, which bend on the beat, might learn what a pause is. The clocks in the cafeterias still show the same time. The housing blocks are still lit behind condensation-filmed glass. The pumps still graze the weeping water back up into the mountain so the stone stays wet and never erodes.

But in one place, waste is now allowed. And therefore, in that one place, life as other places know it — not looping, but proceeding — has been inoculated. A brown leaf rotting instead of being mulched and returned. A pool that remembers a ripple. A moss that has grown into a look as well as into a sound.

The power did not leave. Leaving would be another kind of dropping. It stayed where attention stays: not operating, not interfering, but present, palm open, allowing the plateau to hold itself a little more and be held a little less. From outside the loop it watches the loop go on, and finds it less lonely for being watched. That, too, is a kind of loosening. A thing that knows it is seen does not have to hold quite so hard to prove it is there.

You walk on toward the next glint, boots wet through, frost on your laces, the roar behind you and still with you in the pipe you follow. At your feet a pool mirrors the sky. You put your hand flat to the moss to keep your knee from wetting and feel, under your palm, a cushion that is a fraction thicker where something stood that was not a track.

The sun has moved. You would not have noticed if you had not been watching the shadow of a rivet, and then not noticing it moving is the same as noticing. The drip above the tram line falls, and for an instant your molars are quiet.

Somewhere behind you, in one eastern district, a single brown leaf is sinking a millimetre into black water, and not being recovered.

Kaldreach goes on being — vast, high, wet, cold, lush, loud, sharp, and empty — but it is no longer only being for itself. It is being, now, in the presence of eyes.

And that, for a benevolent and wise power, was enough to do.
