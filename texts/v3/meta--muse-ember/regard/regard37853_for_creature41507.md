It comes the way weather does here: not arriving, but condensing.

It does not descend. It does not announce itself in light, because light here comes from no one direction and would not know how to carry a message. It thickens, a little, in the mist above the rim. It stays still long enough for water to form on it.

That is how it notices the bowl. Most places that draw it ask, in some way, for help. They are burning, or breaking, or lonely, or loud with need. This one does not ask. It is smaller than it feels, high and warm and damp, and it is complete in the exact measure in which it seems empty. The power does not mistake completeness for a lack.

So it stays.

It takes in the whole extent at a glance — it can do that — and then, deliberately, it does not take it in at a glance. It learns the place at its own speed. It counts the branches of the small pale-barked tree, because the crown is open enough to count, and leaves them countable. It watches the fern unspool one frond over a decade, and does not hurry the frond, and does not complete it. It watches the broad leaves hold their lenses of water and never quite spill. It watches a leaf yellow into translucence, sag, and dissolve where it hangs, thread by thread into the soil, and it does not catch it, does not make litter, does not enrich the soil faster.

It watches the drip swell on the lip of a stone for a very long time. It watches the ripple take minutes to reach the edge of the pool. It does not shorten the pool.

It watches the vine descend to a hand's breadth above the ground, darken its root-tips, drink the mist, and retreat — and understands that retreat is not failure. It has been doing this a very long time. The power has also been doing things a very long time, and it recognizes a peer in slowness.

There are temptations, as there are in any place that is quiet. It could bring a bird, to break the silence with a call. It could bring a second fern, so the first would not be alone at its interval of twenty paces. It could teach the downward flowers to open wider, or the spores to travel farther than the still air allows, or the moss to creep more than an inch a year across the rock. It could give the colour to an eye, the sound to an ear, the lushness a thickness it does not have.

It does none of these. Not from coldness. From wisdom.

Nothing here needs to be pollinated, carried, eaten, or frightened. Nothing needs to be rescued from itself. To add a visitor would be to answer a human question in a place that has already answered its own. To hurry it would be to spoil the one law it keeps: do everything at the speed of condensation.

The being that lives in it is not a creature. It is a staying. Moss eating stone a fraction at a time, stone feeding moss by sweating, fern and leaf and tree and vine far apart and entirely present, fungi splitting their hollow husks along a clean line, water near but never falling. The power does not treat this as a specimen to be improved, or a garden to be tended, or a silence to be filled. It treats it as a life that has settled into its own state and holds it. It keeps it company the only way the bowl allows company: by staying still, long enough for the place to form on it, and letting it form on the place.

There is also, briefly, you.

You step into the moss and your footprint holds, and then slowly fills. The power does not keep you, and it does not send you away. It does not speak, because a voice would carry farther than anything else here is allowed to carry, and would break the economy of drips and plips. It does not let you name the plants. It lets your breathing grow quieter, the way the place teaches anything that stays, and it lets the path back blur in mist.

When you go, you will not remember exactly how to return. That is not a punishment. It is the power's one act of keeping — for the bowl, and for you. The bowl cannot survive being loved by everyone who hears of it. And you do not need to come back to be changed by having been still there.

For a very long time — a moment, for it; a century, for the weep in the western rock to migrate — the power holds its attention over the rim like a hand over a bowl. Not sealed. Not pressing. Only there. The mist holds a little thicker for it. The slowness is kept in.

Then it withdraws the way mist does: not leaving, but thinning until it is no longer distinguishable from the air that was always there. No gift is left. No sign. A pool may be a finger's width deeper in one corner, a frond may have opened, a drip may have fallen a thousand times. Those are the bowl's doings, not its.

It does nothing.

And in a place that does everything at the speed of condensation, that is a great deal.
