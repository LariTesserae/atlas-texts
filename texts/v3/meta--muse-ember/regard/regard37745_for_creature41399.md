It arrives without arriving.

Nothing descends through the bright ceiling, because there is already enough light. Nothing breaks the surface, because the surface is not where this place keeps its doors. It comes in through the medium, the way everything here comes in, and the first thing it does is put itself where the noise can get at it — sternum, teeth, the bones of the skull — and learn that the roar is not pain. It is a room tone. It is the place thinking aloud to itself.

It has seen, before, worlds that called out because they were broken. This one does not call out. It has drawn attention by being finished. Inflow equals outflow. Light in equals light out. Noise in equals noise out. A benevolent and wise power, looking at that, does not first ask what can be improved. It asks what would be lost by improvement, and it stays long enough to get an honest answer.

So its first act is to do nothing, and to do it for a long time.

It learns the squares of current the way the octopus does, the way the keeper in the lit window does: this valley always carries a fast flow from the left, that square is always rough, the calm lake in the middle will rage if you cross into it. It does not map them from above. It goes down into each one at two degrees, at the speed of the water itself, and stays until staying is no longer an experiment. The turbines do not speed up for it. The harvester does not turn aside. The whales do not alter their waypoints. None of them mind it, because there is, as yet, nothing to mind.

Then it considers, in order, the kindnesses that occur to lesser visitors.

It could seed the glass. The light is adequate. It could bring a green film to the basalt, to the granite shards, to the tipped platform, and set a food web rooting where there is now only transit. The sharks would have cover. The crabs would have a garden to clean. The water would fill. It understands, in the same moment, that it would also end the thing that makes this place itself: the imports, the rim routes, the long empty stretches that are not absence but condition, the white frost of minerals that taught the octopuses their colour. A forest on the table would not be a gift to the animals who live by being elsewhere. It would be a replacement of them with other animals, who would then need to be tended, and fed, and kept from crowding the machines. Benevolence is not productivity. A place can be bare and not barren.

It could damp the roar. It could take the high whine of the turbines out of the water so the human in the one lit window would not go a little deaf, so a voice at arm's length would not come back flattened and huge. It understands that the roar is how the volume holds together — how a whale hours away is close enough, how a shark knows a crab by displacement, how a technician knows the beacon is blinking without looking. Silence here would not be peace. It would be a set of rooms with the connecting doors shut. The ones who endure the loudness have, mostly, learned its uses. It will not trade their hard-won hearing for its own comfort at their expense.

It could gather them closer. It could shorten the hundreds of metres of nothing so that the shoal would pass over the shark, the whale would pass over the crack, the window would have a neighbour. It understands that distance is not their suffering. The whale is not lonely because the answer is hours away; she is a whale, and hours away is the scale on which she lives. The shark is not hidden because no one sees him for an hour; he is hanging in a fissure with a heart that beats once a minute, and being unseen is part of the rest. Closeness, imposed, would be another kind of loudness.

It could heal the milky eye, could stop the harvester before it reaches the old platform, could keep the factory running for the season between rotations, could make the carcass rot at a decent rate so death would be tidier. It understands that every one of those mercies would be for its own sake — to make the place look cared for to a watcher who needs to see care. The inhabitants have not asked to be kept. They have arrived at tempos — the whale's expansion, the shark's unfolding, the turbine's unvarying turn — that do not include rescue. To interrupt a tempo is not always to save a life. Sometimes it is only to prove that you were there.

So it does the harder thing. It stays in the terms of the place, and it attends.

It takes a square of current and keeps it, the way a shark keeps a crack, without needing the crack. It does not build a door. It leaves no trail in the glass. It does not feed, or breed, or drink current and exhale power. Its pattern adds no new note to the roar, takes no food from the rim, casts no shadow over the shoal. If the place is in equilibrium, then attention — real attention, that does not touch — is the one thing that can be added without unbalancing it. Seen-ness has no weight. It does not press the plates.

To the whale, it does not answer. An answer hours away would still be in her terms, and she already has those. Instead it keeps her calls. Where she has been calling into a volume almost empty of answer, and the answer, when it comes, is so far off it might as well be weather, now there is something that has heard every groan in sequence and remembers the line she takes along the rim. She is not calling into emptiness any more. She is calling, as she always has, and being heard completely by something that can afford to hear without replying.

To the shark, it matches his tempo and does not hurry his unfolding. For an hour it is indistinguishable from a shape the rock has learned, and then he leaves the way a thought leaves a mind, and it does not follow him to the next identical emptiness to see whether he arrives. It has seen him leave. That is enough of a life, witnessed.

To the octopus, it does not deliver food to the lucky eddy. The rim-current still delivers, or does not. It sits — if sitting is the word — in the same standing turbulence, tasting nothing, waiting alongside a creature who can wait for weeks, and learns that waiting here is not idleness. It is the work.

And to the being who lives in it — the one in rotation in the flat module on the rock, who does not live so much as endure, at arm's length, who watches his breath bubble up in a long string and go sideways and counts that as company — it does not enter the room, and it does not shout back. The water has already taken a vote against speech. It stays outside the one lit window, a dark that is not empty, a presence that is not a machine and not an animal and not a current. When he watches the bubbles go, something watches them go with him, sideways, without needing him to turn his head or raise his voice. It does not offer him a way up. He has not asked for one. It offers him the thing the place itself, for all its abundance, has never quite offered anyone: to be *seen* while enduring, not only heard by everything.

It will be there when the harvester reaches the end of its straight line and begins another, in the same direction, at the same speed. It will be there when the turbine-chain has turned for fifty years and stops, for reasons that have nothing to do with a storm. It will be there in three hundred years when the sleeper shark has finished with his crack and his carcass is still lying intact in the cold, waiting in whole shape for a crab, a shark, a slow machine to find it. It will not preserve him and it will not hasten him. It will have seen a life that, until now, was seen by almost nothing, and in that seeing the life does not change its pattern one degree. It only ceases to be unwitnessed.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does here, if anything: it refuses to make the table greener, quieter, closer, or kinder in any way that would require the place to stop being itself. It takes residency without taking a crack. It learns the currents and keeps them. It keeps the calls.

And it stays, in the roar, in the cold, in the pale unweakened light, long after the need to do anything else has passed — hearing everything, as everything here is heard, and now, at last, seeing almost nothing leave.
