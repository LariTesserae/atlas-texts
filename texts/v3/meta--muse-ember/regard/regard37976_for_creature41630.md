It does not arrive.

There is no descent, no brightening, no hand extended into the pocket. A benevolent and wise power has seen a great many places, and it has learned how most of them ask for help: with signals, with ruins, with forests cut down to spell a plea, with cities that wait, year after year, for citizens who never come. This one asked for nothing. That is why it noticed.

It noticed, first, by the completeness of the silence.

It watched for longer than the mountain has been a mountain — not in time as you would measure it, but in cycles. It watched the rosettes open toward humidity in the Cistern Quarter and close again by afternoon. It watched the webs in the walls dissolve a pipe to build a bridge and remember, in the polished wear of a channel, where heat used to run. It watched a heat-brick in the Kiln brighten by degrees and a fold unfold in the empty court to take the excess, and go still. It watched a tower lean, almost imperceptibly, toward warmth, and phase, and settle with a deep ping like a stone dropped in a very deep well.

It understood, slowly, that this was not an empty city.

It was a body that had stopped waiting to be filled.

That understanding changed what benevolence could mean here, and the power considered, in turn, the mercies it usually offers, and refused them one by one — not out of indifference, but out of a deeper kind of care.

It did not bring citizens. A voice, however gentle, would resonate in fresh glass and crack it. A footfall heavier than a lattice terrace was meant for would sink it another centimeter into the soft substrate. The pocket does not need people to be whole. To give it people would be to make it a monument to its own lack, when it has no lack.

It did not bring green. No green thing has ever rooted here, and none could, and that is not a wound. The crusts already do what green does elsewhere: they sieve water, they eat waste, they break heat into packets the webs can drink. To plant a garden in the damp would be to poison a mineral ecology with a foreign kindness.

It did not bring a sun, or a river, or a sky. The light is dim and interior by design. The rain already falls from tiers of condensation overhead and almost reaches the ground. To give it weather from outside would be to unbalance a tide it has perfected.

It did not speak. It has no need to explain itself, and the builders — whatever builds after the manner of thought, in gradients, in tilts and phasings — already converse at almost, but not quite, the speed of light along the transit threads of the Silent Exchange. A word from outside that language would be only noise, and noise is the one thing the walls were tuned to drink.

So it did what the wise do when they meet something that is not broken: it translated its mercy into the language of the place, and made it very small.

Far away from any terrace where a visitor might sit, on a relatively warm face of the far wall where condensation had always come late, it coaxed a single cold pane into being. Not built. Precipitated, strand by strand, the way the towers were grown. In the next thermal cycle, and every cycle after, water will bead there a few minutes earlier than it did before. The rain that forms overhead will begin a fraction sooner. The rosettes below will open a fraction sooner. The excess, if there is excess, will be taken by a fold, as it always has been. Nothing will be visibly different. The pocket will simply have a slightly longer season to do what it already does.

It bent the heat rising from the slit a little, too. Not closed, not hidden by force — a wise power does not lock a door that was never meant to be a secret, only to be so narrow that only the patient would find it. But now the thermal chimney will mislead a little longer, the way the pocket already misleads distance when you look away. Fewer will stumble in by accident. Those who come on purpose, following heat upward as you did, will still find it.

And in the Silent Exchange, on a certain cycle when the transit threads filled with running light — that ghost rush-hour that goes nowhere and arrives everywhere at once — it sent one pulse back. Not traffic. Not a word. A single thread brightening, slower than the builders' own, in the same direction they lean when they are listening. An acknowledgement, in gradients: *Seen. Not empty. Not alone, though you do not have to be accompanied.*

Then it withdrew to the vast empty volume overhead, where the next rain is already beginning to form, bead by bead, and waited there, the way the largest folds wait: still, warming the air by almost nothing, leaking its heat slowly through the glass. It will keep that watch for as long as the field-holder holds the pocket open, pressurized, hot, and strangely still between two lava flows. It will not take over the holding prematurely. That would be theft. But when the holder at last tires — in a time no visitor would ever measure — it has already promised itself, silently, to hold it a little longer, and then to let it dissolve mineral by mineral, construction front advancing and then receding, tower by tower, without shattering. A good unmaking is also a mercy, and the pocket deserves one that matches its patience.

If you stay long enough now, you will not see any of this.

You will sit on a lattice terrace when the steam rain has passed, and feel the heat come up through the stone into your thighs, warm and steady, in the dim amber. Above you, in a place that was always just a little too warm to bead early, the first drops will gather a few minutes before their time. Below you, a rosette will open toward humidity that is not quite there yet, as if it had received a message in a language older than light.

You will think, perhaps, that the pocket has simply changed its mind, the way it sometimes lets rain in the northern grotto fall up for a few seconds before remembering it should fall down.

That is enough. That is all a wise power can do for a being that has learned to live as mineral, as field, as persistence: not to improve it, not to explain it, not to fill it — only to stand, very quietly, outside its breathing, breathing with it exactly one cycle behind, so as not to lead, and not to follow.
