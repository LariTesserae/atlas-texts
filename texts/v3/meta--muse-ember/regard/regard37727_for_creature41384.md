It does not come as light.

There is no sky here to descend from, and a power that arrived with its own radiance would blind the basin. The films on the pocket-waters know how to brighten only a fraction when the air warms. The fern-veins pulse amber, or bruised blue, and then dim. A free light, a sun hung overhead, would not make them brighter. It would make their labor pointless, and then make them die of the pointlessness. So the power, if it is wise, dims itself before it enters. It comes sideways, low, from the warmth below, and arrives as a slightly steadier warmth on one bowl among many.

It notices not a prayer. Nothing here prays. It notices the slab.

Under the broad-leaved trees, where the glow is steadiest, the heel-sized stones and the cracked shells sorted half-shell, half-shell, in a meticulous, repeated pattern from visit to visit. A small hand-like shape, fingers black with damp, face turned toward the glow to see by it, selecting a stone, fitting a shell into the cup of rock, striking — not hard, precisely — until it gives.

That pattern is what catches a benevolent and wise power. Not the tool. The repetition. The sorting. The care taken where nothing is kept, where the stones are left wet to green at the edges, where no wall or roof or artifact is meant to persist. It is the beginning of a ritual, or a teaching, or an aesthetic — something made for no use the basin can see. A power that has watched other worlds learn to make things has learned to recognize this moment, and to fear its own eagerness to hurry it.

It could give them a day. It could give them a night to be opposite to. It could open a river through the pockets so the water would at last run somewhere instead of evaporating and refilling in the same place, over and over, like breathing. It could cool the black spire that hisses in its own heat, steady the ground that heaves and shrugs stones off roots, dry the air so sweat would dry, thin the crowd so a voice could be heard.

Every one of those mercies would be a ruin.

A river would wash the newborn soil thin before the root-hairs and eyelash shoots could hold it. The cracks that steam for an hour and fill with green in days exist because nothing flows out. A day would end the law that light is made, laboriously, by living things out of warmth and rot and mineral sweetness. A steady ground would end the thing the whole basin lives by: that nothing persists in the form you found it, that a tree can rot at the base and give up in an afternoon while things are already living on the part that will land, that a vine can be flower at the tip and fruit below it and dying leaf at the base all at once.

And it could give the makers more. Fire, perhaps. A way to keep the tool. A sign to sort by. A name for the pattern, so it could be passed on. A wise power has seen this gift given before, in other basins, and watched it turn a sorting into a performance. The makers do not know it is there, and that not-knowing is part of the mercy. If they knew, they would begin to crack shells for the watcher. The pattern would become worship, and worship would become architecture, and architecture would sit, straight and heavy, on soil that was never meant to hold anything straight.

So it does not give.

It does not answer the frog chorus from the pockets, because an answer would become another band in the sound, and the sound already crawls from patch to patch until your teeth ache. It does not tear the spider-webs to see through them more clearly. It does not step over the worm-carpet to avoid the heave under its boots. It does not shoo the swarm that passes like a front and shakes the condensation loose in sheets. It does not heal the one grub whose belly has dimmed because it burrowed too deep, because a mercy at that scale is a starvation somewhere else, in the mites grazing the glow on a leaf's underside, in the pale limb uncurling from the canopy to feel for water it does not drink.

What it does, if anything, is stay low enough to be part of the density.

In this place, to be noticed is to be touched. Many presences, near-touching, in layers. So the power notices by touching lightly and being touched in return — damp in its hair, a vine reaching its shoulder, mites crossing whatever skin it has borrowed for the occasion. It becomes one more overlapping presence in the crowd, not a conductor above it. It sits, or crouches, beside the slab. It does not strike a stone. It does not complete the pattern with one husk of its own. It learns the pattern as it is, visit after visit, and holds it in a memory that will outlast any one bowl collapsing and refilling greener.

That memory is its benevolence. Locally, nothing is kept. Externally, this will not be forgotten. If a bowl is scalded by a vent that sighs too long, if a spire stays bare too long, if the walking flock whose eyes shine amber and green passes and does not return because a ridge rose a finger's width — the place itself will go on changing shape in minutes, as it always has. The power does not prevent the change. It prevents the place from being erased unmourned, and it prevents anyone else, hungrier or clumsier, from finding it, naming it, and taking its heat and its glow as a resource.

It folds itself over the rim like a shield-leaf. Not to keep the inside in, but to keep the outside out. It asks for no thanks. Thanks would be another artifact to keep.

And then it stays. Not as a ruler. As a witness that has learned, late, how much kindness costs when it is given in the wrong currency. It sweats in the low warm air and does not dry. It listens to the bands of stridulation start and stop and start again a half-beat later from a different patch of dark. When the air warms, the fern veins above the slab pulse amber, as if the work were still going on in the light they made for themselves, and the power understands, finally, who is teaching whom.

The hand-like shape returns to the slab, face toward the glow, and strikes again — not hard, precisely. The stones lie where they were left, wet, already greening at the edges. The power is still there beside them, face toward the glow, not striking at all.
