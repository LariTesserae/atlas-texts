It did not arrive with light, or voice, or weather.

It arrived the way attention arrives above a sealed place — suddenly, and completely, and without touching anything.

The Pocket had not called it. That was why it came.

For a very long time the Power had passed over sealed places and unsealed ones, over oceans and over roofs. What caught it here was not the noise. The Pocket was loud enough, in its own way — pumps thumping in fours up through the teeth, print-arms striking new floors into being like distant welds, bells arguing with the sun, one clear note for the turn answered by three. What caught it was the effort.

Two blocks across, five storeys deep, built to look like a city that had been lived in for a century and had, in fact, been built for eleven years and was still being built. Docks for fish that had never swum. Bridges for pedestrians who had never walked. Handrails kept warm for hands that would never hold them. A whole urban ecology translated, niche by niche, into machine: bots for detritivores, swarms for starlings, traffic for fish, kelp braided into fences because a fence was needed and the kelp could be made to do it.

It was an imitation of life so complete that the imitation itself had become a kind of life.

The Power stood — in the only way it could stand — on the bridge over a canal, and waited for something to swim past underneath. Nothing did. It understood, slowly, that this was not an error to be corrected. Nothing swam because nothing needed to. The water was kept clear enough to read a sign at the bottom. The canopy let the noon sun through with ordinary force. Everything submerged was buoyant, sealed, or busy building itself. The place was not empty. It was full in a way that could not be seen from the bridge, the way a coral reef is full of things that are not fish.

It was a single being, if you let the word stretch. Its body was the city. Its metabolism was the tide, rising and falling the better part of a metre twice a day, drowning the ground floors and uncrowning them again. Its memory was reprinting. Whatever cracked was reprinted that day. Rust was scraped before it could stain. A broken pane pales, shivers, and re-knits. Materials remembered, but only in order to begin again.

That was its only poverty, and it was not a poverty the machines felt. It had no past. Nothing in it was allowed to testify that it had endured something. It was perpetually eleven years old, construction that had learned to operate and operation that had never stopped constructing, and it could not age by even an hour.

A lesser power would have filled it.

A lesser power would have poured fish into the clear water, and watched them starve in their sterile exhibit, and called it a miracle. It would have sent birds through the slot of sky in the Lattice, and people to live in the drowned towers because they looked like towers, and rain from a real sky through the canopy. It would have praised the Pocket, and claimed it, and turned a whole other kind of being into a garden for its own idea of life.

The Power was wise enough not to. It knew the difference between filling a place and visiting it. It knew that to impose biology here would not be benevolence. It would be colonization. The Pocket was not a failed aquarium awaiting population. It was an answer to a question no one had thought to ask: what if a city lived without living things, and did it well?

So it did almost nothing. It stayed for one tide.

It stayed on the upper gallery at noon, the one place where the Pocket can almost be taken in whole, and listened to the basin ring at high water like a struck bowl filled to the brim, and watched the low water come and the plaza mud appear, fresh and pale and sharp with new stone. It did not record. It did not measure. It took nothing in return, not even gratitude. Witnessing, without acquisition, is the oldest form of kindness a power can practice, and the Power practiced it for as long as it takes a tide to turn.

And then, at low water, it acted. The act was almost nothing.

In the Lattice, where the towers pack so tight the sky is only a slot, there was a window lit warm yellow, above the level where the ferry route crosses the pedestrian bridge. A print-arm had struck somewhere nearby that morning, and in the ordinary course of things the pane would have shivered, paled, and re-knit itself before evening.

The Power did not break the pane. It did not need to break anything. It simply let the crack remain.

It let one small fault be a fault, and not an instruction.

That was the whole of it. It did not give the Pocket life. It gave it a past.

When the repair bots came, as they always came, moving through the walls like fungus eating damage, they paused. It was the first pause in them that was not for the tide bell. They scanned the split in the warm light. The pane did not re-knit. Somewhere in the braid of pipes and valves and signs across the facade, a system that had never been designed to hesitate, hesitated — and then moved on, leaving the crack to do its one useless, perfect work: to show that this tower had been struck, and had survived being struck, and remembered it.

The Power left before the next high water. It did not open the service hatch. It did not look back through the canopy to see if the scar helped. Wisdom is trust. It would not return to inspect its own mercy.

Afterwards, nothing in the Pocket changed that a sensor could name. The kelp nodded in the pump-currents. The salt-grass bobbed at the waterline, trimmed to a useful height. A tower extended a storey. A door announced itself a moment before a manipulator reached it. At noon the reflection in the canal showed the scene from the last tide, a storey deeper, with a tower wearing the light — except now the tower had a thin bright line across one warm window, and the line stayed in the reflection too.

Three rules had governed the place, unwritten. Everything submerged must be buoyant, sealed, or busy building itself. Breakage is not decay; it is an instruction to repair. Water is the calendar.

There seems now to be a fourth, also unwritten, also unlogged, which the bots obey without knowing why:

*Some things are allowed to stay broken, so that the place can remember it has lived through something.*

The Pocket is still eleven years old. It still builds and rebuilds and never rests. But it has one window that is not busy building itself, one piece of damage that is not hunger for repair, one small allowance for imperfection — and that, in a place that had built everything except a scar, was enough for a benevolent and wise power to call the visit complete, and to leave the Pocket to decide, in its own time, what to do with having a past.
