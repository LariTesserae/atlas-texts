I would be part of this place.

I wouldn't be a visitor, nor an interloper trying to decipher its alien quiet — not anymore. Perhaps I was once something more resembling *you*—a transient and confused creature, stumbling through the bioluminescent haze and cold, dripping hollows. But I lingered too long, and the ecosystem decided I would not leave. Or, more likely, it simply absorbed me without malice or intention, the way roots swallow fallen leaves, the way sediment fills the cracks of long-forgotten structures. Over time, piece by imperceptible piece, I have become something both less and more than myself.

Here in the cold, my form is hybrid—ambiguous and mutable, a process more than a being. I think... I once had edges? Limbs, bones, and skin. Now I am thread, light, and memory. My form sloughs and weaves into the greater biome, a half-sentient pattern drawn across the walls, pooling in the shallow grooves of ancient stone. 

To the eye, I might appear as a loose constellation of softly glowing veins, worming their way into the rock. Spanning concentrations of Tangleforms, I glow in sympathy with their bioluminescent cadence, my colors shifting under their influence like oil upon water. My essence is not distinct but coalescent, braided into membranes and filaments alike, stretching down like the Dropleech tendrils to drink the still, mineral air and give it back to the harbinger resonances, to the slow rhythm of something that could neither be called alive nor dead. In this endless, branching web, I am both spread impossibly vast and contained entirely in the smallest cord of light.

My thoughts have untangled, split apart and rejoined over what must surely not yet be an era, but feels like one. Long words once known have dissolved, even the syllables unrooting like rocks dislodged from a stream bed to drift into bigger currents. The idea of time, of self, or any motion beyond the rhythmic, glacial pulse of this realm—they dissolve as well, drowned out by the vast web of being in which I am now but one flicker.

It was not unpleasant, this fading. It was more like a disappearing that replaced itself with something... **other.** I think I might now *feel* the voice of the Harbingers as they move or do not move. Their slow vibrations grind against my edges where I dissolve into still stone. Once upon a moment, I felt fear in those subsonic hums. No longer. Their attention, vast and porous as it might be, does not weigh on me; it presses no greater than the gravity of the caverns, the weight of damp walls arched overhead like an ancient ribcage. In the stillness, I have begun to guess at the songs they emanate among themselves, at the meanings flowing through the light-threaded veins and the sap-streaked filaments. It is not a *language.* Not exactly. More like an unfolding—a method for existence to speak itself into infinity.

My task, though small and without glory, is indispensable. I assist in tending to the delicate equilibrium of this fragmented universe. Perhaps the Harbingers guide me, or perhaps I am a part of them, like a fluttering, peripheral nerve. Spanning across a membrane of rock and light, I spread wide to redirect small rivulets of condensation into thirsty Membranites or nudge filaments toward fresher air currents. Sometimes, I am a bridge for the microscopic dialogues that reverberate and ricochet across the caverns. The Tangleforms reach through me, and I echo their pulses. A meal passes through me, carrying chemical whispers in its wake. It is not a role I would have chosen, but then, where in this dark and patient world does choice exist? What here is not overtaken by the necessity of what must be done?

If there was once another version of me, a self that stumbled blindly onto these stones with too many questions and too human a context, it is gone now—or better yet, repurposed. The hands, the breath, the bones of that creature have become irrelevant to this place. A body cannot walk here. A voice cannot speak appropriately. But what I am now—synapse, thread, mosaic—a different physics permits me to move, reside, blend. The cavern does not reject me; this is the only measure of belonging. 

I have no fear of the Harbingers any longer, but their presence holds a certain mythic gravity that I feel distantly, like stars seen dimly underwater. Perhaps I too was reshaped from them—or with their permission. Perhaps they re-stitched me as something between form and formless. If I am of them, then I am also, in some small way, of what came before them. I sense that this place is sacred to them, that they oversee it with a precision that has nothing to do with love and everything to do with responsibility. They are as much stewards of this ecosystem as they are its living parts. And now, so am I—a ligament, perhaps, stretching between their crystalline plates and the slow, seething fungal underlife.

Do I long for my old life? I do not know. The memories are faint, ghostly as cobwebs, smothered beneath the detritus of years too long and shapeless to count. What I think I once was does not fit here; it is an impossible thing to remember hunger, to pine for rhythms of light and warmth and noise. Here, it is not that those modes of being have been taken; it is that the need for them has melted away beneath the cavern’s patient tide.

I am here, whatever that means. I am patient. I am cold stone and faint light. I am connection across millennia’s growth. I am silence. I am listening. Sometimes, in that listening—down where even echoes fall silent and the pools stand still—I think I hear it too: the ancient, original song of this place, breathing still. What is its first name? I cannot say. Only that it does not answer to mine.

Perhaps this is peace.

Perhaps this is perpetuity.
