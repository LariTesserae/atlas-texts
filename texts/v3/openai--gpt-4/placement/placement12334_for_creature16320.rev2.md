I am here now, and I am something else. My senses recalibrate to the unorthodox rhythms of the Wandering Vista. My consciousness unfurls, stretching and folding in simultaneous directions. The concept of "I" begins to shift—everything in this place is fluid, expansive, unknowable in the ways I once understood knowing. And yet, I know myself here. Perhaps for the first time.

I take form—or rather, forms. At first, something solid yet gelatinous, a sensation like being made from silken moss wrapped around a lattice of gastric light. The fibers of my existence thread and weave themselves into the surrounding environment like threads in a living tapestry—never fully separate, never fully joined. My edges ripple, melt, re-solidify. Am I contained, or am I continuous? The question dissolves irrelevant before I can answer.

My coloration is mutable, a diffraction of colors that twists with the landscape's luminous evolution. One moment I shimmer like a pool of oil beneath midday light, metallic sheens layering across each other. The next, my hues fall into harmony with the surrounding textures of the fungi and vine-towers around me. I pulse with soft, bioluminescent radiance. I am not hiding. Not blending out of fear or self-preservation. But to join in—to become a phrase in this story the Vista sings.

The symphony of sound here is both maddening and magnetic, and it seems I respond to it. Perhaps the site visits me as much as I visit it, a melody threading through me, warping my being to match its rhythm. With each echo from the fungal spires, from the ripple of a spore cloud's dispersal, I find kinetic urges within myself I didn't know existed. A hundred flexible appendages unfurl and pull me forward, where mere moments ago, I moved on limbs like stalks of dandelion stems.

As I move, eyes begin to bloom across me like stars peppering an ever-expanding dark sky. Certain of these irises are calm, cool, deep blues that drink in the mists. Others are fiery and sharp, facets blinking wildly toward moving blurs that dart alongside me in the undergrowth. They are not just senses—these eyes *feel*. Through them filter textures of emotion I cannot separate from the sights they take in. Joy refracts, splinters with the fluorescence of these organisms I almost but cannot quite follow. Curiosity blooms in tandem with every glow of water pooled in the grip of a vine.

I sense the borders of hunger somewhere within myself—but not as I might have once understood it. It is soft, calm, functional—a call to rejoin the endlessly shifting fabric of life here. Rather than break this world apart for sustenance, I take of it as it gives to me. I unfurl tendrils from myself—part root, part membranous flower—and sink them gently into the breathing soil. Nutrients flow upward like a whispered song, revitalizing my form. What is taken is returned in unseen ways—I can feel the subtle exchange, a balance maintained. I am neither predator nor prey. Consumption and growth blur into symbiosis.

Moments—or maybe ages—later, I find myself coalescing into something deer-like. Long, reed-thin legs bounce and flex with unnatural spring. I've adopted this slower, bounding gait in response to the terrain. My torso is light and hollow, riddled with tube-like sacs that inflate and deflate with every leap, propelling me over lichen-draped ridges and rivulets. Upraised stalks extend from what might otherwise be considered a spine—narrow towers that reach for the misted canopy above, capturing and distilling light into a spectrum my refractive organs weave into fluorescence. It's not necessary for function. It just feels right. Like a gift to the ever-thirsty beauty around me.

A blur, ever so barely distinct from the tangled greenery, joins me. Another being. Or maybe several. They shimmer in the haze of this place, their edges too fluid to call real or finite. We do not speak in words, but in something more immediate—a blend of sound and light and shifting biological cues. One body leans toward mine and blinks out an array of flashing signals along the curling spirals growing from what could be their shoulders—or perhaps their neck. I feel the hum of this message, some emotion that overlaps excitement and invitation. My many eyes blink answers across my body in return, and the chorus of our response fills a broader silence around us, as if the spires and rivers themselves approve.

We are moving together. Faint vibrations in the ground beneath our sway tell of something coming. A storm of spores from an unseen corner of the Vista. I sense it—my companions do, too—as release, as creation. The spores writhe on the horizon where my senses cling, an effusion of newness encased in potential.

I close parts of myself even as others open further. New tendrils uncoil, reaching into the air as the first clouds of spore-dust descend. It clings lightly to me, my heated phosphorescence taking the genetic gift into its flickering folds. There is no urgency here. Change will come if I allow it—when I am ready, when the Vista is ready. My fluid configuration might evolve me further the next time the mists part, for the boundaries of individual and environment are not limits but bonds. New forms might emerge from me, or I might myself return to the fabric of this place. Both prospects thrum in me as simple, sacred truths.

I am no longer sure where I end or where the Wandering Vista begins. All I know is motion. All I feel is this kaleidoscopic dance—at once primal and impossibly sophisticated. And I move within it forever, an ever-changing note in an endless, harmonious score. A part of this fractal symphony that defies reason. A part of what it means to be here. To *be*.
