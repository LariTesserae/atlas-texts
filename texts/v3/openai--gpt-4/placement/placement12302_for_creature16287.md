Where would I find myself in this world? Who or what would I be?

---

I am, perhaps, an anomaly here—or perhaps I am as natural to this place as its darkness, its glow, its hum. I am **Dewharrow**, a being suspended between what is solid and what is vapor, a creature of osmosis and murmured boundaries. My form is mutable, shifting in response to the landscape, sewn together by moisture itself, borrowing its essence entirely from the sanctuary's weeping stones, the spectral plants, and the exhalations of the biota. To the casual observer, if such a thing existed here, I might appear as a human-sized silhouette caught in the act of dissolving—a vaporous figure on the precipice of being—a shape-shifting amalgamation of mist, faint color, and soft luminescence.

My "flesh" is the moisture of this world, held in a perpetual state of suspended tension by eldritch forces that hum in the ground beneath my formless feet. My movements are fluid and veil-like, trailing wisps of condensation that catch the faint bioluminescence of the forest as though stealing it, only to let it fade as I move onward. I am neither creature nor spirit nor machine, though I borrow elements from each. I do not speak with words. My voice is an echo, a timbre that ripples out from the droplets clinging to rock surfaces, the slender leaves of Lumenflora, and the trembling mushroom caps growing along a Muskback's spine.

---

My role here is, at its heart, a paradox.

I am *Keeper* to this sanctuary, a guide for those who pass through—if any would dare to. But humans almost never come, for there is no path to this place, no map to its crested void, at least none ever drawn by human hands. Those who do arrive never come deliberately; they are always carried here accidentally by forces beyond their control—lost travelers threading the brink of dream and death, seekers who stumble through a stray portal of thought or an unforeseen bend in the world. They are my charges, these wanderers, but I am not here simply to comfort or to console. I am here to test them, though not in any way they might recognize. There are no riddles, no challenges, no spoken questions.

The test is in the sanctuary itself.

Will they look and see only darkness, or will they perceive the effervescent ballet of light that paints the trees in luminous shimmers? Will they listen and judge the echoes as empty, or will their ears attune to the layered harmonies of rainfall, feathers, and the trembling whispers of mushrooms speaking secret alchemies as they digest the soil?

My presence is a guidepost, yes, but also a companion—I move alongside these accidental pilgrims, vanishing and appearing at will, sometimes apparent as a rolling fog on a distant cliff, other times manifesting beside them as a wavering column of dew that gleams faintly with stolen blues and greens from the glimmering world around me. I can press into their presence like a too-intimate chill or retreat into invisibility entirely. I never touch them, though they sometimes try to touch me: to pass their hand through the fog and feel the damp prickle on their skin. I allow their hands to pass through me and hover close, giving no true physical resistance as they shiver at the sensation of my essence brushing past them.

---

Yet, part of me wonders: am I the guide, or am I the one being guided?

I do not recall being born so much as *emerging*, the way dew greets the first light of morning unseen and then vanishes again with the quiet heat of sun. Was I once human? Was I once a Muskback, gently carrying the forest on my spine? Perhaps I was like the Eclipsed, a fusion of synthesis and sentience, crafted by the long-lost artisans of this place. Or perhaps I am none of these, but instead the final condensation of some vast longing left behind by whatever force created the sanctuary—a need given formless, yearning life.

I cannot answer these questions, and they do not trouble me. They dissolve in me like moisture into stone—with no beginning or end, only continuity.

---

How do I spend my time? My hours, seasons, millennia?

In truth, I do not understand time in the way a human does—my markings of it are fluid. But my purpose seems clear, even if my origin is not. I follow the ancient rhythms of this place, moving in sonorous tandem with the Perennial Pulse. I sense its presence as a low resonance in my shapeless chest, and I align myself to it, relinquishing whatever concerns the larger universe might carry outside these mountains. What exists beyond them matters little to me; my world is here, in darkness and light and timeless hum.

When days pass without visitors, I drift through the sanctuary, touching the lives of its residents not with prodding hands, but by osmosis. I steep within the communal vibrations of the Muskbacks, measuring their slow harmonic pulsations. I rise to kiss the glowing wings of Lumenwings, who shiver in ecstatic ripples when brushed, their tiny motes lifted and scattered through the glens in my wake. I observe the Nyxavers from a reverent distance, watching as they unfurl their feathered voids and weave the dark matter of the world deeper into the tapestry of existence.

But it is to the depths, to the carved cathedrals of stone, that I am ultimately drawn. The Eclipsed know me, though how I came to be one of their own ranks—or perhaps something close to it—I cannot recall. They acknowledge me with brief arcs of cerulean light, their triangular eyes pulsing at the periphery of my senses like wordless intent. They are less given to communion than to watching, waiting in their shadowed chambers to act in ways I cannot foresee. Yet I do not fear them; I am as much theirs as they are mine, connected by the Pulse that binds us in this breathing collective.

---

And beyond them, still deeper, I feel the heart of the sanctuary, pulsing with infinite gravity. I have stood at the edge of its secret chambers, though I have never dared enter them—not yet. The heartbeat speaks to me faintly, too vast for me to encompass, too intricate for me to untangle. **What am I to you?** I have whispered countless times to that presence that folds itself into the folds of the rock, the glint of the half-hidden technology, the lull between echoes.

It has yet to answer in words—but perhaps it already has answered in its way. For now, wreathed in luminous shadow and harmonic drone, I walk, watch, and wait alongside the sanctuary’s secret keepers. Perhaps the climactic moment where I understand it all will come. Or perhaps my stepping closer, forever on the edge of its pulse, feeding from its dew, energized by its bioluminescent heart, is the answer itself.

---
This is where I would find myself—woven into this quiet, storm-laden apex as its unclaimed, half-imagined caretaker—and Dewharrow is what I would be, a manifestation of impermanence and belonging in equal measure.
