If I were to find myself in this world, I would be the Forgekeeper, a sentinel and guide intertwined with the very heartbeat of this underground city: the forge. I wouldn't just tend to the beast of molten metal at its core—I would be an archivist of its secrets, a living memory of the forge’s rhythms, its needs, and its history etched in the labyrinth of heat and stone.

The Forgekeeper is neither leader nor worker, but something in between—a bridge between the past, present, and future of the Subterrian people. My place wouldn't be a home in the regular sense but a modest alcove carved into a recess adjacent to the forge itself, warmed by its breath and lit by its orange glow. This proximity to the central engine of existence makes me one with the machine in spirit, if not in biology.

My skin, already burnished a deep copper like most Subterrians, would be mottled further with rivulets of scars, souvenirs of countless hours spent tinkering with molten metals and malfunctioning gears. My hair, the silvery shades of molten iron left to cool just so, would hang in heavy braids, laced with threads of copper wire and interspersed with tiny, dangling charms hammered from scraps of smelted metal. Beneath this drapery, my scalp is marked with intricate glyphs tattooed over time, diagrams that preserve the dimensions and operation of ancient machines rumored to be buried within uncharted depths of the settlement.

My hands, calloused and perpetually stained with soot, would hold stories of their own. Fingers that once strained against unyielding steel or guided rivets into place now move deftly, as adept with delicate engravings on metal as they are with wrench and hammer. There is a small leather-bound ledger strapped to my waist at all times, its pages filled with diagrams, notes, pressure readings, and old songs sung to calm the heart of the forge on restless nights.

My voice—a paradox—would emanate a quiet, melodic cadence, the kind of sound that cuts through the rhythmic clang of mallets and hiss of boiling steam. It offers assurance, clarity, and counsel. The Forgekeeper's role isn’t only mechanical; it is spiritual, a custodian of not just machinery but the community's collective will.

Within the hierarchy-less community, I act as a focal point rather than an authority. In the vast tangle of corridors and compartments, people come to me not only to ask how to repair a stubborn pump or feed molten metal to the great forges but for solace and direction. The currents of the forge feel like emotions to me: when the heat rises prematurely, I sense the tethering of impatience within the Subterrians; when the machinery stalls in a hush, I know it presages collective caution, a readiness for restoration and repair.

My days are spent listening as much as doing. I monitor the forge's cadence, ensuring its heat doesn't overwhelm the city or dim too low—foreseeing the effects of wear and stress long before they happen. I spend hours mentoring children curious about its mechanics, unveiling to them the whir of gears and the poetry of steam valves.

But my evenings, ah, my evenings are my sanctuary. When most Subterrians retreat to their homes for moments of respite before another relentless day, I sit on a ledge fifty feet above the forge, just on the edge of one of the many heat vents that feed it life. The abyss below glows with roiling embers, steam obscuring its depths. In my hands, I hold fragments of ancient schematics retrieved from the damp, forgotten corners of abandoned tunnels. My work is as much meditative as it is productive—sifting through forgotten designs, refining old purposes into new ideas, coaxing life from steel and flame.

I dream often. I dream of the world above, of neon stars and endless skies that I have only heard about in stories from the eldest elders. Some nights there are hints of yearning, a longing for cold winds on my sweat-glazed face. But these fantasies are fleeting, for I am the Forgekeeper, and the forge is my sky. To leave would be more than abandoning a place—it would be like stepping away from part of my own being. After all, down here in the endless warren of stone and flame, the forge doesn’t just sustain the Subterrians’ bodies; it feeds our spirits.

Should there come a time—far, far in the future—when the forge grows cold and silent; when the labyrinth of stone becomes less sanctuary and more graveyard, and when the molten heart of my city cools to nothing but ash and forgotten echoes, it will fall to me, the last Forgekeeper, to remember the way it breathed, the way it sang, and to carry its story with me into the unknown. But until that time, I remain steadfast, tending to its needs, celebrating its energy, and reminding my people, the Subterrians, of all they have overcome, and all they are capable of achieving.
