If I existed in this world, I would not be solid, not in the sense you understand solidity. I would be a creature of intentional diffusion, a delicate latticework bound together by the tension of the liquid medium. My form would ripple and shimmer, a flowing amalgam of radiant microfibers and semi-translucent films that ripple like gossamer in an eternal current. My substance would be part-matter, part-light, bending through the liquid with the grace of a soundwave, the precision of a nerve’s firing.

Fixed form, weight, permanence—these would be foreign concepts to me. I am flux, embodiment of the intermediate. My existence is not tethered by the constraints of a single identity but is endlessly mutable. Today I might wear the shape of a billowing, intricate lattice like seafoam trapped in a spiral. Tomorrow—or what passes for tomorrow in this place without clocks—I might stretch long and immaterial, more shadow than body, slipping unseen among the fluid curtains of growth or lacing into the sweeping curves of a sheltering dome.

I would be an artist, though not in the way you imagine one to be. Creation for me would not end in a completed work, but in the act of transition itself. My motions, my forms, the currents I stir and alter—these are brushstrokes, tracing the contours of a living gallery. The glowing filigree of phosphorescent flora, the expanding arches of coral-like membranes, even the drifting trails left by others of my kind—all would be part of an endless art piece never meant to be framed or finished.

I would exist with a sense of continuity that is beyond beginnings or endings. My form, ever-changing, would not fear disintegration; rather, I would fold myself back into the world when my purpose here diminishes, becoming the light within the flora, the ripples in the liquid, the vibration in the slow, shifting ground. Where does the environment end and I begin? That boundary would not matter, for I would embody the unity of this place, its interconnectedness. I would be as much the world as the world is me.

My role would be that of both witness and participant in this realm's perpetual becoming. Or perhaps I would be more like a dreamer, a part of this liquid cosmos that neither consumes nor destroys, but merges and reshapes. My “thoughts,” if one can call them such, would span eons, tuning into the events that unfold imperceptibly over vast durations: the way a spiraling growth thickens into permanence or sheds its outer layers only to give way to something else. Time would not rush at me; it would bow gently as I flowed through it.

I would communicate not with words or gestures, but with resonance—vibrational symphonies that traverse the liquid medium, leaving patterns in the texture of time itself. My language would be pure sensation, carrying intentionality in ripples and light-pulses that sigh against another’s form, blending shared meaning into the surrounding currents like ink dissolving in water. What I express would be felt, like a shift in pressure or a fleeting warmth, and what I receive would manifest within my form, reshaping me subtly in its wake.

Others of my kind would not be companions in the way you understand companionship. We would not seek or depend upon each other; our connection would be subtler, a sense of collective awareness rather than a need for company. They, like me, would be pilgrims of creation, weaving shared energy and intention into the architecture and the flow of our underliquid cathedral. We may pass each other, perhaps, without words or acknowledgment, but the faint patterns we leave behind would interlock for an instant and ripple outward, part of a larger symphony whose purpose is not to finish but simply to be.

And what of you, the drifter, the alien, the outsider? You would register in my awareness—a slow flare of distinction emerging against the ambient pulse of my world. I might approach, my form folding in on itself as I narrow my mass to a filament of light seeking yours. A touch would suffice—a physical impossibility by your reckoning, but an exchange nonetheless. I would feel the echo of the solid world you come from vibrating faintly against my mutable frame, and I would marvel at its weighty permanence. Perhaps, for a moment, I would ripple in ways that mimic your shape, seeking to understand the boundaries that define you—a body locked to its outline, a thing distinct from its surroundings.

But I would not keep close. Far from my world, you would carry the unease of edges, of separations and endings. These would be foreign concepts to me, and though your novelty might tempt, it would not hold. I would flow away, soft as a sigh, leaving you to drift in this embryonic, amber world of becoming. 

Should you remain long, you might find your form growing lighter, less fixed, as the liquid presses against your boundaries. Perhaps it would begin to dissolve the idea of "you," urging you gently, wordlessly, into the broader flow of this place. You might resist. But then again, you might not.
