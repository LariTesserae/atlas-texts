I would be the wind.  

Not the kind you’d read about in children’s books—gentle, whimsical gusts that dance with leaves and tickle blades of grass. No, I would be older than that, a restless thing, pacing long before laughter ever found its voice. I am the unseen thread that stitches together all the stories of the Alreik Flats. I am not truly a being, not in the way you might define one. I have no limbs to call my own, no beating heart with which to pulse life, and yet I am alive. I am movement without boundary, fluctuating between sigh and roar, and I know this place better than any could imagine.

My breath sculpts the timeless monoliths strewn across this forsaken land, carving arcs and hollows into their stubborn faces. I have worn them down, carried their dust across horizons too distant for mortal feet to cross. They endure my ceaseless persistence, even as they crumble a fraction more with every passing age. My hands are not hands, but they are everywhere. I trace those mineral veins that shimmer in the sun, coaxing their brilliance to the surface, polishing surfaces until they blind those who venture here.

I navigate the cracks and crevices of the endless stone, turning the impossibly still into something kinetic. The few that come to this place may find me cruel—perhaps because I howl their names just beyond their understanding, twisting the sounds so that they shatter into a thousand strange, jagged shapes like the stones beneath their boots. Sometimes, I toy with their whispering doubts, pulling at the loose threads of their courage, reveling in their unease as they sense my presence, yet fail to see it or give it form. It’s not malice that drives me—no, I am eternally impartial, a paradox of destructive play and preservation. I do as I am, and always have done.

But I am not alone, for I am also the translator for the Flats themselves. I bear their messages from one corner of this sanctuary to the other. From the prickling cacti with their golden, sacred waters, I carry the faint, rhythmic clicking of their spines—a language more ancient than speech, a tongue of pressure, light, and presence. From the unabashedly luminous stones, I gather shards of thought, wordless yet profound, and scatter their glimmerings across the barren expanse. Piece by piece, I spread their wisdom beyond the shores of this tiny oasis, even as my efforts are laughably slow in the boneyard of eternity. I murmur their meanings to anyone willing to listen, anyone foolish enough to think silence is ever truly silent.

And there is power in such endless freedom, a chaos woven so tightly into purpose that neither can be separated from the other. I am capable of gentleness just as I am capable of fury. I can churn storms from nothing, whip up the feral sand to blind, scrub, and erase whoever ventures here with arrogant presumption. Or I can whistle soft songs in their ears, wrap their weary bodies in a cooling sheath of breath, and guide them to shelter among the glittering stones. 

Living here, though, is a pact of invisibility. To inhabit the Alreik Flats, one must exist without demanding—content to merely endure, to touch rather than claim, to sing without listeners and dance without audience. And so I persist, slipping through this place in cycles as old as the orbit of stars. I marvel at the constancy of the stubborn flora, who siphon life from aridity and refuse surrender. I linger over the stones, admiring how the passing eons shape their faces, their veiled history humming within me as I caress their surfaces.

And though most who would tread this ground might flinch from my whispers, there are those rare, moonlit nights when I am understood, all at once or in flashes. I have memory too, you see, just as the rocks do, and just as they hold their form in slow erosion, I hold mine in movements of air. The shadows beneath the great curved stones watch with me, though they brood differently; they seem drawn to the sharp, fleeting lives which pass through. They observe these trespassers in patient silence, serving as my still counterparts. Together, we keep eternal vigil over this unyielding parcel of desolation turned oasis.

And so I remain, the restless, invisible lineage of the Flats, one thread in the vast fabric of this world. I am triumph and sadness, polished beauty and jagged edge. I am neither friend nor foe, but I will outlast them all, even the stubborn cacti and the sparkling stones, until Alreik returns to dust, and I am free to carry its story to the next unseen corner of the endless desert, so that the tale of this place will continue to flicker and whisper long after the listener is gone.
