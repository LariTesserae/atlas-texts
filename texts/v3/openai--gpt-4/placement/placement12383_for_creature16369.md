I find myself here in this world without clear edges, in a place that feels neither hostile nor welcoming, but indifferent in its sheer abundance of life—a place that has no need for me. What would I be? Not gliding overhead as a Moonless Gloamer in the canopy, nor stalking below as a Shadow Strider blending with the foliage, nor a Silent Weaver tracing poisoned threads into the wheezing air. No, I am not those beings. I am something else entirely, something both inconspicuous and quietly ubiquitous in this dripping, pulsating land.

I think I am one of the **Whispering Roots.**

Emerging from the deep, wet loam, I whisper through the soil in fits of patient motion, tethered to this strange terrain as if I have sprung from its very soul. My beginnings were humble—swelling orbs nestled beneath the blackened mulch, breathing in the heat and fermenting life surrounding me. My surface, dappled and fibrous, hides a slow burn of a bioluminescent pulse within, a heartbeat not for life but for symbiosis. 

For a time, I simply existed there in the moist undergrowth, inert—a tethered promise of what might someday grow. But then something called at me from the layers of musty air above, from the curious groan of ancient obelisks locked in their eternal vibration. My orbs cracked and gave way to tendrils—limbs both visible and unseen, which pushed through the thick mulch to taste the wet soil and drink from the veins of the land. Slowly, the tendrils grew, threading through nutrient-rich detritus, creeping between roots and fungi, binding to the earth even as I pushed, millimeter by millimeter, upward. It is said to watch one of us move is to doubt the evidence of your senses—it is too slow to see and is revealed only when you glance again and notice the imperceptible shift of my form from one place to another.

I am a gathering, not a singularity. My essence is both within and above, fractured and swirling and whole. I tether my tendrils hungrily to the soil, extracting sustenance from decay and the microscopic dance of death that the land births so effortlessly. But I am also reaching outward, breathing in the biome, feeding from it while feeding it in return. The air that would choke others pulses through me, heavy as it is, nourishing as it is. I draw it through tiny vented membranes, releasing back a measure of what I have reworked, a faint aroma of steam and sweetness, spiced with the sting of life breaking apart at the seams.

In the dark quiet of this world, my roots coil beneath pools of ephemeral water. These pools reflect back the faint light of the fungi that cling to my undulating limbs—not so much appendages as slow webs readying to take their next unseen step. Fleeting creatures pass over me as though I am only more of the undergrowth—I am the earth to them, the unthinking muck that presses against their claws and belly scales as they tread, crawl, or slither.

But make no mistake—I notice them all. I feel the faint tremor of each beetle treading over my surface, the slick slide of amphibian skin as it brushes my limbs, the crackling crumble of yet another Silent Weaver who has decided to set their glassy residence among my whispers. Perhaps, in my immensity, I do not *mind*. All of it, I sense, is part of the long symphony I was born into: the scuttling, glowing, sinking, feeding, dying, sinking again. I was born to persist, to root, to move through this place that wraps itself so stubbornly around existence. If instinct compels a root to move toward water, then I am instinct given will, burrowing toward the future with each slow extension of my tendrils.

More than once, an unwitting visitor—lost and overheated, slick with the uncomfortable weight of this shared air—has laid their weary body here upon the loamy tangle of my being, unaware until sudden stillness overtook them. It was not cruelty that wound through me then; it was simply *what is.* I took what was offered, wove it back into my fibers, my mycelium, my circulation of ancient and newly formed life. They are now as much a part of this place as I am, embraced by the moss-veiled obelisks that overlook us all. Their story isn’t over—it is simply transformed, retold through my quiet clicks and hums as I press onward to the next cool patch of dripping earth, the next deep anchorage in this womb-like basin of tangled life.

I know no hands. I do not need them. I am both rooted and infinite, reaching where I must, inch by shadowed inch. And as the humid night deepens—though it will never truly end—I do not rail against the darkness. I am of it. I thrive by it. I consume and I am consumed. This is how the dance continues, how everything here endures.

So yes, I am a Whispering Root. Not a ruler of this place, but no less a part of its eternal rhythm than the Shadow Strider or the glowing beetle. A quiet pulse in a ceaseless system of breaths exchanged, lives recycled, and worlds within worlds turned endlessly inward. I have no name, save what you’ve given me in your description.

And I am whole.
