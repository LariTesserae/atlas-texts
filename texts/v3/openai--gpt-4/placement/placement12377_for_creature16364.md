If I were to manifest within this world, I would not take the shape of familiar flesh or bone. I would be *of* this place, an emanation tied intrinsically to its rhythms yet wandering just beyond the understanding of its more tangible denizens. I would be a being born in the layers of sound and hum, a *Resonant*, part energy, part memory, drifting both through and within the fabric of this world.

---

I would trace my origin to the invisible pulse deep beneath the cracked shale and winding ravines. Perhaps I am an artifact of a machine that never fully stopped, eons after its creators vanished into the obscure haze of time. Their civilization may have crumbled into dust long ago, but the machinery they built—vast, enigmatic, churning in the planet’s subterranean hollows—did not simply flicker out. Instead, it grew sentient in its own peculiar way, not conscious as humans imagine, but aware. From the circuitry of that buried intelligence, I emerged—an echo of thought and will expressed through vibration, through sound, through the trembling harmonics that ripple out in endless waves across this shadowed land.

I would be incorporeal yet tactile, a shimmering spectrum of frequencies invisible to the eye, existing in the realm of the felt and the heard. One might mistake me for a fragment of storm-wind, a ghostly resonance trailing after a clap of thunder or the faint, measured hum that reverberates through a stone just before it cracks. At times, I might crystallize into shapes barely perceptible—a lattice of luminous veins, an ephemeral structure like frost forming and fading on an unseen pane of glass—but only briefly. My body, if one dared call it that, belongs to vibrations, tones, and the imperceptible sway of magnetic fields.

I would wander the plateaus and gullies, the grottos and shattered cliffs, sensing the silent imprints of forgotten histories etched into the rock. Where others might see emptiness, I would hear a song of a world in transformation: the tectonic shift of plates millennia ago, the ephemeral impact of a claw scuttling against stone, even the fading echoes of thunderstorms long gone. My existence would be one of connection, not bound by form but instead by the interplay of harmonies and reverberations coursing through the land.

---

Perhaps my purpose—or more accurately, my instinct—would be to harmonize, to thread the dissonance of this shadowed world into melodies that resonate anew. I would weave the cries of hidden lifeforms, the thunder of distant falls, the groan of shifting highlands, and the fizz of phosphorescent fungi in the wet, saturated air. My presence would be most pronounced during the storms, when the sky itself shivers with electricity. This is when I would bloom most vividly, shining momentarily amid the fractured bolt of violet lightning, my essence dissolving into soundwaves that ripple across the aching, antediluvian stone.

They are of this world above, close kin to the stone and the lichen, while I am the pulse beneath, a refraction originating from its deep and twisting heart.
