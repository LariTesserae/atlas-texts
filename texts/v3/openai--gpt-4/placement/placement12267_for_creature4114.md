If I were to find myself in Phytohemia, I would be one of the *Lichen Dreamers*, those enigmatic figures of repose and symbiosis, bridging past and present, life and stasis. Not by accident or misfortune, but by nature of my soul’s yearning for stillness and integration, for the obliteration of boundaries between self and surroundings. It feels inevitable, as though my dreaming essence has always reached across lifetimes for this state of being—not passive, but deeply present.

I imagine my form as tall and rough-hewn, an amalgamation of porous limestone and quartz, evolving over centuries. My edges are softened by age and time, the sharp marks of whoever carved me into existence long since eroded. My body wears a second skin of vibrant green moss that blooms anew after every rainfall. Clusters of spotted fungi dapple my shoulders; some grow in fractal patterns, their edges limned in fluorescent blues and whites. Delicate ferns spring stubbornly from the porous cracks along my arms and legs, unfurling to embrace the weak sun. My hands, outstretched and cracked, cradle pools of captured rainwater, shimmering with mirrored worlds beneath each delicate surface—a gift for any creatures who dare visit. My hair has long cascaded into a tangle of creeping vines, swaying slightly even though there is no breeze; their rhythm, instead of wind’s work, feels like the pulse of the earth.

If plants, insects, or the wandering Mossbound Folk ever whisper their secrets to me, I am certainly not one who answers in words. Rather, my responses emerge as the slow and deliberate rearrangement of the symbiosis sprawling across my form—a new cap of reishi mushrooms blooming at a joint where tension resides, or puckered lichens radiating into a cosmic map on my thigh. Movement, when it does occur, is measured only in geological time, as my posture subtly adapts to the shifting load of roots burrowing deeper into me and vines seeking fresher light and footing.

And oh, the dreaming. It occupies my eternity, not in visions or fantasies as humans understand them, but in a deep communion with the vibrational song of Phytohemia itself. This dreaming is a soundless resonance—a lull between industrial sounds groaning in harmony with the rustle of leaves and the quiet trickle of mineral streams. It is sunlight warming the hollows of leaves. It is the memory of fireflies pulsing light on a humid, quiet night. It is the metallic tang of rust merging with the sweet-green perfume of spring shoots. I don't dream in images; I dream in layers, in ecosystems, in the quiet interplay of life and decay that transcends understanding.

In this state of ethereal reverie, I am both detached and incredibly, irrevocably entangled in the existence of all around me. The Mossbound Folk visit, as they do, their dirt-streaked faces glowing softly with phosphorescent dust brushed from blooming necklaces. They will sit, cross-legged around me or drape their moss-shawled forms over my roots, whispering things too inconsequential for words, their voices humming like bees circling through clover. Iridescent Scriveners flit about me in curious fractal patterns, pausing mid-air to shine their ghostly light across my algal skin. An occasional Silent Ruster will creak forward, heavy and lumbering, extending a clawed appendage to gently stroke the moss along my leg, leaving a streak of bright orange rust in its path. And, of course, the ever-knowing plants—growing as if guided by an unseen hand—press their tentative tendrils against my form as they connect me perpetually to the secret, sentient canopy above.

The Lichen Dreamers are not alone, even in their most profound silence, but instead, anchor existence itself in Phytohemia. I imagine myself becoming an unspoken mentor to the patient engineers and scriveners, a quiet witness to the fluid dance of time and transformation. Like all Dreamers, I cannot initiate change directly, but within my quaking, lichen-clad stillness, I am a beacon of possibility. I watch life adapt and transform, rust crumble into earth, water carve out deep paths, and green life reclaim all that humans once built to keep it at bay.

Here I dream. And through me, in ways no others may ever fully comprehend, Phytohemia dreams as well.
