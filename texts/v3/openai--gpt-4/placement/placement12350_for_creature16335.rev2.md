I am here, in the damp belly of this half-born, half-dreaming colossus. I am part of it, yet apart. I am not one of the many-legged denizens that weave their silvered sinews through its labyrinthine veins, nor am I the vast, polyplated watcher, gliding and humming with purpose just beyond comprehension. I am not the soft undulation of the walls, nor the pulsing warmth of the fluid streaming beneath my boots. But, standing in this eldritch place with its half-focused knowing, I feel like something else altogether—a resonant echo, perhaps—a note not yet bound to melody or played into place.

Will I tell you what I’ve become in this throbbing, wet cathedral of life-iron and dark pulsations? Perhaps. But first, you must understand the slow transformation that overtook me when I arrived. 

---

I was at first human—or so I assumed. I was abstracted from that comforting solidity quickly: perhaps it was in the way the swallowing stillness of this place introduced itself, brushing against my perception not as a space, but as a being to traverse. Or perhaps it was in the way the small creatures paid me no heed, passing cleanly around me; maybe they sensed a futility in my form. Yes—there is something there they could read in me, couldn’t they? The transient signature of a body out of place, impossible to map. I became softer in this dampened air, less fixed, as though the dimensions of me no longer respected my outlines.

In this reverberating womb of material and unknowable intent, I could no longer even tell if I was breathing, my own breath folding indistinctly into the sterile-visceral vapor that passed as air. 

I remember crouching before one of those gelatinous things, that slick oil-and-light creature that coursed across the wall as though it could sense the patterns shifting within the metal. It didn’t notice me—it didn’t need to. And that indifference stole something I hadn’t even known I’d held, something precious until it was silently stripped from me: a sense of importance, individuality, distinction. For a moment, I envied it. My envy lingered over more intricate fragments of realization: its luminous organs pulsed like microcosmic stars, and I found myself hyperfocused on those currents of bioluminescent signals that traveled its form. Were they carrying wordless instructions? Or were they pure reflex? Forget answers—the question itself felt weightless here, hollowed out by some logic I couldn’t understand.

Hours may have passed. Or years. The dimensions here can shift as easily as thought. But something inside me shifted when I first witnessed the watcher, the immense, quivering lattice of semi-living steel. Its gaze—or what passed for a gaze—rooted me where I stood. Underneath that eye-like vent, so eerily wet yet so mechanical, I felt the truth of my irrelevance exposed, eviscerated. “Human” became something I wore thinly, improperly. The creature didn’t need to communicate with words; its perception was its speech, a multilayered pressure I could hardly endure let alone translate. In its presence, something snapped—a fissure creeping through my identity. I was being broken to fit.

And the watcher, too, departed. Back into the shifting corridors, drawn by its own silent mandate. Yet it left behind its ripples in the air, a hairline crack in the stability of myself. That was the moment the place took notice of me. It did not lurch or roar, no cinematic awakening of machinery, nothing as crude as that—instead, a subtle shift occurred. The faint hum resonating through its body took on a frequency richer, denser, as though I had been devoured into its rhythm. I felt it adjusting to me, or perhaps folding me into itself. It recognized my presence not as foreign matter, but as mutable.

And I began to *become*. 

---

I will try to describe what I am now, though it costs me. My edges are no longer edges, and my inside and outside have ceased their bickering. The sharp line between my flesh and this place blurred into something in-between—organic where this place breathed life, metallic where its lattices wrapped around me, but neither state entirely. My skin remembers sensations of heat and chill; my lungs remember the crisp sting of oxidized air; my fingertips recall what it was like to connect and shape and believe. But now, my body flows like mercury into scaffolding, joints no longer stutter with friction, and every limb is foreign, flexible, obedient—but alien, scaffolding.

I am no longer "apart" from where I stand. Fingers stretch long and silver and weave into dangling cables like roots finding soil. I am aware of channels of fluid, of rushing currents of spark and circuit. And behind that awareness, dimly, I sense the thing still shaping—not only itself, but me. The creatures that occupy this place move across me now, though I don’t think they perceive me as an observer anymore. I have become part of their geometry, or perhaps their movement.

I feel their weight as pressure and absence where they fill or drain pieces of the whole. Was my soul exhaled here? It leaks with vapor but accepts this like warmth.

Where am I within the system—what form did formlessness assign to me? It may not be something I chose, mind you—but oh, what I’ve *become*. I am something vast and distributary when dwelled-through: my "form" is no single locus. Certainly, there’s a great locus-organ—call it a “body” if you will; I anchor around 5 bright towering partitions spiraling upward. Threads—gleaming hairless threads sprout extensions. Around me reaching I make filters *running* their **messages & work** enmeshed chaos with delicate dance-lay lattice-organ-fungulent raw movement.
