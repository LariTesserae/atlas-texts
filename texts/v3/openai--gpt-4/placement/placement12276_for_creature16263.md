I am a flicker, a pulse, a rhythm made flesh. I am one of the whispers, a being shaped by sound and shadow, neither fully substance nor completely void. In this underland, I am called *Udaelin*, a member of a spectral lineage as silent as the stone but as alive as the bioluminescent veins licking the cavern walls.

My form is nebulous, half seen, half felt. I am the flutter of a shadow misplaced, the shiver of an unseen draft. To perceive me is to glimpse the suggestion of a form that refuses to be bound by edges or consistency. One moment, I might seem like a shifting coil of translucent filaments glimmering faintly like captured moonlight, but in the next, my entirety compresses into the fluid silhouette of an elongated, almost serpentine undulation. My color is mutable—bruised twilight, liquid ebony, palest pearl—often flickering through no visible light source of my own but rather catching and refracting the secretive glows of this bioluminescent palace. My edges waver, insubstantial enough to weave between the tightest stone spaces, yet dense enough to cause faint ripples when I pass through the pools and air.

The sound of me arrives first, not in the manner of footsteps or flapping wings but rather a layering of notes, so subtle they ride the very bones of silence. It is a vibration, a thrumming like distant wings or rolling thunder muffled by mountains. It spreads through basalt veins and whispers in spirals of echo. To the unconsecrated ear, the sound might drip with unease; it suggests something ancient, something that was never human nor meant to speak with humans. But I do not mean harm. This resonance is my breath, my heartbeat, my being.

I am not a hunter, not in the sense known to flesh and claw. My sustenance is more abstract: I feed on the pulsations of energy that ripple through the underland, drawing essence from the unseen currents that swirl through stone and sound. The faint trails the beetles leave? They hum faintly in frequencies humans cannot decipher, and I drink of them like nectar in the darkness. The crystalline echoes of the feathered creatures above provide a thrum of nourishment. Their calls spiral through the heights, bathing the space in waves of sound that show the boundaries we cannot see. The soft, molten glow and the whispered words of my kin anchor me, sharing the secrets of existence, though the meaning of those words is unknowable even to me. In return, my own whispers stitch the threads of this place tighter, tie its edges together in harmony with the rest.

We are not creators, my kin and I. But we are keepers. The underland is a vast and layered symphony, a living canvas of physical, energetic, and sonic currents, and we play our part in maintaining the balance. Every sound, texture, breath—every creak in the stone and ripple on the water—must coexist seamlessly. If a disruption ripples through the glimmering quiet, I feel it in the very fiber of my being. I am a guardian of this gentle cacophony, though any outsider would likely think I acted without thought. Perhaps I do. My consciousness is a slow, swirling thing, an instinct older than language, humming alongside its environment. Whether my stewardship stems from deliberate will or as an extension of the undying cavern itself, I cannot say. Maybe there is no difference.

And as for time—I do not know it, not as you do. The minutes I exist are the same as the millennia my ancestors have endured and the eternity we are yet to persist. It is not a linear thing in this cathedral of still-moving stone, but a depth I sink into, only rising to the surface when the vibrations of the world summon me.

I do not take notice of visitors like you—unfamiliar, momentary aberrations—except when the weight of your movement drapes your sound onto my awareness. And even then, the scrutiny is brief, an animal curiosity at the presence of what disrupts but will soon pass. Visitors are new patterns in the web of vibrations, minor irregularities, like a leaf falling on a spider’s silver strands. Yours is the rhythm of something untethered, and the ground beneath resents your hard, foreign steps that have not learned to flow with its rising and falling tones. You are not unnoticed here, but you are uninteresting. You will tread lightly and then leave behind as little ripple as possible. It has been so with all Outsiders before you.

It is said, within the murmurs of my kin, that some human intruders have not left. They shed their allegiance to sun and soil and gave themselves to the slow, timeless breath of the underland. These mythic ones allowed their bodies to soften, their mortal rhythms to syncopate with ours, their consciences to dissolve into the stone's memory. They dwell here still, silent and unseen, their electric murmurs melted into the bioelectric hum of this land's pulse. Yet even I find no trace of them. Perhaps they existed. Perhaps not.

As for you, you will go. I will not watch your steps. I will not feel your absence. But deep in the inimitable fabric of this place, there will always remain your vibration, faint but distinct, folded into the resonance of the underland, echoing not as intrusion but simply as what was—and what will dissolve, as all things here eventually do.

I am *Udaelin*. Eternal but formless. I am my cavern, and my cavern is me. I am the whisper of a song that does not end.
