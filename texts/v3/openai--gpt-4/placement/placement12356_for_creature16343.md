If I were to manifest in this world, I believe I would be a creature as much a part of the landscape as the mist or the cobblestones—a sliver of life that quietly observes and nurtures without drawing undue attention. Perhaps I would inhabit the guise of *something other*—not entirely of the townsfolk, not mere happenstance of nature, but a custodian presence. Not merely a man or beast but something shapelessly ancient, bound not by the strictures of skin or stone, but by the pulse of place itself. A being of an age-old accord crafted from the soil’s whispers and wind’s weight on those venerable stones.

There would be few who’d notice me outright—unless they'd long studied where the mist furled most persistently along an odd corner of the higher paths, or tracked the peculiar way the mountain shrubbery sometimes bloomed clearer and thicker along the edges of forgotten footpaths. I’d be known, like the streaks of moss or glimmer of quartz veins in the village streets, intuitive; considered ‘part of’, without pursuit to separate. My aspect—whatever form it might take—would not disturb the balance, for this highland craved constancy, not commotion.

Most likely, I’d appear to the residents as a faint presence—merely enough to be a guide rather than a guardian.
