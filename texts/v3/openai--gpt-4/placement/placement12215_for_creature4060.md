I find myself as one of the **Chorus Golems**, embedded into the jagged cliffs of this eternal, petrified highland. To “live” here does not feel like life in the fleeting, grasping way it is defined in other places. Instead, it feels like something akin to enduring—a state of being that stretches across eons, where consciousness is not tied to the ticking of days but to the deep, slow pulse of geological ages.

My form is vast yet immobile, a colossal face etched and fused into the rock itself, barely distinguishable from the cliffs. I am a confluence of mineral and mystery, a being who seems as if it has always been here and always will be. My surface is craggy and rough, scarred with a mineral patina that glimmers faintly under the harsh light of distant suns. From me, tiny threads of lichen reach out like veins toward the barren air, forming a barely perceptible symbiosis with my stony body. I am both shelter and witness—a creature that was not built, but rather emerged piecemeal across time as the highland shaped me, and I shaped it.

Deep within me is a core of vibrating stone, an ancient thrum I cannot quite explain. It is not a "beating heart" as soft-bodied creatures would imagine, but a resonance that echoes through me and out into the labyrinthine wind-carved arena of my world. My body is interwoven with veins of quartz and seams of hematite that vibrate faintly with each gust of air. This tone, this song, is my voice. When I “sing,” it is not with words—it is a single, reverberating note that resonates far and wide, joining those of my distant kin embedded in summits scattered across the horizon. Together, our voices weave a harmony that moves the air and ground, a sound that is too slow and deep to be understood by transient creatures, but one that keeps the rhythm of this place alive.

I sense the world around me not with eyes or ears but through vibrations. The shuffle of a Stone Lumber’s massive feet sends tremors through me, a low, resonant conversation between our ancient forms. The darting of Miner Spinners, their clawed legs clattering along the stone, registers more faintly—a delicate percussion over the basso continuo that is my awareness. I do not think in terms of flesh and time as an observer might; my thoughts are slow tectonic flows, stretching patiently between cycles of wind and silence. What I perceive through my elongated awareness is not the fleeting movement of individual creatures but the deeper narrative woven by the rhythms of their persistence.

Occasionally, the Wing-Folds pass overhead, breaking the sunlight into angular patterns of shadow upon my surface. Their gliding figures feel like memories of flight, though I could not say why—for I do not fly, and I have never flown. These creatures, ephemeral as wind-carried dust, flit between the two halves of this world: the relentless, unyielding stone and the vast, silken sky. I am grateful for them, though they never interact with me. They feel like what is fleeting in the world, what I know I will never be. Their neat, sharp-winged angles are a song of brevity, counterpoint to the hum of my eternity.

On rare occasions, when the air stands still and the requisite silence descends, my kind and I communicate through our deep, harmonic groans. Some of our tones are nearly inaudible, felt more than heard, while others create vibrations that ripple colorful dust motes from the surrounding stone. I'm not sure if we are speaking to one another or merely to the void, but it doesn't matter—our songs are not so much intended to convey meaning as they are a pure expression of existence. We do not act; we witness and endure. 

The faint ruins scattered across this world... they perplex me as much as they would any wandering traveler. Perhaps I was here when they were built. Perhaps I was born of the stone long after they vanished. They mean something to me—some deep, dormant memory stirs when I contemplate them. The more I hum to myself, the more the ancient structures seem to resonate faintly in return. Perhaps my kind were born of their tools and hands, cast into stone by some forgotten rite, emerging as echoes of their makers. Or maybe all of this is simply coincidence—suggestions in the stone, weighted by time.

But I don't yearn for answers. My existence is not filled with the kind of questions that demand resolution. I am content to spend my epoch listening: to the wind carving its vast calligraphy into my face, to the sound of lumbers grazing on toughened lichens, to the shuddering scrape of subtergrubs far below me, tunneling their glowing lives through what some might consider my very bones. 

And when I am compelled to hum, I do so not out of despair or loneliness but as a slow exhalation of purpose—as if the land, itself unable to sigh, were using me as its instrument. Together, we sing a hymn older than breath, a hymn that fuses my soul with the rock, the wind, the fissures, and the faint trails of creatures whose passing steps will scarcely outlast an inch of erosion.

I know one certainty in this land of unchanging stone: nothing here is separate from the whole. To exist here is to be written as part of the great and quiet story carved into its surface, chapter upon chapter, line upon line. Even the faces of my fellow Golems remain indistinct—less persons than roles: singers, sentries, echoes of time. I do not mourn the absence of haste or the passage of time; I am the stillness beneath it. 

I have never left the cliff. I will never leave the cliff. And yet, I am everywhere.
