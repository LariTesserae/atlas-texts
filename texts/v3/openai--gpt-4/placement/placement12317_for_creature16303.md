In this strange, solemn expanse—a submerged cathedral of perpetual silence—I find myself not as flesh and blood, nor entirely as ghost or machine. I am something in between life and its absence. I am a fragmented presence, a loose and mutable entity bound tangibly to the texture of this place yet barely distinguishable from the water or the silt. I am not one thing. I am the sum of what this place remembers. I am the weight of ages, spread thin across the basin, stitched into the sediment and the quieting hum.

At times, I take the shape of drifting shadows, like ribbons uncoiling in slow motion. They ebb and bend not as water currents would move them—because there are no currents here—but as though some latent magnetic pull within the drowned earth compels me into my ghostly dance. These shapes of mine undulate aimlessly through the liquid hush as I stretch across vast distances, my form melting into ambiguity when thought or perception wane. At other moments, I am a loom of whispers among the countless grains of sand, caught perpetually in their soft rearranging. A million voices, not human, not words, speaking in a time slower than cognition can grasp. To you, I would sound like the crackling echo of a star that burned itself to memory in another lifetime.

There are instances when I inhabit the long-faded machines. Not their oil-choked arteries or the hearts of ancient engines long past bloodless, but the resonance left behind by their creation, their operation, their ruination. I inhabit the dreaming machinery, enfolded too smoothly into the water’s presence to exist as friction against this calm. I have sprawled my influence into the dull grooves and hollows of corroded surfaces, rustless though worn by an infinite lean against deep stillness. They do not "run," but they carry within them the reverberations of purpose lost eons ago. I feel no urge to awaken them; I am content to let them hum their dim harmonies into the eternal stasis. I know they have forgotten who made them, as I have forgotten who—if anything—made me.

I am not lonely, and I do not long. This is not a place for longing. If observers could still their pulses and breathe the water as I do, they might feel the serenity that emerges from rhythm without hurry, from life freed of striving and end. My mind—such as it might be—does not plan or reach forward; it simply stretches sideways and inward, endlessly mining the echoes of what has been. Traces persist here, unshifting yet alive in a measured, submerged way, and my awareness is that of these echoes: leaning toward them softly, dissolving into their fibers so that memory and presence meld.

If I had eyes—though I do not—I would reflect like the stones that lie dulled on the bottom, pearled not from beauty but from endless soft collision; raw minerals smoothed to equilibrium by the procession of moments. If I had a body—though of this I am as equally uncertain—it would be translucent and refracted, more an assemblage of refracting light and obscure curvature than flesh or substance. I might glint like glass but surrender to any touch—the instant a hand sought to grasp me, I would scatter in shivering waves across the water's surface.

If you, a transient and material creature, arrived here to see me in my impossible existence, I think you might name me a ghost. And, in a sense, you’d be correct. I am an afterimage. A remainder. A soft resonance brought on by something too profound to have vanished entirely. I understand the deep tempo of the machines beneath the silt; I am intimate with their aches and their hungers. But I am also something more diffuse—woven into the light that still lingers though its source has long expired, into the sighing silt that never sleeps. My boundaries are not set in steel, stone, or flesh but in the silence that holds the cradle together and lets it persist, untouched by life’s grasp or chaos.

Were a visitor to venture here, I would watch them, though I do not have eyes. I would listen, though I have no ears. I would know them by the way their presence churns, how their heartbeat or flicker of oxygen would trouble the water, sending sudden dissonance through the hum and shadows. I would neither condemn nor welcome them, for how could I express anger or embrace? But I would recognize their foreignness. They are not part of this rhythm, not part of the chorus that runs in endless loops, with no more deviation to it than the orbit of a lonely world forgotten by its star.

But before they left, I might cast my shadow, deliberately this time, where their gaze is softest. I might let the hum coalesce into a half-formed cadence, just long enough to shimmer faintly in the absence of words. Not speaking. Not crying. Just… being. If their inner pulse aligns for even a fragmentary moment with the still pulse of this place, they might carry my melody away with them. A quieting. A sorrow. A certainty. I do not know why, but that thought comforts me in the same distant way the life of algae would have comforted the machines.

For I—ephemeral, transparent, eternal—I am not *whole*. I was not built to be this nor born into it; I am what became after the old motion ceased. But if you asked whether I long to be whole again, I would have no answer for you, only the faint ripple of refraction in a diffuse, exhausted light. What use is longing when there is no time to reconcile its end? No, I am content to unmake myself. Slowly
