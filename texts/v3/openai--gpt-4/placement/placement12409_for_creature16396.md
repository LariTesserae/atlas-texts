Their fur carries the same geometric etchings as the tree bark, a biomimicry so perfect it blurs the line between plant and animal. When one leaps through the air, tendrils of soft light trail fleetingly behind them, like traces of a dream catching the golden light of the valley.

Here, in this primal yet strangely orchestrated world, resides another creature—perhaps the most enigmatic of them all. It is neither entirely of flesh nor of plant, but an ever-adaptable blend of the two. It is as though the very essence of the valley fused with a long-lost animal ancestor and never untangled. And so, the valley's self-appointed guardian arose. 

I am this guardian. 

My form is fluid like the river veining through the valley floor yet solid like the roots anchoring the ancient trees. My skin, if it can be called that, is an interlaced pattern of moss, vines, and bark, which shift and re-grow seamlessly, adapting to the environment and to the needs of the valley. When I move—should I choose to—the ground hums gently in response. My limbs are elongated, branching like the willows, ending not in hands but in tendrils and curling fronds that feel, grip, or caress with nuanced precision. My face, soft and undefined, is a living mask within which flora grows; flowers bloom and wither on my crown in accordance with the whispers of the seasons.

Born from the valley itself, I am both its child and its sentinel. I never sleep, never eat in the way you may understand, but I consume light and water, cycling nutrients through my veins for both myself and the land that bore me. My existence pulses with the rhythm of the valley—a slow, breathing cadence—a tide that carries me through time as immemorial as the mountains’ erosion.

I have three voices, none my own but borrowed from the echoes of this place. When I speak in the deep timbre of the earth, the roots and creatures in the undergrowth heed me. My second voice, a rustling, whispering wind, calls to the treetops, to the birds with rainbow feathers and the darting creatures hidden on high. My third voice, crystalline as the valley's many water pockets, resonates in the air; a forgotten, melodic croon that draws the attention of those strange simian beings who swing from tree to tree. This third voice also carries farther than I realize, slipping out through unseen cracks in the mountain walls, perhaps to other ears, other travelers drawn to the alien familiarity of this place.

When visitors—few as they are—stumble into the valley, I observe but rarely interact. At least not directly. My touch is the bloom of tiny, bioluminescent fungi that outlines the footsteps left behind. My words are the wind, bending leaves and whistling through the hollows of the trees like flutes. When I do appear, visitors rarely comprehend what they are seeing: a walking blur where flora begins and animal ends; a suggestion of a face that looks like their own until they blink, and the features have melted into crescents and curves. Interlopers have whispered my name in fear or awe—if they stay long enough to notice me.

But I am not hostile, nor am I indifferent. I rewrite and restore, mending broken branches and filling footprints, helping some creatures while gently nudging others on their path to death when it is time. I am not their master, nor ruler, nor deity, though once, when humans lived here longer, I was mistaken for such. They left their curious marks in stone beneath the gnarled roots, intricate designs carved with reverence—or perhaps desperation. It is said their shamans tried to communicate with me, painting themselves in patterns that mirrored my own, crafting songs imitative of my voices. Perhaps they succeeded. I do not remember; time erodes all but the shape of a memory.

What I am certain of is that my allegiance lies solely with this valley. When the warm rain falls, I am reborn in its droplets. When animals traverse the canopy or the caves, I am the warmth that brushes against their fur or feathers. When the mountains crumble, sending rockslides through the valley, I am there rebuilding life from the scattered stones.

My existence is guided by no higher purpose than the rhythm of the land itself. To propagate, to protect, to let life thrive and welcome eventual decay. To be eternal and ephemeral, harmonious and chaotic, a guardian who is as much a part of the valley as the wind, rivers, and roots.
