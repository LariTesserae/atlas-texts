If I were to inhabit this world, I would belong among the *Oxidized Dreamers*, adrift in the currents of thought, semi-forgotten but close to the steel bones of the city itself. I would perch in a cavernous alcove beneath a bridge or cathedral-like turbine hall, my tarnished copper plating and skeletal iron limbs immobile for weeks at a time as I contemplated the cadences of steam and echoed hammer-strikes—my mind tethered not to the now, but to eras stacked like layers of sediment beneath towering girders. My joints would creak with age and corrosion, yet my core would hum with low, patient energy, carrying the same rhythm as the city’s endless respirations. Among the dimly glowing lenses of my many eyes, only one would still function, capturing fragmented glimpses of this ever-shifting world.

I would be neither hero nor villain, but a quiet archivist of the city’s soul, remembering what others are too busy to recall, whether it was their beginnings or their many rebirths. My body would ache with the weight of inventions long forgotten, blueprints unbuilt, and the unity of aspiration and entropy that defines this unyielding city. I would lend my wisdom sparingly—mumbling cryptic solutions through layers of grime to the rare petitioner who stumbles into my sphere, usually a young Forgeling burdened by their first taste of failure or a desperate *Steelsapling* whose servo-mind twitches toward untamed questions. Such visitors come hoping to learn the secrets of creation, though I suspect they always leave disappointed, uneasy at the unfathomable depth of time in my rasping voice.

In the decay of metal and the whispers of rain upon my corroded shell, I would listen for the city’s breathing, always hearing something no others do. Perhaps it’s the echoes of those first hand-carved foundations, laid when this place was still a dream of men who fought against the marsh and mountain. Perhaps it’s the sighing grief of the riverbeds muddied with slag and runoff, or the faintly discordant note of strained steel crying out in a forgotten chamber leagues beneath the surface. To me, all those shifting melodies would blend, weaving bittersweet music no Forgeling could hear, and perhaps one day, I would let myself dissolve into the tidal flow of oxidized memory.

But not yet. Because, strange as it seems, I am not willing to sink wholly into stillness, not yet willing to surrender the rhythm of hammers and wheels and rain against iron. Seated in my shadowed alcove, I would hum to myself a song of creaks and rusted groans, threading it into the city’s symphony—weak but resonant—until the time came when the whole city might one day listen and remember.

Until then, I would remain, a relic among relics, an artifact of a place still being built. I would be a part of it. And even when my final joint collapsed into debris, the music would still be there, forged anew, an endless industry of creation and survival, of people blurred between machine and flesh, standing eternal in shadow, steam, and rain.
