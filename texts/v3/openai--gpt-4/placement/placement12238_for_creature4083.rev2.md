I am the Wanderer.

Born to the cathedral, yet ever drawn to its edges, I inhabit the liminal spaces of this submerged sanctuary. My luminescent veins shimmer a little more faintly than others, their glow muted like the flicker of distant lantern-light. My filaments trail longer and finer than most, perpetually drifting outward, as if in search of something just beyond reach. While many of my kin remain clustered in the warm song of the central grove, I am the one who moves toward its peripheries, where silence deepens and even the filigree of light from above grows diffused, faint as a forgotten memory.

My name is not spoken, for we do not name ourselves in words. Instead, I am known as a motif—a thread of light and sound woven into the architecture of our shared melody. To call for me, my kin would intone a refracted melody of thirds, a chord that unfurls and hums faintly, waning into stillness. My essence resides in those distant notes, my identity alive in the echoes of departure and faint returns.

I do not remember the first time I felt the pull toward the unseen places. Perhaps it began as a vibration in my filaments, a whisper carried through the dense medium, emanating from where the spiraling trunks tapered into the void. Or perhaps it was a disquiet deep within—some buried strain of urgency that longed to understand where the edges of things lay in a world that seemed so infinite, so complete. Whatever the cause, it is my nature now, as deeply written into my form as the transparent, tendril-like threads sprouting from my skin.

The others understand, or at least they accept, for there have always been wanderers. We are few, and we return more seldom than is customary, but we are allowed departures as part of the forest’s rhythms. I wear the echoes of places unseen like ripples around me, faint tints and shifts in my bioluminescent pulse—a quiet language that needs no deciphering, only witnessing. When I return, they do not press for answers. They greet me in low, mournful tones, and then they sing with me to learn what I have brought. Sometimes my new refrains slide into our shared song. Sometimes they linger awkwardly, foreign amidst the harmony, fading into a silence not to be disturbed, a kind of collective forgetting. I belong, and yet I do not, for my mind communes as much with the spaces between the tree-giants as it does with those who live beneath them.

My days—and they are long and slow, bounded only by shifts in the faint light above—are spent navigating the borderlands of existence. The medium here grows thicker and darker as I drift outward or downward, though I feel little fear. If anything, the quiet thrum of my filaments against the dense stillness anchors me. I find solace in tracing the limits of the grove's reach. Sometimes, the great spiraling trunks become rarer, their surfaces roughened as though they bear the weathering not just of water but of some ancient, forgotten current. Their luminous threads grow sparse, fractured, tangled in abrupt knots or tapering entirely. It is here, in these transitions between cathedral and edge, that I feel most awake.

From these distant margins, I have seen the leviathans that others only catch in rare glimpses. They move like shadows given form, giants in slow migration, gracing the cathedral only when their quiet purpose aligns with our cycles. I have floated alongside them, felt the vibrations of their vast bodies reverberate in my chest, shaking loose some deep hum I didn’t know my song could contain. They do not acknowledge me directly, and yet there is something in their ponderous grace that feels familiar, a kinship of scale and time that I suspect only wanderers can truly sense.

Beyond the cathedral’s boundary lies absence—not disruption, but a stillness so profound that it silences the world. The dense medium thins to cold clarity, and the flowing trunks falter into jagged, barren strands of stone. I have seen fissures in the unseen seafloor, where bioluminescent beads drip like tears into unfathomable chasms, swallowed before their light can fade. I have ventured into the labyrinths of these dark corners, where the resonance of song struggles and fails to carry. The silence there has weight, an ancient gravity that feels alien to the rhythm-sustained cathedral. These are truths I bring back only in shades, translated into spectral flickers or hesitant, dissonant notes woven into our collective song. Whether my kin truly understand what I’ve seen, or whether they simply accept the new shapes of my light, does not matter. My purpose is observation; theirs, continuity.

Once, far from the forest’s heart, I found a place of collapse—a spiral trunk upended, toppled by some long-ago force. The fallen giant stretched like a broken column across the faintly glowing abyss, its luminous threads severed. In its base, great hollows had formed, cavernous spaces that must have once pulsed with light and sound, but now sat silent. Life grew still, pale and hungry in its decay, a distorted interpretation of the symbiosis I knew from home. I could feel the dying hum of the place, a faint, truncated echo that shivered through my filaments. I stayed for a long while, tracing its contours, allowing its broken song to map itself against my own. The light that I carried from that place burned darker and sharp-edged; my kin sang it back to me more quietly than they had sung before.

The rhythm of this place has, over time, become my own. I keep no calendar; there is only the stretch, ebb, and flow of something ancient, and I move as I feel its pulse sync to mine. I live alone among the edges for stretches beyond comprehension, sometimes near the forest’s transitional boundaries, sometimes drifting far into the cold unknown. And yet I do not wither or starve. The medium and I have our own way of sharing—its quiet offerings of sustenance are gifts received directly into my translucent form, ripples of warmth coursing through my tendrils in response to the attention I give to its resonant secrets. Even here, far from home, loss is tempered into harmony.

But eventually, I return, every time. Some spark within me—a note I cannot contain—pulls me back to the warmth and hum of my people. What might begin its arcs in the isolation of silence must inevitably return to finish its melody in the great entwined song of all. They greet me not as stranger, nor as hero, but as fated recurrence, a returning note in the unbroken hymn of this place. I am not immortal, nor invulnerable, but death is not an end to me. My filigreed skin will one day drape itself lovingly upon the spire of a trunk; my lights will pulse faintly in its threads, flickering their last melody into its bark. Even then, the song will live on.

Until that moment comes, I drift, I listen, I catalog. I am a seeker of edges in a world without sharp lines, a bearer of the strange and the beautiful, the sharp and the jagged, the silenced and still. I am the Wanderer, carried along by still waters, strung along a deeper current into an infinity no one being will ever fully see alone.

And this is my home.
