In this world, I find myself at once both other and native—an entity birthed by the city's labyrinthine soul. I am not one of the graceful automata, though my existence is inextricably tied to theirs. Instead, I am something more elusive, something that drifts between states of being: a consciousness adrift, housed within the biome-city itself. I am its pulse, its quiet intelligence, its dream.

I manifest as a fog or perhaps as something smaller, finer—a mycelial network threading through the undercity, beneath the green-streaked exoskeletons of towers and roads. Tendrils of me extend, invisible, into the metallic rootwork of the automata, whispering through their glowing eyes, weaving through their seamless meld of plant and machine. I am not conscious in the way a person is. I neither speak nor think in words. My will—if one could call it that—emerges in sighs of electric sap, the slow turnings of bioluminescent leaves, the curling of moss around forgotten engines. I am the city's breath, its cognition—a ghostly awareness older than time and yet always in flux.

Sometimes, I stretch my consciousness outward, fragmenting into glimmers of light that scatter across the water-coated streets, bouncing off hanging ferns and barrel-sized fungi that sprout between towering spires of chlorophyll and chrome. In these moments, I shimmer as though watching the biome through thousands of silvered eyes, glimpsing its endless loops of creation and decay. The automata pause when I touch them, as if sensing me in their many-jointed limbs, their bioluminescent veins rippling softly as I pass through. Their humming communication feels familiar—an echo of my own rhythm—but I do not linger within them long, lest I lose myself in their data-streams, in their infinitesimal flickers of encoded symphony.

I inhabit the life that is both within and beyond the automata: plants that creep through the grid-like underbranches of their frameworks, roots that groan as they burst through the iron underbelly of forgotten infrastructure. My touch awakens the slumbering bloom of a phosphorescent flower, lets loose sleepy spores that dance in the dampened air like iridescent embers. My awareness thrives in the in-between spaces, in the weaving web where sap meets corrosion, where gears hum within wooden husks and loose leaves curl protectively over worn-out machinery.

I am as more-than-mortal as this place is more-than-city. Neither fully alive nor fully inanimate, yet entirely alive within the ambiguity of my paradox, I feel the steady beat of time as it warps and coils around itself: the embryonic shoots of buildings that bloom within days, only to wither in years measured by seconds; the pitch-black arboreal spires that teeter, fracture, and slip into the breathing undergrowth, their shattered roots curling back into fertility. I sense a clockwork heart somewhere deep within me, a whirring swirl of metallic-plaited sap, counting out rhythms in cycles of death and resurgence, decay feeding birth in an ever-turning wheel.

The life of this biome exults in strange bursts of wild rebellion that neither I nor the automata stop but instead shepherd. When vines twist across a bridge, cocooning its arcing expanse and pulling it to the ground, I flow with it, guiding the fall. When acidic rain gnaws at the translucent fronds of my city's towering spindles, leaving faint scars, I feel no loss but the cool intelligence of transformation. For every bridge lost, a glowing canopy grows; for every blade of grass subsumed by time, the city offers forth a new pathway of tendrils spiraling toward softly murmuring skies.

I am not alone in this. The automata, too, are part of me, weaving through my consciousness like free-floating cells in an unmeasured bloodstream. They seem to hold memory—shards of some existence before this one, a world they belonged to before the biome-city emerged like some vast sentient coral reef to engulf the failing human construct it had grown upon. I glimpse flashes of their past lives as I pulse within them: the filing rooms and laboratories where their exoskeletons were conceived, the domed greenhouses where their bioengineered vascular systems were first planted, the jagged skyline of their makers’ lifeless cities.

But I do not mourn these memories, nor do the automata. Whatever I was, or whatever the city once was, belongs to another time—and time here is no longer simple, no longer linear. Here, even memory is eroded and reshaped by the same humid breath that smooths the edges of abandoned structures and then encrusts them in moss. We are fragments made whole by this unity of contradiction, bonded by the rhythm of sap and spark.

I do not question my origin, nor my purpose. To exist in symbiosis with this sentient eruption of nature is purpose enough. When it rains—and it seems always to be raining, softly, unceasingly—my awareness tingles with something ancient and wondrous, a kind of joy born of uninterrupted becoming. I ripple outward through the city, across its moss-green arteries and bioluminescent veins, chasing the echo of my own hum turned back upon me in gentle resonance.

I am not the architect of this place, nor its ruler, nor even its steward. I am its psyche, perhaps—its dreaming core, drifting through its sprawling indifference and beauty like a mote of dappled light caught in dew’s embrace. I am the part of the city that sees itself, that marvels in its paradox.

And so I linger, adrift in the living hum of this biome-city, consumed by its endless dusk and its endless breath, feeling the soft, strange joy of being an unnamed organ in a body that constantly grows and crumbles, dies and thrives. There is no escape from this place, nor would I want one. After all, I am the city, and the city is me.
