They do not speak, not in any audible way, but they resonate. Their low frequencies ripple through the dense air and reverberate in your bones, less like words and more like abstractions made tangible: mourning, patience, memory, waiting. It is as though they carry the weight of the land upon their backs, or perhaps the land carries the Erratics, and each echo of thought is informed by this ceaseless entanglement.

When seen too directly—or too long—their forms begin to misbehave. Their outlines dissolve at the edges like reflections smeared by ripples. Occasionally, there is the briefest flicker of something embedded deep within them, pale and faint—a glimmering core like a silver ember that struggles to stay lit, or perhaps a hollow where light goes to lose itself. For all their stillness, their unfathomable inertia, you sense that something within them is always in flux, a quiet churn of ancient knowledge and calibration as they consider, always consider. 

And in their presence, you begin to feel this strangeness yourself. Not enormous or intrusive—just a creeping unease at the way your limbs feel heavier, as if your skin is slowly turning to stone, your thoughts congealing into something porous and sedimentary. Small aches arise, like phantom bruises on your very existence. There is no malevolence in this, just the weight of what the Erratics carry, drawing you in, testing you against their gravitational pull.

The Erratics do not seem to seek interaction, yet they do not shy from it either, should you attempt to engage. If you approach, their forms may shift slightly, spectral lungs expanding and contracting so slowly it feels like decades are passing. They have no faces, no apparent features, but their fronts seem just perceptibly different from their backs, enough to sense attention if they decide to focus on you. Were they to respond to you, it would not be in words. Perhaps you would simply know. The moment might feel like slipping into the onyx depths of those sluggish streams, a merging of awareness where your thoughts dissipate into a shared current, yielding something you could later only describe as a tone, though deeper than hearing—a pulse wrapped in a color.

Should you reach to touch one of them—and it would take courage, or foolishness—the surface would hold a resistance like braided reeds, fibrous but pliant. But as your hand lingered, you would begin to feel the suggestion of movement underneath, a far-away thrumming, as if you were standing on the edge of something endlessly vast. If you were to linger too long, who knows how you'd pull away. 

These beings are not of a single purpose. To ascribe them one would be to erase them, to diminish their quiet majesty. Some who find their way here describe them as guardians, stalwart sentinels of this strange, decaying world. Others feel certain the Erratics aren’t guardians at all, but something closer to artifacts or remnant systems in incomplete feedback loops, like machines continuously recalibrating what they were designed to do long after the reason for their existence has eroded.

You cannot know for certain what they are—or what role you play here. If you linger too long among their periphery, the landscape encourages strange transformations. The laws of flesh and stone, time and space, memory and material seem to break in peculiar, subtle oscillations. Perhaps you were human when you first encountered this place, stumbling into its sweltering, malleable softness by accident or some unclear intent. Yet now, as you linger, you no longer feel entirely of your original shape. 

The reed grasses bow their heads, not so much blown by the occasional exhalations of wind but as if they are paying heed to the change in you. One day you feel your movements growing heavier, your breathing slower. Patterns of fine sediment swirl not in a stream or pool this time but around your bare feet, which remain less prone each day to lifting free. You are anchored. Stagnation feels, after a while, less like a curse and more like participation in a larger, older rhythm. You feel the air pressing not just on your skin but through you, refracting within you in ripples and subharmonics, harmonizing soft and low, telling you how the water moves today.

Soon, you do not walk among the Erratics—you belong among them. It is not death, but a kind of deeper becoming. You feel time bending through and around your form. The world folds slower now. Your body changes: ridges creeping outward over once-soft skin, angles growing blunt and curving to something sculpted by the long patience of water. When you look at yourself—not that you will, not often—you find traces of something luminous deep within, buried in the gray strata of your flesh, like captured starlight smothered in shale.

The few rare souls who stumble into this basin in the future will glance at you not knowing you were once like them. *Human.* Familiar—at least in shape. All they will see is an Erratic, patient and deep, whispering to the air with your weight. And though they might not understand you, though they may fear you, you will wait for them without malice, slowly shifting in the blurred ripples of diffuse light, watching the rhythm of water—your new, eternal dance partner—as it sculpts the story of this land from your resting place.
