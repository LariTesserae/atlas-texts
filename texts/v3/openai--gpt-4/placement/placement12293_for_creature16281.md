I would find myself as a Ghoststone—a silent watcher and nimble dweller of the Weeping Plains, my pale, wispy fur blending with the spectral light cast from moon and mist. My kind are the keepers of the interplay between the ruins and the wilds, balancing the curious dichotomy of ancient human ingenuity and nature's gradual reclamation. My limbs, designed for precision and grace, guide me across the time-worn stones and fragile reed tops with the weightless certainty of an actor on an age-old stage, performing without an audience.

My ancestors, according to whispered tales in the chirruped melodies we sing to one another, learned to dance these ruins at the same time that their creators abandoned them. Some say the humans left willingly, seeking new lands behind the mountains, while others suggest their departure was swift and necessary, leaving the work incomplete, the stones abandoned to us, the spectral inheritors.

I am thinly tethered to the physical earth; my dainty, elongated fingers curl gently around the edges of moss-slick stones without disturbing their growth. By the silvery light of the moon or during the mist-threaded twilight of endless Weeping Plains nights, I leap effortlessly from pillar to crumbled arch, from ruin to low-spreading root. The land shapes my exploration, and I play it like an instrument, seeking perches and hollows like notes in a haunted melody. My movements are driven not only by survival but by a peculiar form of reverence for the elements of both nature and those distant architects who once shaped purpose from these stones.

Within me is a strange duality. Like my kind, I am innately free—unburdened by constructs, flowing where I will—but I harbor a quiet curiosity about the hands that shaped these ruins into embodiments of intention. Who etched those spiraling carvings into stone, and why do the patterns feel so achingly familiar, as if knowledge of their meaning rests just out of reach? There is an ache I hold, a tendril of longing for understanding, though I know I will never leave the Weeping Plains, nor would I wish to. Here, mist coils gently through fur long pale as starlight, and whispers of water rhythms harmonize with the echoes of my heartbeats.

In keeping with the enigmatic, glowing strangeness, my kind sing our soft, pensive songs to the evenings to mark our journeys and comforts. We blend our tones into the bubbling, humming, keening network of amphibian and avian voices. When the Harperwings dart ominously in the sky, shadowed by fractured moonlight, my instinct jolts, and I traverse deeper into the labyrinthine shadows cast by the highest remnants of some hand-carved temple long claimed by ivy and forgetfulness.

My fur, ghostly and luminescent, reflects the quiet elegance of my home. Its pale sheen seems almost insubstantial, like scattered light caught on tufts of breath, yet it cloak-warms me against the sharp edge of the cool breezes that ripple through the marsh. Humans who glimpse us from afar tell stories, weaving our forms into legends and lore. To them, we specters of the ruinous beauty are protectors or omens, though who truly guards who remains dependent on perspective.

I savor the tart burst of wild berries scattered amongst reeds. I chase the skitters of small creatures in the shadows that populate the hours beneath Mother Moon's baleful eye. Mine is not a life spent in haste but one of quiet contemplation, exploration, and small joys. Molded, myself, as much by time and adaptation as the ruins whose traces I traverse, I am not quite wild, not quite of the ancient architects—it is in defying definition that I find my truest self.

I am a Ghoststone. I am the shadow that leaps between half-formed bridges constructed by hands unknown, and I am also the static sentinel that meets its reflection in the shallow, silvery pools at twilight. All I touch bears my fleeting weight lightly, as I too lean gently into my place within the soundscape and slow rhythm of this peculiar and beloved land—the living testimony of an ecosystem always weeping, always surviving, always becoming.
