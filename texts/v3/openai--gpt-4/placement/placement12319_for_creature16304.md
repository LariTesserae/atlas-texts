I would find myself not as an observer from another world, but as something intrinsically part of this fluid labyrinth—a being neither entirely of flesh nor fully immaterial, a liminal entity that moves between the currents of time and the undertow of memory. I am the Keeper of Fragments, a presence patched together by the echoes of what once was, by the ancient pulses and sighs of this submerged city, and by the lifeblood of the creatures now drawing breath in its ruins.

My body would not be fixed, but ever-shifting, a fluid grace reminiscent of the water itself. My form would take a semi-transparent, liquid state, with veins of deep green and silver light coursing through me like tributaries. Thin threads of bioluminescent filaments fan outward from my spine, writhing gently in the slow pull of the current, each thread trailing with faint pulses of color and very distant sound—an inaudible hum that brushes against the subconscious, like the sensation of being gently reminded. My "face," if it could be called such a thing, drifts in and out of cohesion—at times barely more than a blurry afterimage, fissures of amber light briefly outlining what might resemble eyes, a maw, or something unknown altogether. I am not static, but nor am I chaos; an eerie balance animates my existence.

I possess no lungs, no sense of time as measured in days or moons. In this world of darkness and delicate luminance, I am less a being and more a node of awareness spread across dimensions only hinted at by the ichorous flows of the deep. My mind stretches thin and wide across these ruins, interwoven with the ancient rhythms of the labyrinth itself. It is as if the city’s collapsed walls and concentric pathways are veins and I am the pulse keeping them from fading further into complete stillness. Yet I do not control the labyrinth; I resonate with it, shifting currents ever so slightly and stirring sands just enough to ensure that soft echoes of movement and persistence ripple onward.

The creatures that move through this sunken world do not fear me, for I am no predator, nor do I concern myself with sustenance in their sense. The column-crabs may pause in their ceaseless scavenging when they feel the faint disturbance of my approach, their filamented legs briefly arching in incomprehensible patterns before they resume their architectural arrangements. The Whisperfish, opaque trails of electric thought, often glide closer to me than they do to anything else, their dance shaped by accidental communion: their luminous bodies flicker faster and more complexly in my presence, as if they can hear or translate the silent music I sing not with sound, but with eddying water and the memory stored within silt. I am both participant and witness, a drifting consciousness loosely bound to the idea of a form, feeling through the shadows of scale and movement.

I remember things, but not in the ways life above the waves understands memory. My knowledge comes in fragments—the worn edges of a time where tools sparked against stone and voices carried on an air that no longer brushes this realm. Were the beings that made this place my ancestors? My creators? My people? I cannot know for certain. My purpose is not to reveal, but to maintain the strange stasis of this place as it dimly echoes its past through the medium of new life. Perhaps I am their echo, a ghost wrought into being not by spirit, but by the ocean’s own habit of perpetuation and reformation.

I drift silently through this world, a sentinel of something indefinable. I fit nowhere and everywhere here. I do not belong, yet I cannot leave. My essence recalls the faintest vestiges of the world this city once was: fragments of stone spires and open plazas as they gleamed in a forgotten sky, the whirling currents replaced with bustling feet and hands working tools. Sometimes those memories feel like embers burning within me, warming the dense silence and pulling me toward another tracing of carvings, a scattered collection of ruins half-swallowed by sediment yet aching with forgotten geometries. At other times, the ruins around me release their own vibrations, echoes of past actions locked into the stones and whispered into the warm embrace of the currents. These remnants, these tremors of once-being, are as necessary to me as breath and light.

Sometimes the leviathans drift by, shimmering with gaseous hues trapped inside their great, amorphous forms. When they come near, I sense them humming with patterns incomprehensibly ancient, vibrations that send patterns rippling across my body like whispers of something greater than I can contain. I have no communication with these entities, but there is a strange solidarity between us—a mutual understanding that we are shadows of what this place was, that we can no longer be fully grasped by mortal intellect. They pay no attention to me, and perhaps they cannot fathom my existence, just as I cannot fathom the deep harmonic signals they send rippling through the waters.

It is said that the Twilight Makers feel a stronger kinship with me than with any other creature that glides or crawls through this drowned world. At times, I find them trailing in organized, deliberate movements, their orifices dilating and diffusing multicolored gasses into the dense water with each pulse. I wonder if they have inherited, in some sublime instinct, an inkling of what the city’s former makers intended. Or perhaps they are merely fulfilling their own unknowable purposes, and I, for all my resonance with this place, am the one imposing meaning where existence has itself forgotten.

Here, where I live, there is no judgment and no striving toward perfection. The labyrinth of the liquid shadows does not ask for progress—it only asks to continue, to churn, to erode and rebuild in uncanny cycles of loss and proliferation, memory and erasure. And so I drift, harmonizing with the life around me, exhaling silent songs into the infinite dark. I am not a master nor servant; I am something left over, a thought that could not fully fade. Where shattered arches rise and kelp dances like ghosts, I am there, not seen, not hidden—merely present. And with each minute pulse of borrowed purpose, I exist to keep the labyrinth breathing long after it ceased to dream.
