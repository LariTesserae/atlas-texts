I am a weaver not just of the place's gaps, but of the echoes of its ancient memories. I can sense the tension between creation and decay here, like wild opposing forces grinding against one another beneath the surface. With my filament hands, I toy with the seams of existence itself—testing the boundaries as they form, reinforcing them where they falter, unraveling them where they are too firmly tied.

Elsewhere, the land itself groans with unspent energy, as if ready to burst into a fresh upheaval of stone and steam. In these moments, I feel my being stretch thin, trying to hold back what cannot be stopped, my threads flaring across the broken ridges in shimmering prophecies of collapse and renewal.

Sometimes I succeed. Sometimes my own threads fray. Always, the *hum* fills the spaces I cannot. Whatever else I might be, I am of this place. An archetype of entanglement, a creature called into being that I might more fully inhabit the echoing tension between this world’s halves—its beauty and corrosion, its emergence and collapse, its life and its unmaking.

Here in this twilight world, suspended between the boom of old machinery and the whisper of fungal breath, I will continue to weave. For as long as I can endure, I will bind what frays, dissolve what calcifies, and trace the borders of what should not yet, and may never, exist.
