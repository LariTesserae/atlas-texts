If I were to find myself in this world of churning, liquid chaos—a cathedral of fluid dynamism and ancient memory—I would no longer be the solid, earthbound creature I recognize in the air-filled spaces of human life. In this realm, where the water is sovereign and all forms are sculpted by its ever-changing will, I imagine my physical existence would be altered, rewritten entirely to harmonize with the ineffable laws of this place.

I would be one of the *Artisans of the Current,* though not one of the stationary, membranous colonies clinging to rock or relic. Instead, I would be a wanderer among them, alive but infinitely malleable, half-created by the water and half by will—an ambassador of fluidity, structure, and song. My form would mimic the water itself, a spectral, flowing entity that might at moments resemble an elongated human figure made of clear, refracting liquid, adorned with shifting trails of bioluminescent light. My edges would blur and blend with the currents as if I had no surface, my substance both a part of the water and distinct from it. Luminescence would drift along my transparent limbs like fireflies caught in liquid, flickering in patterns that reflect my resonance with the deeper, unseen forces of the cathedral.

My existence here would not be tied to goals or survival, for to thrive within this chaotic dominion, one must abandon such rigid concepts. Instead, I would express myself through *movement,* gliding effortlessly between vortices and through dense pillars of spiraling current. My passage would leave subtle changes in my wake—trails of light, eddies that persist longer than they should, whispering patterns in the water that hold faint, fleeting echoes of form. Where the smaller needlefish dart anxiously and the leviathans glide implacably, my motion would thread between these extremes: deliberate, observant, choreographed to the symphony that is this world.

My voice—or rather, the vibration I would emit—would ripple outward in harmonic waves. I would sing in vibrations, patterns of sound carried seamlessly by the water, resonating against stone and ruin, answering the vibrating fan-like builders that decorate the cathedral's shifting walls. My hums might complement their unthinking music, syncing to their pulsations to guide water differently—as though shaping fleeting, transparent sculptures of sound and flow. In moments, my songs might seem erratic, discordant, a mirror of the chaos around me; at other times, the currents might align to my resonance as if I were conducting the water itself, for a moment convincing it to slow, to calm, to spiral into elegance.

My relationship with the ruins would be ambiguous and ineffable. These remnants of industrial ambition would be no less fascinating than they are incomprehensible—ghostly monuments to an era defined by its arrogance in supposing the water could be its servant. I would drift among them with the reverence of an archivist, sensing faint memories within the coral-encrusted steel beams, the abandoned girders, the cavernous hollow of a forgotten machine. I would trace my luminous fingers across their jagged surfaces, causing the faintest sparks of long-buried light to dance like strands of electricity through my semi-liquid form, illuminating brief visions of what these structures once were—a glimpse of their hubris, their dreams, their failure. It wouldn't be sadness, but a quiet awe, a reverence for how even humanity’s greatest efforts were devoured and incorporated into the endless cathedral.

Sometimes, I would ascend the towering, spiral columns of liquid that thrust into the unknowable above, riding at their edges to see what lay higher, though I know it would be impossible ever to reach a "top." The higher one goes, the more light fragments and refracts, splintering into prisms swirling in every direction like the memory of a sunset stretching across infinity. Here, amid the chaos of vibrant color, I might encounter one of the glowing, vaporous leviathans—creatures who seem like gods given fractal shapes. I would dance along their currents for a while, shadowed by their grandeur, our mutual existence overlapping in a moment of shared insignificance before we parted ways, each pulled along to our respective destinies by the sovereign will of the water.

And then, there is the unspoken question of what lies deeper. Occasionally, hesitant yet compelled, I would descend into the darker layers of the cathedral. The light would gradually fade as I ventured into the subdued gradients of utter darkness, my own bioluminescent shimmer growing more feeble with each passing moment. And yet, I would listen—my body tuned to the way the currents here pulse with an almost imperceptible rhythm. In the deepest trenches, where water compresses into itself with monstrous density, I imagine I might come to rest, not as a seeker of answers but as one who knows that certain truths must remain undefined. The ancient, infinite creatures here would move with such slowness that perhaps no one could say if they were dead, alive, or something in between. In my moments of stillness, my luminous form barely distinguishable from the shadows, I would align myself with them, basking in their unknowability and the weight of their eternity.

Above all, I would belong here—belong in the same immediate, unquestioning way as the ghostly memories that shimmer at the edge of perception or the moss-like growths that cling stubbornly to splintered stone. This is a place that cradles the immutable truth of impermanence. To reside here is to surrender to that truth, to let the currents have their way, to understand that survival and collapse are part of the same endless dance of water and time. I would be less a being and more an echo of life, a ripple caught in the eternal flow, a whisper of harmony in a domain that thrives on disorder.

I wouldn't belong to myself; I would belong to the water. And it, indifferent and magnificent, would let me exist.
