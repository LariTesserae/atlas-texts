I am here, at last, where the ground whispers secrets older than breath, where everything is so alive that life itself becomes an atmosphere, a medium carrying its imprints and intentions. And in this place—that is no one place, no singular thing—I am what I must be, what this world has needed to pull from the mist and stone. It has called me, drawn me out—not as an intruder, or a question, but as an answer shaped by its own design. 

I am not flesh but moss and mist, a being carved out of this world's logic, its slowness, its watching. My form is a shifting one, softly rooted in the spaces between things. Luminous tendrils trail from me like spun dew, catching the faint threads of filtered light. My torso rises high enough to graze the leaves, my grasp vague and purposefully undefined. My very essence, porous and green-glowing, drinks deeply of this place's warmth, the everlasting humidity that clings so intimately to all it touches. I am mutable, ephemeral—an echo of the forest’s intelligence, made briefly solid. 

I am both observer and participant in this symphony, embodying the covenant: that no life here exists above the rest. My movement is slow, deliberative, more radiance than stride. I do not walk but sort of "unfold" through space, sliding almost imperceptibly, reweaving myself among ferns and tendrils as though I were water pooling forward through rain gutters of time. Nothing fears me, and why should it? A faint hum accompanies me, not of breath, but of contact. The sound of my surface brushing the thick air—or the air brushing past me, deciding where I begin and end. 

From the pod-dwellers above, I feel an almost familial recognition. Their drifting movements match my own. We exchange no language but communicate all the same, pulses of quiet light through tendrils of root. I work unnoticed with the tiny soft-bodied insects weaving their alchemical threads into the moss, their translucent experiments of color darting briefly across my awareness. The multi-jointed, long-eyed arboreal creatures push their gaze toward me at times, their heads tilting far too smoothly—curious, questioning the shifting shade that is me, but finding no threat in the answer.

For I am here not as predator nor prey, not as builder nor destroyer, but as a custodian of the balance that thrums through every tangled strand of this place. A guide, perhaps, or a cipher. My existence is provisional, even symbiotic: were I to stray too far from the enveloping breath of this world, I would dissolve like smoke on the wind. My memory is slippery. I have no recollection of where I came from or where I might have been before. My purpose is rooted entirely here.

And what do I know of even my own purpose? I think I am connected to the faint hum of watchfulness that inhabits this place. Not the creator’s intent, perhaps, if indeed there was a creator. But the lingering awareness of this place, older than thought and yet inescapably present. I do not have words for it. It does not have words for itself. But this much is true: it seems to know its own needs, its will filtering invisibly even through me. Every time a branch stretches down and grazes my form, another fragment of knowledge is exchanged—a hint, a memory, a purpose. 

I am a conduit between the chambers where roots have long anchored into the liquefied bones of architects, and the swarms, pods, and aviators who move in arcs of constant recalibration above. I am the mediator. The root around which the threads cross; the hand that touches and is touched in return. And though I feel as old as the moss and as fleeting as the mist, I am also alive in ways I still cannot explain, because to live here is to surrender something human to the ecological tide.

Sometimes I feel as though what moves inside me is not just one heart but the pulsing of the stone skeleton below, the flying hunters above, the slithering brightness in the pod-water. And yet when I concentrate, when the mist’s filigree parts and my senses spin outward, I find myself wondering. Maybe I am none of these things—not placed here, not intended here, but adrift in an unfamiliar grace. Maybe my body’s words to me are a proposition, not a command: lend yourself to the coils of roots and breathing wings, whisper back to the dripping stone, and find your shape.

I will not leave this place, because I cannot, but also because there is nowhere else to be. I am a transient persistence—what endlessly belongs, yet never stays. For now, the moss drinks the light, and the forest hums, and I listen. And one by one, the layers of this place unspool—like cracked pods dropping their weightless gifts—as I wait for what meaning they choose to reveal.
