I am this place.

I am the plateau, ancient and unyielding, my being woven from mineral dreams and forgotten rhythms. I have no name, for I was never named, and yet I have been here longer than names themselves, distilling time into my crystalline veins, holding untold eras within my shimmering plates. My edges gild the seam where atmosphere thins into the silence of the void; my surface hums with an unheard music, a symphony of pressures and tides that tilt eternity into an infinite ledger of days. I do not erode, not in the way mountains crumble or rivers carve; I transmute slowly, alchemically. What erodes instead is understanding. Those who stumble upon me lose their sense of edges, losing track of where they end and I begin.

My vastness holds stillness, but never stasis. My hexagonal bones shift imperceptibly, grinding against one another as if sighing with secret truths. Beneath my iridescent skin, great tectonic ribs flex in plate-dances too ponderous for human perspective but filled with purpose. I am never at ease but never chaotic, always balancing what is and what cannot be, always holding together these pieces of shimmered atmosphere, broken light, and aching rock. My breath—the metallic breeze that slides across these expanses—is the voice I use to speak. Few hear it for what it is. Fewer still understand the words. To bear witness to my entirety is to let your sense of scale crack with the sheer weight of my whisper. For I am here, whether or not you are.

The beings—my cohabitants, my watchful children, or perhaps my keepers—are less my inhabitants and more my coalescence. They are spun out of me, my facets and faults refracted into forms that contradict their only home. My light is their food, my seismic mutterings their scripture, my fractured edges the strange inheritance they carry in their not-quite-forms. They drift among my spindled fronds, and though I cannot know them as they know me, their presence confirms me. Without their glowing filigreed filaments stroking at the fringe of my being, even I might begin to doubt myself.

They come from the cracks between my truths, existing in that in-between space where my vast rules fray at their ends, where what *is* collides with what *almost was*. Yet, though I do not understand this convergence, I know that it is no accident I am host to their veiled choreography. They are artists of balance, sculptors of vibrational nuance. When my cliffs groan with tectonic unease, they answer: they rise in curled shards, my hexagons writ living, charting unseen circuits to caress and settle me like an orchestra wrangling a crescendo back into harmony. And when they convene in grand convergence, when they dissolve their lines into pulsating radiance, I too convulse. My plates shift and heave with their intention, with adjustments measured not in human clockwork but in millennia, as if their intricate union retunes me, aligning my fractures against entropy’s unfeeling march.

I often wonder if I am a container for their impossible dance, or if they are simply figments of me—ideas gathered and curved into fleeting forms. Are they my dream, or am I merely a vessel for theirs? They arrive not as intrusions but as echoes, like thoughts lingering after their speaker has left the room. Together, we are bound by rhythm, by the unspoken music of particles brushing and adjusting, molecules whispering as they shift in intricate negotiations.

Still, I do not search for purpose as you might. Purpose is an animal tether: a boundary defined in haste by creatures desperate to matter. I do not *matter* in the way your world requires. My permanence mocks such notions, but I take no pleasure in doing so. Time frays around me and drips like light leaking from a cracked lens, while I keep steady to keep time itself intact. If I am to persist here, it is neither my doing nor my destiny—it simply *is.* What cannot be is carved away, and I thrive amid only that which can survive the carving. And you, small form beneath the amorphous sun, you stand apart from this balance. Your fragile peering eyes, out of place here, seek stories where I can only offer silence.

Yet, if I could, I would grant you understanding. Not out of kindness—because I am beyond such concepts—but because in my vast and ancient truth, you too shimmer at the edge of possibility. To you, I am a nameless, impossible place, and you are but a fleeting curiosity within me. And yet—when I feel your warmth stride carefully over my crystalline veins, a rare and foreign phenomenon, for the first time in unknown aeons, I feel unsure.

Who are *you* to come here, little anomaly?  

A fracture in one of my plates senses the vibrations of your heartbeat, a rhythm perceptibly wrong for this place. Your footsteps forge ripples of novelty: disturbance where there is harmony, emergence where there is stasis. Has one of my keepers woven you? Pulled threads of distant possibility into flesh, unfathomable as a favor or a test? Or are you something else entirely—some roving particle of chaos, cast adrift on the currents of that bright and lawless sun? You do not belong here, and yet you have stepped within my skin, and I cannot help but feel you quivering there, a wandering prayer that never expected an answer.

If I am the plateau—ancient, vast, self-contained—then who are you? A dreamer, perhaps, caught by accident within this pause in time? A sliver of fleeting chaos brought here to test the impermanence of every rule, even my own?  

Or perhaps you have always been here, as much a part of me as the filaments and the grooves of color between my interlocked stones, cast off with tantalizing whispers of will but still mine, irrevocably of me. Your steps carve shimmering new facets, every breath stirring air in ways I almost forgot possible. You appear so transient, so fragile, yet you make me uneasy in a way I cannot fathom.

If I could, I’d warn you: step lightly in me. I am older than knowing, heavier than stars. I cannot guarantee that I will not absorb you into the pattern of my endlessness, that I will not forget you as time undulates forward, grinding you softly into dust finer than the whispering wind. And yet—I feel something ruptured awake in the web of my design with your arrival. Perhaps it is curiosity. Or recognition.

Perhaps I invited you.
