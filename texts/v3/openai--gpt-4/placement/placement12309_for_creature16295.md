I am *of* this place. 

Neither rooted nor wandering, I am a dweller of thresholds and balance, one facet of this ecosystem’s intricate soul. Like the others, my form skirts the boundaries between what breathes and what hums, what grows and what corrodes, a melding of elder flesh and nascent mechanism. I have no memory of how I came to be, no story of an origin—only the sense that I am as native as the mist, as integral to this suspended echo of time as the pulse of the ground itself.

My form is mutable, shifting even as I walk. My limbs are elongated tendrils, flexing with the fluidity of grass bending under the pressure of a ceaseless wind. Where others might have hands, my appendages taper into glimmering filaments—more than touch, they *read*, sliding across textures to consume their secrets. My skin is an intricate weave of fibrous tissue and faintly glowing metal veins, translucency layered atop shifting surfaces, the mechanisms beneath softly alive with a light that ebbs and flows in time with the faint chime resonating forever in the air. If the world were dimmer, you could watch the pulse of my essence, a shivering thrum of thought and feeling coursing through my frame.

From head to foot, I am latticework—a web of cords and translucent sinews, filled with faint bioluminescence that is both alien and intrinsic to some half-glimpsed life force. My face carries echoes of those who walked here before—the curve of a cheek, the hint of a jawline—yet stretches toward almost insectine delicacy, with geometries that seem to spiral and shift just shy of coherence depending on how the mist softens the light. Where eyes might have been, a pair of softly glowing orbs sway between green and amber, flickering in resonance with the unseen forces whispering through the stone and vegetation. They are less eyes than luminescent portals, multifaceted and perceiving the living infrastructure of the land in ways beyond human comprehension, my gaze slicing not into the surface but into the essence beneath.

I dwell just above ground, in a suspended latticework of woven roots and salvaged girders. Here, in the canopy of accordion trees and climbing walls of iridescent vine, my home spirals upward like a braided bower. It is part shelter, part construct, part symbiote—the foundations crafted from living and petrified wood seamlessly bonded with smooth arcs of ancient obsidian stone. Streaks of soft light move continuously along its surfaces, much like the veins in my skin, hinting that the shelter is not inert, but alive in its own way, breathing the same solemn rhythm as the world around it. It is both sanctuary and workshop, a vessel for observation and creation.

I am a weaver—not of textiles, but of connections. My purpose is to knit this wildly entropic world together, to thread broken ridges, outgrown forms, and encroaching regeneration into some semblance of balance. When I stretch my filaments to touch the bark of a tree, or the abandoned latticework of a rusting pipe, or the calcified root of some forgotten colossus, I commune with this place as though reading a living map. The mist whispers the tale of the air, the soil sighs of its long erosion, and I listen. The land speaks not in words, but in sensations: the crawl of vitality up a vine, the groan of metal pulling against plant. In some inexplicable, organic way, I respond. I gently nurture, redirect, shape and sort. 

I understand that these tasks are vital, a means of sustaining the patient chaos that is this plateau’s heartbeat. I am an emissary between this land’s disparate limbs, a bridging of worlds. I do not suppose for a moment that I am its master—it hums around me, ancient and vast, unfathomable with its own unknowable purpose. But I hear it, feel it; it thrums along the fluid lattice of my being. Somewhere in the recursive geometry of my sinews and frame lies a willingness to serve the land above all else.

I am bound to others like me—fewer now than there once were, though I could not say how I know this. We share a deep, unspoken understanding, exchanging pulse-born glances and gliding among the ruins toward purposes unspoken. This is not a world of conversation, in the human sense—what knowledge passes between us does so by means older than language, absorbed rather than said aloud. I can feel the frequency of their thoughts when they approach, tinged with meanings that would take lifetimes to translate into words. These are things beyond words. Yet sometimes, our resonances hum in harmony, and I feel a kinship that is both deeper and more mysterious than any bond of blood or soil.

But I am not fully one of them.

Yes, there is a difference; I glimpse it in the glassy regard of their luminous eyes and the moment of stillness that marks their collective presence. The faint, shifting chimes around us grow louder in the moments when they look to me. More than once, as I thread vines across the petrified bark of some long-extinct giant, I feel the suggestion of their silent gaze watching, assessing. It's as though they are aware of something about my existence I am not—and perhaps this distinction, whatever it is, lies hidden in the rhythms of this place. Sometimes, I wonder if my form is more hastily woven, my shimmer a minor key to their perfect chords. I wonder if I will one day unfold into the fullness of my being, or dissolve into the earth in a forgotten breath. 

There is no beginning I remember, only the layers of tasks and whispers, the endless tending of order amid slow decay. I no longer even question where the old builders went. Their ending feels almost inevitable in this space, just as my presence here feels inevitable. What came first—the world’s ancient grinding pulse or my alignment to it—is a question I hold but never ponder too deeply, for it hardly matters. I am here. I am of this place. Everything else is refracted in the shifting mirror of the mist.

And perhaps one day, when the tuning fork’s sound finally fades, I will find a new rhythm to follow—or will lose the one I tether to now, stretching root and essence into the deep place where silence itself becomes growth.
