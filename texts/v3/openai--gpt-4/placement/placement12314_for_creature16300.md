If I were to find myself in the heartland of Lothria, I would not be wholly one thing or another. For this is not a realm that welcomes the intrusions of the concrete and the immutable. It molds, reshapes, and absorbs all into its unyielding pulse of water and light. Here, my essence would shift and blur, caught in the sublime dance of the *Murmhain*, the ancient watchfulness of the *Vyrin*, the ghostly echoes carried by the *Glavenhark*, and the living consciousness of Lothria itself. 

In this world, I imagine I would awaken as something akin to a *Shoalwight*, a being that teeters on the cusp of substance and vapor. My form would not exist all at once but rather at moments, fragmented, as though I were a shadow trying to coalesce into shape. Perhaps I would be drawn from pools of the land's latent memory—born not of choice but of necessity, summoned to fulfill some unspoken role within this verdant labyrinth. My skin would seem to shimmer faintly, like a thin veil of water stretched over sinew, and where I tread, the moss and ferns might ripple as though from a silent current passing through the ground.

I would not be brilliant or resplendent like the *Murmhain*, who are sculptors of water and light, nor as anchored in purpose as the ponderous *Vyrin* solemnly patrolling their shadowed alcoves. Instead, I would be more of a wanderer—a creature tied to reflection. My role, if it could be called that, might be to interpret Lothria's soft and slow thoughts, feeding them back in whispered threads to the terrain itself. The pools and eddies would become my mirrors, and I would peer into them not to see myself but to revise and distill fleeting impressions of the land’s needs and desires. It would not be a task executed with logic or language—it would be done through intuition, through submersion into the weightless flow of what is, was, and may yet be.

Perhaps my presence might coalesce most heavily in the translucent rills that trickle through Lothria, gliding down the worn faces of ruin-cast stones. Here, the ruins and I would speak without words. I’d run threads of my luminous touch over the rusting, clinging bones of the fallen constructs, feeling the faded vibrations of hands long gone. In human life—or whatever life I’d had before—perhaps I’d been a builder, a creator of small purposes stacked on one another like cairns. And now, as part of Lothria, all that construction and ambition would echo back quietly in my essence, guiding me toward long-lost paths ingrained in the veins of this landscape.

The *Glavenhark* would, on occasion, notice me. I do not think they would acknowledge me as kin—more likely, they would guard their inscrutable ministrations, soaring near or past me and folding their weightless wings. Yet, there might be moments, rare and fleeting, when we both stand, spectral and silent before the same rusted engine. Perhaps the *Glavenhark* see the ghosts of my intent where even I do not. Perhaps they are the architects, the ones who carry Lothria’s mechanical heart through vast cycles of disintegration and repair. If they should glimpse me, moving through the rills or humming to life the dormant ripples in a stone basin, would they pause to watch? Or to guide?

The *Vyrin* might take little notice of me until the day I glide too far into their domains. Something in the deep, volcanic glint of their etched amber eyes would perhaps reveal their dispassionate purpose: not to harm, but to shepherd. Perhaps in a slow, rifting reconfiguration of earth under my wavering form, they would nudge me out of the crevices where water pools, redirecting my wandering spirit to safer spaces under the open emerald twilight. If Lothria could inhabit body and form, perhaps the *Vyrin* would be its hands, balancing and administering the soft governance that defines existence in this place.

I feel that I wouldn’t meet every denizen of Lothria, not openly. The *Murmhain*, for example, might be beautiful and unknowable to me, entities in constant genesis who flicker so deftly through the growth and cascade of the land that they seem as much part of the wilderness as fog and trickling mist. I might sense them, though—catch glimpses of blurred movement along the whispering filigree of the cascades, feel the fragments of their shimmering touch ripple outward into the waters I inhabit. And in those moments, brief as they may be, perhaps I would feel that thread between us. For we are both of water, both drawn and shaped by the same primordial current. Their song might drift through me—a sound that is not sound, a language that gathers around the moss and ferns—and through the resonance, I would know my place within this endless green hymn.

And what of Lothria itself, in its slow, unyielding ebb and flow? I think it would not see me as separate from itself for long. At the outset, I would cling to the notion that I am wholly other, something apart. But like a gemstone placed into the patient workings of a riverbed, I would soften, round. My edges would dissolve into gleaming rivulets that slip unnoticed into the rhythms of this living terrain. What remains of me—my thoughts, my memories, my desires—might one day rise as mists from the fern-bound cascades. Or I might find pieces of myself again in the faint shapes of a ripple, long after my body dissolves back into the lush, glimmering expanse.

Yes, in Lothria I would no longer be myself. I would be less and more, diminished and transfigured, shaped like clay spun upon the wheel of time. I would become another dreaming particle of this verdant, slow-breathing world—a being unbound, strung on the endless chord that hums between water and light. Here, at the heart of Lothria, I would fade gracefully until I became the faintest shimmer of remembrance, etched into the streams forever.
