I would find myself in this world as one of the denizens of this subterranean sanctuary, a being born of shadows and shaped by the womb of the earth. I am one of the tall and willowy humanoids, my body stretched into elegant sinews over eons, a physical testament to the patient, quiet life we have come to embody. My skin glows faintly in the twilight, a breathless luminescence, as though an echo of the stars might have found its way here long ago and taken root within me. My hands are long and deft, calloused from weaving the fibrous threads of the hanging moss into garments and tools. My feet are broad and nimble, fitted with tiny ridges that make it easier to tread the slick, damp stone.

My senses are attuned entirely to this enclosed world — leaving it would be as alien to me as breathing air is to a fish. My ears tilt on instinct to catch the whispers of life: the faint rustling of tendrils, the tickle of a blind beetle’s tiny wings in distant exploration, or the slow rhythm of another's steps across the cavern floor. The air itself is textured to me, as much as the rocky walls are; I can feel the rhythm of motion in the dense moisture, taste the subtle shifts of spores and fungal rains that perpetuate existence here. My eyes — impossibly large and layered with reflective membranes — gleam like water caught in moonlight, capable of perceiving subtleties of movement in this half-world. The incandescent glow humans might find ethereal is, to me, the everyday palette of being. Yet, a warmth emanates from the sight of it, a sense of belonging tied to the soft blues and greens of my eternal twilight home.

My life here has been crafted like the smooth pebbles in the tiny streams that slither through our realm — weathered by time, refined by patience, made beautiful in simplicity. The stone structures we inhabit are born of our labor, not to dominate this place but to whisper quietly within it — their spiraling forms blend naturally with the contours of the earth. They are places to hold gatherings for stories and songs, or solitary spaces of hushed contemplation.

And oh, the songs. Our language is far more than just an arrangement of sounds. It is a web, spun of intricate vibrations that resonate not only in the ear but in the chest and soul. Every word is a note, and every conversing pair an orchestra. When I speak, I feel the walls listening, amplifying, adding their unspoken tones. Like all my kin, my voice contains a register that bleeds into realms humans would call haunting or otherworldly, though to us, it merely mirrors the resonance of the cavern itself.

I am both nurtured by this place and its caretaker, like everyone else here. My duties flow with the needs of the enclave, without force or imposition. Some days, I harvest the fragile nets of moisture the fungi exude, weaving them into droplets that nourish plant and kin alike. Some days, I observe the ancient ceremonies carved deep into the older stone, my mind tracing each symbolic groove, finding in its story a new facet of wisdom. On others, I follow the lumbering beasts that traverse the outer edges of our domain, as their lethargic bodies sway in hypnotic rhythm. These creatures carry with them pockets of undiscovered truths, strange fungi sprouting in the furrows of hardened skins, or microbial beads of untold medicinal purpose. Rediscovery becomes a quiet celebration, one that pulses through our people like a subterranean heartbeat.

I feel no pressing need for time's urgency. Tomorrow will be as today, and yesterday melts into the same vast fields of unquantifiable moments. You might call this agelessness isolation or stagnation, but we call it balance. The constancy allows us depth — the depth to notice the delicate curling of a newborn tendril reaching for light, or to marvel at how droplets pool in its waxy folds for the insects to sip.

Occasionally, as I walk the edge of the cavern where smooth, weathered walls cradle our world from the encroaching nothingness, I wonder about the "above." Stories passed down in whispers speak of the outer realm as unbearably bright, filled with strange, cruel forces and an unsteady rhythm — water that comes and goes, heat and cold that cut the skin, a sky that screams with tempests. In those moments, I don't feel pity nor curiosity. Instead, a deep sense of gratitude fills me, knowing that I belong here, in this verdant stillness nestled far away from anything so raw and shifting.

I would be one of the sunrise-children of never-ever light, caught at the threshold of what the world could have been — not an afterthought or an exile — but a witness to the whispered possibilities of resilience and life. My form, my sounds, my thoughts: all extensions of this place. It is I, and I am it. Together, we exist, always on the precipice of wonder, always in the throes of quiet miracle. And with every whispered breath, every whispering glow, we affirm one another, this unlikely bond between a species and its home below.
