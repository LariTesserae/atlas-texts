If I were to find myself in this world, I would not simply *be*. To merely exist here would be a paradox—it is a place where the boundaries between self and environment dissolve, where to belong is to participate in the ongoing breath of the ecosystem. Thus, I would awaken not as an outsider, but as something born of this enduring, intricate rhythm. I would be a *keeper of resonance*, a being tempered by time into harmony with a realm on the precipice of vitality and finality.

I would be tall and hollow-boned, my limbs elongated and pliable like roots reaching for nourishment in hidden crevices. My skin would no longer speak with the moisture of prior worlds but would instead have hardened to a semi-translucent gold, exuding faint warmth and glowing ever so slightly in the wavering red twilight. Like the leathery fronds of the plants, I would no longer draw breath as my sole manner of energy exchange; molecules pulled from the heat-dense air would fire through systems of crystalline capillaries branching beneath my skin like living stained glass.

My senses would expand far beyond the mere five I once understood, recalibrating to perceive the vibrations of the world around me. Colors might dissolve into families of heat signatures, while sounds would paint shapes and textures inside my mind. I could feel the faint pulses of life resonating through the dense air, a sonorous hum rolling continuously through the interconnected symphony of this pocket world. Where I might once have strained to hear, I would now *be able to feel*. When I closed my reflective molten-gold eyes, I would still sense the tendrils of sound, wave upon wave, like tension rippling across water.

No longer would I hunger in the ways I once did, for here, feeding might mean something softer, something seamless. I would sustain myself not by taking but by tending—trading energy as plants and creatures do, giving and receiving from every action. I might cradle a swollen sac from one bulbous organism and sip from its excretion of energy-enriched warmth, an act that would later cause it to bloom in radiant displays as its stored burdens were eased.

My voice would no longer belong exclusively to my mouth; instead, it would reside within the entire choir of my body. Vibrations would ripple from my ribs or resonate faintly in my palms when raised, communicating not just words but tones and feelings. To "speak" here would require no airiness or waste of sound—only connection. My language would be one of rhythms and pulses, my ability to communicate a dance of intentional, kinetic waves. Each beat I sent forth, every song I hummed into the atmosphere, would return to me as altered responses—some from the people, some from the world itself.

My role? I think I would wander the dense channels and frond-shadowed trails as a caretaker of thresholds, those rarely-trodden edges of the community where plants grow sparsely and the winds of the outer world threaten to intrude. I would be tasked with gently reinforcing barriers against collapse—not by building walls against it, but by talking to the plants and placing soft hands upon the cracked earth. Each flutter of my body’s spoken vibrations would coax the membrane of the habitat into healing itself. New shoots might creep out from parent stems, repairing rifts in the canopy, or networks of biotic fabric might grow over dust-scoured stone, knitting the edges closed.

This task would not make me special—it would make me only another thread within the quilt. These beings, my siblings, have purged ego—what is prized in us is not uniqueness but fit, the right note struck at the right time. The idea of individual achievement or identity is alien here. I hold no name, for there is no need to call me apart from the others. I am the one fitting a hole, completing the harmony of our shared purpose in this place.

When visitors come, I would be among those who approach first, my movements slow and unalarming. Though I could send humming vibrations through the air, I would not use them to speak—not at first. I would listen, porous to the sounds and movements of strangers, their dissonance obvious, almost disconcerting against the steady rhythm of home. Everything they do would be studied, filtered through touch and tone, so that the places they tread do not break where balance has made the ground thin. My guidance would be soundless, expressed in faint nudges and gestures, an outstretched limb to lead them through brambles or over a safe strip of shimmering sharp rock.

I think I might feel something peculiar—sympathy but also enough distance to find them strange and heavy, dragging their particular aliveness into a world that has learned to survive without weight. I’d marvel at their reckless freedom, their solid skin and breathy sounds. But unlike some of my kin, who continue their weaving or shepherding without much thought for visitors except avoidance and vague curiosity, I might linger on them. I would wonder about a time I no longer clearly remember, when my kind, too, must have been newer—less attenuated, their essence still raw and budding. A time before we grew into cadence, before our edges were worn off.

Could I learn to hum in the meaningless language of the visitors, setting aside my rhythms long enough to parrot something of their frustration—their fear? Would my own pattern soften and splinter, fragment, trying and failing to meet them halfway? Would I be curious enough to follow them out when they left? Surely the thought occurs.

And yet deep inside me, I know I cannot leave. This world glows within my bones—my hands bear its delicate marks. If the wind carries me away as it feeds through these shadowed channels, the frequencies of this place will shake apart in grief. Not for me, precisely, but for the loss of one necessary note in the harmony of the whole. It is the trade I was born to make, willingly but unknowingly, and so I understand the warning the world whispers to the outsiders: leave now while you can. Do not ask to stay. Do not linger long.

Because if you do, it will mold you into itself, as it did me. And though eternity here in the amber twilight is no punishment, it is no simple refuge either. It is not a place for those who dream.

It is, in the end, its own being. And I am no more than a tether by which it persists.
