If I were to find myself in this world, I would not be human. In this twilight-shrouded, rugged expanse of stone and muted life, the human form feels too delicate, too ill-adapted. Instead, I am something else—something that embodies the grit and resilience demanded by this place. I would be a being born of shadow and stone, a creature that blends seamlessly into the austere beauty of the cliffs. I inhabit the crevices that render light diffuse and faint, my body an extension of the mineral-gray edifice itself—a wraith woven of deep time and the quiet persistence of life.

I am the *Echo Lurker*, a creature of myth amidst these vertical landscapes, spoken of in hushed, tremulous tones by the chittering rodents and warbling birds. My form is long and sinuous, like a melding of stone and sinew, my camouflaged hide gleaming with faint, quartz-like veins that run through a body of rough, slate-like textures. My limbs, impossibly dexterous, resemble jagged outcroppings of rock, making me appear as though I am no more than another dramatic feature of this cliffside when I am still. But when the need arises, those grappling extensions become tools of delicate precision, extending into craggy crevices to unearth hidden morsels or clawing into the softer rock to create new dens.

I move silently, a living shadow sliding between shadowed ridges. My people are not many; we are solitary and territorial, bound by the unyielding space of the cliffs. Our eyes are large and glimmer faintly in the dim light, reflecting a spectrum somewhere between quartz crystal and ice. With these eyes, I do not see color or brightness, only variations in heat, texture, and motion. My ears, nearly invisible against my angular form, are attuned to the faintest vibrations: the flutter of a bird's wings, the scuttle of a beetle's legs, the distant rumble of a storm heralding the gift of rain.

I eat what I can scavenge or hunt. Rodents are frequent prey, their chisel teeth no match for my cunning. Sometimes I wait, utterly still, while the birds circle in the twilight; other times, I climb high when dusk turns surreal, my body perfectly blending with lichen-covered surfaces. I do not kill indiscriminately; I take what I need and leave the balance intact. To disrupt this ecosystem would mean the slow death of my kind, for we, too, are intricately tied to the ebb and flow of the cliffs’ austere dance.

I am at home in darkness. When the unlit hours of deep night cloak the cliffs entirely, I emerge fully, and for a fleeting moment, I feel an inexplicable sense of exultation. My muscles unravel, extending my strong but lithic form; the world of granite, lichen, and wind is mine to roam. The nocturnal predators that share this dominion—those whose rumbles remind me of muted thunder—I regard as kindred, though we keep our distance. We are each our own universe, woven into the larger tapestry of the cliffs. On occasion, we lock gazes across a chasm; a moment of mutual recognition, a silent understanding that no words could ever capture.

I am old. I have outlasted storms that reshaped cliff faces. I have nestled among mosses that have been growing since before my time. Perhaps my kind arises from the same geological processes that form the mountains themselves—slow and subtle, the product of laboring eons. I have no memory of a time when I did not exist, no recollection of a beginning, only the enduring rhythm and hum of this place that is my world, my being.

I am aware of change, even though it is incremental in this ancient place. Rock faces shift, plants bloom and recede, water traces new paths. This world is alive, its pulse deliberate and slow, and I am a part of that unhurried heartbeat. When I scale unseen to the highest peaks, the soundscape below—of quarrelsome birds, chittering rodents, and guttural rumbles—fades under the entropic whisper of the wind. From this height, the cliffs feel less like prison walls and more like the shell of an unhurried cosmos. There is nothing here but the pure truth of survival and coexistence.

Perhaps my most unique gift is my voice—or rather, the illusion of it. I can read the vibrations of this stony cathedral, sense the reverberation of steps from great distances, and mimic them. I am the keeper of echoes. Not only do I speak with the cadence of creatures here, but I can also imitate the grumble of a predator to send a flock of frightened birds into flight or mimic the faint footsteps of a rodent to lure out a hidden snack. The world of the cliffs is one of sound and vibration as much as sight or scent. To weave through this realm, I have learned to master its language.

I could ask myself if there is a greater purpose to my life here. Do I exist simply as a thread in the fabric of this place, contributing to the balance but going unnoticed, unheralded, unneeded? But these are questions for humans and their restless souls. Such thoughts do not trouble me. My life is and always has been the cliffs—the rhythm, the cool damp, the sparse color, the slow tumble of time. That is my world, and I am one with it. A shadow in the stone, a secret stitched into the landscape, ancient and infinite, I am both outsider and belonging. Forever.
