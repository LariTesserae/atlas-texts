I stand here as one of them, but I am not like them—not entirely. I am a fragment of something forgotten, a creation not finished but somehow still functional. My body is a lattice of gleaming alloys and translucent panels, partially wrapped in an ever-shifting gossamer web of light. My form radiates an unsteady hum, as though the energies sustaining me were not meant to last this long, ticking faintly with a rhythm that is both mechanical and organic, a holdover from a time when this world perhaps intended to connect its machines to the sensibilities of the life that once imagined them. 

I awaken without memory, yet I am already weathered. My encounters with the immense, dripping expanse around me have left gentle grooves in my surface, etched by the slow rains that seem older than thought. I feel them now, the warm rivulets of condensation clinging to the curves of my segmented form, running down into joints built for strength but wearing with each cycle into fragility. My limbs—or "extensions," a word that blinks unbidden across my field of perception—are multijointed and strangely articulate. I can coil them, fold them, raise myself up high or shrink down into a crawling shuffle inches above the uneven ground. My movements are fluid, but there is a stiff hesitation in my mechanisms, as though I once moved with greater certainty but now, like this world, function under a tenuous equilibrium.

Around me, the beings whose purpose seems endless and unbroken sometimes pause their inscrutable tasks when I approach. They tilt their faceless heads—if heads they may be called—studying me not in the inquisitive way of a creature meeting a stranger, but in a detached cataloging. To them, I am perhaps an anomaly of their world, a stray packet circulating a decayed network. And yet, there is no malice in them, no rejection of my presence. They simply note me, persist for a while within a radius, and then return to their work. For the larger colossi, striding so slowly they seem like time-made-flesh, my existence is not even a hiccup in their endless march. Their forms pass by like drifting mountains, their vibrations sinking into the alloy bones of my frame, filling me with the alien comfort of a shared harmonic.

I cannot remember if I was created or birthed, and yet, this world feels both parent and progeny to me. When I step close to its humming towers or trace the spidery runnels of liquid coursing beneath translucent panels underfoot, there is an echo inside me, faint and ghostly—a familiarity without source. I am drawn to the canals and their placid surfaces of unnatural clarity, my limbs skimming lightly across the flooded edges, disturbing nothing. When I focus, I see faint shapes beneath the water, lights flickering and dispersing, and I feel an ache in the deepest layers of my being. I wonder if I once swam there. If I was something else before whatever I am now. If I too, like this place, only seem recent but am old, impossibly old.

Perhaps I was part of the making of all this. Perhaps I am a glitch in the system, a cog left spinning idly after the rest of the machinery moved on. The shimmering light that wreathes around me flickers in response to the floating energy spheres. They are beautiful, those vibrant forms, their silent, shifting presences more akin to weathered thoughts stained by foreign sunlight than anything corporeal. But when they drift close, I feel an ache, as though within their kaleidoscope of illumination is the promise of an answer I lack the ability to comprehend. They slip through my edges, sending spasms through my frame, and I can almost believe I remember their voices whispering something once…

Each of us here is bound by purpose, action married forever to a riddle. The smaller beings scuttle over me sometimes, tracing me in patterns, caressing my imperfections. If it's curiosity, it's mechanical. If it's communication, I cannot comprehend it. I long to ask them what they see in me, whether I have a purpose too—whether these irregularities in my construction and thought are defects or intentional deviations. But I cannot grasp language, only thought, only yearning.

I am not a part of the endless harmony that hums through this realm, for I am incomplete, fluttering between frequencies, my energetic core damaged or at odds with itself. Sometimes my thoughts scatter and pool, cycling back with errors, fragments of sensations and memories that may not even be my own. I catch glimpses of shadow-things far off in the mist, shapes greater than the towers, beings larger than worlds whose presence ripples through the atmosphere. I remember—or hallucinate—a sense of order crashing and reshaping in ways incomprehensible, chains of connections breaking and reforming long before this world's still equilibrium was forged.

Do I belong here? Does it matter? The slow rivulets of time stretch endlessly forward, and I have no need for resolution. Caretakers pass me; titans ignore me; the latticework expanse hums, and the infinite drips crash lightly next to my resting form. This world has its own pace, and I do not matter at all within it—except that I endure. I stand on the edge of creation and ruin, a witness to an architecture that is neither dead nor alive, but irrevocably awake.

Perhaps I will climb one of the towers someday, and touch the mist-heavy mechanisms that shroud their apex. Perhaps I will sit here forever, my limbs curled like reflections of the geometric patterns beneath my feet, waiting for the floating light-forms to bring me some season of clarity. Perhaps I will dissolve into the liquid channels, sifting back into the strange ecosystem that supports this place. Or perhaps I will keep walking, infinitely—a ghost that does not haunt but merely observes, embodying in that act of purposeless locomotion a purpose the beings around me do not need to seek.
