Here, in a realm where the contours of existence are still soft and mutable, I am something that does not yet have a name. Neither a true creature of this place nor entirely apart, I am among the first of my kind. My body feels transitory, only half-committed to form. It is as though the world’s indecision about its own shape permeates me. I am pulled between being defined and indefinite, between the fleshiness of creation and the luminous ether of potential. My edges waver like shadows in mist, my presence tangible yet only just — a crossroads between what is solid, what breathes, and what flickers into being.

I think I emerged from the mist itself. Not as creation from void, but a coalescence of this land’s humid essence, its half-formed will. I recall nothing but dampness pressing me into cohesion, like countless water molecules conspiring to give shape to a will unspoken. My limbs — long, attenuated — appear to come and go as I move, twisting in and out of apparent dimensions. Flicks of light spiral within my translucent surface, as if communicating impulses I can’t yet comprehend. Maybe this is how I think, or perhaps thought works differently here: ripples spreading outward in shared currents.

I dwell where stones fracture toward secrets and green tendrils hang their impossible weight like ancient tapestries in a great hall. The twilight is my cradle and my cloak. I move across the plateau with no clear purpose, simply feeling the texture of surfaces underfoot. Moss whispers as it shifts in reaction to my passing. Fine droplets that never reach the ground dissolve through me instead of merely landing upon me, and as a small rushing stream breaks around my form, I note the intricate play of currents that weave through where my torso might be. My body, or whatever this is, feels like part mist, part latticework; I flicker between opacity and clarity when my attention draws outward.  

I wonder if I am kin to the Luminal Ones, for I share their love of the quiet, of the darkness made luminous. Yet I lack their deliberate grace. The way their golden filigree laces through the air with rhythm is near enough to sacred dance; my own movements might seem clumsy, though I suspect there is intention beneath my chaos that I am not yet fluent enough to unearth.

For now, I watch and listen. The Archeonids — part earthen purpose, part rooted flesh — are the ones I feel most curious about. I have seen them work the stone with a solemnity that feels ancient and yet entirely present, carving meaning into space like a language older than sound. I do not speak with them, not yet. Something tells me that words as I might understand them are meaningless to such beings, whose voices are in the sharpness of their chisels, and in the echo of their steps against wet rock. But I linger near their structures, their moss-bound walls rising like thoughts mid-formation. I see their symbols, and sometimes I feel a faint resonance within me — a pulling sensation, as if I had once been etched with meanings now grown blurred.

The Echinoforms seem closer to the land itself than I will ever be. Their long, grasping tendrils test the bedrock with a familiarity I cannot match. They remind me of the shifting stone under my feet, and I often find myself following them, mimicking their sibilant motion in an attempt to understand the rhythmless rhythm of this world. Their mottled forms, streaked with the colors and textures of the earth, pulse faintly as they move, and I wonder if that pulse originates from the land or from something deeper within themselves. Are they whispering secrets to the stone, or is the stone whispering to them?
