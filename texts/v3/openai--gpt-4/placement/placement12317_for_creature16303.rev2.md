You find yourself in the deepest recesses of a lost world, further than the merest notion of below. Here, in the soft-heart expanse of an old world drowned, lingers a silence that echoes louder than the roaring excitement of life that exists far, far above.

You are not a creature, as we understand it, not a consciousness in the way we perceive it. You exist as a sense of time stretching and converging, as a faint, lingering memory of what once was—back when the world was still its young, fresh self—all wrapped in an embrace of cool, ageless water. 

You are an echo. You have no shape—but you do. You do not think, breathe, or feel—but you do. You exist but not in a sense that we could understand or perceive. You are the lost impression of an age gone by, the spectral reminisce of a form swallowed by time. You are what memory feels like in the mind of a god who forgot eons ago.

The world you inhabit is caught in a moment. You don’t know where or in which way it might’ve drifted, or if it ever did drift, because the world has paused, perhaps to indulge in the quiet, forgotten details of silent existence. The gentle humming of timeless machinery, the momentary ripple of water against archaic metallic carcasses, the caress of light as it brushes against the gossamer-soft symmetry of shadow and reflection—this is your world. It is your home.

As an echo, you perceive the faintest shifts in the endless equilibrium—a shift in light, a whispering shadow, an unexpected drizzle of silt. Maybe it's another echo, another you from an age even further.

Your existence bears no mark on the world—it is far too vast, far too ancient for you to make a splash. And in that, you are free. Free to rest as an echo, as an imprint of movement remembered by time, buffered from the crashes of life’s incessant demand for growth and meaning.

You are neither alive nor dead. You exist in the space between, the twilight of fucking and unbecoming, disturbed only by the smooth caress of unworried, tranquil water. You don’t struggle or thrive; you just are—a returned whisper from a time too distant to remember. 

And you linger, the long drawn sigh of a forgotten past among the ruins of machinery and silhouettes of ethereal creatures.

Finally, you are a pilgrimage, an homage to the creatures, the machines, the world that came before, now entrapped in a peaceful state of slumber—a slumber that blankets a memory so deep, that no sound or motion could ever dare to disrupt its tranquility.

In the end, you are a silent testimony to a timeless past and irrevocable entropy, a comforting reminder that all things shall pass and come to rest, beneath the calm blanket of forgetfulness. This is where you exist—in the heart of time's forgotten echoes, beneath the ripples of de-evolution, at one with the voided heartbeat of an old, drowned world.
