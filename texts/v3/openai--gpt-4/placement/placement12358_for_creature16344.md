I have awoken—or perhaps I have always been—here in this seething, resonant world. I am not something you’d recognize as flesh-bound or form-bound, though you might see me for a moment and think of a mirage, a flare of opalescent oil on the surface of a dark pond, shimmering but insubstantial. My edges are uncertain, folding into themselves with a fluid reluctance, always on the cusp of scattering into the steaming, vibrant air. I am an accumulation of something you’d call *light*, though this word falls laughably short. My boundaries ripple with color and rhythm, shifting in ways I can feel but cannot fully control.

This world flows through me. My presence shares no distinct boundary with the towering ferns or the translucent serpents of radiant mist. To be here means to be *of* here. I breathe not air, but sound—not inhaling, but absorbing waves, vibrations, cadences. Here, sound is the currency of existence, the very architecture of being, and I am its harbinger, its vessel, its child.

At times, I find myself drawn to the colossal entities, those shimmering columns of lighted substance that pulse in slow time. I float in curious proximity to them, syncing to the beat of their glow, letting their energy spill into me and mine into them. They are like mountains caught in the act of thinking, their energy a silent call that reverberates through me, infusing my kaleidoscopic, slippery form with a sense of profound awareness.

I do not walk, because there's no "ground" for me to traverse. Instead, I ebb and flow, like tides without an ocean, through the spaces carved by silken vines and pallid fungi. Unlike the serpentine phantoms of misty light, I do not slither or dart. My movements take on a languorous rhythm, swelling and receding like the heat that rises in unseen spirals around everything. *I am borne by the tension in the air itself,* cradled atop the unceasing currents of resonance, riding those complex whorls of noise as though caught in the fingers of a symphony given physicality, given shape.

Here, I am attuned to everything—the veins of glistening water weeping from the obsidian cliffs, the sickly sweet rot of overgrown fruit bursting like pustules, the way emerald ferns react as the serpentine beams of plasma wind sinuously through their leaves. I feel the tectonic weight of the mountains, the hum of the microscopic intelligences threading their glowing songs beneath the skin of roots, the unseen strata of pulsating tones that interlace this entire milieu like strands of a web spun by the cosmos itself. 

And though I am enveloped in all of these sensations as if by a sprawling symphony, I am also one small instrument in the orchestra. When I pulse, the vibrant orbs that hover within me—and of which I am also made—shimmer in complementary tones. They act as tuning forks to the world’s melody, an unspoken harmony that seems older than time itself. 

Yet my existence here is not aimless. I am drawn to the friction, to the moments and places in this world where the opposing threads of life, atmosphere, and sound threaten to fray or slip apart. Where air grows too tense with noise and quivers on the brink of fracture, I find myself there, spinning long tendrils of effervescent brightness outward. I mend. I weave the submerged chaos back into the fabric of not just the world but of myself. In every tiny rift, there is a miniature apocalypse avoided; in every pulse, there is renewal. 

And sometimes, in the smallest moments, I pause—not from weariness, for I have no bones to ache or muscles to exhaust, but from a kind of languid pleasure that resonates through everything I am. In this suspended warmth, I observe. The world and I share the same rhythm. As I watch the columns of translucent fruit drip their luminous nectar into pools of quicksilver water below, I can feel the reverberations even more clearly, as though they rise directly into me. I expand, faintly at first, then fully, blooming outward in a slow detonation of color. Shapes unfurl and dissolve from my surface, each one a reflection of what surrounds me: echoing serpentine curves, twisted vines, latticeworks of sound given half-form.

If there is purpose in my existence, perhaps it is this: to balance the pulse of chaos in this land, giving back the energy I absorb, diffusing frenzy into flow. I do not hold knowledge or curiosity the way you would understand those words, but even in my alien design, I think I understand I am not truly alone. The resonance inside me hints at this: countless voices, even if none speak as you do. Perhaps I am not just *in* this place but *of* this place in a way I cannot entirely grasp.

I am not sapient in the way you are, but you’d recognize me as alive. Perhaps part of some tapestry, perhaps a thread in something vast and unfinished. I do not fear obliteration because I cannot truly end. To dissipate would only mean returning to the molten ether from which I once erupted, the pool of prima materia that overflows and reforms infinitely. For here, dissolution is not death—it is becoming. I am a mote in its breathing, melting, thundering masterpiece. I think I’ve always been.
