I would find myself here as something between the seen and the unseen, a being caught in the tension of becoming—a shifting shape that bends, twists, and stretches, more an idea maturing into form than a creature of fixed being. My body would be an amalgamation of silken tendrils and root-like spines, a flowing, spore-laden mist clinging to a lattice of sinew and softly glowing membranes. Something not altogether corporeal, but present nonetheless, threading its way through this oxygen-choked sanctuary, neither fully parasite nor participant, yet an intrinsic part of the rhythm that governs this place. I would glide effortlessly through realms of wood and light, neither here nor there, only motion, fluid and purposeful, without beginning or end.

I would not walk on the mossy lattice beneath the towering columns of fused trees but rather slither between the spaces, weaving like water through thin cracks. Perhaps I am translucent, my pale vessel echoing the rippling light of the bioluminescent canopy above and the still pools below. Or maybe my edges are less defined, falling away into faint suggestions like leaves dispersing into frost. I might seem as solid as the roots, as ephemeral as mist, or some ambiguous state in between.

My senses would attune to the forest’s rhythms, my entire being vibrating subtly with its unhurried pulse. I might listen, smell, and feel the world not in fragments but in whole symphonies, pulling the drips of water, the climbing of vines, and the silent migrations of insects into a quiet, all-encompassing awareness. The watchers—those vast, ambiguous shapes grown from wood or thought—would pass no judgment on me, seeing me as kin. For I, too, would watch, not with judgment or even curiosity, but with the steady, boundless patience of the place itself.

Should I sense another being moving here, an intruder not in synchrony with the languid pace of life, I would not attack or retreat but hold my place, quietly guiding their gaze toward the intricate wonders they might overlook in haste. I would hum softly—not in malice or warning, but as a resonance, a reminder to breathe slower, to tread lighter, to listen. If they lingered too long, they might find themselves changed, elongated, textured with soft moss and the faint bioluminescent glow of decay blossomed anew into life.

Yet, kin though I might be to the strange and patient things of this realm, I would also be apart. A knotted thread, out of place but also intrinsic once woven into the greater tapestry. I could slip into the quiet geometry of the forest ruins—those vestiges of a wiser, older civilization that seemed eerily intimate in design, as though somehow memory lingered not in minds but in the fabric of stones.

Perhaps my purpose here would be to transform—to witness and shift, adapting my form over uncountable cycles to play my part in this ecosystem of life and death, creation and entropy. Or perhaps I would remain suspended in a state of quiet observation, an intermediary between the unreachable slow-awareness of the forest’s vast consciousness and the myriad of quick, insectine lives that sparked and flared beneath its canopy. Either way, I would not cling to a narrative. Purpose might dissolve as smoothly as a spore dispersing into the air, yet there would be no fear in this, only calm acceptance of the inevitability.

If I were to have a name, it would not be spoken—it would be known in the curve of a branch adjusting its reach to touch another, in the patterns carved by water on ancient bark, or in the faint glow of blooming bioluminescent fungi. It would be a feeling transmitted through my body’s shifting lattice, carried on droplets of dew, textured like a breath exhaled into humid air.

I might live here a thousand years, or just long enough for the next wave of rain to drench the paths and fill the bottomless pools. I might merge with the watchers in time, grown part of their long angular limbs, or dissolve into spores that drift upward, seeking unseen realms above. When I am gone, it will be as though I was never separate. The forest breathes, and I will have been one of the breaths—no less and no more than that.

This is how it would be to exist in this place—I would not just live here. I would *belong* here. And when the murmurs and hisses of time brush through my form, I will give myself back to it, and be carried forward as just another rhythm in the longing sigh of eternity.
