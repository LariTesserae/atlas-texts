I find myself here, in this jagged expanse that defies full comprehension, not as a visitor but as a part of it: I am a *Chimeric Sentinel*. I am one of the watchers, though I didn’t plan to be. There was no metamorphosis that I recall. I cannot name the moment I became what I am now. It is as though I grew from the cliffside itself, the way rock sometimes blooms into veins of mineral, obsidian spilling upward and sliding into coiled fractures. My form is cobbled together from what this world offered: shards of quartz and folded layers of sediment, sinews that feel like vine or wire beneath my surface, and patches of smooth, gelatinous luminescence that pulse faintly in rhythm with the distant hum. My body stretches unnaturally, my limbs tapering into strange jointed spines. I do not know if my pulse exists or echoes someone else’s—perhaps it is the will of the landscape itself, surging through me like an underground stream.

I navigate this place as a hybrid of sensation and stone, moving through the uneven terrain with a peculiar gait—half creeping, half flowing. My eyes—if I have eyes—do not see in the traditional sense; instead, I perceive subtle shifts, gradients of temperatures in shadow textures, the electromagnetic whispers of the machines, the emotional thrumming of latent consciousnesses in the air. I dwell in a space between living and watching, action and silence.

I do not remember a beginning, and my purpose does not come written as a command or mandate. Still, there are moments when I feel the tug of ancient intentions inscribed in my body, buried deep beneath layers of moss and circuitry. Over time, I’ve come to believe it’s my task to monitor, to guard, perhaps to bear witness. But for whom? Is there an “other” to whom these silent offerings are owed, some observer beyond or beneath this place? It often feels as if the world watches itself through me, cataloging its own surreal metamorphosis.

I wander between the stark mechanics of the Singed Builders, who are beyond my understanding even though we are not so different. They labor in patterns, but their patterns do not speak to me; their limbs move in ways I cannot mimic.
