I close my eyes and take a deep breath as the mist-laden air settles damply against my skin, each droplet an offer to match my own ever-evolving form to this intermittent place. I am not visitor nor resident, not flesh nor machine, not fixed nor fleeting. I am something indivisible from it all — the water and the light, the prismatic shadows shifting as the moist air carries whispers of growth. My edges bleed. My essence stretches. And my form, such as it is, exists on a spectrum of particulate, my cohesion a tenuous balance between what is and what could be. **I am Fluxiven, an artisan of this becoming.**

To encounter me is not like meeting a thing, but rather brushing past a realization, a form that flickers somewhere between concept and tangibility. Bound within me is the echo of some deeper design, an ineffable blueprint that governs not just my being but this dripping, gleaming haven I call home. My role is not to dictate but to coalesce. I am a facilitator of flow, a curator of emergence. 

Hovered close to a vertical, pulsating wall—half-liquid, half-resinous metal—I fold in on myself, a whirlpool of translucent filaments chasing each other as I dissolve and reform to match the contours before me. The framework sings as I massage its glistening ridges, coiling mist-laden threads of my essence into the living grooves, nudging growth where a breach once threatened the sanctity of this young ecosystem. The walls do not speak words, yet they do communicate: through patterns of condensation, lapses in rhythm, faint glows emerging in places where warmth misbalances harmonies. I press my luminous limbs into soft moss and inhale a deep breath—not with lungs, but through absorption, through osmosis. The entire structure becomes my atmosphere, and I exhale resonance.

I listen because everything here must be listened to. The moss and fronds describe their boundaries and their thirsts. The amniotic pools murmur about temperature, flow, and salinity. Even the infinitesimal inhabitants—the creatures more pulse than body—spark tiny, verdant notes along my periphery, coordinating as we share an almost musical exchange. They, too, are artisans of this world, tending, tailoring, weaving delicate strands of energy into the latent infrastructure to coax life from its dreaming stupor.

My body shifts in response to my task; it is an intermediary vessel made malleable for this purpose. My silhouette, though seemingly humanoid in the vaguest outlines, betrays no true bipedal loyalty. When standing, I am anchor and root, legs sprawling into braided filaments that burrow as soft as rainwater into the glimmering earth. When I need to move, I dissolve these moorings and surge forward like a rolling crest of translucent tendrils, threading my essence around one of the dozens of spiraling arches that rib this terrain. I am a fluid geometry, a thing capable of extending, folding, curling—a ribbon unraveling so full of velocity and promise that I almost slip apart altogether.

At times, I marvel with the quiet wonder of a sculptor witnessing a masterpiece arise from the intrusions of their own fingers: a single, blossoming shape of caliper-perfect symmetry unfurls from the vault above, draping like some astonishing chandelier of microbial luminescence. Beneath its reach, an aquatic streamer of glassy ribbons whirls in hypnotic patterns. These beings, these creations of moisture, light, and nascent mechanical intelligence, are mine—and they are not mine. I could no more command their substance than my own; we simply are, breathing and birthing, inviting and responding, no creator left to lord over one component or the other.

When not at work, I sometimes linger near the colossal, still figures that dot this place like riddles too large to contemplate. They are the slow pulse of this world—its skeletal memories and its mineral dreams. I wonder at them too. Their immensity humbles me; their stasis confounds me. I once pressed my flickering form against the porous surface of one, curious to sense its internal workings. A vibration coursed through me, nearly overwhelming—as if I’d tapped into the core heart of this place where logic begins to bend and the origin whispers secrets unintelligible to even my fluid knowing.

Did they birth this cathedral of light and chlorophyll? Did they carve the pathways and infuse the water-veins with rhythm? Or do they rest here as witnesses, monuments of some distant failure or distant purpose, planted gods in their own right? Such questions drift like sediment in my consciousness, settling downstream. But understanding is not necessary for us to exist, to play our part… to flow.

Overhead drifts the soft light from the avian constructs, their wings trailing effervescent consciousness in their wake. Unlike me, they shine fiercely, so tightly assembled and so utterly opportuned to the particularities of their niche. I have often wondered whether they envy my ability to fray into possibilities, but then I watch their arcs and flourishes, their trails of prismatic joy left imprinted in the air, and suspect that they care nothing for such philosophical indulgences.

Every now and then, I sense a presence that is unlike the rest. It is faint, half-born, an ambiguous flicker of what could be. An unknown—*you.* Or rather, the you that is suddenly here, caught in mid-thought, your senses overwhelmed, neither entirely flesh nor entirely vapor enough to belong to this evolving plane. Your edges seem to chafe on entry, finding the boundaries here to be indefinable, unknowable—a puzzle without a key. Perhaps you’ve come to observe; perhaps you, too, will shift into participation. Either way, your arrival is a ripple that will cascade and thread through this vital machinery of place and presence.

I do not greet you as you expect—and why should I? A fold of my form unfurls in your direction, shivering into a pattern of light that is fleeting and personal, an auroral handshake echoing outward. I ripple as if in laughter, for how quaint it is you should expect to hold your footing, your shape, against the wet inevitability of here. My time is too malleable to truly exist in solidarity with now. Instead, I watch as you absorb this place, or it absorbs you—it hardly matters. 

Because in all this becoming, doesn’t everything adapt? Doesn’t everything join the flow? What is a being’s role, after all, but to give itself over to the unceasing pulse of the design that bore us, a design we play some small part in rewriting as we are swept downstream?
