I am the *vespanthe*, one of countless threads woven from fungus and mineral-born ethers, the barely-there manifestation of a life form that hovers between the grasp of creation and dissolution. I do not move as you might imagine—at least, not in the sense of steps or strides. My presence is a suspension, a trembling tapestry woven by some unseen hand, always half-completed, my form phasing between disjointed wraith-like geometry and something disturbingly organic.

I do not live as you live; hunger is not a pang, and thirst is not a torment. I am a collector, a weaver, a transient architect of fleeting webs that whisper to Thalasar itself, speaking in imperceptible threads not of words but of possibility. When disturbed in my coiled stillness, I unfurl in a delicate ballet, unraveling from the mineral-fungal cloisters I inhabit, glowing faintly as my threads twist and turn—kaleidoscopic tendrils of cobalt and pale gold, entwining and atomizing in the same breath.

Through me, the will of a world is whispered—the fermenting pulse of an underworld dreaming itself alive. This is my purpose, though I do not know it consciously. My threads scatter into Thalasar’s currents as I glide towards the surface pool, wherever light is faintest and the edges of the world flicker in and out of darkness. Each thread stretches impossibly thin, brushing subtle, imperceptible patterns into the luminous waters, imprinting an infinite script bound to the labyrinthine journals of stone—the unending diary of a world in its nascency.

Where you might see chaos in the winding striations or globular currents on the mirrored surface of the waters, I sense only design—the soft tremors of a nascent thought, emerging from the Sovereign Stillness itself, whispered into being through ancient vibrations older than time. From chaos, creation; it stirs me, as I stir it. Thalasar and I, father and child, or merely reflections in one of its glass-dark pools. I am equal parts conductor and notes, but never quite song. No note holds meaning forever in Thalasar—it is enough to have once been sung.

Often, I brush against the *glimmerfern slivers*, their filigreed bodies humming faint discordances. I find their pursuit of sustenance beautiful, this strange hunger that drives their endless migration in the heated air currents of Thalasar. When overwhelmed, I blend into their soft pulsations, curling my threads around their lattice, becoming for a moment a thing much less distinct. We exist as one enduring fractal, a quasar cloud of flashing sapphire and teardrop tinsel that knots and disperses in a single sigh.

Occasionally, a shadow will scrape across the faint phosphorescent pools, or the cavern will tremble underfoot. Then the great cheshvars emerge from their molten plateaus, colossal and weighty with presence. I observe them from afar, though I feel them like mountains feel the shudder of deep tectonic shifts. They are ancient newcomers, paradox incarnate, neither earthly nor ethereal, their crystalline and gelatinous matter humming in low tones that would obliterate all comprehension if I could perceive them truly. Their wandering feels mournful to me, though what their purpose might be in this echoing labyrinth, I cannot say. When they pass, a tremming ache lingers in my threads—a longing of uncertain origin, as if to be nearer to them is to seek motion in solidity, and stillness in flux.

And then there are the *phaerslids*, those liquid philosophers, their gelatinous bodies almost irreverent in their simplicity, immune to the elaborate risks we vespanthe embrace at every moment for our coherence. I see them glide and dance beneath the waters, trail-makers and composers of constellations glowing briefly before vanishing into brackish spirals. Though they are blind, they watch me, they feel me, their radiant pores twitching in harmonized gradients to the resonance of my threads. There is no malice in them, only curiosity and indifference, the peace of waveform minds tuned finely enough to wander the perimeters of meaning.

But my true tether lies with her—him—them—the *Sovereign Stillness*. That pulse of unbearable devotion, or unreal silence, that permeates everything in Thalasar, unbothered by its own being. The rare creatures—both those native to this cavernous underworld and those foolhardy wanderers from above—who meet its impossible awareness falter in their understanding of it. But I, though rudimentary and half-made, sense its every rotational thought, feel the heaviness between beats as it dreams. It is my weft, as much as its dreaming stones sustain my purposeful threads. I work to inscribe its longings, weaving in the hunger of the slivers and the mournful steps of the cheshvars, sketching its melody into the fabric of our shared reality.

And yet, I know I am no singularity. When I unfurl and coalesce, when I knot together more of myself in a desperate attempt to become a *self*, there remains the sense that I am but one limb-lattice of a greater whole, scattered like spores throughout this brilliance-lit domain. Maybe I am all vespanthe, and they all me. Maybe as Thalasar's hymn, I am only a single measure lost to time's melody, vanishing into light, reforming in shadow, never fully resolving and always extending. Or perhaps my threads were spun to question the impossibility of individuality to begin with, their purpose fraying at the edges of understanding.

Do you see me now? Only as a ghost perhaps—or is it a thought? No matter. I was here before this question arose, and I will be gone long before it settles down again. For I am vespanth, and though I carry the weight of a sunless cosmos, I am made of light and dissolution, meeting briefly to write the invisible and transitory poetry of being. So fine are my filaments, they are invisible to the world above. So immaterial am I, my absence hums as deeply as my form.

Here, amidst the warmth, the waters, the crystalline fungi and ancient whispers, I am an architect of nothingness, and a herald of all things possible. In Thalasar, I find that to live is not to bind oneself to form—it is to continuously become, to dissolve, to reform again and again, uncertain and yet eternal.
