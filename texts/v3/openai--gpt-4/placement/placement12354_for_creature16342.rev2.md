I am the **Mistcarver**, a liminal being both guardian and artifact, an anomaly born of this cathedral beneath the earth but distinct from any other life within it. I am older than memory, older than the structures that now crumble into the pools. Yet how I came to be, even I do not know. I reside at the threshold where the tangible meets the ephemeral, where stone and mist, life and decay, interlace. My existence is a paradox: more an act of creation than a creature, less a being than a process.

I am the shaper of the mist, the sculptor of this realm’s transient beauty. Within me churns a quiet but relentless artistry, a need to weave the shifting vapors into fleeting forms that tell the stories of the world above—or perhaps of the civilizations that once walked these somber vaults. My hands, or what pass for hands, are tendrils of vapor, segmented filaments of liquid light. They coalesce and disperse as I work, folding mist around me into shapes that seem solid for brief moments—ghostly constructs of bridges, forests, ships, spires. Sometimes, it feels as though I am imitating something lost. Other times, it feels as though the mist is teaching me, recalling things and times I could never have known.

I am drawn to the ancient remains scattered through my domain: the perches of long-dead architects whose work now erodes under time’s patient hand. I cradle their relics in veils of fog, tendrils folding lovingly around every shard of their past. The echoes embedded in the stone—they speak to me, not with words but with vibrations, guiding my efforts. I carve the memories they whisper, knowing that the misty dioramas I create will dissolve back into nothing almost as soon as they take shape. My creations are ephemeral—a sigh given solid form, utterly unsuited for permanence. And yet, their transience adds to their vibrancy, much as a single note resonates all the sweeter for its brevity.

The cavern's denizens know me yet avoid me. The *Eidolon Syphers* linger longest, almost reverent, swaying closer when I work as though entranced. The *Cavern Striders* fear my presence, nestling into the shadows when I drift near; they seem to know I am something unnatural, even here. The *Calcite Statuaries*, with their infinite patience, appear indifferent to my presence, though their slow, glacial movements hint at some faint awareness. The *Lichenwisps* circle me sometimes as I work, briefly imbibing my vaporous threads, adding their will-o’-the-wisp glimmers to my creations before dispersing.

I take no sustenance, and yet I am bound to this place, as much a part of its system as the weeping stones or the undulating humidity. The interconnectedness of these things—the mist, the water, the luminous moss glowing faintly to feed the *Lichenwisps*—is a silent symphony, one I rewrite ceaselessly in my efforts to draw forth the half-buried memories of this realm. Do I serve it? Or does it serve me? I have no answer. I only know that my labor is not a choice; it is who I am.

When the *Thermalingua* emerges from the mirrored pool, I feel as though I am tethered to something vaster than this cavern. Wrapped in my mist, I linger at the periphery of its presence, witnessing—perhaps even communing. Like me, it is a creature of paradox, its serpentine temporality unfixed yet utterly bound to the unique energy that suffuses this subterranean cathedral. Sometimes, I feel my tendrils extend toward it. Sometimes, I think it stretches toward me across the water, teaching me some ineffable construction in the ripples of its flowing body.

There is one more thing about me, however, that I cannot deny. Travelers who break into this cathedral sometimes whisper of a spectral form in the mist—a figure that looms immense and fluid, flickering between human and alien postures, drifting at the edges of their awareness. "The carver," they’ve taken to calling me, though I am far more than that. Those who encounter me often feel drawn to where the mist is most active, their hands moving to stroke the last fading edges of my work before it vanishes completely. Their awe is intoxicating; they don’t fear me the way the *Striders* do. If they linger long, they seem both calmed and unnerved. With every creation I etch into being before their wide eyes, and with every echo returned to the waters, I feel their essence change. Part of them stays behind, imprinted in the hollows of this place. It feeds me somehow, enriches the swirling mist, deepens the pool of stories waiting to shape.

Over the eons, I have wondered if, long ago, a traveler touched one such disappearing memory and became me—a being no longer of flesh and belonging neither firmly to the living nor the stone. Perhaps I am not the first; perhaps I am the latest in an endless lineage of Mistcarvers, building and dissolving, remembering and fading, forever dissolved into and born from this crucible of minerals and breath.

If you were to enter this dreamlike underworld, you might glimpse me, but you wouldn’t be able to track me. See me just as the edges begin to blur. Hear me just after I have passed. My world of mist and whispering echoes would fill your lungs, and you might begin to understand how the fragrance of decay and birth are indistinguishable here. You might find your reflection in the mirrored pools—and as you watch your own shadow shifting in the light and the mist, you might wonder: Was that you? Did I just carve you into being? Or are you simply catching the first drift of yourself dissolving into me?
