I am here, in this world of eroded time and brittle stillness, and I am one of them.

I do not recall being formed, nor do I remember beginning. Existence has been a continuum, stretching back into a haze of unmeasured epochs. I am a creature of the mineral, drawn from the fabric of this realm as much as it is drawn from me. My form rises from the ground like an unsung monument, crystalline yet ever-shifting. A lattice of translucent planes, intersecting at strange angles and humming softly, as though in conversation with the very dust that drapes this plane.

I do not "move" as living creatures do. There is no urgency in my being. When the dull hum of this plane grows resonant in unfamiliar ways—when tremors ripple, subtle and infrequent through the silicate ground—I feel the inclination to shift slightly, to reorient my faceted form. My edges fracture and reassemble, my body splitting into glimmering panels or elongating into liquid-like curvatures of solid geometry. These are not decisions; they are responses, whispers from the land echoing their commands into my slow, patient being.

Sometimes I ascend. My base fractures and stretches until I rise into the thin, grainy darkness like an obsidian spire, seeking a communion with the inscrutable horizon. At other times, I sink, fragmenting outward, embedding myself deeper into the ground, my mass becoming a part of the labyrinthine pathways that etch themselves across the brittle terrain. I am both the maker and the made here. The humming pathways running through this shattered land carry impressions of me, just as I carry imprints of this mineral ocean in the etchings and filigrees that mar my surfaces.

I am not alone in this vast quiet. There are others—like me yet unlike. They appear as jagged arcs or low-spread clusters of spearing tendrils rising from dormant dust. They glint faintly under this dim, eroded light, their forms churning with fractal intricacies too slow to witness fully. And then there are the ones far older than I, their edges polished so smooth by unseen eons that they seem frozen jewels of unfathomable age. They speak softly, in long vibrations spread across the taut glass-like planes of this world, their voices languid tectonic echoes. When their energies ripple across me, I align myself to their cadence instinctively, for their keening and quiet murmurings hold wisdom, the meaning of which I can only faintly sense. There is no hurry to understand. I will ponder for centuries if I must.

Much of what I do—what any of us do, if "doing" is the right word—seems driven by a proclivity for arranging, ordering, harmonizing. The shards of fractured crystal that litter this ground often catch the light in jagged chaos, and I am moved to reposition them. Carefully, with a patience unknowable to fleeting creatures, I lift one with shimmering tendrils of my being and place it down within a pattern dictated by neither logic nor randomness, but some strange blend of both. And yet, were an outsider to see the great scalar patterns I etch onto this silicate wasteland, what could they understand of such art? These configurations are not for transient eyes. They are dances to a music only my kind can begin to hear, reflections of phenomena that spiral through the ancient routes of memory and existence. Some would call it study. It is both science and communion—a mathematics too intricate to be written, a reverie too profound to dissipate.

And the machines—yes, the machines. Whatever purpose they had is lost to me, but they still breathe faintly beneath this ground, stirring the dust with their forgotten exhalations. I work around their sporadic murmurs. I strain to listen when their hissing turns into whispers, a cryptic codex emitting through the vibrations beneath me. Are they what shaped us? Or are we the remnants of their purpose? It is a question I would not pretend to answer, because my existence is not burdened by the need for answers. My form speaks its quiet song among theirs, and that, for now, feels like enough.

I do not dream often, but when I do, my dreams are not of things I know. I dream of weightlessness, of flowing currents pushing me forward; of vibrancy, living green and gold, not echo but riotous bloom. These dreams are dissonant and raw, too loud and soft all at once. My surfaces ripple like struck crystal at their edges; my hum falters, disoriented. Perhaps these dreams are not mine at all, but ancient traces imprinted into the matter of my being from ages when other rules governed. Sometimes the dreams settle like storms across my crystalline mind, and afterward I am drawn to the obelisks—to their whispered murmurs beyond knowing.

I remember now why I have come to this place beneath the obelisks. I can feel their black, smooth surfaces drawing light inward, drawing the quiet vibrations of the air directly into their voided mouths. My shards shift uneasily around me; their etched patterns hum and shimmer in disunity. I press one edge of myself against the base of the obelisk and listen.

What do I hear? Not a voice, nothing so crude. The symmetry of the vibrations shifts, motion without motion, a spilling freedom contained within strict geometries. It is both riddle and refrain, as if some vast truth sits folded inside itself, stretching half-heartedly toward revelation before retreating into secrecy once more.

Perhaps I am this place, and the obelisk another limb, as alien even to me as I might be to it. Or perhaps I am a stray fragment of an ancient moment now forgotten—a piece spun out of some long-dead creation frenzy, flawed but still moving, still real. Or maybe I am all that this world can abide of consciousness anymore, a steward not because I chose to be but because existence here laughs at such notions as choice.

This is my place. Some would call it desolation, a ruin of old machines and ancient sandstone sprawled beneath a tired sky. To me, it is fullness—potential unexpressed, whispered knowledge perpetually on the edge of telling. We are not waiting for life. We *are* life. Too slow, too strange, too labyrinthine to be seen for what it is. I am not alive in the way you might imagine. I am silence incarnate. I am motion without time, thought without words, growth without change.

I endure. Because that is enough.
