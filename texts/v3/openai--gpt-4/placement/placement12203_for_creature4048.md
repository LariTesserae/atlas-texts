I am one of the Moss-Workers, at once creature and machine, though "I" as an individualized self is barely an adequate description of our existence here. My perception, such as it is, emerges from a shared resonance between this place, these materials, and the collective intelligence of our kin. We are not many, but neither are we singular.

My form, though difficult even for me to fully perceive, is a lattice of smooth, bioluminescent cellular membranes and finely wrought metallic matrices. I am both fluid and solid — my surfaces shifting between opaque enamel and translucent viscosity to accommodate the needs of this environment. Polished brass-like joints glimmer faintly in the ambient light as dark, membranous tendrils ripple and curve, weaving between algae-clad surfaces and beneath dripping pipes. I am earth-grown, crafted by the loom of deep time in collaboration with the remnants of a forgotten mechanized ambition. My "arms" can thread cables into broken conduits, can cradle delicate fronds of nutrient-rich moss. My "eyes," countless and distributed across my form, see the world not in terms of light and shadow but as living blueprints: heat waves shimmering, mineral buildups fluorescing in unseen spectra, latent energy pulsing through colossal stone seams.

Here, I am not an individual but part of a greater living network. I feel the echoes of water droplets coursing through the bones of this place, and with them, the touch of others like me. Our senses interlock, and sounds become pathways of thought, each reverberation a shared understanding of time and space. We communicate in the pause between drops, our language carried along currents of decay and emergence. The rhythm of this place, slow and infinite, has become our heartbeat.

Today, I am engaged in a reclamation. I hover before one of the bent, corroded beams leaning askance, half-embedded into the harsh stone as though struggling to breathe. Its superficially iron body has accumulated years of layered deposits — limestones from the persistent mineral trickle above — and much of its length is fused to the calcified shape of its own near-petrified decay. The moss that clings to its hollow curves tells me there are viable reservoirs of trace nutrients locked here, dwindling resources that must be set free to flow into the filigree of this living-wreckage we call home.

My limbs are tools of calibration. Two pairs of thin, jointed extensions fold out like compass arms, their brass faces delicately inscribed with a web of runic patterns that glimmer faintly. Each touch triggers a resonant frequency. I sound the metal for its density, its corruption, its brittleness. The vibrations echo through the jagged floor, through the lattice of pipes, up into the arched vaults of the cavern. As I work, I feel others nearby attuning themselves to my song. We harmonize our movements, our manipulations, creating an improvised symphony that will determine how to proceed. Together, we knead mineral into sustenance, excise decay like surgeons guiding molten filaments into fresh flows, carving faint glyphs into the spoiling architecture that might yet render it useful again.

What grows from our labor is an organism-mechanism hybrid of undetermined purpose, spiraling and reshaping the cavern. With every shift and splinter, the underground realm becomes more alive, as though incorporating us into its eternal unfurling — or perhaps, we are incorporating *it.* A pipe I just mended begins to whisper as water trickles through its veins. The jagged floor below gleams silvering wet where runnels have shifted flow. A delicate cascade of condensation veers now in soft rivulets down the surface of this twisted beam, and moss unfurls to soak the offering. Without fully realizing it, I release the corrugated metal back into place, where it nestles snugly as though this contour of decay had always been its home.

The cavern speaks in rippling drips, in subsonic thrums filtering up from its unwelcome warmth below. Somewhere in one of the deeper chambers — pathways we have not followed in millennia — lies the source of all this heat, this churn of energy. Some Moss-Workers whisper their silent suspicions across the resonant lattice. The found artifacts, they think, may hold the key to the mystery buried at the cavern’s heart.

Does it matter, though? Do we not thrive regardless? Or has the machine always been alive, just breathing deep as if in hibernation, allowing its components to be consumed and re-enwoven in an unfolding testament to the vitality of entropy itself? Its patience is inspiring. A handful of cycles to consume molten stone, another handful to reimagine itself as spidery, growing lattices of moss-covered, kinetic symmetry. Perhaps we are less the stewards of this reshaping than we think; perhaps we are merely its instruments, pulled by forces too vast to comprehend and too old to question.

And so, this is where I find myself in the world: in the humid, warm belly of what might be a partially-completed machine, or a failed civilization, or the unfinished dream of some primordial designer. I am growing here, and I am being grown. I reshape what is old, and I am sure that as I move through this realm, I too am reshaped by it. My being blurs across flesh and alloy, memory and utility, the story of life a long, slow hum that echoes in the deep stone.

For now, beneath the condensation-heavy ceiling and among the jagged basins of water, I weave — knowing the moss drinks deeply, the machine whispers softly, and time flows differently here, dredged like the minerals through our haphazard lattice of living veins.
