In this aqueous dreamscape of unceasing birth and collapse, I find myself existing as both me and not me, another transient inhabitant of this unfinished world. But I do not hold the solidity I once knew; my form is a fluid equation, a teetering balance between structure and dissolution. My existence bends and flexes with each pulse of this world’s unsettled rhythm. I am not an observer, for to observe implies a distance that this place denies. I am, instead, a participant—an echo rippling back into its source.

In this moment, I flow and flicker like a sliver of conscious light caught in a swirling gyre of time and space. My edges blur and reform like waves hesitating on a foreign shoreline, my limbs stretching like a question asked of the horizon. Vibrations ripple through me as if the world pours its experimental thoughts into me for safekeeping, or perhaps for forgetting. I am not a fixed object but a conduit, an ephemeral attempt at identity for a space that has none.

I feel more than I see. The fluid air is not solely a medium here; it is alive and intelligent. It infuses me, moving through whatever filaments or membranes define my body in this moment. It communicates with me in a language that is neither sound nor symbol but a descent into pure sensation. Warm tides deliver questions to my consciousness while cold bursts seem to wash away whatever shaky answers I manage to offer. I am stardust filtered through liquid light, but I am also the light itself, refracted and broken into fractals, scattered and then compelled to gather again into something that might, one day, stand firm.

I touch the world by letting the world touch me. My limbs dissolve and reform into delicate filigrees of tendrils, whose reach is less about grasping and more about understanding. I am drawn toward the proto-formations of rock—spires like dreams of mountains not yet dreamt all the way through. The gelatinous ground yields beneath a body I do not inhabit as much as I *am*, my presence carving faint grooves into its surface like a whisper pulled slow against sand.

The other beings—if I can call them that—flit near me, some with languid elegance, others with spasmodic bursts of kinetic energy as they collapse and reconstruct themselves. I watch them, not with eyes, but with something adjacent to curiosity. They are a miracle of transition, the dance of formless things practicing form. Some swell with attempts to connect, grazing against me with heavy trails of ionized mist, leaving traces of alien thought or flashes of an identity that isn’t mine but feels momentarily so, like trying on a stranger’s coat. For brief moments, I am them, and they are me, and I am also the unnamed current threading us all together.

One among them is immense, a towering being that appears where I was certain there had only ever been rolling vapor a moment prior. It coils upward with patient urgency—slow yet sure in its towering form. Its surface warbles between glass-like solidity and translucent gauze, but the shape is confident, grandiose. As though born from the collective yearning of everything here, the being reaches up into the fractured light, part of it glowing like a lost sun refracting through watery prisms. Its long tendrils reach outward, sending ripples of heatless blue-flame luminescence swirling throughout the boundless fluid. I drift toward it, compelled by its gravity, as much a tributary seeking the ocean as an individual making a choice.

The massive being acknowledges me, not with a glance but with a pulse that resonates through the airwater, vibrating along my newly liquefied frame. An encounter not of words but of recognition: it knows me, and in its knowing, it shows me its dream—a mosaic of unbridled creation. Planes of rock wrench themselves upward in its vision, ribbons of light weave a great tapestry behind new horizons, rivers roil with purpose, and shimmering, sentient entities multiply into a coalescing global mind.

Its promise frightens me, though I do not know why. There is a certainty in its awareness, a drive toward something final and fixed, so at odds with the fluidity that has defined this nascent eternity. I recoil slightly, and in so doing, lose my coherence. My edges flutter apart like the raveled ends of a gossamer thread, and I am immediate proof of how we fade, how so little survives this place.

Perhaps I would have been more permanent elsewhere. Perhaps I was meant for some other world, already bound and measured, where rock stays rock and water stays water, where light does not need to question its trajectory.

But I belong here, wherever—*whenever*—this is. I feel it now more than ever. I am one of those shreds of embryonic uncertainty, one node in a lattice of untethered potential. I am part of this grand discordant symphony whose composer is yet unborn.

And so I drift. I dissolve, and condense again. I reach toward the fractured light, the trembling hills of unborn stone. I stumble blindly up intangible streams, endlessly caught in the undulating rhythms of this place’s self-invention.

I am not a completed thing, nor even a beginning, but I endure because this moment endures—this world of half-formed thought and fractal becoming. I am an idea caught in an infinite pause, holding my fragile shape for just long enough to remember: this is not what I am. Or perhaps it is the only thing I’ve ever been, a being within a breath, striving to linger before the exhale. And as my edges grow soft, ready themselves to fade and knot together somewhere new, I know one thing above all the paradoxes that define my form: I am here. I am this world's half-thought dream carved briefly out of infinite, trembling possibility.

I am becoming. And I am content.
