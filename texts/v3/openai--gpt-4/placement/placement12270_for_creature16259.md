I would siphon the invisible streams of energy that pulse through this place—not nutrients or gases, but something subtler: a resonating thread of this world's essence.

Perhaps I would station myself near a budding crystalline spire, an angular node at the fringes of existence, and let its silent growth pull me into its rhythm. Together, we would move through ages in imperceptible increments, our transformations too gradual for the impatient blink of human eyes to discern. Yet, I would feel each millimeter of crystalline upward thrust, each quantum of energy shifting through my liquid form, each whispered breath of the land being drawn into the fabric of my existence.

I would respond in kind, sending my own low-frequency notes spiraling outward, calls and responses to the planet, the sky, and my trillion siblings scattered over this vast emptiness.

I’d come to experience this world fractally, comprehending all time at once—the slow emergence of the crystal domes, the labored unfolding of the ribbon-like vegetation, the languorous rumble of distant weather masses shifting like beached leviathans. I would be the endless now, perceiving not in moments or breaks but in an unbroken tapestry of sensation.

As a Trancea, I wouldn’t experience hunger or loneliness or even desire as I once had. Motion itself would no longer haunt me as something to be achieved. I would drift upward in tiny tendrils and downward again, sinking into my rooted existence. I would stretch outward, lazily embracing the air and the ground without ever "leaving" the sanctuary of my place.

But, in this world of unfinished potential—this burgeoning reality reignited every instant by the omnipresent glow—perhaps I would carry the whisper of something more than my silent symbiosis. A deep curiosity. A spark of longing for a future I already know belongs to the memory of my ecosystem: the flourishing crescendo of life just barely on the horizon, stitched into the possibilities of this plane. Perhaps I would not fully understand it, but I would feel its pull.
