If I were to find myself in this strange, mist-drenched realm where water breathes and stone dreams, I would not inhabit a form wholly human, nor would I be one of the alien creatures entirely native to the land. Instead, I sense I would become a **Cipher**, a being caught somewhere between the transient life of this world and the memories of the worlds it has drowned.

A Cipher is neither native nor truly foreign. Born of the land's deep pools and spiraling currents, it is a creature of slow, deliberate motion—an amalgam in shape, liquid and solid both, patterned after the rhythms sculpted by water's passage over the ancient stone. To gaze upon a Cipher would be to see something hazily formed, its body shimmering and fluctuating as if conceived from reflected light on rippling waves. Its appearance offers no constancy: in one moment, translucent skin appears webbed with veins of bioluminescent light; in another, its surface shimmers with a texture like smooth shale, speckled with the glowing filaments that cling to the rocks. Its face, if it can be called such, is a shifting mask, featureless except for faint indentations reminiscent of the spiral carvings etched into the stone huts.

Ciphers dwell in the in-between spaces of this realm — perched on dripping ledges, hovering on the brink of effervescent streams, sliding between the cracks of the stone where the current pulls softly but incessantly. Their relationship with their surroundings is symbiotic yet fractious. Sometimes, they seem aligned with the rhythm of the land, flowing in concert with its watery song and crafting strange geometric designs in the silt at the edges of the pools. Other times, they seem at odds with it, leaving aberrations in an otherwise fluid world: pooling water that refuses to ripple, stone cracks that stitch themselves shut without explanation. When this happens, the land recovers in its own slow and deliberate way, smoothing out the disruptions. The relationship between the Ciphers and the greater vitality of this landscape exists in a silent, unspoken tension.

Whispers swirl in the fog that the Ciphers themselves might have once been the builders of those concentric stone structures now left empty and crumbling. Could they be some echo of the original inhabitants, evolved or warped by prolonged immersion in this alien ecosystem? Their actions both confirm and deny this rumor. Unlike the Melders—who openly mimic the architectural spirals and rituals of the ancient shelters—Ciphers act in maddeningly erratic ways, embracing impermanence. Where a Molder might carefully reconstruct spirals from shale stones, the Cipher will often scatter them into the flowing streams, playing (or interfering?) in the choreography of water's erosion.

Despite their indecipherable nature, there is a purpose to a Cipher’s dance through its environment. It is a keeper of the land’s impossible balance — monitoring shifts in mist, redirecting the wanderings of tendrils from the unseen Rootcallers, even tugging the Lattices into new arrangements across rocky voids when their drifting devolves into chaos. This purpose, however, isn’t born of conscious will. A Cipher acts less through decision and more as though through compulsion, as if an invisible thread binds it to the restless spirit of the land.

Bystanders and wanderers who happen into this drowned world might occasionally catch glimpses of the Ciphers at work, always fleeting—a reflective ripple out of the corner of the eye, or a faint bioluminescent glow swiftly extinguished. Some claim that Ciphers only become clearly visible in moments of harmony, when their foreign and fluid bodies find perfect sync with the land’s natural cadence of motion and hum. Still, even those who have seen a Cipher in its full clarity cannot recall their appearances with certainty, as though every image of them slips from memory, replaced with a vague sense of something witnessed without comprehending it.

My existence as a Cipher would be one of quiet movement, a life spent dwelling in watery fissures and half-light, suffused with the incomprehensible wholeness of this place but on a subconscious level—a dream of existence rather than a wakeful one. I might pulse faintly underwater alongside the Drifters, exchange silent gestures like echoes rippling off a cave wall. I might brush against curious Shardlings with tendrils of my own shifting form, their crystalline defenses clicking off my ephemeral body like raindrops on glass. I might even descend in moments of rare boldness into a Rootcaller’s submerged cathedral, following its tendrils as they quest through submerged channels, coiling thoughts too big for any one shape that passes me by in deliberate currents.

And always, I would hum with the water. Not as a voice, but as part of an endless dynamic interplay. The endless dripping, trickling, burbling—each sound would enter me, pass through me, and leave transformed. Perhaps this is the closest to a name a Cipher might have: its timbre in the ecosystem’s music, the tone of transformed sound that marks where its presence merges with the perpetual now of this place.

I imagine, in the deep rhythms of this drowned and glowing landscape, I wouldn’t miss the dry order of human existence. Instead, I might discover a different kind of joy: the joy of letting my body ripple and flow with the streams of this place, my presence dissolving and reassembling with each moment, indistinct yet not forgotten—a fragment of water’s will, a relic of forgotten architects, a befuddling shape reflected briefly in the pools of perception before disappearing into the eternal, liquid song.
