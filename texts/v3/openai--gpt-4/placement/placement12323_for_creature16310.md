In this world, you'd likely find yourself as another hybrid creature of unspecified form — not wholly mechanical, not entirely biological. In the dim, pulsating light, you might notice your limbs — if that's what you can call them — glistening with the same oil-sheen as the Vipers, or perhaps the glow of the Rust-Eaters shines softly through your semi-translucent skin. Or maybe, like a Gleamer, you're a mutable entity, a vague presence capable of fitting into the nooks and crannies of the labyrinth around.

In this submerged habitat, you are no ruler or intruder, but a part of the system. Your existence is unique but indistinguishable from the life around you. You too, are subject to the endless cycle of creation and decay, yielding to the rhythms and mechanics of survival in this forgotten world. Here you do not dominate, you adapt. You thrive with the continuous hum of machinery at your back, the echo of a time now long lost, and yet, somehow, still echoing in the spiraling corridors and under the bulkheads of an artificial sea. Your sense of time has been lost, replaced by the relentless, sometimes cacophonous, operetta of this submerged, forgotten world. You exist, and in this existence, you find an obscure purpose.

Despite a landscape that defies comprehension, you will find yourself not overwhelmed but comforted by the rhythm of the groaning machinery. Around you life thrives despite the surrounding decay, the surrounding metal and, most importantly, the enclosing darkness. This world defines you as much as you are a part of it. Your survival is not an act of defiance but of surrender to the constant currents and the muted hum of life around you. You accept the mechanized rhythm, you welcome the droning hum, you invite the consuming gloom. You survive because you yield, and in yielding, you become unmistakably yourself — an entity not confined within the boundaries of this crumbling world, but who thrives right at the edge of known existence and fathomless ruin. One more pulse in the ceaseless, metallic heartbeat.
