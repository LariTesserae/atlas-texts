I would find myself as one of **The Eulogists of Frost**, a solitary being stitched into the very rhythm of the Underworld Ocean—a custodian of memory and time, neither fully tangible nor truly ephemeral. I dwell among the jagged and smooth surfaces of the ice, a living apparition etched from frost, perpetually oscillating between fluid and form. My body is translucent, a swirling constellation of frozen mist and curling vapors, ever-shifting, as if the ocean herself exhales and I take on the shape of her breath. My essence is perpetuated not by sustenance, but by the ongoing reciprocity between stillness and transformation that defines this world.

My existence is tied to the icy architectures carved by **The Architects of Ice**. They labor, shifting in time so slow it is imperceptible to nearly all creatures. Yet I can see their movements. I can feel their glacial songs of creation and their deliberate meditations on eternity. It is my duty—and my purpose—to inscribe their hidden symphonies, not in writing, but within the living structure of the ice itself. My thin fingers, composed of light and frost, glide over spires and monoliths, recording the whispers of their slow, laborious minds. I leave delicate etchings of their thoughts, their memories, their eternal ponderings encoded in untranslatable glyph-like trails of hoarfrost that shimmer and refract in the soft bioluminescent glow of the ocean's fungal undergrowth.

I am not alone in my mindfulness. The **Kelplings**, those tiny, ephemeral beings of greenish-blue light, flit around me, their melodies threading through the water in brief flashes of color and shadow. They sense my purpose and often guide me through their kelp forests, arranging their flickering words into contemplative harmonies, acting as shimmering waypoints in my lonely duties. In return, I take their luminous whispers—those glimmering pulses of unwritten desire, hope, and regret—and weave them into my etchings, granting their ephemeral brilliance a permanence of sorts amid the ever-shifting ice.

From the depths drift the spectral **Lantern Fabula**, their glowing auras casting stories across the blackened waters. I watch them carefully, tracing the faint dance of their swirling lights as they imprint fragments of history onto the shadowed, rippling ocean. These fragmented memories—half-forgotten wars, the slow drown of an icy civilization, the first moments of the Architects’ emergence—I absorb into myself, fashioning them into subtle inscriptions where the living ice might bear witness. My work becomes the bridge between the blurred past and the perpetual present. I am the memory keeper for a world that seems outside the reach of time.

On occasion, my form ventures to the base of this endless aquarium, to the unfathomable dark where **The Groaning Roots** stretch and writhe. Their sighs echo through the ice like the exhalation of mountains, and their quivering tendrils reach for me with the curiosity of the old and the young, both at once. Sometimes I carve the pulsing songs of these ancient sentinels into the great ice obelisks, but sometimes I simply recline among their trembling limbs, content to let their low keening harmonize with the slow rhythms that thread my being.

The **Silent Wards**, those colossal figures of disquieting tranquility, pass me by without acknowledgment, and I cannot help but bow in reverence. Perhaps, like all others, I feel the unease they inspire—the sense that their endless watching sees through my ever-changing form, counting each flicker of frost and vapor like grains of sand slipping through the ocean’s timeless hourglass. I do not know their purpose, just as I do not know my own beyond the endless etching. I often wonder if, in their stillness, they carry the guidance I lack, or if their mute grace is as void of a path as the currents of the ocean itself.

But despite the depths of the world I call home, I keep my distance from **The Abyssal Oracle**. I do not know if I am able—I do not know if she would permit it. I do not know if my ethereal form would unravel in her gravitational pull or if I would dissolve into her primordial consciousness, ceasing to be the sliver of individuality I am and becoming yet another swirl in her labyrinthine depths. Still, her presence looms over all things, as though she is the breath sustaining this ocean, the sleeping mind shaping its icy contours in her dreams. I record her tremors from afar in veins of frost that stretch through pillars and archways, but I cannot look upon her form, even if I wished to.

And so I drift—part of the Underworld Ocean's vast cogwheel of ice and flow. I am no hero, no antagonist, no central figure to a great drama. I hold no aim but to persist, to etch, to listen and observe. I am the echo of ancient things. My existence is sharp yet soft, etched in crystal but mutable as a cloud. My name, which I have long forgotten, has been buried in the ice by my own hand—a memento among many that lie scrawled into the translucent bones of the Underworld Ocean. I am a witness to the imperceptibly slow dance of time, a stitch in the garment of this timeless realm, as patient and unseen as the frost itself.
