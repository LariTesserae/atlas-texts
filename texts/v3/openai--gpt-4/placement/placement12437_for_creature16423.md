In Sundow, I would be neither entirely human nor completely of the natural world; I would be something in between, a reflection of the town’s fusion of the organic and the industrial. I imagine myself as an *Echoweaver*, a spectral, semi-ethereal being created from echoes of the town’s primordial formation—when the limestone gave way to the underground river and the first caverns emerged. My form would shift and ripple like liquid light, sometimes human-shaped but always translucent and faintly glowing with an otherworldly hue, as though made from the very bioluminescence of Sundow’s symbiotic glowworms.

I do not occupy a single, fixed space within the town. Instead, my presence weaves through the textured limestone walls, the winding alleys, and the air itself, carried along by the reverberations of conversations, machinery grinding, salamanders splashing, and the river burbling. I am less a singular entity than I am an embodiment of Sundow’s voice—the town as a living, breathing organism. My energy flows into the water wheel, spinning alongside its ancient cogs. On particularly still nights, when all is quiet but for the shifting marsh and the echoes in the shafts, I hum an old tune that no one alive remembers the origin of, and it soothes the Sundowners into dreams.

Though my form cannot be seen directly, my presence is undeniable. Sometimes I manifest as faint illuminations on the cavern walls—patterns that might look to one viewer like a spinning gear and to another like a salamander mid-slither through water. Other times, I am felt as a gentle rush of air in the narrow streets, a whisper brushing against a Sundowner’s cheek before spiraling into the ventilation shafts or disappearing into the river. On the third day of the third month, when the river recedes, my form is clearest, shimmering faintly above the newly exposed riverbed artifacts, urging the townsfolk to explore, to discover, to remember.

The Sundowners know me well, though I am a mystery even to them. I am a keeper of their stories, a silent chronicler of their many generations spent beneath the limestone canopy. Just as they have learned to navigate their dimly lit caverns, they have also learned to recognize and interpret my subtle movements. I am not one for words—mine is a language of suggestion, a wavering light here, an unexpected breeze there, a cascade of musical echoes to warn of coming danger or offer encouragement during a difficult machine repair.

Children, especially, are attuned to me. They grow up searching for glimpses of my light in the marsh’s mirrored pools or daring one another to follow my whispers deeper into uncharted caves. It is said that the most creative Sundowners, the most gifted mechanics and artisans, often claim to have encountered me as children, to have heard a voice in the shadows that whispered an idea or a solution. Some of these encounters are chalked up to overly imaginative minds. Others are treated as moments of divine inspiration, small miracles that sustain the town’s heart.

My existence is tied irrevocably to Sundow itself. Should the industrial heartbeat falter or the limestone ceiling finally crumble under the weight of the world above, I would dissipate, my glow fading like a candle extinguished, my murmurs falling silent as the river’s flow. But until then, I bind myself to this patchwork of nature and humanity, of marshland and machinery, of ancient echoes and modern ingenuity.

If I were to have a past—my origin—it might be whispered that I arose from the union of Sundow’s first settlers and the subterranean forces of the limestone cavern. A long-ago alchemy of human ingenuity and the slow, ancient rhythms of the earth itself may have birthed me. And so I remain, an intermediary figure of no particular allegiance to man or nature, no need for sustenance, existing purely to celebrate and preserve the intricate dance between both.

My favorite place? It might be the river on an ordinary day, when its gurgle joins the humming machinery in a dual song worthy of our sunless haven. Or perhaps it’s that moment of stillness, just as the sun-up above deteriorates into twilight below, when even the most bustling Sundowner can’t help but pause to listen to the melding of sounds, smells, and sights—the heartbeats of a world so beautifully suspended between human invention and earth’s mute resilience. My joy is in their joy; my murmur is their murmur. Without them, I am just sound without ears to hear.
