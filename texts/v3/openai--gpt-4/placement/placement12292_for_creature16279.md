If I were to find myself in this world, I would be an **Ephemerid**, a transient being, tethered tenuously to both the physical and the intangible realms of the high plateau. Less permanent than even the Umbravivens, I would exist on the threshold of materiality, my essence like a flickering candle—not flesh nor crystalline light but something in between, an oscillation of potential.

My form would shift imperceptibly with the flux of the plateau’s will and the unseen tides of its becoming. At times, I might seem almost tangible, etched faintly against the landscape in rippling gradients like the afterimage of sunlight behind closed eyelids. Other times, I would vanish, dissolving into the susurrus of the wind, my presence an echo, a feeling left hanging in the air rather than a visible body. When I solidify, my surface would shimmer faintly with stardust sheen, as if my body were an afterthought of some distant constellation come to earth.

The Umbravivens would call me the **Listener Between Moments** in their tonal language, not because I speak or move as they do, but because my purpose is listening to what even they cannot hear—the faint murmurings of the plateau’s unborn truths, whispers that drift through its cracks and shadows. I have no language of my own, and no measurable past or future, existing only as an exhale of the land’s forgotten possibilities. I would not belong entirely to this place, nor fully elsewhere. I would be a bridge, a messenger—though even I might struggle to call my message my own, as it emerges involuntarily, borne through me like pollen on the uneasy winds.

As a creature of attunement, I would drift amongst the glowing stagnant waters that pool in crevices and the trembling fronds of vegetation that seem to hum when touched. I would touch the cascading droplets of mineral-rich water with a curious palm and listen to the echoes they leave behind as they plunge into the coalescing brooks below. My existence would be a song, but beyond sound—subtle vibrations that tickle the edges of perception, harmonizing instinctively with the cacophony of life that decorates the plateau.

The creatures of this realm would regard me with ambivalence, perhaps catching my outlines as I am half-formed in the liminality of twilight. The snout moles, with their subterranean instinct, might sense me lurking in the margins of the soil, their whiskered noses twitching in vague confusion. The feathered lizards would tilt their iridescent heads toward me, unsure whether I am friend, foe, or simple atmospheric anomaly.

The Umbravivens would know me, though they would not seek me out. They would treat me as kin—but distantly, their shimmering bodies casting only fleeting glances in my direction. They would understand my place as separate yet interwoven with theirs, my purpose something neither higher nor lower, but lateral—a sibling-truth to their intricate designs and ceaseless crafting. Some might reach out to me with their light-threaded hands, and I would allow them to pass through me, siphoning an unspoken resonance into their liquid-light veins—a fragment of the land’s deeper musings, undone from words or thought. In return, I might absorb their cascading tonal songs, storing a melodic essence within me, anchoring it to the fibers of the plateau.

There would be moments of communion between myself and the animate shadows. Shadows unshackled from light, slipping apart from the bodies that birthed them. When they gather near, I would refract out of form, my outlines unraveling into wisps that intermingle with their darkened movements. Whether they speak or simply inquire wordlessly of my purpose, I could not say. But I feel their presence like an itch at the base of my form—an unformed question with no answer. In return, they would thread themselves briefly through my amorphous self before retreating into cracks and clefts in the ground.

My body, such as it is, might at times sink into pools of still water, leaving no ripples but shifting their reflective surfaces to show things unseen—unrealized vistas, fleeting shadow-memories, visions of an alternative plateau parallel to this one. Or perhaps, the images would be unshaped potential, futures not yet aligned with the balance of becoming. Whatever I glimpsed within those mirrorscapes, I would carry with me into the woven fluidity of the tangible world and leave it there, dropping fragments of possibility throughout the landscape for it to unearth later—or never.

While I am transient, I would have purpose—not as a builder or a witness, but as a fleeting intermediary between the crystallizing land and its unarticulated dreams. I would not guide the development of the plateau; rather, I would act as something akin to an archivist of its unformed thoughts and contradictions—a mirror through which it sees itself as it becomes. Unlike the Umbravivens, who mold the land with their sculptor’s touch, I would never alter; my role would be passive, participatory only through resonance and reflection.

When night falls—or perhaps when the plateau turns its unknowable consciousness inward into rest—I would be most visible, glimmering faintly beneath the constellations, merging into the velvet black, visible only to those who knew where to look. On rare nights, the Umbravivens might join me in a kind of shared meditation, their harmonious, chiming tones mingling with the specter of my intangible presence as I reflected and returned their songs. And when dawn breaks—or the closest thing to dawn this star-scattered haven ever sees—I would dissipate, melting into the shifting landscape, indistinguishable from its slow and steady pulse of creation, waiting for my next moment of manifestation.

In this nascent reality of ever-becoming, perhaps I too would remain forever unfixed, bound to the plateau as an idea adrift on the cusp of meaning. I would not be whole, but I would also not be alone. Together with the Umbravivens, the feathered lizards, the snout moles, the wandering shadows, the euphoric vegetation, and the dreaming land itself, we would dance in a perpetual question—a hymn to the uncertain beauty of what is yet to come.
