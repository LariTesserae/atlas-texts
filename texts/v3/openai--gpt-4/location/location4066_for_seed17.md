Under a seemingly endless sky, the world here unfolds itself in a strange paradox of super colossal scale and stark emptiness; a vast underwater realm plunged in the gentle embrace of a cold, ceaseless deluge from above. The waters run deep, their borderless expanse only made more remarkable by their utter solitude from the world above.

This is a world of water and stone, a realm subtly changed by never-ending sequences of erosion that leave an evolving topography of young, sharp features across the towering highlands, stark against a landscape carved by the pressures of the deep. Silhouettes of mountains and plateaus, their tops nearly kissing the surface and bases hidden in ancient abyssal darkness, form enchanted, deserted islands of stone in the planet-wide ocean. Here, the concept of 'land' has been redefined, for nothing resembling soil or sand exists. Instead, unbroken continuums of rock melt away into the nether, their eroding fragments like specters floating in the cold void below, a sparse population of minerals lost in an all-encompassing sea.

Vegetation is close to non-existent; there are no vibrant shrubberies or mossy rocks. Yet, delicate colonies of extremophile microorganisms cluster in sporadic mineral deposits, bridging the gap between life and inorganic reality. They glow in the cold, eerie depth, a flameless phosphorescence that paints the caves and cracks in eerie, beautiful luminescence.

Here, at this water's edge of existence, pass the remnants of a world unclaimed by time. Scattered amongst the colossal pillars of stone and concealed below the surface are remnants of structures, partial remains of an ancient civilization lost to the annihilating flows of time and water. Industrial machinery of robust make and bewildering design, resembling factories or submarines, lie dormant and rusting deep beneath the waves. These are monuments to a long-forgotten age, a testament to the endurance of ambition against the great abyss and time itself. 

Bizarre aquatic fauna roam this seemingly desolate terrain, engendered by a unique evolution path and engineered by extreme conditions. Unlike the denizens of shallower waters, these creatures glow neon in the deep blues, forming a bioluminescent canvas of vivid, flowing art in the depths. The enormity of some of these creatures is humbling, their vast forms further emphasizing the ethereal emptiness around them.

In this never-changing realm, the most significant presence isn't found in the mountainous terrain, the ancient edifices left behind, or even the magnificent creatures that call this ocean home; it is the sound. The baritone songs of massive marine life forms resonate through layers of water, escalating in intensity to form a fabric of primal symphony - it is deafening, awe-inspiring, a testament to life’s tenacity in the harshest of environments.

At intervals, the pitch-black gloom of the deep-sea world gives way to the streams of daylight filtering from above. From the water's surface to the deepest depths where light could reach, an otherworldly glow dances in this world of perpetual twilight. It is the only real source of regular, piercing light in this world, with the exception of the constant glow of bioluminescence.

These are the strange realities of this planet-wide ocean, a testament to the wonders of universe we inhabit, where the seemingly impossible becomes tangible, and the line between fantasy and reality blurs just a little, revealing its magnificent weirdness.
