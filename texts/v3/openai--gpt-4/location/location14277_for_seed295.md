The air sticks slightly to your skin here—warm, moist, and heavy with the earthy breath of a living world. The ground beneath your feet is spongy, near-slick with dampness, the moisture seeping up through cracks and crannies in the outcroppings of pale, ancient stone. It is a place caught forever between rainstorms, where water comes not in rushing torrents or flowing rivers, but in beads and pockets, clinging stubbornly to flat surfaces, pooling in hollows worn smooth over the years. The occasional whisper of rain drifts down, unannounced and unhurried. A fine mist hovers in the air at all times, veiling some things, accentuating others, a soft filter over the life-swollen world.

You are high above the plains, but you would never see them. The sky is pressed flat against this land, thick layers of cloud bending light into a pale, diffuse glow—not so much illuminating as outlining. There are no shadows here, only dimness, with the occasional phosphorescent glimmer where damp shapes catch and hold the faint light. Somewhere within the mist-swaddled distances that stretch before you, edges dissolve and horizons are swallowed. This is a world without faraway things, a broad, open space that nonetheless feels close, coiled and holding its breath. The air hums with weight; nothing ever quite moves quickly, though you feel that a great deal is, quietly, alive.

All around are the remains of a vast construction, though not a single nail or straight edge betrays its origins. It is a place completely made, though nothing "man-made" about it. Here, the materials of an unnamed builder have set into equilibrium with centuries of weathering and abandonment, rendered gentle by time and enrobed in thick, unrelenting green. Every surface is layered—first a damp mosaic of mosses and creeping vines, next the wood or stone or chitin beneath, sometimes crumbled, other times unyielding.

Ferns uncoil across the raised causeway upon which you stand, their vibrant fans brushing your arms as though conscious of your presence. Leaves larger than your outstretched arms drip water collected from the mist; here and there, clusters of weirdly regular pods hang like lanterns from lengths of root that dangle from some unseen ceiling. The built environment seems to rise and fall erratically; narrow archways, crumbled spires, and long-buried vaults break through the greenery. These are not ruins but skeletons, worn smooth by the unhurried breathing of the forest that fills every space between pylons and terraces. Once, whatever made these things might have cast them in geometry, in logic, in lines. These meanings are gone. What’s left is something old, patient, balanced in entropy and life, as if the moss has negotiated carefully with the stone—each giving, each taking.

But the most undeniable thing about this place is its sound: an infinite layering of voices that rolls and folds around you at all times, enveloping you in its tidal embrace. Buzzing, chittering, warbling; the smacking of wings against water, the shivering hiss of scale against bark. Leaves quiver under unseen weight. Drips tap musically on hollow wood. Occasional deep rumbles speak of some larger creature passing unseen, its movement resonating through the ground. The dense population of fauna makes itself known constantly—not in bursts, but as a sustained cacophony that has no edge, no end. Fluttering emerges from one mass of foliage while hisses fade into the creaking growl of something unseen to your left. The soundscape has weight; you feel it pressing on your ribs.

The creatures inhabit every niche, from the shadows within the saturated moss to the dangling strands of roots far above. Something scuttles past your foot—visibly insectile but soft-bodied, its carapace moth-toned with subtle shifts of color. Larger shapes roost unseen in the distant spires; occasionally they unfold jagged wings and displace enough air to leave you trembling. You feel watched, though not hunted, by countless eyes—tiny, compound, luminous dots in the peripheries of your vision—a networked intelligence of life that flows across scales of size and time.

Despite the density of life, this is no riot of chaos. Time moves slowly, a deliberate patience leaking from every creak of a stalwart vine or droop of a pendulous leaf. The species here are harmonious, their cycles of interaction unfolding with the subtlety of tidepools cooling under starlight. Whatever creature built this place may have left eons ago, yet the design—its curves, its echoing chambers—is woven into the ecosystem, as though constructed not as a fortress or temple, but as a conduit for life itself. 

You squint into the dimness, trying to peer ahead, but wonder if this place can be seen faster than it can be listened to or smelled. A curious rule emerges: this is a world where no single shape will reveal itself to you without effort, without stillness. It must be coaxed to the surface, to emerge from the shared alliance of air and stone and beast long enough for your eyes to catch it.

This place is whole. Not in the intact precision of its architecture, which has crumbled to roots, nor in the cleanliness of its atmosphere, which is humid and complex, but in the way it abides—unchanging, expansive, alive. If there is a world here with stories to tell, it will unwrap itself slowly, imperceptibly, like a sheet of bark loosening from the trunk of a rainforest tree, like the slow seep of water through hidden channels. You’ll have to listen through the roar for hours—or perhaps for years.
