It is an ocean not of water but of liquid so thick and rich with life that it feels alive itself. A gelatinous vastness stretches from horizon to horizon, faintly translucent, its depths veiled by the emerald light filtering down through an endless canopy of vegetation. You float in the midst of it, as everything here does — nothing swims in the ordinary sense, as there is no open space to swim *in*. Creatures and plants are ensconced together within the medium: buoyed, suspended, entangled, and entwined in a slow, synchronized drift through biology shaped into architecture on an unimaginable scale.

The canopy above is a tapestry of leaves, fibers, tendrils, roots, and blades, writhing gently in the liquid medium. The vegetation is impossible to escape; it forms the very skeleton of the ecosystem. Some of it dangles like vines hundreds of meters long, forming great shafts of faint bioluminescence in the dim, aquatic light. Other growths form massive plates or spires, hollowed and multipurpose. Some spindly organisms coil into vast hollow columns, their interiors inhabited by creatures of infinite variety, from darting, quick-silvered forms that navigate crevices and stalk prey to the slow-moving giants that twist languidly around their built-up plant shelters.
