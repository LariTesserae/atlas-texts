Beneath the surface of an unknown world, a cavernous expanse stretches out beyond sight. The first impression is of absence — no water, no lakes, no rivers, and no glimmering underground streams. This is a dry world, an airless silence pressing in like an unseen cloth. Movement here feels tentative, the coolness of the atmosphere lightened only by occasional mechanical hums in the mid-distance. 

The ground underfoot feels strange: textured like petrified moss or intricately woven fibers—both yielding slightly and resistant to pressure. It is not soil, rock, or sand but a dense mat of living plant material, endless, stretching across the expanse in undulating forms like a green-gray ocean of frozen waves. This plant-life doesn't grow as upright stems or trees but as a sprawling, self-supporting matrix, a kind of colossal organism whose body serves as the foundation, architecture, and ecosystem all at once. Along the upper cavern walls and arched ceilings, long, shimmering tendrils drip downward like green icicles, their translucent green cores emitting faint, diffuse light. They pulse softly, perhaps signaling some internal rhythm, some life hidden within the world-spanning tangle of biomass.

Rising here and there from this vast substrate are spindly towers of intertwined plant matter — delicate-looking, but dense and strong enough to form platforms, bridges, and galleries beneath the hollowed arc of the cavern dome. A network of aerial vines and massive vascular cords weave the towers and platforms into an intricate, fragile-seeming cityscape. The architecture is alive, perpetually in motion, as the plants slowly grow, retract, or shift themselves. Delicate filaments the thickness of a finger crawl into alignment to repair a broken bridge span. Lattices unfurl like blooming ferns to form new platforms. The engineered components of this place are so thoroughly mingled with the organic that they are indistinguishable—fungal turbines spin lazily along some of the towers, their churning work betrayed only by faint sighs in the humid air.

The inhabitants — fauna, if such a term still applies here — move slowly between the structures. They are elegant, spindly creatures, clearly evolved to coexist intimately with the living city. Their forms have a bio-mechanical elegance, plantlike themselves in many ways: elongated limbs that resemble root systems, plate-like "faces" that unfold like petals to reveal sensitive sensory membranes. They climb along the latticework with quiet deliberation, their motions almost plantlike in their stillness punctuated by sudden shifts. It is hard at first to tell whether they are animals, extensions of the ecosystem itself, or expressions of some strange hybridized intelligence.

And yet, hints of industrial-scale technology are woven here as well. Beneath the largely organic canopy of the city, faint glimmers of metal and ceramics can be found embedded into root-systems, or as smooth gliding mechanisms upon which biotic tendrils rest. By one of the central larger domes lies half-constructed machinery on the floors, surrounded by half-organic and metallic streaking rivets giving the plantmatrix an unusual feel.
