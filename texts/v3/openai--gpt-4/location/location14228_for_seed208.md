A pale, waterlogged world stretches wide, a place of primeval gloom and thunderous sound, where the earth itself seems to exhale in endless, resonant sighs. The sky, heavy and pearlescent, filters a faint and uneven light that rarely strengthens beyond the dull glow of polished bone. Everything feels old here—older than memory, older than reason. The land is so ancient, so worn down by eons of exposure to the slow grind of moisture and time, that it seems more liquid than solid. Stones as smooth as weathered driftwood rise lazily from the surface, rounded and featureless, half-dissolved. The terrain undulates faintly, suggesting the remnants of long-vanished hills now reduced to basins and troughs filled with perpetual, shivering pools. 

Here, water is omnipresent but restless, caught up in ceaseless cycles of seep and flow, evaporation and drip. The landscape bleeds moisture—rivulets cascade down shallow gullies, slicking the rocks with a mineral sheen. Tiny geysers spurt in brief hisses from hidden fissures, their steaming remnants quickly condensing into glistening droplets that cling to bare surfaces. Everywhere, water participates in endless, chaotic chatter, colliding and pooling, falling and gurgling, conducting sound throughout the land like the loud, cracked tones of a bone flute.

The air is damp and distinctly chilly, carrying a mineral tang that stings faintly at the nostrils. It surrenders, at intervals, a thick, organic musk—almost fungal in character but devoid of plants, with no sway of reeds or hint of greenery to soften the impression. In this land, life clings stubbornly but unrecognizably. The nearest things to vegetation are strange mats of slick, translucent growth that cling to the rocks and bubble faintly when they meet water. Pallid, gelatinous lichens smudge the landscape in fleshy tones of pink, ochre, and gray, like a wound scabbing over. They pulse faintly with something less like life and more like persistence.

Scattered amidst this landscape are remnants of a prior world—structures of raw stone and fibrous material that crouch low to the warped ground.
