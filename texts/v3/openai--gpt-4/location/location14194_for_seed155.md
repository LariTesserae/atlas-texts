The highlands stretch vast and vertiginous beneath an even, gray-lit sky. This is a place of bleak grandeur, an intertwined world of plant and stone, old and strange, breathing with its own alien rhythm. The air carries a dampness that clings to the skin, but heavy rains never fall, as if the clouds know better than to flood a land shaped so profoundly by dry time. Instead, condensation beads along every surface: on drooping leaves, pale as memory; along flaking metallic railings; in shallow basins etched into smooth stone slabs. Mist curls and clots in pockets among the deep creases of this realm, whispering against the ridges like an old, forgotten language. 

The entire elevation trembles on the edge of the primal and the post-industrial. The bedrock beneath it all is ancient, flattened into a series of high plateaus and rock shelves that speak of countless millennia under slow, grinding winds, whittled and washed into shapes that are too smooth to seem accidental, but too vast to be the deliberate work of human hands. Yet human hands—or minds—have *been* here, long ago, leaving behind the bones of impossible things: spiral staircases carved directly into rock, doorframes opening onto nothing, rusting scaffolds that claw at the misty sky like the skeletons of decayed giants. 

But it is not these remnants of industry that dominate the landscape. Far more overwhelming is the greenery. The lush vegetation is everywhere—cascading down walls of crumbling concrete, twining tightly around decayed smokestacks, erupting from fractured causeways in enormous, waxy blooms. It is not the chaotic wilderness of untouched forests; no, this verdure has an order to it, a structure, as though it took root in line with some ancient blueprint. 

Trees stretch upward in impossible, accordion-like forms, each segment telescoping out of the other. Vines trail and weave among them, forming erratic net-like veils in the open spaces. Ready-to-fall fronds form natural bridges that oscillate slightly in the mountain breezes, leaking clear droplets into the shadowed spaces below. Beneath the greenery looms something even older: petrified roots and trunks from an eon now unrecordable, calcified down to their marrow. The life sprouting here is grafted not from the natural order, but from the fragments of what was.

Within this tapestry of damp decay and verdured growth, there are traces of order, or at least attempts at it. Ancient infrastructure blends with the biome in uneasy harmony. Aqueducts built long ago now serve as elevated waterways brimming with algae and thriving amphibian life. Coils of ancient piping pulse faintly with rhythmic vibrations, as if unseen engines embedded deep in the rock and roots of the earth are still heaving to life beneath the crust.

Noise is omnipresent here, though far from manmade. The creaking of vines in the wind, guttural murmurs of water siphoning through cracked conduits, distant clangs of unstable structures resettling after eons—these sounds overlap and echo through the space. Through it cuts another sound, sharp, bizarre, like a constant low chime that oscillates unevenly, growing louder and softer in patterns that defy obvious source or logic. It hums through the air like a tuning fork vibrating in a far-off world, just faint enough to make you second-guess its very existence.

Amid this chaos of living forms and aching echoes of invention, voids stretch wide with an almost accusatory vastness, filling the gaps where some vital presence or purpose should have been. A settlement still exists here, clinging to the slopes and curves of the overgrown shapes, almost shy in its subservience to the immensity around it. The structures are modest, crafted from harvested vines or nested into the hollows of enormous hollowed-out tree trunks. They hum with crude mechanical power, lit occasionally from within by the soft orange glow of filament bulbs, the signs of mid-industrial ingenuity still thrumming within the folds of this surreal setting.

Whatever powers this settlement, it is part of the world’s strange, living pulse. Crooked wind turbines spin lazily in an almost non-existent breeze. Small factories rumble, producing substances and devices you know almost instinctually are not meant for this world. The few people—or at least human-like figures—who dwell here go about their business with faces both blank and glowing faintly with a sickly phosphorescent sheen that matches the streaks of iridescence found in the plants. In this place, humanity does not conquer; it adapts until it is difficult to tell where skin ends and leaf begins.

Fauna are nearly absent. Occasionally, between the mists and shadows, you might catch the scuttle of something, low and angular, like a crab crossed with a cuckoo clock, its legs clicking rhythmically with the mechanical beat of the pipes. Other times, an enormous winged shadow passes quickly across the sky, far above the highest reaches of the gleaming vegetation—a creature unseen but palpably heavy, its wingbeats displacing even the cloud cover.

The air smells sharp and herbal, tinged with rust, as if the very act of breathing might invite both detoxification and corrosion. If there was language spoken here once, it has been lost beneath the symphony of growth and ruin. Whatever odd laws of nature hold sway here seem warped—plants grow with too much intention, metal crumbles yet never fully vanishes, and sound travels in waves that loop back and forth, recycled atop itself. 

Time here has layered over itself, smothering memory under leaf and stone. It feels neither fully living nor truly dead—the paused outer reaches of a half-remembered world. It could be dying; it could be waiting. It could already be something other entirely.
