It begins with a sound—constant, organic, reverberating. It’s a deep, thrumming pulse that you feel more in your sternum than in your ears, accompanied by weaker echoes and hums, like a forest orchestra tuning its instruments. Intermittently, a sharp cracking echo calls out, like wood splitting far in the distance, and a teardrop plunk is swallowed almost immediately by the landscape’s heavy acoustics. This is the first sign that this place breathes, though not as you do, yet it exhales steadily.

You stand on an elevated platform of sorts but quickly realize that no solid stone holds you steady. Instead, the ground beneath your feet is fibrous and springy—plant matter braided together with an almost architectural elegance, like living tendons and cartilage woven into a load-bearing framework. The platform rises above a valley-like depression, where massive, sprawled-out structures of a similar plant material descend into a tangle below—not quite a jungle, not quite a city.

The world here is as wet as lungs after deep respiration. The air hangs heavy, saturated with mist so fine it veils the distant reaches of the landscape into uncertainty. Droplets bead and slip along every surface, soaking into the plant-work material as if it drinks its fill before releasing it elsewhere. Water flows constantly, but not chaotically; it seeps and dribbles along smooth curves that braid downward and vanishes periodically into circular wells, as though returning to some unseen aquifer beneath. From time to time, you hear a rushing sound, and the wells exhale steam or push up small fountains of water, only for the cycle to repeat. No rivers, no lakes, no oceans, just... movement within and underfoot.

You are high up—possibly on the flank of one of the tall organic forms that rise out of this sprawling structure like spires or cresting waves frozen mid-motion. The world around you remains embryonic, and these towering "hills" or "trees," despite their immense size, feel young and tender. Their forms are not yet fixed; their movements are subtle but constant, as though flexing microscopic muscles. There's no clear sense of erosion here—only growth. Everything seems to emerge from itself, surfaces extending tendrils and tendrils thickening into limbs, slow yet deliberate. It’s easier, in fact, to imagine this environment forming *upward* rather than wearing down under time’s slow pressure.  

The light is gentler, a muted gold that filters through the dense plant growth and cascades in spattered beams where the structures open up, falling in pools on the braided floor. Shadows shift only when the mist curls or when the plants decide, on some inscrutable, silent schedule, to unfurl new fronds above your head, momentarily dimming the glow. Though the light seems natural, there’s something uncanny about its steadiness, as though it originates from everywhere at once, even in the absence of a visible sun. It could almost be the bioluminescence of the plant-life itself.

The plants... Everything is plant and nothing is plant as you know it. Fleshy, rubbery walls. Spiked, barnacle-like growths clinging to vertical planes. Billowing, translucent membranes puffed out like sailcloth over broad, flexible structures that could be roofs—or leaves—or something else entirely. Some tendrils droop languidly into the open space, dripping water with clock-ticking precision. Others are taut as suspension cables, vibrating faintly as air currents pass, adding their soft moans to the symphony of sound.

It is a built place, unquestionably engineered, but this has not been done by hands. There are no straight lines, no sharp edges. The labyrinth below is molded, guided, coaxed into existence by some unrecognizable force, whether it is the plants themselves organizing their growth in response to an unseen dictate or something else, something stranger. Whatever architects shaped this realm, they left no visible trace of themselves, though there’s a disquieting feeling that the forms surrounding you—arches of living tissue, corridors of feathered vines—are not entirely benign.  

And yet, there is no threat, either, no sense of active hostility. This remains a place balanced on the edge of equilibrium. A visitor could climb the living ramps and cross the bridges of sinewy lattice indefinitely without altering its essential condition. Time here seems dormant, biding. The dynamic snap of change has no place here; each day is an echo, repeating itself without variation.

Plant-life may dominate this place unimaginably, but it is not entirely devoid of animal life. Creatures appear in flashes so brief that you almost question seeing them. An iridescent coil retracts into a fleshy burrow, leaving behind a sticky sheen. Glowing eyes peer out from a cluster of spine-covered spheres, then blink out of sight. A shadow swims across the golden mist, vast and fluid, casting distorted reflections that stretch like phantoms across silken walls and water-slicked channels. What lives here reminds you that life, when rare, has spacious room to become strange.

Perhaps the most uncanny aspect of this landscape is its near-perfect stillness in motion. Everything is resolutely alive, from the smallest tendril unfurling to the massive, arching "roots" that soar above the woven ground. Yet the place exudes no haste, no rush, no sense of linear progression. It *is*, and it will *keep being*, until something unimaginably slow and immense decides otherwise.

This is a world drawing its first breaths, not in violent gasps as you might expect, but with deep and measured intention. A dream of permanence blooming in the soft roar of unseen waters and the minute stirring of things not meant for our eyes. A place at once young and impossibly ancient. A landscape of growing sleep.
