It is a place that feels less discovered than it does half-imagined, as though the rules of its existence are still open to negotiation. Even the word "place" feels tenuous here, as if suggesting it might dissolve it back into nothing.

The air is bone dry, almost brittle, and yet alive with ceaseless, deafening sound. Fluctuating drones pound the ear with low, tectonic resonances, rattling ancient instincts awake in a world devoid of conventional life. The sounds are more visceral than auditory, vibrating through the unsteady ground beneath one's feet and echoing across the great divides of the landscape. Here, the pulse of the world itself fills space with alien rhythms, mechanical yet organic, like the birthing cries of a vast geological engine gearing up to run.

This is a high place, an altitude so immense it borders on the surreal. Jagged peaks loom like the serrated edges of a half-carved sculpture, raw and unfinished, their surfaces shot through with colors that defy natural material: iridescent blacks, jarring violets, and a pale, bone-white that glows faintly in the dim, sourceless light. The air is cool but stale, unsweetened by moisture or life. There is no wind, but a faint, acrid tang, as though the very rocks exhale some gaseous residue of creation. The sky above is not a sky at all but a bruised veil of shadowy hues—a palette that hints at what might be up there but never resolves into stars or sun or cloud.

Despite the arid desolation, vegetation persists—a lushness that defies logic, clinging tenaciously to seams and crevices in the uneven terrain. These are not plants as we know them, nor do they feel planted in the typical sense. They are structures of crystalline filaments and muting colors, sprawling outward rather than upward. Their forms seem to pulse faintly with some inner, deliberate energy—vibrations that mirror, perhaps amplify, the roaring hum of the landscape’s alien soundscape. Where their stems touch, they braid and fuse like molten glass, creating strange, fragile networks that splinter and shatter under nothing more than eye contact.

Water is entirely absent, and so the vegetation here must thrive on something else entirely. Light? Sound? Pure vibration? Whatever sustains it, these crystalline growths do not sway with the non-wind or react to motion as plants might; they stretch hungrily toward the peaks above and occasionally emit a faint glow of their own, as though siphoning energy from unseen currents that flow across this embryonic world.

Erosion has not yet authored this land’s history; the features here feel as if they were invented only moments ago, and some seem incomplete. A great spire, fragile as a needle of paper, rises to absurd heights until it simply… ends, abruptly severed, leaving threads of fractal material dangling into empty air. Edges of cliffs show no sign of having ever broken away, yet they fizzle faintly, as though the process of disintegration has started without any visible motion or cause. Everything here feels raw, not unchanging but *unformed.*

Signs of habitation—or perhaps visitation—dot the landscape like incidental punctuation. A half-buried shard of carved obsidian pierced with alien runes sits atop a fractured shelf of multicolored stone, and nearby, another shard, deeply scored as though in the grip of a colossal being. Stout, primitive structures—half shelters, half sculptures—are scattered sparsely across accessible plateaus. These artifacts are rudimentary but utterly alien; there is the suggestion of form and design, but the method of their construction is indecipherable. At times, they seem to shift imperceptibly when you look away, their geometry resolving incorrectly per your return glance. They resemble nothing animal nor mineral, more like a freeze-frame of a liquid mid-pour.

And so—no fauna roam here exactly, though the landscape itself feels disarmingly animate. A certain tension thrums as if the world is a sleeping thing, holding itself taut, anticipating an inevitable rift. There are no scurrying creatures, no flocks, no hunters; there *has* been no time for them to evolve. Still, there is a sense that something massive—or many somethings—are paying attention, and from time to time, ridges of mountain seem to shift, as though the ground beneath reshuffles tectonically to a new beat. The ground’s trembles are not random; they mirror the drones in the air—a slow and ominously deliberate choreography.

There is no clear sense of scale here. An expanse might seem local, navigable, and then stretch suddenly into a vast unknowable dredge of horizonless nothing. The horizon is almost illusory; distance is a slippery thing in this place where the laws of perspective are young and malleable. Stepping forward can feel like an incremental movement across eons or a sudden vault into immeasurable distance.

And then there is the weirdness. It hums at the edges of your perception, harder to pinpoint than the relentless sound, the volatile terrain, or the crystalline vegetation. It shapes this world as though by whim; the frequency oscillations that shake the very fabric of place and being seem less accidental, more directive, as if someone—or something—whispered each atom into its strange stall. The lack of animal life doesn't mean it’s empty; instead, it stews with obscure intent, a space on the brink of resolving itself into clarity or splitting into pieces again.

Here, nothing is completely real, and yet its unreality is absolute. The sound, the wild under-construction drive of the landscape, the bizarre crystal organisms—everything here seems to be in flux, bound by no laws except the intention to become. The realm is waiting for something—or someone—to solidify it into a proper world.
