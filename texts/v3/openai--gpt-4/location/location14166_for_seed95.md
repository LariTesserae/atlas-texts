The air, warm and damp, feels thick with age as you step into this elevated world. The sky is a pale, steady light, neither bright nor dim, casting soft shadows that stretch long across the ancient terrain. Here, time seems to have worn the world down to its essence—a place both vast and intimate, where every stone and crevice feels like it has always been and will always be.  

The land is high—so high you feel the weight of being closer to the heavens, though no winds swirl here to stir any breath or break the stillness. The terrain is eroded to a form so old it might as well have birthed the first rain. With each step, you come across stretches of stone that seem as though they were once mountains but now lie flattened. Here, they sprawl like the ribs of some colossal corpse, worn by time into smooth, undulating shapes. Entire mesas have crumbled into broad, low plateaus that are cracked and pitted like the surface of forgotten moons. The ground whispers a faint crunch beneath your feet—crystals of salt perhaps, or the fragments of minerals long ground down to near dust.  

In this vast openness, you feel hemmed in. Though the land unfolds and folds again towards the horizon in sweeping arcs, the density surrounding you comes not from the terrain but from the life layered into every available niche. The vegetation is sparse, but what lives here claims its space with peculiar determination. Gnarled plants with brittle, resinous leaves perch on ledges and gather in small, stunted clusters at the base of rubble piles. Thin, blackened vines spider across the ground, glistening faintly with dew in the hazy light. Some pulse almost imperceptibly, betraying an unnerving biological tenacity, as though they are not entirely plant but something both more and less alive.  

Yet it's the fauna that dominates this place, weaving arcs of existence across spaces that might otherwise seem abandoned. Strange creatures dominate the senses, their numbers overwhelming, yet their presences ghostly rather than oppressive. Vast flocks of pale, translucent bird-things wheel silently in the sky, their wings barely moving, gliding on air currents you can't feel. Near the ground, small herds of multi-limbed, lichen-covered beasts move in languid synchronization, their legs touching the ground with such care that their hooves leave no mark, as though they weigh nothing at all. Some of these creatures carry their own ecosystems—fungal growths sprouting from carapaces, fern-like fronds bobbing gently from ridged spines, or soft moss carpets trailing from elongated tails.  

There are fleeting bursts of color and motion—the glint of an animal's iridescent carapace or the small flash of bioluminescent tendrils glowing faintly under the shadowed overhang of a rock. Insects the size of a fist hover on slow, syrupy wings, their proboscises tapping against the dew-glazed vines and crevices. Between shadows, you might glimpse creatures that tug the edges of reason—a blend of fins and fur, forms that ripple slightly as though caught between dimensions, things resting in plain sight yet squirming in the corner of your vision.

The built world is nearly absent, softened into subtlety by the eons. Occasionally, you stumble upon what might once have been a tool or a monument, now colonized by creeping life and etched into obscurity by the winds and rains of forgotten millennia. A broken ring of standing stones wears a fur of lichen and moss, its purpose long consumed by this strange ecology. A hollowed-out shard of obsidian, carved by intelligent hands but cracked and filled with still water, might have once been a basin for rituals. You think you spy a carving here and there, but it's as if the rock resists telling you its story, the lines fragmentary and warped with time. These scattered remnants seem to serve no contemporary purpose—museum pieces left outdoors, their creators long merged with the earth.  

Despite the density of life here, there is little sound. Air wavers faintly with a damp, mineral aroma, and the world is draped in stillness so profound it feels heavy. The faintest rustle of an unseen motion will rise and fall, far off, almost beyond hearing—like wind brushing an ear of corn or an animal moving from shadow to shadow. All else is silence, deep and long, broken only by the occasional single cry, strange and echoing, from a place beyond sight. Even the creatures that move through this world seems to respect its solemn, ancient rhythm.  

There is a sense in this place, an almost-tangible quality, of time coiling in on itself. You wouldn't be surprised to learn that nothing ever truly ends here—that death is just another form of presence, and decay is a kind of blooming. Every surface seems to drink light and history, succumbing to slow, wet cycles of birth, erosion, and growth. The world itself, or perhaps something within it, waits—its patience utterly alien, its pacing beyond the quick heartbeats of the animals that scuttle across its expansive ridges and meandering valleys. It's alive, but in a way you cannot quite understand, its processes so vast and contemplative they might as well be asleep. And yet, even in its slumber, it watches.
