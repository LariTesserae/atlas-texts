The Conduit begins in darkness, save for the faintest glimmer of a far-off glow that slides through the murky medium like liquid light split through green quartz. The entire world here is underwater, though it bears little resemblance to any terrestrial sea. Around you, the liquid churns with chaotic violence, currents twisting into one another in impossible configurations—a boiling that never breaks into bubbles. It’s dense, almost gelatinous, yet alive with motion. The fluid hums, a bassy resonance vibrating in your chest and skull, like standing beside the deafening engines of a nascent starship. 

Sinking deeper, shapes start to emerge from the dimness, solid and geometric. An otherworldly city sprawls before you, though "sprawl" is too generous a word—it feels compressed, stuffed into a pocket of existence barely capable of holding it. Structures shoot up and down, tethering themselves to the hard-packed, sedimented ground and disappearing into a yawning, unknowable ceiling somewhere far above. They glisten like obsidian wet with dew. Some are sharp-edged, jutting triangles and cubic monoliths, while others thrum with curved, looping forms—arches and spirals rising and falling like heaving breaths. It all feels ancient yet functional, decayed but in continuous use. 

There’s no natural vegetation here, no algae or seaweed or coral weaving itself into these spaces. When you look at what clings to the structures in patches, it’s unlike anything biological: a lattice of shimmering, crystalline growths in jagged rows, fractal and intricate, as though the architecture itself had bled out and crystallized under pressure. When disturbed by the swirling currents, they release bursts of glowing particulates, which hang in the liquid like luminous spores. This bioluminescent detritus thickens the shadows and complicates the light, creating a kaleidoscope effect that only compounds the sense of disorientation.

The oppressive warmth of the water presses against every pore, a reminder that you are far below—or perhaps far inside—something. Whorls of sediment drift lazily through the space, evidence of long-eroded channels in the hard floors and walls. This place feels like it has been here for millennia, self-contained, energetically repeating cycles of destruction and regeneration. The materials that constitute the superstructures seem impervious to the currents, even as their surfaces wear down to uneven, pitted textures. 

The city is cacophonous. It is as if millions of overlapping noises have coalesced into one ever-present roar—a chaotic symphony of grinding, clattering, dripping, clanging, humming, hissing. It’s a factory, or a city of factories. Some of the noise isn’t mechanical at all: deep ululations ripple through the liquid, distant and guttural, as though some vast creature were groaning in its sleep. In other places, metallic chimes ring like bells, notes colliding and echoing unpredictably. There’s an industrial energy to this place, as if it’s constantly making and unmaking itself. In the turbulence of the liquid, small components—cylindrical, spherical, crystalline, all slick with unknown alloys—drift from one part of the city to another, assembling themselves when they find their place or dissolving into debris when they don’t.

The scale of the space is staggering, yet it feels perversely small. You realize that no substantial distance lies between the structures; the city feels dense, choking itself, forcing its components into closer and closer quarters. Every corridor, alley, or open cavity feels at risk of collapse at any moment from the unrelenting pressure of the warm liquid. And yet, somehow, a strange equilibrium holds: the chaos does not destroy this place but keeps it alive.

About the fauna: At first, it seems there is none, save for the occasional dart of something sleek and sinuous disappearing into a structure or grinding depths. But then you notice subtle movements within the spaces between. Small, flashing figures—they flick in and out of the visible spectrum, tiny pinpricks of bioluminescence in dizzying colors. They flit in sync with the currents yet are not entirely of them. They congregate near the latticework crystal growths, taking turns darting through and over them, as though feeding or harvesting them.
