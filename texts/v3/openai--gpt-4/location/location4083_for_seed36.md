Far beneath the world you might know, there exists a realm unlike anything stumbled across in the open air. The temperature here is oppressive—not the dry, beating heat of desert suns but a humid furnace that clings to the skin like the exhalation of some deep, sleeping beast. The air is the kind of heavy that enforces its presence in every breath, and there's a sticky viscosity here, as though the atmosphere itself might be considered a living thing.

This place lies under the surface of some higher world, though one might never guess it. No dirt or stone presses above in definitive layers—this is not a carved cavern or a mined hollow. Instead, the space feels like an embryonic void, not yet sure how to define itself. The ceiling, distant enough to suggest vastness but low enough to be felt subconsciously, glimmers faintly, as though thinly veiled by translucent membranes stretched taut over some unseen structure. Sunlight filters through these upper layers—too hot, too pure to feel natural—but never apologetically. The brightness is unrelenting, yet bizarrely diffuse, refracted and softened by the strange semi-opaque dome above. 

The ground beneath your feet is anything but stable. It exhales life in every direction: thick mats of living plant matter braided into one another across miles, pulsating faintly as though breathing with a slow, syncopated rhythm. A rainforest sometimes seems chaotic; not this place. Here, the organismal vastness is cohesive. The very floor rises in gentle arcs under layers of vines, roots, strange coiling stalks, and broad, petal-like plates that are as resilient and flexible as engineered textiles. It is the skeleton—or scaffolding—of some unfathomably large, interconnected body. The plants are neither competing nor distinct; they form a seamless, layered, highly-saturated bioscape in shades of greens you've never seen before, tinged with iridescent yellows and pulsing pink veins. It looks alive because it is alive—but its purpose remains unresolved. It has a strange fragility to it, like it hasn't yet fully become.

Moisture hangs thick in the air yet pools sparsely on the floor. Here and there bubbles or rivulets of water seep from tiny orifices in the living ground. These pockets of condensed moisture dot the space irregularly—small but persistent springs feeding dark pools fringed by bioluminescent mosses and gelatinous lichens. While the water infuses the air with an almost suffocating presence, it is not abundant in its liquid form. It feels captured, entrained within the life mass all around, reluctant to move freely.

The landscape is open in a sense, but never empty. Space stretches outward rather than upward, and the horizon consists of more of the same undulating plant-body stretching endlessly. Here and there, massive fungal forms bulge out of the living mat, like enormous chrysalises caught mid-pulse. Some of these bubbles tower tens of meters, their walls translucent and slimy, blurring the outlines of what seems to be developing within. Others collapse in on themselves like ruptured cocoons, leaving only scar-like hollows fringed by expanding vines. It is unclear if the ecosystem shapes these structures or if these structures shape it; neither seems to hold true primacy. 

Artifacts from an unknown world occasionally disrupt the endless green sea. Twined around the plant mass, or half-melded into it, are metallic forms—a fragment of a spire, the suggestion of a bridge-like beam, the hard right angle of something engineered—but these pieces are enigmatic and failed relics at best. They are not tools, not anymore. They feel like accidents stranded here, half-fermented by the ecosystem but not fully integrated into it. Plant-tissue grows both over and through them, slowly devouring their presence as though trying to decide if they belong in the still-forming intention of this place.

As if in deference to this embryonic quiet, life beyond plant matter is astonishingly rare. Here and there, unseen movements within the mat suggest creatures that burrow through its substance—a brief ripple of stalks arching upward, or a faint tremor traveling beneath you. Tiny motes of life flicker in hotter shafts of brightness—things perhaps insectile in nature, small, erratic, but fleeting. Larger fauna seem entirely absent, though the holes in the plant-mass and the abandoned chrysalises might suggest otherwise. Occasionally, a distant sound—a low hiss, a gurgle, or what might almost resemble a sigh—reminds you that *something* stirs, unseen, somewhere. Yet these are anomalies; the atmosphere here largely rests in sacred equilibrium.

The air feels timeless; this place does not change. Or, rather, it changes so slowly as to defy recognition. There are no gradients here—no weather cycles or seasons to deepen its sense of life. Where moisture beads on surfaces or pools in shadows, it does not gather any more or less than it did yesterday, or perhaps a millennia ago. Time itself feels irrelevant. This is not decay, but liminality, a place in such early formation that no clear rules demand growth, collapse, or renewal.

The silence might be oppressive were it not alive with barely-perceptible resonance—subsonic vibrations that curve through the mat beneath your feet, the faint rush of sap moving through conduits larger than you're accustomed to imagining. Listening closely, you'd swear you could hear a kind of pattern, not unlike breathing, or the slow rhythm of some massive heart. It’s not entirely clear whether this is the heartbeat of many smaller connected organisms or that of one great whole. As unsettling as it is steady, this faint pulse urges the thought that all of this—the light, the heat, the plants, the water, the metallic spires, even the sprayed-bright membranes above you—may belong to one colossal biological intent.

And here you are, in a world that does not yet know itself, beneath the surface of somewhere else entirely, among a latticework of connections waiting for something uncertain—waiting for time to become meaningful. ***It isn’t clear whether this place will be finished — or if, suspended as it is between life and its vast, unrealized ambitions, it might decide it already is.***
