The world in this place feels other than alive, but not lifeless. What brims here does not breathe, nor does it root or sprout. It stretches across high crags and shoulders of ancient stone that have long forgotten their molten past. These heights loom large but rounded, their edges long worn soft by an unhurried relationship with erosion. Things take their time here but are never entirely idle. The landscape has arrived, it seems, at the full maturity of its own intent. 

Here, water reigns. Not in torrents or sheets, but instead as a draping presence, more felt than seen. A cold mist clings to everything, dense and tactile, wrapping the stone in gleaming films of moisture, beading along cracks and crevices. Thin rivulets of water, barely more than continual tears, drip downward from hidden pools perched in tilted hollows. The air is soaked and heavy — a luminous haze refracting the brightness of an ever-lit sky into a white-gold sheen. The light is not harsh but persistent, filling every crack, every shaded recess with an unrelenting glow. 

The terrain speaks of endless patience. Tier upon tier of rising plateaus climb high above unseen seas, their strata marked in muted grays and blues, ochres and blacks, each layer the memory of a forgotten aeon. Rocks look polished, as though shaped by unseen hands, though no such hands exist. The smooth surfaces glisten with captured light, fractured occasionally by long, sinuous fissures that delve into unknowable depths. Shapes are implied where none should be — stone forms that swirl into undulating curves and spirals, their deception born of time, erosion, and the play of water on mineral. Yet they remain resolutely, absolutely natural. Nothing is made here.

What might pass for life exists in the form of faint anatomies clinging to clefts and overhangs: faintly gleaming protuberances like slick cords of gelatin, spindles, and wavering fronds rising from pools that never spill. These organisms, if organisms they indeed are, pulse dimly with inner luminescence, their subtle, rhythmic shifts in brightness giving a sense of time’s passage, though nothing else in this stilled world seems to notice. They are slick, insubstantial to the gaze, and damply malleable, sharing form and texture with the fragile clouds that knot and unravel further up the high paths.

Sounds fill the expanse, crowding every moment until the air vibrates with their multitudinous presence. The rush of unseen wind snakes through narrow passes but is muffled by the saturation of the air.
