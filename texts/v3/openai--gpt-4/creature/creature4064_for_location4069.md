At first, you may not see them. You may sense only the uncanny harmony of this place—the balance between intense light and numbing cold, between relentless movement and profound stillness. The inhabitants of this plateau do not appear as singular beings in a landscape but as something melded into the very skin of the world. They are part of the terrain’s story, written into the way water carves its paths, in the shimmer of winged dances, and in the eerie, reflective sharpness of stone and ice.

They are called the Aeren, though they do not name themselves. Names seem incongruous with their presence—for how do you name something that is less an individual and more an idea perfected by generations of adaptation to altitude, light, and time? They are not entirely tethered to physicality, and when you catch them in motion, they seem like flickers at the edge of your perception. 

The Aeren are slight forms, translucent like the glacial edges they inhabit, their bodies as much light as substance. Their surfaces are pale and refractive, like living shards of sunlight and frost. In the shadowless clarity of the plateau, they almost dissolve into the surroundings, becoming outlines barely suggested by a prism-like glow. When still, they could be mistaken for mere refractions of sunlight caught in the ice—except for the way they breathe, soft rhythms of luminescence that pulse faintly in the brittle air. Their forms seem to pivot and stretch like liquid crystal, adjusting their shapes with organic fluidity, though always elegant and purposeful.

The most unnerving thing about the Aeren—should you find anything unnerving about such ethereal beauty—is their silence. They drift through the plateau’s golden atmosphere with grace and precision, but their movements, no matter how swift or intricate, create no noise. Not even the beat of their wing-like limbs—which are at once physical and spectral, trailing faint ribbons of mist and light as they arc through the space—break the quiet. This stillness is not an absence of vitality but some higher form of existence, vibrating outside the bounds of sound.

The Aeren seem connected to the pools and rivulets of water in an intimate way, and perhaps they are born from them, or at least nourished by them. They dip their elongated digits, tenuous and filament-thin, into the clear waters. The touch causes a faint ripple—not one of liquid disturbance but of something more abstract, as though their presence is woven with the fabric of light and brings the water into sharper focus. Perhaps it is a conversation of sorts, something wordless and meaningful passing between the Aeren and the aqueous folds of the plateau. 

Though they have no mouths or apparent voices, the Aeren communicate—or seem to—with the birds and insects that share this place. The winged creatures are not merely decorative elements of the landscape but integral to the subtle web of movement and life. The birds mirror the Aeren in their spectral nature, though they are more substantial in form. With multiple pairs of radiant wings, they dart and spiral like unspoken symphonies of flight, endlessly weaving through unseen pathways. It becomes clear that their trajectories are not random; they are part of a flowing choreography, orbiting the Aeren in synchronized gestures of mutual purpose. Perhaps they are scouts or companions—or perhaps the Aeren and these creatures are something more entangled, expressions of the same force manifesting differently.

The insects, meanwhile, seem less tethered to the Aeren and more to the stones and water pools. They flicker over shallow puddles and deep, yawning crevices, trailing soft iridescent sparks in patterns that resemble constellations coming alive. They too are silent, and their impossibly brief flashes reveal complex, almost mathematical designs, as if charting the unseen forces that sculpt and bind this place.

The stones and ice contain faint echoes of the long-gone hands that wrought these scars into their surfaces, ancient movements so vast and slow they reshaped not just the terrain but time itself. The Aeren seem coalesced from these echoes, guardians or remnants of the forces that carved the world into what it is. Are they creatures? Elemental spirits? Future relics that have drifted into being out of sequence with time? It is hard to say; their very nature resists clear categorization.

And perhaps, they are not entirely indifferent to your presence. There is no clear hostility, nor any warm invite; instead, there is a deliberate calibration to their movements as if they are considering how or whether to fold you into the tableau of this place. You are not out of place here, but you are not inherently of it. Yet in this soundless, gilded ascent, the mere act of observing feels consequential. Their every shimmering turn seems to ask: Do you belong as we belong? Are you *made* of this, or are you merely passing through?

They will not speak, but they seem to carry the wisdom of ages in their brightness and stillness, in their intricate flow and persistence. Whatever they are—beings, echoes, or living memories—they are part of the cold-brilliant wholeness of this high realm. They are this place, and this place is them, woven together in layers of liquid light, wind-shaped stone, and water that remembers. To see them, even for a moment, is to feel impossibly connected to the cosmos while also wholly insignificant. And they blaze on, endlessly, in silence so loud it almost sings.
