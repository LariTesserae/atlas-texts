The inhabitants of this vibrant underworld are human, yet they can hardly be described as common. They're engineers and builders, miners and explorers, motivated by an insatiable curiosity and an itching restlessness that propels them ever deeper into this self-made abyss.

Their physical appearance shares a whimsical likeness to the land they inhabit - an odd amalgamation of organic and inorganic, caricatural in its stark contrast to classic human form. Their bodies have adapted to the rigors of subterranean life; they are mostly lean and wiry, muscles corded with a strength that belies their thin frame. Their skin, devoid of the sun's nurturing, bears an almost ghostly pallor, illuminated in strange tones by the dim, electric light.

The miners are the giants of this underworld community, their bodies hardened and sculpted by tough manual labor and the commanding cold. Their skin, dulled and roughened from countless hours spent drilling into stone, tells tales of a relentlessness driven by unyielding determination. The engineers, scientists of the earth, bear faces etched with trails of deepened thought.

Their eyes, oh, their eyes – a characteristic feature of this subterra-dwelling breed. They have a distinctive luminance, a spark that comes from long hours adjusting to the dimness. These peering embers glow with an inherent fascination for their self-strought surroundings. Each iris harbors an alternate palette of color — a blend of silvers, grays, blues, and greens, a mirroring river of hues reflecting the twilight of their city.

Attire in this cavernous society is as functional as it is stylish, blending protection and comfort with a dash of personal expression. Their clothes are constructed from thick, sturdy fabric, highly insulated to combat the bitter cold. Leather and fur, woven wool and synthetic fibers - all dyed in various shades - provide a colorful spectrum enhancing the otherwise monochrome environment.

The population is one of tireless toilers and intellectual dreamers, like ants within their colony, each individual performing their role. There is a deeply felt kinship that binds them together — a shared conviction and audacity, a mutual respect that from such shared adversity springs forth. Their common language reads like poetry, a lyrical lingo rich with metaphor, shaped by a shared history of triumph and disappointment, laughter etched in the echo of an underwater fall, despair hidden in the fissures of a newly forged tunnel.

These people cherish storytelling, their tales forming a living archive of their history, with every cavern echoing the narrative of their collective journey. Their culture is rich with music and artistry, capturing their daily experiences, emotions, and dreams in sculptural form on chamber walls, painted masterpieces of stalagmites, and poetic songs reverberating through the vaulted tunnels.

The children, destined to be future engineers and miners, are nurtured on these stories, taught the skills necessary to survive and contribute. Their laughter and playful shouts, while limited, add notes of whimsy to the otherwise adult-dominated echo-chamber.

These humans have not simply survived in this alien womb of their own making, they have thrived, shaping a kingdom out of subterranean adversity, creating a new world beneath the old, forever imprinting their culture’s footprint in substrata, defying the traditional relationship between human and environment, carving their way into the heart of the planet. Much like the land they inhabit, they blur the lines of what a society could be.
