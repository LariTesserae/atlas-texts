Beneath the towering sentinels of age-worn stone, within the fertile cradle of the valley, live inhabitants as varied and mysterious as the realm itself. Their existence is not merely one of survival but of intricate harmony with their environment, as though all who thrive here were threads in a living tapestry woven by unseen hands.

The dominant dwellers of this surreal Eden are a people old as the mountains themselves. These are not mere humans as we understand them, though they might resemble us in form. Their skin is an earthen hue, dappled with marks and streaks that might seem like tattoos but are not: these patterns shift and change with the passing of seasons, mirroring the flows of sap in the massive trees they live among. Their hair, it is said, responds to the light of the canopy like leaves, darkening under shade and gleaming green-gold in the abundant sunbeams that filter through. It's as if the very essence of the valley has seeped into their being, blurring the line between themselves and the thriving life that surrounds them.

They are named, or they name themselves, the Vox Roots — not for domination, but for the resonance they share with this ancient land. Their voices are said to mimic the wind cascading down the mountains; a humming, lilting cadence that can never be truly replicated outside the valley. With it, the Vox Roots communicate not only with each other but also with the fauna and flora of the land, adopting calls, songs, and quiet tones that blend seamlessly into the soundscape. Their language includes clicks like the mandibles of insects, whistles like the flutes of calling birds, and even subtle vibrations that seem to hum through the roots themselves, as if the trees were conspirators in the conversation.

The Vox Roots build no permanent cities of their own. Instead, they inhabit the ancient remnants of whatever civilization carved those primitive marks in stone, weaving live plants and found materials into structures at once functional and transitory. Some of these remnants, jutting forth from tree roots or cradled in the ridges of rocks, now resemble hybrid shapes where human design and nature have overlapped so completely that they seem alive themselves — a low, domed hut with moss that breathes, a lookout tower of petrified vines and bone. In these homes, the people tend simple fires, dye cloth with pigments of pressed berries, and craft strange tools from bark, bone, and the iridescent shells of insects.

Within the canopy, high above the reach of danger, other beings perch and glide. The avian creatures here are vast in diversity, though they are not all of flesh and blood. Some are formed of living leaves sewn by invisible threads, their veins pulse faintly as if a green heartbeat moves through them. Others flash with shimmering, glass-like feathers that refract the damp sunlight into prismatic arcs. They are named "Skycatchers" by those who marvel at their skill in diving and twisting through dense foliage, and legends told by the Vox Roots claim these creatures have no eggs nor nests but instead grow directly out of the canopy during moonlit nights rich with drizzling rain. To tame or touch one is said to invite their fleeting lifespan upon your own — a double-edged gift for those who see beauty and tragedy as two sides of the same coin.

Below, in the shadow of undergrowth and among the low ferns, dwell creatures of shadow and stealth. Compact mammals with fur slick as rainwater streaked with camouflage patterns, they dart and flit, never lingering long enough for a deep look. One of these inhabitants, often heard but seldom seen, are the Nyblen, creatures that sing with mournful clarity as the sun dips low. The Vox Roots revere them as singers of the forest's oldest songs, each note a recitation of the cyclical nature of growth and decay.

Beneath still veins the unseen water-beast: a near-mythical creature whispered about by both Vox Roots and rumor-laden rains. It is said to lie dormant in the inky black pools tucked between the roots of the colossal trees. Its surface is visible perhaps only as a gaping maw, its entire ecosystem clinging outward symbiosis, visibly glimmering unseen bulbs.
