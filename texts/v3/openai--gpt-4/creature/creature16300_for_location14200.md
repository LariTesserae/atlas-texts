And who lives in Lothria? Who breathes within its emerald folds and treads the whispered echoes of its uneven paths?

No single answer. No singular being. This is not a land inhabited by one kind of life, nor a conventional place where creatures are meant to reign. It is a cradle of oddities, harboring those who have unraveled from the usual threads of existence and linger now in this place outside familiar time.

The primary denizens of Lothria are the *Murmhain*, beings who seem half-formed, partially bound to the landscape, as if Lothria itself dreamed them into existence and forgot to finish. Their figures shimmer faintly, outlines flickering like frost against light. They are delicate but not fragile, composed of a malleable, semi-luminous substance that seems closer to running water than flesh. They move in calm, deliberate gestures, their forms perpetually dissolving and revising like liquid sketches scribbled into the canvas of the air. 

When they walk the ground—and they do so with no sound, leaving no trail behind—the moss and ferns beneath them shift ever so slightly, as if bowing with welcome. They’re said to have elongated hands that taper into threads of moving light, fingers gently untangling and reweaving the pulse of water through the land. It is thought that they are shepherds of the streams and cascades, guides for the sapphire rills that shape Lothria continuously, endlessly. Some say the symphony of the water is their language, that when the droplets leap and spiral midair, they are answering the *Murmhain's* song.

The *Glavenhark* are also said to traverse Lothria’s ancient bindings, though one might never catch a clear glance at them. Faint whispers of their forms appear in low light—tall, spindly creatures of pulsing, translucent sinew and a lattice of membranous, spectral wings stretched too thin to be functional. Yet they float at will, carried by a kind of slow internal rhythm, like creatures untethered from mass. Their voices are eerie, a soft and low rumble, speaking to the distant ruins as if those remnants still hold counsel. What they say, if indeed it is language, is beyond all comprehension, stirring only the vaguest inklings of strange and mythic origin. 

It has long been suspected that the *Glavenhark* tend to the dying machinery scattered across the land. They descend on rusted cogs and moss-choked engines like priests, laying some kind of reverent ritual upon these relics. Perhaps they remember the ones who once built the ruins—the humans, or beings that resembled them, who etched their lives into Lothria’s flesh only to vanish. It is even whispered they forged these machines with the *Glavenhark’s* help, a partnership long forgotten. Perhaps there still lingers a bond, as faint as the creatures themselves, one that binds them to these corroded skeletons of a lost age.

Deeper into Lothria, where even the hushed glow of the light struggles to penetrate the long shadows, live the *Vyrin*. They are the solitary dark-cast guardians of the twilight nooks, creatures of earth and dusk molded into uncanny familiarity. Their forms might resemble the hulking silhouettes of bears or wolves, but only in so much as a stone sculpture resembles the flesh it mimics. Covered in obsidian-like bark, they are polished and sharp-edged, faceted creatures who seem to breathe an air of volcanic dusk. Their eyes glow dull amber, two molten pits that starkly contrast the crystalline shimmer of their bodies.

The *Vyrin* don’t move often; their stillness seems eternal at first glance, like part of the ruined statuary scattered all about. But it’s only when the unwary stumble too close that these guardians stir. They do not chase or attack; rather, they enforce boundaries, reordering trails and passageways that lead travelers back to the safer fringes without their conscious knowledge. Some think their vigilance extends even to the ruins themselves, that they heed the faint echoes of a pact made long ago, protecting what remains of ancient endeavors from dissolution.

Perhaps stranger still is the humbling notion that Lothria itself is alive. Not just alive with vegetation or the whisper of life among the foliage, but sentient in ways that defy articulation. The water carries a watchfulness to it; the light feels too precise, sculpting paths and shadows with deliberate intent. Some say the entire landscape heaves and sighs in its rhythm of change, ever so subtly, as though Lothria observed those within its bounds as much as they observed it. Perhaps the *Murmhain*, the *Glavenhark*, and the *Vyrin* are just manifestations of its unfathomable will—a way for it to play host to visitors while preserving its silence and its slow, green thoughts.

Few creatures remain within Lothria long, if indeed they hold the ability to leave at all. For in this realm, time slips like water through mortal fingers. Days might pass as hours, centuries as afternoons. And sooner or later, no matter their original purpose or form, some who enter Lothria find their edges wearing thin. They become immersed in the rhythm of water and light, their bodies and minds drawn into the folds of the emerald expanse. Over long epochs, they begin to fade into the blurry ghosts that haunt its ruins, voices joining the chorus of murmuring streams.

The heartland of Lothria is not for human dominion. It belongs to its dreams and their echoes, the watchful guardians and mournful caretakers. And in that dreaming, somewhere in the endless conversation of water, there is the faint suggestion of all that was lost—and all that remains.
