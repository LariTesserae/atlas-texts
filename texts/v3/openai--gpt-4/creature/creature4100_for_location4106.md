The dwellers of this place are aptly known as the Subterrians—a hardy community of men and women whose lives have evolved around this labyrinth of metal and stone. Their generations have been born, lived, and died in this moist heat, their lineage tracing back to an era their history only vaguely remembers. Their lifestyle has forced them to be amiable, non-competitive, and highly cooperative, with a honed sense of respect for individuality and privacy. Their skin, weathered by constant heat and sporadic moisture, is a sheen of copper-toned toughness, their bodies lean from constant labor, yet robust under the years of urban subsistence. 

Their distinct characteristics shine from their eyes—warm chestnut hues that gleam curiously in the dim, electricity-lit surroundings—an inheritance from a forgotten era. Their hair, various shades of sunless hues, is worn long, tied back, or covered with protective gear as per the job requirements. The faces wear a sheen, a mixture of sweat and constant exposure to heat, becoming a kind of natural signal of the inhabitants' resilience to the inclement environment below the Earth. 

Their attire is half casual, half utilitarian, reflecting a blend of practicality and comfort. Rough, yet ergonomic garments carefully tailored from a sturdy, unique fabric—dyed various shades of earthy tones—adorned with multiple pockets and reinforcement at critical areas. Hints of protective gear like gloves, goggles, waist bands with toolkits—specially designed to cope with both the inherent hazards of their city and their relative occupations.

They are not timid, their spirit far from subdue. They are survivors, builders, artisans; their hands wreak of tenacity, soiled yet strong from years of crafting, reforming, and refining. They are scientists, their minds brimming with ideas hatched from observing and manipulating the natural geological and mechanical aspects of their surroundings. They are engineers, their creations pulsating around them, the cogs and gears of their mechanized city whispering their ingenuity in every clanking echo.

There's a curious affection and respect for the elder generation among them. The elders are these repositories of wisdom and history, the tellers of tales, the guides of their progressively adapting surviving strategies. Huddled in a corner, under the glow of a muted lamp, sharing stories of foundations laid and hardships bested, they are revered, cherished fonts of ubiquitous resilience. Their weathered faces, a roadmap of the past narrating tales of a time lost in the frailty of memory, illuminate under the quiet dedication in the listeners' eyes.

The children, rare but treasured, are sparks of innocent curiosity in this close-knit kinship of survival. They are carefully shepherded, their young minds a rich playground for old stories and new discoveries. The environment, far from smoldering their liveliness, gives birth to a generation of determined observers, tinkerers, driven by an inherent fascination with their subterranean home.

In this unusual, molten, underground existence, there are various roles but no hierarchies, jobs but no classes. Their ingenuity is what defines them, not their origins—for in this radiant labyrinth, their origins are as interweaved as the paths they tread. There's an unspoken understanding, a quiet reckoning that life must and shall carry on, thriving, pulsating under the warm hues of their existence. And it's in this resolve; they find their survival, their identity—the Subterrians, the children of the forge.
