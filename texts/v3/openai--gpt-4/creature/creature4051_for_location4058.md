The things that live here are not easily named. They do not adhere to forms you’d expect, though some flirt with resembling creatures from elsewhere, others don’t seem to care for resemblance at all. What thrives in this tumultuous land does so by embodying its contradictions: shifting and rooted, violent and imperceptible, strange and familiar in fleeting moments that evaporate like morning mist.

High above, just beneath the erratic haze of an unstable sky, glide massive winged forms, too distant and too obscured by haze to be clearly discerned. They are outlines against the pale light, their wings bordered faintly in iridescence that ripples like a slow spill of oil. Some might mistake them for birds, but their flight holds none of the purpose seen in avians. They dip and hover in ways that seem almost experimental, testing the skies more than traversing them. When they swoop lower—those rare moments when they dive into a canyon’s maw—they seem to unwrap themselves into newfound complexities: wings furl into limbs that move with an insectile precision; long necks extend further than they ought to, twisting as if tasting the air below. They do not often land. It feels as if the earth itself does not want them to.

In the gorges below, along ledges of fractured stone, dwell pack-like things that teeter on the edges of being flesh. They are quadrupedal—at least most of the time—but their limbs extend and contract unpredictably, adapting to the craggy terrain with impossible agility. Their bodies shimmer with a carapace-like brilliance, seemingly transparent in patches, revealing glimpses of pale, skeletal frameworks that shift inside like clockwork under skin. These are not predators in the typical sense; rather, they move through the canyons like caretakers or scavengers, prying scraps of energy or sustenance from unlikely sources. Some seem to burrow their long, many-jointed fingers into the damp earth as if "listening" to what lies beneath. Others cluster around the fungal blooms that grow in the shadows of sheer walls, extracting sustenance from them in a manner as much telepathic as predatory. They make no sounds, but their presence hums in your skull with faint, electric unease.  

Beneath the canyon shadows, in pools of condensation that form and vanish ceaselessly, the liquidity of life takes on a literal meaning. Amoebic creatures, transparent and faintly luminous, ripple like fractured light in the shallows. Some take on the barest mimics of forms—an arm here, a torso-like bulge there—only to melt again into aimless flow. These beings: soft, fleeting, are unburdened by permanence. Yet, they are not without intention—they gather together unpredictably, coalescing into clusters that vibrate with purpose: to share heat, or to pool what light they can collect from the diffuse, spectral sky above. For reasons unknowable, some of these creatures leverage themselves almost to the cliffs’ edges just before the mist rolls in, only to let the surging winds carry them off. Do they drift with purpose in those airstreams? Or are they spirits of abandon, carried beyond intention and into the void?

And those other sibilant forms—the faint glimpses of limbs that cling to inverted cliffs like pale question marks, emerging only at the edges of vision? They are perhaps the most unsettling of all. Tall and many-jointed like trees with too many forks, their translucent, sinewed bodies sway dangerously in the buffeting winds.
