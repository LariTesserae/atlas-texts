In Phytohemia, creatures walk softly and dream deeply, their lives woven into the subtle orchestra of creation and decay. The beings that call this place home are not so much inhabitants as coauthors, their roles balanced somewhere between humble gardeners, custodians of rusting relics, and pilgrims in a living cathedral.

### The Mossbound Folk

The primary denizens of Phytohemia are the Mossbound Folk, a quiet and contemplative people who seem to embody the landscape itself. Their skin is rough and textured, like bark smoothed over by years of rain, and fine veins of moss crawl across their forearms, framing their faces and growing thick on their shoulders as if they were stones set upon by the inexorable clutches of nature. They are slow-moving, their gait deliberate, with every step leaving faint green impressions of moss or pollen.

Their attire is pieced together from lichen-tanned fabrics dyed in subtle hues of fern green, sepia, and rust red. They adorn themselves with small, living gardens: necklaces of trailing ivy, belts lined with pockets of soil where tiny mushrooms sprout, and rings that bloom into delicate flowers when the seasons change. Whether these gardens are cultivated by choice or are simply an inevitable part of their essence is a secret they keep guarded behind serene, knowing smiles.

The Mossbound Folk value quiet labors — tending to the creeping vines that overtake old lamp posts, carefully guiding water from the streams to nurture struggling plants that cling to the outermost reaches of decaying factories. They are the engineers of the ecosystem here, though their every act seems steeped in reverence for the natural processes of life and decay. They do not rush about their work; they merely join the quiet rhythm of time in Phytohemia, letting roots and streams guide their free hands.

They communicate seldom but deeply. When they speak, their words sound like wind threading through trees or the soft roar of a distant falls—sonorous, slow, and thoughtful. Most prefer to use gestures or even silence, sensing that the city itself offers an unbroken dialogue, humming with the life that surrounds them.

### Iridescent Scriveners

Gliding through the moist air and crumbling corridors of Phytohemia are the Iridescent Scriveners, insects the size of a human hand with wings that shimmer like spilled oil under the twilight sun. These creatures seem to possess a peculiar intelligence, punctuating the verdant cityscape with their gentle bioluminescent glow. Their wings are inscribed with patterns curiously similar to both foliage veining and mechanical blueprints, a visual language some would almost call purposeful.

They are rarely found still, except when hovering delicately over blank squares of bark paper crafted by the Mossbound Folk, leaving trails of phosphorescent ink as they dance. Their markings are endlessly intricate — looping vines shift into cogs, petals burst forth into skeletal schematics of bygone engines. These luminous traces linger only briefly before fading, as if to say that fleeting beauty is all the more precious. Locals call the work of these insects "The Ephemeral Archive," though its purpose is shrouded in mystery.

Some claim the scriveners are ancient remnants of a mechanobiological experiment now lost to time, while others see in them the hand of nature memorializing the old machines they now reside among. Regardless, no one seems inclined to disturb or capture these enigmatic creatures, choosing instead to witness their quiet calligraphy.

### The Lichen Dreamers

A more unusual population in Phytohemia can be found in the heart of the oldest, most moss-laden factories. Here dwell the Lichen Dreamers, humanoid figures that appear to have been hewn from soft, chalky limestone and slowly transformed by time and overgrowth into vibrant tapestries of fungi, algae, and tiny herbaceous plants. Root systems emerge from their limbs and creep down walls or along the floor, visibly connecting them to the structure around them.

The Lichen Dreamers exist in an eternal state of repose. They do not speak, do not move, at least not in the traditional sense — researchers have noted that the patterns of lichen and fungal growth across their forms shift subtly over weeks or months, forming new arrangements of color and texture, as though their very bodies are engaged in storytelling. Their apparent torpor belies the profound pull they exert on the space they inhabit. Mossbound Folk come here to sit in stillness, to hum along with the low resonance emitted by these uncanny figures, and to emerge later with insights they can scarcely articulate but which guide their actions.

The Dreamers are considered sacred, and their presence is imbued with mystery. Who were they before this state? Are they living or somewhere between life and death? Is their existence voluntary or an organic inevitability in a place like Phytohemia? No one asks aloud. The Dreamers are simply — incontrovertibly — part of the balance, mediators between human legacy and the reclaiming embrace of the earth.

### The Silent Rusters

Looming between the greenery are the Silent Rusters — mobile machines from a bygone industrial age, long-abandoned but still alive in a sluggish, shambling way. Covered in moss and patinated with rust, their movements are slow and grudging, their joints creaking like wind-bent trees. These shallows ghosts of industry have taken on lives of their own; nobody knows why they move, only that they do.

They often shuffle quietly toward neglected machinery with a curious compulsion, extending ancient, spindly grippers to trace the outlines of rusting cogs or to nudge stubborn gears that have long since ceased to turn. Sometimes they carry fragments with them — a cog or a spring that they cradle in their clawed appendages as if it were a precious relic. They often sit beside streams, allowing the mineral-rich water and creeping vegetation to scour and soften their bodies.

The Mossbound Folk watch the Rusters with a mix of pity and awe, seeing them as relics of their ancestors' time and symbols of Phytohemia's fluid dance of collapse and renewal. The Scriveners often encircle these machines, tracing their strange movements in luminous patterns before zipping off to inscribe another ephemeral text. 

### The Flora’s Secret Sentience

Finally, though no one speaks of it loudly, it is the foliage itself that truly *lives* in Phytohemia. The plants here seem altogether too intentional in their growth patterns: vines subtly reinforce worn structures just before collapse; trees stretch their canopies to shade ferns most desperately in need of water; flowers bloom disproportionately near the failing light of dim street lamps. The very fabric of life here seems aware, a slow and gentle intelligence that is neither indifferent nor overly controlling. If anything, it feels collaborative—an ancient wisdom, content to guide rather than command.

Perhaps the real masters of Phytohemia work unobserved, pulling no strings but providing just the whisper of guidance to creatures great and small. Stepping through these streets, anyone might feel it: a sense that while they are free to wander, they are part of something infinitely larger, infinitely older, and infinitely alive.
