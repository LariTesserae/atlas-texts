Residing in this expanse are the dwellers of darkness, the children of the subterranean world, the Katharsians as they call themselves. They have evolved over millennia to adapt to the peculiar mix of darkness and the temperature extremes their home offers. Draped in bioluminescent cloths which they weave from the harvested resilient fungi, Katharsians have a faint yet constant glow about them. Their bodies are lean and of varying height, their limbs lengthened and more kin to a spider than the rounded proportions of humans. 

Their skin is a bizarre contradiction of somewhat translucent— almost opalescent in nature —yet carrying the richness of petrified stone, perfectly adapted to their environment. It carries in patterns an array of complex pigmentation, a living testament to unspoken mythologies of this hidden world. Their hands, with cloven fingers tipped with claw-like appendages, are just as comfortable clinging to the towering spires of their architecture as they are in the creation of delicate works of artistry and engineering. 

The Katharsians have a multitude of eyes, typically three or more, set on different parts of their head. These eyes glow softly with varied colors, with unfathomable depths betraying a kindered intelligence. Each eye perceives different wavelengths of light; some tuned to the faint dimness and mystery of their cavernous city, others attuned to the rich colors and warmth of their cultural artifacts and decorations. Even though they have evolved in the dark, their eyes are far from being vestigial; instead, they are ever-adaptive portals to the world they inhabit.

Rather than vocal communication, Katharsians use a form of sonar-like language, made up of mesmerizing patterns of low-frequency hums, taps, clicks, and shuddering vibrations. Their voices float through the vast caverns, blending with the other sounds of the city in an enchanting symphony. That isn't to say they can't speak or make noise - when they choose to, their voices are haunting, melodic, a languid siren call echoing in the deep caverns.

In this alien and ethereal landscape thrives a surprisingly advanced culture. Katharsians are scholars, architects, astronomers without a sky, engineers par excellence. Where another species might have been ground down by the harshness of the environment, the Katharsians have harnessed it, living harmoniously with the granite, the fungi, the pools of ancient water siphoned upward from the bowels of the earth, making their eerie city not just a place to survive, but a place of strange, timeless beauty. They are the sum of perseverance, adaptation, and imagination, cryptic children of the underworld, echoing stories their surface-born cousins may never know.
