The beings who inhabit this submerged cathedral are neither intruders nor rulers, but quiet collaborators. They are humanoid in form, though their physiology has grown intimately entwined with the peculiar environment over untold generations. Imagine elongated figures, graceful and ethereal, their limbs tapering into velvety webbing that fans out between fingers and toes. Their skin has evolved into a soft, translucent canvas that mirrors the pale, refracting hues of their world. Bioluminescent streaks ripple faintly across their bodies, tracing veins, tendons, and sinew in shifting patterns of light: a language of emotion, mood, and communion visible to those who know how to read it.

Hairlike filaments grow from their bodies, similar to the wandering threads sprouting from the immense trunks that cradle their home. Strands of it drift in the slow undercurrent, trailing like liquid light or the feathery appendages of the anemones anchoring the unseen depths. These filaments are not merely decorative but sensory—a network through which they perceive the subtle vibrations of the medium, communicate with one another, and even attune themselves to the larger pulses of the forest. They are not merely inhabitants of this place but extensions of it, tethered into the system like branches to a tree.

Their lives are guided not by urgency but by rhythm, an intuitive alignment with the world’s glacial cycles. They do not hurry, nor do they waste. They have no need, for all is provided in due course. Their food is cultivated through symbiotic relationships with the living architecture itself: gelatinous fruits and stalks drip from the tendrils and are harvested with care, while small aquatic creatures willingly approach to deposit themselves at hand, as if participating in a mutual bargain. Their tools are grown as much as they are made, coaxed from seeds or shaped from malleable materials derived from the slower-growing plants and molds that creep amongst the trees’ bark.

They spend much of their time in quiet, contemplative pursuits, for this is a culture of thought and observation. The vast stillness of the space encourages them to see and listen deeply, their days filled with a kind of wordless philosophy expressed through touch, shared light patterns, and the collective rhythm of their daily tasks—articulated like a current flowing between them. Music is born not from instruments but from melding vocal tones with the resonance of their multi-chambered chests, creating choral harmonics that shiver through the medium like rippling beams of sunlight. Songs are memory, construction, and celebration all at once, layered onto one another across centuries to form an oral history that weaves both their stories and the forest’s development into a continuous thread.

Ceremonial gatherings mark the rare significant events, such as the slow flowering of a new spiraling trunk or the arrival of a particularly majestic leviathan. The beings cluster on woven platforms dappled with bioluminescent moss, their forms glowing faintly as they arrange themselves into spiraling shapes mirroring the great trunks. Movements are slow and deliberate, their symmetry an act of reverence for the forest’s natural patterns. Blowing gently through hollowed reeds, they create long, low tones that resonate across the fluid space. The vibrations excite the bioluminescent filaments that hang in tangling curtains between trunks, filling the air with shimmering pulses of light—a visual echo of their song. Such moments are their communion not just with one another but with the forest itself, an affirmation that they live within and not above this strange cathedral of time.

While no predator among them takes life as a matter of chaos or hunger, death still exists here—measured and essential. The dead are never abandoned but returned to the forest in intricate rituals. Coiled within the threads of the massive spiraling trunks, bodies are subsumed into the slow arc of vegetative growth, becoming a new part of the cathedral’s living architecture. For a time, the glowing strands may retain echoes of a being’s personal light, flickering softly in remembrance, an ongoing part of the music. This recycling of physical forms is seen as an elegant necessity, and not to be feared.

Among the inhabitants are those that spend long intervals apart from the gathering places, drifting higher toward the faint glimmers that mark the boundary of light or farther outward, tracing the elusive limits of their world’s edges. They are the wanderers, keepers of the unseen. It is said that it takes a specific temperament, a certain resonance, to understand what lies in the empty distances that even light struggles to reach. Whispered tales tell of strange discoveries there—cold expanses where the trunks end and give way to jagged rock, or to corridors leading ever deeper in a labyrinthine descent. Whatever they learn, these solitary figures rarely share what they’ve seen directly. Instead, they return with faint shifts in their luminescence, distilling new notes into the shared melody until others begin to intuit what was found, or what was lost.

Though it feels eternal, this grove is not immutable. Its measured pace masks an intrinsic vulnerability. The balance here is so fragile, so interwoven, that the sharp intrusion of an outside influence—a sudden displacement of the dense medium, a foreign pollutant, the unintended violence of a careless hand—could unmake the entire cathedral in the space of a day. The dwellers of this place understand this intuitively, not through fear but through reverence. They know that their lives are a current within the greater one, a note in a long and ongoing composition, and what empowers them is the gravity of their patience.

They are caretakers, watchers of deep time, fleeting sparks in a world that will outlast them. In their gentle presence, in their deliberate patterns, they ensure the hymn continues, unbroken. Whether their forms are mystical or biomechanical no longer carries meaning; they long ago ceased to distinguish between what is natural and what is made. Their lives are artfully simple and completely symbiotic, an expression of what life might become when it learns to thrive without grasping, to transform and be transformed.

And as the light shifts above and refracts below, washing the vaulted spirals in pale, glimmering waves, they too rise and turn in patient harmony, a part of the hymn, a part of the whole.
