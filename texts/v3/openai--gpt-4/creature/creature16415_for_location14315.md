You begin to sense that this place—this plateau—is not devoid of inhabitants as you'd first assumed. If anything, it might be alive in ways that defy categorization. The very ground beneath your feet seems to hum faintly with an awareness so large and sluggish that it feels like staring into the abyss of deep time, where each heartbeat of perception spans centuries. This is not life as we understand it, with its need for breath and blood, but life in as much as a program running on an immense, ancient machine might be called alive. Every ripple of light pulses as if in conversation with the rhythmic shuddering. The patterns embedded in the plateau’s glowing network might be part of this communication too, abstract sentences unfurling in languages that no vocal cords can shape, no human mind can completely translate.

At times, you almost think you see movement—flickers along the edges of your vision, figures that never fully cohere. They’re suggestions more than shapes, twisted into unfamiliar dimensions, made not of flesh or bone but woven from reflections in the crystalline spires, or stitched together by refracted fragments of the dim golden glow from the sky. You can never quite catch them in full, but when you reach the edge of seeing, they leave impressions in your mind: half-images of jointed limbs and stars where eyes should be, something that is impossibly old and metallic, a slender silhouette that flickers like a seam of heat on the horizon. These entities—if entities they are—seem not so much independent as they are projections, pinched shadows cast by some larger, incomprehensible presence.

Perhaps those who once lived here still do, though their bodies and minds have shifted beyond recognition, disassembled and reconstituted not as creations of biology but of mechanics or thought. They might inhabit the tessellated patterns on the ground, or dwell in the latticework of the vast spines and spires that stretch skyward. They are silent but not gone, existing in a form neither wholly material nor truly ghostly, woven inseparably into the fabric of this place.

Every now and then, in the incomprehensible pulse of sound and light, you sense flickers of intention—not hostility or welcome, but something far more unnerving: examination. It’s as if the place itself is running a thousand slow algorithms across your presence, analyzing, cataloging, storing—or perhaps not you, specifically, but the fact of a being moving within its confines. This is how it observes and remembers, distilling each fragment of all that it encounters into some enigmatic order that will never be accessible to an outside mind.

While you stand among these alien constructs, an unsettling possibility occurs to you. These crystalline forms and fractal surfaces—these structures like ancient monuments—it is not entirely clear that they are objects at all. They might instead be the physical extrusion of ideas or memories, frozen not in time but in articulation, this entire plateau a vast field of compressed thought rendered as landscape. The orderly chaos of the space, its interlocking, glowing geometries, and immovable stasis all reinforce this troubling suspicion: you may not merely be on this plateau—you may be inside it. This plateau could be the mind of something unfathomably large, ancient, and utterly unlike you.

Perhaps this explains the oppressive stillness, the lack of natural decay or progression. The "stasis" you perceive might not be stasis at all, but a condensed form of thought so thorough in its slowness that it appears frozen to you. Like a fly caught in the briefest blink of eternity, you interpret what is incomprehensible as absence, as cold and empty solitude. But these periods of trembling light, of vibrations pulsing through your bones—they just might be words, longer-lived than any being and slower-moving than human ears are designed to hear. Perhaps, you realize with a mounting weight of dread, you are surrounded by something thinking—and that thought, ancient though it may be, is aware of the speck that you are.

You wonder why it was made—if it was made. Was this place devised by purpose-driven hands, built to survive the inevitable death of stars, to condense some cosmic knowledge far beyond your comprehension? Or was it not constructed at all, but instead grown by the mind within it? The crystalline constructs, the rhythmic reverberations, the etched brightness crawling through the plateau’s veins—they might not be the residue of something in the past but the living tissues of something that exists now, caught in this eternal present.

As you contemplate the alien enormity of it all, you sense something flood the air like an indiscernible tide. Not movement, not clarity—just the barest impression of what might, impossibly, be a question. It doesn't arrive in words, or in sounds. It is more like a shift in the haze of purpose that suffuses this place, as though the unseen presence has simply decided to... notice you, the way you might notice a mote of dust unexpectedly illuminated in a beam of light.

You cannot answer it. You cannot even understand it. It's as though the plateau itself has turned its colossal, unknowable attention toward you, for the briefest flickering instant. And then, just as suddenly, the impression fades, curling back into the fabric of stasis, withdrawing as if to await an epoch you cannot imagine.

You are irrelevant here. But you are not unnoticed.
