The life in these spheres consists mostly of bioluminescent marine animals, other deep-sea creatures, and bacteria. Neon tetras are one of the primary residents, shimmering with brilliance in the dark depths. These small, sociable creatures often school together, their shimmering scales creating ethereal, flashing patterns across the black canvas of the sphere. Slightly larger creatures, like the mesmerizing jellyfish, drift languidly through the waters. They lend an eerie beauty to the surrounding darkness, pulsating gently with the rhythm of their own bioluminescent glow, similar to the aquatic version of a heartbeat.

In the deeper parts of each sphere, you can find slow-moving anglerfishes - with their peculiar, light-emitting appendage and fearsome jaws but harmless nature due to their slow movements. Amongst other species, you may find lightless crustaceans - the bottom-dwellers feeding on whatever organic matter sinks from above, and, occasionally, cephalopods with skin that can dazzlingly change color and pattern in an instant, creating undersea light shows that cut through the darkness.

In the shallower areas, smaller, bio-luminous predators reside. Glowing eels circling with deliberate movements, light-piercing mantis shrimps standing sentinel in their burrows, and the needle-toothed viperfish traveling vertically within the sphere chasing their prey are among the most witnessable.

Non-animal life persists in the form of bacteria, luminescent planktons and mineral-crafted corals that maintain the sphere's ecological cycle. These house bioluminescent bacteria and other microorganisms, creating a mutualistic relationship. They filter the water and absorb nutrients, and in turn, provide a home for sea creatures. These microorganisms have mastered chemosynthesis, much like the deep-sea organisms inhabiting the sea floor near hydrothermal vents, turning the darkness and solitude into a thriving habitat.

The residents of the spheres are survivors, living proof of evolution's ability to carve life in the most unexpected places. Their existence in the aquatic spheres signifies a triumph of life over the harsh and onerous conditions of the deep.

Yet, the inhabitants’ existence isn't wholly solitary or harsh; these are social beings too. The occupants exhibit complex behaviors, like exploring, territory marking, hunting together, communicating, and possibly even courting in the darkness, illuminated only by their own or their neighbor's glow. The spheres – in their eerie, somber quietude – beat with the vibrancy of marine life's multitude, glowing out brilliant blues and greens, wreathing life into the darkened depths.
