The beings who inhabit this high, mist-shrouded valley are unlike any seen elsewhere, for they are not wholly separate from their environment. They are quiet presences, woven seamlessly into the fabric of the place — part mist, part shadow, part moss, and part memory. To call them "creatures" would be to miss some fundamental truth about their nature; they have the fluidity of elements and the patience of stone, beings bound more closely to time and place than to flesh and bone.

They are drawn together here, as though this secluded hollow is their harbor — or perhaps their creation. To walk here is to feel their awareness like the weight of the moist air on your skin. They do not move or speak in ways you would readily recognize, but their influence is unmistakable. They dwell most visibly at the threshold where one thing becomes another: perched on the lip of still pools, laying claim to the shifting mist, leaned close along the seams of stone and shadow.

Were you to notice them, it would begin with little things. The light might waver imperceptibly, pooling in places it has no business lingering. A fern frond might twitch as though sighing, despite the utter stillness of the air. The curve of the land, the tilts of the moss-covered stones — all at once these seem less accidental, more deliberate, as though placed knowingly by hands you haven’t seen. The valley shifts its posture when you are not looking, yet it pretends to stay still.

The only way to see them fully would be to sit long enough to let their dream eclipse your perception. You’d need to mirror their patience, to match their unhurried pulse. Only then would their forms tease themselves out of the mist and shadow — tall and lean and indistinct, with their edges shimmering as the droplets in the air cling to their forms. They might appear more as suggestions of figures, more hinted than articulated, cloaked in the same pale greenish haze that hovers over everything here. Their movements are deliberate, in cadence with the slow geology of the place, yet with a grace that belies their weight.

They could be spirits born of the valley's long stillness — not supernatural, but supra-natural, arising from the land’s own dreaming memory. They are curators, caretakers, embodiments of its infinite patience. Or perhaps they are older still, beings that learned to slow themselves into near-stillness when the world elsewhere grew too wild and quickened. Here, where everything is so supple and slow and the air itself seems reluctant to leave, they have remained, blending until they are inseparable from the valley itself.

Their primary concern, if they possess concerns as you would understand them, seems to be the preservation of stasis, the equilibrium of what they must view as sacred ground. Movement is not forbidden here, but it is costly. A careless step might shift the stones in ways that take decades to forget. A loud cry or exclamation, were you foolish enough to let one slip, would feel like a fracture — the valley would seem to pause even more profoundly, the mist tightening around you in something that is not quite disapproval but closer to sorrow: a small wound in its millennia-deep rhythm. 

Perhaps other creatures lived here once, something with fur and limbs and instincts. Perhaps they were ushered out long ago, or perhaps they came to fear these silent wardens and their watchful, slow-curving shadows. If you peered at the ground long enough, you might see a faint outline of an ancient hoof or claw, the remnant of passage that the valley has worked eons to erase.

If you stayed here long enough, the pace of the valley would begin to bleed into your own movements and thoughts. The heaviness of its breath would fill your lungs and lungs would forget how to expel it. Your heartbeat would slow, slower still, until it mirrored their languid consideration.
