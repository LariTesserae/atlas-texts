Deruwa is not a realm of human civilization; it belongs to those who commune with primordial forces, those who dwell not in steel towers or glass cocoons, but in the folds of the earth and the embrace of the trees. Its inhabitants move in a quiet rhythm, their lives seamlessly woven into the fabric of this living, breathing world. Some of them are visible, their forms darting, gliding, or burrowing through the dense labyrinth of vegetation, while others are enigmatic, shrouded in mystery, their presence guessed at in faint impressions on damp soil or fleeting silhouettes in the mist.

The most obvious inhabitants are the creatures that flit and scurry about. Tiny jungle birds, their feathers glossy and iridescent like oiled jewels, hop through the foliage like curious collectors, pausing occasionally to peck at a fruit droplet or a hidden insect. Their eyes are ever watchful, and their songs are vibrant sparks of life that resonate across the green cathedral. In contrast, larger birds like shadowy herons and plumed cranes wade in the shallow reflective pools. They move with solemn grace, their mirror-images rippling below them as they hunt for fish or frogs in the water’s quiet embrace.
