The beings who inhabit Iunarium are as paradoxical as the town itself, their existence a reflection of its peculiar harmony between industrial might and embryonic, vibrant nature. They are not one thing, these citizens of the encoded oddities; they are a spectrum, shimmering between organic and mechanical, thriving contradictions brought to life.

The primary denizens of Iunarium are the *Resonants*. They are tall and wiry, with elongated limbs that seem too delicate, too impossibly fragile to carry out the intense labors demanded by the town's machines. But they do. Their skin reflects the town's palette — shades of tarnished bronze, faded greens, and oxidized silver, with occasional veins of pulsing blue light visible beneath the translucent surface. They wear little save for belts or harnesses crammed with small tools, bits of wire, and miniature cogs, trinkets that seem both functional and ceremonial. Their eyes are large, dome-like, and glistening, not with moisture but with some internal luminance, glowing faintly in hues that shift with their emotions. They are empathetic creatures, attuned not only to each other but to the very rhythms of the town — the low hum of machines, the squelch of mossy ground, the drone of insects dancing through the frosted air.

Their voices are musical yet metallic, a strange blend of melody and static, as though they are both singing and transmitting radio frequencies. When they speak, meaning is carried as much by vibration as by word, and their conversations seem to ripple outward into the environment. You can almost feel their presence on your skin, a faint buzz as though one of Iunarium's fireflies has rested momentarily upon you. 

The *Guldens* are rarer and stranger still. Some residents claim they are merely myths, while others swear to have encountered them in the forgotten alleyways or near the gushing fountains in the dead of night. Guldens are amorphous, flickering forms, fluctuating between liquid and solid states. Their bodies seem to be composed of glossy, black-amber liquid streaked with glimmers of golden light, like trapped sunlight fighting to escape tree resin. When they move, it is not by walking but by flowing—a fluid, rippling motion that leaves behind a faint, glowing trail that vanishes after moments. Guldens are guardians of balance, or so it is said. They appear when the harmony of the town is disturbed, their quiet presence a warning to stop, or perhaps an assurance that equilibrium will return.

Then there are the *Ferro-faun*, a peculiar, childlike species that live within the wild willow forests on the outer edges of Iunarium's winding streets. Their appearance is organic and mechanical all at once — their bodies covered in patches of fur and scales, intertwined with fine filigrees of brass and copper. Their limbs have the sinewy build of organic creatures, but their fingers are tipped with retractable tools: screwdrivers, pliers, tiny hammers. They are the keepers of the trees, seen scaling the willow branches to whisper into the frost-covered leaves or bending low to the ground to sing to the mosses, their harmonic tones resonating within the cobbles. They are shy, seldom interacting with the Resonants, who speak of them with a mix of reverence and curiosity. And yet their work is visibly integral to the ecosystem of Iunarium, as essential as the factories.

The insects, too, are oddly personified. Thoughtless creatures don’t exist in Iunarium. The fireflies move not just in flitting chaos, but in deliberate aerial dances, searing scripts and shapes into the night that some swear convey messages if you're attuned enough to decipher them. The spiders spin silken webs reinforced with fine metal wires, creating tensile bridges between buildings. And the beetles: oh, the beetles are jewel-toned things, their shells enameled with fractal patterns that seem to pulse faintly with life when the light catches them. They busily scavenge tiny scraps of discarded industrial alloys, carting them off to places unknown, contributing to the never-ending recycling process of the town itself.

Some whisper of a hidden class of inhabitants, known only as the *Eidolons*. These are the spirits, if spirits they are, of Iunarium itself. They are glimpsed briefly in windows obscured by steam, or in the hissing jets of factory gaskets that briefly take the form of faces. They never speak, but their presence is felt through faint tugs of intuition, inexplicable but unmistakable, pulling citizens away from danger or toward a new discovery. Some claim the Eidolons are the town's architects, products of an age long passed, their energies guiding the continued vitality of the paradoxical place they built.
