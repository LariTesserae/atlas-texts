The beings who inhabit this place are, if they can even be called "beings," expressions of the same embryonic potential that defines their home. They are neither creatures of flesh nor spirit, but something closer to the unarticulated thoughts of the world itself, dreamed into form by the restless flow of its half-made reality. To describe them is to court contradiction: they are transient yet deliberate, somehow imbued with both intelligence and accident, like sparks flying off the wheel of creation.

Their forms, such as they are, are as mutable as the landscape. They coalesce from the dense liquid air, a mist gathering itself into ribbons of almost-substantiality before unfurling into shapes that flicker, then stabilize—for a time. Most often, they appear as contorted striations of water, vapor, and refracted light, pouring into and out of themselves with a grace that borders on choreography. These "bodies" are delicate and fleeting, their edges dissolving back into the air or the currents of water, leaving behind only traces: ripples, strange harmonics of light, or the faintest taste of electricity on the air. Other times, they seem more solid, drawing the gelatinous, quivering earth into their forms, carving limbs or spines out of the half-made rock, though always as if on loan rather than in permanence.

They are not separate from their environment—in fact, they are difficult to isolate from it. Some seem like extensions of the proto-formations rising and falling under tectonic pressure, their shapes mimicking the upward push of would-be mountains or the curling tremors of collapsing ridges. Others are born of the stillness—wisps and tendrils of refracted light and water that spiral upward in slow-motion, seeming to reject the gravitational whims of the world. For all their eerie beauty, there is something unsettling about their presence, an anxiety in the way they refuse to fully manifest. They are half-there, half-here, lurking at the edges of perception but never crossing fully into certainty.

Their behavior feels less like movement and more like exploration—or perhaps trial-and-error. Some glide in arcs through the fluid space, trailing luminous seams of ionized gas or sparkling particulates, testing the viscosity of their world with flicking tendrils, like blindfolded dancers measuring the contours of an unfamiliar room. Others gather near the embryonic mineral spires, pressing themselves into the young latticework of crystals, sending faint tremors through the formations as though they're experimenting with solidity, testing the boundaries between what they *are* and what they *could be.* Occasionally, one will erupt into a mad, chaotic burst of eerie motion, limbs and forms splitting and recombining, almost as if rejecting itself mid-creation before scrambling back to some semblance of coherence. These bursts are brief, violent, and visceral—the only sign of desperation in beings otherwise as fluid as their surroundings.

Despite their volatility, there’s an undeniable intelligence in them, a purpose that can’t easily be defined. Their "faces," if they can be described as such, are unsettlingly ambiguous: glimpsed reflections of humanlike or animal features that vanish as soon as they are noticed. It's as though the beings momentarily try on forms or personas, shaping themselves into something just barely *recognizable*, only to reject it, slipping into abstraction once again. Some appear to mimic the flickering plasmic condensations of the water-vapor clouds, performing mockeries of sentience, with movements that trail faint echoes of laughter and whispers. Are these mimicries deliberate, or are they simply echoes—mindless feedback loops generated by the world's processes?

They communicate, but not in words. Nor in sound, really—though the chaotic soundscape of their world sometimes seems to react to their presence. Instead, they radiate impressions, feelings, and fleeting flashes of something like memory, projected into the air around them. If you linger near one, you might sense—not see, not hear—an urge toward understanding. An instinctive, unformed desire to make sense of itself. The sensation feels like trying to catch something slipping just outside your field of vision: a blueprint that refuses to complete itself, a pattern half-remembered.

They are artifacts of a process, the byproducts of creation rather than its custodians. Yet they do not feel incidental. Their presence is integral to the balance of possibility that defines this world. They seem to act as participants in, or perhaps even drivers of, the ceaseless cycle of formation and collapse. One might wonder if they are the shapers of this land or merely shaped by it—an unanswerable question, as the boundaries between actor and acted-upon blur as easily here as water blends with air. Perhaps they are both—part creator, part consequence.

Among the transient beings are some that hint at an evolution beyond the fleeting moment. Rare, towering figures form from the dense plumes of vapor and liquid, coiling into structures that seem to endure longer than the others. These entities are immense, reaching upward toward the dim, fractured bands of light with an almost mechanical precision. They are more sculptural than alive, like living monuments to the potential of permanence in a land devoid of it. These great forms, though silent, resonate with an inaudible hum, a keening note that cuts through the world’s chaotic symphony, pulling the other inhabitants into momentary stillness as if listening. Then, without warning, they too dissolve back into vapor—a reminder that nothing here desires or perhaps *dares* to stay.

No single one of these beings is overtly dominant. They seem to exist in harmony, but harmony of a strange kind—improvisational, adaptive, and always in negotiation. They are not predators, nor are they prey. Instead, they seem like collaborators in the world's unsteady self-creation, their existence a kind of dialogue with the half-finished landscape, a testing of limits, and a curious probing of what might yet be. There is no malice in them, nor benevolence. They simply…are.

They are as unsure of themselves as the world is unsure of what it will become. Together, they create a cycle of trial and error that stretches out endlessly, a perpetual beginning. And in this endless liminality, this state of half-knowing, half-being, they and their world dream together—a dream not yet ready to wake.
