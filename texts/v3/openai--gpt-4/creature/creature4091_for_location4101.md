The beings of this place are not creatures in the familiar sense. They do not crawl, nest, or breed. They do not hunt or gather. They do not sleep. Yet they are here—manifestations of a kind that linger at the edges of comprehension, their presence dim, like fading echoes, persistent but subtle. They seem entangled with the place itself, indistinguishable from the environment, like reflections caught in mirrored glass.

They are best described not as bodies but as resonances or patterns, drifting between states of materiality. If one stays long enough, faint impressions can sometimes be seen: shimmering distortions in the air like heat haze, outlines of impossible forms that flicker in and out of view. Sometimes these forms appear vaguely humanoid, but asymmetrically so, as if sculpted from angles that cannot exist in three dimensions. Other times, they resemble branching coral, crystalline fractals that rise briefly from the surface of a pool before dissolving into mist. They are never entirely solid, occupying a liminal space between light and matter.

They do not move with intent, yet they are not inert. When the rhythmic tinkling sounds rise in the air, these beings seem to shimmer more vividly, as if responding to some subtle frequency beyond human hearing. Their presence feels neither malicious nor benevolent; they simply *are*. Their rhythms follow those of the landscape itself—the slow contracting of ice, the exhalation of vapor, the expansion of heat within the stone. But there’s a sense of watchfulness to them, an awareness that permeates the stillness.
