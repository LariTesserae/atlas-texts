Those who call this place home are as much a work-in-progress as the land they inhabit, their natures equally fluid and indeterminate, their forms and identities swayed by the erratic whims of this nascent world. They are not bound by the rigid certainties of the outside world but instead exist in a state of becoming, lives entwined with the raw essence of the terrain, as though they and the plateau are co-authors of each other.

There is a people here, though to call them "people" is a simplification, a concession to language ill-equipped to describe what they truly are. Their forms are mercurial, vaguely humanoid in silhouette but shimmering at the edges. Their skin appears crystalline, shot through with translucent veins of pulsing light that ebb and flow like molten rivers, dimming and glowing with the rhythm of their emotions—or perhaps the rhythm of the land itself. Their eyes, or what serve as eyes, are deep and liquid, like bottomless pools reflecting the star-flecked obsidian sky above. 

These beings are called the **Umbravivens** by those few brave—or foolish—souls who have stumbled upon this erratic plateau. Neither male nor female, they shift fluidly between shapes, adapting to their environment or mood. Sometimes they are tall and impossibly thin, their elongated limbs bending like reeds in the eternal wind; other times, they are squat and sturdy, blending with the rock and soil, grounded and solid as the bedrock beneath them.

The Umbravivens are artisans of mystery, their every interaction with their world an act of creation. Those intricate stone buildings that pave the village—formed by their hands, though not in the traditional sense. Their touch seems to tease the very essence of the rock, coaxing it to reshape itself as though the stone relishes the attention, yearning to be molded into some unprecedented form. The buildings seem less constructed than grown, their walls fluid and rippling in the dim light, as if they breathe in unison. No structure remains static for long; walls twist into arches overnight, while footpaths shift like dancers, their arrangements directed by the instincts of the Umbravivens and the capricious will of the plateau.

The Umbravivens speak not in words, but in tones and chimes, their utterances ringing out in harmonized frequencies that resonate across the rocky expanse. Their language has no fixed meaning in the way we understand language. It’s more akin to music, conveying emotions and concepts through cadences and chords, shifting vernaculars that leave meaning within the heart more than the mind. They seem to communicate as much with the plateau as with one another, their melodies blending with the wind's whisper and the drip of water, as if the land itself were a silent interlocutor in their conversations.

Functioning as both symbiotes and stewards of this fledgling land, the Umbravivens tend to the vegetation with rituals that seem part song and part dance. On nights when the air hums with unseen energies, they weave themselves through the massive, radial fronds, their bodies glowing faintly with bioluminescent light that mirrors the shimmering wing-lights of the enormous insects they revere. These strange creatures, the iridescent fliers with their dancing spore-lights, are both companions and collaborators, their spores seeding new growth as they flit across the landscape, tracing invisible glyphs in the air.

The relationship between the Umbravivens and the plateau is symbiotic. It grants them shelter, sustenance, and the strange clay-like substances they use to bend and build reality; in return, they nurture its growth, expand its boundaries with their willful reshaping of the environment, and lend it energy through their own shimmering essence. They plant seeds in the mineral-rich soil, but the crops they reap are not food in the traditional sense: clusters of calcifying pods grow like living crystals and are tended with the utmost delicacy. When the time is right, the pods hum faintly, releasing a vapor-like substance that sustains the Umbravivens, feeding the ebbs and flows of their liquid-light veins.

There are other inhabitants of this place, too. The snout moles burrowing through the sponge-like dirt are treated with casual affection by the villagers, often coaxed to rest near the Umbravivens’ fires of cold light. The feathered lizards, meanwhile, are held in high regard, their iridescent scales said to house fragments of ancient light captured during apocryphal stellar births. The villagers have crafted long, low flutes from a type of glassy stone found on the plateau and play eerie, trembling notes to summon the lizards, which respond with screeching harmonics that form a mesmerizing duet.

And then, there are the shadows. They worry even the Umbravivens, though the beings never say as much in their music-speak. The shadows here are oddly animate, long and lean like dancers whose limbs stretch across impossible distances. Detached from their objects, they crawl across the plateau, hiding in the clefts of rocks or pooling beneath those floating puddles of transparent water. At times, the shadows brush against the Umbravivens or the wandering creatures, temporarily darkening patches of whatever they touch as though to drink in their light. Such moments are unsettling, but balance always seems to return, the shadows drifting away like flotsam on an ocean current.

The sky above frames it all, eternal night illuminated not by a sun but by countless, uncannily bright stars scattered like spilled diamonds across the velvety black. Constellations shift unpredictably; sometimes they seem to dance or wink, and rumors among the Umbravivens' choral songs suggest that some constellations represent older beings from beyond who watch over this land and guide its unfolding.

The high plateau, with its unreal topography and half-real residents, is not a place for the faint-hearted. To the uninitiated, it might feel hostile or indifferent—in every sense the bleeding edge of creation, where existence itself is raw and unrefined. But for the Umbravivens and their ecosystem of peculiar allies, this place is home, not despite its whims and fluxes, but because of them. They are its stewards, its companions, its progeny and parents all at once. Every grain of sand, every scuttling thing, every thrumming note of the wind and every shifting shadow feels interlinked in a symphony of emergent possibility. This is a land where reality itself has not yet chosen its final form. And the same is true of those who live here.
