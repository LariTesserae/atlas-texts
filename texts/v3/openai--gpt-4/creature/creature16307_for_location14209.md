The creatures of the Realmscape are not like those of unspoiled, natural worlds. Here, life defies its own definition, as though the old machinery and barren soils have conjured beings not born, but assembled, sculpted from the detritus of an era past. They are neither wholly biological nor mechanical, but instead occupy some peculiar niche in between, entities caught mid-step between intention and accident.

Among the ruined machinery, you might first notice the **Sibilants**, ephemeral beings that seem to drift and hover, not as creatures of flesh or metal, but as threads of luminous vapor. Their bodies, if they can truly be called "bodies," are made of diaphanous wisps of light, faintly tinged with the hues of oxidized metal—copper-green, rust-red, tin-gray. They gather near the arches of crumbling chimneys, drawn as if by memory to the once-thriving industrial furnaces. Their movement is languid and reminiscent of exhaled breath on a cold day, swirling in shapes that suggest a haunting intelligence. When nudged—for the wind, or a gesture, seems to repel them—they release a faint, sorrowful hum that harmonizes with the ever-distant throb of the Steamdrums. Some say the Sibilants are fragments of the industrial era itself, the sighs of extinguished engines given ghostly form.

Closer to the ground, jutting from between warped plates of metal and banks of glittering, glass-like soil, walk the **Brassweavers**. Gangly arachnid things they are, fashioned as if by accident from discarded cogs, wire-like appendages, and insectoid joints. Each of them moves with surprising precision, their needle-pointed limbs tapping out subtle rhythms as they scuttle across the land, collecting fragments and detritus to build strange, unknowable constructs. It is as if they labor for purposes lost to comprehension, recreating small-scale versions of the great machines forgotten by time. Occasionally, their movements seem to synchronize with the Steamdrums, as though they are listening to the rhythm and responding in kind, and no living observer has ever managed to disrupt a Brassweaver unnoticed; their awareness appears vast and difficult to comprehend, as if not from instinct but some other governing logic.

Then there are the **Moimotes**, diminutive and glistening creatures that crawl along the sparse vegetation. They look like beetles rendered in tarnished bronze, their smooth carapaces gleaming faintly under the luminous sky. The Moimotes do not skitter like insects of Earthly origin; their movements are strangely considerate, pausing often to press their segmented legs into the metallic stems of the biomechanical flora. On closer inspection, their actions seem intimate—symbiotic, even. The vegetation releases tiny drops of moisture, sucked in thirstily by the Moimotes, but the beetles leave behind glowing trails, chemical imprints that encourage the strange, metallic plants to grow. In this way, they become the unassuming custodians of this desolate domain, carrying life from one brittle plateau to the next.

But no creature in the Realmscape has the same reputation of awe and fear as the **Thrumlords**, great lumbering forms that rise from the shadows of the Steamdrums themselves. The Thrumlords are titanic beings whose silhouettes blend almost seamlessly with the mechanical landscape around them—indeed, many mistake them for dormant wreckage until they stir. Their bodies are massive amalgamations of tarnished steel and dull, bronze-like plating, hammered together in shapes that evoke the anatomy of creatures far too large for this world. Their chest cavities thrum with a rhythmic, almost organic beat that echoes the Steamdrums, suggesting a shared life force. Though they are perceived to be rare, a traveler who watches the pulsing horizon for long enough will likely see one heave itself to its feet, as though summoned by some celestial signal. Where they walk, the earth beneath groans in noisy protest, and yet they seem to move with aim and purpose, though no one can say what that purpose might be.

And yet, for all the marvel and terror of the Thrumlords, the strangest residents of the Realmscape might be those thought lost long ago: the **Remnants**, beings sculpted in the caricature of humanity, but more artifact than animal. They are minimally constructed—each one unique, formed from scavenged pieces of shattered pipes, gears, and shards of broken glass.
