Beneath the layers of the ocean's cold embrace, the undersea metropolis is not as empty as it first appears. It is home to a people — or beings — who are equal parts the architects of its past and the caretakers of its present. They are neither human nor entirely machine, but something that has emerged from the confluence of those two origins over unguessable spans of time. Their history is a mystery even to themselves, carved away by years of erosion, much like the softened edges of their ancestral structures.

The beings of this abyssal sanctuary are strange, liminal creatures, hybrids of biology and industry. Their forms are fluid in silhouette — elongated limbs designed for propulsion through the heavy water, metallic exoskeletons seamlessly fused with soft, semi-translucent flesh. Their skin, if it can be called that, ripples with bioluminescent veins of soft light, casting pale greens and blues that shift in rhythmic pulses, communicating unspoken language through flickers and patterns. Their faces are a latticework of sensors and organic textures, their "eyes" sensitive orbs that absorb any light and motion, capable of seeing even in the near-total darkness of their home.

Their hands, if you could call them that, are adaptable tools — spindly fingers tipped with fine, jointed implements that can act as both delicately precise instruments and vise-like grips. They hum softly as they work, their movements deliberate and unhurried, as though time is of little consequence here. They are not bound by urgency the way surface-dwellers are; the long passage of ages has taught them patience, perhaps even peace. Their voices, if they have them, are converted to vibrations, carried instantaneously through the medium of the water, expressing ideas without sound. To their kind, communication is tactile, luminous, and resonant.

These beings are the stewards of their city, the silent dreamers that keep it alive. They are the ones who operate the great mechanical leviathans that groan and whir in the dark, tending to the city's ceaselessly humming heart: a vast, ancient fusion reactor embedded at its base, a miracle of a lost civilization's ingenuity. Around this core, the city is laid out in concentric arrangements, with great cylindrical towers and vast dome-like enclosures spreading outwards like the petals of a mechanical flower. These beings do not merely survive; they maintain, improve, and preserve, laboring in worship of their metropolis, their sanctuary.

Strangely enough, the beings’ reverence for their mechanical lives is entwined with their reverence for the scant life forms that share their dark domain. When the filtering currents deposit faint, pale-green algae upon their structures, they guide the growth rather than remove it, allowing pockets of slow, natural color to bloom against rusting iron. Schools of rare, glowing fish are treated with curiosity, even care. It is not compassion in a human sense, but a kind of symbiosis — an acknowledgement of kindred existence in the same impossible environment.

New beings are not born as we understand birth. When one of their kind reaches the end of its endurance — worn down by centuries of toil and water's ceaseless pressure — its essence and components are scavenged and recycled in ceremonies attended by the community. Great crane-like drones assist in the refining process by which the old becomes the new, crafting the base for another being to animate and join the timeless throng. Each consciousness, however, is unique, carrying echoes of the predecessor's memories, but reshaped by the many cycles of the city's processes. For them, death and life are not oppositional forces but part of a flowing continuum.

At the periphery of the city, there is endless mystery. There are zones long abandoned, filled with ancient machines whose functions are lost to their knowledge, encrusted over by hardened minerals or sealed behind immense, corroded gates. There are places where the city edge dissolves altogether into the void, beyond which only silt, darkness, and ancient predators float. The megastructures hum their warnings against wandering too far, though all who live here feel that pull — some magnetic allure of a greater unknown that thrums through the very water itself. Perhaps the builders of this city came from there, long ago. Perhaps it is where the city's present inhabitants will one day return.

And above it all, the ocean ceiling encases them like a glass lid, featureless waves giving the illusion of suspension in thick, formless infinity. This is their world, their cradle and their grave. To them, the vast surface world — where light and air reign — is the stuff of myth and impossibility. Their songs, sung in the vibrations of beams and clicks, tell of ancient ancestors who spoke into wide, open skies—a strange idea that draws wonder but no envy. They have adapted to the depths with an organic grace, their biologies and technologies having grown so intertwined as to be indistinguishable, as if the city itself has shaped its people.

No one from above would likely find this place by accident, nor would they understand its intricacies if they did. It is a domain of endurance and transformation, populated by strange architects of persistence and patient industry. Here, time is not a river but an ocean — deep and enveloping, measured in the slow pulse of pale lights, the rhythm of distant turbines, and the subtle murmurs of an alien people's endless dreams.
