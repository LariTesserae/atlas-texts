Existing in this terrain, the city's most intimidating dwellers are mankind itself. Here, in an unbroken dance of harmony and defiance with nature, live a tribal-like people - seemingly human, yet drawn from the clay of a separate reality. With skin the brown of aged mahogany, these denizens have bodies that are sinewy and strong, echoes of a lifestyle shaped by soaring heights and challenging landscapes. They adorn themselves in clothing ingeniously woven from bright flora and odd mountain materials, creating a palette of vibrant hues and patterns that mirror their untamed surroundings.

Their faces are arresting, graced by a feverish intensity mirroring the pulsating fervor of their city. Eyes of startling colors — gold, silver, and even hues that change with the sun — hold depths far more ancient than their youthful countenances suggest. Their eyes, eerily expressive, speak volumes of the untold stories of the mountain and its bizarre reality.

These people are wild, unreservedly part of the cosmic oddness of their environment. Their hands are calloused from labor and their movements suggest an innate connection with the stone and flora in everything they do. They live unhurried, patient lives, mirroring the mountain’s slow dance with time. Just as the city doesn’t conform to the expected norms of urban human settlement, neither do its inhabitants adhere to the typical behaviors of a civilized society. Not primitive, but primal; the line fine but discernible.

Notably, the people bear an uncanny affinity to the omnipresent water elements, mirroring its fluidity and persistence in their mundane tasks and phenomenal feats alike. There is a sense of reverence too; baths, often held in sacred pools, are communal and elaborate, a reflection of their intrinsic connection to the water cycle. Moreover, their bodies appear uniquely adapted to their wet haven; they seemingly do not sweat, do not display discomfort in the ever-present mist that hangs in the air. In fact, they seem almost amphibian at times, dipping in and out of the water bodies with ease.

Their communication is uniquely sensory, a blend of song-like speech, whistles, and a rhythm achieved through percussive body movements that seem to resonate with the music of the environment. Emanating from them is an echo of their city’s symphony, a continual reminder of their boundless ties with the ambience. They do not seem to possess written language. Rather, elaborate stories and histories are immortalized through legacies of song and dance, taking shapes that defy traditional narrative coherency.

Distinctively missing from this community are domesticated animals. In the almost eerie absence of fauna, the inhabitants have become efficient and creative in obtaining sustenance. They consume a diverse diet of local plants, fruits, and mysterious marine creatures from the mountain’s water bodies. They are self-sustaining, intelligent stewards of their environment, their harmonious existence a testament to nature's sporadic yet incessant adaptability.

At night, they gather, their voices harmonizing with the nocturnal sounds of the mountain city, their bodies swaying in the damp shimmering moonlight, a surreal blending of shadows and spectral veridescence. Life and the living city together, singing the hymn of existence.

These folks are uncanny whispers of a reality too fantastical to be human, slipped through the rupture of twisted existence, extant because this city does, and vice versa. They are a manifestation of their city’s integral dualism, living between the paradoxes of the real and surreal, representing the vibrant, contradictory heartbeat of their land. They are the human testimony to the incredible ability to not just survive, but thrive in the most unconventional, living at the extremes of human adaptability and resilience.
