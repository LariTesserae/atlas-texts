Pellopia is not uninhabited—not truly. Its residents are as unexpected, subtle, and mysterious as the city itself. They do not announce themselves with roars or voices or machinery; they drift, nest, flow, and weave through the moist, glistening labyrinth that is their home, like shadows wrapped in fog.

---

### The Quiet Ones
The most numerous, though rarely seen directly, are what some would call "The Quiet Ones." No one has ever truly described them, not in the form of a singular shape, because they are fluid in their being, bending and twisting to match Pellopia's vaulted wilderness of crusted stone and steel. They move much as water itself does—seeping into rooftops, threading through cracks in walls, and condensing into strange, cryptic figures when they choose to pause.

Their forms glisten faintly as though perpetually coated in dew. Sometimes they are planar like sheets of flowing silk, rippling as they move through narrow corridors, while other times they cluster into globs that remind one of luminescent jellyfish, their translucence betraying glimpses of swirling patterns of light and shadow within. They make no sound except for an occasional faint, resonant hum when they are especially active or gathering purpose.

They appear most often during Pellopia’s humid, misty gloamings, when the light is a soft gray-blue, casting no shadows. The Quiet Ones are thought to have an intimate relationship with the moisture in the air, perhaps even existing because of it. If gathered sunlight catches them just right, you might see tiny vascular webs within their amorphous bodies—traces of something organic, something alive but not alive like you or I.

It’s unclear whether they create the city’s weather or adapt to it, but it is said that rain comes more swiftly and lingers longer when in their presence. Vegetation grows healthily in spaces they frequent, suggesting they are agents not of decay but of renewal. Whether they once built Pellopia or inherited it, no one knows.

---

### The Cloudkind
High above, in the upper levels of Pellopia’s towering structures, "The Cloudkind" dwell. Fragile, wispy creatures, barely tethered to the physical world, the Cloudkind drift in the thin, chilly air near the peaks of spiraling skyscrapers. They are gossamer beings, not unlike floating petals of ice and vapor. When sunlight or moonlight refracts through them, they shimmer with pale rainbow hues.

Despite their ethereal appearance, the Cloudkind do not seem fragile. Like the wind and weather they thrive in, they are persistent, drifting deliberately between high pinnacles and sunken rooftops. At times, they cluster and spiral in an elaborate aerial choreography, weaving patterns in the sky. At other times, they drift alone, whispering to the high-altitude mist in sounds too faint for human ears.

The Cloudkind appear to harbor an ancient intelligence or, at least, a sense of memory. Some speculate they are remnants of Pellopia’s architects, their forms dissolved and woven into the atmospheric murmurs of their destroyed machines. Others say they are a consequence of the city’s strange air currents and the life that it fosters—a symbiotic colony spun into ephemeral beings. Regardless, they remain aloof and distant, dwelling in a world above all others.

---

### The Rootborn
In the underground recesses of Pellopia’s forgotten foundations—beneath its rain-polished streets and erratic corridors—another form of life thrives. These creatures, known locally as "The Rootborn," are slow-moving, moss-covered entities that blend seamlessly with the overgrowth. Often mistaken for humps of resilient ferns or boulders thick with lichen, they are, upon closer inspection, living beings with stout yet flexible forms that heave and curl like the seas themselves.

Each has a unique texture, like stone blurred with bark, flesh plated with the scars of an eternity spent pressing against concrete and roots alike. Some Rootborn emit a faint apricot-hued glow from within the heavily riddled craters sunk into their massive backs. Though their large, lumbering forms suggest brute strength, the Rootborn are patient tenders, slowly cultivating patches of greenery hidden within the city’s urban sprawl. They cradle seedlings and saplings in their massive, mossy paws, implanting them in cracks and crevices where the roots can take hold.

Rootborn whispers—low, rumbling tremors—resonate like fading storm echoes in an empty canyon. Some argue they are the soul of Pellopia, that their acts of husbandry keep the city breathing amidst its perpetual decay. Some have tried to follow the paths of these moss-cloaked beings into the catacombs from which they come, but few return. Those who do claim that Pellopia’s underbelly is a cathedral of roots and watery echoes, where time itself seems trapped in the soft shimmer of phosphorescent pools.

---

### The Luminoids
When twilight begins to stretch its indigo arms across the city, the Luminoids wake. They are the only creatures in Pellopia who add their colors to the darkened streets. Appearing only when the air is sufficiently thick with mist and shadow, these beings are strange, willowy forms. They resemble walking lanterns, their thin, almost skeletal shapes glowing with soft, rhythmic pulses of color.

The color of a Luminoid’s glow depends on its state of being—brilliant persimmon if it moves quickly and purposefully, deep cerulean if it rests or lingers in thought, moss green if it brushes against vegetation, seeming to communicate with the sparse flora in tender, silent exchanges. They move serenely, scattering through the streets like streaks of dappled light passing over still puddles.

They are deeply curious but also fleeting, vanishing around corners just as you begin to sense them fully. They leave no trace of their presence behind except a faint luminescence, lingering on the surface of water and stone for moments before being swallowed by the surrounding dim. Some believe the Luminoids are drawn to the past remnants of Pellopia’s human-like habitants, kneeling near the corroding tools and artifacts as if in reverence or sorrow.

Some artifacts glow faintly when touched by them, as though their presence activates some long-abandoned technology hidden deep in Pellopia’s ancient guts. Are the Luminoids accidental keepers of forgotten secrets? Or perhaps the spirits of whatever came before, stubbornly illuminating the decay?

---

Even the most patient observer could spend a lifetime exploring Pellopia and never fully understand its residents. There are endless whispers of other beings—creatures born of the city’s forgotten histories, the mountain peaks, or the very rain—but these are Pellopia’s children who come forth most regularly. They endure within its strange dialogue of stillness and renewal, water and stone, erosion and growth.

Each being seems possessed of a purpose—not chaotic, not arbitrary, but reflective of the mysterious, symbiotic balance that Pellopia itself embodies. To watch them is to watch a world at peace with its endless cycles, a quiet coexistence of the living and the ruinous, the seen and the unseen. One could almost, if one listened closely enough, begin to hear the breath of the city itself—a deep, slow rhythm that chants eternity.
