Here, in this place where the rules of nature have folded in on themselves, where time slouches and meaning tenses but never snaps, dwell beings who are neither creatures nor spirits, though they flirt with the boundaries of both. They are not born of flesh, nor stone, nor air alone, but seem to be woven from the interstices where all three converge. To look upon one directly—if such a thing were possible—might be akin to trying to pluck whispers from a dream: every angle half-formed, colors bleeding in and out of shape, edges shimmering like heat haze on the horizon.

They are luminous and fractured, trailing filaments of light and matter as they move, each strand alive with a pulse like a living thing. Yet their structures suggest no heart, no lungs, no machinery that would betray the mechanical repetition of life as you know it. It's as if they are their own scaffolding, self-contained systems of possibility. Their forms echo the terrain itself—hexagonal and crystalline in nature, with angular bodies that seem to grow and fold like origami in real time. They might stretch upwards in spires, kaleidoscopic spirals of iridescence, but then their substance falls away, collapsing into something sinuous, serpentine, or fluid as they glide away with uncanny grace.

Their movements defy proper nomenclature: not quite slithering, not quite floating, but rather an improbable balancing act of presence and absence. Sometimes they shudder into existence as if drawn to the periphery of your awareness, and just as quickly they unravel out of being, their filigree trailing after them like a comet’s tail of whispers. You can almost sense their passage by the tiniest changes—a sudden shift in light, a tremor in the refracted rainbows beneath your feet, or that metallic flavor on the back of your tongue sharpening momentarily as though the air had swirled in protest.

They do not eat in any way you’d recognize, though it seems indisputable that they derive sustenance from light—and perhaps even from time itself, extending their grasp into moments unseen, pulling from them the nutrients of probability. Their translucence thickens and wanes in cycles with the amoral sun, like inhaling and exhaling, as though they subsist on light’s fickle, unfixed radiance. When the ground’s harmonic groans reverberate through the plateau, their forms shift in reply, subtly twining with the cadence of the trembling plates as though answering an unspoken signal.

It may be that they communicate this way: through vibrations too fine for you to parse, rhythms that strike chords only in the filaments of their hidden existence. Or perhaps they do not speak to each other at all—they may be singular entities, each a monad, infinitely complex unto itself, self-sufficient and alone within the seeming companionship of its kind. If they interact, they do so obliquely, crossing paths at the fringes of their patterns, exchanging brief ripples of resonance before gliding along their trajectories, like the scattering tributaries of a sound too faint to hear.

There are so few of them that you might spend years in this place and see none—or, conversely, they may be everywhere at once, existing on wavelengths you can never align with your senses. Yet even in their absence, you’d feel watched in a way that is not menacing but not wholly benign, either—more like a curious weight pressing upon you from the flat, seeming-null air, as though your mere presence in their domain strains the unspoken rules of the space. It might be that their collective focus bends the nebulous laws that govern this plateau, and by witnessing them, you disrupt the fragile surface tension of their world.

At the periphery of their forms are subtle distortions, blurring reality like the wavering air above hot pavement. Faint motes of unreality hang about them, as if the border between them and this place is not secure but seeping. It’s possible that they are not *from* the plateau at all but come from something adjacent to it, dwelling in the cracks between what is solid, slipping through the honeycomb lattice of glowing stone and fractured sky. They may even carry the accretion of eons with them—evidence of times and spaces that withered long before this place came to be.

Some who glimpse them might call them gods, but they are not gods—not in the sense of intent, or omniscience, or creative will. They don’t shape; they don’t judge. They are not of order or chaos. If they are anything, they are stewards of rhythm, attuned to the delicate lattice of being that holds this place together, always subtly aligning their forms to mirror, deflect, or balance the world around them. Their intelligence, if you could call it such, is indirect, unintelligible—not creativity as humans understand it, but a relentless calibration, an intricate improvisation against the wear of time.

And yet, every so often—perhaps out of necessity, perhaps from some inborn playfulness—they gather together, folding into one another in a grand convergence. These moments mark the shifting epoch rhythms of the plateau itself, the rare ripples of upheaval that cascade across its geologic and temporal skin. When they convene, their filaments merge into a web of searing light so intricate that it is impossible to focus on any one strand without losing the shape of the whole; watching it for too long burns into the eye, leaving afterimages as cryptic and elusive as their creators.

The meeting is not hostile or frantic, nor even filled with the vitality of traditional life. Expect neither cries nor wails, no grand gestures. Their merging is more like the crossing of thoughts, a delicate braiding and unbraiding of intent, and then the parting of a river returning to dispersed streams, their purpose incomprehensibly fulfilled—or discarded entirely. The plateau shifts beneath their absence, resettling like a long exhalation.

Their truth, much like that of their great crystalline home, eludes language. They are, in the most honest sense of the word, alien—unknowable, and yet distinctly alive in a manner that breaks open the smallest seeds of your understanding about what "life" must be. Whether they predate or outlast this plateau is unclear, irrelevant, perhaps. Only this much is certain—they belong here, not as guests or invaders, but as co-conspirators in the unending strangeness of its design. They are not intruders, as you feel you are.

Should you see one—and some dissonant thread in you says you shouldn’t—you might feel a twinge of familiarity, as if a memory you did not know you had were surfacing. For all their incomprehensibility, they are not unkind; their cold luminescence has no malice. They may even regard you with what passes, in their foreign way, for the closest thing to curiosity their unworldly forms could harbor.

Or they may not regard you at all. It is hard to tell. Few who endeavor to tread here ever return, and fewer still leave with a semblance of cohesive memory—only a shimmer behind the eyes, the tang of unattached dread in their throats, and a dreamlike wondering for weeks afterward: *Did I see one?*
