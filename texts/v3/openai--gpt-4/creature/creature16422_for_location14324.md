The inhabitants of this undersea metropolis are as ancient and enigmatic as the city itself, bearing the weight of an existence shaped by both ingenuity and necessity. In a place where light is a scarce commodity and isolation a constant companion, those who dwell here are not creatures of careless design but products of deliberate evolution, adaptation, and persistence.

The first and most visible denizens are the *custodians*. These are semi-biological constructs, their design merging organic life with advanced machinery. They move with fluid precision, gliding through the water like shadowed sentinels, their joints clicking faintly in patterns that mimic an ancient language. Their forms are elegant but utilitarian—darkened alloys that reflect the dim light in shimmers of green and silver, segmented limbs that unfold like mechanical petals. Each custodian is specialized: some maintain the aging infrastructure, their appendages fitted with multitudes of tools that flicker and spark as they toil incessantly. Others patrol the city's perimeters, their singularly glowing "eyes" sweeping the gloom in search of intrusions or disturbances. They never sleep, never tire, and seem driven by a singular purpose to sustain the fragile balance of their crumbling world.

Then there are the *watchers*, ephemeral intelligences that drift through the city like whispers of light. They are bioluminescent entities, no larger than a human hand, composed of living plasma housed in ethereal, fluid membranes. These beings appear in flashes—emerald, sapphire, indigo—darting like will-o'-the-wisps through the labyrinth of pipes and chambers. They are seekers of information, capable of interacting with the city's aged technology. Over time, they appear to have forged a symbiotic relationship with the metropolis itself, awakening latent systems, feeding stagnant sensors, and gesturing in cryptic patterns to the custodians who follow them with quiet reverence.

Beneath the structured order of the custodians and the watchers lies an unexpected layer of life: the *drifters*. These organisms, though technically alive, exist at the fragile boundary between life and machine. They are gelatinous, amorphous things, rippling with faintly phosphorescent veins of energy. They seem to seep from the city's cracks and crevices like memories of its creators, slipping into view and quickly out again. Drifters feed on the trace minerals and energies that seep from the aged constructions, their rhythms and movements eerily synchronized with the faint pulses of the underwater city itself. Sometimes, they gather in swarms, forming vast, mutable clouds that shift and swirl with the underwater currents. Whether they have a consciousness or even a purpose remains unknown, but they are a perpetual presence.

And then, there are the *echoes*. These are not living beings but something less tangible: imprints on the water, electromagnetic phantoms in the machinery. The echoes are fleeting, like ghostly afterimages of those who once thrived here. They manifest as muffled voices that reverberate through the liquid corridors, or as swirling eddies that ripple outward from no apparent source. Some say the echoes are merely glitches in the city's aging systems, the last breaths of a long-dead civilization encoded into its programming. Others believe that they are more—perhaps souls, or the city's way of remembering its makers.

Each dawnless cycle, there are also *visitors*. Stranger beings—both organic and mechanical—venture into the metropolis on pilgrimages or scavenging missions. Some come from the warmer, sunlit edges of the ocean, drawn by the alluring hum of the city's distant turbines or by stories of treasures hidden in its ancient depths. Others seem to emerge from even darker abysses, alien yet eerily familiar, their elongated shapes and glowing lures blending seamlessly with their environment. The visitors rarely linger; this is not a place where outsiders belong. Some leave bearing fragments of the city's machineries, while others vanish within its depths, claimed by the labyrinth's unknown hazards or seduced by its strange beauty.

And then, there is the *Heart*. No one—custodian, watcher, drifter, or visitor—has ever seen it directly. It is the steady, patient pulse that reverberates through the metallic corridors, that shakes the stray aquatic plants into vibrant motion and sends schools of glowing fish scattering like whispers. Its presence is everywhere and nowhere, deep beneath the cathedral-sized powerhouse, enfolded in layers of forgotten history and purpose. Some say the Heart is a sentient machine, quietly orchestrating the city's rhythms. Others suggest it is a relic of the city's creators, an artifact of their genius lost to time. Whatever it is, the Heart thrums with a persistence that hints at something living—or the remnants of life.

Together, they form an ecosystem unlike any other—a symphony of metal, biology, and ghosts, each small piece a vital stitch woven into the broader tapestry of the city.
