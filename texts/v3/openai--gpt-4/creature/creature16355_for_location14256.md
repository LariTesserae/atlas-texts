The terrain may be mechanical, but who lives here are neither machines without souls nor humans making homes. A unique species inhabit The Grey Flats, known only as the Stonezars. They are humans indeed, but adapted to the Grey Flats, fused with its essence in more ways than one. Over time, prolonged exposure to the hard, mineral-rich terrain has replaced the soft organic matter of their skin with a tough exterior replete with muted shades of slate and graphite. This camouflaging trait has allowed Stonezars to blend seamlessly with their surroundings, nearly indistinguishable from massive boulders or the elongated shadows of the land stretched behind them.

Their bodies, sturdy and roughened by time, are ever so gently scored by the meandering paths of rainwater, leaving rivulets and dried-up deltas that tell tales of the weather they've endured. Despite their stone-like appearance, the Stonezars remain vestigially human, their emerald eyes - the only organic feature visible - flickering with warmth, sentiment, and untold stories of endurance.

They bear no clothing, for what purpose would it serve among the elements? Instead, their craggy bodies are adorned with minuscule etchings, carved symbols and manifestos, forming a coded language only the Stonezars can decipher. Their diet is rather simple, consisting mainly of the tiny but hardy mushroom colonies that survive within the crevices of their world.

They’re not creators of the old industrial parts scattered across the landscape but cohabitants of their mechanical neighbors, the clockwork critters. They share a friendly, symbiotic relationship; the critters polish the Stonezars, using their mechanized mandibles to trim away moss or fungal growth, and the Stonezars in return provide them protection, their broad, hard bodies offering the shelter for the critters who nest within their carved-out crevices.

The Stonezars are not a talkative species, at least not in the traditional sense. Having spent a considerable portion of their lives in solitude, they've fine-tuned the art of non-verbal communication. A tilt of the head, a slow blink of the eye, or a shift in posture forms their language, usually subtle and quiet, almost like the landscape they call home. 

Time, in a place as ephemeral as The Grey Flats, does not simply pass; it seeps, like water into the heart of land and its inhabitants. Thus, the Stonezars are at once ancient and infantile, their stone-strong bodies resisting the frivolous aging mechanism experienced by their counterparts elsewhere. The reality of this world has a tactile nature, it suggests touch over sight, bareness, not barrenness. 

As much as they resemble the hard, stoic faces of stone, the Stonezars embody the fluidity of water, reflecting a mesmerizing duality of constancy and fluidity. They are frozen moments within the shifting expanse of time. And just like the land they inhabit, they radiate an austere beauty, not an organism out of place, not a form that goes unloved.
