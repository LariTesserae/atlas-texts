The city-state of Yuralmira is an enigma, and so, too, are its inhabitants. To know the city is to know those who live there—beings shaped by this paradoxical world of damp stone and radiant light, echoes and silences, suspended on a plateau that brushes the clouds.

### The People of Yuralmira
The citizens themselves are striking, though not in ways that are immediately jaw-dropping; their lives have a rhythm and texture that grows on you like the tenacious flora of their land. They carry an air of quiet vigilance, a sense of being simultaneously anchored to the earth and somehow aloof from it. Their figures tend toward the lanky, as if stretched by the vast horizons they contemplate daily from their rocky aerie. Their skin has a pearlescent sheen, not from light itself, but from the constant kiss of moisture in the atmosphere, as though they are perpetually anointed by the city’s damp embrace.

Their eyes, however, are the most disconcerting thing about them. They possess colors that defy simple description—iridescent grays that shift to faint violets, ochres flecked with gold, greens resembling sunlit moss. These eyes, often shaded beneath wide-brimmed hats or veils to shelter them from the endless play of light, hold a wisdom both welcoming and unnervingly observant. It is as though they see not just the world before them, but the epochs that have ebbed and flowed in the city’s unyielding stillness.

Their movements are deliberate, unhurried, and always seem to possess some secondary layer of meaning. Even their conversations are crafted with a poetic economy, sentences polished like the stones of their age-old structures. They are not overly given to laughter, but when it does come, it ripples like unexpected sunlight breaking through fog. Their smiles are soft and knowing, tinged with an understanding of a broader timeline, as if they’ve known countless seasons and you are but one more arrival, one more moment to fold into the collection.

The people’s attire mirrors the city itself—a fusion of the ancient and the intricate. They wear robes of woven moss-thread, shimmering slightly with moisture, laced together with silver fibers extracted from local mountain ores. Their jewelry is minimal, often formed from polished stone etched with arcane symbols—glyphs of “protection,” they say, whose meanings even they seem unwilling to fully explain.

### The Creatures of Yuralmira
The non-human inhabitants are equally curious. Birds of iridescent plumage flit across the sky and perch on the high points of Yuralmira’s towers, their songs oddly modulated, as if stretching across an unfamiliar tonal range. They are not afraid of people and seem to possess a distinct awareness, staring with heads cocked at almost-human angles, as though listening to conversations or silently digesting a passerby’s thoughts.

Among the wildlife that roams freely in the city, the deer and rabbits show curious variations less common elsewhere. The deer have eyes that glitter like faceted gemstones, both unnervingly intelligent and profoundly serene; to meet their gaze feels like looking into a wellspring untouched by time. The rabbits sport velvety coats streaked with silver hues that glint like liquid mercury under moonlight. They are nimble and quick, their footfalls eerily soundless even upon the city’s stone streets.

Monkeys are sacred here, not because they hold any divine symbolism, but because they are seen as fellow city-dwellers, as intelligent and self-directed as their human counterparts. They are notorious tricksters and can mimic human behaviors to unsettling degrees—watching them hold a fruit or a tool in their dexterous fingers is to wonder whether they're copying you, or whether they've simply decided, long ago, to live alongside you as equals. They have been caught, on occasion, carving something akin to the city’s ancient glyphs into the bark of trees.
