The beings that make their home in the Conduit are as alien, enigmatic, and marvelous as the labyrinthine city that surrounds them. You might glimpse them first as fleeting lights—shared pulses of movement and luminescence almost too fast for the eye to track. But patience reveals their forms, though "form" is perhaps too rigid a word for what they are. 

Flickering between states of matter, they are composed of something that defies easy classification—part fluid, part crystal, part energy. When stationary, their outlines shimmer like molten mercury, their surfaces mirroring their surroundings and refracting liquid light into oily rainbows. Scattered across their semi-visible exteriors are prismatic nodules that glow faintly with gentle, ebbing hues. These nodules could be sensory organs, storage vessels, or perhaps sites of cognition. Whatever their function, they pulse in rhythms that seem to cohere into a silent language—one of color and light, too complex for human comprehension.

When in motion, their geometry warps in fractal patterns, elongating and contracting like vivid liquid ribbons. They move not with the undulation of sea creatures but with a chaotic purpose—as though each shift in the currents triggers a proportional adjustment in their forms. A creature might stretch into a thin filament miles long, riding the heaving swirl of the medium, before coalescing back into a tight, pulsing sphere. Their movements are more than defensive or mechanical; they suggest profound adaptability or perhaps an existence in flux, flickering between dimensions.

Their numbers vary wildly. In some sections of the submerged city, weeks or years might pass without a single glimpse, and then—without warning—a great amorphous swarm might gather. Hundreds, thousands, millions of them, moving as one, spiraling and rising in tight coils or spreading like a detonation frozen in time. These gatherings can form temporary architectures of their own, vast churning constructs within the greater city—a transient cathedral of pulsating light, branching filaments, and undulating shapes that bloom for minutes or hours before disbanding.

Individually, they operate with unsettling intention. You notice them at work, weaving themselves into the gaps between structures, embedding those crystalline growths into their surfaces, or siphoning away stray glowing particulates that drift through the liquid medium. When they linger near the obsidian-like monoliths or spiraled edifices of the city, it’s not idle curiosity. They carve into the surfaces, gesturing with lances of dense light or filigrees of crystalline matter, their actions leaving intricate patterns or shifting interfaces. Are they repairing? Growing? Feeding? Communicating? There’s no way to know, but their interaction with their environment suggests a relationship of maintenance or metamorphosis, as though they are both caretakers and organisms inextricably bound to the city, co-creators of its ancient, self-generating existence.

And then there are the massive ones. From the first sighting of these colossi, your sense of scale and awe collapses. These are not the sleek, darting entities that flit between structures; these are titanic presences whose forms move slowly, deliberately, their outlines so vast and transient they exist mostly as a suggestion rather than a fixed state. They stretch beyond structures, their edges indistinct, fading into the swirling dimness of the liquid. Their bodies, if bodies is even the right word, are hollow caverns where the smaller creatures play, as though these titans are ecosystems unto themselves. Occasionally, on these giants’ immense surfaces, the smaller entities cluster in dazzling, spiraling fractals, like sparks on an infinite black sea. The titans do not speak or act in ways that are overtly discernible, yet their ponderous movements resonate deep in the substrate, as though they are the source of the city’s rhythmic, guttural sounds.

Perhaps the most disquieting inhabitants, though, aren’t so easily spotted with the eyes. Every so often, you sense *something else* in the liquid—a presence that floats just on the edge of perception. These are not creatures but more like a wandering will, a thought-consciousness pressed into the very medium itself. These presences bend the liquid in their wake; patterns ripple outward where there are no currents. It is as if the Conduit holds echoes of a once-substantial intelligence, absorbed and disseminated by the very medium that surrounds you. If you drift too long in their influence, you might swear you glimpsed flashes of faces or discerned the resonance of music or speech in the ambient cacophony. It feels as if they notice you, not as a fellow conscious entity, but something akin to observing a falling leaf or a mote of dust—momentarily interesting, ultimately insignificant.

And yet, the sense persists that everything in this strange place—these flickering creatures, the crystalline latticework, the vast shadowy titans, and even the humming pressure of the medium itself—is all interconnected, all part of the same massive, unknowable mechanism. The residents are not separate from their city but are instead aspects of it, extensions that exist not as independent beings but as living, morphing expressions of the same pattern. The city grows itself, it feeds itself, it dreams itself. 

It is alive, and in it lives a community that is more like a breath exhaled from its lungs—a million disparate parts moving together in chaotic unity, a pulse of existence that is as incomprehensible as it is inevitable.
