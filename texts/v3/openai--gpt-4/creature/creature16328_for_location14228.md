Shards of their substance jut from the pools, angular filigree now dulled by centuries of water’s ceaseless erosion. These structures are not entirely inert, though. Soft echoes seem to bounce off them, low murmurs that begin out of nowhere, a resonance built less by design than by the awkward alignment of form and environment. Some pulse faintly beneath a crusting layer of mineral deposits as though participating in the land’s peculiar rhythm—not living, but also not wholly dead.

Here, amidst the unrelenting wet and gloom, live creatures whose forms have adapted to a world ruled by water and dimness. At first glance, they are hard to distinguish from the bleached stones and flowing currents, so intertwined are they with their surroundings. The slender forms of the *Veonix* rise and submerge, moving as if their flesh were an extension of the water itself. Their bodies are translucent, bending light in a way that renders them phantom-like—there one moment, gone the next. Long, serpentine, and segmented, they ripple across the water with silent elegance. Thin tendrils hang from their flanks, skimming the surface, sensing vibrations in the perpetual murmur of liquid motion. Beneath their translucent skin, faint colors flicker when light catches them just so: muted greens and spectral whites, like the fleeting memory of something once vibrant.

The *Veonix* are not alone. Other shapes stir here, though most are too strange or too diffuse to take a form that satisfies the eye. Pliable, mutable beings undulate within the deeper pools, shapes never settling into one. These creatures, the *Umbricks*, wear fluidity like a shroud—it is difficult to tell where they end and the water begins. At times, they seem like large, gelatinous sacs speckled with veins of dark mineral or light-devouring obsidian.
