The inhabitants of this silent world are neither wholly alive nor fully inert. They are beings shaped from the fabric of their surroundings, entities of transition—part material, part memory. You might not see them at first. They are indistinguishable from the mineral terrain, woven into the chalky soil and brittle surface spines that dot the landscape. But they dwell here, not hidden, merely unclaiming of their presence. They exist at the boundary of perception, like whispers meant only for the land itself.

Their forms are subtle and ambiguous, arising from the interplay between this place's timeless patience and its unresolved energies. At a distance, you might mistake them for the crooked vegetation—a trick of silhouette, a parody of stillness. But as you draw closer, you would sense the tremor of their almost-motion, the way their edges seem to blur slightly, as if caught between solid matter and a liquid haze. They do not step, or turn, or lean; their shifts, if they occur, belong to rhythms far slower than eyesight, a movement so gradual it belongs more to centuries than seconds.

They are tall, angular, their bodies formed of the same dark, matte texture as the wiry plant-like structures, though not rigid. When they move—with that creeping, tectonic patience—their surfaces ripple faintly, as if coreless, hollow. Their shapes do not conform to an earthly anatomy; each is a self-sustaining geometry—a twisting latticework of long, irregular arches and joints that converge in a way that defies outright pattern yet feels suffused with purpose. Between their limbs and spans, gaps reveal the perfect stillness of the air behind them. They cast no distinct shadow in the dimness because they are in some ways more absence of form than form itself.

They do not breathe; no rise or fall animates them. Nor do they speak, but those who listen carefully might detect an oscillation of faint sound in their vicinity—an irregular, low hum that mingles with the ever-present vibration of the land itself, but never quite overlaps.
