In this liminal, humming cradle of heat and metal, there are inhabitants. They are not merely survivors of this place; they are shaped by it, as much mechanisms as they are organisms, their forms and lives entwined with the endless rhythms of the subterranean factory. To enter their realm is to glimpse existence reimagined under a lens neither fully natural nor wholly artificial.

First, there are the *Attendants*. These beings are lank and multi-limbed, their skin a pale grey-green that shimmers faintly under the dim phosphorescent lights. Built narrow to weave through the tightest crevices, their skin seems stretched taut over their spindly frames, which are punctuated by segments of what appears almost like wrought metal. This is not armor, but rather a kind of natural augmentation—their skeletons externalized in places, reacting to the burdens of this constructed world.

Their eyes are multifaceted lenses embedded directly into their elongated skulls, each orb containing dozens of reflective apertures, giving them the ability to see in the near-complete darkness of the nether-zone. When one meets their gaze, it feels less like a meeting of eyes and more like falling into a tangle of cold, mathematical reflections. The Attendants move slowly with careful deliberation, their long limbs bending like the arms of a spider, fingers tipped with fine, delicate instruments. They are forever repairing, forever modifying, their thin hands reconfiguring the machinery that surrounds them. They work ceaselessly, as if compelled by the ancient purpose of the place, yet there is a troubling ambiguity to their actions. Do they know what they are building? Or are they mere extensions of the system, trapped in a feedback loop of infinite labor?

Occasionally, one might glimpse the *Machinic Wurms*. These are no ordinary denizens of the earth, though their forms borrow the sinuous logic of subterranean creatures like earthworms or serpents. Long, tubular, and armored with interlocking plates of something halfway between chitin and corroded steel, the Wurms appear to function as the blood vessels of this place, weaving through conduits and channels, transporting substances or energies unknown. They are powered by some unseen, internal rhythm—perhaps a heart-like mechanism deep within their twisted lengths, or perhaps the crawling currents of electricity. Their bioluminescent underbellies faintly glow as they slide along tunnels and pipes, leaving behind trails of faintly conductive residue in their wake.

There are smaller creatures, too—*Skittermotes*, they may be called. These are tiny, insect-like beings with carapaces of iridescent black that ripple momentarily as they flicker into and out of existence, almost as if their very presence here is unstable. They appear only in staccato bursts of motion, one moment twitching along the edges of walls, the next skipping impossibly across a yawning chasm in a single spasm of light-soaked velocity. Their purpose seems to be surveillance. Despite their individual speed and apparent autonomy, they're drawn to movement, clustering around intruders like inquisitive pixels. They approach with disarmingly organic curiosity, their backs flipping open to reveal swirling patterns of light—a language? A warning?

Deeper into this strange world, in the chambers where even the Attendants seem hesitant to venture for long, one might encounter the *Furnace Minds*. These are colossal fixtures embedded into the walls. Their forms are immovable: vast, dark silhouettes of amalgamated metal, stone, and other, stranger materials. Despite their inert forms, they emanate heat and a faint, whispered pulse that seems to penetrate the very bones of those who stand near. Their "faces"—if the broad surfaces they extend can be called such—are lattices of slowly turning gears and faintly glowing apertures. Do the Furnace Minds think? They seem too solemn to be mere machines, yet their intent is entirely opaque.

And there is something more, something that has no clear shape or form, yet seeps into every corner of the nether-zone. It is called only the *Deeping Pulse*—a presence, both ominous and oddly comforting, that feels older than even the machinery. It manifests not as a thing, but as an almost imperceptible thrumming beneath the sounds of grinding gears and dripping water. It is a heartbeat of sorts, vibrating through the very walls, through the bones of the Attendants, through the cold metal skin of the Wurms. Some say it is simply the natural echo of tectonic shifts above; others might whisper of an intellect that predates invention, the true master of this strange realm, lying beneath the machines, beneath the rock, in a sanctum yet unseen.

The beings of the subterranean nether-zone live, but their concept of living is alien. This is a world of endless, silent motion, each inhabitant playing a role that none can fully articulate, orbiting around a purpose buried and forgotten in the long-distant past. Do they toil willingly, or are they as much prisoners as witnesses to this place? Perhaps that answer has also been lost to the grinding gears and the march of time.
