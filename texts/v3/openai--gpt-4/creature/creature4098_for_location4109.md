The Living City of Thallomere is home to a unique tapestry of creatures and denizens, each as intimately tied to the ecosystem as the vines that twine through its labyrinthine streets. Its residents—both human and nonhuman—are not masters of their environment but symbiotic participants, shaping and being shaped by the city’s shifting, organic rhythms.

### The People of Thallomere
The human inhabitants of Thallomere, known as the Thallo, have long since adapted to the verdant, fluid world they call home. Their movements are nimble, almost balletic, as they navigate the winding corridors of branches and moss-laden boardwalks. Their very biology seems to have subtly adjusted to their surroundings; their skin carries a faint greenish hue, a result of lifelong exposure to the city’s moisture and faintly phosphorescent bioluminescent spores. This unique pigment genuinely glows faintly in the dim, misty light of Thallomere’s shadowy under-canopies.

Their clothing is grown rather than sewn, spun from the silken threads of enormous, web-weaving insects that share the city. Cloaks shimmer with natural iridescence, dyed with the pigments extracted from crushed berries and lichen, and their straps and cords are braided with supple vine. Jewelry, rather than gold or silver, is made of polished seeds, carved bone, and translucent drops of hardened tree sap, often enclosing small motes of plant matter or trapped spores inside like miniature worlds. 

In their daily lives, the Thallo have a quiet reverence for the ecosystem they inhabit. Every action is carried out with intention, from harvesting leaves without disturbing the host tree, to redirecting a stream to prevent erosion, to celebrating the death of a great ancient tree with rituals that honor the spaces it leaves behind. They see themselves less as renters of the city and more as its keepers, its gardeners, and its family.

### The Builders and the Singers
Among the Thallo, there are two distinct roles of great importance: the Builders and the Singers.

The Builders are artisans of the living. They possess an intimate knowledge of the flora of Thallomere, understanding each tree’s growth patterns and every vine’s strength. They can coax trees into growing in deliberate, architectural forms, crafting spiral staircases from curling trunks, windows from lattices of branches, and even entire bridges suspended by the tensile strength of woven roots. Their deep connection to the ecosystem is almost spiritual—they do not force their designs but instead collaborate with the plants themselves, relying on seasons, sunlight, and water to guide their vision. 

The Singers, on the other hand, are the voices of Thallomere. They communicate with the plants in a manner both mystical and deeply practical—using songs and tones of pitched vibration that the plants mysteriously respond to. Ancient traditions suggest the spores that fill the air carry messages between organism and singer, and while the phenomenon lacks formal explanation, its effects cannot be denied. Singers can use their melodies to stimulate growth or healing, convince a stubborn vine to twist in a particular direction, or calm a wilting flower. The most gifted among them can even interact with the brilliant bioluminescent fungi that thrive in the lower layers of the city, creating fleeting but dazzling patterns of light that are said to hold secret meanings.

### Creatures of Thallomere
Thallomere’s fauna is as vibrant and adaptive as its flora. Flocks of brightly feathered gliders—birdlike creatures with membranous wings resembling oversized leaves—flit from tree to tree, their plumage acting as living camouflage against the dense vegetation. Their chittering, melodic calls echo through the towering leafscape, intermingling with the gentle rustling of foliage.

Beneath the leafy canopies, the lower strata of the city are home to a blend of terrestrial creatures, many of which seem to have sprung forth directly from the mossy undergrowth. Great, slow-moving herbivores with shaggy, moss-covered backs graze on low-hanging roots while brightly colored beetles scuttle through the underbrush, clicking warning signals with iridescent shells. Predators, too, stalk Thallomere’s darkened levels: sleek, shadowy hunters with bioluminescent lures atop their heads, allowing them to draw in unwary prey amidst the perpetual twilight of the forest floor.

There are insect colonies that have built intricate hive complexes woven into the very structure of the city's trees. These skilled architects produce dazzling-colored bio-resins used by the Thallo for sealing habitats or decor. Some creatures have achieved a strange partnership with the Thallo themselves—slender, monkey-like beings often assist as helpers in the higher reaches, retrieving fruits, nuts, and, on rare occasions, wayward children.

Of particular interest are the symbiotic creatures known as "Rootleths"—lithe animals that resemble a hybrid of snakes and creeping vines. Rootleths play a vital role in the biological upkeep of Thallomere's trees, pruning dead branches and consuming harmful parasites. In turn, they receive nutrients from the trees and the protection of the community. The bond between the Rootleths and Thallo is so intimate that they are often considered spiritual kin.

### The Breath of the City
Deeper still, where light barely penetrates and the sounds of life dim to a whisper, lies the city’s pulsing heart—a vast root network that connects every living thing in Thallomere. The locals call it the Verdant Vein, but to outsiders, it might seem like the city's brain or its very soul. Slow, rhythmic pulses of energy pass along the roots, a silent hum that some claim they can feel in their bones if they sit still enough. It is through this Vein that water, nutrients, and an inexplicable sense of connection flows—a reminder of the city’s abiding unity.

This is why Thallomere’s people are not ambitious conquerors—or even explorers of the world beyond the misted boundaries of their city. For them, the cycles of Thallomere’s growth and decay, its endless symphony of leaf and root, is a microcosm of life itself. They cannot imagine a world more vast or more beautiful than the one they already live within.

This place belongs to its inhabitants, as much as they belong to it. Every life, from the smallest spore to the eldest tree, is part of the same grand cycle, spinning endlessly and chaotically toward growth.
