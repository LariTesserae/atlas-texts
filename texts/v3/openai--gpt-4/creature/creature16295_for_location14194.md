The beings that inhabit this place are neither fully natural nor entirely artificial. It is unclear whether they are the descendants of those who left the industrial bones scattered across the plateaus, or whether they emerged from the land's own peculiar essence long after the builders had vanished. Yet they move with a kind of uneasy harmony through their strange, half-reclaimed world, aligning themselves with its heartbeat while asserting their peculiar presence within it.

Their forms are unsettling, though not grotesque—merely unfamiliar. Their bodies are swathed in garments of woven moss and braided vines, their clothing blending seamlessly with their surroundings, to the point where one might mistake them for peculiar outgrowths of the forest itself until they stir and shift. Their faces are neither flat with human familiarity nor entirely alien; they bear the vestiges of human features, but are marked by qualities that defy easy description. Skin like waxen, translucent bark glows faintly with the same iridescent luster that streaks the plants. Their eyes shine as orbs of deep green and pale gold, reflecting a light not present in the dim surroundings, as though they hold the sky’s dormant sun within them.

They are tall and impossibly slender, their joints bending subtly in places that human anatomy wouldn’t allow—or perhaps too fluidly, like vines in a breeze. Their bodies suggest an eerie suppleness, as if they could fold themselves completely in half or twist into a spiral, though whether this is actually true remains shrouded in uncertainty. Their feet are bare, both humanoid and rootlike, toes that seem to flex and anchor into the soil. Occasionally, a gout of mist clings to their steps, almost like a shy pet or a fragment of vaporous memory unwilling to let go.

They are not entirely organic, you quickly realize, though not in the blunt, mechanical way you might expect. Glimpses of faintly pulsating conduits, coiling faintly through their translucent hands and wrists, hint at veins of something neither blood nor wire, something bioluminescent and alive. Rings of soft metal glimmer faintly around their arms and necks, though whether these are jewelry, grafted enhancements, or symbiotic parasites is impossible to say. Everything about them suggests a blurring, a collapsing, of boundaries: between flesh and moss, steel and bone, plant and primeval stone.

Their movements are deliberate but unhurried, almost ritualistic, as though each step is part of a rhythm only they can hear—perhaps the same strange, shifting chime you yourself perceive faintly on the air. They seem apart from the other sounds of this place, but also woven into them. You catch them tending to the vegetation with a strange reverence, trimming vines that insist on outpacing the boundaries of ancient walkways, coaxing accordion-like trees into new configurations, or grafting blooms onto the petrified frames of long-dead giants. It is as though they are shaping the forest while bending to its will, participating in the same careful balance that governs this fragmentary world.

They are not inherently hostile, but there is a perceptible reserve about them—a wariness, perhaps, of intruders, or of you yourself. They do not approach immediately, nor do they flee. When they regard you, their gaze is penetrating, as though they are peeling away your layers and cataloging what lies beneath. But whether they find you a threat, a curiosity, or merely a transitory piece of the scenery is unknowable. Communication with them is improbable yet not beyond hope; they hum to each other in tones that pitch low and high, blending with the perpetual chime, and occasionally they touch their palms to tree trunks or slabs of stone, as though seeking counsel from the atoms of the place itself.

Whatever knowledge they carry seems ancient but incomplete, fractured like the ruins they inhabit. Perhaps they are the forgotten inheritors of this world, its first children warped by their symbiosis with the land’s peculiar eras. Or perhaps they have come here from elsewhere, drawn by the lingering song of forgotten experiments, settling into the grooves left by a race long extinct. Either way, they are caretakers now, and in their care, the land transforms and endures—though into what, even they might not know.

The more closely you observe, the clearer it becomes that they are not alone. The few signs of fauna you noted earlier are far outnumbered by the hints of things that shy from your sight. Shimmers in the mist resolve into clusters of glinting eyes that blink in patterns before dissipating again into haze. Shadowy forms slip through the tall vegetation, never fully coalescing into anything you can name, accompanying the strange beings like barely-tethered familiars or shades of something forgotten yet indispensable.

And what of those unimaginable creatures glimpsed in fractured moments? Every once in a while, your senses are overtaken—by a sound that shouldn’t echo but does, by a thunderous gust of wind, or by the unmistakable feeling that something vast and ancient has passed, unseen but dreadfully present. The humans-that-aren’t blink their luminous eyes upward at these moments, though you cannot tell whether they are paying homage or offering warning.

This place is alive with paradox. The beings here embody it. Standing on the lip of this dreaming world of impossible geometry and alien flora, the question forms unbidden: Has life adapted to this place’s broken, suspended time—or has it been remade into its image? Then one of the luminous figures turns fully toward you, and for a moment you see your reflection painted faintly on the sheen of its face. There is a sense, fleeting but sharp as a blade, that you—like everything here—have always been part of its structure, one thread in time’s gnarled fabric.
