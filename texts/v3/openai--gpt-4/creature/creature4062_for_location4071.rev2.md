The beings that inhabit this place are as enigmatic and fluid as the grove itself, caught at the junction where biology and mechanics blur into one another. They are not easily seen, not easily understood, and their forms seem shaped as much by the peculiar mechanics of their environment as by necessity or evolution. They are not visitors here—they are as much a part of this strange, self-contained world as its humid air and bioluminescent water.

Among the translucent fronds and viscous vines, the most commonly sighted creatures are the *Orynda*. They are small, perhaps a foot in length, resembling a fusion of insect and crustacean. Their bodies are segmented and encased in what looks like a shifting mosaic of brittle glass and living tissue. The patterns on their crystalline carapaces ripple subtly with light, as if reacting to ambient energy flows or the pulse of the central obelisk. Their legs are jointed and slender, tipped with tiny, fractal-like pads that allow them to cling effortlessly to any surface—stone, wood, or even the shimmering pools of water. Orynda emit faint, high-pitched clicks as they move, communication or signal or song, punctuating the stillness with a curious, electronic staccato. When startled, they vanish so quickly that their forms seem to dissolve into the air, leaving behind a faint trace of their gleaming patterns floating like afterimages to the eye.

Along the tangled web of veins that connect the pools of metallic water, a different kind of life glides with sluggish grace—the *Lumes*. These creatures are ghostly, serpentine threads of gelatinous light, glowing in pale whites, greens, and reds, colors that seem to echo the hum of the mineral veins in the cliffs. They twirl and twist about themselves with hypnotic fluidity as they traverse their living waterways, their translucent bodies flickering with patterns that are almost cipher-like, as though something ancient and incomprehensible is encoded within their very being. A closer look reveals odd regularity within the pulsing lights that travel their length, as though their forms are somehow modulating the energy flowing through the grove itself.

Occasionally—rare, fleeting, and yet unmistakable—a different sort of presence reveals itself along the edges of the grove. Hidden in the mists that rise and fall like sighs from the sponge-like ground, the *Veyrn* are tall, spectral beings with elongated limbs and shifting features. Their silhouettes seem to flicker between states of corporeality, never fully solid yet undeniably there. They wear the grove as a shroud, their forms often blending with the humidity and breathing clouds as if they are born of it, returning to it as easily as shadows fade under sunlight. Their "eyes" are faintly luminous orbs, suspended in an amorphous haze of light and shape, often trained on the obelisk in what can only be described as reverence. When they move, the air seems to part for them, the vegetation swaying in their wake as though adjusted by invisible hands.

It is unclear if the Veyrn are the creators of the obelisk or its custodians—or even if such distinctions hold meaning here. They move with grace but also an unsettling deliberateness, as though their every action ripples through dimensions not visible to the human eye. It is said they sing, though their song exists on frequencies more felt than heard: a hum that resonates through the bones and echoes within the obelisk, as though the two were woven of the same essence. If approached, they do not flee, though they will observe in turn, their luminous "eyes" tilted as if calculating something inscrutable.

Beneath the surface, deep in the pliable ground, there are whispers of something else—immense and silent, dormant yet subtly active. During moments of stillness, when the grove holds its breath and the ambient hum of life falls away, there are faint tremors, rhythmic and deliberate, as though some vast being lies watching from within the earth's cradle. Sections of the sponge-like terrain will swell subtly, altering imperceptibly over hours or days, veins of water redirecting themselves as if responding to the will of something unseen. The pools occasionally reflect irregular shadows that have no source, movements that ripple across their surfaces and vanish before a second glance confirms them.

The obelisk itself cannot be divorced entirely from this ecosystem—it might even be the keystone that holds it all together. Small, insect-like drones with no discernible origin occasionally emerge from its hair-thin strands of circuitry. These "artifacts," for lack of a better word, flit through the grove with whispery precision, collecting residue from the sap-dripping vines or samples of the Lumes' gel-like trails. Their flight paths form geometric patterns that seem to mirror the grooves of the obelisk’s surface, tracing out runes or glyphs only significant to something far beyond human understanding. Once their task is complete, they return to the obelisk and dissolve into its surface like droplets into water.

And the obelisk itself could be said to live—or something close to it. Its emanations are not random but responsive, its low-frequency pulses shifting subtly when the grove's inhabitants move or when an outsider—like yourself—steps into the space. At certain times of day, when the ambient light dims, it flares faintly like embers reigniting in a vacuum, and its connections to the mineral veins deepen, causing the cliffs themselves to light up like a silent thunderstorm frozen mid-flash. Its presence feels parental, as though it is not only the guardian of this place but also its progenitor, or perhaps its true inhabitant. Everything else—the Orynda, the Lumes, even the Veyrn—might simply be extensions, or reflections, of its unknowable design.

To linger here is to feel yourself drawn into its labyrinthine patterns, to feel watched, noted, absorbed, and ultimately judged. It is not a hostile sensation, nor entirely benign; it is the infinite attention of a world that is both young and ancient, alive and machine, teetering on the edge of comprehension. This is no ordinary place—it is a node in some vast system, forgotten or perhaps never known, breathing its strange life in rhythm with the obelisk's pulse, waiting for something you cannot name but can only feel in your bones.

And as you stand there, surrounded by beings too extraordinary to fully grasp, every inch of your instinct whispers of your smallness, your briefness. Whatever has made this place—and whatever lives here now—operates on the edges of a logic that exists just out of reach, pulling at you softly but insistently, echoing in every corner of this intimate infinity.
