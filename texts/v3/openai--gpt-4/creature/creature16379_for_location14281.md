This is where the Erratics dwell, if "dwell" is a word we can use for them. Perhaps it is better to say they *persist* here, folded into the hazy membrane between presence and absence, between the seen and the half-remembered. To call them a people would be as much a concession to familiarity as it would a misstep. They are not human. They are not creatures you would name in the naturalist's lexicon. Glimpsed head-on, they are barely shapes at all, more a suspicion of contours: mounds of overlapping ridges draped in layers of matte-finished gray, like the hide of a stone trying to become flesh—or perhaps flesh trying to forget it was ever something so tactile at all.

They move slowly, but not from laziness or weariness. It’s a deliberate slowness, a drifting inevitability, as though time bends differently around them. From the corners of your vision, you might track their progress with something like fascination, only to second-guess whether they moved at all.
