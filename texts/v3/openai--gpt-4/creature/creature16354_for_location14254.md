The beings who dwell in this place are not to be categorized easily. They skitter at the edge of perception, their presence half tangible, half suggestion, as if the world is shyly reluctant to fully reveal them. They move not in defiance of their environment but as intrinsic threads woven into its tapestry, sharing its rhythms, its quiet insistence on flourishing slowly and profoundly.

The most prevalent and ever-present are the *Tendril-Weavers*. These creatures are less something you observe and more something you sense—soft undulations rippling through the architectural plant-bodies around you, faint disturbances in the golden mist. They are translucent and fleshy, resembling colossal, semi-solid strands of liquid amber, their forms fluctuating between tendrils, webbing, and firm-limbed structures depending on their task. What task that is remains enigmatic, but their movements suggest purpose, flowing over surfaces to smooth, fortify, and reshape the fibrous world. Perhaps they are custodians, or perhaps they are the engineers themselves, coaxing life to bend and grow into order. They leave behind streaks of silken biofilm that harden into lattice-like structures, which shimmer faintly as if charged with latent intention.

Even rarer are the *Pulse-Wrights,* creatures that resemble dense, coralline orbs mottled with bioluminescent patterns that echo the steady rhythm of the deeper world's vibrations. They hang suspended, not in threads but in midair, floating as if held aloft by the mist itself. Their "pulses" cycle in slow waves, causing the ambient thrumming to fluctuate subtly wherever they drift. These changes alter the behavior of water flows, redirects sprouting growths, and even trigger the exhalation of steaming wells or glimmering waterfalls. Are they adjusting the system or in communion with it? Perhaps both. Their glow is not a light meant to illuminate, but a language, searing patterns of thought into the ever-shifting atmosphere.

There are the *Watcher-Coils*, serpentine beings that shimmer with metallic iridescence, as if their skin shifts from fluid mercury to obsidian and back again. Their purpose is harder to intuit. You might catch glimpses of them lying in still loops within alcoves of plant-flesh, resembling sleeping sigils more than animals, but their abrupt movements—so very alive—reveal otherwise. On occasion, one will rise and slither along the built arteries of the place, wrapping and unwrapping itself with surgical precision around vertical pillars or rib-like arches as if measuring the place with its body. Whether they are engineers, guardians, scouts, or silent wardens, you cannot tell, but their sudden, fluid absence when they vanish makes you acutely aware of the space they leave behind, as though it were emptied of some principle you didn't realize was there.
