Beneath the silvery luminescence of this subterranean metropolis dwells a peculiar people, the "Orryn", as they might once have been called by those who left records aboveground. Though the term humankind struggles to fit them now, there are hints in their bone structure—elongated skulls, delicate shoulders, and long, finger-like hands—that suggest a distant lineage tied to the surface world, albeit centuries removed. Evolution, or perhaps necessity, has sculpted them into something otherworldly.

They move in near silence, their bodies shimmering faintly as if dusted with the light of their stone city. Their skin is translucent and fragile, revealing faint rivers of blue veins running beneath its surface, like tributaries echoing the endless streams of dripping water around them. Pale and hairless, with opalescent eyes that reflect the faintest hint of bioluminescence, they are adapted to the eternal twilight of their home. Their ears, tapering into delicate points, twitch and turn like finely tuned instruments to catch every drop's echo, every footfall of a stranger entering their forgotten domain.

Through observation, one might notice that they do not speak aloud but rather seem to communicate via a kind of shared resonance. If you were still, utterly still, in their presence, you might feel it—a shivering hum that thrums not in your ears but in your teeth, your bones. This is how they share their thoughts, their stories, their warnings. To them, words are clumsy, inefficient, an outsider's tool. Their hums are richer, laden with depth and emotion, like an orchestra performing the diary of their lives.

Their homes, though primitive by surface standards, reveal layers of ingenuity. Worn tools speak of purpose-driven hands—chisels of brittle stone, hammerlike implements nearly turned to dust, and even delicate, comb-like devices used to etch their swirling, almost hypnotic carvings onto the walls of their structures. These carvings are no mere decoration; they are history encoded in stone, charts of stars unseen for generations and stories of the world that existed above before they sank into obscurity. Each line, curve, and dot holds layers of meaning lost to the surface's perception.

The shallow pools scattered throughout the city—glasslike, still surfaces that mirror their luminescence—hold sacred importance. The Orryn often sit kneeling at their edges, quietly communing with the water’s surface. Scholars might hypothesize these pools act as mirrors for self-reflection, or even portals for communicating across time. Watch long enough, and you might catch a trick of liquid that is more than mere movement—a ripple forming without cause, an image emerging as if the water had something to say of its own, an echo of lives once touched by the pools' ancient serenity. Could it be memory, preserved in droplets? Or does the water itself listen?

Despite their stoic existences, they are not solitary nor entirely at peace. The occasional rustle in the labyrinth or the subtle glint of reflection in the shadows may reveal companions who lurk lower in the city's hierarchies. Here live the creatures that share this space with the Orryn, yet are distinct from them. Spindly, many-limbed beings, crawling like whispers across the crumbling stone. They are albino nightmares, blind to this world of half-light but aware of its textures, its currents, its vibrations. These creatures, which the Orryn tolerate but do not consider equal, form an uneasy ecosystem where predator and prey roles blur in this world of infinite dampness.

For sustenance, the Orryn depend on an intricate bond with the fungus and lichen that stubbornly cling to these stony walls. They farm it in carefully tended niches, scraping the muted greenery into hand-carved vessels of stone that never leave their side. It is not only food but ritual, a meditative act tied deeply to survival. They do not hunt, for the idea of taking life feels alien to their philosophy; theirs is a quiet endurance, a delicate coexistence with the lifeless and the barely alive.

Yet they are not oblivious to the outside world. Scattered throughout the city are peculiar nodes—archways, columns, and basins—etched with brighter carvings and oriented in ways that suggest not mere construction, but function. Vibrations pass through these structures and reach their senses like songs played on an alien harp. It is rumored that these devices are their connection to the surface world, their way of listening, not for rescue but for memory. And sometimes, very softly, the hums leave their city by way of these columns, finding their way to places far above, where the whispers carry through cracks in the earth, surfacing in the dreams of wanderers who wake with nothing but odd, inexplicable longing.

What do the Orryn wait for? In their luminous eyes, there is a patience that feels eternal. Do they remember the ones who built this place and let it fall? Or are they something else entirely—caretakers of forgotten wisdom, survivors of a cataclysm, or simply a people to whom time no longer holds meaning? They move, they hum, their eyes catch and distort the light, and they endure. So whatever they are waiting for—whether it is some signal, or simply eventual dissolution—they are rooted in this place, as much a part of its damp silence as the water that drips without end.

And rare—so rare—is the intruder from above who is met with anything but distant curiosity. But there is always the hum. The gentle hum. And it is always watching. Always listening.
