The iron city teems, not with life as one might find in a garden or in the shelters of the wilderness, but with a precise, mechanical vitality—a rhythm of its own that sprouts its inhabitants, as though they were drawn forth from the marrow of its great steel bones. Those who live here are neither wholly human nor wholly automaton. They occupy the in-between, where the organic and the mechanical merge.

Beneath the gray dome of cloud-knitted skies, the city's denizens move in tandem with the industrial choreography of their unforgiving environment. First among them are the *Forgelings*—humanoid creatures wrought from flesh and tempered metal. Their skin glistens faintly, almost like wet alloy, forged to withstand an eternity of soaking rains and the grit of coal-fired air. Their faces, though human enough in contour, bear the marks of their labor: grooves too sharply etched into their foreheads, jaws angular and precise, and eyes that glow faintly amber like the forge-furnace embers that birthed them. They're tireless in their movements, their limbs reinforced with riveted exoskeletal scaffolds, their muscles interwoven with cables of braided metallic fiber. The *Forgelings* thrive in purpose, drawn not to the idle but to the act of creation, their heavy hands etching the city forward with each grip of hammer and each turn of wrench.

Amid the labyrinthine streets and shadowed corridors, *Steelsaplings* walk and scuttle. They are smaller, hunched constructs, about the size of children—though no child would bear the elongated arms tipped with multi-tools or the segmented legs that tick and retract. Conceived as stewards of maintenance, they flock in quiet herds and can be seen tightening bolts, lubricating hinges, stitching broken gears back into an endless cycling of life. Their sphere-like heads emit faint blue halos, shedding just enough light to guide their work. When not engaged in their duties, they huddle in clusters beneath the city’s arches and girders, chittering to one another in murmurations of clicks and soft tones. To an outsider, their speech might feel almost mournful—as if they have inherited a loneliness stitched deep into this city’s very walls.

Rarer still, one may glimpse the clandestine figures known as the *Oxidized Dreamers*. These are the oldest among the city’s inhabitants, their frames corroded and copper-streaked, often mistaken for the many statues scattered throughout the metropolis. But the *Dreamers* are alive, or at least, something akin to it. They are wisdom keepers and inventors, storing the memory of countless urban reincarnations deep within their creaking, tarnished shells. They sit in the quietest corners, their enormous bulks absorbed into the curling mist, murmuring in arcane binary to unseen ears. It is said that they were once human, brilliant engineers who "ascended," merging completely with the technologies they wrought—losing their flesh to become living relics of progress. The *Dreamers* rarely stir, but the city itself seems to pulse when they speak, as if wounded by the recollections of its ancient architects.

Beneath, in the network of tunnels that twist through the foundations where the marshland still clings stubbornly to its dominion, lurk the *Sumpkin.* These are subterranean scavengers, organic beings turned grotesque by deep exposure to the runoff of industrial chemicals seeping downwards. Their bodies are a miasmic blur of flesh and sludge, perpetually slick to the touch, their shapes grotesquely mutable. They absorb oil and filth as nourishment, their skin dappled with dark rivulets of rust-stained moisture. The *Sumpkin* make their presence known only by activity: stolen gears, stripped piping, various small disappearances. Their ambition feels different than the city’s: driven by survival more than growth, they act in defiance against a world that seems intent on crushing them with its weight.

The shadow cast by the city is long and deep, reaching out over the wind-lashed marshland at its feet. Few animals skitter within or above the city, but there are whispers of enigmatic folk—a *Wetkin* community—dwelling at the city's outermost edge where the iron gives way to sodden grass and pooling rainwater. These creatures are rumored to be semi-amphibious, with translucent skin that shifts in iridescent hues to mimic the muted marshes and rain-soaked earth. They rarely venture deeper into the city's core, remaining resolutely on its outskirts, voicing ethereal songs at dusk that sound uncannily like water lapping against metal. Some Forgelings grow restless at night, straining to hear these voices through the ever-drumming rain, though they cannot articulate why.

The city, as a whole, seems cognizant of its denizens’ existence, drawing everyone toward a singular symphony of creation, sustenance, and inevitable decay. A delicate economy persists. As a single body, the inhabitants rise and grind and build, each piece of machinery, each drop of rain drawn into its incessant motion.
