Inhabiting the living city of Cannis-Sylvarum is a unique society, as blended with nature and technology as their environment. These inhabitants are neither true plants nor conventional machines. Instead, they are a symbiotic culture of bioengineered entities, seamlessly amalgamating organic structure with synthetic enhancements. They call themselves the Vireons, a nod to their origins that merge 'vireo' (a plant-based world) with 'eon' (an eternal era).

Like the city-plant, they're sculpted by an alien architecture. Their forms are as varied as the foliage surrounding them, many taking on humanoid forms with skin that mirrors the leathery texture of the native plants. Hardened and altered by evolution and artificial design, their bodies are resistant to the harsh climate of their world. They are a resilient and rugged folk, at home amidst their techno-organic metropolis.

Not just their physical bodies, even the consciousness of Vireons seem to blur the line between organic and artificial. Many of them possess an organic brain, while others are fully sentient AI, housed in a body that nourishes from roots burrowed deep into the soil. These dualities harmoniously co-exist within their populace, no Vireon superior to another. 

Some are born from spores, others assembled in the factories nestled in the heart of massive trees. Like the glowing bioluminescent patterns on tree trunks, these beings also harbor a network of glowing circuits, showcasing them as creatures of light in this blend of a world. Their blood is a mix of chlorophyll and viscous mechanic oils, and they literally have electricity pulsing through their botanical veins. 

The Vireons move gracefully among the tangled vines and towering trunks, as if attuned inextricably to the rhythm of Cannis-Sylvarum. Their voices resemble wind swirling through gaps between the leaves, and it fittingly matches their language, an aural art form resembling rustling leaves and whistling winds, interspersed with the whirrs, clicks, and static of machine language. 

Quiet yet sublime is their society; tech-filled yet plant-like, where symbiosis is not merely biological but cultural. They work harmoniously, managing the city's bio-industries, maintaining the factories in tree trunks, and overseeing the growth of gardens in the constructed domes. The Vireons have pushed the boundaries of existence and given life an innovative meaning, one drawn equally from nature and technology.

In the shadow of twilight, under the glow of industrial luminosity, the denizens of Cannis-Syvalrum dance along the edge of the known and unknown, the natural and the manufactured. The harmony of their existence, their willingness to embrace both ends of the spectrum, resonates throughout the city as it pulses with an energy of its own. It is a testament to life's innate ability to adapt, to evolve, and to intertwine with its surroundings, no matter how foreign or inhospitable it may seem.
