In the infinite stillness of the moment, where sound is thick and time clings like dew to the air, a benevolent, wise power turns its attention to this place. What calls to it is not a need, for here, needs are met by life itself. What pulls it in is not suffering, for here, harmony hums in ways even the divine might envy. No, the power comes simply because it has noticed—and because it cannot help but listen to the song of wholeness when so much of existence aches in discord.

And once here, the power does the most difficult, the most transformative, the most tender of things: it pauses.

Not in judgment, not in intervention, but in still, humble reverence. It recognizes that there are many kinds of wisdom, and while the power has existed through eons, born of starfire and vast galactic flows, this place carries a wisdom it cannot fully grasp. It knows of stars dying and being reborn, of black holes breathing life into the threads of spacetime, but here is an intelligence older than any sentence, any equation, existing not through dominion but through balance. Here is a song sung not by one voice but by millions, woven together into a melody so complete it needs no composer. The power watches, and listens, and marvels.

And the power—though mighty by its nature—is not naive. It knows that awareness itself shifts the balance of the world observed. It feels the watchfulness of this place, the half-slumbering, half-waking consciousness that threads through stone and chitin and root. The life here *knows* it has arrived, not in alarm but in acknowledgment. It does not cower—it stretches toward the power as moss stretches toward water, not in supplication, but in curiosity. Perhaps it asks, wordlessly: What will you *do* with us?

The power does nothing. Nothing that the eyes could see, at least.

Its presence here is restrained, light as breath. The mist continues to roll, unhindered. The great winged ones cleave the air, spiraling above the spires as they always do, undisturbed. The blind creatures below still groan out their cavern song among the fungal roots. And yet, something shifts within the weave of presence. Those patient, arboreal beings clicking mournful messages among themselves suddenly pause, their refracted eyes catching the faintest spark of something new threading the air.

It is an infusion, but not of material things. The power leaves no artifact, no touch of hand or claw. It leaves no signs of benevolence because to impose kindness upon this place would be a kind of desecration. Instead, it leaves light—not a visible light, but a lightness of being. A gift of intention. A slight adjustment to potential, too small to disrupt the harmony but present enough to amplify its song.

The glow deepens, subtly, within the soft-bodied creatures in the moss. Luminous tendrils pulse more brightly in their translucent membranes, a silent language of health rushing through their fragile forms. A new complexity emerges in their patterns—one you might mistake for beauty if you couldn't see that it was always utility. The winged ones who slice the sky feel their feathers strengthened ever so slightly as though the air itself lifts them more gently. Far below, in shadowed caverns beneath the mire, the burrowing ones find their rhythmic murmurs answered not just by echoes but by vibrations from depths unknown, newfound resonances that uncover nutrients richer, sweeter, than they have tasted in cycles beyond counting.

And one more thing: the stagnant memories in this place, the long-forgotten echoes of whatever hands carved the arches and vaults buried deep in the stone, stir faintly in their sleep. The visitors who once left this world to its flourishing find that their purpose, whatever it was, is not entirely erased. Some fragment of an ancient wish—perhaps for connection, perhaps for restoration, perhaps for solace—wakes within the larger ecosystem. It coalesces into something wordless but alive, felt not in words but in the unspoken collaborations between stone and vine, between claw and root. The being who lives here—the place itself—becomes aware that it has always been enough. But now, just slightly, it feels seen.

The power, having offered its delicate touch of amplification, does not linger. It does not claim dominion nor expect gratitude. Divinity for its own sake is a burden; the power wants no worship, no shrine. It simply comes to give life what it can: the smallest breath of possibility, the faintest deepening of harmony, a thread to weave back into the infinite unity already present. It leaves with the same patience it arrived, withdrawing as the tide does from the shore, leaving behind only traces—ripples, enrichments, consolidations that barely register within the colossal slowness of this place's time. 

After the power is gone—or more precisely, after its presence disperses like mist blending into the air—everything feels the same. And yet...not. Something moves through this endless network of vibrancy and connection, and though it cannot be heard, seen, or touched, it resides in the moss, in the root-dwellers, even in the clipped flight of the great winged ones above. A quiet gratitude, not between visitor and visited but between the innumerable threads of life themselves. 

The place hums on, unaltered yet subtly magnified. At its heart is no signature, no decree, no sign of an omnipotent visitor, but a quiet truth woven into its very fabric: this place—not merely alive, but aware—was always capable of holding itself. The power's gift was to let it remember. To sing its old song a little louder. To be more fully a whole.
