Night spills over the Weeping Plains like an obsidian veil, dappled with silver from a moon that peers curiously between clouds. The mist dances, the rivulets hum, and the old ruins—silent witnesses to millennia of mystery—seem to shift with the shadows. It is in this moment of soft, surreal stillness that the Wise Power arrives.

It does not arrive as a storm, nor a comet from the heavens, nor a blinding light. It arrives as a feeling—an almost imperceptible stirring in the air, a presence that seems to draw every element into quieter focus. The reeds cease their rustling; the rivulets still their bubbling; even the Harperwings, guardians of the sky, falter mid-flight and alight silently upon the ruins to observe.

The Wise Power is not a being, not entirely. It is neither god nor spirit, neither omnipotent nor mortal. It is a convergence—a mosaic of compassion, wisdom, and intention, drawn like a magnetic force by the Weeping Plains’ strange harmony and its simultaneous ache. For the plains, in all their beauty, are marked by a wound written in every whispered droplet, every lichen-covered ruin. This is a place of memory, of loss as much as of life, and the Wise Power has come to listen.

To the Amphijacks skimming the mirrored waters, it appears as ripples that glow faintly under moonlight. They pause in their darting, instinctively drawn to the presence that feels more ancient than even the stones beneath them. The Wise Power speaks to them, not in words, but in the silent language of empathy. It sees the Amphijacks' perpetual vulnerability, their constant dance for survival, and hums a resonance of reassurance. It deepens the glow of their iridescent skin, a new radiance to signal to one another in the deepest, murkiest waters.

For the Nutricrawlers, the presence manifests as a barely perceptible warmth against the cool stones they traverse, a subtle easing of their endless labor. The Wise Power touches the rhythm of their existence, slows it just enough to grant them the smallest luxuries: shorter paths between nourishment and repose, shelter made gentler by mosses that grow just slightly thicker beneath their feet. They, too, are woven into the fabric of balance.

The Ghoststones freeze, their eerie, pale forms still as marble on the ruins they traverse so gracefully by night. The Wise Power knows them intimately—their nimble hands, their clever eyes, their quiet mourning of something even they cannot name. It breathes into their melodies, expanding their chirps into more layered, resonant chords. Where before they sang to one another in whispers, now their voices stitch through the twilight, flickering like faintly lit constellations that guide the lost.

Above, the Harperwings spread their shimmering wings, holding still as the Wise Power brushes against their nature. These creatures of beauty, of deadly grace, of ethereal song, feel the presence less as a touch and more as a mirror. The Wise Power does not change them; it sees them as they are—predators cruel and breathtaking, givers and takers in equal measure. Instead of transformation, it enfolds them with understanding, affirming their place at the apex of a delicate, glittering web.

And then there is mankind.

The Wise Power turns its attention to the descendants of ancient builders, those peculiar custodians of the marsh-edge dominion, the keepers of memory and quiet reverence. It sees their weathered faces, their hands thick with the work of coexistence, their eyes lined with the quiet wisdom of many generations spent in communion with this land. It searches their hearts and senses their unease—the knowledge, buried deep within their songs and stories, that this place holds something they have forgotten. Something vast. Something painful.

The presence of the Wise Power brushes their dreams that night. It conjures visions of the past: the mountains birthing this marsh-edge land with ferocious rains; the first tentative steps of human feet upon its trembling earth; the first whispered bargains with its creatures. It reminds them of the laughter that once rang out among the ruins, of the prayers carved into stone, and of the calamity—the loss, sudden, like the breaking of a dam—that silenced it all. But even in that destruction, the Wise Power shows the endurance: the first stalks of reed returning, the first Nutricrawler inching through moonlit water again.

In the dream, one among them—a child, unmarked by the burden of knowing—becomes the vessel of the Wise Power’s message. The child awakens at dawn with whispers in their soul, and they rush to the marsh-edge basin. Before the adults can follow, the clouds withdraw slightly, and the first rays of sunlight in generations strike the plains. The stone ruins gleam gold for a single, fleeting moment. The Amphijacks glitter in the canals like rivers wrought of stars. The Nutricrawlers pause, their shells radiant. The Ghoststones cry out joyous chords that ripple through the reeds, and the Harperwings take to the skies in an arc that mirrors the sun’s ascent.

The child stands at the center of it all, facing the beautiful, tragic basin. In their small hands is a fragment of ancient stone, broken long ago—the missing piece of one of the ruins. The Wise Power has given no words, only this: the fragment, the dream, and the knowledge not to repair, but to complete the story.

From that day onward, the people of the Weeping Plains remember. They remember not just what they lost, but what they preserved. They build no new monuments, but neither do they let the old ones crumble. The canals grow clearer, their flow swifter. The Nutricrawlers thrive. The Amphijacks shimmer. The Ghoststones sing fuller, deeper songs. The Harperwings soar higher.

And the Wise Power—its work complete—dissipates into the realm of possibility once more, leaving behind only the faint impression of warmth where coldness once reigned. The Plains continue their quiet weeping, yes, but it is not only sorrow they weep for now. It is the gift of life itself: strange and beautiful, fragile and enduring, ever-changing, ever-connected—an eternal, haunting symphony.
