When the attention of the wise and benevolent power fell upon this place—a land suspended between ruin and renewal, where moss cradles rust and the past hugs the present with gentle hands—it did not arrive with trumpet blasts or blinding flashes of revelation. It came softly, as if spiraling in with the mist, its presence felt through subtle shifts in the air and light, a hum that resonated deep within the bones of this strange and patient world.

The power did not wish to destroy, nor to reshape. It had not come to impose its will upon the residents of this place. What it sought, instead, was to listen, to understand the rhythm of life here, and to act, only if such an action were needed. There was no arrogance in its wisdom, only a profound sense of curiosity and the quiet conviction that every place, every being, deserved to be seen, understood, and cherished.

It began by observing.

It watched the **moss-dwellers** carry out their slow, deliberate work, their stubby fingers pressing moss into cracks, their silent presence a balm to the brokenness they touched. The power marveled at the way they lived in service of reclamation, of care, of weaving life into decay without ever pausing to claim dominion over it. It saw how they shaped the land—not with violence or urgency, but with reverence for the unhurried flow of time. The power knelt among them unseen and whispered a small blessing into their mossy coats, a soft encouragement for their ancient work.

In the air above, the **hollow birds** moved with uncanny grace, their rusted forms creaking faint hymns carried by the wind. The power listened to the intelligible meaning within those rattles and hums—the symphony of decay becoming life, of loss finding beauty in its aftershocks. With a quiet touch, it extended the vitality of their fungal hearts, ensuring that their strange blooms would never fade prematurely, that the connection they wove through the marsh would hold firm in the face of entropy.

The **echo-hands** puzzled the power for a time. These non-corporeal murmurs of memory whispered in the language of longing, of purpose once fulfilled and now bereft. It saw in their ghostly motions a glimmer of hopefulness, an unforgotten yearning to be more than relics of past labor. The power lingered in the rusted factory, moving through the mists of their spectral form. It did not dictate an answer but instead left within the pipes and gears a faint memory of warmth, the deep resonance of steam and vibration as it once had been. The promise of perhaps—of some distant moment when the echo-hands might rediscover what gave them joy.

The **root-knitters**—those tall, arboreal silhouettes bending over their weaving like penitent monks—drew the power’s particular respect. It saw not only what they mended, but what they intentionally left broken. Cracks in the land that water needed to seep through, cavities where air and light nourished unseen microbes—these were choices, the power realized, decisions made in accordance with the will of the land rather than its own. From these beings, the power took a lesson, restraining its omnipotence and simply breathing with the slow, deliberate rhythm of their work. Yet it offered one small boon—the gentle lifting of fatigue from their bark-haunted limbs, so their purposeful tasks would carry less burden, and their instinctive artistry would persist for as long as the marsh itself.
