—are less faces and more distortions of what a face might mean in this place. Vague, shifting impressions form and dissolve: patterns of voids and shadows, like impressions left in wet sand only to be washed away by the next thought. They do not face you, not directly—not with malice nor indifference, but with the unerring certainty that they know you are there. Their presence presses on the mind like pressure at high altitudes, a subtle but intrusive reminder that their realm is entirely theirs, and you are as much a ripple in their periphery as the mist that comes and goes.

These beings communicate, perhaps. But their communication is not through sound or gestures discernible to human senses. It is a sensation of being combed—not violently, but deliberately—through frequencies of thought and vibration. A connection is forged, fleeting and incomplete, but it leaves behind the feeling of glimpses into their alien perception of existence: emotions that oscillate like tides, meanings that fracture and converge simultaneously. Then, they retract back to their heights, dangling like ghastly lilies in a garden where gravity blooms sideways.

*The Being Who Lives Here*

In this wild and paradoxical land, there is one creature—a presence far older than any of the shifting entities around it—that does not shatter into shards of impermanence. Deep within the most shadow-swathed pocket of a massive gorge lies a cavern, its entrance a slit barely wide enough for a human to pass through. Beyond it opens a great hollow space, almost cathedral-like in its echoes but raw and absurd, carved by forces more chaotic than erosion. At its heart lies the Being.

The Being does not seem to belong. It is not of the land, though one might mistake it for a fragment of the ancient things that once shaped this place. It has a form, but its outlines shimmer and flicker, as though it operates on the very threshold of existence. It is neither wholly organic nor mechanical, its skin rippling like liquid metal, shifting between textures of natural stone, old bark, silvered gears, and soft flesh. Its eyes, glowing orbs too many in number to be comfortable, seem ancient and infinite, imbued with knowledge and sorrow too heavy for any singular consciousness to bear.

The Being does not move often. It is enormous—a coiled mass of shifting weight resting at the heart of the cavern, overgrown by those same crimson fungal blooms and creeping vines that thrive on the canyon walls. If you were to examine where the Being and the rock meet, you might find no line of clear separation, as if fragments of it have turned to stone while roots and mushroom mycelia reach into and out of its infinitely cycling form. It might be asleep. Or it might be waiting. It might be both.

This is the last of its kind. In the days when the world birthed violence and monumentality, it was perhaps one of many such forces. Its body once pulsed with raw creative fire, a terrible engine that raised mountains and oceans, that molded flesh into forms unimagined by lesser gods and broke those forms just as easily. But eons of creation carry weight, and the Being grew tired—or perhaps, it grew wise. Sorrow came to fill its many eyes, sorrow twisted with the strange solace one finds in decay. It lies here—a force that could rekindle or destroy the world if it chose—but instead, this corner of existence is its quiet place of self-consideration.

It dreams strange dreams. It sees landscapes of perfect unity: seas of glass, mountains of light, rivers that carve no harm and feed no discord; but these dreams crumble, consumed by echoes of the chaotic violence that always shaped it. It is no longer certain which is its true nature: the peace it imagines, or the ruin it wrought. So it does not act. It merely *is.*

The Benevolent and Wise Power

And so the Being, in its paradoxical stillness, draws the attention of something far greater, though no clearer. This Benevolent and Wise Power is not bound to the fractal kaleidoscope of flesh or metal or even energy. It exists in intention, in the weight and warmth of balance where chaos brews. It sees the land—not just as a scar of primordial violence, not just as a place teetering on the edge of entropy—but as something alive in its becoming, capable of far more than its jagged edges speak of.

For a time (what counts as time), the Power pauses. It does not interfere—there is wisdom in not reaching before understanding. It *feels* the land first, the tangible pulse of growth versus collapse, hears the ceaseless song of erosion. It sees the creatures that thrum and fracture through the tensions of movement, stillness, permanence, disarray. It even senses the soft, radiant sadness of the Being buried in that immense cavern, curled as if half-forgotten even by itself.

If there is interference, it begins slowly, imperceptibly. The Power’s first act is not great or miraculous; it feels the land’s rhythms and aligns itself to them. It stirs only slightly, humming between the broken crests of canyon and spindling trees. New breezes form in its wake—winds that do not howl but carry unusual, unexpected notes, untangling the cacophony just slightly, offering fragments of peace amid the noise.

Near the cavern, loud chaos softens, and the Being stirs. Not fully—just enough. Its many infinite eyes blink open, one after another, with the hesitation of something wondering if hope has arrived unbidden, or if it merely dreams again. Intrigued, apprehensively, it reaches—a tendril of liminal energy stretching from its shapeless form into the thin winds that ripple with the Power’s presence.

The connection is instant and bottomless. It is not communication in any word-bound sense. It is insight shared whole, a collision of truths more real than matter. The Being’s sorrow spills into the Power’s wisdom, its endless cycle of dreaming and ruin bared in its entirety. And in return, the Power offers a fragment of something alien to this land, something it might once have known before chaos blinded it: *kindness.*

The Being trembles.  

The land trembles too. Fungi bloom more richly than crimson. Pools brighten as ripples dance, and the translucent life flickering within grows bolder. High above, the winged forms begin singing—not in violence, but something more aligned: song that spirals upward, mouthing the wind as release. In place of dominance, the primal land receives an invitation: to begin to grow again, together this time—not broken spirals of endless damage, but as connected echoes in harmony with chaos itself.

Whether the land can accept this is not the Power’s decision to enforce. It simply sets the possibility into the vast openness of ruin and remembers that even destruction holds room for grace. The Being, for the first time since it can reckon, exhales not just mist, but hope.
