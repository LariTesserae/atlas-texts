The benevolent and wise power who turns its attention to this unfinished place would not come as a conqueror, a sculptor, or even as a benign gardener seeking to guide it into completion. Instead, this power would come as a listener, an observer, a witness to the tides of creation. It would approach not with plans to fix, but with patience to understand. For it knows that this primordial world, teeming with dynamics and unresolved becoming, is not broken—it is alive in a way that perhaps no other place in the universe has ever been.

In its great wisdom, this power understands that intervention without understanding carries the risk of collapse, stunting what might one day bloom into incomprehensible beauty or significance. And so, like a calm presence in the chaos, it settles on the edge of this world—not to alter or interrupt, but to hold space for the grand experiment to unfold. It comes in the gentlest possible form, so as not to impose. Whether it takes a shape at all depends on the world itself. Perhaps it manifests as a faintly glowing warmth in the air, a soft, resonant hum that harmonizes subtly with the orchestral sounds of shifting water and cracking stone. Perhaps it is nothing but awareness, invisible and incorporeal, a presence that tugs lightly at the world's own nascent consciousness.

And yet, this power does not remain entirely passive. Though it does not carve, it does not shape, it does not impose—it reflects. It mirrors back to the world its own restless, unformed beauty. This reflection is subtle, a gift of awareness rather than a forceful redefinition. Where the liquid ribbons of air swirl, it offers unresisting pathways for their flow. Where the embryonic spires of mineral struggle to hold form, it radiates silent encouragement, stabilizing them not through force but through the quiet assurance that they are seen, they are held, they are valid.

To the beings, subtle and fleeting as waves, the power offers a kind of low, vibrating resonance—a communication that is neither spoken nor telepathic but born of pure feeling. It “speaks” in ways that mimic their own strange flashes of impression but carries the weight of something deeper, more ancient. Its presence embodies the idea that they are not mistakes, nor are they failed attempts. They are perfect as they are, even in their imperfection. It conveys to them one truth: they are free to explore, free to learn from their endless birthing and rebirthing. No single moment in their existence will be wrong. They are integral to the world's dreaming, and the dream will become what it must in time.

In its reflection, this power fosters faint awareness within the world itself, encouraging it to explore its chaotic possibilities more boldly. It is not a sculptor, but a gardener of realization. When the layers of water and steam begin to shift too wildly, unsure of where to flow, this power floats gently into the rift and helps align the jagged currents without disrupting their nature. When fragments of crystalline structures collapse on themselves, it holds their potential for a little longer, allowing the debris to reform—should they choose to.

To the rare, towering beings who endure longer than others, embodying the possibility of permanence in a realm of ceaseless change, the power grants something extra: memory. Where once these monumental beings would dissipate entirely, leaving no trace but the quiet hum of their fleeting resonance, now echoes of their presence remain subtly woven into the world's fabric. The tremors of their ascent, the shimmer of their light, the faint heat of their presence—all of it lingers, like threads sown through the tapestry of becoming. The world carries these impressions forward, and in doing so begins to learn of itself.

But for all its power, this presence does not stay forever. It knows that its role is transient, like a midwife aiding the birth of something far greater. Once the world begins to wrap its embryonic self in a fragile sense of identity, the power recedes like the evening tide, leaving only the faintest trace of its presence. Its gift is this: balance, a stabilizing hand that never grasps, the seed of self-awareness growing not just in the beings but in the world itself.

As it departs, the power does not seal anything shut or lock the place into a predetermined future. Change will continue, and it will remain messy, restless, beautiful, and strange. That is the nature of this world—to remain liminal, alive in its incompleteness, an eternal canvas on the edge of becoming. But now, just faintly, this liminality is touched with intention. The world dreams its dream, and now, it is dimly aware that it is dreaming. Its forms come and go, its beings twist and turn, and its light dances endlessly through the water-thick air—but now, they are no longer mere accidents. They are choices.

And somewhere, beyond what could be called space or time, the benevolent power listens still—its work done, its presence no longer needed, but its attention never wavering from the unsteady beauty of a world learning to dream itself whole.
