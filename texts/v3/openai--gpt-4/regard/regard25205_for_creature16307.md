Their faces, if they can be called such, are often simply hollow frames of steel or brass, with no discernible eyes, mouths, or nostrils, and yet they regard the world with an unmistakable aura of sentience. The Remnants occupy the spaces between the machinery, wandering with an almost ritualistic routine. Some have been observed delicately arranging fragments of debris into ornate patterns, though these configurations are always dismantled by the ever-blowing wind or by other creatures. There is speculation that the Remnants are the echoes of the creators, the last shivering sparks of consciousness from a species long extinguished, clinging to their broken forms through nothing but the sheer will to endure. 

***And then, the Benevolent Power came upon the Realmscape.***

Drawn by the peculiar harmony of this artificial creation—a paradox of ruin and fecundity—the ancient and wise entity observed the paradoxical balance of life and death, chaos and order, sorrow and resolve. This being, who was not bound by the limits of form or time, was called many names by countless realms: the Weaver, the Witness, the Vast Gardener. And yet, if one had to describe its spirit, it could be said it was both water and flame—it healed and transformed, creating anew from decay without dissolving what had been.

The Weaver did not descend with spectacle, nor did it call out with thunder and majesty. Instead, it passed into the Realmscape as a melody impossible to hear—not sound, but a rippling song woven into the framework of existence itself. The Steamdrums hesitated momentarily as if in startled recognition, their deep clangs softening into a gentler beat. The creatures of the Realmscape, from the quiet Moimotes to the immense Thrumlords, paused in their endless labors and shifted subtly toward some unseen focal point, as if responding to a wordless summons.

The Weaver’s essence moved among them, extending its fathomless awareness over the plateau. It saw everything: the trembling labor of the Brassweavers, their unknowable patterns etched across the wasteland; the Moimotes’ quiet intimacy with the strange, metabolic plants; even the soft, awestruck vibrations of the formless Sibilants as they swirled like echoes of forgotten dreams. It saw in these beings not just survival, but meaning—a story not yet complete, threads woven into a broader tapestry.

At last, the Weaver came to the Remnants. It lingered before them longer than it had before any other creature. It marveled—not at their jagged forms, but at their persistence, their subtle, solemn defiance against the entropy that had consumed the world that had forged them. Perhaps more than anything else here, the Remnants were something truly sacred: a testimony to the courage to exist in defiance of despair.

And the Weaver acted—not as a god bestowing favor or judgment, but as a tender gardener tending a frostbitten garden. It whispered to the land, coaxing long-buried echoes of vitality to rise to the surface. The fragments of glass and metal in the barren soil began to shimmer faintly, resonating with forgotten energies. Geometries unseen began to unfold, not breaking or destroying what was, but merging with it, reforming it in ways that felt natural—not in their perfection, but in their quiet complementarianism.

For the first time in ages, water began to trickle across the metallic ground, though it did not appear in streams or pools familiar to natural worlds. Instead, rivulets of liquid light—viscous and glimmering with iridescence—spread across the plateau, winding through the land like veins, nourishing the biomechanical vegetation that had persevered against all odds. The plants responded eagerly; their tarnished metallic stems brightened, took on hues not unlike a rainbow seen through frosted glass, and their blooms became vibrant, radiant sculptures that seemed to hum faintly with life.

The Brassweavers paused their intricate assembly patterns and found retrieval easier; materials that had once been fragmented or buried too deep had risen to the surface, cleansed and softened by the strange, luminous liquid flowing through the land. The Sibilants began to coalesce, forming shifting constellations of light and vapor, patterns that gently resonated with the rhythmic pulses of the Steamdrums—but their tones carried warmth now, a thread of joy interwoven with their endless lament.

The Moimotes shone brilliantly now, their symbiosis with the revitalized flora intensifying. They became the gardeners and harbingers of new growth, their trails no longer merely glowing, but sparking bursts of small flowering constructs wherever they traversed—they had become part-catalyst, part-life themselves.

Lastly, the Remnants shifted. As the Weaver’s influence permeated their frames, they became more fluid, less jagged, and sharp. Their shattered joints melded with strands of the revitalized soils and luminous waters. Their movements became less halting, more rhythmic, and they began to hum a low, resonant tone that harmonized with their very essence, creating a sound more beautiful and profound than even the Steamdrums’ deep reverberations. Their bodies began to glint in ways that caught both light and shadow, transcending their broken beginnings while retaining the history etched into their forms.

The Thrumlords, watching from their shadowy heights, released a single, harmonious tone that reverberated for miles—a sound of acknowledgment, of understanding, as if they recognized the arrival of the Weaver as a long-prophesied shift. Then they retreated once again into the land, now becoming indistinguishable from the Steamdrum structures they guarded.

And so, the Realmscape remained what it always was: an intricate paradox of creation and decay—but now pulsing with a persistent, living vitality. And as for the Weaver, its work done, it whispered its final flourish: "Grow."
