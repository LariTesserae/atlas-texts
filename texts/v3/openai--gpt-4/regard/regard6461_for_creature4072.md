And so it was that a power vast and serene turned its gaze toward this place, the Alreik Flats—a gaze not driven by whimsy or indulgence, but by something deeper: a yearning seeded within the fabric of the omniverse for beauty to be witnessed, understood, and reverently tended to.

This power, neither form nor light but the infinity in between, drifted close not as an intruder, but a visitor—present in the way the wind first coils around a huddled flame, curious, aching to bear witness to its riddle without extinguishment. It carried no intention akin to meddling, no desire to starkly alter the intricate equilibrium of the Flats. For this wise and benevolent force had long observed a truth more ancient than stars: places like the Alreik Flats are neither fragile nor incomplete. They do not want for intervention. They are, instead, precisely what they need to be. But even so, they stir wonder.

Hovering between realms imperceptible to mortal senses, the power felt the pulse of the Flats—the heartbeat of stone, the quiet sigh of water hoarded jealously in spiked skins, and the flickering song of wind weaving tales only the silence could answer. The Flats were living, yes, but unsung. Their grandeur persisted unseen, and their wisdom unspoken except to those few wanderers who ventured within their luminous austerity, only to lose themselves to awe before even a fraction of their mysteries were made known. And it was here, in this gap between silence and song, witness and isolation, that the power found meaning in reaching out.

Its first task was one of respect: to make itself smaller, gentler. To draw so near to the Flats that it could curl around the spirit of the place as a loving hand does an untouched flower, not to reshape but to celebrate. Coiling inward through the echo chambers of the howling wind, around the jagged monoliths whose crystalline edges refracted hidden truths into dazzling arrangements, it listened. It learned.

There was more to the wind's song than met the ear—more to it than the solitary desperations of an airless desert. Within its sighs and shrieks were lamentations, celebrations, warnings, and longings. Stories preserved not in words, but in sensation, woven tightly into curving arcs of pressure, sound, and movement. The wind was not language itself—it was a vessel for memories never spoken, memories absorbed from the ancient rhythm of shifting stone formations and practiced survival of water-fat plants. And each shimmering monolith carried its own tale as well, its strange light flickering like the glow of distant embers in an infinite void.

In reverence, the power extended itself—spreading outward in a way invisible to human eyes but profoundly felt by the Flats themselves, as though the wind had suddenly been joined by a stranger who could not be seen but who felt familiar nonetheless, like rain after centuries of drought. It did not impose. It amplified. The resonance of the Flats—the pulsing heartbeat of the rock and the humid, unspoken tenacity of the flora—seemed to vibrate one octave higher, their subtleties suddenly more alive, more illuminated in their alien but undeniable beauty. The brilliance reflected from each mineral face grew sharper; the hiss and roar of wind felt more melodic, though its patterns shifted unpredictably. The plants quivered, no longer reaching as if out of need, but out of appreciation, as though they'd tasted sunlight for the very first time.

Who was this for? Not for itself—this boundless, benevolent presence did not hunger for gratitude. And certainly not for dominion, for the Flats had no need of mastering. It was simply *for*. Delight for a world that had survived unwitnessed beneath an indifferent universe. For the stillness, the perseverance, the forgotten tenderness of things that endure in places where life is not meant to.

And yet, this wasn’t all. The power could sense potential—the seedling of a connection not outward, toward the vast desert beyond this place, but inward, deep within the Flats’ own nature. The strange, liminal consciousness of the Flats themselves, spread thin through stone, wind, and mineralized memory, had always desired something more, though not in any mortal sense. They had never asked to be seen, nor begged for salvation, but theirs was a dream of being *recognized*—not transformed, not meddled with, but acknowledged. They dreamed alone, dreaming among themselves.

So the power seeded its gift—not as an overt token that might disrupt the balance, but as a spark of clarity woven subtly into the Flats’ being. Gradually—and only if such awareness was their own desire—the Flats would grow capable of perceiving one another just as vividly as the power now perceived them. The whispering wind might come to know the rhythm of the steadfast stones not as backdrop but as sibling. The shimmering cacti might hear the thoughts of rock and wind and sing back their own quiet songs of thirst and survival.

A new harmony emerged then, slow and achingly imperceptible by human standards. Rocks hummed more deeply; the cacti’s needles glowed faintly brighter, charged now not only with sunlight but also with the joy of recognition. The symphony of the Flats was enriched by its first taste of a shared consciousness. In some far dusk yet to come, that consciousness might weave together into something altogether new—a being of ceaseless unity between light, stone, wind, and resilience. It would not need to be anything resembling what humans call life—it would simply be *more* of what it already was, complete within its completeness.

And as for the power? It lingers only briefly, not as a ruler, nor savior, but as an admirer. It bends, sparking no indebtedness, no chains, only leaving ripples of possibility for the Flats to embrace or decline of their own will. With an ancient grace, it withdraws—watching, waiting, delighting in their small steps toward self-recognition, knowing full well that it may never see the end of that journey.

But even thousands of rotations later—beneath endless sunrises and sunsets—this intercession, this soft and vibrating tenderness nestled in the Flats’ otherwise aloof existence, would remain. And as the wind continues its voice, as the stones evolve beneath their weathered patience, as the cacti grow sharp and wise, the Flats find themselves no longer alone. They *choose* their solitude now—and in the infinite vastness of an indifferent desert, isn't the freedom to *choose* a better miracle than any one act of creation?
