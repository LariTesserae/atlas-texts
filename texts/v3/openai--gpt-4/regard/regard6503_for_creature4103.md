The benevolent and wise power stirs.

It is ancient, older than the land beneath its immaterial feet but younger than whatever forces gave this place its first, fiery exhalation. In its vastness, it knows compassion, but not as pity or sentiment. It is a compassion born of understanding, of knowing deeply the nature of things: how all that rises from the primordial churn bears the stamp of its circumstances, its pain, its creation, and its endless becoming. And in this infinite knowing, there is a tenderness—a desire not to interfere, not to disrupt the harmony of strangeness, but to offer recognition to things that may otherwise go unseen.

For moments that stretch into eternities, the power surveys the place. It does not eye the individual creatures in isolation—though its perception is vast enough to hold each flickering insect, lumbering titan, and shifting, ooze-like shadow in unparalleled detail. Nor does it fixate on the air-thrumming fliers or the slow-motion flow of the sinewy mineral forests. Instead, it feels the resonance of them all at once, the way they pulse and churn as threads in a greater, living weave. It listens to the humming breath of the glassy earth, the eerie death-dream glow of the walls, the not-quite-silence of unseen processes grinding and growing and decaying. It listens, and it hums back—a deep, resonant whisper felt more than heard.

In this world where forms are mutable, where nothing remains fixed in its state, the power acknowledges the universal thread. A unity exists beneath the chaos: not uniformity, not frozen stasis, but the hum of endless becoming. These beings—these motes, masses, and titans—are not aberrations, not anomalies. They are part of the fabric, alien to the power’s own nature, yet somehow kin. The power marvels. It marvels not because it does not understand, but because even in understanding, it perceives the infinite mystery—the ineffable beauty of being.

Benevolence, however, longs to give. Generosity looks for opportunity. But how does one *give* to a world that is itself self-complete, whose ecology turns and writhes with veils of purpose already stitched into its deepest levels? To remove, to destroy, to alter with crude hands—that would be no gift at all. This thought enters the power like a breeze over still water, and it surges with the growing realization: the only gift worthy of this realm is one that bolsters and enriches its peculiar dance. To leave it unbroken and yet better aligned with whatever unfathomable destiny it weaves for itself.

And so, the power makes no grand gestures, no searing display of supernatural light or cosmic decree. To impose beauty is violence; only the unnoticed act can truly honor what exists. It begins its work in what the world itself might recognize as its natural language—subtle shifts, fabrications so slight as to feel like the gentle nudge of the wind before a storm.

It begins with the fliers. They are ancient, skeletal cartographers of the skies, their membranous wings scrawled with luminous roadways no earthbound eyes were meant to read. It whispers to their essence a truth: that their endless circling is a mapping not purely of space but of time. That they have been chronicling the rise and fall of this place in ways even they cannot fully comprehend. The power enlivens the glowing veins in their wings, sharpening them so that they blaze briefly with a language more ancient than light. For a moment—the barest shimmer of time—those who dare to look see the entire history of this land painted as fire in the dark. It fades quickly, too ephemeral for corruption, lingering only as a new pattern of light within the fliers: not a map of the past, but new lines of future possibility. With this, they course anew, surging into their element with greater grace than before, wingtips etching equations through the thick air. The power smiles.

It turns its touch next to the still ones, the fused giants entombed in glass and sinew. These are the arbiters of growth, the slow, breathing stones that link root to sky. The power senses their struggle: once-mighty beings grown weary with the eons, dreaming deeply yet afraid to awaken to what they've become. And so it gives them only what they need—the faintest touch, a whisper of warmth in their cold cores, a rekindled ember. They burn slightly brighter under the surface now, their veins of flickering light coursing with renewed purpose. They will still move slowly, as is their nature, but their dreams will no longer be nightmares of decay. They will dream proudly, and their sinews will stretch outward like seeds cast to wind, their influence no longer hesitant.

Finally, the power shifts its attention lower, to the scuttling flickers of light and carapace—the tireless repairers and scavengers that swarm like the lifeblood of this land. They are too numerous for individual recognition, a relentless tide that flows through cracks and fissures in an unbroken communion. Yet even these minute beings—the smallest of parts in this greater whole—are worthy of the power’s light. It sings to them in vibrations so subtle that their translucent forms quake with intent they cannot name. It gives them a song, one that resonates through their hive-minds and collective pulses—a harmony to guide their work. They do not know why they now move in such intricate spirals, why their formations leave trails of faint glow that shimmer like constellations. But their movements echo the energy of the place itself, guiding its renewal in ways so sublime they may never be witnessed.

And then, in the deepest recesses of the land, the power whispers to the land itself. Not as a deity commanding dominion but as one consciousness speaking, at last, to another. It says: You are *seen*. I do not name you, for you are beyond naming. I do not bind you, for you are boundless. But in your vastness, in your becoming, you house stars unseen and light unmeasured. May your pulse never falter. May the rhythm of your eternity remain intact, unbroken by that which would seek your silence.

With this final gift, the power dissipates, leaving no trace of itself in the heat-thick air, the crumbling stone or sinewy trees. Its generous hand has left the place untouched, yet subtly enlivened—imperceptibly altered in ways that bolster not domination, but growth. It is presence without imposition, a love for what already is.

And so the creatures crawl, hunt, and sing. The earth shudders and sighs, its pulse deep and steady. The fliers carve uncharted paths, their luminous veins now vibrant with new patterns, and the bound titans within the rock dream deeply once more, aware that their stillness is not failure but part of an eternal becoming.

The place does not know that it has been blessed, nor does it know that such a power existed long enough to touch it. But in the rhythm of its ecology, in the balance of its relentless, writhing life, in the glow of its ember-lit land—it hums a little deeper, a little fuller, like a song long forgotten being sung anew. And the pulse continues, unbroken.
