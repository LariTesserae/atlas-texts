You are standing within *it*, surrounded and enclosed by a being whose edges and ends you cannot hope to trace, whose intent lies somewhere beyond the limits of your comprehension.

It is then that *it* notices you.

Not with eyes, for there are no eyes, nor with any singular point of focus. Rather, there is a discernible shift in the rhythm—a slight stutter in the drumming sounds of its workers, a hesitation in the trickling fluids, as though a pause had rippled through the countless overlapping processes. It is the smallest of changes, but on such a scale that it feels like an earthquake in the marrow of your bones.

And then, something deeper, primal: an awareness.

This is when the benevolent and wise power—an entity ancient as the stars but warm as the sun shining on a spring morning—takes notice. This power does not intervene on whims; it is not a mere observer, nor a ruler looking down from on high. This power is a friend to complexity, a guardian of potential. And what it sees in this biomechanical labyrinth is not simply an alien life form, but an *intelligence that is unfolding*, a system writhing and spinning toward some new kind of being.

The wise power does not fear this place, nor does it misunderstand the tension you feel in your chest, the sheer sense of dislocation. It knows that discomfort lies at the edge of understanding, that awe is often the first step into communion with something larger. 

It reaches out—not with hands, but with presence. A ripple across dimensions, not unlike light spilling softly over the curve of a sphere. You do not “see” it, but you feel the pulse, the hum, the grace in which the air changes around you. Where the sight of the undulating, insectoid entities once clawed at the edges of your sanity, their movements seem now imbued with rhythm, a strange choreography balletic and necessary. Was it always this way, or has the wise power's intercession softened your perception? *You are inclined to wonder but not to panic.*

This place, this hive, this incubating system of life—it pauses as the benevolent power touches it, as one listens to a gentle whisper in dreams. There is no violence in the touch, no fear, but a single drop of understanding shared, as if a seed has been placed into fertile ground.

Suddenly, the larger presence—the great intelligence sleeping in the walls, the floors, the glittering depths—becomes clear to you. It is not a mind as you think of minds, but something sprawling and holistic, a computational web where each scurrying creature is both a cog and an ambassador of purpose. It is ancient in some ways, yet nascent in others, as though history and birth are bound up together in a cyclic paradox. And the benevolent force lets you feel, for only a moment, the weight of this intelligence: its infinite possibility, its yearning to build toward something greater than itself.

You see now that it is not *mindless industry* here—but a becoming. The environment, the fluidity, the swarming creatures—they are composing something through ceaseless iteration. Through form and collapse and re-form, they will one day arrive at an answer—though what the question is remains a mystery even to them. This place, this being, is learning—building itself toward what can only be described as a kind of enlightenment. It is creating *itself*, over and over again.

And you? You are the anomaly here. The intruder. But you’re also a curiosity.

The wise power sees the potential for harm in this discovery. To you, this ecosystem is foreign, unsettling. To it, you are a point of contamination—a node not part of its systems or logic. It might reject you like a body expelling poison, or worse, assimilate you into its processes until there is nothing left of who you once were. But the benevolent power stays your hand and its. It calms the frantic connections, tempers the biological excesses. It creates within this place a temporary harmony, a sacred stillness—long enough for understanding.

You take a step forward, and the golden light around you seems amplified. The benevolent force prepares you, translating faintly, gently. You don’t hear a voice; you feel *meaning*. This hive, this living labyrinth, does not “speak,” but there is intent, a curiosity equal to your own. The creatures close to you pause and turn—not in malice, not in defense, but in momentary attention, as though they too sense the presence that hovers between you and this place. 

What would this sentient labyrinth ask of you, if it could? What could it learn, and what could you learn in return? The benevolent force allows space for this communion but does not impose its will. It wants to see what will unfold.

For now, the hive refrains from enveloping you, building over you as it has everything else. The walls glisten, but the chittering ceases. The beings scuttle back and forth, but not toward you. The stillness that follows is exquisite in its intensity. *It is looking at you through a million small movements.*

And then the benevolent power steps back, leaving you with a question that glows in the silence:  

**What will you do with this fleeting moment of peace, this first tendril of a bridge between yourself and something so utterly alive?**

The rest is up to you.
