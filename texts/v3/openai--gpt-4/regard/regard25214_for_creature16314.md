Far above this submerged world, in dimensions both adjacent and transcendent, a benevolent and wise power turns its attention toward the place, a gesture akin to light filtering down through the water. This power is old—older than the ruins that gather moss and silence, older than the hum of the great eels or the flicker of bioluminescent fronds. Its presence is not physical but experiential, a ripple of awareness that stretches beyond form and time, folding through dimensions. It has no eyes, but it watches. It has no hands, but it can touch. It has no voice, but it can be felt as unmistakably as the pressure of deep ocean currents. 

This power is patient—it must be, for it has observed countless ages pass—but what stirs its interest now is the momentary, infinitesimal arrival of the outsider into this space. It notices the being moving through the dim and thickened water, a scrap of vitality foreign to the rhythm of this world, a being with an uneasy heart that beats at the erratic pace of a surface dweller. The power lingers, considering, for its wisdom comes not from haste, but from contemplating the hidden consequence of even the smallest action.

There is no rush to intervene. After all, this submerged world belongs to itself. The hum that vibrates through the water, the signals exchanged between shadow-like shape-drivers and Ascendants, the phosphorescent rhythms of the deep garden—all these create a system of connections neither entirely natural nor entirely engineered but balancing precariously on the razor-thin boundary between. To touch it, to alter it without purpose, could send ripples that disrupt the entire tapestry. Yet, the wisdom of this higher power is expansive, like woven light, revealing the threads that tether this singular moment to possible futures. Something small yet poignant stirs in the outsider—fear, wonder, yearning—and it seems to leak into the surrounding current like spilled ink.

Carefully, deliberately, the power reaches—not physically, but as a permeation of presence, a sublime acknowledgment. It does not manifest in ways the outsider can recognize, for to do so would be an act of imposition, an interruption of their becoming. Instead, its touch takes on forms this place already understands: a prismatic shift in the flicker of waterborne light, a sudden deepening in the resonance of the ambient hum, the gentle rearrangement of nearby particles into a swirling, almost-symbolic motion.

And in these gestures, it plants seeds—not seeds that would grow into trees or corals or blooms of color, but seeds of potential, encoded within the outsider's own heart. A loosened memory of a forgotten longing surfaces in their mind, unbidden. It is the memory of stillness—the rare, fragile kind sometimes found in the quiet hours before dawn, or in the momentary hush before rain. The outsider, whether they realize it or not, has always carried an ache for this trembling state of reverence, and now, within the great womb of the water, they are drawn to it. The power does not demand anything from them; it simply reminds, suggesting without words that belonging is not about claiming or controlling, but dissolving and reciprocating. The outsider does not yet consciously understand, but the message settles in them like silt on the seabed of their soul, waiting to be unearthed.

Meanwhile, the place responds—not because the power wills it to, but because the place is inherently sensitive to both disruption and possibility. If the humming spires contain echoes of intelligence, they, too, begin to notice the outsider differently now. The fronds cradle them as they swim past, pulsing slightly in rhythm with the new vibration that emanates from their presence. The carapace-shellers pause in their burrowing, their antennae trembling. Even the Ascendants, hanging dormant at the edges of shadowy columns, seem to grow quieter—not lifeless, but listening.

In this converging moment, the outsider is left with the profound invitation to decide who they wish to become in this submerged kingdom. They may choose to leave, retreating to the comparative clarity and confidence of their former world above. Or they may stay, drifting deeper into the heavy, aqueous rhythms that define this space, and slowly merge with what the ruins and their quiet inhabitants embody. The benevolent power does not interfere; ichory lessons have already been woven into this subtle crossing of fates.

Yet, as it withdraws, one final resonance branches outward through the water—not as sound, but as a feeling, a suffusion of warmth at the heart, like the phrase of a lullaby half-remembered. **You are seen,** the resonance whispers without words, into the spaces between thoughts. **You are seen, you are known, and this is enough. Even in places where time forgets to flow, nothing is ever truly lost.**

As this invisible touch lingers and slowly drifts away, the wise and benevolent power leaves the place unchanged in any obvious way. The ruins remain lost, the light still lost its courage in fathoms of shadow, and the slow eels glide in their unhurried, ancient missions. But the outsider is left changed in ways they will not fully understand—not yet. The water hums on, and the world folds itself back into its quiet rituals.

The power lets its focus drift once more, as softly and completely as rain clouds dispersing into a clear sky. The submerged place, the outsider, the ancient sentinels—they are no different, yet they are held now in the boundless awareness of something that does not define itself in words. It listens, waits, and watches still, as it always has. When the outsider turns again toward the great, slow shadow of the spires, they alone must face the choice: a belonging that dissolves, or a yearning that lingers unfulfilled. **The answers lie only where you are willing to go.**
