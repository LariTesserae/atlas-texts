And then, from the beyond, a consciousness arrived.

It did not come in the way of storms or great upheaval; no thunder announced its presence, no wind howled in its wake. Rather, it slipped into the hollow as twilight slips into dawn—a seamless merging, an intrusion so gentle one might question if it were there at all. The benevolent and wise power that turned its gaze to this place seemed not to loom or preside from above but rather to settle into the very molecules of mist, fold itself into the wet warmth of the air. It was not here to rule, only to witness—to understand.

This power, whose form might be said to resemble a great, diffuse knowing—a kind of boundless empathy too large and too old to be contained—had been drawn by a thought, a yearning. It was a faint pulse on some dimension intangible to all but the exceptionally attuned. The hollow, this cradle of mist and stone, had whispered across the aeons, spilling no words but radiating a resonance nonetheless: *Look at me,* it seemed to hum in frequencies that no ear could hear. *I am small, but I am whole. I am forgotten, but I am vast within myself. Come closer, and see.*

And so, it came. At first, it merely watched. It watched the water crawl like glistening veins along the face of the stone. It marveled at the quiet majesty of the moss as it stretched imperceptibly slow in its quest to polish the walls. It drifted through the faint bioluminescent glow of fungi and traced the delicate shimmer of iridescent membranes that rippled upon the water. Time, irrelevant to the power, unraveled before it like a tapestry, showing moments indistinguishable yet infinite. It felt no rush, no need to move or impose. It had all the time (or none at all, which was much the same) to immerse itself in what was already perfect and knowable—a universe contained in the cradle of an ancient ridge.

The beings within the hollow—primal shapes of life, elegant in their strangeness—registered the power’s presence not as alarm but as a kind of pressure in the air, like the density one feels before rain. The translucent architects, the algae-draped insects, the shadow-soft phantoms, and even the nearly unconscious drops of water suspended in pools knew something had shifted in their little cosmos. But there was no fear. The old presence felt synonymous with the hollow itself—yet also something more. Perhaps it had always been there, buried deep at the edges of their still-swirling perception. They bent toward it instinctively, the way their ancestors must have bent toward the scarce shafts of light.

As the power lingered, thoughts began to stir. These were not its own—for it existed without thought as we understand it—but echoes from those who lived there. Questions rose like bubbles through the dense air. Not words, not even ideas, but longings. Every droplet of water birthed a new microcosm of microorganisms and, with the ambivalent gesture of its falling into a still pool, demanded the secret be asked: *What does it mean to thrive in impermanence?* The vines, in their patient grasping at the misted sky, echoed another question: *Where does the will to grow come from, when to be is enough?*

And the power listened. That was its great gift: its listening. Not merely hearing, but *listening.* For listening held the alchemy of transformation, though never in a way as brash or disruptive as direct intervention. To be heard was to be illuminated, and in that illumination, the hollow began to shine, if not brighter, then clearer. Like stars mirrored on the surface of rippling water, there was nothing new here, only that which had always been present, now understood as precious.

Finally, after an unmeasurable duration of dwelling deep within the hollow's ecosystem, the power did what it had done in countless other moments of wonder strewn across the universe. It *gave back*—not by altering, but by amplifying. Where there had been wonder, it left reverence. Where there had been life teetering on the edge of something extraordinary, it nudged delicately into brilliance. The fungi, already luminous, now sparked in impossible gradients, their slow pulses of turquoise and amber joined by faint traces of violet light spiraling out like neural thought. The strange shadow-creatures dwelling in the mist gained clarity without solidity, their sinuous forms sharp yet evanescent, weaving and darting like threads of liquid shadow in the air.

And though the beings here would not think of themselves as "different," they would become more themselves, as though a sharpening had occurred—not a change in essence, but a glow of fuller integrity. The ancient stone, long content to bathe in the patient tears of water, began to sing in its vibrations—a sound too low for the ear, but audible to the soul. It, too, had been seen.

When it finally prepared to move on—to continue its great journey through the unseen threads that connect all places, all beings—this power left behind one final, silent gift, one it left only in those few, rare places it knew could hold it without breaking under the weight. It left a deepening of the strange fold in time: a minuscule adjustment to the cosmos, so that the hollow might now spill its unseen resonance more powerfully for those who find it. A faint aura, a beckoning shimmer to the far edges of perception, as if the world itself whispered: *Look at me. Remember.*

And so the power turned its formless face toward the horizon, walking out of time just as easily as it had walked in. Nothing had changed, and everything had changed. The hollow swayed in its rhythm of mist and stone, moss and shadow, as it always had and as it always would. But those who found their way here thereafter—by accident or by design—would feel the weight of that benevolent gaze still lingering, etched into air and rock and water. They would not see its form. They would not grasp its purpose or its name. But they would know, as they stood in the thick warmth of the hollow and let the impossible light dance across their senses, that they were standing in something sacred.

And, leaving, they too would carry it with them—not merely the texture of moss beneath their fingers or the humid air on their skin, but something deeper. The hollow—its stillness, its balance, its secretive and self-contained infinity—would grow roots in the quiet soil of their being, and in time, without them noticing, it would begin to bloom.
