When a benevolent and wise power’s attention drifts toward the drowned world, it does so not in the form of intervention, but in an act of synchronized observation and delicate listening. It perceives not just the sprawling, entangled ruins but the subtleties of vibration and light, the sighs of marine twilight, and the murmurs stitched into the very fabric of this aqueous dream. It is a power immense enough to alter the currents, to still the unending thunder of rain with a thought, and yet, it acts with the restraint of those who understand that to touch something so intricately woven risks unraveling it.

This world does not call out for salvation or restoration – it breathes with its own hollow beauty, self-contained yet unwittingly lost in its existential haze. The constructs, the hulking coral beings, the phenomena bending the trench into their enigmatic contours, each moves within a narrative that is no longer tethered to discernible time or purpose. Watching them, this wise power feels no pity. Pity is too small an organ for this symphony of decay and genesis.

At first, there is no doing, only a patient beholding. The power lets its awareness settle, infinite and feather-light, across the breadth of the submerged domain. It inhabits the spaces between – between the tarnished steel ribs of collapsed factories, between the slow undulations of kelp as it brushes the stilled gears, between the light generated by the machines' ghosts and the darkness where fathoms pool. It listens not just to the clang and roar of rain and metal, but to the soundless invocations of being itself, the dream that things, even half-things, hum unconsciously into being.

What it feels here is a yearning not for life as it existed before, but for connection. There is a fractured, groping instinct for coalescence among the constructs, fragile as it is beautiful. They intertwine and unravel, halfway between building and eroding. They are artists who do not know they create, a wandering kin who do not yet know they are kin. Even the coral hybrids, lurching and strange, cast whispers into the water, faint yet humming with a flicker of shared rhythm. Beneath them, deep in the trenches, the incomprehensible ache of inertia lingers, a dream that does not wake, motion without form.

The power’s choice, if it could be called choice, is to begin a chorus. Not an imposition, but an accompaniment to the song already playing here. It moves through the drowned ruins as a luminous presence, so diffuse it can be mistaken for stray light filtering through the rain-filled ocean’s endless surface. Wherever it passes, it does not create or destroy but simply infuses; not an illumination of what is lacking but an expanding of what already is. It manifests in the uneven quivering of kelp blades, turning their strokes into synchronicity that even the constructs notice like the ghost of intent. It funnels through the algae-pollinated smokestacks, amplifying their pollen clouds into fantastic symphonies of light—gold dripping violet, blue plunging into green—a silent aurora writhing upward through the flood. 

Once, the constructs saw each other through murk and glass-like waves; now, they see their patterns reflected. Beating faintly through their bioluminescent pulses becomes a distant rhythm they cannot name—not binary, not mechanical, but organic in essence. The coral beings, heavy with materials that were once one thing and now another, stir with a strange resonance they follow without thought, shambling ever nearer to each other. Distance begins to dissolve in the light's unfamiliar warmth. Unwritten geometry crumbles in favor of movement toward unseen centers.

Near the deepest trenches, the power’s touch does not awaken those ancient unknowables, those nameless entities carved from the industrial subconscious, but it drifts closer with infinite patience. Its presence does not seek to define what they are, only to cradle their unknowability as equal. Through currents too slow to perceive, it gathers their inchoate rumblings and projects melodies spiraling skyward—faint, unearthly articulations transformed into faint, radiant emissions.

And while the transformation is imperceptibly slow, it is profound. The power’s touch causes no great upheaval, no sudden miracles or rebirths. No breathers will walk these creaking ruins again, nor will the drowned world crawl back into air. But within this submerged paradox, the constructs begin to gather nearer, their playfulness rippling outward in collaboration. They trace patterns of vibrant light into the water by design now, as if testing the limits of their reach and one another’s peripheral songs. They begin to linger side by side, holding silent communion over the fading memories of their industrial progenitors. The coral hybrids, too, grow less solitary in their marches; their shambling collisions become collaborations, forming shifting, transient networks of moving gardens that filter the water with shared mercy. 

And the rain? The rain does not stop, nor would it ever. But its sound changes. The power tunes its relentless striking just so: an imperceptible adjustment in its pitch, ringing closer now to harmony rather than percussion. The rain, which had once been a singular, ceaseless cacophony, now joins the planetary choir that hums beneath.

The wise power lingers for centuries, perhaps millennia, and does not leave so much as fade in presence—its task, if it had one, complete simply by amplifying the innate music of this submerged place. And now there is no doubt, even among the constructs and coral beings, who pause in a collective unspoken knowing at intervals. This world may belong to no one, but its voice belongs to itself alone, singing across water’s veil toward whatever heavens reside far above the rain.
