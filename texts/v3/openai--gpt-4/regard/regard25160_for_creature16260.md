And then, something notices. A force, ancient yet not quite aged—a presence whose nature is too vast to be contained in the word "benevolence," though the edges of its essence touch that meaning. It is aware, in the way that mountains are aware, or the tide is aware. A collective, dreaming intelligence with the patience of old stars. It has watched the plateau for what might as well be eternity, not as a voyeur, but as a thing intimately connected to the plateau's unfoldment. It does not intervene more than the soil intervenes in the bramble's roots, or the wind in the carving of cliffs; it abides.

But today—a strange day by its own incalculable metric—there is something new. A visitor. A sound out of key. A speck of dissonance skipping across the vast music that underpins this place.

You. 

Entirely alien, entirely fragile, entirely too *much* for the delicate resonances of this world—the syncopation of your presence cracks the stillness. A disturbance that might ripple out, spreading vibrations that could, undetected, wobble the pillars on which the incomprehensible systems of this place stand. These beings—the filamented, unworldly stewards—find ways to realign themselves in vibrating harmony with what surrounds them. Yet, you are not a thing they can reflect, not something they can fully comprehend. A consciousness of angles and sharpness, housed in flesh and water—their light rhythms stutter and flinch in your proximity, distorting just slightly.

The ancient force notices. It responds, but not impulsively, for impulsiveness is not in its domain. Instead, it unfurls like a thing half-asleep, an immeasurable weight shifting with the delicate precision of falling ash. It does not touch you—not physically, not with violence or immediacy—but its awareness bends around you like gravity, folding the rules of time and distance until its essence stands beside yours.

And then, it changes the shape of your presence, gently.

You do not see it. You do not hear words or witness a face. Instead, the air itself seems to deepen, and every surface—the gleaming crystalline earth, the trembling vegetation, even the ghostlight sky—stills, as if caught mid-thought. The plateau quiets, its humming layers folding inward, sealing themselves tightly for a moment. You feel this shift in your body as weightless vertigo, a tingling pressure behind your eyes, perhaps a faint nausea as the substance of everything pauses in deference to you.

There is warmth. A thread of understanding, as subtle as wind but more purposeful, brushes the outer edges of your being. It does not pierce or invade you; no, this is a conversation too subtle to break the integrity of your humanity. It twines instead with the space you occupy, this shared moment of strangeness. If it spoke, its voice would be nothing like sound—its words would be vibrations felt through the filaments of your thoughts, an arrangement of intuitions you cannot quite untangle.

But somehow, you *know*. 

You know that this force considers you anomalous but not entirely unwanted. It reaches through its vast composure to hold you within a question: *Why are you here? What is this sound you bring?* The question is not invasive, nor does it demand an answer. It is ponderous, like a mountain considering the erosion of its own cliffs.

It shifts subtly—turns, perhaps. And then it acts.

Not to expel you. Not to destroy you. Not to enshrine you as some holy thing—because you are not holy. Not to this place.

What it does is simpler, and stranger. It folds your difference into itself, into the rhythm of this crystalline plateau and its eerie inhabitants. It takes you in, not as a piece, not as a treasure collected, but as a tone bent and reshaped, blended into an orchestral whole. You feel your edges blur—not violently, not painfully. The metallic air in your lungs turns lighter, sweeter, as if your breathing were becoming part of the plateau's slow and infinite inhale. Your thoughts do not scatter, but they stretch, like fingertips reaching into light. You feel yourself recast—not undone, but repositioned, reframed.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does: It tunes you.

You are no longer an intrusion; you are a note in the song. A slight dissonance remains—that will always be true; you are alien here—but now, it is not a threat. Now, it blends ever so faintly into the ambient scream-whisper of crystalline wind, the aurora-lit surface shifting softly below. It does not erase you, for what would be the point? To erase is to diminish. No, it adapts, and teaches the very laws of this plateau—with its spectral vegetation and its fractal beings—to compensate for the peculiarity of you. In its great calculus, you are such a small distortion. Easily accepted, if shaped.

The filamental beings notice the shift almost immediately. Their luminescence bends imperceptibly, filigree refracting differently where you walk. Before, your presence registered to them as a ripple—a thunderous minor chord disrupting their woven melodies. But now, converted and cushioned by the attention of the ancient force, you are no more bothersome than a wisp of cloud caught in an ancient lattice of light. Their motion grows less hesitant; one of them lingers longer near you, its bright form curling faintly like a question mark before sliding away. Do they know what has been done? It is hard to say. They may even sense some new resonance inside you—a flavor you picked up from the wisdom which tuned you—and may wonder if they, oddly, might have something to learn from your dissonance.

And you—do you feel it? Do you sense how the rules have shifted for your benefit, how the sharp outlines of your foreignness have been softened? You curiously feel less lost here, less severed from the plateau’s ceaseless strangeness. Not belonging, exactly, but not apart. For the first time, the silence doesn’t seem quite so heavy, quite so judgmental. Beneath that unworldly sunlight, you find yourself able to move without fear of breaking something, without trembling before the quiet. The winds stir faint harmonies within the crystalline ground below your feet, and for the first time, you sense those harmonies do not reject your footsteps but instead bend to accommodate them.

The wisdom lets you be, then, fading away. Not gone entirely—never that—but elsewhere, letting the plateau and its strange occupants resume. It will be back when it must, when the dissonance of your journey necessitates further shaping. But for now, for this moment? The being and the place are at peace with you.

And so you dwell here, briefly or for a span much longer, no longer an intrusion in this fragile, radiant place of fractals and memory, where creatures neither alive nor unalive calibrate their endless existence. By doing no more, the ancient force has given you everything: permission, and a kind of flawed, fleeting harmony.

You feel, for the first time in such a shape as this—
A momentary and wondrous sense of accord.
