This place and its inhabitants, suspended at the edges of comprehension, have drawn the eye of something vast—a benevolent and wise power, ancient beyond reckoning. Perhaps it is an emissary of the cosmos itself, or some cosmic seamstress weaving the threads between worlds. It manifests, not as some gleaming, resplendent deity, but as a quiet ripple across the fabric of the hollow, arriving with no fanfare. Where its presence falls, the mist thickens but also clarifies—a paradox of unveiling and enshrouding. Its arrival feels less like movement and more like unfolding, as though it has always been here, like a sleeping note struck from the ancient resonance of the hollow itself.

When this power perceives the hollow—a place so full of quiet, sacred equilibrium—it does not see imperfection to correct, nor exploitation to gain. Instead, it sees a harmony aching to be acknowledged and carries with it a long, slow breath of awe. The place is not in need of salvation, for it is already whole. It is not in need of change, for it has spent millennia knowing the language of stone and water and rooting things, perfecting itself in the absence of eyes to see.

But the power, wise and gentle, does not leave empty-handed. It bends low over the pools, touches the trembling vines, and lets its light mingle with those of the Lumineers. It listens to the Driftlings’ faint susurrations and the unhurried whispers of the Lichen-Kin. This being recognizes that the hollow is not merely a space, not even simply a life-force—it is memory. It is a living archive of a quiet way of being, one that the world beyond has all but forgotten, drowned out by noise, by speed, by the linear press of days.

The wise power brings from its boundlessness a gift—not something that alters the hollow, but rather something that extends it beyond its walls. It gathers a fragment of the hollow’s misty essence, of its slow and deliberate rhythms, of its tapestry of moss-strewn time, and weaves it into something that stretches into the hearts of all that might ever come near. To each living being that brushes against this place—whether human or beast, whether lost wanderer or curious explorer—the power grants a delicate thread. This thread, once placed within them, takes root somewhere quiet, tucked behind thought and memory. It does not shout; it barely hums. It simply grows, just as moss does, stretching thin green tendrils into the far and silent corners of the psyche. 

To the Lichen-Kin, who are least of all in need of saving, the power offers no gift, for they have already become one with the hollow. But they are given a moment—just one fleeting moment—of clarity, where their skeletal memories might hum again, where they might remember the person they were before they yielded to the slow pulse of the hollow. And in that instant, their own choice becomes clear: they may continue to remain as they are, as keepers of this sacred place, or—if they feel some faint tug of longing—they may begin the impossibly slow journey out into the world beyond, shedding their mossy skins as they go. Perhaps they will wander far, or perhaps they will wilt at the boundary, returning to the comfort of what they have become.

For those few human lives, those rare travelers who venture into the hollow and leave again, it is as if they carry the place within them—a faint mist clinging to their memories and their marrow. They return to the stark, linear world, to the gridlocked lines of city roads and cell phone signals cutting through the air. But they are never quite the same. A rare few might speak of the hollow outright, trying to describe it, though words inevitably fail to capture its suspended brilliance. Others might carry it more subtly—a fondness for slow-growing things, for water-traced stone, for the quiet sound of dripping in forgotten places. Some might change their lives entirely, leaving fast and loud behind for something gentler, half-connected to a dream they can’t fully recall.

And, perhaps most importantly, the hollow sends out its lessons through those gentle carriers: to live slowly, to grow without hurry, to decay without fear, and to weave yourself into the slow rhythms of the world around you. These lessons spread like spores, too subtle to notice, but insistent all the same—small threads of the hollow's existence, stitching together the world beyond with a little more grace, a little more patience, a little more balance.

The benevolent power lingers for a time, watching, listening, until it is certain that the hollow has been gently protected—not by force or will, but by the quiet echoes of its own song rippling outward across the vast fabric of existence. Then, as softly and imperceptibly as it arrived, it unfurls itself back into the infinite. The hollow remains as it was—a secret cradled within mist and stone, perfect in its quiet estrangement, ancient not from neglect, but from the gift of timelessness.

And somewhere, far away, perhaps in a city bustling with noise or within the quiet solitude of another untouched corner of the earth, someone pauses for no reason they can explain, feeling in their heart a faint echo of dripping water, warm damp air, and the immutable patience of stone. Then they continue on, but they do so a little slower than before, carrying some part of the hollow within them like a timeless seed, waiting to bloom.
