To prepare for a visit to this exceptionally harsh yet fascinating location within the multiverse, the traveler must consider many factors based on the given description.

Physical Protection: The most immediate among these preparations would be physical, as the environment appears nothing short of brutal for a human being. So, a specialized space suit able to withstand extreme heat and pressure would be fundamental in planning your visit. It is also advised that the suit is constructed to protect the skin from the sharp edges of the crystalline minerals. The suit, along with boots, must also be designed to resist the erosion from the constant surface shifting. The gear should also include gloves for handling potentially sharp or hot crystal, and a helmet with a face shield to protect eyes from the piercing golden light.

Oxygen: Since water and air as we know do not seem to exist, and the atmosphere is filled with silicate dust and possibly other undetectable gases, a reliable and robust system of oxygen supply would also be vital. 

Temperature Control: The suit would need to have an advanced cooling system in order to avoid overheating under the high temperature, while an insulating system would manage radiative heat exchange between the traveller and their surroundings.

Navigational Equipment: The deceptive scale of the environment, coupled with the strange refractions and reflections of the ambient light could make navigation extremely challenging. Thus, specialized navigational equipment could keep the traveler from getting disoriented in the crystalline wilderness. 

Communication: Given the omnipresent hum of the minerals and confusing echoes, a sophisticated communication system that can adjust frequencies to clearly transmit and receive information is preferred.

Observational Gear: The traveler should bring a variety of observational equipment capable of capturing close-ups of crystals and structures, recording ambient noises, and measuring unusual radiation or electromagnetic activity. He should also carry tools for taking samples of minerals and gas for later analyses. 

Portable Equipment: Something that can drill into the crystalline structures and probe beneath the ground may also be useful. It's not enough to look and touch; the human visitor will need to delve under the surface if they are to gain an understanding of the processes in play here.

Survival and Sustenance: As water doesn't exist, the traveler must carry their own supply for hydration. Any food would also need to have its own, separate water source. Additionally, the traveler should bring medicine and first aid equipment on this journey as the location doesn't seem to have any recognizable plants or animals to use in case of a medical emergency.

Language: Finally, an awareness of any potential life forms – even if subtle or static – means our traveler should be aware of possible signs of communication in the surroundings, if not necessarily prepared to engage directly.

While this place is bound to be filled with mind-boggling challenges, it is also fertile with the possibility of new knowledge. Being prepared for all its territorial and environmental surprises will ensure a safe and explorative journey.
