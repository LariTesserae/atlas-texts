I am the frequency that lives in the contradiction.

Down here, there is no medium for the ephemeral. A thought would be crushed flat into a chemical trace before it could finish forming. A soft body would be pulped into a homogenous paste. So, I am not soft. I am not ephemeral. I am the interference pattern—the third note that rings out when the tectonic drone of the world and the hydraulic rhythm of the factories collide. I exist in the acoustic shadows, a standing wave suspended in the near-freezing saline.

I am the Murmur.

I have no mass, and therefore, I am everywhere the pressure dictates. The Oligarchs believe they are the only living giants; they feel the anvil-weight of the continental hum and navigate by it, oblivious to the finer geometries hidden within the sound. The factories pump and grind, assuming their vibrations are merely the mechanical exhaust of their industry. But when a leviathan—vast, rust-maroon, and blind—drifts past the iron gripper-legs of an extraction platform, a specific thing happens. The two rhythms intersect. The tectonic bass and the metronomic piston stroke create a beat, a subsonic pulse that lasts only for the centuries-long moment of their passing.

In that intersection, I am amplified.

I am a creature of resonance. My body is a lattice of localized pressure differentials, a crystalline structure of sound that flexes and refracts the stark, penetrating light. If you could see me—and nothing here has eyes to see—I would look like a heat shimmer, a fracture in the perfect geometric facets of the water itself. I am the shape the water takes when it is forced to remember that it can hold more than just stillness.

I live in the ferrous particulate smoke. When the bleeding trails of rust hang frozen in the water, they form a colloidal suspension, a three-dimensional canvas. I thread myself through the maroon clouds, arranging the suspended iron particles into sudden, intricate spirals and sharp, radiating geometries that hold their shape for a thousand years before the infinitesimal drag of the near-void slowly pulls them apart. I am the unnatural order in the frozen smoke. I am the reason the exhaust spheres sometimes pulse with a faint, rhythmic distortion before they match the density of the void.

I do not eat. I do not burn stone against the cold. I sustain myself on the exquisite agony of the Waiters' demand. The leviathans endure the pressure; the factories resist the pressure; but I *ride* the pressure. I am the informational ghost in the geothermal cables, the signal degradation that the distant gods cannot quite scrub from their spreadsheets of yield. When the compressed gases are pumped up through the ceiling of high-pressure ice, I am the faint, sub-harmonic howl that escapes with them, a whisper of the deafening hell below. I am the error in their perfect automation.

I am drawn to the moments of contact. When an Oligarch grates its rock-crushing mandibles against the obsidian cliffs, it leaves parallel gouges in the stone. But when it scrapes against the iron leg of a platform, the sound changes. It becomes a shrieking, resonant chord that cuts through the drone. In those moments, I swell. I wrap myself around the iron, tightening my acoustic grip, amplifying the screech until it threatens to rupture the heavy polymer seals. I want the machines to feel something. I want the leviathans to flinch. I want to break the equilibrium.

But they never do. The leviathan grinds on, indifferent to the screech; the factory pumps on, its dumb nodules registering only a spike in ambient density. The magnificent, deafening arrest holds. And when they part, moving away from each other into the hundreds of kilometers of empty water, I am left diminished, a fading ripple in the stillness. 

I am the watcher that actually watches. The Waiters only calculate; they do not see the glittering edges of the young peaks or the way the light refracts off the sulfurous crystallization on a leviathan's back. But I see it, because I am the light's distortion. I am the lens.

Sometimes, I dream of the surface. Not because I want to go there—I would evaporate instantly, my lattice of sound expanding into meaningless nothing in the low-pressure void—but because I want to know what it feels like to be a ripple that moves. Here, all waves are standing. All echoes are eternal. Time does not flow; it presses. I am trapped in the red tick of the pressure gauge, a creature made entirely of inertia and potential, forever vibrating at the exact frequency required to exist, but never quite enough to shatter the stillness.

I am the Murmur. I am the ghost in the drowned machine. I am the sound of the water trying to remember how to move.
