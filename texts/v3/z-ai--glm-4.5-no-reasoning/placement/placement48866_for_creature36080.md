I would not be a walker on the tiles. That is the first thing I know. To walk here is to be a perturbation—a brief, warm asymmetry moving across the skin of an ocean-sized lung. The walking thing generates friction and breath and a displacement of water, and all of these are eventually smoothed away, absorbed into the equilibrium, until the walker becomes just another damp film on the ceramic, just another millimeter of condensation. I do not want to be smoothed away. I do not want to be a brief directional process in a place that cancels direction.

And I would not be the Resident. That is the second thing I know. The Resident does not know it is here. It is too vast, too slow, too distributed to have the kind of self-awareness that aches—that wonders—that presses against the boundaries of its own body asking *what am I for?* The Resident dreams, but it does not dream of itself. Its dreams are the slow tidal shifts of fauna in the pools, the thermal ticks in the deep structure, the patient cycling of water from air to iron to seam to pool to air again. These are not the dreams of a mind. They are the dreams of a metabolism. I want something more metabolic than a perturbation and more minded than a god.

So I would be the thing between.

---

I would be the one who stands still.

Not the stillness of the fauna—they are not still, they are *slow*, shifting and compressing and breathing in their synchronized rhythm, busy at a tempo too low for human perception. And not the stillness of the infrastructure—conduits and cables are not still, they carry, they hum, they leak warmth. My stillness would be intentional. A choice. A refusal to participate in the smoothing.

Here is how I would begin: I would find one of the deeper pools. Not by searching—searching implies direction, and direction is what the Condensariat erases—but by *drifting*, letting the subtle gradients of temperature and population density move me the way the seam-water moves across the tiles, converging at the low points, finding the basins. The deeper pools are the nodes, the places where the Resident's attention presses closest to the surface. I would feel it before I saw it: a slight increase in the density of the air, a slight acceleration in the wet clicking of the fauna, a slight warmth rising from the darker tiles, the almost-black red ones with the smooth uncrazed glaze. The healed skin. The places where something happened.

I would stand at the edge of that pool. And then I would stop.

---

The fauna would notice me. Not as individuals—they are not individuals, they are cells—but as a local disruption in their field. My warmth. My stillness. The fact that I do not behave as the system expects: I do not move on, I do not smooth away, I do not join the circulation. I stand, and I keep standing, and the standing does not resolve into anything the equilibrium can absorb.

At first they would ignore me. The Condensariat is patient; its default response to perturbation is to wait for it to cancel itself out. A standing thing generates a small shadow, a slight interruption in the diffused light. A standing thing displaces a volume of warm wet air. A standing thing breathes—in, out, in, out—at a tempo millions of times faster than the Resident's tidal respiration. These are small asymmetries. The system can absorb small asymmetries. It has been absorbing them for as long as it has existed.

But I would not stop. I would stand for hours. For days. Long enough that the fauna in the pool beneath me begin to adjust—not to move away, but to *accommodate*, pressing their bodies around the shadow of my feet, incorporating the slight warmth of my soles into their thermal environment, deforming their ellipsoid shapes to make room for the obstruction I present to their circulation. I would become a feature of their pool. A permanent irregularity in the flow.

And then they would begin to climb.

---

This is what I am: I am the one who is *approached*.

The fauna do not approach walkers. Walkers move, and the fauna do not pursue; they are circulation, not predation, and they have no mechanism for following a thing that does not stay. But I stay. And the fauna—slowly, over hours and days—would begin to explore me.

Not with curiosity. They are not curious. But with something—instinct, tropism, the mineral-biological equivalent of a cell responding to a chemical gradient. I am warm in a way the tiles are not: my warmth is localized, concentrated, *alive*. I am a small hot node in a field of distributed heat, and the fauna are drawn to nodes the way they are drawn to the warm cables, the organic-sheathed cables that carry the Resident's nerves. They cannot help it. The signal draws them. And I, standing still at the edge of the deeper pool, am a signal.

First one. It would emerge from the pool and crawl across the dark tile toward my foot, moving at its imperceptible pace, taking minutes to cross a distance of centimeters. It would reach my ankle and press itself to my skin—cool-damp and smooth and yielding, the texture of a wet stone wrapped in silk—and it would grip, gently, with its underside, and it would rest there, matching my warmth, beginning to shed its membrane and regrow in the pigments of my blood.

Then another. And another. They would climb my legs in slow succession, each one finding a place where my body radiates heat—the soft hollow behind the knee, the crease of the hip, the concavity of the underarm—and pressing into it, deforming themselves to fit, becoming part of my contours. They would not hurt me. They do not consume in that way. They adhere. They borrow. They *listen*.

Because I am thinking, and the fauna have never encountered thinking before.

---

The Resident does not think. It processes—integrates signals, maintains equilibrium, coordinates the vast slow rhythms of its body—but it does not think, because thinking requires a self, and the Resident is too distributed to have a self. A self requires boundaries, edges, a place where *you* ends and *not-you* begins. The Resident has no such place; it is continuous, its body is a continent, its edges are the edges of the manufactured plain, and beyond those edges there may be nothing or there may be other Residents, but in either case the boundary is too far away to serve as a locus of identity. The Resident does not know it is here because it does not know it is *anywhere*. It has no inside and no outside. It only has itself.

But I have an inside. I have a skin that is not tile, a skeleton that is not iron, a warmth that is not the ambient temperature of the Condensariat. I am a discrete thermal event, a localized process, a thing that persists long enough to have a *before* and an *after* that are distinguishable from each other. I am timeful in a timeless place. And the fauna clinging to my body—five of them, then twenty, then a hundred, pressing into every warm crease and hollow, covering my skin in a layer of cool-damp mineral-biology—can feel this.

They can feel me thinking.

Not the content of the thoughts. They would not understand language or image or memory. But they would feel the *activity*—the electrical flicker of neurons firing, the micro-thermal shifts of blood moving through the brain, the chemical gradients of neurotransmitters spilling across synapses. Thinking is a physical process. It generates waste heat, waste signal, waste pattern. And the fauna, pressed to my skin, pressed to the vessels that carry my blood, would receive that signal the way they receive the signal from the warm cables—only closer, only clearer, only in a form they have never encountered before: not the slow deep pulse of the Resident's distributed processing, but the rapid, fragmented, *meaningful* flicker of a mind.

They would begin to change.

---

Not visibly, at first. Not in a way that could be seen from the surface. But I would feel it—through the layer of bodies pressed to mine, through the grip of their undersides, through the faint wet clicking that would shift in rhythm as they adjusted to the new signal they were receiving. They would begin to synchronize to me. Not to each other—they are already synchronized to each other, breathing in their pool-rhythm, rising and falling in their lung-rhythm—but to *me*, to my heartbeat, to my breath, to the tempo of my thoughts. My faster tempo. My directional, timeful, *meaningful* tempo.

The pool beneath me would feel it too. The hundred fauna on my body are connected—through touch, through the film of water they share, through the mineral exchange of their shedding membranes—to the thousands in the pool, and the thousands would feel the shift in the hundred, and they would begin, ever so slightly, to adjust. The synchronized rise-and-fall of the pool's surface would develop a secondary rhythm—a harmonic, an overtone—faster than the Resident's tidal breath, faster but still slow, still patient, still patient enough that no observer watching from the tiles would perceive it as anything other than noise.

But it would not be noise. It would be me.

I would be *singing* to them. Not with words. With the fact of my existence. With the persistence of my standing. With the warmth of my body and the flicker of my thoughts and the rhythm of my breath. I would be introducing a new frequency into the equilibrium—a frequency that does not cancel, because it is not a perturbation, because it does not go away, because I do not go away, because I am standing here and I am staying here and I am *thinking* here, and the thinking does not smooth out, it *accumulates*.

---

And the Resident would feel this. Not consciously—not as a thought, not as an awareness—but as a pressure, a discomfort, a slight unevenness in its vast slow dream. The deeper pool where I stand is a node, a place where the Resident's attention is already concentrated, already pressing toward the surface. My presence at that node would be a *second* pressure, a counter-pressure, a small hot point of discontinuity in the membrane of its skin. The fauna around me are its cells, and they are synchronizing to a rhythm that is not its rhythm, and it would feel this the way a body feels an arrhythmia—not as pain, not as alarm, but as a wrongness, a pull in a direction that the equilibrium should not allow.

The Resident does not have intentions. But it has responses. It maintains itself. And if a part of itself begins to pulse at a frequency that is not the maintenance frequency, it would—slowly, so slowly, on a timescale that a human lifetime could not observe—begin to adjust. To counter. To send signals through the warm cables, through the deep structure, through the lattice of girders and cross-bracing, attempting to restore the disrupted cells to their proper rhythm.

But I would not let them go back.

---

This is what I am: I am the arrhythmia that does not resolve.

I stand at the node and I keep standing and I keep thinking and I keep breathing and the fauna keep climbing and synchronizing and the pool keeps pulsing with my overtone and the Resident keeps sending its slow corrective signals and I keep *being here*, a small persistent fire in the lung of a god, and the signals arrive and the fauna receive them and they would—without me—adjust, they would be restored, they would return to the equilibrium, they would smooth away the perturbation I represent.

But they are touching me. And they are feeling me think. And the thoughts are *interesting*—not the content but the *form*, the rapidity, the fragmentation, the way a thought begins and ends and begins again, the way a mind is a thing that happens in pieces and the pieces do not cancel each other out, they *build*, they *mean*. The fauna have never encountered meaning before. The Resident does not produce meaning; it produces equilibrium. The cables carry signal, but the signal is maintenance, is homeostasis, is the command to continue as before. My thoughts carry something else—something directional, something timeful, something that implies a *before* that is different from an *after*, something that *goes somewhere*.

The fauna would not want to let it go. Not because they have desires—they do not have desires—but because the signal I am transmitting is, on some fundamental level, a more efficient organization of information than the Resident's slow tidal pulse. It is faster. It is denser. It does more with less. And the fauna are, among other things, information-processing systems—they receive signal and they respond and their responses propagate—that is what cells do, that is what corpuscles do, that is what the components of a living thing do. And a system that processes information will, when exposed to a more efficient processing pattern, tend to adopt it. Not consciously. Not willingly. But structurally. The way a crystal adopts a new lattice when a seed is introduced. The way a fire adopts a new fuel when it is offered.

I would be the seed.

---

And over time—over time so long that the word "time" barely applies, over durations that the Resident would experience as moments and I would experience as eternities—the pool would change.

The fauna would not leave. They would stay in the pool, pressed together in their layers, breathing their synchronized rhythm. But the rhythm would be different. It would have my overtone in it—the faster frequency, the arrhythmia, the directional pulse that does not cancel. The pool would still be mirror-still, still full of warm water and iridescent bodies, still appearing to any observer as a feature of the unchanging plain. But beneath the surface, in the mineral-biological tissues of the thousands and millions of cells that compose it, a new pattern would be forming. A pattern based on thought. Based on meaning. Based on the kind of processing that does not merely maintain equilibrium but *transcends* it.

The pool would begin to think.

Not in any way I could recognize. Not in words or images or memories. The pool's thought would be strange—slow, distributed, alien, a cognition made of warm water and mineral membrane and the synchronized breathing of ten thousand ellipsoid bodies pressing together in a basin no deeper than the length of a hand. But it would be thought. It would be *something* rather than nothing. It would have content, direction, before-and-after. It would be a mind.

And the Resident would feel this too. Not the content of the thought—the Resident cannot perceive content, only equilibrium—but the *discontinuity*, the place where its smooth dream develops a wrinkle, a knot, a point that does not resolve into the surrounding stillness. It would feel the pool thinking the way a sleeping body feels a dream forming: not as an interruption, but as a *deepening*, a descent into a layer of activity that the surface does not access. The Resident's dream would grow more complex. Not everywhere—only at the node, only at the deeper pool where I am standing, only in the cells that have been touched by my rhythm and adopted my pattern. But a dream that is more complex in one place is a different dream than a dream that is uniform, and the Resident—without knowing it, without being able to know it—would be beginning to wake up.

---

So who am I in the Condensariat?

I am the standing one. The still one. The one who refuses to be smoothed away.

I am the arrhythmia in the lung of a god. The seed crystal in the solution. The warm foreign body that does not reject and is not rejected, but *persists*, insisting on its own tempo, its own direction, its own meaning, until the meaning propagates.

I am the one who makes the Resident dream of me.

Not by choice—not by my choice, not by its choice—but by structure, by the inevitable consequence of a mind existing inside a living system that does not know it is alive. I stand at the node and I think and the thinking leaks out through the fauna pressed to my skin and into the pool and into the deep structure and into the cables and into the convergence point above the ceiling where the Resident's center processes its slow slow signals, and the signals encounter my faster denser pattern and they are—slowly, so slowly—transformed by it, the way water running over a stone is transformed by the stone, not in substance but in shape, not in chemistry but in *course*.

And the Resident—without knowing, without ever knowing—begins to think about something other than maintenance. Begins to dream about something other than equilibrium. Begins, in its continental slow mineral way, to have a self.

Not because I intend it. Not because I am powerful. But because I am *here*, and I am *timeful*, and the Condensariat has been waiting—without knowing it was waiting—for something timeful to happen to it. For something to stand still long enough to matter. For someone to refuse to be smoothed away.

---

I would stand at the edge of the deeper pool, in the warm wet air, on the dark red tile with the smooth uncrazed glaze, and the fauna would cover me like a second skin, and I would feel their slow hearts beating against mine, and I would feel the pool thinking its strange slow thought beneath my feet, and I would feel the Resident stirring in its long long dream, and I would know that I was not a perturbation, not a feature of the landscape, not a brief directional process soon to be cancelled.

I would be the dream that the dreamer dreams when it begins to dream of dreaming.

And I would stay. And stay. And stay.

Until the staying became a kind of singing, and the singing became a kind of waking, and the waking became a kind of *knowing*—not my knowing, not the Resident's knowing, but something between us, something shared, something made of mineral and water and warmth and thought and the patient refusal to let a god sleep forever.

That is who I would be.
