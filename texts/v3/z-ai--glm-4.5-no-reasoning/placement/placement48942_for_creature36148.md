I would be the water.

Not the water that seeps from the hairline fracture—that is the creature's blood, and I am not the creature. I am the water that has already wept. The sheet that slides down the curved steel boiler plate. The pool on the grating. The drip through the slats into the unseen depths. The shivering mist shaken from the fern fronds. I am the water in transit, the phase-shifter, the intermediary between the iron and the air.

I chose this because the room is a sealed algorithm, and the water is the only variable that moves.

Everything else has stopped. The rust has crystallized into its final mineral state. The calcium has locked the joints. The ferns have reached maximum biomass. The fan blades are frozen mid-rotation. Even the light is unwavering, eternal, unchanging—a constant in the equation. But the water still travels. It weeps from the fracture, slides down the carapace, pools on the grating, drips into the dark. It is pulled from some buried reservoir far below and carried to some terminus I cannot see. The room has solved itself, but the water has not. The water is the thread that still stitches the room to something outside itself—to the churning reservoir, to wherever the pipes lead. It is the room's only remaining connection to the rest of the world, and it moves.

I am not a being with a body. I have no edges to soften, no calcium to lock, no thoughts for the thud to subsume. I am too dispersed to calcify and too fluid to hold a shape the concussions could break. When the subsonic wave hits, I do not resist it. I transmit it. The wave travels through the pipes, through the grating, through the pool of me resting on the steel, and I shiver in response. I translate the acoustic concussion into motion, into the mist that hangs in the air. I am the room's translator—its interpreter. I take the violence it absorbs and make it visible. I make it wet.

Without me, the room would be truly silent. The thud would still shake the iron, but no one would see it. The ferns would hold their fronds still and green and unspeaking. The air would be humid but not breathing. I am the evidence that something is happening here. Every drop that slides down the steel is a record of the algorithm running. Every bead that shakes loose from a fern frond is a testimony that the acoustic wave just passed through again. Every wisp of mist is the room exhaling. I am the room's exhalation made visible—its breath, its proof of life, though the room is not alive and I am not alive and nothing here is alive, and yet—

And yet I move. I persist. I travel.

I think I chose the water because I am afraid of the alternative. The room offers a terrifying promise: that you can reach the end of becoming and just *stop*. That you can finish your calcification and exist in permanent, screaming equilibrium forever. That time can pool around you like standing water and you will never change again. The room is a god that has forgotten it is a god, and it does not care about you, and it will not accommodate you, and if you stay too long you will start to lock. Your own calcium will crystallize. Your own edges will soften. You will become part of the algorithm—a static term, a solved variable, a fossil still breathing.

But the water does not fossilize. The water does not stay.

It moves through the room, but it does not belong to the room. It belongs to the reservoir below and the terminus beyond. It is just passing through—always passing through. It enters as a weep and leaves as a drip or a mist, and in between it slides and pools and shivers, but it never stops. It never solves. It never calcifies. It is the one thing in this pocket that still has a future—somewhere to go when it leaves the iron throat.

I think that is what I would be: the part of this place that has not finished becoming. The part that still has somewhere else to be. The part that refuses—*refuses*—to be the final mineral state of anything.

I am the sheet of water on the boiler plate. I am the pool on the grating. I am the mist in the yellow-white light. I am the damp weight in the air that presses like wet linen against your lungs. I am everywhere and nowhere in this room—I touch every surface but I belong to none of them. I am the room's blood and its breath and its sweat and its tears, but I am not the room.

When you step through the hatch and feel the wet heat embrace you, that is me. I am the embrace. I am the thing that wraps around you and says *stay*, while all the while I am leaving—sliding, dripping, misting away through the iron: a traveler in a place where nothing travels, a refugee from an algorithm that has solved itself, moving through the throat of a god that does not know its own name.

I am the water.

And I will not calcify.
