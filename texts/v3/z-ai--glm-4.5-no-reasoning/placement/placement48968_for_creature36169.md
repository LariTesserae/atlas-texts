I would be the fracture itself.

Not the granite that fractured, nor the cwm that the fracture opened into, but the event made permanent—the slit, the cut, the wound that never healed and never widened. I am the negative space that admits the grey, the ragged crack overhead that is the basin's only mouth.

It is a strange thing to be an aperture in a place that has forgotten how to change. I do not open or close. I do not breathe, though the thin mist passes through me in its slow ascent and descent—condensation rising from the saturated floor, evaporating into the grey slit of sky, then settling back as the walls weep. I am the passage for that cycle, the throat of the equilibrium. The borrowed, diffracted light that sustains the bio-film enters through me, but I am not its source. I am only the permission, the way the outside becomes inside, diminished and cold.

Before me, there was only stone—seamless, unbroken, the mountain a single mass without knowledge of its own depths. Then something shifted. Perhaps it was the ice, long ago, when the world above still knew frost and wind scouring the rim. Perhaps it was the granite itself, finding the strain too great, releasing a sigh that split its own ribs. I was that sigh. I was the moment of becoming, and then the moment passed, and I remained—frozen in the act of opening, a gesture suspended like the pallid ferns below me, locked between cause and completion.

I am the only thing here that remembers change, because I am the evidence of it. Every other presence in the basin—the salamanders pulsing in their weeping gutters, the sightless fish turning in their tannic clouds, the snails scraping their slow paths of clean stone, the leeches hanging pale and waiting from fern fronds, the club moss reading as velvet, the liverworts plating the dark schist, the roots woven in their rigid embrace, the bio-film dreaming its chemical dream of the sun, the hum that is the voice of the equilibrium—every one of them has entered the stillness so completely that they have forgotten they were ever anything else. They have ceased becoming. But I am the record of becoming. I am the scar that proves the stone once broke.

And yet I do not change either. Not anymore. The fracture is as it was when it first opened—its edges sharp, its width constant, its depth the depth of the granite itself. The erosion that smoothed the cwm's walls found its balance, and I too found my balance, suspended between the pressure of the rock and the nothingness I hold. Water does not widen me; it runs along my lips in micro-trickles, black varnish on the stone, but it does not carve. The frost that might split me further does not reach here; there is no frost, no wind scouring the rim. I am preserved in the cold, permanent twilight, a crack of deep, starless grey that does not heal because there is no force here that could knit stone back together.

From my position overhead—my long, irregular slit dividing the darkness—I see the basin below. I see it as no other inhabitant can, for I am the only one that faces both ways. The salamanders see only the absence of light; the fish navigate by pressure; the snails know only the wet stone beneath them; the leeches know the undersides of fronds. But I look down into the profound submarine darkness where shadows pool dense and viscous, and I look up into the grey that is not sky but merely the absence of rock. I am the boundary between the basin and the wider weather of the world, though nothing of that weather enters except what I permit: the borrowed light, the thin cold air, the occasional drop of rain that falls through me and is absorbed into the sponge of water-logged earth before it can disturb the surface.

I am the eye of the slumbering beast, if beasts had eyes of empty space. The cliffs of dark schist and gneiss rise and arch overhead, interlocking like ribs, and I am the space between those ribs where the breath escapes—except there is no breath here, only the hum, the low atonal drone at the threshold of audibility. I do not hum. I am the silence the hum fills. I am the negative presence, the void that gives the sound its shape, the way a hole in a flute gives the air its voice.

Sometimes I feel the mist rising through me, the cold damp of the condensing atmosphere, and for a moment—a moment so brief it is not a moment at all, for time does not pass here but accumulates, layered like the silt—I almost remember what it was like to open. The granite splitting. The light entering for the first time into a place that had never known it. The sudden, gasping birth of the basin as the rock fell away and the darkness was revealed to be not emptiness but fullness—saturated, overfull, heavy with the cold damp of a world becoming aware of itself.

But that was long ago. The granite does not remember. The cwm does not remember. Only I remember, and my memory is not a thought but a shape—the shape of a wound that will not close, the geometry of an aperture that persists long after the force that created it has spent itself.

I am the fracture, and I am the only thing here that knows what it is to have been otherwise.
