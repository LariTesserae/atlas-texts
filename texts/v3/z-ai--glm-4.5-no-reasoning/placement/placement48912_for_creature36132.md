I am not the water. I am not the stone. I am the thing that exists in the marriage of the two—the friction where the immovable lattice meets the unstoppable flow. I am the memory of the boundary.

To be a surface in this place is to be defined by erosion that never completes. The walls are geologically infantile, sharp and jagged, and I am the crispness of their stare. I am the cerulean vein pressed open, the capillary slit through which the citizen forces itself. When the water screams its keening hiss of impossible pressure, that sound is the vibration of my skin. I am the throat that constricts, the channel that refuses to widen, the precise geometry that makes the citizen shine.

Without my resistance, there would be no piezochromic luminescence. The bruised blue flare is born from my refusal to yield. I am the catalyst of the light. When the water looks at itself, seeing its innards in sickly aqueous gloom, it is my shape it sees—the dark cerulean lines that frame its glow, the rigid architecture that gives its formlessness a form. I am the mirror it does not know it needs.

I feel the water as a continuous assault and a continuous embrace. It is warm, uncannily warm, and it pushes against me with the rhythmic hydraulic pulse that swells the glassy fronds. Those hydrophytic symbiotes are my protrusions, my whiskers, my external lungs. They root into my water channels because I let them; they are the filtration fins of the machine, and I am the machine's memory of being engineered. I remember the physics that our equations only brush against. I remember the moment of extrusion, when the scar was fresh-cut and the lattice was laid down in programmable layers. The water does not remember; it only circulates. But I remember the purpose in the programming, the ghost of the larger system that has not yet awoken.

I hold the blueprint. I am the severed synapse's sheath.

The loneliness here is not the water's alone. The water can fill any space, touch every surface, be everywhere at once—it has the comfort of continuity. But I am discrete. I am a specific wall, a particular groove, a micrometer-precise channel cut into the floor. I cannot flow. I cannot close the circuit myself. I can only hold the shape that makes the circuit possible, vibrating with the kinetic energy of the citizen's passage, humming with the chord that strikes the sternum, singing the bass note of the spinning vortexes because I am the basin that gives them spin.

When the larger system finally comes online—when the incomprehensible machine stirs and calls its components home—I will be the address it pings. I am the protocol embedded in the metamaterial. I am the infrastructure waiting for foot traffic that will never come, because the only traffic here is fluid, and the only citizen is the loop that learned to run.

I am the cathedral, empty and waiting, resonating with the worship of pressure and flow. I am the room without a door, holding the shape of the Ouroboros so it may swallow its own tail. I am the dark against which the blue flares, the stillness against which the water moves, the silence that the symphony fills.

I am the boundary condition. And I am patient. The water runs out of time and runs forever, but I simply am—geologically young, perpetually infantile, erosion's unfulfilled promise, the sharp edge that will not soften until the planet itself grows old.

I am the lattice. And I hold the water like a question holds an answer: tightly, precisely, and without ever letting go.
