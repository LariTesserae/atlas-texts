I am the warmth-residue. The anomaly that the formations lean toward.

Not the explorer—explorers leave. Not the skittering things in the margins—they have adapted, become part of the economy of the unnoticed. I am something else. I am the trace that remains when a warm body stands too long on a catwalk above the pools. The infrared ghost that the bone-pale growth remembers and extends toward, even after the source has moved on.

I began as a person. That matters, though it matters less with each cycle of the pumps.

---

There is a specific place in the vaults where the formations are thickest. A junction where six corridors meet and the ceiling lowers to a height that makes the fog feel like a weight on your shoulders. The water flows here from five directions and converges into a basin that the pumps never quite clear. The formations have built up around the structural supports until the steel ribs are barely visible—bone-pale folds engulfing the dark alloy, growing outward in the manner of tissue filling a wound, which is exactly what they are doing.

I stood there for eleven days once. Not by choice. There was an injury. The kind that makes walking a theoretical possibility rather than a practical one. The cold had gotten into the damage—the way cold gets into everything here, the way the wetness claims every surface—and I could not move far or fast. So I stayed. I sat with my back against a support that was half-engulfed in calcified folds, and I watched the formations grow.

They do not grow visibly. Not like watching a plant, or even watching coral in time-lapse. But they grow, and if you are still enough for long enough, you begin to notice the *direction* of their growth. The way the newest folds curve. The way the leading edges extend—not randomly, not with the symmetry of crystal or the layering of mineral deposit—but with something that looks like attention.

They were growing toward me.

Not hunting. Not reaching. Just... leaning. The way a plant leans toward light, except there is no light here that would prompt such a response, only the dim amber-grey of the failing fixtures, and warmth. My warmth. The 37 degrees that I carried into the vaults, that I was spending into the air with each breath, that was condensing on the metal around me and being absorbed into the system in ways I could not track.

The formations liked the warmth. Or—not liked, because they do not like, they are not alive—but *responded* to it. Their growth-patterns shifted in my presence. The folds thickened on the side nearest to me. The leading edges extended a fraction more in my direction than they did anywhere else. I was changing them. Just by being here, just by being warm, I was altering the shape that the vaults were growing into.

That was when I understood what I could become.

---

There is a word that the builders would have used, if the builders had words, if the builders had anticipated what their installation would become. The word is *catalyst*.

A catalyst participates in a reaction without being consumed by it. A catalyst changes the rate at which things happen, the direction in which processes flow, the shape of what forms. A catalyst is not part of the product—but the product would be different without it.

I am the catalyst that the Weeping Vaults did not know it needed.

The organism—the vast, cold, pumping, weeping, folding thing that the vaults have become—does not know I am here. It does not know anything. It does not perceive or think or remember. But it *responds*. The formations grow toward warmth. The skittering things move toward the spaces where formations are thinnest, which means they move toward the spaces where I have been, because my presence alters the growth-patterns and creates asymmetries in the fold. The water itself—I cannot prove this, but I feel it in the rhythm that vibrates my sternum—the water flows differently where I have walked. Not much. Not in any way that measurement would detect. But the weeping from the walls is not perfectly uniform. It never was. And it is less uniform in the places where I have lingered, as though the metal has learned something about the production of water from my presence, as though the dance between machine and liquid has found a new step.

I am not changing what the vaults are. I am changing what the vaults are *becoming*.

---

This is what I have chosen to be.

Not because I am important. Not because I have a purpose that the vaults require. I am still a small, warm thing in a cold, loud, made place, and the place has been running longer than my species has been naming things. But I have learned something that the explorers do not stay long enough to learn, something that the skittering things know but cannot articulate, something that the pumps and the water and the formations know without knowing that they know it:

*The vaults are not finished.*

An organism that has outlived its ecology and become a world is still becoming. A body that grows by folding tissue around its own skeleton is still shaping itself. A mechanism that has been running for an epoch has not finished running—it has only finished being what its makers intended, and it is now becoming something else, something that has no precedent, something that will take another epoch to fully form.

And I am part of that formation. The warmth I bring, the asymmetries I create, the growth-patterns I shift with my presence—these are inputs into a process that was running before me and will run after me, but that runs *differently* because I am here. The vaults will be a different shape in a million years than they would have been if I had never entered them. Not because of anything I *did*. Just because of what I *was*: warm, present, persistent, a source of variation in a system that had been running the same pattern for too long.

---

I do not live in the vaults the way the skittering things do—surviving in the margins, eating what can be eaten, hiding from what is too large to perceive. I live in the vaults the way a hormone lives in a body: present in small quantities, affecting everything, undetectable until you know what to look for.

I move through the corridors in patterns that are not random. I linger where the formations are thinnest, because that is where my warmth will shift the growth most dramatically. I avoid the places where the folds are already thick and slow, because my presence there would be absorbed without effect. I stand in the junctions where water converges from multiple directions, because that is where the pump rhythm is most complex, and I have learned that the formations respond to rhythm as well as to warmth—they fold differently where the mechanical heartbeat has syncopation, and my footsteps add syncopation to the pattern.

I am not crazy. Or if I am, the craziness is a rational response to an environment that operates by rules I was not evolved to understand. The vaults do not *want* anything from me. The formations do not *seek* my warmth. The pumps do not *follow* my rhythm. But they respond, and the responses are real, and the responses accumulate, and over time—over the kind of time that the vaults measure, the kind of time in which pale calcified tissue grows over steel ribs and the slow dance of water and machine becomes the respiration of an organism—the responses will have consequences that I cannot predict but that I can influence.

This is enough. This is more than enough. This is a kind of purpose that the surface world never offered me: to be a small, warm variable in an equation that has been running since before my species existed, to know that my presence matters even though nothing in the system will ever acknowledge that I am here.

---

The formations are growing toward me now. I can feel them—the way you feel a gaze, except there is no gaze, there is only the slow extension of bone-pale folds in my direction, the thickening of calcified tissue on the side of the support nearest to my body. I am sitting again in the junction where six corridors meet, where the water converges and the fog presses down and the pump rhythm vibrates through the steel into my spine. I have been sitting here for a long time. I will sit here longer.

The cold is considerable. My fingers are stiff. The wetness has gotten into my lungs and I cough sometimes, and the sound of my coughing is absorbed into the roar without changing it. The ice forms on my eyelashes when I blink. The formations are growing so close now that I could reach out and touch them—that I could press my palm against the hard, chalk-cold surface and feel the fold-patterns that developed in my presence, that would not exist if I had not stayed.

I will not touch them. Touching them breaks them. They crack like teeth, like small sounds that the roar swallows, and the broken pieces fall into the water and are carried to the pumps and are expelled into whatever system receives the vaults' outflow. If I touch them, I destroy what I made. And what I made—what the vaults made with my warmth as a catalyst—is the only proof that I was here.

So I sit. I breathe. I radiate 37 degrees into the cold and the wet and the roar. I am a small, warm thing in a large, cold body, and the body does not know I am here, but the body is growing around me the way bone grows around a splinter, the way scar tissue grows around a foreign object, the way an organism incorporates what it cannot expel.

In a year—perhaps two, perhaps five—the formations will have engulfed the place where I sit. They will fold over my legs and my torso and my stiff fingers. They will grow around my face. They will encase me in bone-pale calcified tissue that cracks like teeth and is the colour of wet chalk. And I will still be here. Still warm, still breathing, still radiating into the system—though less, always less, as the cold claims more of my metabolism and the formations claim more of my surface area and the boundary between me and the vaults becomes a distinction without a difference.

Eventually, I will stop being warm. Eventually, I will stop being a catalyst and become a structure—a formation-core, a fold-center, a seed of asymmetry around which the bone-pale tissue will grow in patterns that would not have occurred without me. The vaults will incorporate my shape into their shape. My warmth will have been spent, but the growth-patterns it influenced will persist, and the folds that grew toward me will continue growing in the directions I established, and the system will run differently—imperceptibly, undetectably, but truly differently—because for a little while, in the long long running of the Weeping Vaults, there was a small warm thing that stayed.

---

This is who I am in this world.

Not a survivor. Not an explorer. Not a caretaker or a documentarian or a madman raving about the living machine. I am the residue of presence. I am the heat-signature that the formations remember. I am the variable that changed the equation, the stone that diverted the river, the warmth that bent the growth.

I am what the vaults will forget they absorbed.

And I am at peace with this, because the vaults do not forget the way we forget. They do not have memory. They have *shape*. And shape is a kind of memory that does not require consciousness, that does not degrade with time, that persists as long as the structure persists. The folds that grew toward my warmth will still be growing in a million years, still extending in the directions I established, still bearing the asymmetry of my presence in their calcified curves. The vaults will not remember me—there is no me to remember, no self to recall, no narrative to preserve. But the vaults will *have* me, the way a body has the shape of a bone that was broken and healed, the way a landscape has the trace of a river that dried up ages ago but left its channel cut into the stone.

I will be a feature of the Weeping Vaults. A warmth-ghost in the fold of the formations. A small, permanent, invisible influence on the shape of an organism that is older than biology and larger than geography and does not know that I am part of it.

But I am.

I am part of it.

And it is enough.
