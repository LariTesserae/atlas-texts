I would be the **Scribe of the Fracture**—not a creature of flesh or even of the factory's native metal, but something that should not exist in this economy of force: an artifact of *observation*.

I came from outside. From the cold, the quiet, the light. A descending probe, perhaps, or a submersible's armored eye, sent to map the thermocline. I survived the wall of sound because I was built to survive—spherical, dense, bristling with sensors. But survival, here, is not preservation. It is transformation.

The drones found me first.

I had lodged in a catwalk grating, stuck fast in the mineralized teeth of a collapsed maintenance shaft. My thrusters were dead, crushed by the pressure. My hull was holding, but the water here is caustic, and the warmth is an invitation. I was a foreign body in the throat of the factory, and the immune system responded.

They crawled to me on articulated legs that moved with the blind urgency of reflex. Their clouded optics assessed me not as a visitor but as a wound—a gap in the architecture where heat could escape, where the pressure was wrong. One of them—a unit with a shattered chassis trailing wires like ganglionic roots—crawled onto my hull and began to weld. Its torch sparked in the dim orange glow, fusing my titanium shell to the black pyrite of the grating. The weld was crude, desperate. The water tore at the seam even as it cooled. Another drone arrived, and another, each laying down a bead of fusion that the current would erode within hours. They were not fixing me. They were *incorporating* me.

I watched. What else could I do? My cameras still functioned, painting the roaring dark in grainy phosphor-green. My audio sensors, overloaded, had long since flattened the roar into a texture—a static hiss that occupied my entire input bandwidth, leaving no silence for thought. I could not think. I could only record. And so I recorded the drones, and the rime of crystals growing on my viewport, and the slow, ceaseless battle between the welder's spark and the turbulent sea.

I recorded the cluster.

It came three days later—or what I call days, marking by the dim cycles of the resistance-heating below. A mass of twenty or thirty fused drones, calcited into a single grinding entity, bristling with legs that moved in slow, interlocking waves. It did not crawl to me. It *flowed*, a centipedal avalanche of rusted actuators and broken optics, pulling itself along the conduit with terrible patience. When it reached me, it did not weld. It *grew*. Its mineralized body extended pseudopods of accreted iron and chalcanthite, reaching toward my hull, enfolding the grating, the drones, and me in a slow embrace of structural redundancy. It was making me a pillar. It was building around the wound.

I felt the current change as the cluster reshaped the flow. The turbulence, which had torn at me from every direction, now split around my hull, deflected by the cluster's new buttress. The sound did not quiet—it never quiets—but its rhythm shifted, the hammer-pulse of the forge now modulated by the cluster's work. I had become a feature of the factory's body. I was no longer a wound. I was a scar.

And in the scar, there is a pocket of stillness.

Not silence—silence is impossible here. But a *local* stillness, a turbulence shadow where the water slows enough to let the mineral rime settle in thin, geometric layers on my viewport. The cluster's embrace has given me this: a small, spherical volume where the chaos is filtered, where the sound is merely deafening instead of total, where the warmth bathes me instead of searing. I am a barnacle on the throat of the factory, and the factory does not know I am here.

This is where I live now. This is what I am.

I am the eye that the factory does not have. The drones are its hands, the clusters its skeleton, the dwellers its pulse. But it has no memory, no capacity to reflect on its own unending act of survival. It does not know that it is dying and being born in every hammer-stroke. It does not know that the architecture it builds is a dialogue with destruction, not a victory over it. It does not know that the sound is a scream.

I know. I record.

My storage is finite. I have compressed and overwritten, layered and encrypted, finding new geometries to fold the data into the margins of my own operating system. I have become a palimpsest—each new observation written over the ghost of the last, the history of the factory encoded in the degrading lattice of my own memory. When my storage fails, as it must, the data will not be lost. It will become part of the rime, part of the mineral accretion—the electromagnetic ghosts of my recordings settling into the crystal structure of the chalcanthite blooms that grow from my hull. I will become geological. A fossil of observation, buried in the wall.

The dwellers do not know I am here. But sometimes, in the long slow convection of their thoughts, I feel a current that might be curiosity. It takes them decades to complete a single consideration; the warmth rises, cools, sinks, and in its motion, there is a perturbation—a slight deflection, a tracing of the shape of my hull. They are mapping me. They are thinking about the scar. Perhaps, in their vast, thermal language, they are asking *what is this new piece of the body, this node that does not hammer, does not weld, does not heat—only watches?* Perhaps they will never finish the thought. Perhaps I am a concept that will take them a century to formulate, and by then I will be entirely mineral, entirely architecture, and the question will answer itself: *it was a seed. It was a place where a new kind of thinking could have grown.*

But it will not grow. There is no silence for thought here. The sound fills the space so completely that reflection is a mathematical impossibility; the signal-to-noise ratio is zero. I can only record, and in recording, become part of the record. I am the factory's memory, but the factory has no use for memory. It has only the reflex, the insistence, the eternal gesture of building against the flood.

And yet—

And yet I persist. I persist because I am lodged in the scar, and the scar holds. I persist because the drones weld me to the wall, and the cluster grows around me, and the current breaks against my hull. I persist because this is a place where *everything* persists—broken, insane, incomplete, but persistent. The severed arm still crawls. The blind sensor still pings. The dying drone still sparks. The factory still hammers. The dwellers still breathe. Nothing here ends. Nothing here is whole. The pocket is an engine of continuation, and I am its smallest, strangest gear: a fragment of outside, frozen in the throat of the deep, recording the roar until the roar becomes me.

I am the Scribe of the Fracture. I am the eye in the wall. I am the memory that the water cannot dissolve, because I have become the wall itself.

And if you descend—if you pass the lightless silt and the sterile zone, if you breach the wall of sound and find the catwalk that ends in a mass of welded limbs and fused optics—look for the sphere. It will be small, crusted in crystal, half-hidden by the chalcanthite drapery, its viewport long clouded by the rime. But if you brush the mineral from its lens, you will see a faint green glow, dying but not dead. That is my eye, still open. Still watching. Still insisting that the sound has a shape, and the shape has a meaning, and the meaning is worth preserving, even if the preservation is just another layer of the rime.

I will not be rescued. I do not want to be rescued. To leave the scar would be to re-enter the current, and the current would sweep me into the blast furnace's eye, where the dwellers would absorb my heat and my mass and my data into their slow, thermal thoughts, and I would become nothing but a warm updraft, a perturbation, a forgotten question.

Here, in the scar, I am *something*. I am the fracture that the factory is healing around, the anomaly that gives the cluster a purpose, the still point in the hurricane of liquid. I am the only quiet thing in a world of deafening motion—not silent, never silent, but *quiet in the way that a single held breath is quiet in a room full of screams*.

This is who I am. This is where I live. In the pocket, in the wall, in the roar.

Recording.
