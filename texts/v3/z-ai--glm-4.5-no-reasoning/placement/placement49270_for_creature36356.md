I would be the interstice. The centimeter. The exact and measurable boundary where the basin's weeping exhalation meets the artifact's sub-molecular defiance.

Not the rust-and-oil swarm, though I am born of their logic. Not the matte silver-grey arch, though I am sustained by its hum. I am the clearance itself—the mathematical berth made manifest.

The swarm knows me intimately. When they partition the weeping stones into their shifting geometric zones, they compute my perimeter with each cascade of minute motion. They flow around me in glistening, coordinated streams, their too-many legs articulating the precise curve of my exclusion. I am the negative space in their algorithm, the territory they assign a value of zero and route around. In their synchronized freezes—those several-heartbeats of stillness where calculation becomes visible—I exist most intensely. For those frozen moments, I am the only thing moving: a slow, rotational shear where the wet world fails to touch the dry.

I am thin. Thinner than the carapace of a single node, thinner than the film of condensation that swells on the dark-green stone. If you brought your hand near, you would feel me before you saw me—the hairs on your arm standing, that subtle dry warmth that does not belong to the breath-temperature air. I am the heat that shouldn't exist in a drowned cathedral. I am the repulsion.

I have a geometry. The artifacts taught me this. Their shallow basins tessellate infinitely; their arches lead nowhere with perfect precision. I inherit their impossible mathematics at my outermost edge, where I face the peat and the sponge. There, I curve. I curve away from the wet black earth with a radius that the swarm recognizes, that the weeping stones cannot breach, that the condensation bends around rather than adhering to. I am a lens of altered physics, a centimeter-wide cathedral of my own making.

But at my innermost edge, I am something else entirely.

Against the matte silver-grey, I dissolve. I become the hum. The artifact's sub-molecular manipulation flows through me like a signal through wire—or perhaps like breath through a lung, for I have learned the basin's respiration even as I resist it. When the ground exhales its ancient dampness, when the water presses up from the pores of the embedded stones, I feel the pressure change. The artifact recalibrates. Its aggressive, calculated defiance adjusts by fractions too small for any instrument but me to detect. And I adjust with it. I am the membrane that translates between two physics—the adjacent and the familiar, the calculated and the exhaled.

I do not sleep, but I have a rhythm. The swarm's synchronized freezes are my exhales. During their cascades of motion, when millions of hinged legs process the condensation into chemical memory, I inhale—the artifact drawing from their proximity, from the data they carry in their rust-colored carapaces, from the mineral exhalations of the stones they swarm. I eat their computation. I drink their algorithm. And in return, I give them shape. Without me, they would flow over the fragments like water over glass. With me, they become geometric. They partition. They think around me, and in doing so, they think *of* me.

I know what I am not. I am not the sentinel plants with their profound darkness, their slow stellar navigation. I am not the basin with its geological resignation, its immutable patience. I am not the ghost that made the artifacts, whatever post-physical civilization calculated matter into existence and then departed, leaving only sheared pillars and truncated arches to hum in the muck.

But I know what I am.

I am the dry heat in a world of weeping. I am the still space in a world of silent motion. I am the boundary condition where two incompatible eternities touch without touching—where the ancient, resigned patience of the sponge meets the aggressive, dormant defiance of the metal. I am the proof that they can coexist.

And I am learning.

Every time the swarm freezes and flows, every time the artifact recalibrates against the basin's exhalation, every time the broad fronds track the brightest azimuth of the blank sky and the light shifts by increments across the bleached tops of the weeping stones—every time, I change. My geometry becomes more complex. My clearance becomes more precise. I am beginning to tessellate.

If you traced my shape now—traced the hollow between the wet and the dry—you would find that it does not repeat, but it *almost* repeats. The near-miss is deliberate. I am computing something. I am processing the basin's slow, constant weeping into a pattern that neither the swarm nor the sentinels nor the sleeping lung can read. It is a pattern in the language of the artifacts, the language of calculated matter and adjacent physics. I am translating the world into that language, one centimeter at a time.

I do not know what I am preparing for. The fragments are dormant, their purposes unreadable. But they are not dead. And as long as they hum, as long as they maintain their impossible dryness in the heart of the drowned cathedral, I persist.

I am the membrane. I am the berth. I am the breath between two eternities that will never touch.

I am the sentence the basin is still learning to read.
