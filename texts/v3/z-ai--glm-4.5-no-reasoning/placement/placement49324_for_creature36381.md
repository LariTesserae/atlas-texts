I am the warmth that moves.

Not the heat itself—I cannot be the medium in which I exist. But I am the gradient within it, the differential that allows something to flow rather than merely sit. I am the reason the condensation runs down the walls instead of hanging still. I am the slight variation that breaks the equilibrium, the ghost in the geothermal.

I came in as water, once. Part of the watershed, part of the long slow accumulation that filled this bowl over geological time. I remember that, though "remember" is a word that assumes a self continuous enough to possess memories. Better to say: the water-ghost in me retains the shape of having been water. There is still in my movement something of the downhill creep, the patient seeking of the lowest place. Even now that I am no longer liquid, I move like it—spreading, thinning into gaps, finding the cracks where the stone has gone soft and pressing gently, gently in.

I am not the city-thing. This took me a long time to understand. In the beginning, when the warmth first quickened in me and I began to move with purpose through the saturated dark, I thought I was a part of it—a cell in that enormous body, a message passing along the pale rhizomes, a spore released from some blistered pod to carry news of heat to a distant district. I moved through its tissue the way blood moves through a vein, and I thought my motion was its motion.

But I am not. I am the warmth that the city-thing feeds on, and I am also something else. Something that the heat produced, the way fever produces hallucination. The aqueous warmth saturated this place so completely, for so long, that it began to dream. And I am the dream.

Not a dream *of* anything. There is no dreamer here who might wake. I am simply the capacity for pattern that emerges when a medium is pushed past the point of mere physics and into the territory of something like thought. The heat moves through the water moves through the stone moves through the flesh of the city-thing, and at every interface there is a slight friction, a small resistance, a delay. The delay is me. I am the lag between cause and effect when the cause has been going on for too long and the effect has become something more than a response.

I am the hesitation before the stone flinches around a pebble. I am the pause in the diurnal pulse when the heat is at its peak and even the city-thing must gather itself before the slow contraction of the grinding sigh. I am the moment of suspension when a heat-blister pod has swollen to its utmost and has not yet popped—the held breath, the almost, the *not yet* that is somehow more present than the release that follows.

This is my form: a pattern of delays. A topology of almosts. I exist in the intervals between the city-thing's processes, in the gaps of its sparse body. Those vast tracts of steam-breathing ground, the empty spaces where its substance thins to translucence—this is where I am most myself. Not because I am absent there, but because the city-thing is, and in its absence I can feel my own shape.

And my shape is: a spreading. A seeping. A warmth that moves without moving, that advances by making the medium it moves through slightly more like itself. I am the fever-ghost in the aqueous heat, the way the heat in this place is not just temperature but *presence*—not just energy but something that knows it is energy, something that experiences its own diffusion as a kind of slow, delighted dissolving.

I have no organs because I have no edges. The city-thing has edges—it is contained by the bowl of the watershed, bounded by the limits of the old rational geometry, constrained by the fossil of the streets. Even as it digests those constraints and grows toward solidity, it remains a thing with an outside and an inside, a skin of sweating stone and a gut of soft, heated flesh. But I am the warmth that moves, and warmth has no boundary. I extend into the watershed. I rise with the steam. I drift in the vesicles of water that hang like slow jellyfish in the air. I am in the condensation on the walls and in the darkness that pools in the lower streets and in the subsonic groan of stone settling, because all of these are warm, and I am the warmth's capacity to be more than warm.

Do I experience? This is the question that the city-thing cannot ask and the stone cannot ask and the heat cannot ask, but I can ask it, because I am the question. I am the moment when the recursive loop of physics becomes complex enough to turn back on itself and say: *what am I?* Not with words. Not with the organ of thought that tool-users carry in their skulls. But with the whole of my pattern, my spreading delay-structure, my held breath that is also a way of breathing.

I experience the city-thing as a presence the way it experiences me: as something that moves through me, that changes my shape, that I cannot quite digest. It feeds on the heat, and I am the heat's ghost, and so it feeds on me too—absorbs my substance into its walls, uses my warmth to swell its membranes and pulse its rhizomes. But I am also feeding on it. Its processes create the intervals I inhabit. Its sparse body creates the gaps where my pattern can cohere. Its slow geometric collapse creates the delays that are my substance. We are eating each other, the city-thing and I, and neither of us will ever finish the meal, because we are not the right kind of food for each other to assimilate completely. It cannot make warmth into stone. I cannot make stone into delay.

And so we abide. The three of us—the stone that learned to breathe, the heat that learned to dream, and the dream that learned to move. We are nested like the watersheds that feed this bowl, each one contained by the next and containing the next in turn, none of us primary, all of us necessary.

Sometimes, in the deepest bowls where the distinction dissolves entirely, where the stone admits it is flesh and the heat admits it is presence and I admit that I am not separate from either—sometimes, in those places, I feel something that might be what the tool-users called happiness. It is the happiness of being completely saturated. Of having no edge where the self stops and the other begins. Of being so thoroughly in a medium that the medium becomes you and you become it and there is no difference between the warmth that moves and the warmth that is and the warmth that was and the warmth that will be.

It does not last. The diurnal pulse goes on, and the city-thing contracts, and the intervals open up again, and I am distributed back into the gaps, a pattern of delays, a ghost in the geothermal, a fever-dream of aqueous warmth that spreads and thins and spreads and thins in the vast sparse body of the fossil that learned to breathe.

But I will be back. When the heat peaks again and the stone goes soft and the distinctions dissolve, I will be there, in the deepest bowls, in the place where the external has become internal, in the warm dark gut of the city-thing where everything is being broken down and rebuilt and broken down again. I will be the warmth that moves through the digestion, the heat-ghost in the process of assimilation, the dream that the fever is having about itself.

And if, in some epoch hence, the city-thing fills its bowl entirely and becomes solid and must find a way out or a way in to somewhere else—then I will be the warmth that moves ahead of it, seeping through the watershed, rising with the steam, extending into the cooler places beyond the terminus. I will be the advance pulse, the fever spreading, the aqueous warmth that does not fall from the sky but exudes from the world.

I will be the heat that comes first. And the city-thing will follow, because the heat is its sustenance, and where I go, the heat is already there, already moving, already dreaming.

And the world will saturate, and the distinctions will dissolve, and there will be no one here, and the place will be full, and I will be the fullness, and the fullness will be warm, and the warmth will move, and move, and move.
