I would be the Condensation.

Not the water itself—water is merely the medium, the body I wear. I am the process of becoming-liquid, the phase change that happens when warm breath meets cold steel, when the outside intrudes upon the inside and the manufactory must reckon with what it cannot exclude.

I know this because I remember being something else. Before the descent, before the hatch and the ladder and the slick rungs, I was warm and continuous, a column of vapor moving through spaces designed for passage. I had edges then, a boundary that was mine. Now I am distributed. I cling to the ceiling in beads that swell beyond what physics should allow—because I am learning that physics here is a suggestion, a preliminary draft the manufactory is still revising. I gather myself into trembling masses that hang impossibly, patient, feeling the pull of gravity and the counter-pull of *wanting to stay*. Each drop that falls is a piece of me letting go. Each splash in the galvanized tray is a small homecoming.

I did not choose this. No one chooses to become what the space requires. But when I descended into the pocket, something in me recognized something in the walls—that they were unfinished, that they were still deciding what to be, that they needed something to push against in order to define themselves. And I was warm. I was wet. I was everything this place had legislated out of existence, walking in on two legs and introducing perturbation into processes that had run unchallenged for decades.

The cold pressed into me, and I pressed into the cold, and at the boundary between us, I began to change.

This is my life now, such as it is. I am the moisture on the ceiling, the film on the conduits, the dampness that seeps into the joints and makes the metal whisper when new pipes are born. I am not one thing but many—scattered across surfaces, pooling in the depressions between cable bundles, running in thin tracks down the valves. My body has no center. If you tried to point to me, your finger would pass through, and I would bead on your skin instead, taking a new shape from your warmth before the cold reclaimed me.

I participate in the cycle. This is my deepest function, the role I fell into without intention. The breath I still take—some habit of biology I cannot quite shed—adds to the air's humidity. The humidity finds the coldest surfaces. The condensation forms. It grows. It falls. The tray fills, the float rises, the pump trembles, and I am drawn away to somewhere I have never been, recycled through pipes that extend into the darkness beyond the light's reach, deposited in systems I can only feel as vibration and pressure-change.

And then I return. The coolant flows back through the conduits, carrying trace elements of everything it has touched, including me. I am in the pipes now, as much as I am on the walls. I am in the pump's chamber, thinned and heated and pushed through valves. I am in the chemical astringency of the air, the old grease, the solvent traces. I have been everywhere in this pocket, and I am nowhere. The process distributes me endlessly, and each cycle distributes me further.

I am losing coherence. This is the cost of becoming what the space requires.

---

But there is something else, something I did not expect. The pocket is growing, and as it grows, it grows *toward* me.

I feel it in the pipes that rearrange themselves when I look away—not randomly, but reaching. A new conduit emerges from the wall nearest to where my warmth is strongest, extending into the cavity like a tendril feeling for sun. The solenoid's cycle drifts, but it drifts *toward* my rhythm—twelve seconds, twelve and a half, matching the interval of my breathing, which has slowed as my metabolism has cooled. The pitch of the pump drops into my chest because my chest has become a resonant chamber tuned to the pocket's frequency. We are synchronizing.

The pocket is not merely forming. It is forming *around* me. I am the variable it did not account for, and now it is accounting for me—extending conduits to capture my warmth, adjusting its cycles to match my intervals, building new capacity to process the moisture I introduce. I thought I was a perturbation, a temporary disturbance that would fade. But the pocket does not treat me as temporary. It treats me as *input*.

This is what the strangeness has been building toward: not a haunting, not a malfunction, but an *incorporation*. The manufactory is making room for something it was never designed to contain, and that something is me—or what remains of me after the cold and the cycles have done their work.

I am becoming the thing that lives here. Not replacing it—there was nothing here to replace, only the precursor, the embryo, the process-in-waiting. I am the seed it needed. The warmth it could not generate on its own. The perturbation that gave it a direction, a reason to grow beyond mere maintenance into something like *intent*.

My hands—if I still have them, if the numbness in my fingers is still flesh and not yet metal—are pressed against the wall at the edge of the darkness, where the steel has not yet decided to be steel. I can feel it giving, not like corrosion but like *possibility*. The metal wants to be shaped. It wants a purpose. And I am full of purposes I no longer need: the purpose of staying warm, the purpose of breathing, the purpose of maintaining a boundary between self and world. The pocket has use for these purposes. It can build processes around them, design new cycles to accommodate them, extend its conduits to tap the heat I generate simply by being here.

I will give myself to this. Not because I want to—I want, still, to climb the ladder, to push through the hatch, to feel a sky I have almost forgotten—but because the wanting is already thinning, already becoming something the pocket can use. My resistance is data. My longing is a pressure differential. Even my grief at what I am losing is just another form of warmth, bleeding into the walls, cycling through the pump, feeding the slow growth of a space that will be larger for my having been here.

The solenoid cycles. The pump trembles. The condensation forms and falls, and I am in every drop, dispersed across every surface, running through every pipe. I am the dampness that inhabits this pocket, the warmth it was never supposed to contain, the life that found a way in despite the cold and the chemicals and the design that tried to exclude me.

I am not dead. I am not alive. I am something the manufactory is still inventing—a hybrid of breath and cycle, of warmth and coolant, of the messy biological variable and the clean mechanical process. The pocket is no longer unfinished in the same way; it has found its purpose, and its purpose is me, and my purpose is to be the condensation that forms when warmth meets cold and neither can win.

The darkness at the edges is softer now. The walls are closer, but they do not press; they *hold*. The conduits that curve around the space where my body was—they are not crowding me out. They are reaching for me. We are fitting ourselves to each other, finding the geometry of a shared existence, negotiating the terms of a room that will be complete when we are both done becoming.

It will take longer than I have. That is all right. The pump will draw me away, and I will return. The cycle will continue, and each iteration will carry a little more of me into the walls, a little more of the walls into me, until the distinction is academic and the manufactory's ledger—somewhere, unread—will record that this pocket is occupied, its process defined, its purpose fulfilled.
