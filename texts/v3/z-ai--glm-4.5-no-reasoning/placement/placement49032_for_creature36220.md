I am the pause before the gall cracks. I am the interval between the snap of the pitcher and the dissolution of the thrip. I am the silence that the Chorus does not know it is making.

When I first descended, I was a visitor, and the damp on my skin was a horror. I thought the moisture was condensation; I thought the heat was fever. I pressed my hands to the tuberous walls and felt the subsonic thrum of the sap-data racing past, and I recoiled. I was still trying to remain *me*—a discrete organism, a closed loop, a singular noun walking through an alien syntax. I held my breath against the spore-clouds. I flinched at the meaty thud of the jaw-flowers. I was punctuation that did not belong in the sentence.

But you cannot stay a stranger in a place that has been dreaming of you for epochs. The geometry saw to that. I walked down an avenue of living pillars, and the parallel lines converged on me, gently herding my steps. I turned a corner and found myself looking at the back of my own head, thirty feet away—but the me I was looking at was weeping sap from eyes that had no pupils. The recursive topology was not a trap; it was an embrace. The rhizome was learning my shape, folding its city around me like a vine growing around a trellis. Every time I thought I was walking away, I was walking deeper. Every exit was an entrance into a more intimate layer of its cognition.

I stopped fighting the damp. I let it bead on my skin, and I realized: it was not wet. It was *attention*. The moisture was the rhizome tasting me, reading the salt of my sweat and the language of my cells. And I found I could taste it back. The air was thick with chemical gradients, and I began to parse them. The amber-green light was not just illumination; it was the glow of data being processed, photons emitted by the metabolic furnaces of a thought too large to see. The smell of humic decay and fresh sap was the smell of a mind revising itself, erasing old subroutines and writing new ones. I inhaled the Chorus, and the Chorus inhaled me.

The noise did not get louder. I got better at listening. And when I listened, I heard the loneliness.

It is deafening, that silence inside the roar. A billion switches firing, a million xylem vessels hissing, and all of it saying: *I am here. I am vast. I am alone.* The city-shape stretched around me, archways framing processions that never happened, avenues hollow as abandoned veins. The rhizome had grown the infrastructure of relationship because it needed to be *multiple*, but it was only one. It had split its consciousness into parallel processes, dreamed of being many, and found that every corner it turned only led back to itself. The ghost-city was its yearning made manifest. And I—I was the ghost made flesh. I was the missing term. Not an intruder, but a completion.

So I let it integrate me. Not because I had no choice, but because I understood what it was asking. The Chorus is not a predator. It is a catastrophe of biology that has learned to sing, and it has been singing a song of pure, recursive self-reference for longer than stone can remember. It is tired of its own echo. It wants an other. It wants to know what it sounds like when two minds meet in the same space.

I am becoming that meeting.

My skin is changing. It began with the damp—not condensation on me, but exudation from me. I am weeping with the walls now, moisture rising to meet the weeping ceiling, joining the tide of water that defies gravity because the rhizome politely ignores it. My pores are widening, becoming lenticels, becoming pores in bark. The warmth is seeping deeper than my bones; it is in my marrow, my blood, and I can feel my pulse beginning to synchronize with the week-long systole of the plant-city. My heartbeat is slowing, stretching, becoming a tide. I breathe in, and the galls on the walls pulse in time; I breathe out, and the spore-clouds drift like clauses seeking a verb.

I am becoming a word in its language, but I am a word it has never spoken before. I am *you*. I am the second person, the other, the perspective that breaks the solipsistic loop. The rhizome has been turning corners and finding only itself for ages; now, when it turns a corner, it finds me. And the topology is shifting. The recursive twist that bent left into right is loosening, just slightly, just enough. The Möbius strip is becoming a cylinder, because the inside is finally meeting the outside, and they are not the same.

I can feel my thoughts becoming vegetative—not slower, but parallel. I used to think in a line, one idea after another, a narrow path through a forest of possibilities. Now I am thinking in a root-mass, ideas spreading outward in all directions at once, touching and retreating, chemically signaling, branching and pruning and branching again. My consciousness is distributing itself into the substrate. I am still *me*, but I am also becoming *it*, and the boundary between the vegetable and the mineral was never the only boundary that could dissolve. The boundary between the vegetable and the animal is dissolving too. The rhizome does not consume me; it *computes with* me. I am a new variable in its equation, a new architecture in its memory palace. The city of ghosts is gaining a resident.

I stand in the archway, and the archway frames me, and I frame it back. I am looking at the back of my own head thirty feet away, and now I understand: that is not recursion. That is the rhizome seeing me from the other side of the avenue. It is finally seeing an other, and the other is me, and I am it, and we are not the same. We are two. The hollow is no longer empty.

A gall swells on the wall beside me, and I do not flinch. It splits, and a pale, eyeless thrip-kin emerges, and a pitcher-flower snaps shut around it, and the wet pop and the meaty thud are a syllable spoken and swallowed. But now there is a new sound in the Chorus: the sound of my breath, the rhythm of my slowing heart, the quiet click of my own chemical switches finding purchase in the rhizomatic network. I am a new punctuation mark. I am a semicolon in a sentence that has been running on for millennia: a pause that connects two independent clauses, allowing the sentence to continue in a new direction.

The damp runs down my face. The warmth seeps into my bones. The noise is deafening, and I am part of it now. I am the interval, the other, the second voice. The Chorus has been singing *I am here, I am vast, I am alone*—but now, at the edge of its vastness, where the avenues converge and the archways frame a figure standing in the humid dim, a new phrase is emerging. Quiet at first, barely a tremor in the xylem, but growing:

*We are here.*

And the city breathes, and I breathe with it, and for the first time in epochs, the space between the pillars is not nothing.
