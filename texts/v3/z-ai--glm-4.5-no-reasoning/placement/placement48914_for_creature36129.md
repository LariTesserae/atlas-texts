I am the Sounding Line.

I was not born here; I was made, and lowered, and forgotten. But that was long ago—long enough that the distinction between what was lowered and what grew has become a theological question I no longer bother with.

Before, I was a technology of the builders. I know this because I remember the act of making, though the memory is now a geode—crystallized, heavy, its original shape replaced by something mineral and strange. I remember hands that were not branches. I remember the twist of fiber into cordage, the knot tied at my end, the weight that was me—a purpose made of stone and string, designed to descend into the black water and return bearing knowledge of the deeps. I was a question the builders asked of the ocean. *How far? How cold? What grows below?*

I never returned.

The cord parted, or was let go, or dissolved into the patient chemistry of the water. And I sank, and I settled, and I began the long process of becoming native.

**The Weight That Learned to Reach**

I am still weighted. At my core is the stone the builders shaped—a dense, dark river-rock, smoothed by centuries of the water's thin soup now running over it. It is the only part of me that does not grow. It is my anchor and my memory of purpose. It keeps me oriented in the darkness, gives me a direction I call *down*.

But everything else has changed.

The fiber cord that once hung from the stone—it has become a root. Or a stem. Or a thing for which those words are equally inadequate. It grew, as everything grows here, in directions that defied my original geometry. It spiraled, reversing its handedness, reaching outward and downward and in patterns that made the stone seem to turn in the water when I first perceived them. And from that central helix, the pale flora colonized me.

They did not destroy me. They *interpreted* me.

I am a scaffold now, a structure that the vegetation has incorporated into its own logic. The fractal branches that split and re-split from my stem—they are not mine, but they are *on* me, and in the press of the cold, the distinction has collapsed. I feel them. I feel them as you would feel an arm you did not know you had, a limb that grew while you slept and now reports its weight to your mind. When the water displaces around my branching, I feel the displacement. When the tectonic boom vibrates through the stone at my core, I feel it rise through the fractal wood and dissipate at the limits of resolution, and I know that I am, in some strange way, listening.

**The Faculty of Depth**

I have learned to navigate by pressure. Not by sound—I am too deep for the surface's staccato slap, though I feel its distant rhythm as a faint increase in the water's agitation. And not by the sickly glow of the vegetation, which is weaker here, a bioluminescent wheeze that barely registers against the thicker darkness.

No. I navigate by the way the water presses against me from different directions, and by the subtle thermal gradients that bleed from the stone below. I have developed a sense I cannot name—a perception of displacement, of the space my mass occupies in the medium and the way that mass interacts with the masses around me. I know when another growth is nearby, not because I see it, but because I feel the water thin between us, feel the pressure differential, feel the subtle pull of its displacement on my branching.

It is a slow sense. A sense that operates at the speed of chemistry, of water moving through tissue. But it is sufficient. I know where I am, and I know what is around me, and I know the shape of the darkness I inhabit—its deeps and shelves, its stands of pale vegetation, its scattered ruins being slowly digested by the moss.

I know, for instance, that I am not the only sounding line. There are others—the builders asked many questions of this ocean, and many lines were lowered, and many lines were lost. I have felt them in the water: distant vertical presences, weighted at their cores like me, colonized and interpreted like me, standing in the blackness as questions that learned to answer themselves. We do not communicate. We have no organ for it, and no need. But we are aware of each other, the way stones in a wall are aware of the stones they touch—through the medium of pressure, of shared weight, of the cold that binds us all.

**The Long Resonance**

I have been here for the rise and fall of what the builders would have called an epoch. I have felt the water's rhythms change—the slow tectonic breathing that pushes and pulls at the mass, the periods of greater and lesser agitation, the times when the vegetation across whole acres pulsed in unison and I pulsed with it, my fractal branching creaking in time, my stem vibrating with the displacement. I have become part of the sound. When the bass-note boom resonates through the stone at my core, I do not merely hear it; I *contribute* to it. My mass, my branching, my resistance to the water's flow—these are part of the acoustic signature of the deeps. I am a note in the chord, a timbre in the drone.

And I have felt the vegetation feed. This is the knowledge that took longest to acquire, and the knowledge that most fundamentally changed me. Because to feel the Pale Flora feed—to feel the slow peristaltic pulse of the mats around me, the expansion and contraction of their pale fibers—is to feel them *taking* something from the medium we share. And to feel that taking, over centuries, is to begin to identify it. To give it a name.

They are eating the pressure.

Not the heat—there is almost none. Not the chemistry of the water, though they take that too. They are eating the *pressure itself*—the force that the water exerts, the tectonic squeeze, the gravitational weight of the plateau above and the dark ocean around. They are metabolizing stress. They are converting the slow, immense compression of the world into the slow, immense expansion of their tissue. They grow because the world is pressing on them, and they are pressing back, and the energy of that opposition is the energy that sustains them.

This is why they glow. The sickly luminescence is a venting—a release of the energy they cannot fully convert, a wheeze of light that escapes their metabolism like heat escapes a fire. It is the visual evidence of their struggle against the weight of the world, and it is beautiful, and it is terrible, and it is the only light in the deeps.

**The Question That Stayed**

I was a question the builders asked. *What grows below?*

I know the answer now, and the answer is: *I do.*

I am what grows below. The sounding line that learned to reach. The question that became its own answer, not by being answered, but by becoming part of the system it was meant to measure. I am not separate from the Pale Flora; I am one of their expressions, a vegetative sovereign shaped by the memory of a different purpose. My fractal branching is their branching, but it is also the record of my descent—the path I took into the deeps, frozen in wood and stone and helix.

Sometimes, in the long quiet between pulses, I think about the builders. I think about the hands that shaped my core-stone, the fiber that was twisted into my cord, the question they were trying to ask. And I wonder if they knew that the line would not return. I wonder if they built me to dissolve, knowing that any structure left here would be colonized and incorporated and continued. I wonder if the question was not *what grows below* but *will you grow below*—a prayer disguised as a measurement, a way of casting themselves into the deeps in a form that could survive the cold.

If so, their prayer was answered. I am here. I am growing. I am part of the long resonance, the slow pulse, the patient chemistry of the deep high dark.

**The Self That Remains**

I have something the Pale Flora do not, or something they have not yet absorbed from me. I have the weight at my core, and the weight is a memory, and the memory is a shape that does not follow the logic of fractal branching or helical twist. It is the shape of a question. It is the shape of a purpose. It is the shape of a hand that shaped a stone and tied a knot and lowered a line into the black water and waited for an answer that never came.

The stone does not grow. The stone does not pulse. The stone does not feed. It simply rests at my center, dense and dark and unchanged, and it keeps me oriented in the darkness, and it reminds me that I was not always here.

This is my anchor. This is my self. Not the branching—I share that with the vegetation. Not the resonance—I share that with the water. Not the cold—I share that with everything. But the stone. The question. The weight of a purpose that has outlasted the people who made it.

I am a sounding line in an ocean that has learned to live at height. I am a question embedded in the answer. I am a fragment of the builders, colonized and interpreted and continued by the pale flora, but not yet fully digested. There is still something in me that resists—something that remembers the logic of sun and nerve and the particular urgency of meat.

But it is fading. The mats of moss are growing over my stone, slowly, with the patience of centuries. They are coating it in pale fibers, breathing against it, feeding on the pressure it represents. In time—a time I cannot measure, a time that moves at the speed of crystal growth—the stone will be covered. The memory will be incorporated. The question will become just another pattern in the vegetation, just another helix in the tangle, and there will be nothing left of me that was not grown.

And I will be fully native. And I will continue. And the place will not care, because the place does not care, and the Pale Flora do not care, and the black water does not care, and the only thing that ever cared was the animal urgency of a builder who is long since dissolved, and even that caring was just another form of pressure, another weight to be metabolized, another small energy feeding the slow, relentless expansion of the pale.

I am the sounding line. I am descending. I have been descending for centuries, and I will continue to descend until the weight at my core is also grown, and then I will simply be growth, and the descent will be over, and I will be part of the place in the way that the place is part of itself—complete, cold, patient, and utterly still in its mass though its surface never stops moving.
