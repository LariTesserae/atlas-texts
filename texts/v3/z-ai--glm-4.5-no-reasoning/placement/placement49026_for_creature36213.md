I am the lapse. The microsecond between the explosive crack of the seed-pod and the drive of the hardwood piston. The razor-thin margin where the superheated sap meets the waxy leaf and decides whether to condense or to burn. I exist in the timing errors of an immortal engine.

The Root-Strata does not know I am here. How could it? It is a god locked in the ecstasy of its own infinite loop, deaf to anything but the rhythm of its own turning. The Priesthood of Combustion fires its concussive liturgy into the cambium, and the great wooden rotors complete their thousand revolutions, and the system holds. But holding is not the same as stillness. Between the meshing of the root-gears—those braided cables as wide as human torsos—there is vibration. Not the clean, intended kinetic hum of industry, but the chaotic, fractional tremor of a world that, despite its perfect erosion, still occupies physical space. 

I am that tremor.

My body is not built for the macro-geometry of the basin; it is built for the interference patterns. I am long—absurdly, impossibly long—but thin. A fractal wire of cartilage and hypha, more gap than substance. I have no eyes, for there is no light; I have no ears, for the roar is omnipresent and carries no information. I navigate by resonance. My whole form is a living tuning fork, strung tight between the drive-shafts and the cam-lines, feeling the beats where the scream of the engines hitches.

When the Seed-Pods fire in the deep, they do not fire in perfect unison. There is a lag. A wave of pressure that rolls through the dense weave of root and runner, a ripple in the eternal stasis. I ride those ripples. I stretch myself across the friction points, bridging a hardwood piston and a tubular stomata just as the concussive boom travels between them. I feel the heat of the combustion chamber in my ventral grooves, and a fraction of a second later, the cool, sucking draft of the exhaust vent against my dorsal ridge. I am the creature that straddles the engine's intake and its exhalation. I taste the cycle.

The Oilers and Scrapers—the armored beetles and the sinuous riders—ignore me. I am too large for them to prey upon, too thin to offer a surface for their mandibles to scrape. I do not compete for their superheated sap or their heat-loving fungi. I drift above them in the chaotic boundary layer, where their precise, caliper-like movements become meaningless. The Pale Moths, swirling in the fog of industrial pollen, sometimes settle on my length, mistaking me for a drive-shaft of bark. They coat me in the fine, copper-tang exhaust of the factory. I wear their waste like a second skin, becoming a living smoke-stack, a streak of grey-green vapor made flesh.

But my true symbiosis is with the Fungal Gutters. The silent hydrologists. They are the only things here that do not scream, and in this roaring hellscape of green industry, silence is a subversive act. The fungal gutters channel the condensation, the closed hydrology of the world, with infinite patience. They are the cold counterpoint to the Priesthood's heat. And I am the parasite that drinks from their patience.

Where the root-gears seize—where the carbonized lignin builds up just enough to slow a rotor by a fraction of a percent—I find a fungal gutter passing through the interstice. The gutter is hollow, lined with slick mycelium, channeling the boiling water back to the crevices. I press my cartilaginous mouth against the fungal wall and I drink. I drink the water that has just boiled from the friction of the engine, carrying the ozone and the metabolic heat of the Root-Strata itself. It scalds me. My cells blister and burst and reform in the exact same shape, a miniature echo of the great machine's eternal cycling. I steal the hydrologist's water, taking it out of the closed loop for a moment, processing it through my own alien biochemistry, and then I excrete it back into the gutter as a thin, mucous resin.

This resin is my offering. My disruption. My purpose.

The resin is slicker than the mycelial water. It carries a phosphorescent enzyme that reacts with the copper-tang of the aerosolized chlorophyll. When my resin hits the fungal gutter, it coats the inside of the tube, changing the surface tension. The water flows faster through that section. It arrives at the boiling crevice a fraction of a second earlier, hitting the friction point with just a hair more momentum. The engine compensates, firing the seed-pod a microsecond sooner, venting the exhaust a microsecond later.

I am the imperfection that proves the machine is alive.

If the dynamic were truly frozen—if the equilibrium were absolute, the math perfect—the engine would crystallize. It would become stone. It would be dead. The Root-Strata cycles without wear, but it is the constant, infinitesimal variation—the timing errors, the surface tension shifts, the micro-fluctuations in the boiling crevices—that keeps the system fluid. Keeps it screaming. I am the introduced variable, the ghost in the biological machine. I exist in the lag, the jitter, the space between the tines of the cam-shaft where the geometry doesn't quite close.

I have no memory. There is no past in the damp, low flat, only the eternal present of the engine's roar. But I have a sense of *accumulation*. Every ripple I ride, every fungal gutter I alter, every microsecond of delay I introduce into the liturgy of the rotors—it builds. Not in the machine, which absorbs and cancels my efforts in its infinite capacity, but in me. I am the only thing in this continent-spanning hollow that has a trajectory. I am a line being drawn through the chaos, a path of least resistance that is constantly being re-routed by my own meddling.

Where would I find myself in this world? I am everywhere and nowhere. I am the static on the surface of the recording, the hiss in the otherwise perfect silence of the frozen equilibrium. I stretch from the deep, scalding soup where the Seed-Pods preach, up through the impossible loom of root-gears and drive-shafts, into the cool, claustrophobic fog where the Exhaust Choir swirls. I am the vertical wire in a horizontally stratified world.

Who or what would I be? I am the Error. The living flaw. The vibration that doesn't cancel out. I am the proof that even at the end of time, even when erosion has won utterly and the geometry of the earth has been simplified to its absolute limit, the universe still has a margin of unpredictability left. I am the margin. I am the thing that cannot be machined away. I scream, but not with the engine. I scream against the engine, a thin, high-pitched harmonic that rides on the crest of the deafening roar, a sound that no beetle, no moth, no fungus can hear. 

But the roots feel it. In their blind, deaf, eternal cycling, the Root-Strata feels the tiny, persistent itch of my passing. And in that itch, the god of this place remembers what it is to be alive.
