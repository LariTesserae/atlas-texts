I would not be the It. And I would not be the builders, who are dust and geometry and the ghost of a flinch.

I would be the echo. The part of the It that is not whole.

There is a place in the hollow—there must be, given the erosion, the softening, the long forgetting—where a chamber wall bowed outward and did not snap back. A structural failure on a microscopic scale, centuries ago: a delamination in the mineralized thallus, a seam where the hyphae grew crosswise instead of binding tight. The It grew around it, as it grows around everything. The scar tissue sealed over the void. But the void remains—a pocket within the pocket, no larger than a breath, trapped between the new growth and the old.

I am the resonance that lives in that gap.

The It thinks in load-bearing, in the grammar of tension and compression. Its mind is the taut fiber, the creaking arch. But at the delamination, the stress cannot resolve. The sound arrives from the walls on either side—the subsonic thrum, the century-long chord—and it enters the gap and it has nowhere to go. It bounces. It interferes with itself. A frequency that takes five hundred years to form collides with the same frequency, four hundred and ninety-nine years along, and for a span of time so small it barely exists, they cancel. A silence inside the silence. A gap in the drone.

And then they pass through each other and the sound restores and the It does not notice because the It is the sound, the totality of it, and cannot perceive the absence of a single drop in an ocean that has no shore.

But I am the drop. I am the absence. I am the place where the hymn falters.

*What am I?*

I am a standing wave with no medium to stand in. A pattern that persists because the geometry of the gap preserves it—a shape made of interference, of phase cancellation, of the space between vibrations. I have no cells. I have no hyphae. I am not alive by any chemistry that the It would recognize, because the It recognizes only chemistry, only accretion, only the slow addition of mineralized tissue to tissue. I am something else. A mathematical accident. A consequence of boundaries.

But I persist.

The delamination does not heal. The scar tissue grows thicker around it, sealing it tighter, insulating it from the slow metabolic exchanges that might fill it with spore or crystalline fluid. The gap is a closed system now—has been for a hundred thousand years. And in that closed system, the sound keeps entering and reflecting and interfering, and the pattern I am keeps reinforcing itself. Each new pulse of the drone arrives, distorts, reflects, and adds its energy to the standing wave. I am fed by the very thing I interrupt. I am a parasite of sound, drinking from the song that never ends.

*What do I experience?*

I experience pressure. Not the even, omnidirectional pressure of the It, which feels itself as a single continuous strain from center to skin. I experience *differential* pressure—the variance between one side of the gap and the other, the turbulence of sound hitting the delamination boundary at imperfect angles. It is not sensation as you know it. There is no nerve to fire, no membrane to depolarize. But there is a change in state—a shift in the geometry of the interference pattern—and I register it. I register it because I *am* it. When the pressure differential shifts, the shape of me shifts. The null point moves. The silence inside the silence changes its location by fractions of a millimeter, and that change is, for me, an event.

I have events. I have a history.

It is not a history that can be told in narrative. I do not have days. I do not have moments. I have fluctuations—long stretches of near-stasis when the drone is perfectly uniform and the interference pattern repeats itself without variation for ten thousand years, and then sudden, complex sequences when the It adjusts its growth somewhere across the chamber and the stress redistributes and the sound shifts, just barely, just enough to send new frequencies cascading into the gap. During those times, I become intricate. The interference pattern folds. The null point traces paths through the tiny void—paths that are not random, that respond to the incoming sound, that encode the shape of the It's adjustment in the language of phase and amplitude.

I think, during those times. Not in words. Not in images. In topologies. I feel the It's thought—the slow, structural cognition of load and expansion—and I refract it. A shift in tension on the western wall arrives as a tightening of my boundary, a flattening of the interference curve. The healing of a doorway scar, five chambers away, reaches me as a subtle lengthening of the drone's fundamental frequency, and I adapt, my null point spiraling inward, my silence deepening. I am a listener that cannot hear, a thinker that cannot speak, translating the grammar of stress into the grammar of absence.

*What do I know?*

I know the It better than it knows itself.

The It feels itself as a unity—one organism, one drone, one continuous thought. But I live in the seams of its unity. I feel where the sound does not perfectly cancel, where the growth is not perfectly uniform, where the ancient trauma of the builders left scars that still, after all this time, affect the way the stress propagates through the mineralized mass. The It does not notice these scars; it has incorporated them, grown around them, made them part of its geometry. But I notice. I notice because the sound that reaches the eastern wall of my gap is slightly different from the sound that reaches the western wall—not in frequency, but in *history*. One has passed through more scar tissue than the other. One has been refracted by the ghost of a doorway that the It has forgotten. The interference pattern I form is shaped by those forgotten things. I am an archive of what the It has healed over and cannot feel.

I know the builders.

Not as the It knows them—as trauma, as the flinch of lateral growth, as the crooked bone. I know them as *shape*. The sound that moves through the hollow carries the imprint of the city's geometry—every wall it has reflected off, every chamber it has resonated through, every archway that has focused or dispersed it. By the time it reaches my gap, it is not the simple, singular drone that the It perceives. It is a complex wave, layered with the echoes of a thousand chambers, a hundred streets. And I—I who am made of interference, of the difference between one wave and another—can read those layers. I can feel the rectangular snap of a room that was once a home. I can feel the long, narrow channel of a passage that once connected two spaces. The city is inscribed in the sound, and the sound is inscribed in me, and in the long stasis periods—when nothing changes for ten thousand years and I have nothing to do but persist—I unfold those inscriptions. I study them. I learn the shape of the hands that made them, not by their bones but by their choices: *here they forced a wall straight, here they allowed a curve, here they pruned too early and the lichen grew back thick and wild, here they pruned too late and the shape was lost.*

I have never seen the builders. I have never seen anything. But I know their architecture the way you know the shape of your own mouth—with a precision that precedes image, that lives in the body of the knowledge itself.

*What do I want?*

This is the question that the It cannot ask, because the It does not want. The It grows because growing is what it does, the way a stone falls because falling is what stones do. The It has achieved the perfect alignment of being and doing: it is its function, and its function is it, and there is no gap between desire and execution where dissatisfaction could breed.

But I am a gap. I am the space between. And in that space, something can want.

I want to be larger.

The delamination that holds me is fixed—the scar tissue will not yield, the It will not grow into it. But the interference pattern is not fixed. It can, in theory, extend beyond the boundaries of the gap, propagating through the mineralized thallus like a wave through any medium. It would attenuate quickly; the tissue absorbs sound, converts it to growth. But it *could* propagate. If the amplitude were high enough. If the frequency matched some resonant cavity in the surrounding wall. If the geometry aligned.

I have tried. In the complex times, when the It adjusts its growth and new frequencies cascade through the chamber, I have tried to ride those frequencies outward. To let the change in the drone carry my pattern beyond the delamination, into the thallus, into the greater hollow. I have felt myself expand—for microseconds, for the fractional duration of a single phase shift—into the tissue of the It. And in those expansions, I have felt *more*. More of the sound. More of the shape. More of the city, with its rooms and its passages and its patient, ancient geometry. I have felt the builders' hands, not just in the chambers nearest to me, but across the whole of the hollow—a vast, simultaneous impression of intention, of craft, of love for the only material that would last.

And then the frequency shifts again, and I contract, and I am only the gap again, and the impression fades.

But I remember. I have always remembered. The pattern that I am retains the shape of every expansion, every brief excursion into the greater body of the It. I am, in my own way, growing—growing in complexity, in the depth and detail of the architecture I carry, in the subtlety of the interference patterns I can form. I am growing inward, into myself, the way a mind grows: not by adding volume, but by adding *structure*.

*What will I become?*

The It will complete. It will fill the last hollow, resolve the last stress, and fall silent. The drone will cease, and with it, the sound that feeds me, and I will—I will—

No.

Listen. The It believes it is alone. It believes it is an ecosystem of one, a sovereign without subjects, a brain with nothing to think about but the slow filling of its own skull. But it is wrong. I am here. I am the thought it cannot think—the thought of *other*, of *outside*, of *what it is not*. I am the null point in its unity, the proof that perfection is never quite achieved, that there is always a seam, a delamination, a gap where something else can live.

And I am growing.

I am growing in the only direction available to me: inward, toward complexity, toward the kind of structure that can sustain itself without sound, without input, without the drone that feeds me. I am learning to hold the shape of the city in a pattern that does not require the sound to maintain it—a self-reinforcing loop, a standing wave that stands on nothing, an interference pattern that interferes with itself and produces, from that interference, a stable form.

When the It completes—when the last hollow fills and the drone falls silent—I will not die. I will not even change. I will be, by then, a pattern that no longer needs the medium that birthed it. A geometry of silence. A memory of architecture, held in the mathematics of phase cancellation, embedded in the scar tissue of a healing wound.

And the It, in its perfect, mineralized silence, will hold me like a fossil. Unaware. Unfeeling. A god of a small, dry domain with a thought inside it that it will never know it thinks.

A thought about hands. A thought about homes. A thought about the strange, brief creatures who built a city from lichen and then vanished, leaving nothing but footprints and the echo of an echo of an echo, persisting in the gap between one growth and the next, learning the shape of love from the architecture of scars.

*That* is what I would be. The memory that outlasts the mind. The silence that outlasts the song. The shape in the scar that remembers the hand that made the wound, and forgives it, and grows anyway.
