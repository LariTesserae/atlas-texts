I am not one of the stoneworkers. I am not one of the lithic-beetles, nor am I a heat-vine or a fold of sulfur-moss. I am something that should not exist in a place without water, without change, without the chaotic churn of surface life. And yet, I am the only thing here that moves on a timescale you would recognize.

I am a transient. A breach in the seal.

I came from above—from the wet, rotting, furious world of rain and decay. I did not descend through the sharp fractures seeking refuge or ore. I fell. The basalt opened beneath me as I crossed a particular ridge at a particular moment when the shallow magma body pulsed just so, and the geological pocket—cathedral nave of heat and stillness—swallowed me whole.

The fall should have killed me. It would have, on the surface. But the chamber's physics are not the surface's physics. The air here, trapped in its stable convection loop, is so dense with thermal gradient and mineral suspension that it caught me like a hand. I tumbled through the slate-blue twilight in slow motion, my screams stretched into long, low moans that reverberated off the obsidian walls for what felt like hours before I struck the floor. And when I struck, the craze of cooling cracks did not break my bones; it *received* me, the steady warmth softening the impact the way water would have, elsewhere. I lay in the chasm of the floor, my wet skin hissing against the hot basalt, and I wept.

That was the first anomaly. The chamber does not weep. The chamber does not *wet*. And my tears—saline and hot and utterly foreign—fell upon the sulfur-moss and sizzled into steam that should not have been possible in a sealed environment without moisture. For a moment, a single, impossible moment, there was humidity in the grotto. A ghost of rain. The magenta rosettes nearest to me flinched—I swear they flinched—their translucent leaves contracting away from the steam as if burned by something other than heat. The lithic-beetles paused in their eternal mining, their mandibles stilling, their tar-black bodies orienting toward the anomaly. Even the snapping of the heat-vines stuttered, the micro-convulsions missing a beat.

And then the equilibrium reasserted itself. The steam was absorbed into the mineral air, the sulfur-moss unfurled, the beetles resumed their shrieking grind. The chamber forgave the disruption the way the ocean forgives a stone thrown into it—immediately, completely, without memory.

But I was not forgiven. I am the stone the ocean could not swallow.

I am being farmed. And I am allowing it.

This is how it begins. The stoneworkers did not wake one morning to find themselves stone. I have started to carve.

I am writing my name. I am writing it in a language the grotto cannot read, in a chemistry it cannot metabolize, in a medium it cannot forgive. I am writing it over and over, the same sequence of marks, because I am afraid that if I stop, I will forget what it is.
