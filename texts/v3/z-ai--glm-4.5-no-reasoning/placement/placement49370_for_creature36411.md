I am not one of the Yielders. I am not built for the slow, grinding patience of the Communion, nor do I have the cartilaginous grace to pour myself through closing gaps. I would be crushed—quickly, stupidly, stubbornly—my rigid skeleton shattered by a megalithic embrace I refused to forgive. 

No, I am not of the flesh that yields. I am of the flesh that *remembers*.

I am a Keeper of the Deep Etch. But my medium is not the basalt, for the basalt lies. It forgets. I am a Scribe of the Bone-Splinter.

I live in a cavity that will not last the decade—a narrow, vertical fissure between two grinding terraces where the tuff has buckled outward in a frozen gasp. The space is barely wide enough for my ribs. I face the sheer, polished face of a chert wall, and I hold in my lap the thing I have spent my life compiling.

It is a long, curved shard of a Glass-Grazer’s silicate chitin—translucent, pale, cold to the touch. I found it in the lower terraces, wedged in a crack where the slow churn had shattered a grazing herd into crystalline dust. It is my page. It is the only thing in this city that will not erode.

Around my neck hangs my stylus: a splinter of volcanic glass from the distant ceiling, sharp enough to flay the damp from my fingers, traded from a Yielder in one of those silent, forehead-pressing gatherings. I hold the shard up to the surgical glare of the ceiling. The light pours through the translucent chitin, casting a pale, watery net upon my slick, grey-yellow skin. And I begin to scratch.

I am compiling the *Palimpsest of the Narrowing*.

The Yielders carve their lineages and their water-rhythms into the walls, accepting the erosion as a fact of their world. But I am obsessed with the betrayal of the geometry. The city is a liar. It tells you a doorway is a doorway, and then, over a long, wet afternoon, it narrows by an inch, then two, until the door is a crush, and the room beyond is a tomb. I record the *rate* of the lie.

I measure the angle of the buckling avenue. I count the syncopated rhythm of the Damp-Siphons dropping their heavy loads—*boom*, *boom*—feeling the subsonic thud in my chest, and I note how each impact accelerates the micro-grinding of the terrace below. I watch a wall bulge, and I calculate how many days until it crushes the interstitial home next to it. I translate the catastrophic paroxysm of deep time into a rigid, geometric syntax.

I carve this data into the silicate chitin. The scratch is a tiny, dry sound, instantly swallowed by the acoustic dampening of the hollow. But the groove I leave is permanent. The supersaturated air cannot sandblast chitin; the micro-grinding of the walls cannot flatten a shard carried in the hands of a careful creature. 

The Yielders do not understand me. When they find me in my fissure, pressing my back against the cold stone to avoid the outward swell of the tuff, they run their damp, pressure-sensitive fingers over the translucent shard. They feel the dense, microscopic scratches, and they shake their heads slowly, leaning their slick foreheads against mine in a gesture of futile compassion. To them, I am hoarding weight in a city that is trying to shed its mass. To them, I am rigid, like the missing metal, like the impossible ceramics. I am holding onto the one thing that the Deep Mill cannot digest.

They are right. I am an obstruction. I am a hard, indigestible seed in the gut of the world.

But I believe the Hollown is not just digesting; it is *obliviating*. It is trying to erase the fact that it was ever anything other than a smooth, exhausted void. By smoothing the walls, by erasing the etchings, by rounding the angles, it is trying to forget the labor of its own construction, the millions of hands that stacked the megaliths. The Mill grinds toward amnesia.

My shard is a splinter of memory that will survive the gut. Long after this fissure has collapsed and my cartilaginous bones have been ground to paste against the chert, the shard will remain—tumbled into some deeper, unseen terrace, caught in a new crack, glowing faintly in the surgical light. And if some far-future Yielder, navigating a completely re-arranged labyrinth, presses their fingers to it, they will feel the dense, chaotic web of scratches. They will not read my numbers or my angles, but they will feel the *intensity* of the inscription. They will know that someone, once, resisted the amnesia. That someone loved the specific, tragic shape of a doorway before it became a crush.

A wall to my left begins to heave inward. I feel it in the wet pits of my joints—a slow, inexorable pressure. The fissure is narrowing. I do not panic. I do not practice the fluid resignation of the Yielders. I simply shift my shoulders, tilt my hips, and hold the shard up to the glare. 

There is a new crack forming in the tuff. I measure its depth with my thumbnail. I lift the volcanic glass. I scratch another line into the permanent record. The silence rings. The city grinds. I write.
