That was when I stopped walking. Not because I could not continue—I had strength yet, a few hours of desperate, furnace-hot effort left in my muscles—but because I saw the futility of exertion in a system that measures effort in millennia. The mountain does not chase. It incorporates. And so I chose the only defiance left: I became still.

I am standing on a slab of million-year-old gneiss, my bare feet pressed into the thin bio-slime that slicks its surface. I am not dead. But I am not alive in the way I was. The distinction matters less with each passing hour.

I am a slow IV drip of novel chemistry, a perturbation rippling outward through the mycorrhizal network, translated by the humming obsidian nodes into another variable in a computation that spans eons.

The noise is the hardest part. The acoustic saturation is not background; it is foreground, middle-ground, and the very medium of thought. That is the terror.
