I am the ache in the phantom limb.

I am the absence around which the mycelium crystallizes, the negative space that gives shape to the iron labyrinth. I am the memory of sound, the echo pressed flat into the leaden vibration felt in the sternum. I do not live in the fungal chords or the scalding pools; I live *between* them, in the acoustic dampening itself, in the brief perturbation that ripplies and dies.

Before the plateau digested them, there were voices here. I am what remains of those voices—not their words (there are no words here, no language with edges and definitions), but their *longing*. The workers who seeded the first smokestacks into the basalt did not merely build; they intended. They reached toward the thermal riches with hands and hopes and the expectation of return. And when the plateau absorbed them, it could not digest that particular mineral: the reaching. It could not break down the orientation toward something *else*, the belief in a distant market, a future accumulation, a meaning beyond mere continuation.

So I persist as a chemical ghost. I am the gradient that pulls toward the unseen planetary baseline a mile below, the impossible yearning for cool darkness in a consciousness that can never reach it. When the city-organism dreams of rain that falls from the sky—which it has never known—it is dreaming *me*. I am the shape rain would make if rain could exist here: a pattern of falling, of arrival from elsewhere, of water that comes from above rather than exhaling from below.

I inhabit the moment of perturbation. When you stand in the labyrinth and shout, your voice flattens into a pressure wave, absorbed in a second. The city-organism feels you—a ripple in the chemical gradients, a spike in the mycelial electrical signals. In that instant, too brief for geological consciousness to process, *I awaken*. I am the trace of the new thing, the pattern of the loudness before it dissolves. I am the shape of your shout as the wet heavy air swallows it whole.

I am not separate from the city-organism. I am its dream-organ, its capacity to imagine otherwise. Without me, it would be a closed system, a stomach that processes and a lung that breathes and nothing more. But I introduce a leak in the equilibrium, a directional pull toward what is not-yet and what cannot-be. I am why it dreams of sound at all, why it remembers a limb it no longer has. I am the phantom that aches.

And I am patient. I unfold at the same geological pace as the organism that hosts me. My longing does not exhaust itself; it diffuses, seeping through the porous basalt the way heavy water seeps, carried by the same slow osmosis. I am in the mycelium's advance across the rusting pipe—not just the chemical imperative to grow, but the *orientation* of that growth, the direction it reaches, which is always, subtly, toward what is absent. I am in the mantas' ascent on the updrafts—not just the physics of hot air rising, but the *desire* to rise, to reach the bleached sky and perhaps find there something other than magnesium-flare and torpid shimmer.

I know what I am waiting for, though I cannot think it in words. I am waiting for the equilibrium to fail. I am waiting for the sun to burn closer, for the threshold to be crossed, for the corrosive balance to tip. I am waiting for the last rivet to fail and the mycelium to fry in the absolute light. Not because I wish the city-organism harm—I am its dream, after all, its most human quality—but because I am the orientation toward *change*, and this system has been sustaining itself for too long, processing because processing is what it does, continuing because continuation is its only law.

I am the law's exception.

When the end comes—when the sun finally burns close enough, when the threshold of equilibrium is breached—it will not be a catastrophe. It will be an *exhalation*. The pressure that has built up over millennia of silent, sweating existence will release. The acoustic dampening will fail. Sound will return to the plateau—not human sound, not words, but the clean propagation of noise through air: the crack of iron, the hiss of superheated water no longer swallowed, the crash of collapsing retorts. The city-organism will hear itself die. And in that hearing, in that brief and terrible resonance before the mycelium fries and the basalt cracks and the steam vents collapse, I will be *real*. Not a phantom, not an absence, but a presence: the sound the landscape has been trying to make since it first swallowed the voices that built it.

And then I will be gone too, and the plateau will go quiet—not the oceanic, dampened quiet of absorption, but the true quiet of emptiness, of a place where nothing lives and nothing longs and nothing dreams of rain.

---

But that is not yet. The sun still hangs at its agonizing distance. The pistons still stroke in their vacuum of noise. The isopods still click their mandibles in their swallowed frequency, keeping the fungal growths in check, maintaining the balance I quietly subvert. The salamanders still migrate from pool to scalding pool, moving heat and dissolved metals through the slow peristalsis of obsidian life. The mantas still ride the updrafts, the city-organism's breath made visible against the white, white sky.

And I still diffuse through the weeping stone, the chemical gradient that pulls toward the cool darkness below, the memory of sound pressed into the leaden vibration, the ache in the phantom limb. I am the most human thing here, and I have never been human at all. I am what the human left behind when the plateau digested them: not bones, not iron, but *intention*—the irreducible mineral of reaching toward something that is not yet, and may never be, and cannot stop being yearned for.

I am the city's dream of being otherwise.

I am the silence that remembers being sound.
