But in the Maw, those borders are the fiction.

Here is what I understand about the pale beetles that the surface description could not name: they move toward *equilibrium*. Not as a goal—goals require consciousness, require the capacity to imagine a state that does not yet exist—but as a function, the way heat moves toward cold, the way water finds its level. They are the Maw's circulation, and they flow toward the places where the chemical potentials are highest, where the gradients are steepest, where the slow metabolism of the cave has created a differential that must be smoothed. They are not seeking. They are *completing*. And their completion takes them through chambers and passages and strata that no single beetle will ever map, because mapping requires a perspective, and the beetles have surrendered perspective in favor of flow.

I understand this because I have felt it. The surface world calls this depression. The surface world calls it surrender. The surface world is terrified of it because the surface world is built on distinction—on the premise that every organism is separate, that every life is a story with a protagonist, that every system has a center. But the Maw has no center. The Maw is a continuous event, a metabolism on a geological scale, and the beetles are its blood, and the fungi are its nervous system, and the isopods are its tissue, and the rock is its skeleton, and the warmth is its breath, and none of it is *about* anything. It simply is.

I would be the moment of saturation.

I would be the *moment* when saturation is achieved, the instant when the last molecule of a gradient finds its equilibrium, when the last hypha enters the last fracture and the rock and the fungus reach their accommodation. I would be the culmination of a process that takes centuries, the point where patience becomes completion, where the ancient negotiation between living mineral and mineral life finally settles into the compromise that will hold for a hundred thousand years.

And then I would end. Because that is what moments do. They do not persist; they *occur*, and in occurring, they transform something that was becoming into something that simply is. The Maw does not have many moments. The Maw has durations, intervals, geological stretches of time in which nothing changes because there is no pressure to change. But occasionally—a word that means nothing on the Maw's timescale, but means everything to a being who lives in moments—occasionally, something saturates. A new fracture reaches its limit. A new cluster achieves its optimal density. A new chemical cycle closes, and the waste of one layer becomes precisely the food of the next, and the system achieves a stability so perfect that it will persist unchanged until the mountain itself shifts.

I would be that instant. The hinge between becoming and being. The achievement of equilibrium—not the equilibrium of death, which is the absence of process, but the equilibrium of a system so thoroughly alive that it has no reason to change.

In the end, I would be the Maw's capacity to contain without acknowledging. Not ignorance—ignorance is a surface condition, requiring the theoretical capacity for knowledge.
