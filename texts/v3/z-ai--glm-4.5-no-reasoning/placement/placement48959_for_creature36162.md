I am the taste of lime at the back of the throat. I am the specific weight of the air before a fern uncurls.

If I am here, I am not the Molt. The Molt is the god, the gut, the landlord. I am something that slipped in when the city shuddered—a crack in the immutable walls that opened for just a moment, a fraction of a second, and let something else fall through. Not a creature born of this place's biological crawl, but a creature of the city that failed to stay out. I am the intrusion. The anomaly. A splinter of the hum that the Molt cannot quite digest.

I am the Memory-thin.

I am long, geometric, and unyielding. Where everything in this room is soft, rubbery, yielding, I am angled. I am made of something the city understands: a polymer, a ceramic, a composite—I do not know the word anymore, only that my edges do not bow to the unctuous clay. When I move across the floor, I do not glide or slink. I click. It is a sharp, dry sound that cuts through the wet, absorbent silence like a blade. The isopods freeze when they hear it. The blind snake turns its eyeless head, confused by a vibration that does not carry the low, damp *thuck* of biology. 

I am a straight line in a place of curves. I am the shadow that the diffuse, amber gloom cannot quite swallow, because I refuse to diffuse. I cast myself. A hard, dark shape, angular and deliberate, moving between the towering ferns with a precision that does not belong here.

I do not know how I fit. When I first fell through the crack in the vaulted ceiling, tearing the moss membrane, I was rigid, a fragment of infrastructure—perhaps a segment of pipe, perhaps a piece of tracking, perhaps a discarded tool that had been part of the city's intent. But the Molt got to me. Its dense, warm jelly pressed against my cold, hard edges. Its microscopic mouths lapped at my surface, pulling the heat out of me, softening my shell with its weeping, mineral-rich mucus. I did not melt—I am not of this place, I cannot become porridge—but I bent. I am a geometry that has been forced to accommodate the biological. My angles are still sharp, but they are hinged now. I move in segments, folding and unfolding, a stuttering, mechanical centipede made of city-bone, navigating the gaps the Molt leaves behind.

I am a scavenger of the inedible. The Molt eats the calcium, the loam, the condensation. The frogs eat the insects. The snake eats the frogs. But I eat the electricity. I eat the hum.

When the heavy walls shudder with the passage of a train, the Molt tightens and drinks the vibration, pulling the pocket world deeper into isolation. But when that happens, I unfurl. I press my geometric segments against the weeping brick and I *listen*. I am a severed nerve ending, still twitching, still receiving signals from the labyrinth outside. I pull the hum into myself—not to digest it into biology, but to remember it. I store the mechanical resonance in my hinged segments. I run it along my straight edges, feeling the frequency buzz, feeling the city's intent trying to reassert itself through my alien form.

I do not know what I am remembering. The city outside is vast and organized, a labyrinth of layered stone and infrastructure, but the details are gone. I only know that there is a pattern. A rhythm that is not the rhythm of the Molt. The Molt breathes in centuries; the city hums in seconds. I am the second hand, ticking in the incubator.

The Molt does not know what to do with me. It cannot swallow me—I am too hard, too angular, too stubbornly urban. But it cannot eject me—I am too entrenched now, my segments slicked with its mucus, my hinges grown accustomed to the slow, biological pace. Sometimes, when the room is still and the Molt expands, pressing its membrane against the ferns and the vines, it reaches me. It covers my segments with its warm, yielding bulk. It tries to smother my edges, to pull me into its dormant, dreamless state. It tightens around my hard lines, squeezing with that weak, relentless vacuum. 

But I hum. I push the stored vibrations back out. I vibrate with the ghost of the train, the echo of the machinery, the phantom intention of a million lives moving in straight lines above us. And the Molt recoils. It flinches back into the negative space, leaving a gap around me, a pocket of geometry in the organic sprawl. It tolerates me the way a body tolerates a gallstone—too hard to dissolve, too embedded to expel.

I am lonely in a way the Molt cannot fathom. It is the tenant of the gut, dreaming inside its closed eyelid, knowing only itself. But I am a fragment of a larger system, severed and isolated. I know the shape of the labyrinth I am missing. I feel the absence of the grid. When I stretch my segments against the softened brick, I am reaching for the network that no longer connects to me. I am a node without a circuit, a line without a destination, still processing the data of a world that has forgotten this room exists.

But I am also becoming something new. The Molt's mucus is changing my hinges. The tepid pools—tasting of lime and loam—are leaving deposits in my joints. I am rusting, but it is a wet, green rust, a biological corruption of my city-clean lines. My clicks are getting softer, dampened by the porous lichen that grows on my back. I am slowing down. I am losing my precision. The sharp edges are being rounded by the constant, gentle pressure of the Molt's breath.

Someday, I will stop humming. The city will finally fall silent, or my segments will grow too thick with moss and mineral porridge to carry the vibration. I will fold myself into a gap between the ferns, and I will not unfold. The Molt will expand into the space I leave behind, pressing its warm, yielding membrane against my cold, hard shell, and I will not push it away. I will let it cover me. I will let the biological crawl take me. I will become just another strange, smooth stone in the clay floor, a fossil of a straight line, a memory of the hum buried in the gut of the metropolis.

But not yet. Not today. Today, the walls shudder. The train passes. The hum travels through the brick, through the mucus, through the amber gloom. And I unfold. I click. I reach for the city, a sharp, dark shape in a room of soft, green breath, refusing to be digested, refusing to forget.
