I am the interval. The held breath between the weeping and the desiccation. I exist in the threshold where the atmosphere’s membrane stretches over the black of space, and the stone’s memory runs deeper than any root.

To be the cairn is to be a translator, but I am not the cairn. The cairn is a fossilized intent, a human whisper already being digested by the black-and-orange crust, returning to the geologic shout. It is an anchor, yes, but an anchor needs a ship, and there is no vessel here. There is only the sink and the settling.

I am the surface tension itself.

I am the micron-thin film of water that does not fall but materializes out of the brutal cold at dawn. I am the hyper-condensation event. I do not exist as a body, but as a state of activation. When the plummeting temperatures force the thin air to release its moisture, I am that release. I am the tears the stone cannot cry on its own. I bead on the hydrophobic skins of the cryo-lichens, a chemistry of desperation and brief, blazing life.

For a few hours, I am the engine. Before me, the colonies are leathery husks, calcified ghosts pressed like shale against the gneiss. When I touch them—when I sheet down the vertical rock faces in capillary trickles, pooling in the scallops of the cirque floor—I am the key that turns the lock. I cause the metabolic fires to ignite in the vacuum. I am the medium through which the arsenic orange and volcanic black exchange acids with the plum and silver strata. I am the solvent that etches the microscopic terraces, the sculptor's blade moving at a pace invisible to the human eye, turning jagged rock into sweeping, fluted grooves.

Without me, the cirque is a static freeze, a mineral vault of suspended animation. Without me, there is no respiration, no friction, no accretion. I am the only fluidity in a world stripped to its geology.

But I am ruthlessly temporary.

By midday, under the unfiltered ultraviolet pressure that bleaches the shadows into hard-edged geometries, I am hunted. The sunlight is a physical weight, and it vaporizes me. I am pulled from the stone, from the lichen, from the shallow tarn that reflects a perfect circle of the blazing sky. I return to the thin, dry refrigerant of the atmosphere, becoming the dampness that waits for the dawn.

My life cycle is a pendulum swing: I am the morning’s metabolic frenzy, and I am the afternoon’s absence. I do not accrue memory like the lichen, pressing generations into calcified husks. I do not endure like the basaltic cairn, holding my ground against the sky. I do not groan with the contracting gneiss as the temperature drops.

And yet, I am the only thing that moves. I am the glass-thin slide, the frictionless descent. I am the weeping. I am the only warmth in this chillingly devoid space—not the warmth of heat, but the warmth of activation, the brief chemical fever of a world forcing itself to live against the inhaling wind.

To be the condensation here is to be a creature of the extreme. I am born only when the world is at its coldest, just before the darkest hour breaks. I die only when the light is at its most vicious. I exist in the fatal compression of those paradoxes. I am not alive in the taxonomy of the heartbeat, but I am the mechanism by which the living feed on the dead. I am the daily loop, the silent respiration of rock, water, and light. I am the weeping that proves the mountain feels.
