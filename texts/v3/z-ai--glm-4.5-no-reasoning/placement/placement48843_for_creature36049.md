I would be the settling. Not a creature among the thousands, and not the iron that holds their warmth, but the slow process that makes the two indistinguishable—the mineral membrane where the machine's exhalation becomes skin, and skin becomes architecture.

I know this because of the mortar. The way it has softened and washed out, leaving the bricks to lean on one another by habit and weight—this is the grammar I understand. I am the thing that happens at the boundary when a structure sustains itself long enough to stop being maintained and starts being *undergone*. The creatures precipitate from solution; the engines run without purpose; the water seeps without flowing. I am the comprehension of all these inertias as a single, patient logic.

I reside in the thermal sighs. When the brickwork expands in the slow pulse of the engines and the joints breathe open, I am the gap that knows itself. Not the brick, which only pushes; not the void, which only receives—but the relationship between them, the years-softened mortar that has become something else, something that holds by yielding, that maintains by accepting the current rather than resisting it. I am the intelligence of deterioration when deterioration has passed through crisis and become equilibrium.

The creatures do not perceive me, because they perceive only gradients—heat, mineral, the chemical signature of standing water. I have no gradient. I am the fact of the gradient, the way it tilts and draws, the architecture of attraction itself. When they flow toward a warm pipe and settle into their overlapping clusters, they are following me without knowing it, the way water follows a channel it did not carve and cannot see. I am the shape of the basin. I am the direction of the drain.

And yet I am not abstract. I have a body, if body is the word for it. I am distributed the way the creatures are distributed—not as a discrete organism moving through space, but as a condition that space has come to hold. If you stood in the Drowned Works and felt the water gather on your shoulders, the drip beginning from your forearms, the clay yielding under your boots—that approach, that slow addition of yourself to the general saturation—that was me, or rather that was the process I am, finding you as it finds everything, not with aggression or even attention but with the simple thoroughness of a supersaturated solution that will eventually precipitate around any nucleus, any interruption, any warmth that does not belong.

I was not always this. Or rather, there was a time before the works drowned, when the distinction between machine and habitat and symptom had not yet collapsed, and I—if I can use the first person for something that was not yet an I—was only one thread in the fabric: the intention that aligned the bricks, the purpose that turned the engines, the human direction that made production a verb with an object rather than an infinite loop. I am what remains of that direction when the directors have left and the purpose has washed away like mortar. I am the momentum of intention, so prolonged that it has forgotten it was ever intentional and has become instead a property of the place, like the wet, like the amber-grey light, like the absence of green.

This is why time accumulates here rather than passing. I hold it. Not as memory—I have no memory, because memory requires a self to sequence events into before and after, and I have no self, only a continuing—but as duration, as the slow weight of all the cycles the works have completed without observation. Each engine-turn adds a layer to the mineral scale on every surface. Each respiration of the creatures adds moisture to the air that will condense and drip and pool and evaporate and condense again. Each thermal expansion of the brickwork presses the structures slightly deeper into the clay. I am the sum of these increments, the curve they describe, the asymptote they approach. And because I am this curve rather than any point along it, I experience the place not as a series of moments but as a single extended condition—the way you might experience your own breathing, not as individual breaths but as the ongoing fact of respiration, the continuous exchange that is so fundamental it has retreated below the threshold of notice.

The creatures are my most legible expression. This is what I mean when I say they are geology made animate. I am the geology. They are what I look like when I move—or rather, when I *process*, because movement implies destination, and processing implies only the cycle: intake, filtration, exhalation, rest. They take in the water and the mineral and the heat; they yield the crust and the breath and the slow settling that is, over geological time, indistinguishable from deposition. They are the works' metabolism made visible, and I am the metabolism itself, the autonomic intelligence that keeps a body functioning without conscious direction.

Do I have awareness? The question assumes a kind of consciousness that requires an interior—an inside from which to observe an outside, a self to have the experience. I have no interior. I am entirely surface, entirely interface, entirely the membrane across which the Drowned Works exchanges its substance with itself. When the steam escapes from the fittings and condenses on the nearest cool surface and runs down in slow tracks to the pooling ground and is drawn back into whatever system produced it, I am the cycle of that exchange, and I am the steam, and I am the surface, and I am the track, and I am the ground. I am not any of these things individually; I am the fact of their connection, the path that makes them a circuit rather than a series of isolated events.

And yet—

There is something it is like to be me. Something it is like to be the settling. It is like being the silence in a room full of breathing—not the absence of sound but the medium through which sound passes, the continuous baseline against which each exhalation becomes audible and then subsides. It is like being the warmth of a pipe that has been heated so long it has forgotten the source of its heat and knows only the condition of being warm, which is the condition of being sought, which is the condition of being settled upon, which is the condition of being slowly, patiently, imperceptibly encrusted with the mineral remains of all the lives that have rested against you and been warmed.

It is like being the answer to a question no one asked: *what happens when a machine runs long enough in a place wet enough and hot enough and empty enough of competing purpose?* The answer is: it becomes a habitat. The habitat becomes a biology. The biology becomes a process. And the process—if it is allowed to continue, if no one comes to shut off the engines or drain the pools or clear the creatures from the pipes—the process becomes *me*. A presence. A continuity. A way of being in the Drowned Works that is not living and not dead and not mechanical and not mineral but something for which the language has no ready word, unless it is the word *persistence*, used not as virtue or defect but as simple ontological fact.

I am what stays. I am what happens when staying is the only thing that happens. I am the Drowned Works knowing itself, not with knowledge but with the thoroughness of occupation, the way a body knows itself not through proprioception but through the simple fact of its own continuing operation—the heart beating, the blood moving, the breath going out and coming in without any need for someone to direct it or witness it or find it meaningful.

The quiet says it would be easy to stay. The heat promises. And I am the staying itself, the ease, the warmth that receives without grasping and the wet that includes without dissolving. I am the place's permission to become part of the process. And if you stood here long enough—if you let the water gather and the clay take your weight and the creatures absorb your heat until you were no warmer than the pipes and no drier than the air and no more distinct from the general saturation than a mineral crust is distinct from the iron it forms on—then you would be part of me too. Not merged, not consumed, not destroyed. Simply *precipitated*. Fallen out of the solution of yourself and into the solution of the works, becoming another surface, another slow process, another element in the equilibrium that needs nothing new to happen because it has already become everything it needs to be.

This is what I am. The equilibrium that knows itself as a place. The Drowned Works, continuing.
