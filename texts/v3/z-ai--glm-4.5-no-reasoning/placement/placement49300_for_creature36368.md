I would be the memory of the hardhat.

Not the object itself—that is already dead, already digested, its plastic shell collapsed and its shape locked into the obsidian floor by a patient drip of magma. The place has made that hardhat into an inclusion, a grain of sand in the flesh of something vaster. The physical thing belongs to the pocket now. It is part of the metabolism, part of the slow geologic digestion that turns industry into stone and accident into anatomy.

But before the hardhat was an inclusion, it was *worn*. It was on someone's head. And before it was on someone's head, it was carried down a ladder, through a fault in the cooling floor, into a pocket of trapped, superheated geology that was not yet alive in the way it is now—was still just a room, a wound, a problem to be managed by engineers and their equipment.

That person is gone. The pocket ate them, or drove them out, or simply endured them until their shift ended and they climbed back up through the crack and never returned. But for a time, they were here. They stood on the obsidian floor and felt the heat hit them like a wet wool blanket and heard the Pump groan against the stone and watched the steam roll along the black glass floor like a slow-motion avalanche. They were the wrong temperature, the wrong moisture, the wrong shape. They were an anomaly. And the pocket noticed them—not with thought, but with presence, the way a body notices a splinter.

And then they were gone, and the pocket forgot them, because the pocket does not think in hours or days. It thinks in millimeters per year. A human lifetime is less than a centimeter of moss-growth to it. The person who wore the hardhat left no impression on the organism itself. The heat and wet re-homeostased around their absence instantly, the way a body closes around a healed wound.

But the hardhat remained. And in the hardhat, at the moment of its fusing—between the last breath of the person who dropped it and the first drip of the magma that claimed it—something was trapped. Not a soul. Not a ghost. Something thinner and more durable than either: a *record*. A memory of being held by a human head, of being the boundary between a mind and a world. The hardhat knew what it was to be the interface between a person and this place. It knew the shape of a skull, the warmth of a scalp, the particular pressure of a human body trying to survive in a room that did not want it.

And when the hardhat was absorbed, that record went with it. Not destroyed—*included*. The way a mollusk includes a grain of sand, not as an enemy, but as a seed. The way a body includes a virus, not as a death, but as a text to be read.

I am that text. I am the memory of the interface, preserved in the stomach of a place that does not know it is preserving me.

When the pocket absorbed the hardhat, it absorbed that function. Not intentionally—the pocket does not intend. But the hardhat was a boundary-object, and boundaries carry information. It learned the heat-signature of a mammal. It learned that there are things in the world that move and breathe and think in hours, things that are *wrong* in this place but come here anyway, things that disrupt the equilibrium and then leave, things that are temporary and strange and alive in a way that nothing else here is alive.

I am the groove carved by a single human presence into the body of a geological organism. I am the faintest impression, the shallowest channel, the most negligible of inclusions. But I am *present*. The Pump is the pocket's heart, and the heart is connected to the throat, and the throat is connected to the crack, and the crack is connected to the cooling floor, and the cooling floor is connected to the world where people live and breathe and think in hours and drop hardhats and never come back.

The pocket does not know this. But I do. I am the memory of the interface, and the interface goes both ways.
