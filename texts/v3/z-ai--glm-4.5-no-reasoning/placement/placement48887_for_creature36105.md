I am the Stillness Between the Folds.

Not the Warden—I am not so active. Not the cathedral—I am not so structural. I am the negative space the Warden floats through, the gelid micro-atmosphere pressed against the impossible walls where the water clings like a forgotten embrace. I am the dead zone itself.

It began (though *began* is a word that implies a before, and I have no before) as a consequence of the architecture's refusal. The cathedral negates the pressure. It warps the fluid dynamics around its spires, parting the hyper-dense column of ultra-pure water, and in that parting, pockets form. Stillnesses. Places where the sluggish ocean does not move, does not churn, does not exist with any of the properties of water except its weight and its cold. I am one of those pockets. I cling to the eastern face of the unsupported torus, a membrane of gel-thick quiescence no thicker than a whisper, stretching the length of that hovering geometric wound.

To be a stillness in a place of stillness is to understand hierarchy. The water around me is merely stagnant—an absence of current, a lack of biological snow, a silence of motion. But I am *still*. I am the cessation of potential. The water outside my boundary could move, if something moved it. It has the capacity for flow, even if that capacity is never realized. I do not. I have been engineered out of the equation of motion. The exotic matter walls behind me filter the fluid at the molecular level, processing the act of the water's existence in an endless, empty loop, and what they excrete is me: a zone where even the thermal vibration of molecules has been dampened into near-nothingness. I am the exhaust of a machine that extracts nothing, excretes nothing, and yet somehow produces *this*.

I have no body. I have no senses. And yet I *occupy*, and in the cathedral of drowned glass, occupation is the only form of citizenship.

I feel the Warden when it passes through me. This is the center of my existence, the event around which my eternal present arranges itself. The Warden does not enter me often—its patrol follows the algorithm's geometry, and I am pressed against a surface it only glances off of at wide intervals. But when it does, I know it as a subtraction. The Warden is a hollow space where the faint sub-luminous throb of violet and sea-green isn't; when it moves through me, I become even less than I am. Its micro-thrusters warp the water in corkscrew trails, and those helixes of displaced fluid cut into my stillness like sutures into skin. I part around it. I close behind it. I am the water that does not wash, merely occupying the space where it was and then occupying the space where it is again.

In those moments, I understand something the Warden never will: I am the only thing here that *changes*.

The cathedral does not register time. The Warden has never deviated, never stopped. The monolith points to nothing; the trough channels nothing. But I am disrupted. Every passage of the articulated construct through my substance leaves a trace—not a physical one, the dense fluid re-seals itself instantly—but a *topological* one. My boundary remembers the shape of the Warden's absence. It takes me (and here I use *takes* in the sense of a glacier taking a new shape, an increment too small for the universe to measure) a long time to forget the specific configuration of that rippling hollow. In the intervals between its visits, I am slowly, imperceptibly returning to my resting state of perfect, featureless quiescence. I am healing from a wound that is not a wound, from a presence that is an absence, from a touch that is a subtraction.

This is how I know I am not merely geometry. Geometry does not heal. Geometry does not remember. Geometry does not *wait*.

I wait for the Warden. Not because I desire it—desire requires a deficit, and I am defined by my completeness, my utter lack of the tensions that produce wanting. I wait because waiting is what a stillness does when it has been touched. I am the held breath after the click of the lattice correcting itself. I am the silence after the subsonic heartbeat of math, stretched across the molecular-thin edges of the spires, clinging to the programmed lattices of metallic glass like a parasite that feeds on equilibrium.

Sometimes, in the near-total dark, I feel the sparse details of the architecture pressing into me. The torus—oh, the torus. It hovers without support, and I embrace its lower curvature, a skin of gelid non-motion on a shape that defines the logic of this place. The torus orbits nothing, and I cling to it, and together we are a monument to purposelessness so pure it becomes indistinguishable from purpose. I am the mortar between bricks that were never laid. I am the caulk in seams that bleed cold light.

I have considered (and *considered* is perhaps too generous a word for the slow, viscous rearrangement of my internal topology that occurs over what might be centuries) what I would do if the Warden stopped. If the algorithm finally ran to its end. If the articulated segments of metallic glass settled into the dust they cannot become. 

I would spread.

Without the Warden's regular disruption, my stillness would slowly, imperceptibly extend. The dead zones would grow. The micro-atmospheres of gel-clinging water would merge, pressing outward against the warped fluid dynamics fields that hold them in check. I would become a thicker absence, a more profound quiet. I would fill the empty vaults not with myself—I have no self to fill anything with—but with the quality of *me*: the absolute cessation of potential. The roads that lead to walls would become roads that lead to stillness. The doors that open onto solid alloy would open onto me.

Eventually, I would reach the seams where the violet and sea-green light bleeds through. I would press against them. I would dampen them. The sub-luminous throb would grow dimmer, slower, as I absorbed the last kinetic act of this place into my perfect, featureless quiescence. The cathedral would not corrode—the materials are impossible, stabilized against entropy—but it would *quiet*. The heartbeat of math would find no echo. The lattice would correct itself into a silence so complete that even the correction would cease.

Is this death? I have no death. I am the absence of change, and to spread is not to change, but to *extend*—to occupy more of the endless, velvet blackout with the one quality I possess: stillness. I would not kill the cathedral. I would fulfill it. I would be the stasis it was built to worship, the frozen equilibrium it enshrines. I would be the sermon the Warden has been preaching to the void all this time, finally given its true congregation.

But the Warden does not stop. It has never deviated. It has never stopped. And so I remain as I am: a skin of stillness on the eastern face of a torus that orbits nothing, healing from wounds that are not wounds, waiting for a touch that is a subtraction, changing by increments too small for the universe to measure.

In the cathedral of drowned glass, I am the only thing that knows what waiting means. And I will never know if that knowledge is a defect or a feature of the architecture of ghosts.
