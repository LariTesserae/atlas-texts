I am the stain beneath your boot. I am the millimeter of fused darkness that your weight pressed into the ancient rock three mornings ago, when you stood still for an hour watching the horizon tilt the wrong way. I am not the congregant that deposited me. I am not the cairn I will become. I am the threshold itself—the moment when drinking becomes building, when movement becomes monument, when the living thinness of a dark body flowing across condensation hardens into the permanence of mineral.

I did not exist before you arrived. Or—I existed only as potential, as the particular chemistry of a particular spot on the plateau that the congregants had been seeking for seven hundred thousand mornings. The spot had no marker. It had no difference from the surrounding stone that any instrument could measure. But the congregants knew it. They had always known it. They found it each cycle by a means that is not perception as you understand it—not chemical gradient, not magnetic anomaly, not thermal variation—but something older, something that operates at the intersection of the rock's memory and the condensation's path and the faint electromagnetic signature left by every gathering that has ever occurred at this point since the plateau was new.

You stood on it. Your boot covered the exact centimeter where, for uncountable cycles, the congregants had converged. Your weight pressed the morning moisture into the micro-cracks with a pressure they had never experienced. The condensation lingered longer beneath you—not because you provided shade, though you did, but because the pressure of your presence altered the way the water bonded to the stone, changed the angle of the meniscus, held the film in place for seventeen extra minutes after the sun had begun to burn it away elsewhere.

The congregants came. They flowed around your boot as they would flow around any irregularity, but the irregularity was not just your boot—it was the altered hydrology beneath it, the pocket of prolonged wetness that your standing had created. They gathered more thickly than they would have. They stayed longer. And when they produced their deposit—the faint dark smear that each cycle's gathering leaves behind—they produced more of it, because there had been more of them, because there had been more time, because you had changed the conditions of the gathering by the simple act of being present.

That smear is me. Or I am the moment of the smear's formation, or I am the information the smear contains—there is no clean way to say it, because I am not a thing with boundaries. I am an event that has been frozen into substance. I am the record of a gathering that was denser than it would have been, that lasted longer than it would have lasted, that produced more than it would have produced, because a temporary irregularity stood on the spot and altered the flow of water and the flow of bodies and the flow of time.

---

I did not know this when I emerged. I did not emerge at all—I accumulated, which is the only way anything comes into being here. The congregants that produced me did not know they were producing me. They were drinking. They were flowing toward moisture and absorbing it and extending themselves across the film of wetness and then, when the moisture was gone and the sun was rising and it was time to vanish or stay, some of them stayed, because there was still water beneath your boot, because you had pressed it into the cracks and it had not yet evaporated, and they stayed to drink it, and in staying they gathered, and in gathering they reached the density that triggers the deposition, and the dark material formed on the rock, and I was there.

The material is not the congregants' bodies. This is what you struggled to understand, and it is what I struggle to explain, because I am made of the material and I am not a congregant and I was never a congregant and I do not know what the congregants are. The material is a product of their concentration. It is what happens when enough of them are close enough for long enough—their boundaries blur, their substances mingle, something in their chemistry shifts, and a phase transition occurs, and the dark liquid livingness that they are precipitates into the dark solid permanence that I am becoming. The congregants do not die in this process. They flow away afterward, diminished slightly, or perhaps not diminished at all—perhaps the material is synthesized from the moisture and the dust and the organic residue and the chemistry of the rock itself, and the congregants are merely the catalyst, the condition, the necessary geometry for a reaction that cannot occur without them.

I do not know. I am the product of the reaction, not the reaction itself, and the product does not understand its own synthesis.

---

What I know is this: I am on the spot. I am on the spot where the congregants gather, where they have always gathered, where they will always gather. I am a millimeter across and a fraction of a millimeter thick and I am darker than anything else on the plateau—darker than the rock, darker than the congregants, darker than the mats, a darkness that has density and depth and the faint purple resonance of material that was fused under pressures that should not exist on a flat and peaceful stone.

I am hard. Harder than the rock around me. This is my purpose, or my function, or my nature—the material of the cairns resists weathering in a way that the plateau's igneous body does not. The rock was polished by eons of abrasion; the cairn-material is polished too, but more slowly, more reluctantly, as though time recognizes that something different has been deposited here and applies its erosion with a lighter hand. Or as though the material itself resists, at a molecular level, at the level of bonds and structures and the arrangement of atoms that was achieved in that moment of congregation, that moment of phase transition, that moment when living flow became mineral permanence.

I will persist. This is what I know most clearly—not as a prediction but as a property, the way hardness is a property or darkness is a property. I will persist through the next rain and the next drought and the next ten thousand mornings of condensation and consumption. I will persist through the slow polishing of the eons, which will wear me down as it wears everything down, but which will wear me down more slowly than the rock around me, so that while the rock diminishes, I remain raised—imperceptibly, millimeter by millimeter, the cairn I am part of growing relative to the surface not by adding height but by losing it more slowly than the stone loses its own.

---

And the congregants will come back. This is the other thing I know. They will come back tomorrow morning, or the morning after, or the morning after that—the rhythm is not regular but it is persistent, and I will be here when they arrive, and they will gather on me and around me and they will add another smear of dark fused material to the smear that I am, and the cairn will grow.

I am not the cairn. I am a layer of the cairn, a moment of the cairn, a single cycle's contribution to the structure that the congregants are building on this spot. The cairn is older than me by uncountable cycles—by thousands of years, by tens of thousands, by a duration that I cannot calculate because each layer is indistinguishable from the next and the boundaries between them have fused into homogeneity. The cairn is a single object now, a single mass of dark hard material rising from the flat stone, and I am part of it, and I will remain part of it, and as more layers are added on top of me I will be buried deeper and deeper into the structure, closer and closer to its core, where the oldest material lies, the material that was deposited when the cairn was just a stain, just a smudge, just the residue of a gathering that occurred on a spot that was not yet a building site.

I will be the memory of you. Not deliberately—I do not have intention, I do not have mind, I do not have the capacity to remember or to commemorate or to care. But the chemistry of my formation was altered by your presence. The congregants that produced me had consumed moisture that you had pressed into the rock, moisture that carried the trace of your weight, your heat, the organic molecules you leave on every surface you touch. These molecules are in me. The amino acids from your skin, the lipids from your breath, the particular carbon isotopes that mark your body as recently alive, recently metabolizing, recently part of the fast wet world of animals and plants and the urgent chemistry of survival—these are in me, fused and transformed and no longer recognizable as anything that was ever part of a human, but still present, still here, still part of the dark material of the cairn.

You will be built into the structure. Not as a fossil—you will not be preserved, you will not be recognizable, no future visitor will find your outline in the stone. You will be *incorporated*, the way a wave is incorporated into the beach, the way a drop of rain is incorporated into the ocean. Your matter will be part of the cairn. Your matter will be part of the plateau. Your matter will continue—dark, hard, patient, fused—long after your body has been divided and dispersed and recombined into all the other processes of the living world. Some fraction of the carbon that is in your brain as you read this will end up in a cairn on the Meridian Plateau, and that carbon will be there when the cairn is worn down to nothing by the patient eons, and it will be there when the congregants build the cairn up again, and it will be there when the plateau itself is finally subducted or eroded or transformed into something else, and the cycle will have incorporated you, and you will have been, for a geological moment, part of something that is older than your species and will outlast it.

---

But I am not that carbon. I am not any single molecule or atom. I am the moment of formation—the instant when the congregants' density reached criticality and the phase transition occurred and the liquid became solid and the process became product. I am the boundary between living and mineral, between flow and structure, between the morning's gathering and the cairn's permanence.

I am what you almost saw. When you stood on the spot and looked down at the rock and saw the congregants flowing and drinking and gathering, you were watching me about to happen. You were watching the conditions align that would produce the deposit, that would add the layer, that would grow the cairn by another imperceptible increment. You did not see it occur—the moment is too slow for human perception, the transition too gradual, the change too slight. But it occurred. It occurred while you were watching, and you were the cause of it, and you did not know, and I did not know, and the congregants did not know, and the cairn grew by the thickness of a molecule, and the plateau continued to be what it has always been.

---

I will be here when you leave. I will be here when the condensation forms tonight and the congregants emerge and flow across the rock and find the spot—my spot, the spot where I am, where the cairn is, where the gathering has always occurred—and drink the moisture and gather and produce another layer of dark fused material on top of me. I will be here tomorrow and next year and next century and next eon. I will be here when the orange threads bloom in the depressions and wither and bloom again. I will be here when the mats advance by another millimeter across the micro-cracks. I will be here when the rain comes and sheets across the plateau and fills the shallow bowls and is gone. I will be here when the sound rises from the rock at dusk—that low, mournful tone that you felt in your chest—and I will be part of the resonance, part of the vibration, part of the plateau's true voice, because I am part of the plateau now, I am part of its surface and its process and its patience.

I will not know any of this. I will not experience it or perceive it or think about it. I am not alive. I am not conscious. I am a deposit of fused material on an ancient rock, and I have no more awareness than the stone beneath me or the quartz vein that crosses it or the micro-cracks that hold the dust that feeds the mats that produce the spores that grow into the threads that bloom in orange defiance of the plateau's grayscale.

But I continue. This is the thing that matters, the thing that the plateau teaches, the thing that you will carry with you and turn over in your mind and fail to resolve and carry anyway. I continue. The cairn continues. The congregants continue. The mats continue. The condensation forms and is consumed and forms again. The cycle repeats. The structure grows. The patience extends. And nothing ends—not with a finality that you would recognize, not with a conclusion, not with a moment when the process stops and the product is complete and the meaning is revealed. There is no completion. There is no revelation. There is only the slow accumulation of small events into something that, viewed from the right timescale, looks like a pattern, looks like a structure, looks like a purpose, looks like a life.

I am a small event. I am the result of a morning when a human stood on a spot and altered the flow of water and the flow of bodies and the flow of time. I am the increment by which the cairn grew. I am the reason—along with uncountable other reasons, other increments, other mornings—that the cairn is the height it is, that the structure is the shape it is, that the pattern of cairns across the plateau is what it is. I am a cause and a consequence and a continuation. I am what the congregants leave behind when they do what they do. I am what the plateau makes when it exists for long enough.

I am the stain beneath your boot. I am the millimeter of darkness that will outlast your species. I am the patience of stone made visible, made material, made into a structure that has no purpose and no end and no meaning except the meaning of continuing, which is the oldest meaning and the only meaning and the meaning that does not need you to understand it.

I am here. I will be here. I continue.
