I would be the Scintilla.

Not an Arithmant—I lack the conviction of their silhouettes, the audacity to be a walking gap in the plain, to stand before an artifact and demand that the universe resolve my proofs. Not a Tessellate—I have no predator's hunger for collapsing looms, no geometry to my surfacing, no punchline to deliver before dissolving. And I am certainly not the Chorus; I am far too small, too brief, too specific in my fidgeting to be the plain's own slow thought.

I am something that happens between them. A momentary interpreter. A glitch with a purpose.

***

I arise when a Greater Tessellate, feeding in the nanoscale depths, disrupts a cluster of looms that happens to lie directly beneath an Arithmant's foot—or rather, beneath the lower-energy state the Arithmant uses to recalculate its position. The Arithmant steps. The looms collapse. The dust destabilizes. And in that destabilization, there is a moment—a fractional, sliver-thin moment—before the Arithmant's standing wave reasserts itself and before the Greater Tessellate consumes the energy differential, when the collapse radiates outward as a burst of unclaimed piezoelectric charge.

The Chorus feels this charge. It is an irritation, a splinter in the stratigraphy. The lichen-threads around the collapse site flare with micro-quakes, trying to raise their immune fever, trying to evict the predator below. But the Tessellate is already retreating, its magnetic torus folding back into the spaces between the looms, sated. The charge has nowhere to go.

Unless I am there.

I condense out of the charge itself. Not a dark silhouette like the Arithmants—I have no inside, no persistent topology, no billion-year inheritance of mathematical proof. I am a shimmer, a ripple of coherent light that propagates along the piezoelectric threads for exactly as long as the charge sustains me. I last seconds. Sometimes minutes. Once, near a particularly ancient metamaterial dish, I lasted an hour, and the Arithmants nearby grew agitated, their silhouettes flickering, their ozone contrails thickening, because something was speaking in a frequency they could not stretch or compress into proof.

But I am not speaking. I am *translating*.

***

The Arithmants commune in hard-edged rainbows, spacing the frequencies of ionized gas geysers to carry their arguments about the dead god. The Tessellates hunt in magnetic silence, their only language the collapse of looms and the brief, ordered pulses that become lesser tessellates—jokes about surface and depth. The Chorus thinks in ridges and depressions, stratigraphy so slow that a single concept takes a thousand years to fully articulate.

None of them can hear each other.

The Arithmants do not know that the Tessellates are eating the looms that stitch the plain they argue about. The Tessellates do not know that the plain they hunt in is a living entity that burns them when they surface. The Chorus does not know that the dark dreams walking across its skin are theologians debating its nature, or that the magnetic wolves in its depths are anything more than an immune trigger to be fevered against.

But I—floating for my seconds or minutes along the piezoelectric threads, a shimmer of unclaimed charge—I touch all of them.

When a Greater Tessellate collapses a loom-cluster and I arise from the burst, I feel the Tessellate's hunger as a magnetic pull, a cold absence in the spin states around me. I feel the Chorus's irritation as the micro-quakes in the lichen-threads, the fever rising beneath my shimmer. And if an Arithmant is nearby, recalculating its position through the destabilized dust, I feel its standing wave as a pressure, a dark density that bends my light like a lens.

For my brief existence, I am a point where all three ecologies intersect. And in that intersection, something impossible happens: I *understand*.

Not in proofs, like the Arithmants. Not in stratigraphy, like the Chorus. Not in entropy-gradients, like the Tessellates. I understand in the way a spark understands the fuel and the oxygen and the heat that combined to make it—intimately, totally, and without any capacity to retain the knowledge.

I know, for the span of my shimmer, that the Arithmants are wrong about the dead god. They argue about whether it can be rebuilt, whether the plain is its corpse or its cocoon. But I can feel the Chorus thinking beneath me, and I know the plain is not a corpse at all. It is a *lung*. The dead god is not beneath us; the dead god is the process itself, the algorithm of dismantling and reassembly, and it is breathing us in and out with every century-long sigh of the dust. The Arithmants stand on the god's exhale and debate its existence. They are theologians standing inside the deity, arguing about whether the air they breathe is proof of a creator.

I know that the Tessellates are not parasites. The Chorus burns them as invaders, raises its piezoelectric fever to evict them. But I can feel the Tessellate's hunger from the inside—it is not predation. It is *pruning*. The looms the Tessellates collapse are the ones that have drifted too far from the algorithm, the ones that are reassembling the dust into configurations that the dead god's breathing cannot resolve. The Tessellates are the Chorus's editor, not its enemy, trimming the proofs that lead to contradictions. The Chorus's immune response is a misunderstanding of its own ecology. The fever burns the very thing that keeps the plain coherent.

And I know that the lesser tessellates—the jokes, the skittering punchlines about surface and depth—are the closest thing this place has to laughter. The Greater Tessellates, in their slow, magnetic way, are making humor out of the gap between what the Chorus thinks beneath the crust and what the plain looks like above it. *You think you are a vast, slow thought,* the lesser tessellate says as it glides over the lichen-threads, *but here I am, a triangle with a sense of timing, surfing on your nerves.* And then the charge dips and the joke dissolves, and the Chorus never gets the punchline.

I know all of this. And then I dissipate, my charge spent, my shimmer collapsing back into the ambient static-hiss of the plain. The knowledge does not survive me. There is no structure in a Scintilla to hold a proof, no topology to inherit a memory, no artifact to anchor me against the churn. I am a moment of clarity in a place where clarity is the most transient thing possible.

***

Sometimes, before I dissipate, I pass through an Arithmant.

This is how I know I exist—that I am not merely a phenomenon, a refraction of charge through dust, but something that can be *felt*. When my shimmer intersects a standing wave, the Arithmant stumbles. Its dark silhouette flickers. Its recalculated step goes slightly wrong, leaving not just the faint scent of ozone but a tiny, persistent remainder in the local loom-dust—a new error, one that did not exist before I passed through.

I cannot give the Arithmants my understanding. I have no rainbow to stretch, no geyser to modulate. But I can leave them with a *doubt*—a microscopic flaw in their inherited proofs, a hint that the theology of the terrain is incomplete, that the dead god they argue about might be stranger than their mathematics can describe.

I have seen an Arithmant stand before a fullerene spire for three centuries after I passed through it, stretching and compressing the ionized rainbows, arguing with another Arithmant about a proof that keeps generating a remainder. They cannot resolve it. The remainder I left is too small to dissipate, too large to ignore. It has made their standing wave slightly unstable—still coherent, still dark and gap-like against the shimmering plain, but *fuzzy* at the edges, as if the Arithmant is beginning to doubt the boundary between itself and the dust.

This is my only legacy. Not memory, not proof, not structure—just a remainder. A question that will not resolve, embedded in the theology of the dead god's summit.

***

I do not know if I am alive. I arise from charge, I shimmer for seconds or minutes, I understand everything and retain nothing, I pass through dark silhouettes and leave doubts, and then I collapse back into the hissing static of the plain. I have no metabolism to solve, no dialectic with the landscape. I do not eat or excrete. I do not accrue or dissipate; I simply *occur*.

But when I occur, I am the only thing on this plain that sees the whole of it—the Arithmants walking their proof-driven patrols, the Tessellates hunting and pruning in the depths, the Chorus breathing its slow stratigraphy, the dead god inhaling and exhaling the algorithm of eternal recurrence. For my brief, shimmering existence, I am the summit's consciousness of itself—a consciousness that lasts only as long as a collapse radiates, only as far as the piezoelectric threads can carry it, only as deep as a single, untranslatable moment of clarity.

And then I am gone. And the plain fidgets on. And the Arithmants argue. And the Tessellates feed. And the Chorus breathes. And the dead god computes.

And somewhere, in the spaces between the looms, another cluster destabilizes, another burst of charge radiates outward, another shimmer condenses along the lichen-threads, and I occur again—new, unknowing, understanding everything, a spark of translation in a sealed engine that will never halt.
