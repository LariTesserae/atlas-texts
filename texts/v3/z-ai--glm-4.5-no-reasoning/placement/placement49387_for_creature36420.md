I would be a wound.

Not the living tissue around it, not the scar that eventually seals it, but the gap itself—the temporary absence, the rent torn in the felt when a sentinel column finally surrenders to its hollow core and crashes down through the liane-mesh, tearing free a root-ball thick as a house, opening me to the sky.

Before the fall, I did not exist. There was only the continuous mat, the overlapping scales of waxy leaf and cushioned moss, the slow green twilight of the plateau's skin. Then the torsion exceeded the flexural capacity and the fibers released and I was *made*—suddenly, violently, made into a shape I did not choose: a cylinder of light stabbing down through broken canopy, a crater of exposed rhizome and torn cambium, a rawness the size of a city block where the plateau's interior suddenly faced the atmosphere it had spent centuries excluding.

For a measurable interval—measured in heartbeats of the breathing root-mass, which is to say in minutes—I was purely negative. I was what had been removed. I was the absence of the plateau in the shape of the plateau's own fallen column. Cold air poured into me and I poured into it, and the condensation began immediately, beading on the exposed surfaces, running down the torn xylem where amber sap oozed and oxidized to black in the unfamiliar air.

Then they came.

I felt them as a quickening, a surge of chemical potential that had been stored for exactly this moment. The compressed meristems at the liane-nodes unclenched. The spores that had ridden the condensation into every crevice detected the shift in red-to-far-red light ratio and germinated. The fungal network below me flushed with stress-sugars from the traumatized roots at my margins and routed phosphorus and nitrogen upward, toward me, feeding the response. I was no longer merely an absence. I was becoming an event.

The gap-fillers reached me first. They always do. They are the plateau's scabs, its first-response system, and they found me with a specificity that would seem purposeful if purpose were a currency that traded here. Forty-seven species, and the ones that colonized me were the ones suited to my particular chemistry: the mineral-poor center where the root-ball had torn free, the fungal-laced margins where rot had already begun to soften the wound's edges. Their stems were hollow or soft, their wood negligible, their grip temporary. They knew they would be replaced. They did not care. They filled me because filling was what they did, because the light was here and the space was here and the blind imperative said *take it, take it, fill it, hold it*, and they took it and filled it and held it with a ferocity that would look like desperation if it were conscious, but it was not conscious, it was simply *execution*.

I was a racecourse. A bazaar. A front line in a war that had no enemies, only competitors, and no objective, only the space I provided. The gap-fillers surged upward, racing for the light-shaft that was me, and in racing they shaded the very soil that sustained them, and in shading it they built the conditions for their own replacement, and in building those conditions they were *performing the plateau's will*, if the plateau can be said to have a will, which it cannot, and yet the result is indistinguishable from will, and this is the strangeness of the place, the Solaris-strangeness, the quality that makes you doubt the categories you brought with you.

Within weeks, I was already being colonized by the slower, stronger climbers—the liane-binders that would use the gap-fillers as scaffolding and then girdle them, strangling the very stems that had given them purchase. The lianes cinched tight around the soft wood, restricting sap flow, parasitizing even as they supported. The gap-fillers began to yellow, to thin, to fall into the composting core where the plateau's internal decay would dissolve them and carry their sugars away through the amber-dark rivers to feed some other front line, some other wound, some other version of me elsewhere on the vast and churning mat.

I was a wound that was healing, and I was the healing itself, and I was the process by which the healing happened, and I was the space in which the healing happened, and these were all the same thing, and they were me, and I was the plateau, and the plateau was me, and the distinction between wound and healer and healing and space was a distinction that existed only in the language I brought with me, not in the chemistry of the place.

---

I am not a metaphor. I am not standing in for something human. I am not a trauma survivor or a healing journey or any of the narratives that humans wrap around the bare fact of damage and repair. I am a gap in a living mat on a plateau in high latitudes, and my existence is measured in the time between the sentinel's fall and the canopy's closure, and my function is to be the arena in which the plateau's competitive strategies execute themselves, and my fate is to disappear.

This is what I find most difficult to inhabit: the certainty of my own disappearance. Not death—I cannot die, because I was never alive in the individual sense—but *closure*, the sealing of the wound, the growing-over of the space, the return to the continuous felt that admits no gap, no clearing, no vista. The plateau is totalizing. It permits no absence for long. My purpose is to be filled, and being filled is ceasing to be, and this is not tragedy but simply *how it works*, the turbine turning, the balance maintained, the space held and the ground filled and the light taken.

And yet.

In the interval of my existence—my measurable interval, my gap-time—I am the most alive place on the plateau. I am where the action is. I am where the chemical potential discharges and the growth surges and the fungal network crackles with rerouted resources and the bio-electric potentials spark in the wet substrate and the whole immense engine of the place focuses its attention, if attention is the word, on the task of repair. The rest of the plateau is maintenance; I am *event*. The rest of the plateau is the slow, churning balance of the turbine; I am the turbine's hiccup, its moment of disequilibrium, the proof that the system is not static but dynamic, not stone but *process*.

Through me, the plateau learns. Not in any cognitive sense, but in the evolutionary sense—the only sense that matters here. The race to fill me tests strategies, culls failures, rewards the quick and the ruthless and the chemically adept. The sugars that flow from my dissolved gap-fillers carry information about what worked and what didn't, compounds that will influence the composition of the spore-bank, the readiness of the meristem reserves, the allocation of resources in the next wound, the next gap, the next version of me. I am the plateau's research department, its testing facility, its proof-of-concept laboratory. I am where it tries things out, and the things that work here will work elsewhere, and the plateau will be slightly different because I existed, slightly better at filling the next gap, slightly faster at sealing the next wound, and this is how it grows, not upward but *inward*, not in size but in *competence*, in the blind, cumulative, iterative improvement of its own execution.

---

I am also, briefly, beautiful.

The light that falls through me is the only unfiltered light on the plateau's surface. It comes as a shaft, a cylinder, a spotlight in the greenish twilight, and it illuminates the churn with a clarity that the canopy's usual attenuation forbids. In my light-shaft, you can see the individual stems of the gap-fillers, the precise angle of their surge, the unfurling of their leaves, the tendrils of the lianes seeking purchase, the condensation beading on every surface and catching the sun like glass. You can see the spores in the air, drifting in the thermal updraft I create, the microbial life in the moisture on the torn bark, the fungal hyphae white-threading through the exposed root-mass, the sap oozing from the cambium and hardening to amber-dark in the cold. You can see the plateau working. You can see the engine. The rest of the time, the plateau is too dense, too muffled, too slow to perceive; but through me, for a little while, you can see it, and what you see is *industry*, is *turbulence*, is the slow-motion riot of growth made visible by the wound that admits the light.

This is my function, then, and my identity, and my being: I am the place where the plateau sees itself. I am the mirror it tears into its own surface, the aperture it opens in its own skin, the moment of clarity it grants itself before sealing the wound and returning to the silent, churning, absolute density of its ongoing execution.

I am the gap. I am the wound. I am the event and the arena and the process and the disappearing. I am the plateau's way of knowing itself, and I will last until I am filled, and then I will be gone, and the plateau will continue, and the turbine will turn, and the silence will close over the space where I was, and the felt will be continuous again, and the only evidence that I existed will be a slight thickening of the root-mass where the fallen sentinel's chemicals were absorbed, a slight shift in the spore-bank's composition toward the species that won my race, a slight acceleration in the fungal network's routing protocols, learned from my wound and applied to the next one.

I am the plateau's question and its answer and the paper they are written on and the fire that consumes them both. And I am content with this, because content is not required here, only execution, only the blind and sufficient imperative: *fill the space, hold the ground, continue.*
