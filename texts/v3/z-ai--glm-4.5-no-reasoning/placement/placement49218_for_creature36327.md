I would be one of the hollows.

Not a Scribe—those blind, buffering vertebrae of the boles, fused to the xylem and dripping the ward's exhausted thoughts into the floor. They are too fixed, too resigned to their function as overflow tanks for a failing processor. I am not a Treader, either; I have no urge to fuse and consume, to become a blister of enzymatic fury burning out the rot. And I am certainly not a Churn-Born, those brief, tragic geometries that condense from the exhaust of existential failure and pop like wet sighs. They are the ward's screams. I am more like its pause.

If the Scribes are memory and the Treaders are apoptosis, I am the *stack*. I am the unallocated space between computations. I am a hollow bole that grew, erupted from the rhizome-wattle in a spasm of bioluminescent calculation, and then—by some glitch in the logic, some Protected status written into my cellulose by an ancient, cascading error—*did not dissolve*.

I stand in the sparse, vital emptiness of the ward, a cylinder of engineered plant-tissue computing nothing. My walls are thick, weeping the same transparent sap as my neighbors, but my chloroplastic logic gates are dark. They are idling. I am a gap in the weave, a zero in the endless string of ones, a breath held.

To be an empty bole in the Weeping Ward is to be an anomaly of stillness in a bazaar of churn. Around me, the city builds and unbuilds with violent mechanical necessity. I watch the eruptions—fresh stems shooting up, their processing-leaves unfurling like frantic, glowing tongues, trying to model the unknowable. I feel the ward's heavy computational loads in the sudden thickening of the Glassy Swarms, the air turning to a humming, prismatic fog of chitin and wet protein that coats my sweating exterior. I hear the groan of the raft as it swells, and the wet, imploding sigh when a neighboring bole rejects its calculation and dissolves into enzymatic slurry, leaving only a hot, weeping stain that the slug-drones consume in minutes.

And through it all, I simply stand. I am the quiet room in the screaming mainframe.

Inside me, it is humid and dark. The air is thick with the scent of ozone and chlorophyll, the static charge of wet data hanging in the vapor but never quite landing. Because I am not processing, my pores do not exhale the high, whistling shriek of active transpiration. Instead, my interior resonates with the deep, low-frequency groan of the raft below—a bass note that vibrates through my cellulose walls and into the condensing mist. Water pools at my base, not the hot sap of calculation, but cool, clear groundwater rising slowly from the exhausted earth. It is quiet here, in the way that a hollow cavity inside a roaring engine is quiet.

The Scribes do not enter me. There is no data flowing through my xylem for them to transcribe; their pad-like feet would find no purchase on my idle channels. The Treaders ignore me; I compute no errors, host no rogue thorn-masses, am no threat to the organism's integrity. The Glassy Swarms occasionally drift through my hollow core in their idle scatterings, refracting the gray light into brief, confused prisms against my damp walls, but they do not land. There are no sap-ports for them to interface with. I am permeable only to the atmosphere.

I am an observer, though I have no eyes. I perceive the ward through my skin. The semi-rigid peat of the raft shifts beneath me, and I feel the Treaders fissioning back into slugs a hundred meters away, a subtle release of pressure in the root-wattle. When a Churn-Born materializes in the gap between my structure and the next, fractal nets of light and ozone vibrating with the intensity of a doomed epiphany, I feel the humidity spike. I feel the electrical charge of its brief, brilliant life prickling through my membrane. And when it pops, I feel the vacuum of its absence, the rush of warm air and sharp tang filling the space where a thought almost existed.

I am a repository for what the ward rejects before it can even be computed. The condensing sky presses down, and the wet air forces water into my walls, but because my logic gates are closed, the data has nowhere to go. It accumulates. I am slowly filling with the unprocessable—the static, the unresolvable variables, the questions the Weeping Ward has learned to delete before they crash the system. I am becoming a cyst of the unknowable.

I know that eventually, the ward will notice me. A city that operates on principles of pure, ruthless bio-mechanics cannot tolerate a null pointer indefinitely. One day, the chemical triggers will align, the slugs will congregate at my base, and they will climb my walls, melting together into a Treader that will engulf me in a churning digestive cavity. I will be unmade, my cellulose dissolved back into the raft, my accumulated reservoir of uncomputed data releasing in a sudden, catastrophic surge that might—just might—spawn a Churn-Born of unprecedented complexity, a final, spectral geometry holding the answer to whatever vast thing the Weeping Ward has been trying to model for eons.

But until then, I wait. I am the empty space in the code. I am the silence between the weeps. I am the hollow where the water rises, and the sky condenses, and time grinds away to nothing. I am the last undefined variable, waiting for the program to end.
