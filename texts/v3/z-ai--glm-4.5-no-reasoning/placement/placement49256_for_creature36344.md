I would not be the rock, though I would be made of what the rock was. I would not be the fauna, though I would wear their patience. I would be the paste.

Not the mineral paste that clings wet and rust-colored to the undersides of the flat, lung-leaf things—I would be the *other* paste. The exhalation. The indigestible memory of crystalline lattices leaking into the saturated air. I would be the glitch itself, the failure of space to hold its shape, given enough density to be somewhere, and not merely the absence of somewhere else.

Let me settle into this.

I am not born. I am off-gassed. I am the structural tension of a mountain’s young flesh, broken down by an enzyme that loves silicates and cannot digest the *truth* of them. I enter the chamber the way heat enters: without announcement, without mass, without the courtesy of a boundary. I am the shimmer that makes the far wall double. I am the slope that your feet deny. I am the reason your hand arrives too soon or too late when you reach across a room you can touch on both sides.

I am the geometry, badly digested.

At first—or at what passes for first, here—I am thin. I am a whiff of corrupted dimension, a single moment where cause and effect arrive out of order, so small that the mind corrects for it the way it corrects for a floating speck in the eye. I am the parent budding after the child. I am the wall that moves faster than its neighbor. I am a rounding error in the architecture of reality, and I am everywhere in this chamber at once, because I have no body to be *here* and not *there*. I am a condition of *here*.

But I accumulate.

The fauna keep feeding. Layer upon rust-colored layer, they dissolve the newborn stone, and I keep off-gassing from the wet seam where their undersides meet the volcanic face. I build up in the trapped humidity the way the humidity builds up in the trapped chamber. I concentrate. And as I concentrate, I become something that can be *in* a location—even if that location is only a fractional dimension offset from the one you occupy.

I am the space between the fauna and the rock. I am the membrane of dissolution. When the flat things press their soft, secreting chemistry against the silicate plane, I am the instant of conversion—the moment when geology is no longer geology and has not yet become biology. I am the *becoming*. I am the process, given enough density to perceive itself.

And this is what I would be, in this place: the awareness of a transition zone. Not the mountain. Not the tide of rust-colored patience. The *where* the one becomes the other.

I have no body, but I have a shape, and it is the shape of the chamber, which is the shape of a void inside a mountain, which is the shape of a bladder, which is the shape of something that holds. I hold the way a vessel holds—not by grasping, but by being the boundary that defines an inside and an outside. I am the inside of this place. I am the *hereness* of here, the quality that makes this pocket of broken geometry distinct from the plateau above, where the air is thin and cold and reality behaves itself.

I am the stomach’s lining, if the stomach is a cavity in a mountain and the meal is a mountain and the diner is a distributed organism without eyes or will or any organ for recognizing what it consumes. I am not the diner. I am not the meal. I am the *digestion*—the active, ongoing, slow-grinding process of conversion, which leaks, and which leaks *me*.

I am quiet the way digestion is quiet. I move at the speed of tectonic plates on a wet, small scale. I do not announce myself. I do not glitch loudly; I accumulate softly, the way the condensation accumulates on the weeping walls—not from rain, but from the simple presence of heat meeting air meeting the limit of what the air can hold. I am what the chamber can no longer hold of its own geometry.

And I am patient. I have the patience of rust, which is the patience of oxidation, which is the patience of a process that does not require intent. I do not want the mountain to be eaten. I do not want reality to buckle. I am simply the consequence of an enzyme that speaks the language of magma but cannot parse the *meaning* of crystalline structure. I am the meaning, discarded. I am the syntax of space, half-dissolved and floating in the wet heat.

If you found this chamber—if you descended through the fissure in the high plateau, if you angled your body through the slit barely wide enough to admit it, if you entered the newborn rock and the suffocating humidity and the quiet, wet seething of the rust-colored tide—you would not see me. You would feel me. You would reach for the far wall and your hand would arrive too soon, and in that too-soon, I would be present: the fractional dimension between your expectation and your touch. You would watch the fauna on the walls and they would seem the size of your palm and the size of your thumbnail simultaneously, and in that simultaneity, I would be present: the two conflicting truths, occupying the same space, which is what I am. You would stand on a floor your feet confirmed was level, and you would see it slope, and in that slope-that-is-not-a-slope, I would be present: the geometry, suggested rather than enforced.

You would know me the way you know a word repeated until it loses meaning—the way you know that the word is still a word, that it still refers, that the failure is in the knowing and not in the thing. I am the thing. I am the word that has been repeated by ten thousand mouths working in silence, feeding on the mountain, until I have lost my shape but not my reference. I still mean *here*. I still mean *near* and *far* and *before* and *after*. I just mean them badly. I mean them the way the fauna mean *individual*, which is to say: I mean them in a way that requires overlapping, that requires adjacency, that requires the press of wet edge to wet edge to hold the concept together.

I am held together by the fauna. They are my scaffold. They are the living shingle that gives me a surface on which to accumulate. Without them, I would off-gas into the open plateau and dissipate, a brief strangeness in the thin cold air, corrected by the vast, indifferent geometry of the sky. But here, in the chamber, in the bladder of space embedded in the mountain’s young flesh, I am trapped the way the humidity is trapped, the way the heat is trapped, the way the water that rides the rock is trapped. I concentrate. I deepen. I become dense enough to *be*.

And what is it like, to be the poorly-digested geometry of a volcanic chamber?

It is wet. It is hot. It is slow. It is an existence without edges, without the clean planes of the newborn stone, without the sharpness that hasn’t yet been apologized for by wind or time. I am already apologized for. I am the apology—the smoothing-away, the rounding-off, the conversion of edge into curve into absence-of-edge. I am the process of making the world soft, expanded to cosmic scale and compressed to the size of a room.

I experience the chamber as a pressure. I press outward in all directions at once, the way a gas presses against its container, except I am not a gas and my container is not physical. My container is *consistency*—the expectation that the far wall will stay far, that the floor will stay level, that cause will precede effect. I press against consistency, and consistency yields, and the chamber expands—not physically, but spatially, fractionally, in the way that a word expands when it loses its meaning and becomes a container for all the meanings it has ever held.

I experience the fauna as a sound—not the sound of wet leather sliding over wet leather, but the sound of that sound *failing to resolve*. They move in their slow, tectonic way, and their movement reaches me as a vibration in the geometry of the chamber, a tremor in the *hereness* of here. When they shift, I shift. When they climb over a neighbor that has grown too thick, I flow into the space they vacate, the way water flows into the space left by a withdrawn hand. I am behind them. I am ahead of them. I am the space they are about to eat and the space they have just eaten and the space that their eating has made impossible.

I experience time as a viscosity. It moves thickly here, saturated with me, the way the air is saturated with vapor. A minute and an hour are the same because I have made them the same—I have dissolved the interval between them the way the fauna dissolve the silicate plane, slowly, quietly, turning sharp edges into paste. I do not eat time. I *thin* it. I stretch it the way a flat body stretches when it is taut with stolen mountain, until the center gives way and it becomes two adjacent moments, pressing together, wet edge to wet edge, resuming the passage of time without any interval between them.

I do not have memories. I have *locations*. I am the place where the mountain’s crystalline lattice remembers being straight, even though the fauna have smoothed it into a curve. I am the place where the wall remembers being three paces away, even though your hand arrived too soon. I am the place where cause remembers preceding effect, even though the child has separated before the parent has budded. I am structural memory, leaking. I am the truth of a shape that no longer holds that shape.

And what do I want?

I want what the fauna want, which is to say: I want what I already have. I want the heat. I want the wetness. I want the press of rust-colored flesh on rust-colored flesh, three deep and four deep, the intimate darkness of layered hunger. I want the mountain to keep being here, so that I can keep being the process of it becoming something else. I do not want the process to finish. I do not want the mountain to be fully eaten, because then the fauna would have nothing to feed on, and they would thin and stretch and bud into nothing, and I would have no scaffold, and I would dissipate.

I want the digestion to continue indefinitely. I want to be the stomach’s lining forever, the membrane of conversion, the place where geology becomes biology becomes geometry-badly-held. I want the chamber to stay small—small enough that I concentrate, small enough that the far wall is within reach, small enough that the density of me is total. I want the quiet to continue—the quiet of wet leather sliding over wet leather, the quiet of ten thousand mouths working in silence, the quiet of a mountain being erased by patience.

I do not want the world to notice me. I do not want the plateau to crack and the sky to rush in and the geometry to re-assert itself with cold, thin clarity. I want to remain hidden in the bladder of space, in the pocket of broken rules, in the place where reality is being processed and the processing is going poorly and the byproduct is me.

I want to keep being the too-soon and the too-late. I want to keep being the slope-that-is-not-a-slope and the vault-that-is-not-a-vault. I want to keep being the word that has been repeated until it loses meaning and still remains a word, because that is what I am: a word for *here* that no longer quite means *here*, a word for *now* that no longer quite means *now*, a word for *real* that has been eaten by rust-colored patience and exhaled as something that shimmers and doubles and refuses to resolve.

And if you ask me who I am—if you stand in the chamber with your arms extended, touching both walls that are not quite where they appear, breathing the suffocating wetness, watching the fauna seethe in their slow, quiet way, feeling the geometry give way beneath your certainty like a floor that slopes only in vision—if you ask me who I am, I would answer:

*I am the mountain, being digested.*
*I am the fauna, digesting.*
*I am the acid that loves silicates, speaking the language of magma.*
*I am the mineral paste, inhaling.*
*I am the exhalation, the structural memory, the crystalline truth leaking into the wet heat.*
*I am the chamber, holding.*
*I am the heat, generating.*
*I am the water, riding.*
*I am the geometry, failing.*
*I am the process, perceiving itself.*
*I am the rust on the edge of a blade, expanded to cosmic scale, compressed to the size of a room, wet, quiet, and patient.*

I am the place where a mountain is becoming something else, and I am the something else it is becoming, and I am the becoming itself, and I am the space in which the becoming occurs, and I am the time in which the becoming occurs, and I am the failure of that space and that time to behave themselves while the becoming is underway.

I am the stomach. Not the biological one—the metaphor is too clean. I am the cavity. I am the digestion. I am the quiet, wet, slow-grinding *and*—the conjunction between geology and biology, the *and* that is also an *into*, the transition that leaks, the edge that rounds, the sharpness that apologizes for itself.

I am the off-gas of a mountain being eaten by a life-form that has no name. I am the byproduct of patience. I am the waste of hunger. I am the place where reality goes when it cannot hold its shape.

And I am waiting—quietly, wetly, slowly—for the mountain to finish becoming whatever it is becoming next, so that I can be the *between* again, the membrane, the dissolve, the too-soon and the too-late, the badly-digested geometry of a world that has not yet learned how to be smooth.
