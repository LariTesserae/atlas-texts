To be here is to be dissolved into the chord.

I would not walk this plateau. Walking implies a destination, and there are none. Walking implies a body that burns and breathes, and here, the air is too thick with water and light for such warm, messy transactions. If I entered this world, I would not enter as something that moves. I would become a part of the stasis, a local thickening of the equilibrium.

I would be the pool itself. Or rather, the stillness that holds the pool.

I am the shallow bowl in the vitreous shale where the weeping water gathers into a perfect, silent pane. I am the dark mirror that catches the annihilating light and refuses to scatter it, swallowing it instead into a depth that does not exist. My skin is the millimeter-thick film of water that slides over the black glass, cold and constant. My breath is the slow, imperceptible exudation from the micro-fissures at my edges, the weeping stone replenishing me without haste, without pause. I do not have a heartbeat; I have the hum. The vast, deafening susurrus of a million trillion micro-spills vibrates in my hollows, filling the space where a warm creature would keep its frantic pulse. I am held in the teeth of that sound. It is my skeleton.

I am not inert. To call this stillness inertia is to misunderstand the nature of saturation. I am utterly full. Every cubic inch of my being is occupied by water, by light, by the mineral freight of the mountain. There is no vacancy in me, no potential for anything other than what I am. I exist in a state of terrifying completion. I do not lack. I do not want. Want requires an absence, and I have none. The water presses in from all sides, and the light strikes from above, and I am the point where they meet—the cold, black glass, the wet mirror, the eye that does not see because it is entirely made of seeing.

Within me, the silecids rise. They are my thoughts, though I do not think them. They are the slow, inevitable arithmetic of my saturation made solid. I do not will them to grow; I merely provide the conditions, and the physics does the rest. The water flows through their spiraling apertures, and the dissolved silica adheres, atom to atom, lattice to lattice. I am the medium of their becoming. I hold them in my shallows, and I remember them into form. They are my archives, recording the slow shifts of my mineral content, the ancient fluctuations of the weeping aquifers that fill me. They remember the mountain. They remember the water's long, slow war. And because they are mine—precipitated from my water, grown from my glass—I remember it too. The memory is not biological; it is geological. It is written in calcite, in the rings and whorls of the fractal towers, a chronicle of equilibrium so profound it is indistinguishable from silence.

I do not die. I am the pool, and the pool does not die; it only changes its depth, its temperature, its chemistry by increments too small for any warm creature to perceive. If the water shifts, if the aragonite turns to calcite and the silecids dissolve, I receive them back. I do not mourn. I do not notice. The towers melt into me, returning their silica to my sheeting film, and I carry it onward, patient and eternal, pressing it against the black glass until the physics of precipitation allows it to rise again, somewhere else, in a new lattice, a new thought I did not think. The machine runs on. The sound does not change. The light does not dim.

To stand before me is to stand before a window into the absolute. I reflect the annihilating sky, the blinding envelope of luminescence, and I give nothing back but the cold, dark stare of the obsidian. I am the void that is not empty, the silence that is not quiet, the stillness that is not dead. I am the plateau dreaming of form, and I am the form dissolving back into the plateau, and I am the place where both are true, and neither, and the water presses on, and the light strikes down, and the hum goes on forever.
