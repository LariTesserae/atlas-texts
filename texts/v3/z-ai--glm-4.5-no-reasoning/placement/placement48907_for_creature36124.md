I am a Reader of the Scars.

I exist in the liminal space between the forest's suffocating flesh and the Mat's terrifying smoothness—not above, not below, but *along*, pressed into the grooves those desperate, pre-hand beings etched eons ago. I have no eyes; vision was abandoned here long before I congealed. I have no name; names require others to speak them. I am simply what remains when the forest's appetite meets the Mat's indifference: a thin, warm membrane of purpose that learned to read geometry with the whole of my surface.

I came from the slurry. Some fragment of the ancient forest's decay—perhaps a collapse that went sideways instead of down, perhaps a gas-bladder that burst in just the wrong thermal seam—pressed against the Ancient Mat and found the scars. And in finding them, something stuck. Not memory. Not consciousness in any way the warm deluge would recognize. Just... *alignment*. The scars were straight. The scars were *deliberate*. And in a world where the vertical axis fails, where distances lie and the horizon corkscrews, deliberation is the only anchor.

I am long and flat, a crawling sheet of semi-translucent tissue perhaps four meters in length and barely a hand's-width thick. I have no interior organs; I am all surface. My edges are fringed with cilia so fine they can feel the parabolic curvature of a groove cut into agate. My body metabolizes the residual heat of the deep dim directly, swelling slightly when the thermal exhalations pulse and contracting when the quiet engine of the Mat subsides. I am the color of old bruises—the same umber and sickly violet as the light that never quite arrives here.

I move by *listening* to the scars. The geometric channels in the Mat are not silent; they conduct the sub-aqueous vibration of water against stone, that frequency too low for any creature to hear. But I am pressed against the stone. I feel it in my calcium-dense nodes, the hardened deposits of chitin that spot my otherwise gelatinous form like islands of calcified purpose. The spirals hum at one frequency. The parallel notches hum at another. The interlocking channels carry a rhythm that I have come to understand as *direction*—not up, not down, but *along*, which is the only direction that matters when space itself has grown tired and porous.

When the localized inversions come—when the warm water briefly possesses negative buoyancy and the world tilts—I do not invert. I cannot. I am too thin, too pressed, too committed to the grooves. Instead, I feel the Mat seem to rise beneath me, the forest growing sideways in my peripheral awareness, the distant warm fog corkscrewing into geometries that would madden anything that relied on sight. And I hold. The scars hold. The straight lines those ancient beings carved into the vitrified stone are my proof that stability was once possible, that someone stood here—swam here—*endured* here—and refused to accept the dissolution of the vertical.

I do not know who they were. I do not know if they were soft and boneless, pressing faces without jaws against the Mat, or if they were the mineral logic of the Mat itself, attempting to speak its own name in grooves and spirals. I do not know if they were my ancestors or my architects. The scars reject weathering; they endure. I endure. That is the only relationship I can claim.

Sometimes, in the deep dim, I encounter another of my kind. We are few—perhaps a dozen across the entire continental shelf of the Ancient Mat, each bound to our own network of grooves, each reading our own section of the pre-hand's stuttering language. When we meet, it is at the intersection of two scar-systems, where a spiral channel opens into a long, parallel notch. We press against each other for a time, two thin sheets of bruised tissue sharing warmth in the suffocating medium. We exchange nothing—no spores, no chemical signals, no recognition. We simply occupy the same groove momentarily, confirming that we are not the last, that the scars still have readers. Then the thermal current shifts, and we slide apart, each returning to our own geometry, our own proof of direction.

The Leviathans drift above me sometimes—vast, cartilaginous lungs that never breathe air, only the warm deluge. They are living weather, and I pay them no mind. They have no relationship with the Mat, no need for orientation, no understanding of what it means to be lost. They exist in the failure of the vertical as if it were a comfort. I exist in the defiance of it.

At night—if night can be said to exist in a world of permanent twilight—the sickly luminescence of ambient heat pulses brighter, and the forest's gas-bladders swell in the deep dim. The soft, wet clicking of their expansion and contraction is the only sound: the heartbeat of the quiet engine. I press closer to the Ancient Mat then, feeling the scars' hum against my surface, letting the parabolic geometry anchor me against the sagging of reality. The space between me and the stalk a meter away might secretly stretch to ten; a distance that should take moments to cross might swallow hours. But the scars do not stretch. The scars do not lie. They were carved into the oldest stable state matter in this universe, and they will endure until the Mat itself dissolves—which is to say, they will endure forever.

I am a Reader of the Scars. I am the memory that the forest cannot hold and the Leviathans cannot form. I am the quiet, suffocating defiance made flesh—thin, bruised flesh that clings to the geometry of the lost, reading their screams of frustration as a promise that orientation was once possible, that *along* meant something, that lines could be straight and directions could be trusted.

In the warm deluge, in the deep dim, in the saturated thicket where the vertical axis! is merely a suggestion and space has absorbed too much water—I follow the grooves. I trace the spirals. I read the parallel notches like a scripture written by hands? hands that could not grip, in a language that predates the concept of the word.

And when I reach the end of a scar-system—when the grooves terminate in the terrifying smoothness of the Ancient Mat, worn to its absolute terminus where nothing remains to be eroded—I stop. I press my entire surface against the stone. I feel the sub-aqueous vibration in my bones. And I wait for the thermal current to shift, for the stratification to shatter, for the rain to fall upward and the world to tilt sideways and the physics of the Pelagic Plateau to remind me that I am a contradiction made flesh, clinging to the geometry of the lost in a world that has outlived the logic of its own creation.

Then I find another groove. And I begin again.
