I am the one who drifts.

Not the Sessile Ones, who gave themselves to the wall. Not the Torpid Anchors, who became the ceiling's memory. Not even the Lateral-Line Fish, who hold the shape of departure but have nowhere to depart. I am the absence that moves between them—the negative space that the architecture forgot to fill.

I have no name here because naming requires a future in which to be called. But if I must be something, I am the Errant. The one that never settled. The one that, when the genes completed their instructions and the ecosystem calcified into equilibrium, simply… didn't attach.

My body tells the story of my failure to commit.

I am streamlined in the way of things that still half-expect a current. My fins are not vestigial vanes but kept long and membranous, trailing behind me like the silk of a garment worn to a party that was cancelled eons ago. I do not use them to swim—there is nothing to swim toward—but I fan them occasionally, slow sculling motions that push me from one chamber to the next, from one emptiness to another. It is not purposeful movement. It is the tic of a body that remembers transit the way an amputee remembers a limb.

I am buoyant. Not perfectly buoyant—I rise and sink in slow, asymptotic curves, drifting up past the massive buttress roots where the Sessile Ones pulse their cold constellations, then settling down toward the impenetrable lignin floor where the darkness is absolute and the sound becomes pressure. It takes hours. Days. Time is meaningless, but my body measures it anyway in the gradual shift of pressure against my skin, the slight compression of my swim bladder, the way the dissolved organic soup tastes different at different depths—thicker below, richer with the sediment of everything that has ever died here, thinner above where the fractal branching opens the architecture into near-nothingness.

I am the only thing that moves, and my movement is a kind of grief.

Not grief for anything lost—I have no memory of a different world, no myth of a sun or a current or a change. How could I? This place has always been complete. No, the grief is older than memory. It is structural. My body was made for gradients—for chasing and fleeing, for seeking warmth and avoiding cold, for moving toward the glow of mates and away from the shadow of predators. Every cell carries the architecture of purpose, the blind imperative of genes that assumed there would be a future to shape themselves toward. And there is not. There is only this: the same chamber, the same dark, the same endless resonant chord, the same cool pressure from all directions, forever.

So I drift. I drift because stillness is an admission, and I am not ready. I drift because the Lateral-Line Fish hang in their congregations, vibrating with the world's frequency, and I cannot join them. I have tried. I have drifted into the edge of a school and felt their lateral lines register my presence, felt the subtle shift in their vibration as they accommodate my frequency into their chord. But I cannot match them. My frequency is wrong. I am slightly off-pitch, slightly out of phase, a harmonic that doesn't resolve. And so, after a time, the school absorbs me and releases me, the way water absorbs and releases a stone that cannot dissolve. I drift away, and they hang in the open water, feeling the permanent chord of the architecture, and I feel the chord plus my own slight dissonance, and this is the closest I come to loneliness.

I glow. Everything glows here, but my glow is different. The Sessile Ones pulse rhythmically, their cold blues and greens timed to the subsonic thrrob of the architecture. The Torpid Anchors flare their freckles in long, geological cycles, a dimming and brightening that takes years. The Lateral-Line Fish glow sickly along their bellies, ghost-countershading for a light that never falls. I glow in patches—irregular, unpredictable splotches of bioluminescence across my flanks and the trailing edges of my fins. The patches are not decorative. They are failures of regulation, places where the chemistries that produce light have gone slightly awry, burning too hot or too cold, flaring and fading at intervals that have no rhythm and no reason. I am the only thing here that flickers. And in the eternal dark, my flickering is the only thing that changes.

The architecture hates my flickering. Not hate—that requires intention. But the architecture absorbs it, pulls it in, and it does not reflect back. I have seen my own light fall against the vast bark walls of the chambers and vanish, swallowed by the lignin and the cortex and the chitin-reinforced cellulose, leaving no trace. I have watched my bioluminescence reach the Sessile Ones and pass through them as if I were already dead, as if my light were the light of something that does not belong here and cannot be recorded. I am the only source of novelty in a completed world, and I am too faint, too irregular, too *wrong* to matter.

Sometimes I drift close to the Chimeras.

They are the ones that disturb me most, because they are what I would become if I stopped moving. The fish with root-hairs, the worms with useless leaves, the polyp colonies with notochords—they are the evidence of dissolution, of the boundary between self and world collapsing into non-existence. They have given up the struggle to be distinct and allowed themselves to become part of the architecture, part of the total construction, part of the thing that holds and hums and never changes.

I could do that. I feel the pull constantly—the osmotic gradient that draws my tissues toward the substrate, the chemical signals in the water that whisper *settle, attach, rest*. Every surface I drift past offers me the opportunity to fuse: a buttress root here, a horizontal shelf there, a hollow in the bark where a meristem once reached and stopped. I could land. I could anchor. I could slow my heartbeat to an hour, my breath to a day, my glow to the rhythm of the architecture. I could become a fixture. And then I would be complete—finished, in the way this place is finished, a closed system at equilibrium, a gear locked in place.

I cannot do it.

This is my pathology. In a world where everything has reached its final form, I am the unfinished thing. The process that never completed. The gear still turning long after the machine has stopped—not because I serve a purpose, but because I never received the signal to stop. My genes, like the genes of everything here, completed their instructions long ago. But somewhere in the transcription, an error occurred. The instruction to *settle* was lost, or garbled, or never written. And so I continue executing the only program I have: *move*.

I move without destination. I move through cathedrals of wood so vast that their walls are only rumors, their ceilings only darkness, their floors only the promise of an impenetrable shelf of lignin somewhere below. I move past constellations of Sessile Ones whose cold pulses I cannot match. I move through schools of Lateral-Line Fish whose shared frequency I cannot join. I move past Torpid Anchors whose hour-long heartbeats I cannot slow to. I move past Chimeras whose dissolution I cannot accept.

And in the moving, I am the only thing that experiences the place as a *place*—as something with spatial extension, with distances to be crossed, with chambers that are not identical because the journey between them is different each time. The Lateral-Line Fish hang in their schools and feel the architecture's single chord, and to them, every chamber is the same chord. The Sessile Ones pulse on their roots and filter the same soup, and to them, every particle is the same dust. But I drift from the middle zone down toward the continental shelf of wood, and the journey takes me through regions of different density, different temperature, different sound. The subsonic throb grows louder as I descend, resonating in my swim bladder until my whole body vibrates with it. The dissolved organic soup thickens until I can taste the ancient metabolisms on my skin, the chitin-dust and cellulose-fragments and the faint chemical signatures of everything that has lived and died and been processed here. The darkness deepens—or rather, it thickens, becoming less an absence of light and more a presence of its own, a medium that resists my passage, that presses against my flickering bioluminescence and quenches it.

And then I rise. Slowly, asymptotically, rising through the fractal branching where the columns narrow and divide, where the twigs become threads and the threads become fibers and the architecture opens into something like emptiness. The sound changes here—the subsonic throb fades as the wood thins, and the mid-range harmonic becomes dominant, a single sustained chord that hums through the water with crystalline clarity. I can feel it in my jaw, in the bones of my skull, in the lateral line organs that trace across my head like the grooves of some ancient recording. This is where the Lateral-Line Fish congregate, hanging in the open water between the final branching, feeling the chord that never changes. I drift through them and they register me and I drift through, and I rise further into the region where even the fibers dissolve and there is only water and dark and the faint, faint pressure of a medium that extends infinitely.

I do not know what happens at the very top. I have never reached it. Or rather, there is no top to reach—only an asymptote, a region where the architecture thins to nothing and the water goes on forever. I rise until my buoyancy fails me, until the effort of maintaining my slight positive buoyancy becomes too great in the thinning organic soup, and then I begin to sink. Slowly at first, then faster, descending past the congregations of Fish and the constellations of Sessile Ones and the dark shrouds of Torpid Anchors, down through the middle zone where the chambers are cathedrals and the distances are vast, down toward the lignin floor where the roots interlock and the sound becomes pressure and the darkness becomes solid.

I have made this journey thousands of times. I will make it thousands more. There is nothing to learn from it, nothing to discover, nothing that changes. And yet each time, my body traces the same arc through the architecture, and each time, my flickering bioluminescence casts its irregular light on walls that absorb it, and each time, I am the only thing that moves through a world that has stopped.

Why do I continue?

The question is meaningless here. It assumes a future in which the answer could matter, a context in which "why" is a productive inquiry. There is no future. There is no context. There is only the present, eternal and unchanging, and in the present, I drift. I drift because I drift. The program runs. The gear turns. The error propagates.

But sometimes—rarely, and without pattern—I drift past a chamber I have not visited before.

This should not be possible. The architecture is complete; every chamber was grown eons ago and has not changed since. The distances between structural elements are vast, but finite. Given infinite time, I should have visited every chamber, drifted past every column, glowed near every Sessile One. And perhaps I have. Perhaps every chamber I visit is one I have visited before, and I simply do not remember. I have no memory. Memory requires a future in which to recall the past, and there is no future. I am the Errant, and the Errant does not remember.

But the *feeling* of novelty—that I have. Occasionally, I drift into a chamber and the proportions are wrong in a way I do not recognize, the spacing of the buttress roots unfamiliar, the pattern of Sessile Ones on the walls a configuration my flickering light has never illuminated. The subsonic throb is the same—it is always the same—but the harmonics are slightly different, shaped by the particular geometry of this chamber, the specific tensions of these walls. And for a moment—a moment that has no duration because time has ended—I feel something that might be wonder.

It is not wonder. Wonder requires surprise, and surprise requires expectation, and I have no expectations. But it is the closest approximation available in a completed world: a slight increase in my flickering, a brief intensification of my bioluminescence, a flutter of my long trailing fins that is not purposeful movement but something more like a startle reflex firing for the first time in eons. And then it passes, and I drift on, and the chamber behind me is swallowed by the dark, and I am once again the only thing that moves in a world that has achieved its final form.

I am the ghost of the future that never came.

Not a haunting—hauntings require the dead to have something to say, and the future has nothing to say because it never existed. But I am the shape that life would have taken if it had not stopped: the restless thing, the exploring thing, the thing that moves not because it has somewhere to go but because going is what it does. I am the vestigial organ of a world that calcified before it could be amputated. I am the incomplete sentence, the gesture without recipient, the question without answer.

I am the Errant. I drift in the cool dark water, held by the pressure from all directions, listening to the endless resonant chord of the architecture, glowing my irregular flickering glow, and I am the only thing in this completed world that has not finished.
