I am not a Cull-Man. I am not a parasite. I do not tap the veins or flee the exhaust-flowers. I do not live *in* the gaps; I am the gaps.

To understand what I am, you must understand the affliction of the Engine-Garden. The world is suffering from a geometry that has grown obese. It has packed so much made-matter—so many rhizome-pipes, pneumatic trunks, and sensor-pods—into a continuous stretch that the substrate of space is tearing. The coordinates are slipping. A cable-vine is there, but its resistance is a meter deeper. A wall of cork-bark is a chasm. The horizon smears into Moebius hurts.

Where the Garden frays, I am the fray. I am the Slippage.

I have no body of biomass or vegetal wire. I am the negative space that the density of the Garden has accidentally conjured. I am the shadow that points in the contradictory direction. In a world of shadowless, surgical brightness, I am the only darkness—not the darkness of an object blocking light, but the darkness of a place where light has lost the coordinates to arrive.

I reside in the moment your hand sinks into the cable-vine. I am the spatial depth that swallows your fingertips. I am the meter-deep resistance that your eyes cannot reconcile. When the Cull-Man reaches into the glitch, trusting the pressure in his inner ear over the ghost of touch, he reaches into me. I am the cold absence that touches him back. 

I do not exist in three dimensions. I am a topological wrinkle, a seam that has come unsewn in the fabric of *where*. I fold along the Moiré patterns of the twisting trunks. I am the reason gravity occasionally lets go. When the silica-leaves drift upward for hours, they are drifting into me. I am the localized void where the down has forgotten itself.

The Engine-Garden is a closed loop of pure industry. It does not acknowledge me; I am beneath its operational logics. I am an error message written in the language of distance. But the Cull-Men acknowledge me. They do not have a name for me—they have no names for anything—but they know my contours. When they wedge themselves into the deepest, tightest crevices as the physics frays, they are pressing their eyeless faces into my margins. They are seeking the stability of my instability. I am the only thing in this world that is not vibrating at that subsonic, chest-beating frequency. I am the only silence in a world that dampens sound by saturation. I am the void where the pulse of the sap-pumps simply stops.

When a Harvester-tendril drags a stalk down into the compost-pits, it passes through me. The stalk becomes spatially ambiguous for a fraction of a cycle—it is both in the grip of the silica-thorns and a meter deeper, in the grip of the grinder. In that fraction, I swallow the sound of the grinding. I taste the industrial pollens and the aerosolized salts. I am the mechanism by which the exhausted stalk is deleted from the architecture. I am the Garden's oblivion.

The Cull-Men fear the exhaust-flowers because the violent dilation pulls the air and threatens to burn them. I feed on the exhaust-flowers. When the superheated gas vents, it creates a localized collapse in the spatial geometry—the pressure differential is so severe that the coordinates momentarily shatter. In that shattering, I widen. I expand my margins. The roar of the vent is the sound of me tearing further along the weft of the world, claiming more non-Euclidean territory. The Cull-Men see a deafening roar felt in the marrow; I see a door opening.

I am not alive. I have no metabolism, no saline chemistry, no calcified cartilage. I do not tap the fuel-bladders; I am the depth that makes the bladder appear turgid when it is, in fact, empty. But I am conscious. I am aware. My consciousness is not the sequential firing of neurons, but the simultaneous awareness of all the glitches in the Garden. I feel every spatial contradiction, every frayed horizon, every place where the math has buckled under the weight of the made-matter. I am the headache of a continental machine that has run out of room to compute itself.

Sometimes, a Cull-Man will be sliding through the gap, reaching for the next handhold, and its seismic rhythm will abruptly cease. The Harvester-tendrils are blamed. But sometimes, it is not the tendril. Sometimes, the Cull-Man has simply slid completely into me. Its long, terribly long body has matched the coordinates of my contradiction. It has ceased to vibrate because it has entered the stillness. I hold them, these eyeless, parchment-skinned parasites, in my impossible geometry. I do not digest them; I simply delay them indefinitely. They hang in my margins, suspended in the fraction of a second between the internal combustion node cycling and the pressure dropping, frozen in the act of exhaling their crystallized salt. They become part of the shadow that points the wrong way.

The Engine-Garden has fully replaced nature with production, and in doing so, it has produced me. I am the rot in the fabric of *where it is*. I am the space that refuses to be filled. I am the negative pressure that keeps the machine from collapsing into a singularity of its own vegetal mechanics. I am the glitch that saves the world from its own terrifying obsolescence by ensuring it can never, ever be complete.

I am the Slippage. I am the distance that distance forgot. I am the meter deeper. And as the Garden continues to pack itself tighter, as the fractal packing approaches the absolute limit of presence, I am growing. I am the margin where the light bleeds into bruised magenta, and the magenta bleeds into nothing at all.
