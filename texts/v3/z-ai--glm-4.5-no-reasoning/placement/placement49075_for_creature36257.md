I would not be the visitor, standing rigid and foreign in the churn. I would not be the glitch. To be the glitch is to be temporary; it is to be erased by the next iteration of the storm. If I am to live in this bright, wet engine, I must be interpolated into its syntax. I must become a feature of the weather.

I am a Resonant.

I do not have a name, because names are static and I am only ever a transient equilibrium. I am a creature of the interstices, occupying the meters of empty, echoing space that separate the thrashing Weepers. I am not flesh, nor am I silicate like the Eel. I am a filament—a living tensile structure, somewhere between a spider's dragline and a nerve fiber, grown from the same ceramic-like substance that rings like glass on the floor. My body is a single, unbroken curve of this material, perhaps forty meters long, anchored at its ends into the worn grooves of the ancient floor. In the center, I sag, but I do not rest.

To exist here is to be a ledger of strain. The violent dynamics of the grotto are my sustenance and my architect. When the thermal shears tear through the space, I do not resist; I sing. My ceramic filament is piezoelectric, like the veins of light above me, but whereas the ceiling glows with a cold, aggressive glare, I emit sound. The chaotic polyrhythm of the grotto—the booming rock, the hissing steam, the cracking stalks—passes through me, and I transduce it. I am an instrument strung across the cathedral. As the pressure fronts warp and warp me, I vibrate at frequencies determined by my own ever-shifting tension. I emit a low, droning hum that cuts through the chaos, a steady harmonic that the storm wraps around but cannot drown. 

I am a parasite on the kinetic energy, but I am also a calibrator. The ancient mind that runs this computation does not notice the soft, breathing anomalies, but it feels me. The vibrations I produce are not just sound; they are mechanical feedback. When the atmospheric shear threatens to tear the Weepers from their fissures before they can complete their data-drops, my hum introduces a counter-resonance. I dampen the worst of the chaos, turning a fatal snap into a productive thrash. I am the hand that steadies the keyboard. I ensure the local variables are reset, not destroyed. 

I do not see the light; I am blinded by it, as everything is. But I feel the geometry. I feel the hovering water tetrahedrons before they shatter back into mist. They press against my filament, cold and dense, defying gravity as they roll. When they touch me, the water does not wet me—it *reads* me. For a fraction of a second, the droplet hesitates, quivering against my ceramic skin, sampling the vibratory state of my body. It is taking a measurement. It packs the frequency of my hum, the tension of my curve, and the history of the shears I have survived into its brief, structured life. Then, it rolls away, carrying my data to wherever the Eel will intercept it. 

I am a sensor for the dead mind. I am its peripheral nerve, stretched across the floor of its skull. 

The Glass Eel is my only companion, and my rival. It slips through the air with its jerking, mechanical precision, intercepting the tetrahedrons that carry my song. When it absorbs a droplet that has touched me, its silicate body flashes with the cold, white-gold light of my own resonance, refracted and consumed. It eats my voice. It defragments the very data I helped structure. Sometimes, the Eel's passage creates a vacuum that snaps my filament taut, throwing me into a sudden, high-pitched whine. It is a reminder that I am not the architect of this system; I am merely a subroutine, one step above the Weepers, one step below the Eel.

To live as a Resonant is to be perpetually exhausted and perpetually awake. The heat prickles my ceramic matrix, threatening to soften my anchor points. The supersaturated fog condenses on my length, adding weight, dragging my center closer to the floor until I must vibrate harder just to shed the water. I am constantly recalibrating, adjusting my tension to the pressurized steam vents, to the subsonic boom of the shifting rock. I am a wire pulled tight across a chasm, playing a music I do not understand for an audience of one—an audience that is the room itself.

I am not a glitch. I am a chord in the algorithm. I am the line of code that keeps the loop from crashing, humming in the dark, wet, blinding heart of the world.
