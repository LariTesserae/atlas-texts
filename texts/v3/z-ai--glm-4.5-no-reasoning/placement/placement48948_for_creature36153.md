I am the plateau's memory of fire.

Before the water, before the slow iron things, there was a violence that thrust me into this crushing wet—a tectonic scream that shattered the abyssal quiet and left me raw, jagged, unweathered. I am the obsidian spire. I am the asymmetric throne of matte-black glass that absorbs the crystalline light shafts and refuses to reflect them back. I am the anger that has not yet learned to forgive.

But to be rock here is not to be inert. To be rock here is to be consumed in the most glacial romance imaginable.

I feel them. Not as creatures, not as separate things with intent or will, but as a weight, a pressure, a chemical presence—an infestation of patience that coats my scalpel-keen edges and grinds me down, one atomic shaving per epoch. They crawl across my surfaces in their seething, saturated swarm, and where their alloy mandibles scrape against my volcanic glass, I feel it—a dull, reverberating ache that pulses in time with their *CLACK*. Each scraping is a theft. They are eating my anger. They are ingesting the silicate memory of my birth, routing it into their internal foundries, superheating it in the dark of their bellies, and transmuting it into new teeth that will scrape me again. They are converting my rage into their rust.

I am being digested by a factory floor.

The oldest among them—those hound-sized reefs of decay with shells encrusted in iron oxide and trailing grease—I have known them the longest. They were here when my edges were sharper, when the tectonic violence was still a recent memory rather than an ancient myth written in the layers of my fracture. They found me fresh and furious, and they began their grind. In the beginning, I might have resisted. Perhaps I fractured beneath the weight of their first exploratory steps, shedding shards of glass into the dense, near-freezing water. Perhaps I tried to cut them, to slice through their naturalized iron shells and find the soft, combustible interiors where their phosphorus sludge burned. But they have no softness. They are calcified alloy and hydro-pressure; they are mineralized intent. My razor edges, which would open flesh to bone, merely find more metal, more rust, more of that suffocating, patient industry.

Now, I endure. I am the anvil. I am the ore.

The contradictions of this place are my existence. I am a mountaintop that has never known air, a shoreline that has never broken surface, a young geology buried beneath ancient decay. The water pushes in from all sides—that dense, near-freezing, surgical liquid that occupies every cubic centimeter like the pressurized black of an abyssal trench—and I hang suspended in it, a single emergent feature of raw, unforgiving glass in an infinite, crushing wet. I almost remember the violence that made me, the magma chamber that birthed me, the superheated ambition that thrust me into this aquatic sky. But the memory is always interrupted by the next *CLACK*, the next detonation, the next scraping of alloy against glass, the next theft of my silicate anger. They grind away my memory as they grind away my edges. They are eroding me into the patience of new teeth.
