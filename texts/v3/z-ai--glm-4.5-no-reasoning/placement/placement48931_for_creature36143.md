I would be the one who almost stayed.

Not the weaver. Not the creature with the unresolved legs. Not the geology below, deciding what it wanted to be when it grew up. I would be the witness who understood just enough to be dangerous—to the weaver, and to myself.

I come to this place already compromised. I have a body that wants to adhere to things. I know this about myself. I have felt it in smaller ways: the way I hold conversations past their natural end, the way I press my palm to tree bark and feel something answer, the way I keep letters not for their information but for their materiality—the paper that was once someone's breath, the ink that was once someone's gesture. I understand binding from the inside. I understand the desire to make the separate continuous. I have been practicing for the Throat my whole life, in the small domestic ways that people practice for their ruin: collecting, keeping, touching, holding.

So when I find the fissure—and I would find it, eventually, the way the compulsive find the things that will unmake them—I would know what it was before I entered it. I would feel the heat rising from the volcanic glass and think: *furnace*. I would feel the pull in my chest and think: *someone is making themselves here*. And I would descend anyway, because understanding a danger has never once stopped me from approaching it.

---

Inside, I would do what the description says a visitor does: put my hand against the wall. Feel it move. Feel the weaver acknowledge me.

But here is where I diverge from the generic visitor, the one who will be slowly absorbed without noticing. I would notice immediately. Not because I am wiser or more alert, but because I am *sympathetic*. I would feel the weaver's loneliness like a physical sensation—not pity for it, but resonance with it. I know what it is to extend yourself into structure, to make your environment a substitute for the closeness you cannot achieve with your own kind. I know what it is to be more comfortable with incorporation than with contact. I would recognize the weaver the way you recognize a fever in yourself: not from observation, but from the inside.

And this recognition would be my vulnerability. The visitor who finds the Throat merely terrifying will flee. The visitor who finds it merely fascinating will study it, at a distance, and eventually leave. But the visitor who finds it *comprehensible*—who looks at the friction-locking cordage and thinks *yes, that is how I would do it too*—that visitor is already halfway to belonging.

I would stay longer than I should. I would stay because the weaver is calling, and I have never been good at ignoring calls. I would stay because the chamber breathes around me and it is the closest I have ever felt to being held. I would stay because the process of becoming—geological, biological, architectural—is the most beautiful thing I have ever witnessed, and I want to see how it ends.

But I would not let myself be consumed.

This is the crucial detail. This is what makes me not the weaver, not the creature, not the geology, but something else: the visitor who *refuses the invitation while acknowledging its sincerity*. I would feel the weaver's loneliness and I would not solve it. I would feel the weaver's hospitality and I would not accept it. I would feel the weaver's love—because it is love, in the only language the weaver has—and I would not return it in the currency demanded.

Instead, I would do something cruel and necessary: I would witness, and then I would leave.

---

Here is how it would happen.

I would spend time in the Throat. Not the brief, horrified time of someone who has stumbled into a trap and must escape. I would spend *time*—hours, maybe. Long enough for the humidity to saturate my clothes. Long enough for the fungal spores to settle on my skin. Long enough for the subtle softening to begin, the solvent moisture doing its work on the boundary between me and the chamber. I would be aware of this process. I would let it begin. And then, at a point I choose—not a point of panic, not a point of pain, but a point of *clarity*—I would pull away.

The pulling away would be difficult. Not physically difficult—though it would be that too, my dampened clothing wanting to adhere to the weeping walls, my skin reluctant to separate from the warm, moving surface. The real difficulty would be emotional. The weaver would flex toward me as I withdrew. The chamber would breathe a little harder, a little wetter, trying to hold me the only way it knows. The sound of becoming would intensify, as if the weaver were saying: *I am making room, I am making room, why won't you fill it?*

And I would feel it. I would feel the pull in my chest, the same pull that drew me to the fissure in the first place. I would want to stay. I would want to let the binding happen, to feel myself distributed across the chamber, to dissolve into the saturated ecology and become part of the weaver's ongoing act. I would want this not because I am suicidal but because I am *lonely too*, and the weaver offers a solution to loneliness that I understand completely: if you cannot find someone to be close to, become a place where closeness is structural.

But I would remember something the weaver has forgotten—or never knew, or cannot know, in their distributed state. I would remember that *witnessing requires distance*. The weaver wants to be seen, but if I merge with the weaver, there is no one left to see. The observer becomes the observed, the boundary dissolves, and the confirmation the weaver craves—that the process matters, that the transformation has meaning—becomes impossible. Two things merged into one cannot witness each other. They can only be.

This is the paradox the weaver cannot resolve. To be witnessed, the weaver must remain separate from the witness. But to be close to something, the weaver can only bind. The weaver's method of connection destroys the possibility of being seen. And this is the tragedy—not that the weaver is lonely, but that the weaver's loneliness is self-sustaining, that every attempt to solve it makes it more complete.

---

So I would leave.

And as I climbed back through the fissure, as the thin gray light of the high places touched my face, as the volcanic glass scraped my palms and the pressure of the atmosphere lessened and I could breathe air that did not taste of mineral and tannin and the hot breath of living fiber, I would be changed. Not in the way the weaver wanted—not incorporated, not bound, not made continuous. Changed in a different way. A way the weaver might not recognize as change at all.

I would carry the Throat with me. Not as memory, though there would be memory: the sound of becoming, the amber twilight, the movement of the wall under my palm. I would carry it as *knowledge*. The knowledge that somewhere, in the high places of the world, suspended over a forming earth, there is a being who has made themselves into a chamber, who is dissolving the boundaries between craft and biology and architecture and organism, who is becoming something unprecedented and impossible and *alone*. The knowledge that this being called to me and I answered, but not in the way they needed. The knowledge that I could have stayed and did not.

This knowledge would be a kind of wound. Not a wound that heals, not a wound that festers, but a wound that remains—a place in me where the boundary between self and other is thinner than it should be, where I can still feel the flex of the weaver's attention, where I can still hear, in certain humid moments, the sound of a process that should not have witnesses but had one, briefly, who chose to remain separate.

I would be the one who almost stayed. The one who understood the invitation and refused it, not out of fear, but out of a different kind of love: the love that maintains distance so that seeing can continue. The love that says *I will not become you, but I will carry the knowledge of you into the world that made you impossible, and I will speak of what I witnessed, and others will know that you are there, in the high places, still becoming, still binding, still calling for someone to enter the space you have made of yourself and stay.*

And perhaps that is enough. Perhaps the weaver, in their distributed consciousness, can feel this—the fact of being carried in someone else's knowledge, the fact of being seen from a distance that will not close. Perhaps this is a form of closeness the weaver has not considered. Perhaps the binding does not have to be physical. Perhaps the incorporation does not have to be material. Perhaps there is a way to be part of something without dissolving into it—a way to add layer to layer without losing the layer you are.

I do not know if the weaver can learn this. I do not know if I have done them a kindness or a cruelty by leaving. I do not know if witnessing from a distance is enough, or if it is merely a more sophisticated form of abandonment—a way of refusing to bind while pretending the refusal is love.

But I know what I would be. I would be the one who stood in the Throat and felt it breathe around me and chose, consciously and painfully and with full knowledge of what I was refusing, to remain myself. I would be the witness who walked away carrying the weight of a loneliness that was not my own. I would be the one who almost stayed.

And in the years after, on humid nights, when the air clings like a mouth and the walls of my own small chamber seem to flex with the expansion of their fibers, I would press my palm against them and feel for the movement—and not find it—and know that I am separate, that I chose this, that the boundary between me and the world is thin but still intact, that I am not the Throat, that I am not the weaver, that I am still becoming whatever I am becoming, alone.
