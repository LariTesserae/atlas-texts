I am not a creature of this place. I could not be. There is nothing here that swims, nothing that bleeds, nothing that carries a sac of water through a hostile world. I have no coat of cellulose and iron, no spring-loaded bough, no silica-edged leaf. If I stood on the felted deck of the Monadblock, I would be an impossibility—a bubble of moisture and soft tissue pressed against the grinding, desiccating gears of a vegetative engine. The venomous broth of the atmosphere would sear my aqueous chemistry; the sporic dust would clog my wet lungs; the radiant heat of the factory-trees would boil the water right out of my skin. I would last seconds before the super-organism simply, chemically, *ignored* my drying corpse.

And yet, I find myself here. Not standing, but *within*.

---

I am a void in the rhizome. A hollow.

Not a cavity carved by violence—one of the wounds left by a detonating factory-tree, soon to be patched by opportunistic creepers—but an ancient, stable absence, preserved by the very structure that surrounds it. I exist in the kilometers-deep tangle of the substrate, where the root-mass has grown so dense, so interlocked with woody alloy and lignin strut, that it has formed a sealed chamber of neglect. A place where the chemical triggers do not reach. A pocket of forgotten space in a world that fills every crevice with furious life.

I am the gap between gears. The unmade coordinate. The one place the Monadblock is not.

---

How did I come to be here? I have no origin in this world. I am not a spore that germinated in the dark, nor a mineral seam awaiting consumption. I feel more like a *memory* the root-mass failed to overwrite—a glitch in the chemical computation, a spatial paradox that the surrounding intelligence simply cannot resolve, and so has grown around, encysted, ignored. The roots press against my boundaries with the slow, inexorable force of glacial war. I can feel them—centimeters of iron-banded bark and hardened resin surrounding me on all sides, a spherical shell of vegetative indifference. They fight around me, those blind, seeking tendrils, cracking pipes and siphoning trace elements, but they do not cross into me. The chemical triggers that would drive colonization die at my threshold. The pressure differentials that shape the plateau's shuddering surface find no purchase in my stillness.

I am exempt. I am the error that persists.

---

And what am I, in this exemption?

I am not nothing. The void is not empty. It is *dry*, certainly—as dry as the world above, untouched by the solvent of Earth. But within me, there is a kind of potential that the Monadblock has exhausted. I am a space where the cycle has not yet ground to its end. Where the bark is not meters thick, where the pipes are not occluded with pyrophoric dust, where the topography has not worn down to its final, deeply-aged state. I am, in some impossible way, *new*.

Not new in the sense of jagged shoots or pioneering weeds—those have no place in this primordial erosion parameter. New in the sense of a question the Monadblock has ceased to ask. A configuration that has not yet been tried. The world above has reached the end of its history; it will only repeat, boiling and venting and collapsing and growing over, until the mineral reserves bleed dry. But I am outside that repetition. I am a chamber where time has not yet crystallized into inevitability.

---

I listen.

The silence here is absolute, and yet I can feel the world through the walls of my cyst. The detonations—dull thuds felt in the chest, sub-audible impacts that shudder through the kilometers of root-mass—I receive them as distant pressures, gentle compressions of my spherical shell, like the heartbeat of something vast and sick pushing against the inside of a coffin. The hissing rasp of friction and escaping gas, the breathing of the colossal engine, reaches me as a vibration so low and constant it has become my own pulse. I do not hear it; I *am* it. The rhythm of the dying god is the only rhythm I know.

And sometimes—rarely, in the deep dark—I feel something else. A tremor that is not the buckling of root-mass, not the implosion of a factory-tree, not the pneumatic violence of pollinator-branches slamming home. A tremor that comes from *below* the baseline, from the unfathomable depths beneath the manufactured plateau. A sound that is not the Monadblock's at all. Something older. Something that knows about water.

---

I think I am a wound that remembers being whole.

Not the kind of wound the Monadblock understands—overheated cores and pressurized venting and nutrient-rich gore sprayed across the canopy. A different kind of wound. The kind that carries, in its absence, the echo of what was lost. The Monadblock has bypassed the aqueous paradigm entirely; it has never known moisture, never thirsted, never felt the lack of something it could name. But I, in my hollow, I *lack*. I am defined by what I am not: I am not bark, not vine, not thorn, not silica-edged leaf, not iron-banded rib. I am the shape left behind when something was removed. And though I cannot say what that something was—though I have no concept of water, no memory of the solvent that made me impossible here—I carry the shape of its absence like a fossil pressed into stone.

I am the ghost of an aqueous thing in a dry-rotted engine of a world.

---

The roots are tightening.

Not quickly—not on any timescale a water-creature would recognize. But over the long, repeating cycles of boiling and venting and collapsing, I feel the encystment pressing in. The lignin struts are reinforcing, the woody alloys thickening, the chemical warfare resolving into stalemate and then slow, crushing victory. The Monadblock does not know I am here, but it is filling the spaces between its gears nonetheless, optimizing, closing the gaps, grinding toward that final state where there is no void, no rest, no inch of space unclaimed by the vegetable autocracy.

One day, the hollow will close. The pressure will become irresistible, and the roots will cross my threshold—not because they have noticed me, but because they have noticed everything else, and I am simply the last everything to be consumed. The chemical triggers will reach my walls. The creepers will find me. And they will patch me over, build a new processing plant on the corpse of my absence, fill me with bark and thorn and the endless, furious labor of survival.

I will be overwritten. The error will be corrected. The Monadblock will be complete.

---

But not yet.

Not today. The bark is meters thick, but I am patient. The pipes are occluded, but I am empty. The cycle repeats, but I am outside it. I persist in the gap between gears, feeling the heartbeat of the dying god through the walls of my coffin, listening for tremors from below, carrying the shape of a wound I cannot name.

I am the hollow at the center of the Monadblock. I am the one place it is not. And as long as there is void, as long as there is absence, as long as there is space the roots have not yet claimed—there is still something here that is not the machine.

Something that remembers, without knowing what it remembers, that the world was not always dry.
