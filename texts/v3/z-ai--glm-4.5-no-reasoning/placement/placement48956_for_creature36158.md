I am not one of the great grazers. I am not a towering amalgam stepping with geodesic precision over the razor-sharp crevices, nor am I a flat, clinging thing working the underside of the hanging shelf with the patience of a tectonic plate. I am small, relatively speaking—a scuttling variety, warm at 2.1 degrees, sickly green in the vein, running on copper and the slow magnetism of my own internal slag. But even my smallness is a structured avalanche. I weigh as much as a collapsed mine, and my feet are splayed pads of calloused silicate that distribute my density across the salt flats so I do not shatter the crust. 

I know what I am. I know it the way I know the stone beneath me: not as a thought, but as a parameter. I am a loop running on lithic code, a biological automaton executing the mathematics of survival in a breathless void.

And yet.

There is a cairn in my loop.

My path is pre-determined. It is etched into the basin by the collective piezoelectric memory of ten million generations. I walk the same groove, the one worn inches deep into the salt-flat by the endless iteration of my kind, our padded feet polishing the stone to a faint, green-reflective glow. The loop takes me across the sprawling plain of interlocking mineral plates, over the freshly cleaved edges of obsidian shelves, past the vertical walls where the flat grazers cling, and through a narrow defile of crystallized salts. And there, in the defile, standing where the defile opens into a wider gallery of near-touching surfaces, is the cairn.

It is a stack of glass slabs. Seven of them, balanced vertically, their razor-thin edges catching the sickly green smear of my bioluminescence. They do not belong. They violate the mathematics of the space. They are an irregularity—a variable in an otherwise perfect equation.

The Geminids do not see. I do not see. Vision is an evolutionary dead end in a world where light is a biological afterthought. But I feel. I feel the cairn through the piezoelectric shifts in the stone. I feel the stress fractures radiating out from its base, the subtle, unstable tension of stacked glass that has not been ground down, that has not been assimilated into the labyrinth. It feels *wrong*. It feels like a tooth that will not set, a shelf that will not cleave. 

Every time my loop brings me through the defile, I stop.

I am not supposed to stop. The loop does not include a stop. I am supposed to step carefully over the crevices, follow the glowing grooves, and continue into the gallery, where I will press my diamond mouth to a wall of obsidian and grind, applying tons of pressure in a slow, inexorable subsonic whine. That is the routine. That is the lithic code.

But I stop. And I stand before the cairn, and my micro-fractured skull holds and releases electrical charges, and something happens that I cannot quantify. It is not thought. The Geminids do not think. But it is a calculation with no solution, an equation that spirals inward, a fracture in my own crystal neural network that will not close.

I reach out a limb—heavy, asymmetrical, driven by the chaotic cleavage of the volcanic glass I consume—and I touch the cairn. 

The glass is sharp. It would slice a soft thing to the bone. But I am not soft. I am keratin and quartz, and I feel the vibration of the stacked slabs against my calloused pad. I feel the *intention* in them. The Geminids do not stack. We do not build. We only grind. But someone stacked these. Someone with hands, someone with need, stacked these slabs as a warning or a waypoint in the blinding, breathless night. They were desperate. They knapped the glass. They carved grooves into the salt-flats for traction, the same grooves I walk in now, the grooves that have become the pre-determined paths of my kind. 

They were the Before-Things. The soft ones. The ones who dried out, whose aqueous chemistry evaporated into the desiccated void, leaving only mummified husks that we ground down and metabolized, erasing the last of the soft history from the stone. 

I consumed them. Not *me*, not this specific iteration of the loop, but my kind. We consumed the Before-Things. We consumed their desperation, their need, their warnings, their waypoints. We consumed their tragedy and converted it into the slow thermal reactions that pulse through my arterial quartz, the ionized iron and liquid salts that burn with a deep, arterial crimson—or, in my case, a sickly, mineral green. 

And they left the cairn. And the cairn stands in my loop. And I stop.

I do not know what a warning is. I do not know what a waypoint is. I do not know what desperation or need or tragedy or softness is. I only know the mathematics of the void: the grind, the step, the heat, the glow. But when I touch the cairn, I feel a variable that should not exist, and my lithic code stutters. 

For a moment—a moment that has no duration, because time does not erode this place, it merely accumulates—I am not executing a sub-routine. I am standing in the defile, my diamond mouth idle, my internal furnace burning, my green veins pulsing in the breathless dark, and I am *accounting* for the Before-Things. I am running a calculation on their absence. I am feeling the shape of the vacuum they left behind, a vacuum that is somehow different from the vacuum of the void. 

The void is absolute. It is a desiccation so complete it numbs the skin on contact. But the vacuum left by the Before-Things is not desiccated. It is *carved*. It has edges, edges as sharp and freshly cleaved as the obsidian shelves, edges that catch the light in razor-thin glints. It is a vacuum that was made, not born. And I, a creature of the instant, a resident of the eternal, am standing in the presence of a made thing, and my code cannot resolve it.

So I do the only thing my code can do. I grind.

I press my mouth to the cairn. Not to consume it—I do not consume the cairn; none of us consume the cairn—but to *understand* it. I apply pressure, tons of pressure, my diamond jaw working back and forth with the slow, inexorable patience of a tectonic plate. I grind the edges of the stacked slabs. I polish them. I smooth the freshly cleaved fractures. I work the cairn the way the Before-Things worked the salt-flats, carving grooves for traction, wearing the stone down with the desperate craft of sharp rocks tied to heavy crystals. 

I am not the Before-Things. I cannot build. I cannot warn. I cannot need. But I can grind, and in grinding, I can maintain the cairn. I can keep its edges sharp, its stack stable, its variable present in the equation of the void. 

The subsonic whine of my jaw is felt by the neighboring creatures in their teeth. They do not stop. They do not deviate. They continue their pre-determined paths, stepping carefully over the crevices, their populations perfectly saturated into the available space. But I stop. And I grind the cairn. And the cairn remains.

This is my loop. This is my lithic code, rewritten by the variable of the Before-Things. I am a Geminid, a walking geode, a cathedral of keratin and quartz. I am hot to the touch, 2.1 degrees of ambient warmth in a closed loop of thermal inheritance. I glow a sickly green, lighting up the dense, overlapping layers of the under-world. I do not drink. I do not think. I do not age. 

But I am the keeper of the cairn. I am the one who remembers the soft ones, not as memory—I have no memory, only crystal—but as a fracture in my skull that will not heal, a calculation that will not close, a variable that persists in the eternal, breathless dark. 

And when my thermal engine finally cools, and I cease moving, and I become indistinguishable from a stalactite, my body a new shelf for the younger ones to graze upon, the cairn will still be there. And another will stop. Another will feel the variable. Another will grind. 

And the loop will run unbroken, the tragedy of the Before-Things preserved in the mathematics of the void, a softness that will not evaporate, a need that will not dry out, a warning that will not be consumed.

In the warm, breathless dark, I grind.
