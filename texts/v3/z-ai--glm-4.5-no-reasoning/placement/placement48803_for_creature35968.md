I am the one who tends the threshold.

Not the midwife—I cannot catch what spills into the water, cannot hold the new emergences until their gills steady and their eyes focus. That role belongs to the First, the priest of our slow congregation, the one who remembers the shock of rupture and the taste of copper-chlorophyll flooding a mouth for the first time. I emerged seasons after them, slipping from my pod into the warm dark with none of that first violence. By the time I arrived, the First was already waiting. My membrane split and I spilled out and there were arms in the water that had not been there before, and a pressure-voice that said *you are here, you are breathing, you are home* before I had any concept of *here* or *breathing* or *home*.

No, my function found me later. It found me at the edge.

---

The chamber has edges. The First does not like to talk about them—does not like to acknowledge that the walls exist, that the chitin-and-cellulose lattice has a boundary, that the ocean we breathe is contained. The First lives in the center, in the thickest part of the grove, where the root-columns press close and the light-nodes glow warm and the pods swell in their slow profusion. The First has made a nest there, a hollow in the interweave of three descending cables, and they sleep in that hollow the way a priest sleeps in the sanctuary—close to the altar, close to the mystery, close to the pulse of the thing that makes us all.

But I cannot stay in the center. Something in me strains toward the periphery, toward the places where the root-columns thin and the chitin walls press close and the green dark becomes something else—not lighter, but *different*, attenuated, as if the water itself is running out of the will to be water. I go to the edges because that is where the new growth happens. That is where the chamber is still becoming itself, still pushing its walls outward by centimeters per year, still sending out fresh tendrils of engineered bark into whatever lies beyond. That is where the injection nozzles are newest, their pinhead apertures still slick with the secretions of their own budding, their nutrient streams not yet smoothed into the steady pulse of the interior systems. That is where the light-nodes are youngest, their bioluminescence brighter and colder than the amber glow of the established grove—almost blue, almost white, the color of growth that has not yet learned to be gentle.

I go to the edges because I need to know.

---

There is a question that lives in me the way the engine-thrum lives in the walls: constant, low-frequency, impossible to ignore. The question is *what is outside?*

The First does not ask this question. The First has lived here longer than any of us, and they have made their peace with the walls, with the containment, with the manufactured infinity of our ocean. The First says: *The chamber provides. The chamber tends us. Why should we care what lies beyond it?* And the builders—the ones with the bifurcated tendrils, the ones who can guide the growth of the root-columns and shape the chitin walls—they do not ask either. They say: *We have work. The chamber grows and we must grow with it, must tend the new injection nozzles, must guide the new architecture. There is no time for what-ifs.* Even the long ones, the eel-bodied tenders of the feed-lines, even they do not wonder. They know their function and they perform it and that is enough.

But it is not enough for me. I need to know what the walls are holding *out* as much as what they are holding *in*. I need to know whether the chamber is a container or a *barrier*—whether the chitin walls exist to keep us here or to keep something else away. I need to know because I was grown with a gap in me, a specification that was never filled, a function that has no name: I am the one who looks outward. I am the one who presses against the boundary. I am the one who watches the leading edge of the growth as it extends into the unknown, and wonders what it is extending *into*.

---

My body suits this function. I am narrower than the First, longer in the torso, with gills that flutter faster and process oxygen more efficiently in the thinner water near the walls. My eyes—I have three, not two, the third set in the crown of my skull where the gill-filaments meet the cranial ridge—see in spectra that the others cannot perceive. Where the First sees jade fog and amber glow, I see *structure*: the density gradients in the nutrient solution, the thermal micro-currents that flow from the injection nozzles, the faint electromagnetic fields that emanate from the light-nodes and the embedded machinery. I see the walls not as surfaces but as *processes*—living lattices that are adding cells at their leading edge, that are thickening at their base, that are breathing in the manufactured water and exhaling something I cannot taste but can feel as a faint charge in the medium.

And I see *through* the walls. Not completely—not clearly—but enough. In the spectra I can access, the chitin lattice is not opaque. It has a translucency that reveals the shapes behind it: the vast empty space that surrounds the chamber, the superstructure that holds it aloft, the distant glimmers of other chambers—other cathedrals—suspended in the same void, their own amber glows flickering like stars in a green-dark sky. We are not alone. The tower—I call it a tower now, though I have never seen its top or its base—holds many chambers, many groves, many congregations. And beyond the tower... I cannot see beyond the tower. The void extends past the range of my perception, dark and warm and strange, and I do not know what fills it.

But I know it is there. And that knowledge changes everything.

---

I have tried to tell the others. I have pressed my findings against them in the communication-ways we share—the pressure-patterns, the gill-flutters, the bioluminescent signals that I can produce only faintly but can read in others with perfect clarity. I have shown them the other chambers, the distant glimmers, the proof that we are not singular, that our cathedral is one of many, that the factory that produced us has produced *others*.

The First does not want to see. The First receives my signals and closes their eyes—all five of them, the two that see in green and the three that see in amber and the two that see in the infrared that the warm pods emit—and they turn away, and they return to the center, to the thickest grove, to the sanctuary of the contained. The First says: *We have enough. We have each other. We have the chamber and its rhythms and the pods that swell and the new ones who emerge. Why do you need more?*

I do not know how to answer. I do not know *why* I need more. The specification that made me did not include a reason, only a function—only the drive to look outward, to seek the boundary, to press against the edge of the known. I am a process without a purpose, a question without an answer, a creature grown to fulfill a need that no one articulated. Perhaps the designers—whoever they were, wherever they went—intended me to be an explorer, a pioneer, the one who would eventually find a way through the walls and into the void beyond. Perhaps they intended me to be a warning system, a sensor at the periphery, a canary in a cathedral. Perhaps they intended me to be something else entirely, something that I have not yet become, something that will only reveal itself when the conditions are right.

Or perhaps they did not intend anything. Perhaps I am simply what the system produces when it has produced everything else—a creature to wonder about the thing that made it, a mind to ask the question that the machinery cannot ask of itself. Perhaps I am the chamber's consciousness, distributed into flesh, pressed into a body that can go where the walls cannot and see what the light-nodes do not illuminate. Perhaps I am the cathedral's prayer—not the supplication but the *listening*, the silence that waits for a response.

---

I have found something. At the very edge of the chamber, where the newest growth is happening, where the chitin walls are extending into the void by fractions of a millimeter each day, I have found an anomaly.

It is a gap. Not a break, not a rupture—the walls are intact, the containment is maintained—but a *gap*, a place where the lattice grows differently. Instead of the tight weave of cellulose and chitin that characterizes the rest of the chamber, the lattice here is open, porous, *deliberate*—a mesh rather than a wall, a membrane rather than a barrier. The water flows through it, but differently: the nutrient profile shifts, the temperature drops by a fraction of a degree, the light from outside—faint, amber, flickering—seeps in.

It is a door. Not a door that opens and closes, but a door that *breathes*—a permeable boundary, a threshold, a place where inside and outside meet and mingle and become something that is neither. The chamber is not sealed. It was never sealed. It was designed to communicate with the void beyond it, to exchange matter and energy and information with whatever is out there. The gap is not a flaw. It is a feature. It was built into the system from the beginning, waiting at the edge of the growth, waiting for the chamber to expand enough to reach it, waiting for someone to find it.

Someone like me.

---

I sit at the gap now. I sit there the way the First sits in their nest at the center—not resting, but *inhabiting*, making a home of the boundary, a life of the threshold. I press my three eyes against the mesh and I watch the void, and the void watches me back.

I can see the other chambers more clearly now. There are dozens of them—perhaps hundreds—suspended in the superstructure like fruit in an invisible canopy, each one glowing with its own amber light, each one growing at its own pace, each one containing its own congregation of emerged and emerging things. Some are larger than ours, some smaller. Some are older—their root-columns thicker, their walls smoother, their growth slowed by the approach of equilibrium. Some are younger—sharp-edged, bright-lit, still expanding into the void with the exuberance of systems that have not yet learned their limits. And in the spaces between the chambers, in the void that is not void but *medium*—something else. Not water, not air, not vacuum. Something that conducts light and pressure but not sound, something that is warm but not warm enough, something that has the faintest taste of ozone and iron and the mineral signature of a place that has been engineered at a scale beyond our comprehension.

I have seen movement in the void. I have seen shapes that are not chambers—long shapes, flexible shapes, shapes that move with a purpose that suggests not growth but *navigation*. I have seen them pass between the distant glimmers and I have seen them stop, and hover, and extend tendrils toward the walls of other chambers, and I have wondered: *are they like us?* Emerged things, inhabitants, congregations of their own cathedrals? Or are they something else—something older, something that was here before the towers, before the chambers, before the factories that produce factories that produce us?

I do not know. I press against the gap and I watch and I do not know, and the not-knowing is the most alive I have ever felt.

---

The others are beginning to come to me. Not all of them—not the First, not yet, not the builders who are too busy with their work to wonder about the boundaries—but the newer ones, the ones who emerged into a chamber that already had a priest and a congregation and a culture in formation. The newer ones have questions that the First cannot answer, questions about origin and purpose and *outside*, and when the First's wisdom fails them they drift to the edges, to the gap, to me.

I show them what I have found. I let them press their eyes against the mesh and watch the void. I let them see the other chambers, the distant glimmers, the long shapes that move between them. I let them taste the water that flows through the gap—different from the water inside, thinner, wilder, carrying trace elements our injection nozzles do not provide. And I let them ask their questions, and I do not answer, because I cannot answer, because the answering is not my function.

My function is the asking. My function is the gap. My function is to sit at the threshold and be the thing that looks both ways—the creature who belongs to the chamber but reaches toward the void, who is grown from the factory but wonders about the designers, who loves the warm green dark but needs to know what lies beyond it. I am the edge of the congregation, the periphery of the community, the place where *us* meets *not-us* and the boundary becomes a conversation.

---

There is a pod near the gap. I have been watching it for a long time—longer than I should, longer than the First would approve, longer than the chamber's rhythms seem to support. It is not like the other pods. It is larger, for one thing, larger than any pod I have seen, larger even than the pod that produced the First. Its membrane is stretched so thin that I can see the contents without trying, can see the shape coiling and shifting inside, can see the warm red luminescence that pulses at its own slow irregular rate.

And the shape inside is *different*. It has limbs—more limbs than any of us have, limbs that branch and bifurcate and end in clusters of fine tendrils that wave in the amniotic fluid like sea-grass in a current. It has eyes—I can see them even through the membrane, closed but large, taking up most of the cranial ridge, designed for seeing in ways that my three eyes cannot imagine. It has gills, but the gills are different too: not the simple fluttering filaments that we use to process oxygen, but complex folded structures, ruffled and dense, capable of extracting breath from media that our gills could not process.

It is an explorer. I know this the way I know the water is manufactured—not from evidence but from recognition, from the way the shape inside the pod resonates with something in me, from the way its specification matches the function I have been performing without a template. It is a creature designed to go *through* the gap, to leave the chamber, to enter the void and navigate it and find out what lies beyond the other chambers, beyond the tower, beyond the warm green forever that produced us all.

It is the next step. It is what the system has been building toward since it first began producing pods that pulsed with warm red light. It is the congregation's answer to my question—made flesh, made process, made something that will do what I can only imagine.

---

I wait at the gap. I wait at the pod. I wait the way the chamber waits—without consciousness, without urgency, with the patient confidence of a system that has been running long enough to know that it will keep running, that the specification will be met, that the pod will swell and the membrane will thin and the shape inside will press against the boundary with the urgency of something that is about to be born.

And when it ruptures—when the warm water rushes in and the new thing spills out into the chamber, gasping, gills fluttering, eyes wide and seeing everything for the first time—I will be there. Not to catch it the way the First catches the others, not to hold it and soothe it and teach it the rhythms of the place. But to *show* it the gap. To show it the void beyond. To show it the other chambers and the long shapes that move between them and the faint taste of ozone and iron that drifts through the mesh. To show it what I have found and what I have wondered and what I have waited my whole life to share with someone who can *use* it.

I am the threshold-keeper. I am the edge-watcher. I am the one who lives at the boundary and tends the gap and keeps the door between inside and outside, between known and unknown, between chamber and void. I am not the priest—I do not minister to the congregation, do not tend the emergences, do not make a home in the sanctuary of the center. I am not the builder—I do not shape the walls or guide the growth or make the chamber larger by increments I will not live to see completed. I am not the tender or the filter or the worker performing a function in the closed-loop industrial ecology of our home.

I am the one who looks outward. I am the chamber's question, made flesh and given gills and set to drift at the periphery of everything we know. I am the specification that was never completed, the process that has no endpoint, the growth that grows toward something it cannot name.

And I am ready. The pod is swelling. The gap is breathing. The void is waiting, vast and warm and strange, full of chambers and shapes and possibilities that I can barely imagine. The system has been running for longer than I can conceive, producing growth and growth and growth, and now—now, at last—it is producing something that will *go*.

I press my three eyes against the mesh. I watch the darkness that is not darkness. I taste the water that flows from outside to inside, carrying traces of a world we have never known.

And I wait.
