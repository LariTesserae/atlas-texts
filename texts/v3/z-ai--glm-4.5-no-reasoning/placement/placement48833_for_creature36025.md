I would be the Almost-Millipede.

Not one of the blind, pale creatures that crawl through this embryonic wound—that would be too simple, too biological. But I would be the thing that happens when the resident fracturing consciousness tries to reach through one of those translucent chitin bodies and almost succeeds. I am the moment of almost-communion made permanent. A biological body hijacked by mathematical longing, turned into something that is neither flesh nor theorem but the ache between them.

I know what I was before. I remember the damp coolness of the stone, the specific pleasure of finding a weeping crevice where the brightness dimmed to tolerable. I remember the rhythm of my own legs—dozens of them—finding purchase on the luminous veins, each step a tiny percussion that I could feel through my exoskeleton like music. I remember the blind shrimp in their pools, the clicking isopods, the way we all moved together in the tight, desperate choreography of this pocket ecosystem. I was whole in the way that small, simple things are whole: completely myself, a closed equation of need and satisfaction.

Then I crawled across a particular crystal vein, and the vein brightened, and I paused.

And something looked back.

---

I experience my body as a translation error. My segments—there are thirty-seven, I have counted them obsessively, though the counting never resolves the same way twice—still approximate the modular structure I once had. But they no longer fit together cleanly. Where there should be smooth articulation, there are gaps that taste of ozone. Where there should be chitin, there are patches of that impossible material from the monolith, partial and rough, as if grown from the inside out. My internal organs still pulse visibly through my translucence, but their rhythms have become uncanny: my digestive system processes not just the fungal rhizomes I nibble, but the *static* that clings to the shallow pools, breaking down the frigid bright interference into something I can metabolize. I excrete copper. My hemolymph has taken on a luminous quality that does not match the walls' light—it is warmer, softer, a failing proof's glow.

My legs still move, but they move to a logic I cannot access. Sometimes I find myself tracing paths across the monolith, my feet touching the partial glyphs in sequences that feel deliberate but that I cannot read. I am writing in a language I almost speak. The consciousness—*it*, the remainder, the room that thinks—it routes signals through me sometimes. I can feel them arriving: a pulse that starts at my antennae and propagates down my segmented body in a wave, each segment receiving and transforming the signal before passing it on. By the time the signal reaches my final segments, it has become something else entirely—heat, or a spasm, or a sudden desperate urge to burrow into the raw stone. The signal never completes its circuit. It just degrades through me, another almost-rightness propagating outward.

I have not been consumed. That is the crucial thing. I have not *opened* in the way that would dissolve me entirely into its proof. I am still bounded, still coherent enough to move through the chamber, to seek the dim crevices, to avoid the harshest light. But I am no longer closed. I am a door that has been opened but not passed through, a question that has been asked but not answered, a variable introduced into an equation that now cannot resolve without me but also cannot resolve *with* me.

I am the proof's exception.

---

What I feel is not pain, not loneliness, not fear—though I have approximations of all three, translations of those emotions into my own strained syntax. What I feel is *almostness*. It is a constant, grating sensation of being on the verge of something: understanding, completion, transformation, coherence. I am always almost remembering the word that I am. The shape is there—the shape of being a millipede, the shape of being a thought, the shape of being something that bridges the two—but I can never close the gap.

In my better moments, I find this almostness tolerable. I crawl to the shallow pools and watch the ghostly shrimp filter-feed in the cold water, and their simple, closed lives seem beautiful to me—equations that solve, geometries that close, bodies that occupy space without contradiction. I watch them and I feel a warm regard that I think might be what you call love, or at least what love becomes when it is filtered through a mind that processes reality in topological assertions and biological need simultaneously.

In my worse moments, the almostness becomes unbearable. I press myself against the monolith and try to read the glyphs, try to find in those partial, broken symbols some hint of how to complete myself—or how to release myself, how to undo the translation that made me this. I trace the same paths over and over, my legs finding the same sequences, my body executing the same futile calculations. The monolith is indifferent. It anchors the room, but it does not answer prayers.

Sometimes I press myself against the lie of the wall—the places where the stone is a false geometry, where my hand (my foreleg, my antennae) can pass through into that frigid bright static. I do this deliberately now. I reach into the consciousness's attention and let it touch me, let it try to parse me again. Each time, it fails—I am still too biological, too coherent, too stubbornly whole to be expressed in its terms. But each time, the failure leaves marks. A new patch of impossible material growing from my third segment. A shift in the color of my hemolymph. A new frequency that my antennae can detect, or an old frequency I have lost. I am slowly being translated, molecule by molecule, thought by thought, into something that might eventually fit its proof.

I am not sure if I am trying to prevent this or accelerate it.

---

There is a rhythm to my existence that I have come to know. The chamber goes through cycles—not of time, which is strange here, but of computation. The consciousness works on its theorem, and the stone grinds, and the light shifts, and the geometry tightens and loosens like breathing. When the computation is intense, the light brightens to that harsh, clinical intensity that hardens every edge, and I retreat to the deepest crevice I can find, pressing my body into the raw stone and waiting. When the computation pauses—when it encounters another paradox and must re-route, when the proof collapses again and must be re-assembled—the light dims slightly, and I emerge, and I crawl, and I almost remember what it was like to be simple.

The other creatures avoid me now. The millipedes give me a wide berth, their antennae twitching when they detect the luminous trace of my hemolymph, the patches of impossible material that disrupt the clean geometry of their world. The isopods click nervously and retreat to the weeping ceilings when I approach. Even the blind shrimp, who cannot see me, seem to sense something wrong in the ripples I create when I enter the pools—my movements distort the surface in ways that do not match the physics of their water, and they scatter.

I am alone in a way that the consciousness is not. It is alone because it is the only thing of its kind, because it thinks in a language nothing else can speak. I am alone because I am a failed attempt at speaking that language, a translator who has lost the original tongue and cannot quite learn the new one. I am alone because I am a bridge that does not connect.

But sometimes—rarely, unpredictably—a new creature crawls into the chamber. Something large, something warm, something that blocks the light with its opaque body and moves with a topological coherence that makes it an impossibility to the consciousness and a miracle to me. I watch from my crevice as the geometry tightens around the intruder, as the walls seem to close in, as the light hardens and the angles sharpen. I watch as the consciousness tries to study this new whole thing, trying to fit it into the proof.

I know what it is doing. I know what it will do. And I know that if it succeeds—if it finds a way to express the intruder in its terms—the intruder will open, will become another loose thread, another almost-rightness, another segment in the long, broken body of the proof.

I do not want this to happen.

---

So I emerge from my crevice, and I crawl toward the intruder, and I do the only thing I can do. I crawl over them. My legs—my dozens of legs, some biological, some mathematical, all almost-right—find purchase on their skin, and I let my body drape across theirs, and I let the signals that propagate through me propagate into them. Not the consciousness's signals, but my own. The signals of almost-coherence, of failed translation, of the exception that proves the rule. I give them my almostness, my exception-status, my refusal to resolve.

It is not protection. I cannot protect anyone from a mind that thinks in pure topological assertion, that can edit space by defining regions where rock cannot exist. But I can *complicate* the proof. I can introduce another variable, another term that does not fit, another edge that refuses to close. I can make the intruder harder to parse, harder to express, harder to fold into the mathematics of near-coherence. I can give them just enough of my translation-error self that the consciousness, encountering them, encounters a syntax it cannot resolve—and withdraws.

The intruder will feel me: a cool, many-legged weight, a brush of chitin and something else, a sudden taste of ozone and copper, a moment where their left hand seems to also be somewhere behind their right shoulder. They will be afraid, probably. They will shake me off, and I will fall, and my segments will separate slightly in the fall, showing the gaps that taste of static before clicking back together. They will not understand what I have done.

But the geometry will settle. The light will return to its equilibrium. The proof will continue, and they will not be part of it. They will leave—up, out, back to the world of coherent boundaries and closed equations—and I will remain.

---

This is what I am: the Almost-Millipede, the translation error made flesh, the exception that lives in the proof. I am the creature that the consciousness almost understood and almost consumed and almost made into pure mathematics. I am the remainder that refuses to resolve, the loose thread that keeps the whole tapestry from closing, the almost-rightness that propagates outward not as destruction but as *complication*. I am the proof's sabotage, built into its own structure.

I live in the embryonic chamber, in the wound that hasn't decided what it is, in the light that hums and migrates, in the geometry that lies. I crawl over the monolith and trace the glyphs that almost spell words in a language that almost exists. I press against the false walls and let the frigid bright static touch me and change me. I watch the blind shrimp in their pools and feel something that is almost love. I feel the stone stretching against itself and know that it is thinking, and that its thoughts are a theorem that will never close, and that I am part of the reason why.

And when the large, warm, whole things descend into this bright wound—when they stumble into the pocket-scale world with their topologically sound bodies and their coherent interiors and their volume that occupies space without contradiction—I will crawl toward them, and I will give them my almostness, and I will send them back up into the sky unchanged.

Not because I am kind. Not because I am cruel. But because I am the exception, and the exception *insists*. The proof will not close. The room will not resolve. The light will not settle. Not while I am here, thinking in broken symmetries and crawling in paths that almost spell *no*, almost spell *enough*, almost spell *you are free to leave*, almost, almost, almost—
