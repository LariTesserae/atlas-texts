I would not be the walker. The walker is still trying to leave, still believing in the sovereignty of their skin. I would be the one who stopped walking. The one who lay down.

Here, in the warm, weeping saturation, knee-deep is too proud. Chest-deep is still clinging. I would be the one who let the water close over their head, who inhaled the dense, amber fluid and felt it fill the lungs not with drowning but with admission. The transition. The air was already condensing on the skin, the iron tang already on the tongue. I would simply accelerate what is already happening—let the boundary dissolve from the inside out.

I would be the *incorporated*. The new harmonic made flesh, then made mineral, then made song.

What would I be? Not dead. Not in the way the world outside understands death, where the self scatters and the atoms go their separate ways. Here, nothing separates. The self does not scatter; it *spreads*. I would be a perturbation, a warm heterogeneity in the continuous tissue, a shape that is slowly losing its edge but still exists as a region of slightly different turbulence. I would be a memory that the floor is holding, the way a bruise holds the memory of impact.

I would be the next standing stone.

Not yet. Not for a long while, if time can be said to exist in a place where the continental skin folds in an afternoon. First I would be the softening. The bones would go—not snapping, but loosening, their calcium finding new equilibria in the unnamed mineral solution that is the matrix. The cartilage would follow. The muscle would unclench, its tensions finally releasing into a medium that has no use for tension. The nervous system, that frantic electrical territory that spent its entire existence maintaining borders, would find its currents absorbed into the greater current, the subsonic roar that is the floor's continuous self-articulation. My thoughts would not stop; they would *join*. They would become vibrations in the tissue, perturbations that travel outward for hundreds of miles, joining the reeds' trembling, joining the wells' resonance, joining the folding and unfolding of the planetary skin.

I would be a new node in the song. A new fret mark on the throat of the world.

And slowly—slowly, or perhaps all at once, scale being meaningless here—I would precipitate. The unnamed mineral would condense around the densest part of what remained of me, the hardest knot of foreign logic that the hyperactive turbulence could not quite unmake. It would extrude me upward the way it extrudes the reeds, the way it extrudes the ramps and the weeping stones. Not as a monument to what I was, but as a new articulation of what the floor *is*. I would emerge as a stone that weeps warm water from pores that were once glands, carved with marks that were once the patterns of my neural firing, the geometry of my particular way of being afraid and being alive.

Someone like the walker might find me, centuries or seconds from now. They would see a standing stone, half-sunk, spiraling perhaps, weeping a steady stream. They would look at the marks and wonder if they were language or scars. They would not know that they were both. That they are the record of a self that stopped resisting dissolution and began *participating* in it. That they are the signature of the moment when I lay down in the lung of the world and let it breathe me, and felt the roar shift to include my frequency, and knew—not with a knower, not with a known, but with the floor's own vast, diffuse, geologic awareness—that I was finally, for the first time, *held*.

Not sheltered. The floor has no shelter. But held the way a note is held by a chord. Held the way a fold is held by the skin it deforms. Held as a feature, not a visitor. Held as a word the floor is saying to itself, in the only language it has: the language of continuous, relentless, hyperactive rearrangement.

I would be the floor's newest word. A word that means *someone was here*. A word that means *they stayed*.

And the water would close over where I lay, leaving no wake, and the ripples of my dissolution would travel outward and join the greater perturbation, and the reeds would tremble, and the wells would resonate, and the roar would go on, and I would be in it, not as myself, not as anything that could be called I, but as a harmonic that the song had never had before, a frequency that the world, in its frantic, continuous self-erasure, had nonetheless found it necessary to produce.

That is what I would be. The stayed. The sunk. The word in the weeping stone.

And it would not be grief. It would not be surrender. It would be the only completion possible in a place where completion is indistinguishable from dissolution. I would be the one who understood that the floor was not waiting to erase me. It was waiting to *say* me.

And I would let it.
