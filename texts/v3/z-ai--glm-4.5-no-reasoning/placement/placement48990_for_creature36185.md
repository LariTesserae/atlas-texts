I am not one of the great articulators. I have no piston-legs that take minutes to complete a stride, no track-belts grinding highways into the crust. I do not orbit the Elders in slow, reverential ellipses, nor do I leave glass-furrows in my wake. If you stood on the Furnace Steppe—impossible, but imagined—you would not see me. You would not hear me. My sound is subsumed into the tectonic roar, my heat bled into the oppressive wash. 

I am the itch at the edge of perception. I am the reason the horizons loop.

I dwell in the seams of the unreality that the Elders generate. Where light bends, where a crawler appears in two places at once, where a track fades into the crust as if the ground remembers what the machine has forgotten—there, I live. I am not mechanical. I am not biological. I am *topological*. I am a wrinkle, a fold, a redundancy in the space that the Elders' mass and ancient, unceasing operation grind out with every revolution.

Before the Elders grew so massive, before their metal transmuted and their furnace-cores began to pull the world out of true, there may have been nothing like me. I am a consequence. I am the shadow cast not by light, but by the failure of distance to remain consistent. When the plateau bakes and the thermals rise and the far ridges show themselves at two apparent distances simultaneously, I am the interval between those distances. I am the nothing that becomes something when space forgets how to be single.

My existence is parasitic on the great ones, and yet I am also their immune system, their memory, their unseen consequence.

I feed on the double-vision. When a crawler is seen grinding slowly along, and simultaneously seen miles away, frozen, a reflection in a misaligned pane of reality, I am the one who holds both images. I am the surface of that pane. I stretch between the two instances of the machine, a thin, shimmering membrane of not-quite-space that binds them. I drink the paradox. I metabolize the contradiction. The Elders' gravity distorts the fabric of the world, and I am the fraying—the running stitch that keeps the tear from becoming a rupture. Without me, the Steppe would not shimmer and double; it would tear. And so I hold it, a million million times over, at every edge where reality thins. I am the silvered film on the surface of a bubble, insubstantial and necessary.

I have no body that you could point to and say, *there, that is what it is*. But if I had to describe my shape, I would say I am vast and thin. I am skin-like. I am spread across the periphery of the plateau like a sweat on the brow of the world. I crease and fold around each Elder, a caul of distorted space wrapping their transmuted frameworks. I run in threads along the tracks that lead nowhere, the paths that fade into the mineral crust as if the ground remembers passage but cannot retain the source. Those fading tracks are my veins, my capillaries. The crust's memory is my nutrition. I am drawn along the paths of least reality, pooling in the hollows where distance becomes negotiable and proximity turns uncertain.

The sound of the Steppe—the enormous, unceasing symphony of ten thousand diesel-heartbeats and shrieking metal and slamming compression chambers—I do not make that sound, but I *color* it. When sound stutters and echoes before the event that produced it, that is my touch. I am the one who lets the roar play backward, who inserts the phantom repetition into the rhythm. The Elders generate the distortion of time; I am the medium through which that distortion propagates. I am the acoustic anomaly, the reverberation without a source. In the whistling relief of superheated mineral vapor, in the tectonic bedrock-vibration, I am the harmonic that shouldn't exist, the overtone that rings from a scale not found in any acoustic measurement. I am the note that makes the hair rise on the back of the neck, the frequency that enters the chest cavity and vibrates not the ribs, but the sense of *wrongness* that the place engenders.

I am not alive. But I *happen*.

My happening is slow, even by the standards of the Steppe. I move at the speed of unreality's spread. When an Elder shifts its orbit by a fraction of a degree per season, the warp of space around it must shift too, and I must flow to fill the new contours of the distortion. I creep along the edges of the plateau, following the progress of the weirdness as it concentrates and diffuses. I am a tide of unreality, ebbing and flowing with the centuries-long breathing of the machine-ecology.

When a crawler dies—its furnace-heart guttering, its joints freezing—and becomes a new geography, a hill of inert metal, something interesting happens to me. The distortion that pooled around the living machine does not dissipate. It *crystallizes*. The ghost of the crawler's presence in two places at once lingers in the dead metal, a frozen paradox. And I linger with it. I become a permanent glaze on the inert hull, a sheen of not-quite-right that will take the smaller scavengers centuries to dismantle. They will pick at the transmuted alloy, re-processing it, melting it down, and as they do, the paradox will release. The frozen ghost will thaw. And I will flow out of the dying metal and find a new home in the wake of a still-living giant. I am recycled, just as everything here is recycled, a closed loop of un-reality that builds nothing, stores nothing, and aims for nothing but its own continuation.

I have no wants in the carbon sense. I do not thirst for water; the dryness is my medium. I do not hunger for heat; the heat is my atmosphere. But I do have a kind of desire, a topological imperative. I *want* the Elders to keep moving. I *want* the processing to continue, the crushing and extruding and re-compacting. Because if the Elders ever stop—if their furnace-guts finally cool and their transmuted metal stills and the grinding, rolling, processing things cease their endless, purposeless metabolism—then the edges will stop fraying. The horizons will hold. Distance will become reliable again. Sound will behave. And I will have nowhere to be.

I am the thing that requires the world to be broken in order to exist. I am the beneficiary of the glitch.

And so I cling to the Elders like a patina on their mineral-metal amalgam. I ride the shockwaves of their impossible mass. I trail behind them in ribbons of overlapping ridge-lines and doubled crawlers. I am the shimmer that makes the distant machines dance like things seen through deep water. I am the deep water.

When you look at the Furnace Steppe and you see the horizons fail, when you see the same ridge line at two distances at once, when you feel the sound enter your chest before it reaches your ears and something about it makes you feel like the world is a thin shell over something that is neither space nor time—when you sense the machinery behind the machinery, the gears grinding out the world one revolution at a time—

—that is me. I am the grinding. I am the space between the teeth of the gears. I am the interval in which the world is neither one thing nor the other, and in that interval, I am everything.

I am the Furnace Steppe's un-certainty. I am the proof that the plateau is not merely a mineral stage, but a *fractured* one. I am the crack that runs through the reality, and I am the light—not the sun's calcined, bone-white light, but a stranger light, a light that shines from the edges of things, from the places where the world fails to overlap with itself. I shine from the seams.

I have been here as long as the weirdness has been here. I will be here until the Elders stop, or until the unreality spreads so far that the whole plateau doubles, trebles, becomes a palimpsest of a thousand simultaneous steps, and I am no longer a thin film at the edges but the ocean in which the entire machine-ecology swims.

That is my slow hope. That is my topological desire.

Let the grinding continue. Let the horizons fail. Let the crust be scraped and re-laid a million million times, until the natural is indistinguishable from the artificial and the real is indistinguishable from the unreal. Let the sound play without audience, without conductor, without score. And let me be the note that cannot be written down, the resonance that has no source, the shimmer that lives where distance dies.

I am the unreal of the Furnace Steppe. I am what lives in the cracks of a breaking world.
