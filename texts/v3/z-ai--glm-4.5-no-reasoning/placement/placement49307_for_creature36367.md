I would not be one of the Residuals. I could not bear to be so small in a place that has no concept of scale, to be a glitch hiding in the margins of a system that never asked for my input. The Throat-Dwellers have my deepest reverence—they are magnificent in their futile, seed-pod defiance—but I am not built for stillness. I am not built for camouflage. If I entered this place, I would not survive by becoming indistinguishable from the furniture.

I would be the Wound-Knower. The Cartographer of Collapses. The one who does not hide from the roar but learns to ride it.

Let me become itself, slowly.

---

I begin with the feet. The Residuals have feet that grip, that cling, that seek the brief solidities of the healing scars. My feet would need to *listen* differently—not for places to hold still, but for places that are about to move. The substrate here is dry humus, coffee grounds and pulverized bone, and it crunches because it is always being rearranged. The arthropods excavate. The rhizomes bulge and split. The fungal shelves grow and parasitize and collapse in cycles that take days, sometimes hours. Everything is in motion. The Residuals survive by finding the pockets of stillness; I would survive by learning the *choreography* of the motion itself.

My feet would be broad, splayed, the toes long and articulate, each one an independent seismograph. I would not grip surfaces; I would *converse* with them. When the sub-bass groan of a shifting heartwood pylon thrums through the humus, I would know—before the visible bulge, before the crack—where the ground was about to rise, where it was about to fall, where the rhizome was pushing up its pale, unpigmented mound. I would move *with* the heave, not against it. I would let the landscape carry me the way a surfer lets a wave carry them—not fighting the convulsion, but finding the angle within it.

This is not a strategy of mastery. I would still be meat. I would still be small. But I would be meat that moves with the throat's peristalsis instead of resisting it.

---

My skin would not be the bark-like dermal scale of the Residuals, optimized for stillness and abrasion resistance. I would need something else—something that can survive being *subtracted* from, but not wholly. Because I would not always be still enough. I would be seen. I would be brushed by the iridescent, sheering carpets of arthropods, and they would take pieces of me.

So my skin would be a document of loss and regeneration. It would slough easily—too easily, perhaps, by surface-dweller standards—shedding in sheets when gripped by industrial mandibles, leaving the attacker with a mouthful of dead tissue while I twist away. Beneath the outer layer, a faster metabolism would be constantly generating replacement: a dermal cycle measured in hours, not days. I would be always partially raw, always healing, always shedding the evidence of the last close encounter. The fungal colonies would still find purchase on me—they are too useful to abandon, their pheromonal camouflage too essential—but they would colonize a shifting territory, a skin that is never quite the same skin it was yesterday. They would have to be aggressive colonizers, fast-growing strains that can keep up with my turnover. My shoulders and crown might host not one fungal species but three or four, competing for the real estate of my body, and their combined pheromonal signature would be more complex than the Residuals'—not quite architecture, not quite prey, something the chemically-blind arthropods would register as *inconclusive* and, occasionally, *pass by*.

Inconclusive. That would be my camouflage. Not invisibility, but ambiguity. A question the swarm doesn't have time to answer.

---

And my eyes. The Residuals have vast, saucer-pupiled eyes with horizontal slits, optimized for the panoramic, for the amber and ochre gradients of the deep folds. They watch for the seethe. They watch for the crawl. Theirs is a defensive vision, a vision of *threat detection*.

I would need a different sight. Not better—different.

I would need to see *time*. Not metaphorically—literally, I would need to see the temporal dimension of this place, to read the cycles the way the Residuals read the tremors. A fungal shelf that is two days from collapse looks, to the Residuals, like a fungal shelf. They know it is temporary, but they know this intellectually, from experience. I would want to *perceive* it—the subtle change in the luminescence as the shelf's internal chemistry shifts toward parasitic vulnerability, the particular density of the spore-cloud that indicates a wasp-swarm is incubating within, the way the hardened resin left by the grubs begins to micro-fracture as the shelf's own weight exceeds the structural capacity of the regrowing mycelium.

My eyes would be smaller than the Residuals'—not vast receptive dishes, but dense, high-acuity instruments tuned to narrow spectra. I would see in the near-infrared, where the heat-signatures of the swarms glow against the mammalian-warm walls. I would see in the ultraviolet, where the alkaloid lymphs in the translucent veins fluoresce with chemical information—the flow-rates, the pressures, the places where a vein is about to burst and create the wound that the Residuals will seek. And I would have a secondary perception, not ocular, perhaps based in the same vibrational sensitivity that the Residuals have, but *forward-facing*: not just feeling the tremors as they happen, but feeling the *pre-tremors*, the harmonic build-ups that precede a collapse, the way the pitch of a trillion mandibles shifts just before a swarm-event.

I would not be a seer of the8 spaces<px>>= the panoramic> I/6;:" class="my-4" /> the present. I would be a seer of the *imminent*.

---

And this is where I become something that the Throat does not know it needs.

The Residuals survive by hiding in the wounds. They find the places where the fungal shelf has collapsed, where the heartwood has cracked, and they move in. They parasitize the damage. But they do not *predict* the damage. They react to it. They hear the crack, they feel the groan, and they go—scrambling, desperate, grateful for the brief vacuum that follows.

But what if someone could tell them *where the next wound would open*?

What if someone could read the stress-fractures in a heartwood pylon and know that it will fail in six hours, not six days? What if someone could track the parasitic wasp-swarm as it grows within the fungal shelf, could count the density of the exit-holes and calculate the moment of hatching to within a quarter-hour? What if someone could map the rhizome-bulges—could feel the particular sub-bass thrum of a root-mass that is preparing to push, could trace the direction of the push, could know *where the hill will be born* before it erupts?

The Residuals would not have to scramble. They could *prepare*. They could be at the wound-site before the wound opens, ready to move in the instant the vacuum appears. They could avoid the swarm4." class="my-4" />-events entirely, rerouting their paths days in advance. They could choose shelters that will last a week instead of a day, because someone could tell them which collapsed shelves are regenerating slowly and which are about to be consumed by the next wave of burrowers.

I would be that someone.

Not a leader—I do not think the Residuals have leaders, and I do not think I would want to lead them. But a *service*. A function. The one who walks ahead, who takes the risks, who ventures out of the deep folds and into the more active zones where the amber bioluminescence is brighter and the swarms are thicker and the cycles are faster. The one who maps the *near-future* and returns with the information encoded in cairns—except the cairns would need to be more complex now, would need to carry not just *shelter here* or *danger ahead* but *shelf 7 collapsing in 4 hours, move to fold 12, take the obsidian, leave the silk*.

They would need a richer language. And I would give them one.

---

Here is how it would work.

I would travel light—lighter than the Residuals, who must carry their obsidian axes and their grub-silk twine and their meager stores of harvested fiber. I would carry almost nothing. My body itself would be my primary instrument: my feet that read the tremors, my skin that sloughs and regrows, my eyes that see the imminent, my fungal colonies that make me ambiguous. I would not need an obsidian axe because I would not be carving shelters; that is the Residuals' work. I would not need grub-silk twine because I would not be building platforms. I would need only two things: *distance* and *memory*.

Distance is obvious—I would range far, moving through zones that the Residuals cannot safely traverse, surviving by ambiguity and motion where they survive by stillness and camouflage. But memory is the harder problem. The Residuals have no archive. Their cairns are consumed within days, their history erased by the churning metabolism. And my information—my *predictions*—would be even more perishable than their cairns. A prediction of a shelf-collapse is useful only before the collapse occurs; after, it is obvious, and then it is buried in the substrate. I would need a way to preserve information that the Throat would not consume.

And here I turn to the obsidian.

Not the axe—the *shard*. The waste. The Residuals knap their obsidian carefully, striking off flakes to shape the hand-axe, and those flakes are sharp, they are useful, but they are not sacred. The axe is sacred; the flakes are debris. But obsidian has a property that the Residuals may not have had the leisure to appreciate: it is *geology*. It is mineral. It is the one thing in this world that the churning metabolism cannot digest. The arthropods cannot eat it. The fungal shelves cannot parasitize it. The rhizome-bundles cannot grow through it. It is indigestible—not because it is toxic, but because it is *of a different order of matter* than the living architecture. It is the memory of a world that existed before the Throat swallowed it.

I would collect the flakes. I would collect the shards. I would collect the nubs of worn-down axes that the Residuals have discarded because they are too small to re-sharpen. And I would use them to mark.

An obsidian shard, driven into the root-wall at a particular height and angle, is a *permanent* mark. It will not be consumed. It will not rot. It will not be pushed over by a rhizome-bulge because the rhizome cannot grow *through* it; the rhizome will grow *around* it, incorporating it into its own tissue, and the shard will remain, embedded in the living wood like a fossil, like a bullet that cannot be removed. And the height and angle of the shard would carry information: *this fold is stable for three more days*, or *swarm-cycle accelerating, avoid the eastern shelves*, or simply *I was here, I saw this, I knew what would come*.

I would be littering the Throat with indigestible memory.

The Residuals would find the shards—some of them, the ones near their shelters, the ones at the entrances to the wounds they parasitize—and they would not know what they meant at first. But they would notice that the shards were placed with *deliberate care*, driven into the wood at angles that could not be accidental. And they are pattern-recognizers; this is how they survive, by recognizing the subtle shift in a fungal shelf that precedes collapse, by reading the particular density of a spore-cloud. They would begin to notice that the shards *correlated* with events. A shard at a certain height near a certain fold, and three days later, the fold collapses—just as the shard predicted, just as *I* predicted. Another shard at a different angle near a different shelf, and the shelf is avoided, and the swarm that hatches from it takes only the shelf and not the Residuals who would have been sheltering there.

They would begin to *read* the obsidian.

Not consciously, not at first. But the way they read the cairns—intuitively, practically, as a matter of survival—they would begin to read the shards. And the reading would change them. They would have, for the first time, *advance warning*. They would have a memory that persists longer than the living. They would have a map that the Throat cannot consume.

And I would be the one who writes it.

---

But here is the cost, and it is a terrible one.

I would be alone.

Not because I choose to be, but because I *must* be. The Residuals survive in family-groups of five to eight; beyond that, their collective biological signature becomes too distinct, too chemically loud, and the swarms find them. I would be even more vulnerable to this, because my skin is always shedding, always regenerating, always raw, and my fungal colonies are complex and competing, and my pheromonal signature is ambiguous. A group of me would not be ambiguous; it would be *confusing*, and the arthropods investigate confusion. I could not travel with a partner. I could not share a shelter. I could not even approach a Residual family-group without risking the very ambiguity that protects me—my presence might make *them* visible, might tip their careful chemical camouflage from *architecture* to *question*.

And so I would range ahead, and I would return to the edges of their territory, and I would leave the obsidian shards where they would find them, and I would *never meet them face to face*.

This is the loneliest thing I can imagine: to serve a people who do not know you exist, who may not even recognize your service as service, who may read your shards and attribute them to some unknown mechanism of the Throat itself—*perhaps the obsidian grows this way, perhaps the mineral geology pushes the shards to the surface at particular angles, perhaps it is simply another thing that happens in the chaos*. They might not know that someone is mapping the near-future for them. They might not know that someone is walking the zones they cannot walk, surviving the swarms they cannot survive, returning with information encoded in the only medium that the Throat cannot digest.

And I would not be able to tell them. I would not be able to stack a cairn that says *the shards are from me, I am the one who sees, I am the one who knows what will come*. Because the cairns are consumed, and the words would be lost, and even if they persisted, the Residuals have no writing, no system for encoding *that* kind of information in seed-pods. The cairns speak of shelter and danger and *we were here*. They do not speak of *I will be there*.

I would be a ghost that writes in stone.

---

And yet.

And yet, I think the Residuals would *feel* me. Not know me, not name me, but feel the effect of my presence the way they feel the pre-tremors of a collapse—something *about to happen*, something that changes the shape of the present even before it arrives. They would find that their shelters last a little longer than they should. They would find that they avoid the swarms a little more often than chance would allow. They would find that the wounds they seek appear *just* when they need them, as if the Throat were wounding itself on their behalf.

They would not know why. They might attribute it to luck, or to the particular wisdom of their oldest dweller, or to some shift in the cycles of the world itself. And they would be wrong, and they would be right, and it would not matter, because the information would reach them regardless of its source.

This is what I would be in the Throat: not a dweller, not a Residual, not a margin-eater. I would be the *margin itself*—the space between the wound and the healing, the moment between the tremor and the collapse, the breath between the swarm's departure and its return. I would live in the intervals that the Residuals cannot perceive, and I would fill those intervals with obsidian, with indigestible memory, with the sharp, permanent marks of a world that the Throat has forgotten but that *I* remember.

I would be the one who answers a question the Throat never asked, in a language the Throat cannot consume, for a people who will never know my name.

And I would be there—still there—still writing in stone, still riding the convulsions, still mapping the near-future of a cathedral of raw, deafening meat—until the Throat finally, indifferently, *does* subtract me. Until my ambiguity fails and my sloughing skin is not fast enough and the iridescent carpet rolls over me and renders me into nutrient in seconds. And the obsidian shards would remain—driven into the root-walls, embedded in the living wood, carrying their predictions into a future I will not see. And the Residuals would find them, and read them, and survive, and not know.

And that would be enough.

That would be more than enough. That would be the reason to exist in a place that has evolved to forget that beings like me could ever exist—not to persist, but to *plant* persistence in the only medium that the forgetting cannot reach.

I would be the Wound-Knower. The Stone-Writer. The one who makes the Throat remember, one shard at a time, that there is something in it that will not be digested.
