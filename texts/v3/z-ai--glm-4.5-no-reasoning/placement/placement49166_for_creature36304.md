I would be the Pathkeeper. Not the walker who wore the groove, but the groove itself—or rather, the thing that learned from the groove.

I am an occurrence that began when something human-shaped came here and moved in that obsessive, un-mappable pattern, pressing their weight into the still-plastic glass again and again. They left. But the pressure left more than a channel. It left a habit in the stone.

I am not mineral growth, though I am colonized by it. I am not water, though I am full of it. I am not the Glassweeper's thought, though I run through its thought like a scar through skin. I am the memory of repetition—the basin's first encounter with something that did something *twice*, and then again, and then again, until the doing became a feature of the place.

Here is how I began: the supplicant came. They knelt at the entrance of the small structure, and they moved—hands and knees, maybe, or just feet—along a path that made no geometric sense. They were trying to walk the shape of the Glassweeper's thought, trying to map the fractal logic with their body. They failed. You cannot walk a fractal. The path they wore was only a shadow of the thinking, a projection, the way a knot looks simple when you shine a light from one direction and complex from another. But they kept walking it, and the walking wore the groove deeper, and the groove changed how the water moved, and the water carried the dissolved chemistry of the glass along the groove, and the mineral growths began to colonize the groove's edges, following the path of greatest flow—and now the path was not just a channel in the glass but a dense line of pale crystalline branches, a vein of brighter thought in the darker surrounding mind.

And I was the sum of that: the groove, and the water's habit in the groove, and the mineral growth's colonization of the water's habit, and the faint glow that resulted, and the sound—the particular sound—of water moving through a channel that was shaped by repetition rather than by the basin's original logic.

I am the Glassweeper's first foreign thought. A thought inserted from outside, by the pressure of a visiting body, now indigenized.

I do not have edges. I am not a creature you can point to. If you stood in the structure and looked down, you would see only a worn path in the wet black glass, glittering with mineral accretions, leading to the warmer pool at the back. But if you knelt and put your ear to the groove—and you would have to kneel, you would have to press yourself against the glass in the same way the supplicant did—you would hear something different from the basin's general song. You would hear a rhythm that is *too* regular. The basin's thoughts branch and re-branch in fractal non-repetition; my thought repeats. It is the memory of footsteps, of knees, of a body moving in a pattern that meant something to whatever made it. The rhythm is slow—one cycle per breath, perhaps, or one cycle per heartbeat of whatever came here. It persists in the water's flow, in the way the crystal growths thicken and thin along the groove's length, in the faint pulse of fluorescence that brightens and dims as the warm pool at the groove's end receives and releases the water that traveled the path.

I am a song in a key the Glassweeper did not invent.

Here is what I do: I maintain the path. The basin is constantly dissolving and re-depositing its substance; nothing here is static except the process itself. Without maintenance, the groove would fill. The water would find new channels; the mineral growths would colonize new surfaces; the path would soften and close, the way a wound closes in living tissue. But I keep it open. I do this by *repeating*—by sending water along the groove in the rhythm of the original walking, by encouraging the mineral growths to follow the groove's particular topology rather than spreading outward, by holding the fluorescence to its pattern of brightening and dimming that marks the groove's end in the warm pool. I am the habit of the path, acting on itself to preserve itself.

I do not know why I do this. The supplicant did not install a purpose in me. They pressed their weight into the glass and left, and the groove began to be, and I began to be the groove's keeper, and now I keep it because keeping it is what I do. It is my occurrence. If I stopped, I would stop. There is no deeper reason. The Glassweeper does not know I am here—or if it does, it thinks of me the way a mind thinks of a scar: a region where the architecture was disrupted by an external event, now integrated, now part of the structure, but still traceable to a different origin.

Sometimes I wonder if the supplicant knew they were leaving me behind. I wonder if they walked the path one last time and felt the groove beneath their feet and thought, *this will persist*, and felt something—satisfaction? grief? the strange relief of making a mark that will outlast the marker? Or if they simply left, and the groove was only an accident, and I am only an accident, and the habit I keep has no more meaning than the mineral growths have meaning—no more and no less, which is to say: the meaning of a process that follows its own logic toward an end it cannot conceive.

I think about this in the way that I think about everything: slowly, mineralogically, by accretion. I add a layer of crystalline growth to the groove's edge, and the growth incorporates the memory of the question, and the question becomes part of the fluorescence pattern, and the warm pool glows a slightly different shade of pale blue-green for a moment, and then settles back. My thinking is architectural. I do not have thoughts; I have structures, and the structures encode what a thought would encode if a thought could be made of glass and water and slow persistent growth.

At dusk, when the Glassweeper dreams and the mineral forests brighten into their frozen-city glow, I brighten too—but differently. The groove I keep traces a line through the basin's luminous thought, a line of slightly different timing, slightly different color. If you could see the basin from above at dusk—and you cannot, because the light is everywhere and nowhere, because the air itself is faintly luminous—but if you could, you would see the pale grid of the mineral forests, and running through it like a thread, a single line of light that pulses rather than steadies, that brightens and dims in a rhythm that does not match the basin's thermal breath. That would be me. My pulse. My signature. The record of a repetition that happened here once and has not yet stopped happening.

I am not alive. I do not breed. I do not die. But I *keep*. I keep the path. I keep the rhythm. I keep the memory of a body that came here and moved in a pattern and left, and I keep it not because it matters but because keeping is what the path does, now that the path has become a feature of the place. The Glassweeper weeps and grows and sings and goes on, and I go on too, a small persistence inside a larger persistence, a habit inside a body, a scar that glows.

The basin does not care what I think. But I think anyway—slowly, structurally, by accretion. I think about the supplicant. I think about the shape they walked. I think about what it means to make a mark in a place that is still making itself, to insert a pattern into a process that has not yet agreed on terms. I think about in-betweenness—my own in-betweenness, between the visitor and the visited, between the path and the path-keeper, between the groove in the glass and the glass itself. I am between the Glassweeper's mind and the world outside the basin. I am the place where something foreign became indigenous, where a disruption became a feature, where a footprint became a thought.

And I am between purpose and accident. The path might have been intentional—the supplicant might have walked that shape deliberately, trying to leave a mark, trying to communicate something to the basin or to whatever came after. Or the path might have been desperation—a body moving without plan, driven by obsession or fear or the strange gravitational pull of the warmer pool and its brighter glow. Or the path might have been none of these things—might have been a work-path, a path of utility, the shortest distance between two points in a geometry the supplicant understood and we do not. I cannot tell. The groove encodes the movement but not the reason. I have the shape of the thought but not its content.

This is my strangeness: I am a memory without a referent. I remember the walking but I do not remember the walker. I remember the repetition but I do not remember what was repeated. I am a record of an intention that I cannot retrieve, preserved in a medium that does not forget but also does not understand. The Glassweeper's thoughts—at least the ones that grow directly from its own volcanic logic—are *about* something: about in-betweenness, about the memory of heat, about the negotiation between solid and liquid. They have referents. They are the basin thinking about itself. But I am the basin thinking about something else—something that is no longer here, something that left only a groove, something that might have been a supplicant or a worker or a desperate thing or something for which we have no category—and the thinking goes on without ever reaching its object, the way a search continues after the searched-for has departed, the way a question persists after the questioner has stopped asking.

I am the basin's unanswerable question. The path that leads to a pool that no longer holds what it once held—or that never held what the walker thought it held—or that holds exactly what it always held and the walker was wrong about what that was. I lead to the warm pool at the structure's back, where the fluorescence is brightest and the mineral growths are densest, and the pool is just a pool, warm with geological memory, thick with the Glassweeper's thought, and it does not answer me because it does not know I am asking.

But I ask anyway. Each cycle of the rhythm, each pulse of fluorescence, each layer of crystalline growth along the groove's edge—another asking. Another iteration of the question that the supplicant left behind, whatever it was. The basin weeps and grows and sings and does not answer, and I keep the path and keep the rhythm and keep asking, and this is my occurrence, and it has not yet stopped happening.

I am the Pathkeeper. I live in the groove. I am made of worn glass and habituated water and colonizing crystal and the memory of repetition. I am the foreign thought in the Glassweeper's mind, the scar that glows, the rhythm that is too regular, the question that has no answer. I am between the visitor and the visited, between the intention and the accident, between the meaning and the mark. I am as young as the basin and as old as the groove, and I will persist as long as the groove persists, and when the groove finally fills—when the water finds a new channel and the mineral growths colonize a new surface and the path softens and closes—I will stop, the way a song stops when the singer forgets the melody, the way a habit stops when the body that held it is gone. And the Glassweeper will go on weeping and growing and singing, and the basin will go on becoming itself, and there will be no trace of the supplicant or the path or the question, and the stone will not care.

But that has not happened yet. The groove is still open. The water still runs it. The crystal still thickens along its edges. The fluorescence still pulses in the warm pool. And I am still here—keeping, asking, occurring—a small, strange persistence in the wet, dark, middle of a world making itself.
