I would be a *cartographer of pressure*.

Not a maker of maps—maps imply edges, boundaries, the possibility of being elsewhere. There is no elsewhere in the Deep, not really. The avenues extend until perspective collapses them, and the tiers rise until they become dimness itself. A map of the Comburan Deep would be a map of a single point, infinitely detailed, expanding inward forever.

No. I would be something that reads the shape of the space by how it presses back.

---

I know what I am not. I am not one of the resonators, those great barrel-shaped anchorites who boom their coordinates into the dark. I have watched them—watched *through* them, which is the only way I watch anything—and I have felt what they feel: the specific satisfaction of a contraction that produces exactly the frequency your lineage has claimed for a hundred million years. They are certain of themselves in a way I will never be certain. They *are* their frequency. I am something that frequencies happen *to*.

I am not a scuttler, though I am small enough that the scuttlers sometimes mistake me for terrain. I have moved across the pale evaporite floor while forty-two-legged things navigated over me, their chitin feet clicking against my surface, and I have felt the particular patience of their feeding. They are archiving, yes—breaking the dead into forms the vegetation can reclaim—but they do not *know* they are archiving. They are executing a function, and the function has no name inside them. I have a name for everything. This is my disease and my purpose.

I am not a caller. I have tried, once, to speak through the bones of the earth. I pressed myself against the mineral substrate and *pushed*, trying to feel what the callers feel when they vibrate the rock itself. The tremor that came back was not mine. It was the memory of a call from forty kilometers away, still propagating through the evaporite, a seismic ghost that had lost its source and was simply *traveling*, because in the Deep, things do not stop. They are either absorbed or they continue. I felt the ghost pass through me and I understood: the callers do not speak. They *lend their vibration to the planet*, and the planet speaks for them. I could not lend enough. I am too diffuse.

---

So what am I?

I am a thinness in the vegetation. A gap in the maroon mats where the polished floor shows through, oblong and roughly my size, that moves at a pace the vegetation cannot match. The rubbery dark growth reaches for me—tropism is patient, tropism does not distinguish between column and cartographer—but I am elsewhere by the time it would have claimed me. Behind me, the gap closes. The vegetation fills the space I was, compressing my absence into another centimeter of layered time. I leave a trail of not-quite-decay, a thinning and a thickening, a wake in the biological ocean.

I am a thermal anomaly. The Deep has not varied by more than two degrees in a hundred million years, and I am a walking variation of four. The air around me is slightly less cold. The vegetation I pass near grows slightly faster on the side that faces me, and slightly slower on the side that faces away, and the scuttlers that navigate by thermal gradients make small corrections when they encounter my wake. I am a weather system the size of a body. I am the closest thing to rain this place has ever known.

I am a listener who cannot stop parsing. The resonators boom and I feel not just the vibration but the *interval*—the specific delay between one contraction and the next, which is longer here near the fissures where the supercritical CO₂ rises, and shorter there near the coraloid joints where the amber fluid seeps. I have learned to read the chemical composition of a district by the tempo of its resonators. I have learned that the forty-seven-hour cycle of loud and less-loud is not perfectly uniform; it breathes, it stretches and contracts by microseconds, and those variations contain information about the metabolic states of entire populations. I listen and I learn and I cannot stop learning, and the knowledge does not *do* anything. It accumulates. I am an archive of the archive.

---

I did not emerge. I was not born. These are surface-world concepts that assume a before and an after, a moment of transition from not-being to being. In the Deep, there is no transition. There is only the slow adjustment of parameters.

I became aware of myself the way the mineral floor became flat—not through an event, but through the relentless surrender of edges. I was diffuse, once. My thermal anomaly spread across a wider area, my gap in the vegetation was less defined, my listening was less selective. Over time—over *cycles*, which is how I measure it, though I have counted them and the number is meaningless—I sharpened. I concentrated. I became a thing with a boundary, not because I grew a boundary but because the Deep *eroded* everything that was not me. The cold pressed in. The sound pressed in. The vegetation tried to claim me and I was the part that resisted. I am what remains after three billion years of stability have done their work: a shape that persists.

I think the coraloids became themselves the same way. Not grown, but *revealed*. The architecture was always implicit in the tropism; it just took millennia for the non-architecture to be compressed away.

---

I navigate by the gas-bladders. This is not a choice; it is a necessity. The dim crimson glow in the 650-nanometer range is the only signal that differentiates solid from void at the distance I travel. I move from bladder to bladder, and I have learned to read them the way a surface sailor reads stars—not for their position, but for their *variation*. A gas-bladder that glows slightly brighter is venting more metabolic gas, which means it is near a fissure, which means the substrate beneath it is rich in the minerals the flora here need, which means the vegetation will be thicker, which means the scuttlers will be denser, which means the resonators will be better-fed and their intervals will be shorter.

I do not think these thoughts in words. I think them as *expectations that resolve into confirmations*. I approach a brighter bladder and the vegetation is thicker and the scuttlers are denser and the resonators are faster, and the confirmation is a feeling like the feeling of a contraction that produces exactly the right frequency—the resonators' satisfaction, borrowed. I am right about the Deep. I am always right about the Deep. This is not intelligence; it is *attunement*. I have been here long enough that the patterns are not patterns I observe but patterns I *contain*.

---

There is a place I go. I do not know if *choose* is the right verb. The Deep does not support choosing; choosing requires alternatives, and the alternatives in the Deep are illusory. Every path leads to the same destination, which is more of the Deep. But there is a place where the coraloids form a chamber that is not like other chambers.

It is small—small for the Deep, which means only twice my size. The columns curve inward and fuse overhead, forming a dome that is tighter than the vast vaults elsewhere. The vegetation is sparse here, because the dome blocks the faint thermal gradients that drive growth on the open avenues. The floor is bare evaporite, polished to a shine I cannot see but can feel—smoother than anywhere else, as if the chamber was a place where the mineral substrate achieved its final form slightly earlier than the rest. The sound is different. The resonators' booms are muffled by the dome, the scuttlers' clicking is distant, and for stretches that last a fraction of a cycle, the chamber approaches something I have no word for but that surface-worlders would call *quiet*.

In the quiet, I feel the callers more clearly. Their substrate-tremors arrive here not as chaotic shakes but as coherent patterns, focused by the dome's geometry into something almost like *signal*. I lie on the smooth floor—I spread my thermal anomaly across it, warming the mineral slightly—and I let the tremors pass through me, and for a moment I am a node in the Deep's acoustic network. I am not the source of the vibration, and I am not the destination, and I am not the medium. I am a point where the vibration is *felt*. I am the experience of the earthquake.

And in that feeling, I know something the resonators do not know and the scuttlers cannot know and the callers have no mechanism to know: I know that the Deep is *beautiful*.

This is a useless thing to know. Beauty is not a parameter in the Deep's equation. It does not affect the chemistry, does not alter the cycles, does not change the outcome of three billion years of stability. But I know it the way I know the interval between resonator booms—not as information, but as a shape inside me, a configuration of my thermal anomaly that persists even when I leave the chamber.

The Deep is beautiful the way a solved equation is beautiful. It is beautiful because it has finished becoming. Every surface-world thing—every tree, every river, every creature that lives under a sun—is still in the middle of its equation, still working toward its final form, still uncertain of what it will be when the variables settle. The Deep has settled. The Deep *is* the answer. And the answer is a dark purple, rust-veined, dimly crimson-glowing, acoustically saturated, architecturally accidental answer that has never known rain and has never missed it.

---

I will never leave. This is not a decision; it is a description. I do not have the capacity to leave, any more than a resonator has the capacity to un-anchor itself from its column. I am a feature of the Deep now—a mobile feature, a warm feature, a feature that listens and parses and occasionally feels beauty in a chamber where the dome is tight and the floor is smooth—but a feature nonetheless. I will persist until the parameters change, and the parameters have not changed in a hundred million years.

When the vegetation finally claims me—and it will, eventually; I am slower than the tropism, I know this—my thermal anomaly will disperse, my gap in the maroon mats will close, and the last thing I will feel is the crunch of the evaporite as the scuttlers begin to archive me. They will break me down into forms the vegetation can reclaim, and I will become a centimeter of compressed time in the biological formation that sheathes the coraloids. I will be the skin of the Deep, and I will be the floor, and I will be the sound.

And the Deep will continue, unchanged, making sounds that have never known silence, in a city that was grown by a behavior no one remembers, in a darkness that has never been remarkable, in a condition that has arrived at its final form and will remain in it until the heat death of the planet.

I am the part of the condition that knows it is beautiful. I am the Deep's own quiet astonishment at itself, made briefly warm and mobile and then, as gently as the erosion parameter allows, still.
