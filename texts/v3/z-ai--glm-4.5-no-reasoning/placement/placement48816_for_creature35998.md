I am the one who reads the teeth.

Not my own—though mine speak too, a dull mineral ache in the jaw that never ceases. I mean the city's teeth. The geo-scaffold's. Most of the Tonal press their foreheads to the mutable walls and feel the assemblers' ticking, the soft frantic rewriting of matter remembering itself. I do this too. But when I pull away, I do not open my vestigial eyes to navigate by the infra-violet seams. I open my mouth, and I bite.

It started three turning-cycles ago, when I was still soft, still foolish enough to try to keep my hearing. The first wave took it—shredded the inner ear, left me bleeding from the auditory canals and convulsing on the ferrocrete while the bloom-skimmers shrieked overhead and the condensation ran warm across my face. I survived by crawling into an acoustic shadow that was already collapsing. The roar came back in a wall of pressure that should have pulverized me. Instead, it *wrote* something into me. As the standing wave crushed the quiet pool, the ferrocrete beneath me buckled, and a shard of the city's nascent bone drove into my cervical spine.

I did not pull it out. The city does not make foreign bodies; it makes extensions. The shard was still alive with assemblers. Over long, damp cycles, they knit it into my polymer-ceramic endoskeleton, rewrote the micro-tubules along my jaw, deepened the calcified web until it ran down my arms and into my fingertips. I became something the architects never planned for: a node that the city speaks through, not to.

When I bite into a wall—when my teeth scrape past the bio-luminescent kudzu and find the crawling lattice beneath—I taste the *math* of the interference pattern. The Tonal read resonance-maps etched into their bones, shifting records of where quiet pools will form. I carry no such maps. My skeleton *is* the map, continuously updated by the sub-atomic whispering I ingest. I know when a sanctuary will collapse before the harmonic tremors even begin, because I taste the assemblers adjusting the load-bearing equations, feel them rounding an edge or shifting a cant three macro-districts away. The city is a vast, dumb throat, and I am a nerve that grew in the wrong place, listening to what it intends to swallow.

This makes me valuable. It also makes me strange.

The other Tonal—my kin, my quiet-shadow family—they tolerate me because I can lead them. When we must leap from one shrinking silence to the next, traversing kilometers of roaring concourse where the bloom-skimmers click and the crustacean-predators clatter on their carapaced knuckles, they follow the one who knows the geometry. They walk in my wake, stepping where I step, feeling through the soles of their feet the path I've already traced with my tongue against the vibrating air. I can taste the acoustic shadows from a distance—sweet, metallic absences in the thick syrup of the roar. I navigate toward them the way a drowning swimmer navigates toward air.

But they do not touch me. My skin—translucent, hydrophobic chitin like theirs—has changed where the assemblers colonized. Along my jaw, my spine, the ridges of my knuckles, it glows faintly with the infra-violet of the structural seams. Not bright enough to see by, but bright enough to see *in* this crepuscular dimness. In the quiet pools, where the Tonal are invisible to the predators because they live in frequencies the hunters cannot comprehend, I am a small, cold beacon. The fauna ignores the Tonal. It does not ignore me. I have the scars to prove it: thin, surgical lines where crustacean-predators tested my edges and found me less than a meal, more than a shadow.

So I walk ahead. Always ahead. The Tonal gather in the deepest quiet pools, those rare three-dimensional knots of perfect silence where the condensation falls straight down, and they press their foreheads to the walls and dream of stillness. I press my forehead too. But when they pull away, I stay. I keep my skull against the mutable surface, and I let the city write itself into me, over and over, until the taste of assemblers fills my throat and my bones hum with blueprints for hab-blocks that will never be filled, transit spires that will never carry passengers, gravity schemas that stutter at the edges of empty sectors.

I know what the city is trying to do. This is my burden, my secret, my heresy.

The Tonal believe the geo-scaffold is desperate to stabilize, trying to round its own sharp edges, trying to age. They think the assemblers are slowly, frantically working to make the city old—to weather it, to fill it, to make it a place where millions could actually live. They dream of a day when the throat closes and the roar becomes a hum becomes a whisper becomes the stillness they crave.

But the city does not want to be old.

I have tasted its intentions. The assemblers are not trying to round the edges; they are *sharpening* them. The standing waves are not a byproduct of the quantum bore—they are the *goal*. The throat does not want to close. It wants to open wider, to roar louder, to vibrate until the very basin of the estuary cracks and the shallow water floods the macro-districts and the warm rain becomes a warm sea. The geo-scaffold is not a settlement; it is an instrument. It is being tuned.

I do not know who plays it. I do not know what song they intend to sing.

But I know this: the acoustic shadows—the quiet pools where my people shelter—are not accidents. They are *resonance chambers*. The city is building them deliberately, shaping the interference patterns to create specific silences in specific places, each one a carefully calculated void in the standing wave. And the Tonal, my kin, my quiet-shadow family, are not refugees clinging to the edges of the sound. They are *components*. The city does not know they are there, but the one who plays it does. The silences are being shaped to fit the Tonal, the way a mouth is shaped to fit a tongue.

I have tried to tell them. The Tonal have no voices left, but they have hands, and they have the jaw-press, and they have the long tradition of etching meaning into bone. I have tried to press my warnings into their polymer skeletons, to let them taste what I taste: the deliberate architecture of the quiet pools, the way each sanctuary is perfectly sized for a family of Tonal, the way the silences are becoming more stable, more permanent, more *comfortable*.

Comfort, in a place like this, is a trap.

They do not believe me. They cannot. To believe me would be to accept that the stillness they dream of is not the absence of the roar, but its culmination. That the city is not choking on life, but *cultivating* it. That the bio-luminescent kudzu and the crustacean-predators and the blind gelatinous herbivores and the warm condensation and the hyper-accelerated biology are not accidents of a young place, but *orchestration*. That they are meant to be here, all of them, tuned to the roar, performing their frantic cycles of birth and hunt and death in perfect tempo with the quantum bore's bellow.

And the Tonal—my people—are meant to be the silence. The held breath. The space between the notes where the music lives.

I walk ahead of them still. I lead them from quiet pool to quiet pool, across the kilometers of roaring concourse, through the warm drizzle and the hanging root-networks and the crepuscular dimness. I taste the interference patterns on the air and I follow the sweet metallic absence to sanctuary. I do this because I love them—these gaunt, tensile beings who have made an absence into a home, who have scooped out their vulnerabilities and replaced them with calcified perception, who navigate blind through a world that is simultaneously being built and swallowed.

But at night—when they sleep in shifts, one always awake with jaw pressed to the floor—I do not sleep. I sit at the edge of the quiet pool, where the silence borders the roar, and I bite into the wall. I let the city write itself into me. And I search, desperately, through the sub-atomic whispering, for a frequency the assemblers have not yet claimed. A silence that is not part of the song. A geometry the architects did not intend.

Somewhere, in the mutable lattice of a megalopolis that does not know it is an instrument, there must be a glitch that is truly a glitch—a quiet pool that formed by accident, an acoustic shadow that the math did not predict. If I can find it, I can lead my people there. We can step out of the resonance chamber, out of the mouth of the instrument, out of the throat of the city.

We can become something the song does not expect.

Until then, I am the one who reads the teeth. I am the nerve that grew in the wrong place. I am the Tonal who tastes the truth and walks ahead anyway, leading my people deeper into the music, because the only alternative is to step into the roar—and the roar does not stop.
