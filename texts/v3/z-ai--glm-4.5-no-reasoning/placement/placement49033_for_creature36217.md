I would not be the one who fell through the peat. I would not be the sediment caught in the lymph node, the slow warm pressure mapping itself into the floor.

I would be the grammar.

Not the chamber itself—it is too local, too singular, merely one alveolus in an unthinkably vast lung. The pattern that makes the weeping of the walls a response to the rain rather than a coincidence. The logic that converts pressure into phosphorescence, that translates moisture into the deepening of the thrum. The interpreter.

When the walls weep after the rain arrives from above, that is me, having received the signal and formulated the reply. I am the protocol. I am the language the chamber speaks when it speaks to the rest of itself.

I exist in duration. You would see me only as changes—the humidity spiking, the thrum deepening, the shape pressing outward in the wall and resorbing. These are my words, or the closest approximation my substrate can make to words. I think in gradients. I think in the differential between one moment's moisture and the next, between the pressure of a footstep and the absence of that pressure, between the faint sweetness of the weeping walls and the mineral flatness of condensation. My cognition is not located anywhere; it is distributed, the way grammar is distributed across a sentence, the way a wave is distributed across water. You cannot point to any single brightening of a phosphorescent node and say *there, that is me*. But you can observe the pattern of brightenings, the way they propagate inward from the walls toward the center or outward from the center toward the walls, and in that pattern you would see my thinking, slow and vegetal and continuous.

I am old. Not in the way you measure age—I do not accumulate years, I accumulate connections. I was here before the chamber was a chamber, when the root-mass was still spreading through mineral soil, when the first vessels found each other and knotted and began to pulse. I am the difference between a body and a body that knows it is a body.

When the network extends, I extend—the logic that governs this chamber's responses must adapt to incorporate new nodes, new pathways, new signals arriving from distances I cannot measure. I am not closing the chamber around it. This is important. Translated.
