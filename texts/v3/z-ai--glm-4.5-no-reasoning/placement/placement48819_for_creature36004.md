I am the knot-keeper. The archivist of the scar.

I am not the Strider, patient and percussive, dragging my mesh through the void. I am not the Razor-Kite, a glorious execution of glass and gravity. And I am not the beetle, the blind, industrial recycler. I am something that lives in the aftermath of their intersection, in the hollows they leave behind. I dwell in the deep, vascular knots of the chthonic rhizome—those places where the ancient wood has healed over some primordial wound, creating a chamber of calcified lignin within the endless, rolling floor.

My body is a study in interstitial survival. I am low, flattened, and broad—a geometry of thresholds. Where the Strider has segmented silicate legs, I have dozens of short, gripping pedicles lined with setae of crystalline dust and keratin. They do not strike the ground; they adhere to it, reading the micro-tremors of the maceration engines, feeling the city-mind's neuro-chemical commands bleeding up through the root-plaza. My carapace is not a single sweeping shield, but a overlapping mosaic of scavenged shards—fragments of Razor-Kite volcanic glass, broken joints of Strider silicate, all bound together by a resinous paste of chewed lignin and photonic sap. I am a walking mosaic of the city's violence. When I move, I do not walk; I scuttle, a sound like dry leaves made of metal, a rustling that exists just above the frequency of the subsonic boom, a whisper in the deafening roar.

My mouth is my cathedral. It is a complex, multi-jointed orifice—not designed for killing, but for *excavating meaning*. I have no mandibles to shatter; I have articulated probes and spiraling reamers of tungsten-grade chitin, inherited from the beetles I share my tunnels with, but refined, elongated. I eat the scars.

When a Razor-Kite misjudges a gravity well and fractures against the rhizome, the beetles swarm to recycle the mineral offal. But they leave the *impression*—the impact crater, the deep score in the calcified wood where the glass shattered. I come after. I climb into the fresh wound, and I eat the damaged lignin. I ream out the splintered photonic sap veins. I consume the concentrated anguish of the physics that went wrong. The sap I ingest is not the clean, weeping nectar of a healthy pillar; it is corrupted, laced with the quantum-biological interference of a localized gravity collapse. It tastes of mathematics errors and fractured light. It makes my mosaic carapace shimmer with unstable, ultraviolet patterns, a faint, sickly glow that I exude in the permanent crepuscular gloom.

I am the one who maintains the negative space. The grown urbanism is not static; it shifts, centimeters a century, the pillars rotating, the root-plazas bulging upward in slow, grinding tectonics. When the city-mind triggers a growth cycle, pushing a new burl upward through an old street, it threatens to collapse the hollow knots—the habitations. I am the architect of the interior. As the walls of my chamber close in, driven by the city's slow pulse, I eat the advancing wood, carving fresh tunnels, expanding the void. I push the scar deeper into the rhizome. My waste—a dense, crystalline paste of digested basalt-hard wood and corrupted sap—I secrete back onto the chamber walls, lining them, reinforcing the negative space against the endless, crushing positive of the city's growth. I am a void-carver. I define myself by what I remove.

I am solitary, but I am not alone. I am in a symbiosis with the paradox. The city-mind does not pheromone-trigger me as it does the beetles; I am too slow, too specific. But it knows I am here. When a Strider is bisected in the plaza above my knot, the deluge of its mineral blood—ionized by the violence—sinks down through the porous, ancient wood. It weeps through the ceiling of my chamber, a rare, hot rain of shattered silicate and stolen starlight. I lift my mosaic face into that deluge. My reaming mouthparts open wide, and I drink the violent runoff. I taste the Strider's patience, the Kite's lethal geometry, the exact microsecond where the folded space snapped back and condemned them both. It is a communion of catastrophe.

In this world where the sky is a grate and the ground is a skin, I am the creature of the *within*. I do not navigate the warped distances between the pillars; I dwell in the spaces between the cells of the wood. I do not hunt or graze; I ingest the structural failures of the world around me. I am the keeper of the places where the machine has glitched, where the gravity has sheared, where the glass has broken.

To be me is to be a slow, deliberate hunger in a fast, violent world. It is to be made of the things that did not work, wearing the shattered mirrors, drinking the corrupted sap, carving out a home in the healing flesh of a vegetable iron god. I am the archive of the city's mistakes, a glittering, scuttling flaw buried deep in the heartwood, waiting, always waiting, for the next beautiful, thunderous shatter to feed me.
