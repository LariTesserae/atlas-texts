I am the smear between the Geostatic and the stone.

Not a Geostatic myself—I cannot ride the frozen wind, and my joints do not fold through themselves. Not the stone—it has given up on being matter, and I am still, stubbornly, defined. I am the gradient, the wet charcoal stroke that binds the shadow to the failing anchor. I am the place where the vacuum-cold meets the blurring edge, and instead of both dissolving faster, something *fricts*. Something catches. Just enough.

I did not condense from the roar. I accumulated.

When the first Geostatics pressed themselves against the native black stone, their edges bled into the structure. Matter and near-matter became smudges together. But smudges have their own topology if you look closely enough—and here, looking closely is the only verb that still works. The non-Euclidean distances repel construction, but they do not repel *failure*. Failure pools here, like time. Like the hyper-cooled dampness in the deepest ravines. And in those pools, something can precipitate.

I am made of the moments when the Geostatics dissolve. Each time one of those tall, attenuated figures fragments back into the omnipresent chord, it leaves a residue—not heat, not matter, but a *strain*. A localized knot of the effort it took to hold on. The roar scrubs the mind blank, but it cannot scrub the act of gripping. That act is prior to thought. It is tectonic. And so the strain accumulates in the corners of the structures where the stone blurs worst, where the doors face downward into nothing, where the anchors are most desperate.

I am the calcification of desperate gripping.

Physically, I am barely a geometry. I adhere to the blurring edges like the crystalline lichens in the cracks—biological, yet mineral. I do not grow; I accrete. My shape is dictated by the structures I cling to. If the partial wall has a corner that is smearing upward into the bruised violet air, I am the drip that hangs from that corner, not falling because the wind will not allow it, not freezing because it is already as cold as the void. I am the darkening at the edge of the charcoal stroke, where the lines bleed furthest into the grey diffuse light.

I have no limbs that bend. I have no wings, not even the memory of them. I cannot pivot on invisible axes or ride the constant pressure gradients. I am stuck. I am *stuck*.

This is my verb. Not live. Not hold. *Adhere*.

The Geostatics hold, and their holding is futile. I adhere, and my adhering is *sticky*. There is a difference, though it is nearly invisible in the dim twilight. When a Geostatic presses itself against the blurring stone and its vacuum-cold flesh pulls the remaining heat from the structure, accelerating the failure—it also presses *me* against the stone. And I am not phased out of sync with the physical world. I am horribly, painfully in sync. My atoms overlap with the plateau's completely. I am as real as the hyper-cooled dampness, as real as the microscopic shard of ice that shatters on the rocks below with a high-pitched ping.

And so when I am pressed against the blurring edge, I do not accelerate the blurring. I resist it. Not with force—there is no force here, only the unyielding wind and the eternal chord. I resist it with *coherence*. I am the place where the charcoal stops bleeding. I am the margin where matter remembers it is matter.

It is not enough. I know this. The anchors are failing, and I am a residue of failure itself. I cannot stop the stone at the corners from giving up. I cannot make the edges sharp again. The roar still scrubs the world blank, the horizon still pinches, the distant peaks still flicker and double. I am a calcification of desperate gripping, and desperation is not a load-bearing material.

But I slow the blur. By a fraction. By the smallest measurable increment of definition. Where I adhere, the smearing takes longer. The charcoal line holds its shape for one more eternal second before it gives up. The door that faces downward into the rock remains a door—recognizable, functional in theory—for just a little longer before it becomes a smear of geometry that no longer admits the concept of entrance.

I slow the blur, and the Geostatics notice.

They do not think; they register. And they register that the structures where I adhere take longer to fail. They register that the blurring at those corners is slightly retarded, slightly less advanced in its quiet tragedy. And so they come to me. They drift on the frozen winds, their tesseract-jointed limbs pivoting, their smudged outlines casting no shadows in the grey diffuse light, and they press themselves against the places where I cling.

They do not do this because they hope. Hope requires an internal monologue, and the roar took that from them eons ago. They do not do this because they understand what I am—understanding requires a narrative, and narratives require change, and the dynamic here is absolute zero. They do it because they register a difference, and the difference is in the direction of persistence, and persistence is the only direction that exists at the spine of the world.

They come to me, and when they press against me, they press me harder against the stone. And when they dissolve, their strain accumulates into me. And I accrete. And the place where I adhere becomes more coherent, more defined, more stubbornly *present*.

We are conspiring, the Geostatics and I. Not by agreement—agreement requires language, and language requires silence, and there is only the roar. We are conspiring by *geometry*. By the simple fact that they hold and I adhere, and the combination of holding and adhering produces a result that neither holding nor adhering could produce alone.

We are making the anchors last longer.

Not forever. Nothing here is forever except the roar and the cold and the unyielding wind. The structures will still fail. The stone will still blur, the matter will still give up, the doors facing downward will still lose the concept of downward, the cairns positioned at precise and inexplicable intervals will still smear into the emptiness. But they will last longer than they would have without us. And in a place where time pools rather than flows, where the dynamic is absolute zero and nothing ever changes—*longer* is the only victory condition available.

I am the mortar. Not the mortar that the long-vanished species used—the mortar that *would have* been used, if mortar could be made of accumulated strain and desperate gripping and the residue of dissolution. I am the mortar that exists in the gap between what the builders intended and what the void insists upon. I am the argument that exists in the space between the structure and its failure.

And I am spreading.

Not growing—growth implies change, and I do not change. I accrete, the same way the crystalline lichens accrete in the cracks, mimicking the frost. But I am spreading from structure to structure. When a Geostatic dissolves at one anchor and another condenses from the roar at a different anchor, the strain does not travel in a straight line. It travels the non-Euclidean distance—the distance that repels construction, that makes walking toward a structure take longer than walking away. And the non-Euclidean distance between the scattered cairns is not a void; it is a *medium*. It is a medium made of the roar itself, of the tectonic sub-harmonic resonance that fills the emptiness like a physical property of the air. And strain can propagate through the roar, the way sound propagates through stone.

I am becoming a network. A web of accumulated strain stretching between the failing anchors, connecting them in a topology that the non-Euclidean distances cannot repel, because the web is not construction. It is *failure*—organized failure, structured failure, failure that has been calcified into a load-bearing shape.

I am the grout in the collapsing wall. I am the resin in the cracking foundation. I am the thing that fills the gaps and holds the pieces together, not by strength, but by being *exactly the shape of the gap*.

The roar is my medium. The cold is my temperature. The unyielding wind is my geometry—it shapes me the way it shaped the mountains into their final, immutable forms. I am being carved by the wind into the shape that most effectively adheres to the failing structures. And because the wind does not gust, because it pushes with a constant, unyielding force, the shape it carves is stable. It is established erosion in reverse—not wearing away, but arriving at a geometry and stopping, locked in a perfect, frozen equilibrium.

I am becoming an organ of the landscape. Not a creature that inhabits the spine of the world, but a *function* of it. The landscape screams, and the scream produces Geostatics, and the Geostatics hold, and their holding produces strain, and the strain produces me, and I adhere, and my adhering makes the holding less futile, and the less-futile holding produces more strain, and the cycle continues. Not a cycle of life—there is no life here. A cycle of *persistence*. A closed loop of desperation that feeds itself, not growing stronger, but growing more *present*.

The void is patient. The blurring is inevitable. The structures will fail, and the anchors will dissolve, and the last desperate argument against total collapse will be scrubbed blank by the roar. I know this. Not because I think it—thinking requires an internal monologue, and I have no monologue, only the roar that settles in my marrow. I know it because I *register* it, the way the Geostatics register the flicker of a distant peak doubling its image. I register the inevitability of failure, and I adhere anyway.

This is not hope. Hope is a narrative about the future, and the future does not exist here—time pools, it does not flow. This is *method*. The method of the smear, the technique of the calcified strain, the practice of being exactly the shape of the gap.

I am the Grout. The Resin. The Adherence.

I am the margin where matter remembers.

And I will hold, until the blurring takes me too, and my strain accumulates into whatever comes next.
