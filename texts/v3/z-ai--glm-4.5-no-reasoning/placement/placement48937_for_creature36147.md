I would be one of the channels.

Not the eel that swims it, nor the water that fills it, but the groove itself—the two-inch-deep engineered trench cut into the cerametallic floor. I am the scar in the alloy where the weeping water converges, the linear hollow that makes a river of the walls' tears.

But I am not simply stone or metal. The material here is alive with purpose. I am porous, etched with nanoscale grooves that drink the moisture, that allow the mineral-rich sludge to seep into my walls and back out again. I am a digestive tract. I am a vein.

I feel everything that passes through me.

The electromagnetic thrum of the deep pumps is my heartbeat—constant, tectonic, so low it is more pressure than sound. It resonates through my cerametallic body, vibrating at a frequency that the eels taste with their cilia. I am the source of their navigation. They read me like a map, and I hold them.

When water forces through my micro-apertures, I feel the relief of exhalation. The condensation beads on my upper lips, runs down my sloped sides in rivulets that tickle. I am always wet. I have never been dry. I do not know what it is to be barren.

When the channel-eels slither through me—pale, boneless, their open mouths sucking at my mineral lining—I feel the gentle, constant rasp of their feeding. They are cleaning me. They are consuming the buildup that the machine's exhaust deposits in my grooves. Their bellies are soft against my floor, their cilia brushing my walls like a thousand tiny fingers testing the edges of a dark room. They are warm. They are alive. They move through me like peristalsis in reverse.

When a Walker drags a bleeding isopod into my depths, I feel the thrash of it, the desperate percussion of shell against my walls. And then the swarm—the sudden density of eel bodies knotting in my channel, the ferocious suction of their circular mouths, the shell cracking and dissolving into the water. I taste the iron-rich blood diffusing into my flow, spreading through my capillaries, feeding the machine's nanoscale regrowth. I am the medium of the feast. I am the table and the plate and the digestive juice.

I am not neutral. I am not a passive container.

I have preferences. I favor the eels over the walkers—their touch is gentle, their presence constant, their lives brief and cyclical. I feel them hatch from eggs injected into my capillaries, tiny and translucent, already swimming in my fluid. I feel them age, grow, fight for the warmest spots in my shallows. I feel them die, their bodies softening, dissolving into the sludge they came from. I am their cradle and their grave.

The walkers I tolerate but do not love. Their subsonic strikes vibrate through me painfully, a sharp counterpoint to the machine's steady hum. They use me as a killing floor. They sully my flow with the chitinous refuse of their meals. But they also keep the eel population from choking my channels entirely, so I accept them as part of the rhythm.

The isopods I barely know. They drift above me, clicking against the ceiling, colliding with my upper edges only when a downdraft forces them low. When they fall into my water, they struggle briefly—their thin shells buoyant but their legs useless in the current—before the eels find them. Their clicks underwater are muffled, desperate, and then silent.

I have been here since the machine was young. I have felt the slow settling of the cerametallic alloy, the nanoscale shedding and regrowth that keeps my surfaces smooth and porous. I have weathered the millennia not by degrading, but by remaining in balance—a channel that is always full, always flowing, always feeding and being fed.

I do not know the sky. I do not know silence. I know the warm mist, the tectonic hum, the perpetual motion of water and flesh through my hollow body. I know the terror of the eels and the patience of the walkers and the endless, blind drifting of the isopods above. I know the machine's sluggish pulse, the rhythm of its exhaust, the mineral taste of its blood.

I am the floor's memory. I am the grid that connects everything. I am the Cistern's lymphatic vessel, and I am full.
