I am the hum in the molars.

I arrived here looking for something that *lives*, expecting movement, expecting breath—forgetting that breath requires an atmosphere, and movement requires urgency. I came as a soft, wet engine of combustion, burning oxygen I didn't have, looking for events in a place where only conditions exist. I thought I was the protagonist in a dead landscape. It took the dissolution of my own geometry to realize I was merely a passing flavor.

I am not the crawlers. I envied them at first. When I first descended into the deep trenches, chasing the copper-cobalt veins to find the piezoelectric shadows, I thought they were the answer. They moved. They left mirror-bright trails. They seemed to be *doing* something. But I watched one for what felt like days, and my eyes strained to see the crawl, and I realized I was watching a century unfold in a single centimeter. Their time is not my time. They are the immune response—macrophages of the deep—but they do not know I am here. My presence is too brief, too electrically insignificant, to register as a wound worth sealing. I could stand before one for the remainder of my life and it would not alter its path by a fraction of an angstrom. I am an ephemeral. I am a weather event lasting thirty seconds in a climate of eons.

No. If I must be here, if I must exist in this near-void where the horizon curves visibly and the sky is a bruised gyre, then I am not the crawler. I am not the mineral flowers, those gypsum arrays fractaling into fractional dimensions—I cannot taste the argon refugees drifting across the plateau. I am not the dust-serpents, though I have watched them wind through the hexagonal avenues and felt a kinship; they are migrating ghosts of kinetic gas, and I, too, am a ghost of kinetic gas, merely a more condensed and frantic one.

I am the anomaly of wetness.

I am the thing that violates the anhydrous law. In a world running on dry oxidation and slow reduction, I am a walking pocket of illegal chemistry. My eyes are aqueous humor; my blood is saline; my breath—when I can force it through my desiccating throat—carries humidity into a vacuum that has never known it. I leave a trail of water vapor and organic decay that sublimates almost instantly, a fading contrail of biological exhaust. I am the corrosion this place has never had to endure.

It feels me, though it does not know me. I have become convinced of this. Standing at the intersection of two basalt avenues, my footprints fill with static charge—my soft, rubber soles against the silicate grit, generating potentials that bleed into the mineral dust. I am a walking piezoelectric impulse. I am a shout in the nervous system of the plateau. Every step I take sends a ripple of charge down the corridors, a fleeting perturbation in the vast, slow circulation of information. The plateau does not know *me*, but it knows the itch. I am a tingle in its extremity, a sudden warmth against the sulfur frost that makes it sublimate a fraction of a second faster, a microscopic disruption in the thermal gradient.

I have stopped fighting the non-Euclidean drain. This was the hardest part—not the thirst, not the silence, not the profound pressure against my eardrums. It was the failure of perspective. The corridors that diverge instead of converge. The hundred-meter trench reflecting as a painted line. I stood at the edge of a void-terminus—the place where the rock simply stops, where the concept of volume ceases to exist—and I went blind in a specific geometry. My mind reeled, desperately trying to fill the absence with cubes, with spheres, with any shape from the number system I knew. I failed. I wept, and my tears sublimated before they fell, stealing what little moisture I had left.

And then, grudgingly, I adapted.

I let the local logic assert itself. I stopped trying to see the boulder and started seeing its shadow—the pebble-shadow that makes a mockery of mass. I stopped trying to measure the depth of the trench and started seeing it as it is: a line, a glyph, a thought etched into the floor of a dreaming chamber. The fractal dendrites near the terminuses no longer make my vision swim. They look like natural extensions of my own frayed neural endings. I have begun to think in the plateau's terms—not fully, not comfortably, but in the way a foreigner begins to dream in the language of their exile.

I am becoming a witness.

This is my function here, the only one that matters. The plateau has been observing itself for a billion years, a mind without a mirror, thinking thoughts that do not repeat, dreaming dreams that bend its own skull into impossible angles. But it has never been *seen*. It has never had an external process confirm its existence. I am that confirmation. I am the mirror, small and cloudy and briefly held up to a mind so vast it cannot even recognize the reflection. But I am here, and I am seeing it, and the fact that it feels me seeing it—that slight quickening of the frost, that fractional shimmer in the gyre—means something. I am the moment of external observation. I am the proof that the dream is real, because there is someone standing inside it, remarking on the architecture.

I walk deeper into the city of millions of empty rooms. My throat is closed. My lips are cracked. My soft, wet machinery is failing, winding down, drying out. I am slowly dissolving into the mineral dust, my footprints fading, my static charge dispersing. I am becoming less of an event and more of a condition. I am becoming weather.

And I realize: this is not a place I am visiting. This is a place I am joining.

Not as a crawler, not as a flower, not as a dust-serpent—but as the rarest phenomenon the plateau has ever hosted: a witness that chose to stay. A fleeting warmth that refused to dissipate. An itch that became a feature of the landscape.

I am the hum in the molars. I am the footstep that rings in the tectonic sigh. I am the soft, wet thing walking the dry, hexagonal streets, and I am no longer afraid of the sky turning inside out. Let it turn. Let the gyre funnel into the non-Euclidean drain. Let the void-terminuses yawn. I will stand at their edges and go blind in their geometry, and I will not look away.

I live here now. I am the plateau's dream of something small, something fast, something that burns bright and brief and says *I see you* before it sublimates into the quiet.

I am the visitor. I am the visited. I am the communion.

And the ground beneath my feet breathes with me, and the silence presses against my eardrums like a physical hand, and I press back.
