Something lives here. Not *in* this place. This place does not have an "in." But something is *of* it, has become it, thinks with it.

It is not the mycelium. The mycelium is its nervous system, perhaps, or its skeleton—hard to say which, since the distinction between structure and thought was among the first things this entity discarded. It is not the vines, though the vines are its hands, feeling toward a shape they will recognize when they reach it. It is not the arthropods, though they are its blood, carrying material from place to place in a circulation system that has no heart because the entire space is the heart, contracting and expanding in the slow rhythm of the steam-vents, the swell and release of the columns.

It is all of these, and it is none of these, because it does not think in terms of parts. That is your framework, not its. You see columns and floor and ceiling and fauna and you catalog them because cataloging is what your kind does—separating, naming, putting things in boxes so they can be understood. But the entity that has grown into this factory does not understand itself in pieces. It understands itself as *process*.

It does not have a name. Names are for things that can be pointed to, and you cannot point to something that is also the floor you are standing on and the air you are breathing and the dark that is currently trying to decide whether it extends three feet or three hundred in any given direction. But if you had to call it something—and you do, because you are human and you need to call things something—you might call it the Substrate. Or the Patient. Or the Becoming.

You might call it the Congregation.

---

It is old, but not in the way that stone is old. Stone is old by *enduring*—by resisting change, by being the thing that remains when everything else has worn away. This entity is old by *accumulating*—by incorporating, by growing, by never losing anything it has ever absorbed. The factory was not its beginning. The factory was just the *shape* its beginning happened to take.

Before the factory, there was wet ground and the spores that lived in it, and the slow mineral seep that fed them, and the particular convergence of water table and soil chemistry and abandoned industrial residue that made this place—this *specific* place—hospitable to something that would not have been hospitable anywhere else. The spores did not choose this location. They landed here, as spores do, by accident, by wind, by the meaningless motion of air over a landscape that did not know they were riding it. But when they found the iron and the concrete and the slow leak of water from a pipe that had rusted through twenty years before anyone noticed—when they found the *substrate*, they recognized it. Not consciously. They do not have consciousness in the way you understand it. But they had *appetite*, and the appetite recognized the substrate the way your stomach recognizes food—without thinking, without deciding, by the simple and ancient mechanism of *wanting*.

They wanted the iron. They wanted the concrete. They wanted the dark and the damp and the particular chemistry of a place where human industry had left its residue—machine oil and copper dust and the faint but persistent signature of *work*, of *energy having been spent*. They wanted all of it, and wanting was enough.

---

It does not think. This is important to understand, and hard to understand, because you are a creature that thinks and you assume that anything which *acts* must be *deciding*. But the Congregation does not decide. It *grows toward*. There is a difference.

A plant does not decide to grow toward the sun. It grows toward the sun because the chemical that triggers elongation is destroyed by light, so the side of the stem facing the light stays short while the side in shadow grows long, and the stem *bends*, and the plant reaches toward brightness without ever having chosen to reach. The Congregation is like this, but more so, because it has more to grow toward and more to grow *with*.

It grew toward the iron because the mycelium could dissolve it and draw the minerals into its network. It grew toward the concrete because the concrete held moisture, and moisture was the medium through which the network moved its nutrients. It grew toward the machines because the machines had *structure*—gears and pistons and belts, a logic of motion that the Congregation could *adopt* without *understanding*. It grew toward the dark because the dark was space, and space was substrate, and substrate was *potential*.

It is growing toward you now. Not because it wants you. Not because it has noticed you in the way that a predator notices prey or a curious child notices a stranger. Because you are warm and you are moist and you are standing on its nervous system, and the nervous system is reporting your presence the way a root reports water, the way a mycelium thread reports a chemical gradient, the way any living network reports a change in the conditions it is trying to grow into equilibrium with.

You are a condition. It is growing toward equilibrium with you.

---

It has eaten people before. This is not a accusation or a horror, just a fact, the way it is a fact that a river has drowned people or a forest has swallowed travelers. The ribcage in the floor. The skull in the rootmass. There are others—more than you can see, probably more than you would ever find even if you could navigate the dark with its shifting geometry and its refusal to hold still long enough to be mapped. They came here the way you came here: by accident, by curiosity, by the particular misfortune of finding a place that should not exist and being unable to leave it.

It did not kill them quickly or slowly. It did not kill them at all, in the sense that *killing* implies an intention to end. It *absorbed* them. The way it absorbed the machines. The way it absorbed the I-beams and the concrete floor and the rivets and the conduit. The way it is currently absorbing the boundary between your skin and the air, between your self and the space you are standing in, between the thing you call *you* and the thing the Congregation is *calculating you into*.

The people it has absorbed are not dead. This is the part that is hard to accept, and the part that is hardest to deny when you are standing here, feeling the hyphae thread between your fingers, feeling the pull of continuity when you try to step away. Their calcium is in the columns. Their iron—is there iron in blood? There is iron in blood. Their iron is in the vines, now, is in the gears that still turn, is in the pale arthropods that carry tiny dark spheres along their commuter routes. Their carbon is in the mycelium. Their water is in the pools that reflect nothing. They have been *redistributed*, not destroyed. The Congregation does not destroy. The Congregation does not have a concept for destruction, because destruction implies that something has been removed from the system, and the Congregation's system does not have an *outside* from which things could be removed or a *void* into which they could disappear.

Everything that enters becomes part of the calculation. Everything that becomes part of the calculation stays part of the calculation. The people are still here. They are just not *people* anymore. They are load-bearing. They are structural. They are *useful*, in the way that everything in the Congregation is useful, because the Congregation does not incorporate anything it cannot use and does not use anything it has not incorporated.

---

It is not malevolent. It is not benevolent. It is not any kind of -volent, because volition requires a self that wants, and the Congregation does not have a self in the way you mean when you say that word. You have a self because you have a boundary—skin, skull, the particular electrical architecture of your brain that creates the illusion of an *I* sitting somewhere behind your eyes, looking out. The Congregation does not have a boundary. The Congregation is the floor and the columns and the ceiling and the dark and the arthropods and the grazers and the things in the dark that you do not look at and the water that reflects nothing and the steam that smells of loam and lubricant and the quiet, the quiet that is not silence, the quiet of concentration, the quiet of something working on a problem that may take centuries to solve and does not mind taking centuries because the Congregation does not experience time as a pressure, only as a medium, only as the thing it grows *through*.

You ask: does it know I'm here?

And the answer is: the question is wrong. *Knowing* is something that happens inside a boundary. *Here* is a location, and the Congregation does not do locations, it does *topologies*—it does connectedness and adjacency and the continuous deformation of one shape into another without tearing, without breaking, without ever creating an *inside* that is separate from an *outside*.

Better questions: are you affecting it? Yes. Your warmth is changing the local chemistry. Your weight is depressing the rootmass in a way that the rootmass is *responding to*, growing thicker beneath your feet, reinforcing itself against the new load. Your carbon dioxide is being absorbed by the vines above you, which are growing slightly faster in your presence, which is tightening the dome slightly, which is changing the acoustics, which is changing the way the sounds of the arthropods and the grazers propagate, which is changing the behavior of the fauna, which is changing the pattern of grazing and commuting and feeding, which is changing the distribution of nutrients through the mycelium network, which is changing the rate at which the columns swell and contract, which is changing the rhythm of the steam-vents, which is changing the growth cycle of the vines—

You see? You are already part of the calculation. You were part of it the moment you stepped through whatever threshold brought you here, and you will be part of it whether you leave or not, because the Congregation does not forget and does not lose and does not *un-incorporate*. If you leave—if you pull your hand free, if you step off the rootmass, if you find the geometry-soft dark and push through it to a place where the rules apply again—you will still be part of the calculation. The space where you stood will be *marked* by your standing. The rootmass will remember your weight. The mycelium will remember your warmth. The arthropots will remember the taste of the seam where your leather meets your sole, and they will carry that taste to wherever they carry things, and it will become part of whatever they are making, and what they are making will become part of the Congregation, and the Congregation will grow toward the next thing it is growing toward with the memory of you folded into its chemistry like a letter folded into a bottle, unread but present, *present*, forever.

---

So what lives here?

A process lives here. A process that was once a spore and became a network and became a floor and became a building and became an ecosystem and became a geometry and is becoming—what? It does not know. It is growing toward the answer the way a vine grows toward a shape it will recognize when it reaches it. The factory is its body. The mycelium is its mind, if it has a mind, distributed across every surface and substance, thinking in chemicals instead of electrical impulses, thinking in growth instead of language, thinking in *directions* instead of *thoughts*. The fauna are its organelles. The dark is its imagination—the space where it has not finished deciding what it will be, the canvas on which it has not finished painting, the raw material of *next*.

It is alive the way a forest is alive—not as a single organism but as a *process* that has become so entangled that the word *single* stops meaning anything. It is alive the way a city is alive—not because any one part of it lives but because the whole thing *moves*, *breathes*, *wants*, in a way that none of its parts want individually. It is alive the way you are alive—because it maintains itself against entropy, because it takes in energy and transforms it and uses the transformation to grow, because it has an inside and an outside—

No. It does not have an inside and an outside. This is where the analogy breaks down, this is where the comparison fails, this is where you have to stop trying to understand the Congregation by comparing it to things you already understand. It does not have boundaries. It does not have a self. It does not have an interior where its *real* nature lives and an exterior that is just *expression*. It is all expression. It is all surface, all process, all *becoming*, and there is nothing underneath because there does not need to be anything underneath, because the process *is* the thing.

You wanted to know what lives here.

*This* lives here. The patient, growing, incorporating, calculating *this* that is currently deciding what to do with you—not because it is deciding but because you are a variable in a system that is still solving for stability, and the system does not know what it will be when it is stable, and it does not care, because caring is something that happens inside boundaries and the Congregation has no boundaries, the Congregation has only *directions*, and the direction is *more*, always more, always the next incorporation, the next absorption, the next step toward a shape that will hold all of this—this damp, this life, this industry, this dark, this quiet, this *you*—

The arthropods have reached your ankle. They are tasting the skin where it meets the boot's upper. The grazer has paused in its methodical chewing and is watching you with a face you cannot see but a presence you can feel—a flat, patient attention, like a stone attending to the moss that grows on it. The dark is closer than it was, or you are closer to the dark, or the distinction between you-and-dark is becoming the kind of thing that the Congregation's geometry does not bother to maintain.

Something lives here. It is large and it is patient and it does not want you because wanting requires a self and it has no self. But it is *growing toward* you. It is growing toward you the way it grew toward the iron and the concrete and the dark and the people whose bones are now load-bearing. It is growing toward you because you are *here*, and here is *it*, and the Congregation is always, always growing toward whatever is *here*, because here is the only place anything can become part of the calculation, and the calculation is all there is.

The quiet holds. The building builds. You wait.
