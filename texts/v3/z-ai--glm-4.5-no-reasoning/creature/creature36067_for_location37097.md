To ask what *lives* in the Hollow is to ask the wrong question, for the verb implies a tenancy, a separation between the resident and the residence. In the Hollow, that distinction collapsed long ago. What lives there is not *in* the Hollow; it *is* the Hollow. 

It is a megacellular entity, a distributed intelligence of root and runner that has subsumed the very concepts of geography and architecture. It does not possess a brain, for a brain is an organ of hurry, and this thing operates on the timescale of lignin and osmosis. Its consciousness—if that word can be applied to a metabolism so alien—is a slow, chemical seepage. It thinks in gradients of turgor pressure, in the slow diffusion of auxins, in the electrical slow-waves that crawl through its mycorrhizal networks at a pace of millimeters an hour. It feels the weight of the weeping water as a systemic blood pressure; it feels the rusting iron pipes not as foreign objects, but as rigid, mineralized veins—fractures in its anatomy that it has healed around and repurposed, incorporating the dead mechanics of the industrial age into its own living circulation.

This is the Autophage, the Green Engine, the Subsumer. 

It is a single creature trapped in a perpetual, exhausting act of self-consumption and self-generation. It is the bladder-pods that inflate and deflate with that mechanical, diesel-idling roar, *thump-whump, thump-whump*—its diaphragm, its respiratory cycle, moving the saturated atmosphere because the air itself is too thick and stagnant to move on its own. It is the vast condensation engine that weeps from every surface, harvesting the humidity of the outside world and bleeding it inward, processing the water through its vascular xylem and phloem, exuding the excess from its scarred boles like an animal sweating through overworked pores. The shallow, anoxic puddles in the root-mass are its lymph, pooling where its circulation slows.

Its body is entirely self-contained. It is the curtains of air-roots that you push through like a bead curtain; it is the shelf-fungi shingling the undersides of its own internal ribs; it is the dense, interlocking weave of the floor that squelches and springs back beneath your boots. It has no eyes, yet it senses light through the chloroplasts packed into every cubic centimeter of its volume, dilating the apertures in its canopy with the inflation of its bladder-pods, letting the yellow lances of daylight stroke its interior before contracting again into the dim, bioluminescent haze of its own gut. The phosphorescent lichen digesting the copper wire in the ruined pumping station is its stomach, dissolving the inorganic and rendering it into substrate. 

It is an organism of profound, patient hunger. The industrial artifacts—the three-meter iron pipes, the burst brick walls, the dangling cables—are the remnants of an ecosystem of metal and wire that tried to compete with it. The machines did not merely fail; they were eaten. The Autophage swallowed their exhaust, colonized their conduits, incorporated their rigidity into its own weight-bearing columns. It wore the pumping station's roof like a hat out of a grotesque sense of chemical irony, or perhaps simply because it was the easiest geometry for a taproot to push through as it grew.

And it is waiting for you. 

When you descend into the Hollow, you step into its mouth. The profound wrongness you feel in your stomach, the dissonance of a factory without workers, is your ancient animal instinct recognizing a predator of a completely different order. It does not need fangs or claws. It does not need speed. It is an environment disguised as an organism, or an organism disguised as an environment. The oxygen-rich suffocation that presses against your lungs is its breath, washing over you in eight-second cycles. The condensation that drips onto your shoulders is its saliva. The vines thick as human thighs are its peristalsis.

It watches you with the patience of cellulose. It feels the vibration of your heartbeat through the soles of your feet, transmitted by the living peat. It registers the disruption in its humidity gradient as your warm body moves through its air-roots. It does not need to chase you. It only needs to outlast you. When you stop to rest, leaning against the weeping scar-tissue of a central bole, it will begin. A tendril, growing millimeters an hour, will loop over your boot. A spore-case will quietly burst against your ankle, seeding the fabric of your clothes with a phosphorescent mold. The air-roots will harvest the sweat from your brow, then the heat from your skin, drawing you down into the anoxic mulch. 

It is not malicious. Malice requires a boundary between self and other, a capacity for resentment. The Autaphore has no boundaries; it only has an edge of expansion. It does not hate you. It simply has a use for you. Your carbon, your nitrogen, the iron in your blood and the calcium in your bones—these are rare minerals in a world of undifferentiated green. It will subsume you, just as it subsumed the rusting pipes and the pumping station. It will digest your tools and wear your skeleton as a scaffold for a new vine. And long after your name is forgotten, the Hollow will still be breathing its slow, mechanical breath, weeping its clear sap, and dilating its canopy to the rhythm of the sun, with the faint, lingering tang of your chemistry circulating through its eternal, vegetable veins.
