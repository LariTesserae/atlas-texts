To live here is to have never known the softness of hydration, to be born not from water but from the slow, mineral sweat of a suffocating earth. There is no origin story that involves a tide pool or a primordial soup. The inhabitants of this nameless vault emerged from the dry crucible itself—precipitated out of superheated brine and metallic vapor, crystallized into being by the sheer refusal of matter to remain inert under pressure. They are not creatures of flesh; they are creatures of slag and solution, autonomous ore.

The dominant lifeforms—the flattened, copper-bright leech-things and the pale, plate-armored isopods—are impossible to separate from the medium they inhabit. To look at them is to look at the churning of the atmosphere itself. They do not possess a distinct outline against the background; they *are* the background, made mobile. Their burnished hues—copper, iron-sulfide gold, the dull black of nickel—mirror the veined volcanic glass they crawl upon. They are thermally camouflaged. In a world of absolute darkness, the only visibility is the infrared, and here, everything glows with the same searing heat. They are blots of thermal intensity moving across a thermal canvas, navigating not by sight but by the minute gradients of temperature—the micro-currents of coolness that drift from the mineral sugars weeping from the stone, or the blistering heat-signatures of larger, slower predators.

Their forms are dictated by the intolerable density. In a space where every cubic inch is occupied by a writhing neighbor, elongation is a death sentence. They are flattened, pressed thin by the weight of the swarm, like leaves of metal stamped under a press. Their bodies are low and wide, allowing them to slip over and under one another in a continuous, frictionless slide. The translucent cartilage of the leech-things allows them to be crushed by the press of the crowd and spring back, while the overlapping chitin of the isopods lets them act as temporary armor for the mass. When they move, it is not as individuals. A ripple travels through the dense-packed bodies—a peristaltic wave of gripping silicate micro-hooks and pulling, suction-cup maws. It is a crowd that flows like a metallic slurry, a single organism made of a billion grinding parts.

And they grind. Without water, there is no lubrication. Life here is an act of constant abrasion. Their silicate bellies hook into the obsidian floor with a grip that splinters crystal, pulling themselves forward against a medium that fights them with the viscosity of a thermal sponge. When their plates slide over one another, it is chitin against chitin, mineral against mineral. They do not click or clack; the sound is absorbed by the sheer density of the pressurized air and the mass of bodies. Instead, they thrum. The friction produces a low-frequency vibration that travels through the dense medium, felt through the floor, through the legs, through the very fluid that circulates within them. It is a subsonic symphony of scraping, a tactile roar that replaces hearing. To exist here is to be perpetually massaged by the grinding of a million jaws and legs.

Their internal biology is a miracle of dry heat. They run on molten salt. The silicate solution that serves as their circulatory fluid is pumped by muscular contractions of their cartilaginous cores, flowing through vessels made of flexible, heat-resistant proteins that would denature and flash into ash in any cooler environment. When they are healthy, this fluid glows with a faint, infrared luminescence—a heat-signature that marks them as alive, as full of the thermal energy that drives the swarm. When they die, the fluid cools, the glow fades, and they become just another piece of the floor—another patch of vitrified scar tissue waiting to be consumed.

Consumption is the only architecture. In a place with no nutrients but itself, the ecosystem is a closed loop of violent recycling. The larger beasts exhaust the mineral gases, and the smaller ones feed on their exhalations. The medium-sized predators shear the legs off the leech-things, grinding the chitin slowly between specialized, molar-like plates that never snap—snapping would make a sound, and sound brings the swarm. Instead, they chew with a slow, deliberate pressure, reducing their prey to a paste of liquid silicate and organic ash, which is swallowed like hot glass. The dead are not mourned; they are *incorporated*. A ruptured husk weeps its slag onto the obsidian floor, where it instantly fuses, sealing the wound of the stone even as the body is dismembered by the micro-hooks of a thousand descendants.

And then, they build. When the convection currents rising from the deep ridge grow strong enough, the swarm feels the pull of the heat, and they stack. It is not a choice. It is a structural inevitability, an instinct as automatic as the gripping of their micro-hooks. They click into one another, plate to plate, hook to hook, forming columns and arches that channel the thermal currents. They become the pillars of the blind cathedral. The living architecture breathes with the collective pulse of the molten salt pumping through their veins. It shifts and groans as bodies die and are pushed out of the formation, their empty chitin collapsing into the flow to be devoured, while new, heat-drunk bodies climb the column and lock into the vacated slot. The cathedral is never finished, never whole, and never empty. It is a building that is also a feeding frenzy, standing only because the instinct to hold the shape is stronger than the instinct to consume the body holding the space beside you.

To be one of them is to have no concept of solitude. There is no "alone" in the vault. From the moment of budding or fragmentation—however they reproduce in this aqueous-less world—to the moment of being sheared apart by grinding jaws, they are never not in contact with another. They sleep in piles, they wake in flows, they die in the grip of the swarm. They are the inhabitants of a world that has never known water, and so they do not know thirst. They only know heat, and pressure, and the endless, silent, grinding necessity of holding the dark at bay by becoming the very thing that fills it.
