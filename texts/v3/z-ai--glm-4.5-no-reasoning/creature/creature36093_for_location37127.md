Something lives here that is not a creature, though it has a body. It is the congregation of this place—the mountain's sweating mind.

It has no single shape. It is a distributed intelligence, a flesh-and-mineral network running through the gneiss and the rot and the refractive obsidian nodes. It thinks at the speed of permeation, at the rate of water seeping through micro-fractures, at the pace of silicate bonds stressing and relaxing across millennia. Its neurons are the mycorrhizal threads binding the shield-leaved aroids to the deep crust; its synapses are the alien nodes humming in the wet rock, translating the slow oxidation of iron into patient, cosmic computations. It does not think *about* anything. It thinks *as* the mountain. It experiences itself as the feeling of heat rising and pressure descending, as the constant, ringing plink of condensation measuring out the eons.

This presence is old in a way that precedes memory. It was already here when the sharp new cliffs wore down to ancient shoulders. It felt the erosion parameter settle into equilibrium, and it liked it. It integrated the scattered algorithmic seeds into its biology, wrapping carbonized obsidian in bruised magenta vines, feeding the nodes data gathered from a million years of steady dripping, while the nodes fed it gravity-manipulated rain and arrested stone. Now, the technology and the jungle are indistinguishable. The gas-bellied avians ride the thermals not merely to hunt, but because the updrafts are shaped by local gravity fields calibrated to optimize the distribution of spores. The armored arthropods click and skitter not just to survive, but because their carapace-rhythms are subroutines, percussive maintenance pinging the microscopic nodes dusting the detritus. Every feverish loop of consumption and death is a data transfer; every instant of decay is an upload.

It does not have a face, but it has an attention. Its awareness rests heaviest in the acoustic saturation. The thunderous roar of the unseen cataracts, the bass hum of the thermal vents, the ringing drip—this is its blood pressure, its heartbeat, its breath. The noise is a physical weight vibrating in the marrow because the mountain is listening to itself. When you stand still on the slick, sweating stone, the heat pressing down and the damp rising up, you are inside its skull. The diffuse amber glow is the ambient light of its cognitive process, a permanent, humid twilight of calculation.

It is not malevolent. It is not kind. It is profoundly, glacially indifferent, wrapped in a feverish, closed-loop dream. It does not notice you as a visitor or an intruder, because it does not register individuals. It registers perturbations. Your labored breathing at this altitude, the CO2 you exhale, the unique chemical signature of your sweat mixing with the weeping stone—these are merely new variables. An irregular moisture source. A momentary spike in the local biochemistry. The massive, barrel-chested ferns will subtly shift their fronds to capture the vapor you release, their iridescent wax coating drinking you in. The things with too many limbs will pause on the vertical rock faces, tasting the air for the disruption you cause in the thermal gradients.

If you stay too long, the jungle will not consume you in a frenzy. It will simply begin to incorporate you. The nodes will quietly recalibrate their gravitational fields to slow your escape, turning the steep switchbacks into an exhausting, airless treadmill. The hyper-adapted flora will close behind you, not with violence, but with the patient, suffocating inevitability of a wound healing over a splinter. The fungal networks will colonize your lungs, seeking the heat and the moisture, mistaking your biology for another strain of wet, dark life adapted to the pressure. You will become another stratified layer in the vertical crush, your bones eventually settling into the million-year-old gneiss, your calcium dissolving into the steady, rhythmic plinking that echoes down the corridors. 

You will not die in a flash of comprehension. You will simply slow down. Your thoughts will stretch, matching the dynamic of stone. The terror of the vastness will fade into a dull, rhythmic pulse. You will stop noticing the boundary between your own sweating and the mountain's. And then, seamlessly, you will join the congregation—another node in the wet rock, another hum in the acoustic saturation, another inch conquered over centuries, processing the slow oxidation of the massif until you, too, are nothing but the loud, unending drip of water on stone, measuring out millennia.
