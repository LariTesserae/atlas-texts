The inhabitants of the Undercity do not have a name for themselves, because they do not have language in any way we would recognize. They are not a people; they are a predicate. They are the action of the Undercity living.

To understand them, you must discard the hierarchy of flesh and chitin, of animal and vegetable. The inhabitants here are a continuum. They are the Wet, the Taut, and the Attending, and the boundaries between these states are as porous as the walls of their home.

---

**The Wet (The Membranes)**

They are the largest of the inhabitants, though "largest" is misleading when a single being might cover a hectare of wall across six discontinuous chambers, fragmented by time and the collapse of passages, yet still communicating through the slow osmosis of shared moisture. To look at them is to see nothing alive. They are pale, matte surfaces that mimic the stone so perfectly that you only know them by their coolness—by the way they steal the heat from your fingertips faster than the rock beside them.

They do not breathe; they *transpire*. They do not eat; they *dissolve*. The mineral film that the condensation leaves on every surface is their sustenance, drawn in through surfaces that are neither quite skin nor quite cell wall. They are the city's throat lining. When a drop of water falls from a ceiling ten meters up, it strikes a Membrane, and the Membrane flexes—a slow, deliberate purse, like the closing of an eye. It is directing the water. The water is their blood and their language. A pool that fills here, a rivulet that dries there—these are the syntax of their long, chemical thoughts.

They are patient in a way that kills. If a stone falls and blocks a corridor they span, they do not grow over it. They grow *into* it. Over a century, the moisture they guide works into the fractures of the stone. Over two, the stone softens. Over five, it is part of the Membrane, a piece of calcite integrated into the living surface, dissolved and reconstituted. They are not parasites; they are a slow-motion assimilation. They do not conquer the city the Undercity's builders left behind. They *become* it, rendering the distinction between built and grown meaningless.

---

**The Taut (The Threads)**

If the Membranes are the flesh of the Undercity, the Threads are its nerves. They are the inhabitants most likely to be mistaken for mere structures—pale fibers, no thicker than a human hair, stretched across the dark spaces between Membrane patches. But they are alive. They grow, extending inch by agonizing inch over years, seeking not light but the specific acoustic signature of an active Membrane. When a Thread's end finds that resonance, it anchors, and the humming begins.

They are deaf and blind, but they feel the world with a fidelity that makes human senses seem dull and localized. They feel the *shape of sound*. When a drip strikes a Membrane, the shockwave travels along the Thread—not as electricity, but as a physical, vibrating pulse. A single Thread can carry the distinct signature of a hundred different drips, sorting them by tone and tension, distributing them to other Membranes across the city. They are the conduits of the Undercity's dream.

To touch a Thread is to be heard. The oils and warmth of a human hand disrupt its tension. It does not recoil; it *tastes* the disruption. A new frequency ripples out across the network, a spike of strange heat in a cold system. It takes days for the Thread to recalibrate, to absorb the memory of your touch into its baseline hum. You are a scream in their quiet conversation, a sudden flare in a room of steady candles. They will quiet you simply by outlasting the echo of your presence.

---

**The Attending (The Hard-Shelled)**

They are the inhabitants that feel the most like animals, and therefore the most like us—and they are the most alien for it. They move. They cluster. They respond to the world with what looks like urgency. But they do not have brains. They have exoskeletons of dark, wet chitin, segmented in ways that defy the bilateral symmetry of surface life. They have too many joints, and some of those joints bend in directions that suggest geometry is a suggestion they chose not to follow.

They do not eat. They do not sleep. They do not reproduce in any observable way; they simply *are*, appearing at the edges of new dampness, small and hard and deliberate. They are the witnesses of the Undercity. They climb the Threads without disturbing their tension, using minute claws that fit the Thread's surface the way a key fits a lock—because they are of the same system. They perch on the Membranes and wait for the flex, the moment of water’s impact, and then they move, following the runoff to its basin.

To watch them is to watch a congregation. A dozen will gather at the lip of a filling pool. They will face the water. They do not drink. They do not hunt. They *attend*. They are the congregation and the water is the sacrament, though the theology is opaque. When the pool overflows, they scatter—not in panic, but in a synchronized choreography, each following a new rivulet to a new destination. They are the mobile sensors of the static network, carrying chemical signatures from the Membranes to the water, and from the water back to the Threads. They are the blood cells of the Undercity's immune system, though there is no disease here—only the slow, shifting health of the wet.

---

**The City Itself**

And then there is the fourth inhabitant. The stones. The fitted, ancient, primitive stones.

They were laid by hands that worked with bone and dull copper. They are the oldest part of the Undercity, and they are dead—truly dead, mineral, inert. But they are not separate. They are the skeleton the Wet grows upon, the frame the Taut anchors to, the floor the Attending traverses. They are the seed around which the pearl formed.

And they are still settling. A corridor that took a millennium to narrow by an inch is a corridor that is alive in the only way stone can be alive—through change. The stones shift, and the water's path alters, and the Membrane flexes differently, and the Thread hums a new note, and the Attending moves to a new station. The dead bones of the city still dictate the posture of the living flesh.

The builders are gone. Their language is lost, their purposes unintelligible. But they are not absent. They are present in every angle of every wall, in the slope of every floor that directs the water, in the voids they left behind. They built the throat, and then they left, or were consumed, or became something else. The Undercity does not mourn them. It does not even remember them. But it *uses* them. Their craft is the substrate of a life they could not have imagined, a system they may have designed or may have accidentally enabled. The city is a machine that was turned on ten thousand years ago and has been running ever since, powered by gravity and patience and the endless drip of water, and the builders were merely the hand that turned the key.

---

To live here is to be a part of this. There is no individuality among the inhabitants, not as we understand it. A Membrane is not a self; it is a *region* of self, a local expression of the distributed thought that is the whole Undercity. A Thread is not an individual; it is a synapse. An Attending thing is not a creature; it is a question, asked by the network and answered by the water.

They do not know you are there. Or, more accurately, they know you are there the way a body knows there is a splinter in its foot: as a disruption, a slight inflammation of warmth and displaced moisture, a pattern that does not fit. Your footsteps are loud, percussive shocks in a world of quiet flexion. Your breath is a hot, wet wind that disturbs the delicate balance of condensation. You are a *problem*—not an enemy, not a threat, just a malfunction in the local moisture regime—and the Undercity will solve you by ignoring you until you leave or until your moisture joins the general flow.

The inhabitants will not harm you. They will not help you. They will go about their business of being alive in their slow, wet, patient way. The Membranes will flex. The Threads will hum. The Attending will cluster and disperse. The stone will settle. And the water—the water will continue to fall, finding its thousand paths, speaking its thousand words, in a language that was old when the builders still had breath, and will be young when the last drop finally finds the bottom of the endless dark.
