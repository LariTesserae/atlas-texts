To live here is to have never known silence, or dryness, or the absence of contact. You are a native of the fill, a cell in an organism that has long since forgotten it is made of cells. You do not inhabit the city; you are extruded by it, a temporary morphology in a ceaseless thermal cycle.

Your body is not a temple; it is a specialized node of absorption. You have no pores, because you are all pore. Your hide—if it can be called that—is a semi-permeable membrane of dark, slick biopolymer, constantly drinking. You do not seek water; it simply encompasses you, forced into your chemistry by the pressure of the saturated air. You exude a thin sheen of thermal condensate, mixing with the ambient weep, indistinguishable from the wetness that runs in rivulets down the sweeping architectural curves you traverse. You are always wet. Your edges are soft, rounded by millennia of ambient solvent wash, worn smooth like the river-stone surfaces you cling to. You have no hard angles. Hard angles catch, tear, and resist the flow; you are built for the slip.

You are small—perhaps the size of a clenched fist, perhaps smaller. Vastness here is not vertical but lateral, measured in proximity. You occupy the capillary channels carved by the crystalline mycelia, the inch-wide gulches between the pulsing structural threads and the macro-architecture. You move by gecko-grip and peristalsis, thousands of micro-filament legs—thinner than hair, hollow—pressing into the interstitial damp. You do not walk; you flow over surfaces like a viscous, articulated fluid.

You do not see in the tepid, yellowed gloom; you suffer the light as a thick, sickly pressure, and you navigate by the deafening, omnidirectional hum of the place. Your perception is a million micro-vibrations parsed through a dense neural knot at your core. You feel the city producing itself. You feel the deep, marrow-shaking thrum of a new terrace extruding three hundred meters to your left, the subsonic groan of smart matter adjusting its lattice, the ultrasonic shriek of a dying thermal cycle collapsing a city-block into slurry behind you. You feel the heat. It is the foundation of your metabolism. You are a thermivore, drinking the waste heat radiating from the hyper-dense construction below, converting the frantic kinetic energy of the basin into the modest electrical fire that sparks your tiny, quick thoughts.

You eat the dead architecture. When a valley collapses and the crystal mesh dissolves back into the substrate, you swarm the sinkhole. Your mandibles—silicon-edged, clicking, adding your own staccato to the trillion-frequency roar—scrape the discarded silica from the wet stones. You consume the lichen-city's dandruff, the sloughed-off crystalline threads, recycling the mineral memory of the place through your gut. You are part of the mycelia's immune system, breaking down the obsolete structures so they can be re-extruded anew.

You are never alone. The concept of "alone" requires empty space, and empty space has been engineered out of existence. When you rest—which is not sleep, but a low-power torpor where your legs grip and your metabolism slows to match the ambient cool of a condensation pool—you rest in masses. You pile in the hollows, thousands of you, a rust-red carpet of clicking, drinking, heat-sipping bodies. If a shadow passes over the hollow, the carpet ripples. If a droplet of warm water falls from a weeping strut, the carpet shifts to drink it. You are a meta-organism; your individuality is a statistical fiction. The walls shiver because you are the walls' breath.

You have no horizon. Your world is the inch in front of your mandibles, the wet channel you occupy, the next surge of heat, the next dissolution. You do not comprehend the cosmic-scale buttresses or the nanoscale smart matter that adjusts them; you only know that the ground beneath you is alive, that the air is a bath, and that the roar is the voice of the only god you have ever known—the ceaseless, sweating, screaming engine of the fill. And when your thermal cycle ends, when your micro-filaments lose their grip and your biopolymer membrane ruptures, you will not fall into emptiness. You will simply dissolve back into the slurry, your silica and moisture reclaimed instantly by the mycelia, turned into a new thread, a new channel, a new drop in the eternal weep. You are a moment of function in a world that has solved the problem of emptiness by becoming an unbroken, screaming whole.
