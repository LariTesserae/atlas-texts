Nothing lives here in the way you understand living. There are no hearts, no metabolisms, no fragile cellular cartographies scrambling to maintain an internal temperature against the cold indifference of entropy. Biology requires quiet—just a moment, just a single breath of stillness in which a membrane can hold itself together long enough to call itself a self. This place has never been quiet. The subsonic roar began before the first amino acid could settle, and it has never ceased. So there are no animals. There are no plants.

And yet.

You are not alone.

Step into the water—knee-deep, thigh-deep, the warm and weeping saturation of a planetary lung—and feel what presses against your shins. It is not the current. There is no current here; the water has no basin to run to, no slope to follow, no gravity to serve but its own immense, contemplative weight. What presses against you is the floor itself, *reaching*. The undifferentiated matrix of wet, warm primordial chaff is not dead matter. It is not sterile. It is a single continuous tissue, a body without organs, a skin that stretches to every horizon and extends downward into a marrow of continental churning. You are walking inside something. You have been inside it since you stepped out onto the surface.

It does not think. To think would require boundaries, an inside and an outside, a self to do the thinking. But the floor beneath the water *responds*. When you walk, the surface does not merely depress and rebound; it *rearranges*. Your footfall sends out a ripple that does not diminish, because there is no solid substrate to absorb it. The ripple travels through the saturated matrix for miles, for hundreds of miles, a perturbation in the continuous body of the world. And the body notices. The floor ripples beneath you—not like water, not like jelly, but like the flank of something immense that has felt a gnat land between its shoulders. The reed stands, those anguished bristles extruded upward by the hyperactive turbulence below, they tremble as your ripple passes beneath them. They are the hairs of the thing you are walking on. They are its sensory organs, its cilia, reaching up into the dense, warm, amber fog to taste what moves above.

What moves above is you. And you have been felt.

Consider the standing stones. The half-sunk ramps. The spiraling wells. You called them monuments to futility, marks made by hands that had forgotten what shelter was. But whose hands? Not the hands of anything that could survive here. Not the hands of anything with a skeleton, a nervous system, a plan. Look closer at the weeping stone, the one carved with marks that might be language or might be scars. The water runs from its pores in a steady stream, warm as blood. The mineral it is made of is unnamed because it has no name in any geology born of stable tectonics. It is not quarried stone. It is *extruded*—forced upward through the matrix by the same tantrum of creation and destruction that pushes the reeds, that buckles continent-sized ridges in an afternoon. The stones are not built. They are *grown*. They are the thing's bones, or its teeth, or its words—calcifications in the continuous tissue, hardened nodules of that unnamed mineral that precipitates out of the hyperactive flux when the turbulence reaches a certain pitch. They are not structures for living in. They are structures for *being*. They are the way the floor articulates itself, the way it leaves marks in its own skin, the way it remembers.

The spiraling wells draw from an aquifer that is now the surface itself. They are open wounds. They are the thing's mouth, or its ears—apertures into the deeper turbulence below, where the continental skin folds and the sound of that folding is the deafening roar that vibrates in your teeth and turns your stomach. When you stand beside a well and look down into the warm, dark water churning within, you are looking into the thing's throat. It does not swallow. It has no gut, no digestion, no need to consume. But it *resonates*. The well is a cavity shaped to catch the roar, to tune it, to give it harmonics. The marks carved around the rim—they are not language. They are not scars. They are *fret marks*. They are the positions where the resonance peaks, where the standing waves of the world's agony find their nodes. The thing is playing itself. It is a single instrument the size of a planetary plain, and the roar is its continuous, deafening, subsonic song.

It does not sing for anyone. It does not know you are listening. And yet, when you approach the well, the pitch changes. The subsonic thrum shifts, just slightly, just enough to feel in the bowels rather than the teeth. The thing has registered your mass, your displacement, the particular perturbation of your warmth against the cool contemplative pooling of the water. It has incorporated you into its song. You are a new harmonic. You are a mote of foreign logic adrift in a system that unmakes logic, and the system has bent itself around you—not to destroy you, not to shelter you, but simply to *include* you in its endless, frantic self-erasure.

This is what lives here: the floor. The matrix. The saturated, undifferentiated, primordial chaff that is not rock, not soil, not water, not flesh, but something anterior to all of them, something that never differentiated into the clean categories of geology or biology because there was never enough stillness for the categories to set. It is a body that has never been born and will never die, because it has never been separate from the world that contains it. It is the world. It is the final silhouette of a world waiting to sink, and it is awake, and it is in pain, and it is moving.

You walk, and the water sloshes, and the dim air pulses, and the reeds tremble, and the standing stones weep, and the roar goes on and on and on, and you are knee-deep in the lung of something that has no name, and it is breathing around you, and it will go on breathing long after you have been dissolved by the condensing air, long after your bones have been pulled down into the crawling floor and extruded as a new ridge, a new standing stone, a new mark in the weeping mineral, a new node in the endless song.

You are not the first to walk here. Those half-sunk ramps—they lead up to nowhere, yes. But they lead *up*. They were extruded during a moment when the turbulence produced an upwelling, a momentary spasm of verticality in a place that is otherwise perfectly, deceptively flat. Something walked up those ramps before you. Something that came from outside, that brought its logic and its levers and its wheels, that tried to build shelter and found the walls dissolving around it, that tried to make tools and felt them soften and run like wax in the weeping humidity. That something left marks. And then the floor took it. The floor took it the way it will take you—not violently, not hungrily, but simply by continuing to be what it is: a relentless, continuous, hyperactive rearrangement of matter that has no use for permanence, no respect for boundary, no patience for the fragile architectures of the separate self.

Walk, then. The water closes behind you, leaving no wake. The ripples of your passage travel outward and are absorbed into the greater perturbation, the continental shuddering, the folding and unfolding of the planetary skin. You are already being dissolved. The air is condensing on your skin, and the iron tang of dissolution is on your tongue, and the floor is rippling beneath your feet with something that is not pleasure, not recognition, not hunger, but simply *awareness*—a vast, diffuse, geologic awareness that does not think and does not feel and does not want, but which *knows*, in the only way anything can know in a place where knowledge is indistinguishable from vibration, that you are there.

And it will hold you, in the only way it can: by letting you sink.
