To live here is to be a creature of the edge—the razor's edge of shattering mirrors, the bleeding edge of warped geometry, the leading edge of an ecology that runs on thunder and starlight. You are not soft. You cannot be. Soft things dried into dust eons ago; their memory is locked in the crystal lattice beneath your feet. What remains is mineral, chitinous, and deliberate.

You are the Striders. You move through the echoing plazas and root-streets with the patience of geology and the necessity of breath. Your six legs—segmented silicate, each joint a precision bearing of keratin and quartz—strike the calcified rhizome in a percussive rhythm entirely swallowed by the deafening, subsonic boom of the maceration engines. You do not hear your own footsteps; you feel them in the vibrations that travel up your glassy limbs and rattle against your neural ganglion. You navigate not by sight—the permanent amber bruise of the dying dwarf star offers only gloom, and the violet anhydrous sky is a barred ceiling of spathes—but by the localized gravitational gradients. Your body knows when space is folding inward. Your internal architecture is a living plumb bob, a dense deposit of magnetite in your core that feels the pull of the graviton-weave engines. When a pillar appears a mile away but takes only three steps to reach, you do not marvel; you simply adjust the tension in your silicate joints and step across the compressed distance. 

Draped across your broad, dorsal carapace is your filtration-cloth—a sweeping, porous mesh of living keratin and bio-crystalline threads, grown from your own follicles over decades of molting. It is your lung and your stomach. You drag it through the hygroscopic void, harvesting the microscopic trace elements and the weeping photonic sap that drifts like intangible pollen from the fractal heartwood of the pillars. You graze on the physics of the place. When a dry-lightning flash sublimates the air into a violent, ultraviolet detonation, your mesh captures the fleeting, ionized nutrients born in that instant, absorbing the plasma-hot energy directly through your carapace. You are a vacuum cleaner walking through a cathedral, sifting the ruins of water and the exhaust of stars.

But you are not the apex. Above you, in the structural canopy where the iron-bark plates interlock, the Razor-Kites roost. To be a Razor-Kite is to be an exercise in aerodynamic lethality stripped of all weight. You are a glide-body, a living kite of stretched, translucent chitin pulled taut over a skeleton of hollow volcanic glass. You have no voice; your presence is announced only by the sound of your own body—the faint, crystalline shriek of your leading edges micro-fracturing as you carve through the dense, dry air. You are a mirror shard given appetite.

You hunt by the rhythm of the engines. When the graviton-weave maceration engines cycle, they emit a subsonic boom that fogs the minds of the Striders below, causing a momentary hesitation in their hexapedal gait. In that fractional pause, you drop. You fall not through air, but through the warped space between the pillars, utilizing the localized gravity wells to accelerate your descent far beyond terminal velocity. Your leading edges—serrated, monomolecular planes of obsidian—slice through the filtration-cloth and silicate joints of the Strider with a sound like a chandelier collapsing. You do not grapple; you bisect. Your prey shatters into mineral offal before it can register the strike, scattering calcium and keratin across the root-plaza. You drink the photonic sap that weeps from the exposed, fractured ends of their silicate legs, a hot, luminous nectar that stains your glass wings with shifting, quantum-interference patterns.

And beneath you both, always, are the beetles. They are the sanitation department of this grown metropolis, the fist-sized detritivores with mandibles of tungsten-grade chitin. They are the closest thing this place has to a working class. They do not hunt; they inherit. When a Razor-Kite shatters a Strider in the open plaza, or when a Kite's own fragile glass skeleton fractures upon the rhizome after a misjudged gravity-well dive, the beetles swarm. They emerge from the hollow knots in the chthonic wood-mass, pouring out in a tide of glossy, dark shells. They dismantle the crystalline corpses with industrial efficiency, chewing the calcium and silicate back into a paste that they secrete directly into the burls of the rhizome. They feed the city. They are the mechanism by which the animal is recycled into the botanical, by which the machine sustains its own infrastructure. They walk the streets worn into the living lignin, wearing them deeper, obeying the neuro-chemical commands of the city-mind that triggers their swarms with pheromones exuded from the slowly rotating pillars.

To live here is to be entirely native to a paradox. You are flesh made of glass; you are bone made of stone; you breathe an atmosphere that has no water, yet you are drowning in green. You do not look up to pray, for the sky is merely the underside of the city's roof. You do not look down for shelter, for the ground is the skin of the beast that ate the mountains. You simply move, you tear, you consume, you dissolve—all to the deafening, mind-fogging heartbeat of a botched physics, an engine of gravity and light buried in the heartwood of a tree that was never really a tree, in a city that was never really a city, on a world that has forgotten what it means to be wet.
