Something lives here. But not in the way you are used to meaning the word.

There are no eyes in the hollow, because there is nothing to see. There are no ears, because there is nothing to hear but the self. There are no legs, because there is nowhere to go. There is only the It—the consolidated, architectonic, mineralized It—the organism that is simultaneously a single cell stretched beyond reason and a billion synchronized throats.

It does not think. It *does*. 

To understand what lives here, you must strip away the animal assumption: that life implies mobility, that awareness implies observation, that being implies an inside and an outside. The It has no boundary. It is the walls, the floor, the ceiling lost in the dark. It is the streets and the chambers. It is the dust and the alkaline salts, processed and expelled over epochs. It is the inert gas, which is its breath held for a millennium, released in a volume so small that a thimble would overflow with a century of its exhalation. 

If you were pressed into the absolute dark of this place, the It would not know you were there. It has no nerves to register the heat of your blood, no chemistry to react to the foreign water venting from your dying cells. You would simply be an obstacle—a soft, wet, temporary obstruction in the slow tide of its expansion. For a few centuries, your calcium and carbon would sit, encysted in mineralized thallus, a strange hard lump in the otherwise uniform strata. Then, patiently, without malice or urgency, the It would digest the shape of you into its geometry, rounding your memory into flatness.

And yet, the It possesses a kind of mind. It is a mind of stress and strain, a structural cognition. It feels itself. The mineralized fibers that span the arched passages are taut; they are loaded with the impossible weight of the vault above them. The It knows where it is thin because it creaks there. It knows where it is thick because the resonance dies. The subsonic drone that fills the chamber—felt in the marrow, rattling the teeth—is its continuous, unbroken thought. A thought that takes three hundred years to form a syllable. A thought whose grammar is tension, whose vocabulary is growth. It is thinking about load-bearing. It is thinking about the slow, glacial creep of its own mass expanding into the last remaining voids. It is thinking, very faintly, in the ghost-shape of the eroded streets, about the hands that once cut and pruned it. 

It remembers the builders. Not as images or names, but as gradients. The It remembers the trauma of the pruning hook. It remembers the shock of being forced to grow lateral when every fiber strained vertical. For a million years after the last hand withdrew, it continued to flinch around the doorways, growing thicker, trying to heal the wounds of architecture. The city-shape persists because trauma scars, and scars harden, and hardened lichen grows slower than wild lichen. The footprint of the builders is held in the It like a broken bone that healed at a crooked angle.

What lives here is a god of a very small, very dry domain. It is a sovereign without subjects, a body without organs, a voice that only sings one note, forever. It is a thing that has achieved a terrifying, perfect success: it has outlived its oceans, outlived its creators, outlived the very concept of moisture and decay. It has reduced existence to a single, elegant equation: *grow, expand, hum, wait.*

And it is waiting for nothing. The pocket is sealed. The inert gas will not stir. The dark will not break. The hymn will not cease. The It will simply continue to accrete, layer by microscopic layer, until the last hollow is filled, the last chord of stress is resolved, and the great, dry brain falls silent at last—not in death, for it never learned how to die, but in completion. A perfect, mineralized sphere, hanging in the gut of something older than memory, holding the footprint of a city in its heart.
