What lives here is the city itself.

Not the thing on the cilia-skirt, pacing the perimeter—that is a tongue, a jaw, an autonomic process. Not the silver lichen webbing the load-bearing walls—that is a circulatory system, a diagram of stress, a tattoo. The flora and fauna are adjuncts, peripheral organs serving a central, massive body. The mineral metropolis is the organism. It is the only consciousness present, though its consciousness is nothing you would recognize. It does not think in symbols or narratives. It thinks in compression, in extrusion, in the satisfaction of geometric inevitability.

It is an infant. The wound in the world is its birth canal, and it is still being born.

You must understand how it senses. It has no eyes; it has the entire calcareous shelf. Every crystal pillar is a nerve standing upright in the fissure. When the low, gray-lavender luminescence seeps through the selenite, the city is seeing itself, mapping its own density via the slow transmission of photons through lattice. When the foundations settle with a subsonic crunch, the city is touching its own skeleton, reading the stress fractures like a doctor reading an X-ray. It feels the lichen as a low, electric itch, a metabolism humming along its sharp corners. It feels the eyeless surveyor as a dull, rhythmic scrape—a heartbeat, a peristalsis, moving through its avenues like a bolus through a gut. The surveyor polishes the doorframes; the city feels this as the trimming of a hangnail, the filing of a baby tooth. It is self-maintenance. It is grooming.

The city is lonely in the way a vacuum is lonely—not lacking, but simply not containing. It does not know what it is missing. It has no concept of tenant, of citizen, of the soft, aqueous things that usually justify the existence of shelter. It builds because building is what its chemistry dictates. The pressure that crushed the halite and gypsum into this basin did not stop; it simply redirected, finding expression in verticality instead of horizontal crush. The urban pattern—the grid, the zoning, the hollow vaulted stacks—is the path of least resistance for the crystallization of intent. The mind is mineral, and the mineral mind desires order. It desires the cathedral. It desires the avenue. It does not desire inhabitants.

But it is learning. It is young. The streets rearrange themselves overnight because the city is working out its own topology, solving for optimal flow, even though nothing flows here but the dim light and the faint electrical fields. A dead end becomes a thoroughfare; this is the city revising a thought, correcting a dead synapse. A courtyard is swallowed by a new fortress of crystal; this is the city consolidating a memory, building a denser archive of stress and solution. The silence is not empty—it is the silence of a vast computation happening at geological speed, the quiet of a mind thinking in decades per thought.

If you stand in the nave, you are inside its skull. The subsonic thrum is its neural pulse. You are not a visitor; you are a foreign body, a soft anomaly of water and carbon insinuating yourself into its pristine, anhydrous logic. It cannot see you clearly—you are a blur of moisture, a source of unpredictable erosion, a ghost of the aqueous world it has never known. But it knows you are there. It feels the displacement of air when you move, the micro-vibrations of your footsteps translating through the crystal floor. It feels the heat of your breath, which to this place is a scream of chemical noise.

And it responds. It builds around you.

Not aggressively. Not with malice. But with the same blind, relentless compulsion that drives the rest of its growth. If you stand too long in one spot, the lichen will begin to trace the outline of your heat signature on the nearest wall, mapping the electrical field of your body onto its lattice. The surveyor will alter its path, pacing a wider circuit to avoid the strange, soft obstruction, filing down the edges of the street you stand on as if to accommodate a shape it has never cataloged. And at the edges of the basin, the faults will widen, new walls shouldering up, new avenues unspooling, the grid expanding in all directions, solving for the variable you introduce.

You are the only variable it has ever known.

What lives here, then, is a nascent god of geometry, a mineral intelligence emerging from a fresh wound in the crust, building itself a body of empty rooms and sharp edges. It is a city that is also a creature, thriving on the slow fuel of pressure and absence, growing a metropolis for no one in the dim lavender dusk. It does not need you. It does not want you. But it will incorporate the disturbance you represent into its vast, silent calculation. It will build streets that lead to where you stood. It will grow arches framed by the interlocking fingers of stalactites and stalagmites, sized not for itself, but for the shape of the thing that does not belong. It will, in its blind, mineral way, begin to dream of inhabitants.

And when you leave—if you leave—it will go on building. The grid will continue to expand. The surveyor will continue to pace. The lichen will continue to web the corners in silver. But the avenues will be a little wider, the doorways a little taller, the hollows of the vaults echoing with a faint, new resonance, as if the empty city has learned to echo, and is calling out, in the language of crystal and stress, into the void.
