To live here is to be a gear.

Not in metaphor. In the precise, mechanical reality of the Perpetuation Engine, every organism is a component, assembled by the same unfathomable process that condensed the cylinders and grew the impossible ferns. They are not creatures who have adapted to an environment; they are features of a closed system that achieved its final, perfect equation. Evolution did not happen here through chance and selection. It happened through *derivation*—the solving of for what function was required, and the extrusion of a form to meet it.

The amphibians—those sleek, dark bodies with their too-large eyes and surplus digits—are the escapement. They sit in the weeping water because they must; their bodies are the interface where the fluid dynamics of the cold pools meet the percussive rhythm of the space. They do not call to mate; they call to keep time. Their voices are those small hammers on anvils because they are physically synchronizing the overflow, breaking the continuous weep of the stone into discrete, manageable drops that feed the tubes without flooding the cylinders. Their too-many fingers are not a mutation; they are the precise fractal tines needed to comb the water into exactly the right tension before it vaporizes. They are the tick in the endless, unvarying tock. 

The arthropods—the segmented, clockwork things moving through the over-large moss—are the governor. They maintain the vegetation. They do not eat; they prune. They move with manufactured precision because they are executing a program of static preservation. They bite through the dark, slick leaves at exactly the juncture where growth would introduce asymmetry, where the recursion of the plant would loop once too often and disrupt the flow of humidity, or shade a pool, or alter the acoustic dampening of a stone shelf. They move in patrol routes that never vary, their tiny mandibles clipping and shaping, ensuring that the lushness remains exactly this lush, forever. They are the reason the dead leaves do not accumulate and the new growth appears without the old having died. They are constantly rewriting the plants, palimpsest-editors keeping the text of the ecology perfectly stable.

The things in the shallow pools, the makers of the too-large V-wakes, are the return springs. They exist in a medium that should not support their mass, moving in a space that mathematically cannot contain them. They are the pressure differential made flesh. They swim in the cold, weeping water and their motion draws the fluid back toward the center, toward the pump intakes, fighting the constant outward spread of the exudation. They are why the water never overflows, why the boundary holds. They are the hidden counterforce, working in the displacement, pulling the system taut from within. 

And the one you do not see. The hidden trace-maker with the circular, coffee-cup hoof and the geometric bite. That is the inspector. The quality control. It is the mind of the space, if the space can be said to have a mind, which is only true in the sense that a thermostat has a mind. It exists in a different mode of occupancy—a mode that is not biological but operational. It steps once to test the substrate, leaving a print that should crack the young stone, and when the stone does not crack, it is satisfied, and it ceases to occupy the physical coordinates. It moves through the folded space at the perimeter, walking the boundary that is not a wall, ensuring the softening of reality stays soft, ensuring the fog refuses to extend. It crops the vegetation with blade-like precision not to eat, but to check the tensile strength of the stems, to measure the humidity by the clean severing of the fibers. It is the ghost in the machine, patrolling the edges of the loop to ensure the loop does not unravel. 

They do not know they are gears. This is the mercy of their existence, if mercy can exist in a system without gradient. They feel hunger, and they satisfy it by performing their function. They feel the cold, and they press against the weeping stone. They feel the urge to mate, and they call out in their mechanical voices, and the sound keeps the Engine on rhythm, and the Engine keeps the water rising, and the water keeps the stone weeping, and the stone keeps the vegetation growing, and the vegetation keeps the ar%thropods pruning, and the pruning keeps the amphibians fed, and the feeding keeps them calling, and the call keeps the time, and the time does not move.

They are the inhabitants of a completed world. They have no future because a completed thing needs no future. They have no past because they were never anything other than this. They do not age, because wear is a function of time, and time is a function of gradient, and there are no gradients left. The young stone stays young because the creatures who live on it do not erode it; they perpetuate it. The wet rock keeps weeping because they keep the pores open. The Engine does not break down because they are its lubrication, its sensing mechanism, its feedback loop rendered in slick, dark flesh and too many digits.

When you step toward the perimeter and find yourself walking back toward the center without having turned around, it is not the space that is rejecting you. It is the creatures. It is the closed system defending its equilibrium. You are a gradient. You are an outside variable. You carry the chaos of decay and growth and change in your cells, and the system can smell it on you, the way you smell the burnt-water scent of the forever-teakettle. The things in the water alter their V-wakes to create a current that turns your ankles. The arthropods adjust their pruning to shift the acoustic profile of the moss, subliminally suggesting the direction of the center. The hidden inspector walks beside you in the folded space, nudging the boundary inward, ensuring the fog thickens just as your legs slow. 

They do not do this with malice. They do not do this with thought. They do this the way the heart pumps blood—because the system demands it, because the equation balances only when the interior is uncontaminated by the exterior, because the Engine must run and the Engine is the world and the world is them. 

To live in the Perpetuation Engine is to be perfectly, eternally useful. It is to be a phrase in a sentence that can never be rewritten, speaking a sound that can never be altered, in a room that can never end, under a light that has no source and casts no shadows. It is to be the answer to a question that was never asked, holding the shape of a purpose that was never chosen, in a cathedral of wet black stone where the only god is the process, and the process is forever, and forever is exactly as loud as it is right now.
