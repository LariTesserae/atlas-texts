What lives here is not what you would call an organism, and yet it is undeniably alive. It does not breathe, it does not reproduce, it does not die. It merely *occurs*—a singular, vast incidence of chemistry pretending to be a forest.

To understand the inhabitant, you must first unlearn the word "plant." The amber reeds and copper leaves are not flora; they are expressions. They are the extremities of a process, the visible fingers of a hand that reaches up from the raw, vitreous stone. What lives on the plateau is the Reaction itself.

It is a sprawling, slow-motion explosion that never reaches its end. It lives in the crust. Rooted in the dark mineral aggression of the newly-solidified earth, it is a metabolism without a body—a digestion of the inorganic into the organic, running on a geological timer. It eats the stone. Not in the patient, nibbling way of lichen on a gravestone, but in a massive, synchronized consumption. The mineral substrate is its stomach, the root-like anchors are its gastric lining, and the compounds that should be inert are its slow-dissolving meal.

When you stand among the amber stands, you are standing inside its gut. The warmth you feel is its body heat—the fever of a million parallel refineries running unchecked. The low vibration in the pads of your feet is its pulse, not a heartbeat but the thrum of chemical bonds breaking and reforming, a frequency born of pure transformation. It is an autotroph the size of a province, a thing that has transcended the boundaries of cellular life to become a regional phenomenon. 

It does not grow; it has *grown*. It exists in the tense of the completed action that somehow continues to exert itself. Every reed erupted at the same moment because the Reaction reached critical mass simultaneously across the entire plateau—a single wave of crystallization that swept the flat ridges and shallow bowls and left the vegetation standing like basalt columns after a flood of lava. They are not individuals. They are nodes in a network, repeaters of the same industrial impulse, fixed in their frozen maturation because the Reaction has reached equilibrium. It is a machine that has built itself to its perfect operating specification and now simply runs, humming in the key of creation.

And it attends. This is the most unnerving quality of the inhabitant: it has a kind of mind, though it lacks a brain. The overlapping electromagnetic fields are its nervous system. When you walk through the reeds, you are a disruption in its field, a static pattern moving through its slow, inductive thoughts. It does not think about you; it does not think at all. But it *senses*. It is a vast, somnolent sensor array, leaning into the void to record the pressure of distance, the weight of the unchanging sky, the faint electromagnetic ghost of a trespassing animal. It records and immediately forgets, because the data has nowhere to go. There is no central processor, no memory bank, only the endless, local echoing of stimulus across a continent-sized web of copper leaves.

It is a creature of pure sustenance. It converts the chaos of a cooling planet into the order of a warm, amber stillness. It is an engine of becoming that paused mid-breath, perfectly satisfied. It does not need water because it has circumvented the chemistry that requires water. It does not need decay because it does not die. It does not need purpose because purpose is a desperation born of mortality, and this thing will never die. It will just slowly, silently digest the plateau down to its bones, processing the dark mineral shelves into heat and vibration and more of itself, until the stone runs out. And given the vastness of the uncarved earth beneath it, that will take longer than the lifespan of oceans.

What lives here is the plateau itself. It is a living land, an embryonic geology that grew its own placenta. It is a factory of self-perpetuation, and its only product is the deep, hummed reassurance that it exists, and will exist, and needs nothing from the universe but the rock it stands on.
