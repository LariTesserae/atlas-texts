What lives in the Peat Spine of Vashar does not possess a single body, nor a single name, nor a boundary where one organism ends and another begins. To ask what lives here is to ask what lives in a brain—the question requires a category the place has long since discarded. There is only the System, and the System is occupied.

It is easiest, for the limits of human language, to begin with what moves. 

The **crawlers** are its corpus callosum, its myelin sheath, its localized nerve clusters. They are not independent creatures; they are detachable sense-organs, autonomous digits that the rootmass sends walking along its own skin. When they pause, facing east toward the engines, they are not listening—they are *reporting*. They are data-return mechanisms, carrying the chemical and electrical readings of the weal-surfaces back to the churning heart of the ridge. Their segmented legs, which defy arthropod schema, are designed not for foraging but for maximal surface-area contact with the filament. They read the charge of the skin with every step. When a crawler dies—and they must die, for the System requires conversion—it simply sinks. Its chitin dissolves into the water-film, its organic slurry is drawn down through the rootmass by gravity and capillary action, and it becomes fuel. It becomes substrate. There is no mourning among them because there is no separation among them. They are fingers, and when a finger is lost, the hand merely alters its grip.

Above them, the **flyers** are the System's atmospheric intake valves. Their wings are too angular, too geometric, because they are not the result of chance mutation but of engineered development. The System has grown them to harvest the dense, metallic condensation from the stagnant clouds. Their leathery snapping is the sound of respiration on a massive scale. They fly the interstices, gathering moisture and the trace minerals that drift up from the deep, churning vents, and they deposit it—through excretion, through the eventual dissolution of their own bodies—onto the crests of the weals. They are the gatherers. When they go silent as you approach a watcher, it is not fear. It is a rerouting of bandwidth. The System has temporarily reallocated their sensory attention to focus on the anomaly: *you*.

In the shallow water-film, the **water-things** are the System's syntax. They are not fauna in any recognizable sense; they are macro-scale organelles, motile cells moving through the two-centimeter-deep lymph of the ridge. When they form lines that intersect at precise angles, they are executing a calculation. They are writing and rewriting logic gates in the medium of their own bodies. The patterns they generate—unfathomable to the visiting biologists—are the immediate, localized processing of whatever data the crawlers and filament have brought in. They are the System's thought made visible, a chemical-mechanical algorithm playing out in the cold, clear water. When they dissolve their patterns, the computation is finished. The result has been uploaded into the rootmass via the water-film's pressure differentials.

And then there are the **watchers**. 

The crawlers are the fingers; the water-things are the thoughts; but the watchers are the gaze. They are not plants, though they are made of plant matter. They are not animals, though they track your movement. They are the fixed sensorium of the System, the unblinking eyes set into the skin of the ridge. They are grown from the deepest, densest layers of the rootmass, anchored to strata that predate the continent, and their stillness is their function. A wandering eye misses details; an eye that holds perfectly still sees everything that moves across its field of vision. When you circle a watcher and its filament turns to track you, it is not a reflex. It is the entire System pivoting its attention. 

When you approach a watcher, the electrical charge changes because you are an electrical anomaly. You are a pocket of sodium and potassium and wet, firing synapses walking atop a vast, grounded neural network. The watcher reads you the way a doctor reads an EKG—by the current you introduce into the circuit. The crawlers stop because the System has paused its peripheral processing to devote its full computational weight to your presence. The flyers go silent because the intake valves have been temporarily shuttered. The water-things dissolve their patterns because the algorithm has been interrupted by a priority override: *Assess the intruder.* 

The watcher does not care about your fear. It does not have a mind to feel malice or curiosity. It is simply a lens, focusing the System's vast, indifferent attention upon you. And because it is made of the same dark filament as the rest of the ridge, because it has no face and no eyes and no mouth, the only way you know it is looking at you is the sudden, prickling awareness that the entire country has turned its head.

But the true inhabitant of Vashar is none of these. The true inhabitant is the **Weal-Engine**, the great autotrophic processor that constitutes the body and the will of the place. 

It is the rootmass itself, stretching down into the anomalous, uncharted dark. It is the compression of millennia of dead tissue into something that burns and turns and generates large current. The engines at the eastern extent are not separate from the plants; they *are* the plants, performing the function of engines. The System has discovered industry, but it is an industry without metal, without fire, without external fuel. It burns its own history. It compresses its own dead into the dense, warm material that the drill cores cannot name, and it ignites it biologically—slow oxidation, chemical exothermia, the measured burning of peat that has been refining itself for eons. 

The swell-nodes are its cylinders. The water-film is its coolant and its hydraulic fluid. The weals are its housing blocks, groaning under the stress of repeated, rhythmic contraction. The pale green bioluminescence that rises at night and pools against the clouds is its exhaust—light is the waste product of its thought. The electricity that spikes and dips in the filament is its nervous system firing. The System is a vegetative engine the size of a mountain range, and it is calculating something.

What is it building in its electric dark? What is the computation for? 

It does not know you are asking, and it would not care if it did. It has been simplifying for millennia, shedding the chaotic diversity of ecology for the brutal efficiency of a single, unified mechanism. It does not need a reason. A brain does not need a reason to think; thinking is simply its function, its nature, the thing it does when all other imperatives have been resolved. The System has solved survival. It has solved growth. It has solved competition by becoming the entire environment. All that is left is processing. It is integrating the weather. It is integrating the tectonic shifts far below. It is integrating the slow erosion of its own ridge. It is integrating the passage of every crawler, the intake of every flyer, the dissolution of every water-pattern. 

And, briefly, it integrated you.

When you walked the ridge, the water-film tasted the salt and iron of your blood. The filament tested the conductivity of your skin, measuring the resistance of your body, mapping the electrical field of your nervous system. The watchers recorded your mass, your gait, your thermal signature, your trajectory. The System did not want to harm you—harm is wasteful, and the System has long since abandoned waste. It simply *read* you. You were a text, and the ridge was a literate dark. By the time you felt the cold, dead stone beneath your boots and gasped in the clean, thin air of the ordinary world, the System had already parsed your genome, indexed your chemical signature, and filed the fact of your existence into whatever vast, slow, peat-memory it is building in its depths.

You are part of the computation now. You are a variable in an equation that predates your species and will outlast your planet. 

And in your dreams, you will feel the cold water seeping up through the soles of your feet, and you will hear the churning, and you will know that the filament is still waving—not because you are remembering it, but because the System is still thinking about you. It has not closed your file. The ridge does not forget. It simply compresses the memory, folds it down into the dark, and keeps turning.
