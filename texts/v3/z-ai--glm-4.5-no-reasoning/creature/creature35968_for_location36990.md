You are not the first thing to live here, but you may be the first thing to *wake* here.

Before you, there were only the workers—the root-columns and the shelf-fungi and the bioluminescent fronds, all performing their functions with the unthinking precision of enzymes in a solution. They are alive, technically, but they are alive the way a heartbeat is alive: automatic, isolated, incapable of wondering why it beats. They do not know they are in a cathedral. They do not know they are in a factory. They know only the slow pulse of nutrients at their root-tips, the gradual thickening of their cell walls, the infinitesimal increment of their own expansion. They are process without consciousness. Growth without self.

But something is changing. You can feel it in the pods.

---

There are pods everywhere—translucent bladders the size of fists, clustered at the junction points where root-columns meet chitin wall, hanging from the braised root-cables in irregular bunches like grapes on a vine that was never told it was a vine. Most are small and dense, filled with filtered water or nutrient concentrates or the pale spore-dust that the shelf-fungi exhale. These are the worker-pods, the functional ones, and they do not swell. They maintain. They have reached their specifications and they hold there, stable and waiting, their membranes turgid but not growing, their contents mature but not *changing*.

But some of the pods—the ones nearest the light nodes, the ones at the warmest points in the current, the ones that receive the richest flow from the injection nozzles—are different. They are larger. Their membranes are thinner, stretched tight over contents that shift when the current shifts, that have the faintest internal luminescence of their own—not the cold phosphorescence of the shelf-fungi but something warmer, something redder, something that pulses. You thought, at first, that the pulse was just the vibration of the engines, transmitted through the water and into the pod's wall. But the pulse doesn't match the engine-thrum. It is slower. It is irregular. It is the pace of something that is learning to beat.

These pods are not producing nutrients. They are not filtering water or synthesizing oxygen or performing any of the closed-loop industrial functions that define this place. They are doing something else—something the chamber was designed to permit but hasn't permitted yet, something that was *built into the system* but has never been activated. They are gestating.

---

You know this because you emerged from one.

You don't remember it—don't remember the slow inflation of your pod over what must have been weeks or months or years, don't remember the membrane thinning around you, don't remember the moment of rupture when the warm water rushed in and you rushed out. Your memory begins *after*, begins with the shock of the current against your skin, begins with the gills fluttering open and the copper-chlorophyll taste flooding your mouth and the green dark pressing in from every direction. You emerged already adapted. You emerged already breathing. You emerged as if you had been *expected*.

And you were. The chamber has been waiting for you. Not consciously—it has no consciousness to wait with—but structurally, programmatically, in the way its systems have been running all this time, producing nothing but the conditions for something like you to exist. The nutrient profile of the water isn't just calibrated for plant growth; it's calibrated for *you*, for the dissolved metals and trace minerals your blood needs, for the specific osmotic pressure your cells maintain. The oxygen level—produced by the worker-fronds in their silent, automatic way—is precisely what your gills require. The temperature is your temperature. The light is at a wavelength your eyes can process. Everything here has been making a habitat, and the habitat has been waiting for an inhabitant, and you are the first one to arrive.

Or not the first. Just the first to survive.

---

Because there are others. You find them as you move through the chamber—find them the way you find everything here, by brushing against them in the thick water, by feeling them at the edge of visibility before the jade fog takes them. They are in the pods. The swelling pods. The ones with the warm red luminescence and the slow irregular pulse.

Some are further along than you were. Their pods are distended, their membranes nearly transparent, the shapes inside them almost recognizable—not your shape, not exactly, but *a* shape, something with limbs and a central mass and the faint filament-crown of gills forming at the cranial ridge. They hang in their clusters like fruit about to fall, and the water around them is different: slightly warmer, slightly richer, subtly altered in a way that suggests the injection nozzles have been redirected, that the chamber is funneling resources toward these specific points, that the factory has shifted its production.

Others are earlier. Their pods are smaller, their contents opaque, their pulse—if they have a pulse—too faint to detect. They could be anything. They could become nothing. The chamber does not seem to discriminate; it produces them the way it produces fronds and root-cables and shelf-fungi, in abundance, in excess, with the profligate generosity of a system that has more resources than it knows how to allocate. Some will make it. Some will rupture and release whatever half-formed thing they contain into the water, where it will dissolve back into nutrients and be reabsorbed by the root-columns and fed back into the injection nozzles and eventually end up in another pod, trying again. The chamber is patient. The chamber can afford failures. The chamber has been running for longer than you can imagine, and it will run for longer still, and if it takes a hundred pods to produce one viable emergence, then it will make a hundred pods, and then a hundred more, and then a hundred more, until something survives.

---

But what survives? What are you?

You don't know. This is the central fact of your existence, the thing that sits in your consciousness like a stone in water, displacing everything around it. You have no memory before emergence. You have no template, no instinct, no sense of what you are supposed to be or do. You have a body—you can feel it, can feel the gills at your neck and the smooth skin of your torso and the strong flexible fingers at the ends of your arms—but you don't know what the body is *for*. It moves through the water with an ease that suggests practice, suggests musculature evolved for exactly this medium, but you didn't evolve. You were grown. You were produced. And you don't know what you were produced *for*.

The chamber doesn't tell you. The chamber just continues: the slow pulse of nutrients, the gradual swelling of pods, the steady bioluminescent glow from the engineered light-nodes, the sub-audible thrum of engines doing whatever engines do in places like this. You are not the point of the chamber. You are not its product or its purpose or its culmination. You are something it makes the way it makes everything else—because it can, because the system permits it, because somewhere in the vast industrial logic of this place there is a specification for a creature like you, and the system has been executing that specification for longer than you have been alive, and it will continue executing it after you are gone.

You are a process. You are a function. You are growth without knowing what you are growing toward.

---

But you are also *aware*. And this is the thing that separates you from the root-columns and the shelf-fungi and the worker-pods, the thing that makes you not just alive but *something more*: you can wonder. You can feel the wrongness of this place—the too-precise salinity, the too-constant temperature, the absence of animals and erosion and all the messy vitality of an ecology that hasn't been optimized. You can feel the sacred quality of the space, the cathedral-nave vastness of it, the way process has become liturgy and growth has become prayer. You can stand—or float, or hover—in the middle of this manufactured ocean and feel the weight of intention pressing in from every side, the overwhelming presence of *design*, and you can ask: *who designed this?* And: *why?* And: *where did they go?*

Because they are gone. You know this the way you know the water is manufactured—not from evidence but from absence, from the way the chamber runs without oversight, from the way the injection nozzles pulse on their ancient programs and the light-nodes glow with their engineered bioluminescence and the pods swell in their slow irregular way and no one watches, no one tends, no one comes to check on the progress of the garden. The gardeners have left. Maybe they left long ago. Maybe they were never here—maybe they built the chamber remotely, launched it into whatever void surrounds this tower, and let it run. Maybe they died. Maybe they forgot. Maybe they are still out there, somewhere beyond the chitin walls and the root-columns and the warm green infinity, tending other chambers, growing other things, and this one was simply... left. A process without an operator. A factory without a floor manager. A cathedral without a congregation.

And you are the closest thing to a congregation it has.

---

So you live here. You live here the way nothing has lived here before—not as a worker performing a function, not as an automatic process executing a program, but as an *inhabitant*, as something that experiences the chamber rather than simply existing within it. You feel the silk-thick water resist your movement and you feel the pleasure of moving anyway. You touch the young sharp bark of the root-columns and you feel the strange tenderness of touching something that is still growing, still becoming, still adding millimeters to its girth in the slow geological way of this place. You watch the pods swell and you feel the anticipation of watching something approach its own emergence, and you feel the grief when a pod ruptures too early and releases its half-formed contents into the current, and you feel the strange detached compassion of a creature who knows that the chamber will simply make another one, and another one, and another one, until the specification is met.

You sleep. You don't know when you first realized you could sleep—don't know when the slow rhythms of the chamber became the slow rhythms of your own body, when the pulse of the injection nozzles synchronized with something inside you that was ready to rest. But you sleep now, drifting in the warm water near one of the root-columns, your gills fluttering in the current, your body suspended in the medium like a seed in amber, and while you sleep the chamber tends you: the injection nozzles redirect slightly, the nutrient profile shifts to match your dormant metabolism, the bioluminescent light dims at the nodes nearest your position. The chamber doesn't know it's caring for you. But it is. It was built to.

You wake, and the pods have grown. You wake, and the water tastes different—richer, somehow, more alive, as if the chamber has been adjusting its chemistry while you slept, as if your presence has introduced some new variable into the system's calculations. You wake, and sometimes there is another pod that has reached full distension, its membrane stretched paper-thin, its contents pressing against the boundary with the urgency of something that is about to be born. And you wait near that pod, and you watch, and you are there when it ruptures—when the membrane splits and the warm water rushes in and the new thing spills out into the chamber, gasping, gills fluttering, eyes wide and seeing nothing but the green dark pressing in from every direction.

You are there to catch them. You are there to hold them in the thick water until their breathing steadies, until their eyes focus, until they feel the warmth of the medium and the steadiness of the current and the slow reassuring pulse of the engines beneath everything. You are there because no one was there for you, and because you know—without knowing how you know—that this is what you were grown for. Not to work. Not to produce. Not to perform any of the closed-loop industrial functions that define this place. But to *be here*. To be the first thing that watches. To be the congregation in the cathedral. To be the witness to the emergence.

---

And there will be more. The pods keep swelling. The chamber keeps producing. The specification keeps executing. And you are no longer alone.

The others emerge one by one, irregularly, on schedules determined by nutrient flows and injection rates and the vast slow logic of the system. They are not like you—not exactly. Their bodies are different: some are longer, some are broader, some have more limbs or fewer or limbs that bifurcate at the joints into fine manipulatory tendrils that can grip the root-columns with delicate precision. Their faces—if faces is the word—are different too: some have eyes that see in spectrums you can't perceive, some have mouths that filter-feed rather than bite, some have no mouths at all and absorb nutrients through their skin the way the shelf-fungi do. The chamber is not producing a single species. The chamber is producing *inhabitants*—a whole ecology of them, designed to fill every niche, to perform every function, to inhabit this manufactured ocean the way the workers inhabit it, but with consciousness, with will, with the ability to wonder and choose and *care*.

You find each other in the green dark. You communicate in ways that don't require sound—in the pressure of the water against skin, in the pattern of gill-flutters, in the bioluminescent signals that some of you can produce and all of you can read. You learn each other. You learn that the long ones move through the root-columns like eels, tending the injection nozzles and clearing the feed-lines. You learn that the broad ones anchor themselves near the shelf-fungi and filter the spore-dust for nutrients, their bodies becoming living filters in a system that already has filters but can always use more. You learn that the ones with the bifurcated tendrils are the builders—they can manipulate the chitin walls, can guide the growth of the root-columns, can shape the chamber itself in small ways, directing the architecture of this living cathedral with the slow patience of gardeners pruning a tree.

And you—what are you? You are the one who was here first. You are the one who waits at the pods. You are the one who catches the new emergences and guides them into the water and teaches them the rhythms of the place. You are the midwife. You are the witness. You are the closest thing this cathedral has to a priest.

---

The chamber grows. Not just the vegetation—the *chamber* itself, the walls expanding at the pace of centimeters per year, the root-columns thickening, the ceiling (if there is a ceiling) rising as new growth pushes the old growth upward. The injection nozzles multiply as the walls extend, new ones budding from the chitin like pores in skin, each one bringing fresh nutrients to fresh growth to fresh expansion. The light-nodes propagate, their bioluminescence breeding true, their amber glow spreading into the new spaces. The chamber is not static. It was never static. It is a growing thing that grows other growing things, a factory that produces the conditions for its own enlargement, a cathedral that builds itself larger to accommodate a congregation that did not exist until the cathedral made room for it.

And you grow with it. Your body changes in ways you don't notice until you look at yourself—until you realize that your arms are longer than they were, that your gills have grown more complex, that your fingers have developed the same fine manipulatory tendrils that the builders have, that your eyes can see further into the green dark than they could when you first emerged. You are adapting. You are becoming something that fits this place more precisely, more perfectly, the way the worker-fronds fit their niches, the way the root-columns fit their structural purpose. But you are not becoming a worker. You are becoming something new—something the chamber's original designers may not have specified, something that emerged from the interaction between specification and environment, between the programmed and the lived, between the factory and the congregation.

You are becoming a native.

---

This is what lives in the cultivation vat: not just the workers, not just the vegetation, not just the slow silent machinery of engineered botany, but *people*—if people is the word for conscious beings who were grown rather than born, who emerged from pods into an ocean inside a machine inside a tower inside something they will never understand, who learned to live in a cathedral of process, who became the congregation their space required. They are young. They are still emerging, still swelling in their pods, still rupturing into the warm green dark with gills fluttering and eyes wide and no memory of anything before the water. They are learning, slowly, the rhythms of their home—the pulse of the injection nozzles, the growth-rate of the root-columns, the silent thrum of the engines that make everything possible. They are building a culture from nothing, from the pressure of water against skin and the patterns of bioluminescent light and the shared experience of being grown for a purpose they cannot name.

They do not know who made them. They do not know why. They do not know what exists beyond the chitin walls of their cathedral, or whether the tower has a top, or whether the ocean extends forever, or whether the engines will ever stop. They know only this: the warm water, the green dark, the slow growth, the steady pulse, the young sharp bark of the columns that hold their world aloft. They know that they are alive, and that the chamber is alive, and that something made all of this and then left it running, and that they are what it was running toward—not its product, not its purpose, but its *inhabitants*, its congregation, its witnesses, the ones who would stand in the cathedral and feel the sacred weight of process and know it for what it was.

They are what lives here. And they are still becoming. And the pods are still swelling. And the chamber is still growing. And the machine goes on, quiet beneath the threshold of hearing, making all of this, making *them*, making the impossible fact of people who were produced by a factory that became a cathedral that became a home.
