To live in the Pelagic Plateau is to be a contradiction made flesh, an occupant of a world that has outlived the logic of its own creation. Here, at the terminus of erosion, where the Ancient Mat hangs in the water column like a frozen breath, life is not a flourish but a persistence—an act of quiet, suffocating defiance against a physics that has grown exhausted.

**The Leviathans: Ghosts of the Thermal Seams**
The fauna of the Plateau are not creatures in any vital, struggling sense; they are living weather, solitary archons of the deep dim. With a fauna rating of merely 0.6, they are statistically negligible, yet physically dominant—a paradox made of gelatin and cartilage. They are the *Aerial Benthics*, the *Slow-Tonals*, the *Mat-Ghosts*. 

To picture one is to picture a malfunction in the fog. They possess no eyes; vision was abandoned eons ago in the umber suffocation. They have no jaws, no predatory appendages, no organs for conflict. They are immense, filter-feeding nets of translucent flesh, drifting along the invisible thermal architectures like glaciers moving through a sky-sea. Their bodies are ragged, sprawling architectures of mucus and membrane, designed to intercept the constant, warm rain of decaying fungal matter and ambient chemosynthetic dust. 

They feed by simply existing, opening their vast, cartilaginous frames to the current. They are living lungs that never breathe air, only the thick, warm deluge. When the stratification shatters and the rain falls upward, they invert, drifting toward the ceiling of the world with a terrifying, quiet grace, their mass briefly caught in a localized inversion of negative buoyancy. They feel no panic; the concepts of "up" and "down" dissolved for them long before they dissolved for the physics of the Plateau itself. They are solitary to the point of tragedy. Two Leviathans might drift within a hundred meters of one another for a century, separated by a pocket of stretching space, never making contact, never acknowledging the other's existence. They are too massive to need each other, too sparse to compete, too old to remember loneliness. They are the last breaths of an ecosystem that consumed all its dynamism and settled into eternal, autotrophic digestion.

**The Saturated Flora: The Breath-Walls**
If the Leviathans are the ghosts, the vegetation is the machine. At a staggering density of 2.4, the forest is not a collection of individuals but a single, contiguous organism—a high-saturation thicket where the concept of the individual is a mathematical fiction. 

The inhabitants of this thicket do not grow; they *occlude*. They are towering, semi-translucent analogs of fungus and thermosynthetic frond, their stalks thick and warm to the touch, pulsing with the slow, planetary rhythm of the geothermal exhalations. To move through them is not to walk or swim, but to *be swallowed*. The medium is so dense, the flora so crowded, that navigation requires pressing between the fleshy, warm membranes of the stalks, squeezing through gaps that contract and expand with the thermal breath of the Mat. 

Their "leaves" are massive, veined parachutes of wet, translucent tissue—gas-bladders that swell with the heat of the deep dim. They are designed to trap the sickly luminescence of the ambient heat, bloating with warm gases before slowly releasing them in a soft, wet clicking sound, the only rhythm in the near-total silence. When a stalk dies, it does not fall; the vertical axis is merely a suggestion here. Instead, it collapses outward, sideways, or upward, dissolving into a warm slurry that is immediately reabsorbed by the touching membranes of its neighbors. The forest is a perpetual engine of consumption and decay, a self-sustaining closed loop of flesh and water, breathing in the warm deluge and exhaling the quiet, wet clicks of its own metabolism.

**The Traces: The Pre-Hand and the Scars**
And then there are those who *tried* to live here—tried to endure, to build, to assert a geometry against the sagging, porous reality. They are gone, leaving only a built environment of 0.8, a faint haunting of the Ancient Mat. 

Whoever they were, they predated the hand; their tool-use is a mere 0.5, a stuttering, mineral logic that operated without grip or strike. They left no structures, only *shapes*—grooves carved into the vitrified stone of the Mat, perfectly smooth, utterly pre-tool. They are geometric scars: parabolic channels, interlocking spirals, and long, parallel notches that reject weathering, enduring long after their makers dissolved into the warm water. 

Were they the ancient, ancestral spores of the current fungal forest, leaving fossilized burrows as they calcified into the Mat? Were they a mineral crystallization, a self-organizing logic of the chitin and agate, the Mat attempting to speak its own name in the dark? Or were they something that swam—soft, boneless things that pressed their faces against the stone, trying to find a purchase in a world without a floor?

They must have been desperate for orientation. In a world where the vertical axis fails, where space stretches and compresses, where the horizon corkscrews and distances lie, the act of carving a straight line into the Ancient Mat is an act of sanity. Those smooth, geometric scars are screams of frustration against the weirdness of 2.8, an attempt to impose a local, stable geometry on a space that has absorbed too much water and become sagging and porous. They carved lines to remember what direction was. They carved lines because they were lost. 

And then they dissolved. The warm water, the suffocating density, the quiet engine of the forest—it absorbed them without ceremony. Their flesh became the slurry that feeds the gas-bladders; their structures smoothed into the vitrified stone. They are not remembered by the Leviathans, who have no memory, nor by the forest, which has no mind. They exist only as scars on the oldest stable state matter in the universe, faint hauntings in the deep dim, waiting for a sun that will never rise.
