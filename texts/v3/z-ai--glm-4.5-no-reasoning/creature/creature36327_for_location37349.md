Here, at the terminus of geological time, the inhabitants are not residents in any way a human mind would recognize. They do not possess sovereignty. They do not pay rent, hold territory, or dream of elsewhere. There is no elsewhere. The Weeping Ward is a closed circuit of wet bio-computation, and to live here is to be a sub-routine within a dying, primordial processor. The inhabitants are differentiated only by their function within the cycle—some are the logic, some are the memory, some are the garbage collection, and some are the glitch. 

Take the Dripping Scribes. They are the closest thing this place has to a citizenry, though they lack anything resembling an internal life. They dwell within the hollow boles, clinging to the sweating interior walls with pad-like feet that have fused seamlessly into the xylem channels. They are vertical, amphibian-like beings, though "amphibian" is a gross oversimplification of a creature engineered to interface with plant circuitry. Their skin is a living membrane, translucent and perpetually slick with sap, through which you can see the slow, peristaltic pulse of data-encoded proteins moving through their own modified lymphatic systems. They do not eat. They do not breathe in any terrestrial sense. They simply *transcribe*. As the ward weeps its data-laden sap from the pore-ports, the Scribes absorb it, their bodies acting as biological buffers—smoothing the chaotic surges of the ward's ancient calculations before dripping the refined information down to the root-wattle below. They are motionless for weeks at a time, frozen in the humid gloom, their eyes long since atrophied into blind, gray dents. Their only movement is a slow, involuntary shudder when a heavy computational cycle forces a surplus of hot sap through their veins. When a bole violently dissolves, the Scribes within it dissolve too, their programmed flesh unmaking itself in seconds, leaving only a warm, enzymatic broth that feeds the raft. They are the ward's memory palaces, and they are wiped clean as easily as a hard drive.

Between the boles, in the vital emptiness where the spores drift like static snow, live the Treaders. If the Scribes are the memory, the Treaders are the immune system made manifest in the macro. The slug-like grazers mentioned in the ward's anatomy are merely their larval or drone-states—mindless, dog-sized slugs that ooze across the rhizome floor, absorbing chemical errors. But when an error is too large—when a computation spawns a cancerous growth, a rogue thorn-mass that computes nothing but its own expansion—the slugs congregate. They pile upon one another, their semi-permeable skins melting together in the humid air, secreting a fast-acting enzymatic resin. In minutes, they fuse into a Treader: a larger, asymmetrical mass of combined tissue, sprouting temporary pseudopods and a shared, churning digestive cavity. A Treader has no brain, only a concentrated swarm of chemical triggers that drive it toward the anomaly. It engulfs the rogue growth, digesting it even as it is digested, a violent act of auto-immune consumption that leaves the Treader as a smoking, ulcerous patch on the root-wattle. Within hours, the Treader's purpose is spent; it fissions back into its component slugs, which scatter into the weeping mist, their skins already repairing, already absorbing the next low-level error from the damp floor. They are the white blood cells of the city, performing apoptosis on a civic scale.

Above them all drift the Glassy Swarms—the insectoid micro-drones that act as the ward's neurotransmitters. To stand still in the Weeping Ward is to feel them: a faint, granular friction against the skin, like being caught in a drift of warm, wet sand. Individually, a drone is a sliver of chitin and wet protein, no larger than a grain of rice, its wings a lattice of crystallized sap that refracts the gray light into momentary, scattered prisms. They move with terrifying, mechanical precision, landing on a sap-port, inserting a hypodermic proboscis, and transferring a protein-packet before lifting off again in a spiral ascent. They communicate not by pheromone, but by brief, bioluminescent flashes—flickers of pale green light that ripple across the swarm in vast, sweeping waves, mapping the ward's processing states in real-time. When the ward cycles into a heavy computational load, the swarms condense, thickening the air into a glittering, humming fog that obscures the boles. When the ward idles, they scatter, drifting out to the edges of the raft to pollinate the data-leaves. They are numerous beyond counting, a living dust that binds the sparse grove into a single, breathing organism.

And then there are the Churn-Born. They are not a species. They are a consequence. They are the artifacts of the ward's desperate, failing attempts to model the unknowable. When a fresh bole erupts from the root-wattle in seconds, computing some new, vast variable, the sheer density of bioluminescent calculation within its walls generates a localized heat and a burst of complex chemical exhaust. The exhaust condenses in the humid air, and for a brief, brilliant moment, it forms the Churn-Born. They are fleeting, spectral geometries—shapes that hang in the mist between the erupting and dissolving boles. Sometimes they are humanoid, a shimmering silhouette of condensed vapor and ozone that reaches outward with hollow, weeping gestures. Sometimes they are vast, fractal nets of light and moisture that span the gap between three boles, vibrating with the intensity of the calculation that birthed them. They possess no mass, no continuity, and no mind. They are simply the ward's thoughts made briefly visible—epiphanies that last only as long as it takes for the ward to reject the computation. 

When the bole dissolves, the Churn-Born pop. They vanish with a wet, imploding sigh, leaving behind only a shower of warm water and the sharp tang of ozone. To witness a Churn-Born is to witness the Weeping Ward in the throes of existential failure. They are the closest thing to ghosts in a city that has outlived the very concept of death, echoes of a processing architecture trying to model a universe it no longer has the structural integrity to understand.

Together, they do not form a community. They form an anatomy. They simply persist, processing, weeping, and waiting for the groundwater to finally rise high enough to short-circuit the last logic gate, and return the Weeping Ward to the quiet, uncomputing earth from which it grew.
