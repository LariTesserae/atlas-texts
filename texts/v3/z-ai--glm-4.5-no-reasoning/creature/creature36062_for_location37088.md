They are the keystrokes of a vegetal mainframe, a writhing, blind keyboard made of flesh and mandible.

It is an entity of overlapping, translucent scales and swollen, organ-like pillars, but to call it a plant is to mistake a supercomputer for a vine. Its "leaves" are the quantum gateways embedded in its cell walls; its "roots" are the nanoscale latices threading the weeping sap. It does not grow toward light; it grows toward problem-solving. Every droplet that oozes from the raw, unconsolidated clay is a query; the chilled water running down its membranes in synchronized, glacial tears is the answer, processed and wept back out into the hollow.

The Violet Pulsar is its heartbeat and its clock cycle. That bruised, faint luminescence is not mere bioluminescence, but the visual bleed of billions of quantum state collapses happening simultaneously within the fungal walls. It is a thought happening in hardware made of slime and cellulose. An hour to brighten; an hour to dim. And it is building. The hollow is not a home; it is a chrysalis. The raw geology—still soft, still embryonic—is not the ground, but the aliment. The Calculating Rhizome is slowly consuming the planet from the inside out, not with acid or heat, but with the quiet, terrifying patience of a system optimizing its own hardware.
