# The Perpetuation Engine

It fits inside a cathedral. That is the first disorienting thing. A landscape — a whole environmental system of water, vegetation, creatures, and atmosphere — contained within a space no larger than a basilica's nave, yet it does not end where the walls should be. There are no walls. There is only the condition of boundary: a softening of reality at the edges, a dimming into a fog that is not fog but the simple refusal of the space to extend further. Step toward the perimeter and the ground continues, the plants continue, but your legs slow, your vision hazes, and you find yourself walking back toward the center without having turned around. The space folds. It is its own world, and it is the size of a grand room, and both of these facts are true at once.

The ground is young. That is the second thing. The stone — if it is stone — has the sharpness of recent fracture, edges that haven't had time to round, surfaces that still hold the memory of breaking. It is a dark rock, almost black, shot through with veins of something that gleams dully like wet graphite. The formations are abrupt: shelves and steps and small cliffs that rise and fall without the gentling of age, without the patient reshaping that erosion gives over millennia. There has been no millennia. There has been only the process, and the process is young, and the stone bears its youth like a child bears a newly shed tooth — raw, exposed, not yet accustomed to the shape of its own absence.

And the water. The water is everywhere, and it comes from the stone itself. The dark rock weeps. Not in the slow seep of a hillside spring, but in a constant exudation, as though the material of the formation were simply water in a very patient state of waiting. Every vertical face runs with it. Every shelf holds a thin standing film. The air, such as it is, condenses against the cold surfaces, adding its contribution to the wetness — a dim, cool atmosphere that never quite resolves into droplets but exists in that humid space before rain, that space where the air has made its decision but hasn't yet committed to falling. You breathe it and it is like breathing inside a cloud. Your skin films over within moments. You are, very quickly, as wet as everything else.

---

The vegetation should not be possible. That is the thought that arrives, and then arrives again, and then becomes a permanent background condition of consciousness here, like tinnitus or the awareness of your own heartbeat. The rock is cold — not cool, but *cold*, the kind of cold that stores meat, the kind of cold that slows enzymes. And the light is dim. And the space is small and bounded and, by any rational assessment, sealed. But the vegetation is *lush*. It is not the straggling, heroiclush of alpine survivors or the pale reaching of deep-shade fungi. It is the lush of tropical ravines, of monsoon valleys, of places where growth is a violence committed against stillness and stillness never had a chance.

The plants are dark-leaved, almost black in the dim light, and they are slick with the constant water. They grow from every surface that is even slightly horizontal, and many that are vertical. They grow in rosettes and spirals and forms that start familiar and then don't finish familiar — leaves that begin as ovals and then develop a twist, a recursion, a partial echo of themselves at the tip. Mosses that are too large, each stem a centimeter tall, a miniature forest that is, from the perspective of the small fauna, simply a forest. Vines that do not climb but *adhere*, pressing themselves flat against the wet stone like dark veins, like the stone's own circulatory system exposed. And everywhere, everywhere, the ferns. Not ferns. The things that are doing the job of ferns here, filling their niche, occupying their structural role in the ecology, but doing it with too many fronds, fronds that subdivide too many times, that reach a fineness beyond the capacity of real ferns, a fineness that becomes, at the tips, something like fur, something like the feelers of moths, something that trembles in air that does not seem to be moving.

---

The sound defines the space more than the visual does, and this is the third disorienting thing, though by now you have lost count of the disorienting things, which is itself a disorienting thing. The sound is loud, and it is mechanical, and it is unchanging.

The Engine — and it can only be called the Engine, though no one built it, though it has no maker's marks, though it is as natural to this place as the water and the stone — the Engine occupies the center of the space. It is not constructed. It is *grown*, or *precipitated*, or *condensed* from the conditions that obtain here. It is a mechanism the way a crystal is a mechanism: it has moving parts, it does work, it proceeds according to principles, but no hand assembled it.

It is difficult to see clearly in the dim light, and more difficult because the eye keeps refusing to accept what it sees. There are cylinders. There are reciprocating arms. There are tubes that carry the weeping water and transform it into steam — or something that behaves as steam behaves, expanding, pushing, yielding pressure — and the steam drives the cylinders, and the cylinders turn the shafts, and the shafts work the pumps, and the pumps draw more water from the stone. It is a closed loop. It is a perpetual motion machine, which cannot exist, and it exists. It exists in the way that this whole place exists: impossibly, but with the absolute authority of presence. You can touch the housing. You can feel the vibration in your molars. You can smell the hot metal, the mineral steam, the particular burnt-water scent of a teakettle left too long on a stove, except it has been left too long forever.

The sound is the sound of industry. The sound of a factory floor, of a steam plant, of a mill in full production. But there is only the one machine, and the space is the size of a church, and the sound should not be this loud, and it is this loud, and it does not vary. It does not slow. It does not speed. It does not hesitate or catch or surge. It is the sound of a process that has achieved equilibrium so complete that time has stopped meaning anything to it. The Engine is not running. The Engine *is*. It is a state, not an activity. It is the way this place breathes, and this place breathes exactly the same breath, in and out, forever.

---

The fauna are the proof that something is deeply wrong, though by now you do not need proof. They are abundant — shockingly so, given the smallness of the space. You hear them before you see them: rustlings in the over-large moss, clickings from the dark-leaved canopy, the splash of small bodies entering the standing water. They have adapted to the dimness. They have adapted to the noise. They have adapted to the wet and the cold and the strange light and the boundary that returns you to the center without your consent.

They are not quite right.

There are amphibians — or the things that occupy the amphibian niche. They are sleek and dark and they sit in the film of water on every shelf and in every small pool, and their eyes are too large, and they have too many digits on their forelimbs, and when they call, their voices are mechanical, percussive, like small hammers on small anvils, like the sound the Engine would make if the Engine were the size of a thumb. There are insects, or arthropods, or the segmented many-legged things that fill the spaces between the moss-stems, and they move with a precision that seems engineered, a clockwork regularity to their paces that makes you think of escapement wheels and governor arms. There are creatures in the water that you never see clearly, only the displacement they create, the V-wakes they send across the pools, and the pools are too shallow for anything to be that large, but the V-wakes are large, and the math doesn't work, and the water doesn't care about the math.

And there is one creature — or there is the evidence of one creature — that you never see at all. You find its traces: places where the vegetation has been cropped, the cut stems too clean, too geometric in their truncation, as though harvested by a blade. You find impressions in the moss that are too regular to be resting places, too patterned, stamped with a geometry that repeats like a manufactured thing. You find, once, a single footprint in the wet stone, and it is a hoofprint, and it is perfectly circular, and it is the size of a coffee cup, and it has a depth that suggests a weight that should have cracked the young stone, and the stone is not cracked, and the print is there, and it is the only one, as though the creature stepped once and then simply ceased to occupy space, or entered a different mode of occupancy that does not leave prints.

---

The light has no source. This is the thing you eventually accept, after searching for it, after looking up and around and behind every formation and through every stand of dark foliage. There is no lamp, no aperture, no glowing fungus, no chemical reaction, no distant opening to a brighter world. The light simply *is*, at a low and constant intensity, the way gravity simply *is*. It is a field condition. It is a property of the space, like its moisture, like its temperature, like its refusal to end. It is enough light to see by, barely. It is never more or less than that. It casts no shadows, because it has no direction, because it comes from everywhere and nowhere, because it is not "light" in any way that optics would recognize. It is visibility, ambient and undifferentiated, and in it, things are seen, and that is all that can be said.

---

And the weirdness — the weirdness is not an addition to these conditions. It is not a quality layered on top. It is the explanation for them, or the absence of explanation, the place where explanation cannot reach. The weirdness is the fact that the Engine has no builder and no fuel source and no waste and no wear. The weirdness is the fact that the vegetation grows in sealed darkness at temperatures that should arrest it, and grows lush, and grows always, and does not rot, and does not change, and the dead leaves that should accumulate do not accumulate, and the new growth that should replace them appears without the old having died, as though the plants are being written over, as though they are a palimpsest, as though something is continuously reauthoring them at the same state. The weirdness is the creatures that are almost recognizable and then are not, that have the right number of features in the wrong configuration, as though evolution happened here but from a different set of first principles, or as though the same principles were applied by a mind that understood them only approximately.

The weirdness is the boundary that is not a wall. The weirdness is the sound that never changes. The weirdness is the fact that you have been here for — how long? You do not know. You cannot know. There is no time gradient here. Nothing decays. Nothing grows. Nothing erodes. The young stone stays young. The wet rock keeps weeping. The Engine keeps its rhythm. The creatures go about their precise, mechanical lives. The fern-things extend their impossibly fine fronds into the still air. And the light stays dim, and the water stays cold, and the sound stays loud, and the space stays the size of a cathedral, and the landscape stays itself, perfectly, completely, without variation, without tendency, without the slightest inclination to ever be any different than it is right now.

It is not frozen. That is important. Frozen implies a pause, a before and after, a moment excerpted from a process. This place is not paused. It is *completed*. It has arrived at the condition it was always going to arrive at, and it has stayed, and it will stay, because there is nowhere else for it to go. It is a terminus. It is an answer. It is the landscape that remains when all the gradients have been followed to their ends, when all the energy has found its loop, when all the processes have closed upon themselves like the Ouroboros, like the water that weeps from the stone and becomes steam that drives the cylinder that works the pump that draws the water from the stone.

It is the Engine's world, and the Engine is the world's engine, and they are the same thing, and they have always been the same thing, and they will always be the same thing, and the sound is loud, and the light is dim, and the rock is young, and the water is rising, and rising, and rising, and always has been rising, and never overflows, and never will overflow, and the boundary holds, and the space folds, and you are here, and you have always been here, and you have just arrived, and both of these are true, and that is the last disorienting thing, or the first, or the only.
