At two thousand meters above the datum line, the city is still being born. It is a place of raw geology and half-finished infrastructure, where the crust itself is an unyielding dark basalt, shot through with veins of volcanic glass that haven't yet felt the patient touch of wind or water. This is a young world, or at least a newly exposed one—the erosion parameters are so low that the edges of every blasted terrace and drilled arcade are razor-sharp. Nothing here has been rounded by time. The mountainside is a staggered ziggurat of black rock and iron, still half-chiseled from the earth, expanding outward into the thin, superheated air.

The heat is oppressive, a dry, radiating force that turns the city into a slow oven. At an extreme elevation, one might expect cold, but here the temperature is pushed to 2.7 by the planet's deep thermal bleed and the exhalations of the city's own industrial heart. The air shimmers visibly over the black stone parapets. There is no vegetation—not a single spore, not a trace of chlorophyll, not even the hardy scum of extremophile fungi. This is a mineral world, sterile and absolute. The only carbon is locked in the grease that slicks the elevator shafts and the soot that plates every horizontal surface.

Yet the place is dense. It is built upward and inward, a staggering honeycomb of interlocking platforms, hanging freight galleries, and vertical tenement stacks. The constructions are pressed near-touching, layered so thickly that from a distance the city looks like a single crystalline growth of iron and slag protruding from the mountain face. Walkways cantilever over half-mile drops, their undersides bolted with humming condenser coils. The streets are not avenues but ravines of metal and stone, barely wide enough for two workers to pass shoulder-to-shoulder. Overhead, a madness of pipework and cable looms, knitting the districts together in a circulatory tangle of steam mains and high-voltage conduits. 

Water is a sporadic, almost clandestine presence. In the midst of this blazing, metallic dryness, moisture manifests only as a persistent condensation. Deep in the guts of the city, where the sun never reaches and the thermal differential is violent, the pipes sweat constantly. Walls weep a dark, mineral-heavy drip. The water gathers in rusted gutters and runs in thin, oily trickles down the stairwells, pooling in the hollows of unused valve-casings. Occasionally, a brief, anomalous rain falls—not from clouds, but from the city's own upper atmospheric exhaust, where superheated steam hits the cold thin air of the peak and precipitates back down as a brief, warm, greasy drizzle that stains the iron darker. 

The technology is strictly industrial, but of a massive, brutalist scale. The city is an engine whose only product seems to be its own continuation. Giant bore-machines, now idle, cling like mechanical ticks to the raw rock faces at the city's edge, their cutting heads embedded in the stone they were mid-process of carving. Factories sit inert, their blast furnaces glowing a low, threatening cherry-red through the vents, burning without urgency. The electricity is a low, static thrum that makes the hair on one's arms stand on end, arcing occasionally between mismatched junction boxes with a sharp, blue-white crack. 

The light is strangely normal, an ambient, diffuse gray-white that filters through the thermal haze, casting the enormous shadows of the vertical stacks across the raw valley walls. But under this ordinary sky, the weirdness of the place asserts itself. It is a geometry of the unfinished. Entire districts are uninhabited, not because they are abandoned, but because they have not yet been completed—scaffolding reaches up hundreds of meters to nowhere, empty window frames look out onto raw, unblasted cliff faces just meters away. The city feels like an embryonic organ, grown in a vat of iron and heat. 

The fauna is rare, and what little there is has been entirely subsumed by the city's function. Pale, eyeless drakes—half-organic, half-mineral in their carapace formation—roost in the hottest exhaust vents, feeding on the airborne slag. They move only when the heat gradients shift, crawling with agonizing slowness from one vent to another. 

For all its industrial density, the city is overwhelmingly quiet. The dynamic is nearly zero. This is a place of patient processes and weak gradients. There is no roar of furnaces, no clatter of production. The machinery operates at a sub-audible frequency, a deep, tectonic vibration felt in the molars and the breastbone rather than heard. The loudest sounds are the metallic tick-tick-tick of cooling ducts contracting in the shade, the hollow, rhythmic plink of condensation dripping into a steel drum, and the occasional, far-off groan of a geological shift as the mountain settles around the new intrusions. It is a silence of immense pressure, of heat and metal waiting.

This is a city that does not sleep, yet does not wake. It is a slow-motion eruption of architecture, a constructed lava flow of iron and stone, slowly freezing into its final shape over a timescale no human lifespan will see completed. To walk its cramped, sweating corridors is to walk inside the throat of a vast, mechanical volcano that is barely holding its breath.
