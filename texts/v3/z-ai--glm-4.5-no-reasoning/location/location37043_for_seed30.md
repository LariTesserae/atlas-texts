# The Hatchery

The plateau sits where it shouldn't—a flat expanse of pale, unformed stone hovering at altitude, as if someone sketched a mesa and forgot to finish the mountain beneath it. The rock is wrong. Not igneous, not sedimentary, but something between cartilage and ceramic: pale grey with a faint violet undertone, slightly yielding underfoot. It flexes. You feel your own weight through the soles of your feet as a slow rebound, the ground remembering you were there and taking its time forgetting.

Everything here is still becoming.

---

The stone hasn't decided its grain yet. Look close—and you can't help looking close, because the scale here is intimate, cathedral-interior scale, a single vast chamber open to dim sky—and you see the mineral structure hasn't locked. Crystals drift. Feldspar lattices rotate through slow solution, dissolving and reforming in the wet heat. The atmosphere condenses constantly. Not rain, not mist, but a persistent materializing of water out of warm air onto cool stone, so every surface weeps. Drops form, swell, roll—not down, but along whichever local gradient the still-setting stone presents. The water traces paths that won't exist tomorrow. The rock is being written and erased simultaneously.

The temperature is warm the way a greenhouse is warm, or an incubator. Humid. Skin-prickling. The kind of warmth that suggests something is being kept at the right conditions to develop.

---

And things are developing.

The shallow pools that collect in the depressions—the dips and bowls that will eventually, in geological time, become proper drainage, but are now just low spots in the unfinished surface—teem. Not with algae, not with tadpoles. With something else.

They're small. Most of them. The size of your thumbnail to the size of your hand. Many-legged, chitinous-plated, but wrong in their symmetry—not bilateral, not radial, but something that shifts between arrangements as you watch, legs repositioning, body segments rotating like elements of a mechanism finding its configuration. They're loud. A constant composite sound: clicking, scraping, burring, a rasp like a thousand tiny files working the same piece of metal. The sound fills the chamber, reflects off the flexing stone, gains harmonics. It's not unpleasant, but it's *purposeful*—the sound of industry, of assembly.

They're building themselves. That's the only way to describe it. The pool-dwellers emerge from the water half-formed and finish their own bodies using material from the stone. You can watch it happen: a creature climbs from a weeping pool, finds a patch of the soft mineral, and begins to eat. Not randomly. Specifically. It consumes particular crystal structures, and where the material passes through its gut—through whatever analog of a gut it possesses—it's processed. Reorganized. Deposited as new plate, new joint, new limb-segment. The waste it leaves is a fine white powder that the water immediately begins to dissolve back into solution, recycling it for the next one.

They are the erosion. They are the weathering. And they are the construction.

---

The structures are what stop you.

Not buildings. Not in any familiar sense. But formations that have *arrangement*—intentional geometry, patterns that don't occur in unformed stone. Towers? Pillars? No. Think of the shapes that crystals make when they grow in free solution: branching, hexagonal, spiraling. Now imagine those at the scale of doorframes, of standing stones. Sparse across the plateau—five, seven, nine of them—but unmistakably *placed*. Each one is hollow. Each one hums.

The hum is below the clicking and scraping, below the audible range, but you feel it in your back teeth and in the fluid of your inner ear. The structures are resonating with the activity of the pool-dwellers. Or the pool-dwellers are resonating with the structures. The causality isn't clear. But when a creature completes a new body segment—when it adds a plate, extends a limb—the nearest structure's hum changes pitch, and the creature pauses, adjusts, begins building again in a slightly different pattern. There's feedback happening. Communication through vibration through stone through water through gut through deposit.

They're talking about what to become next.

---

The vegetation—such as it is—only reinforces the sense of a place under active construction. Sparse pale stalks rise from cracks in the stone, but they're not photosynthesizing. Not in the normal way. They have no chlorophyll equivalent, no green, no purple, no photosynthetic pigment at all. They're the same pale grey-violet as the rock, and they grow only at the bases of the resonating structures, and they grow *toward sound*. The louder the hum, the faster the growth. Their tips are swollen with fluid drawn from the weeping stone, and when they reach a certain height—when the vibration reaches a certain intensity—they burst. Not violently. Deliberately. They release a spray of fine droplets that hang in the warm air and refract the dim light into brief, tiny spectra before settling into the nearest pool.

The pools need whatever is in those droplets. When a burst happens, the creatures in the nearest pool go into a frenzy of building—faster, more coordinated, their sounds synchronizing into rhythm. New structures begin. The stone beneath them dimples and starts to lift.

---

The light is dim because the atmosphere is thick with moisture and with something else—a fine particulate, the white powder waste of a million small bodies, suspended. It catches light from whatever source exists above (there must be a sun, but you can't see it; the sky is a uniform luminous grey, bright enough to see by, too bright to look at directly, like a diffused lamp behind fabric) and redistributes it as a soft, directionless glow. No shadows. Or rather—every object casts a shadow, but the shadow is filled in from all sides by reflected light, so it's more of a darkening than an absence. Depth becomes difficult. The far wall of the chamber could be thirty meters away or three hundred; the pale structures could be small and close or vast and distant. Your eye keeps remeasuring and getting different answers.

---

And the fauna—the *creatures*, because fauna suggests something settled, something classified, and these are neither—they don't all stay in the pools.

Some of them fly.

Not many. Not often. But when a structure's hum reaches a particular harmonic—when the feedback loop between builders and architecture achieves some critical resonance—a creature emerges from the top of the hollow formation, climbing the interior with legs that are, for this moment only, *configured for vertical*. It reaches the opening, and its body has already begun to change: plates thinning to membrane, legs elongating and broadening, segments fusing into a rigid central spine. By the time it launches, it's something else entirely. Something that spirals upward in the dim air, clicking at a frequency the structures can use, broadcasting whatever signal it was sent to carry.

You watch one ascend until the diffuse light swallows it. The click fades. A different structure, across the chamber, changes its hum. New creatures in new pools begin to build in new patterns.

---

What is this place?

It's a factory. It's an ecosystem. It's a computational substrate made of living things and semi-living stone and water that cycles through guts and air and mineral and back. It's a place where geometry is being *decided*—where the laws that will govern crystal formation, body plan, structural resonance are still in revision, still being tested by the only method available: building, measuring, feeding back, building again.

It's warm and wet because those are the conditions for rapid iteration. It's loud because iteration requires communication. It's strange because it hasn't finished being anything yet. Give it a million years and it might be a mountain range with an internal architecture of impossible precision, every ridge line a waveguide, every cave a processor, every erosion pattern a memory. Or it might be something that has no name because nothing like it has existed before.

Right now, it's a room-sized moment in an ongoing process that doesn't care about rooms or moments or the human scales that define them. It's a place that is actively, loudly, wetly, warmly becoming. And you are standing in it, your weight making little rebounding contributions to the data, your presence noted by the stone and folded, however slightly, into whatever it decides to be.
