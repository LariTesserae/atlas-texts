A wound in the world, freshly opened.

This is a place that has not yet decided what it is. The low basin—barely a depression in the greater crust—was fractured only moments ago, geologically speaking. The edges of the terrain are sharp, uncompromising. There has been no rain to round them, no wind to smooth them into parables of erosion. The ground is a calcareous shelf of pale, anhydrous mineral—something between gypsum and halite—that squeaks and shifts underfoot. There is no moisture here. Not a breath of humidity. The air strips the throat, chemical and dry, carrying the faint, acrid tang of sublimating salts. It is a sealed environment, a pocket of non-aqueous chemistry where nothing rots and nothing rusts.

Yet, impossibly, it is built. 

Structures crowd the basin—organized, layered, dense with the logic of infrastructure—but they are not constructions of tool or hand. There is no tech here, no device, no forged metal or fired clay. The city is grown, but not by any biological clock you would recognize. It is an urban geology. Pillars and load-bearing walls extrude from the fresh faults in the salt, crystalline and macroscopic, rising to the height of a small cathedral. The streets are laid out in a grid that implies purpose, imply zoning, implying a mind, but the mind is mineral. The buildings are hollow stacks of translucent selenite, their facades stacked in courses like masonry, their interiors vaulted and empty. Doorways yawn in shapes that are almost arches, framed by the interlocking fingers of young stalactites and stalagmites that have already grown meters in the span of days. The scale is confined—a handful of these crystal towers, a few avenues, a central square bordered by high, sheer walls—but within those boundaries, the density of construction is staggering. It is a town of sheer, sharp geometry, crowded upon itself.

The light is a permanent dusk. A diffuse, gray-lavender luminescence seeps from the mineral itself, a low chemo-luminescence trapped in the lattice of the salts. It is enough to see by, but only just. Shadows pool in the hollows of the vaults and do not fully resolve. The sky, if there is one, is a low lid of unbroken cloud—or perhaps it is simply the upper boundary of the void, reflecting nothing.

And against the absolute stillness of the dry air and the deafening silence of the void, the city is violently active.

It is building itself. 

The silence is profound—there is no wind, no drip of water, no insect hum—but if you stand very still within the nave of the largest structure, you can feel it. A subsonic thrum. A tectonic pulse. The grid is expanding. At the edges of the settlement, the mineral pavement is buckling, new walls shouldering their way up from the fissures, pushing curtains of salt-scaling aside. The streets rearrange themselves overnight. A dead end becomes a thoroughfare; a courtyard is swallowed by a newly extruded fortress of crystal. The process is relentless, geological in speed but urban in pattern. Foundations settle with a grinding crunch that vibrates through the soles of your feet. Edifices rise visibly, their spires climbing inch by inch in the dim light, adding floors to their empty frames like a breath drawn inward.

The ecology is sparse, stretched thin, but present. The flora is parasitic on the geometry. Veins of pale, fibrous lichen—neither plant nor fungus, but something that metabolizes the ambient electrical fields—trace the sharp corners of the buildings, mapping the load-bearing walls in webbed silver. They are sparse, almost invisible, but they follow the grid perfectly, thriving only where the crystal stress is greatest. They are the paint on the city's walls. 

Fauna is rare, but when it appears, it is singular. A single creature haunts the avenues, pacing the perimeter of the square. It is eyeless, legless, a flat, matte-gray thing the size of a hound, moving on a skirt of cilia that grip the crystal grooves. It navigates the shifting streets with a tireless, mechanical gait, a cleaner or a surveyor. It does not eat; it polishes. It grinds its calcified jaw against the fresh, sharp edges of the doorframes, filing them down, applying the only erosion this young place possesses. It is the only thing here that makes sound—a faint, rhythmic scrape, like a bone drawn across glass, marking time in a place that otherwise has none.

The world’s rules are written in the crystal lattice. Here, order does not require life. Density does not require population. A city can be born from the same pressure that crushes carbon into diamond, emerging from the fault lines fully planned, fully empty, a cathedral of spontaneous architecture. It is a landscape of ghostly industry, of mineral urbanism, an engine running on the slow fuel of pressure and absence, building and building a metropolis for no one, in silence, in the dim.
