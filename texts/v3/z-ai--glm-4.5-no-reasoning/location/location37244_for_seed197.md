You descend into a hollow that should not exist. It is roughly the size of a cathedral nave, yet it breathes like a lung. This is a place where the boundary between the vegetable and the mineral has not merely blurred—it has dissolved entirely. Welcome to the Chorus.

**The Substrate and Structure**
There is no ground here, no soil, no stone. You walk on root-mass. The entire hollow is a single organism, a sprawl of interlocking rhizomes and cortical vines that have grown into the shape of a city. There are avenues, colonnades, archways—urban geometries repeated in living wood—but they are empty. The density here is near-void. The structures stretch away into a dim, humid obscurity, but they are unoccupied by any intelligence. It is a metropolis built for ghosts, or perhaps built *by* a ghost—the ghost of a settlement that never existed in stone, only in the imagination of the root. 

The scale is intimate yet distorting. The archways are tall enough to frame a procession, but the spaces between them are cavernous. The avenues stretch wide and hollow. There is nothing per volume. You can walk for minutes down a corridor of living pillars without encountering a single obstruction, a single door, a single resident.

**The Damp and the Warmth**
The air is heavy, warm, and slick. Water does not flow here; it exudes. The wood-roads are damp with intermittent condensation that weeps from the vaulted ceilings of vine-braids. Moisture beads on the pale, tuberous walls, gathering into trembling globules that reflect the dim, amber-green light before falling. It never quite rains, but you are always getting wet. The temperature is a blood-heat, feverish and still, trapped in the low elevation of this world-chasm.

**The Ecological Motion**
Life is abundant, but it is entirely subordinate to the plant. This is a pure-plant ecosystem, yet it teems with fauna—but only the kind that can be hosted, parasitized, or generated by the vegetation. The air is thick with spore-clouds that move like flocks of insects. Galls the size of fists pulse on the walls, incubating something inside. When a gall splits, a pale, eyeless thrip-kin emerges, flutters erratically on translucent wings, and is immediately consumed by a pitcher-flower that has evolved to snap shut with the force of a jaw. The predator-prey dynamic is frantic, continuous, and entirely botanical. Fungal hyphae crawl across dead bark at visible speeds, dismantling and recycling in real-time. 

**The Sound**
It is deafening. The noise is the first thing that breaks your mind, and it never stops. It is a symphony of biological processes amplified to a roar. The sap is rising—and you can *hear* it, a million xylem vessels hissing and clicking under pressure. The galls are cracking with wet, explosive pops. The pitcher-flowers are slamming shut with meaty thuds that echo through the hollow avenues. The condensation drips in a thousand isolated spots, creating a polyrhythmic deluge. And beneath it all, a low, subsonic thrumming—the heartbeat of the rhizome network, vibrating up through the soles of your feet and into your teeth. It is a noise that makes thought difficult, a wall of biological static that forces you into a purely sensory state.

**The Breakdown**
And then, the geometry stops making sense. The weirdness of this place seeps in through the corners of your vision. You look down an avenue and the parallel lines of the vine-pillars converge, then diverge, then twist like braided hair before straightening again. The urban planning of the root-city is recursive. You turn a corner and find yourself looking at the back of your own head, thirty feet away, standing in an identical intersection. 

The structures are weathered, established, in-balance—they have been here for epochs, undisturbed—but their existence defies topological logic. An archway leads into a room that opens onto the ceiling of the avenue you just left. Gravity feels like a suggestion that the roots politely ignore; water drips upward from the floor to meet the weeping ceiling. 

A gall swells on a nearby wall, but as you watch, the swelling reverses, the creature crawls back inside, the split skin knits shut, and the spore-cloud reverses into the flower that birthed it. Time is not linear here; it is tidal, pulled by the metabolic rhythms of the substrate. 

You are standing in the center of a room-sized city, made of one single, ancient, roaring plant, entirely empty of civilization, vibrating with deafening life, in a space where left loops into right and up folds into down. The damp runs down your face. The plant-city breathes. The chorus of sap drowns out your sanity.
