This is a place of frozen thunder.

The elevation is extreme—a continental shelf thrust so high it scrapes the bottom of the troposphere, sitting in a permanent stratospheric dusk. It is young, violently so. The basalt and gneiss were sheared and uplifted mere geological moments ago, and the rock has not yet learned to be round. The mountains here are not mountains; they are city-sized shards, towering aretes and Matterhorn spires that cut upward like broken obsidian teeth. The features are sharp enough to slice leather, their flanks unmarred by the patient smoothing of ages.

And yet, the place is dripping. 

With a temperature hovering just below the freezing point of brine, and an atmosphere pushed to the condensing limit, water is everywhere and nowhere all at once. It does not flow; it clings. A hyper-dense fog, nearly liquid, perpetually rolls through the black canyons. Every surface—weeping rock, iron girder, shattered glass—is coated in a thick, glacial rime that never truly builds into ice, but never melts. The air is a saturated sponge. Breathe it, and your lungs fill with the sensation of wet wool. Moisture condenses on the stone in real-time, beading and weeping down the fresh, jagged faces in an endless, silent weeping. 

Against this razor-wire geology and oppressive cold, the vegetation is an impossibility—and yet, it is legion. It is not the green of earth, but a cold-adapted, parasitic lushness. The flora are massive, vein-like lianas and sprawling mats of lichen that feed on the static electricity in the air and the iron in the rock. They drape over the lethal spires in thick, heavy curtains of deep indigo and bruised violet. The leaves are translucent, resembling frozen blisters filled with antifreeze sap, trembling in the dense fog. It is a jungle of cold, a tangle of wet, fleshy plants that grow at a glacial pace, so tightly interwoven that they form a tensile net across the chasms. 

There is no fauna. Not a single insect, bird, or beast breaks the surface of the water-beaded leaves. The ecology is a closed loop of rock, vapor, and indigo vine. It is utterly, oppressively empty of breath.

But it is not empty of habitation. 

Humankind—or something with a similar hunger for order—has built here. Draped over the razor-sharp ridges and suspended above the bottomless, fog-choked canyons is a city of iron and brass. It is an industrial settlement of staggering scale, a continent-spanning lattice of catwalks, smog stacks, and processing plants. The construction is layered, built into the fresh rock with brute force, rivets the size of a man's head driven into the unyielding stone. The architecture is brutalist and utilitarian, designed to withstand a humidity that rusts everything and a cold that makes iron brittle. 

And the city runs. It is mechanized to an extreme, powered by colossal, low-thrumming engines that shake the very bedrock. Plumes of thick, white steam—the only visible marker of distance in the infinite fog—vent from a million smokestacks, instantly absorbed into the weeping atmosphere. Automated trams run on rusted rails along the precipice edges. The factories are lit from within by the sickly, normalcy-defying glare of sodium vapor and arc-lights, casting a flat, orange pallor across the wet, violet vines and the black rock.

But the factories produce nothing. The trams carry no passengers. 

The dynamic of this world is zero. It is a frozen equilibrium, a machine locked in a single, eternal cycle of functioning stasis. The engines have run at the exact same RPM since they were switched on. The condensation falls at the exact same rate. The vines grow by microns a year, locked in a perpetual standoff with the frost. There are no gradients left to exploit, no weather changing, no seasons turning. It is a snapshot of a world, laminated in ice and steam, caught in the act of roaring but never moving forward.

And the sound. The sound is the worst of it.

It is deafening. A continental-roar of industry and atmosphere locked in a feedback loop. The low, stomach-vibrating thrum of the engines blends with the shriek of wind forced through the sharp, fresh canyons at hypersonic speeds. It is a noise so vast and so constant that it ceases to be heard and becomes a physical pressure, a weight on the chest. There is no silence here; silence is an abstract concept that has never existed between these jagged peaks. The sound is the sound of a world trying to tear itself apart, but lacking the gradient to actually do so.

You stand on a slick, wet iron grating, hundreds of miles from the edge of the plateau. Below you is an abyss of shifting, condensing fog and purple vine-tangled darkness. Above you, the sky is a leaden dome of un-yielding cloud. Around you, the rusting, steaming city carries on its eternal, purposeless labor, screaming into the void. And nothing—nothing at all—ever changes.
