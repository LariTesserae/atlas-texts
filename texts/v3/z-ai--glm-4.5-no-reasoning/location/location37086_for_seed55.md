A cold so profound it has become a physical medium. The water here does not crash or churn; it hangs, an infinite, near-freezing syrup of dark liquid that fills the cosmos from edge to edge. This is a drowned world, not by a flood, but by an ocean that simply is—the final, primordial state of a place where the land surrendered eons ago. The temperature hovers just above the freezing point of fresh water, a constant, glacial chill that permeates everything, slowing molecular motion to a crawl, making time itself feel viscous. 

There is no sky. Above, the water simply continues, an atmospheric ocean pressing down with the unimaginable weight of a world-scale depth. Light cannot survive the journey here. The realm exists in an absolute, velvety darkness, the kind that renders the concept of sight obsolete. You do not look; you feel the pressure, the cold, the faint stirring of currents against your skin.

And yet, the world is not empty. It is densely, claustrophobically packed. Every square meter is occupied, layered, saturated. The landscape is a vast, continental shelf of the made. There is no natural substrate, no naked rock or sediment—only architecture. Before the drowning, before the cold settled into its final state, someone—something—built. The entire planet is a construction: a labyrinth of cyclopean masonry, interlocking megalithic blocks, and crude monolithic pillars. The technology that shaped this world was rudimentary, born of muscle, leverage, and simple hand-tools. No metal alloys frame these walls; no engines power this place. It is the work of a million lifetimes of chisel and haul, a primitive obsession carved to a planetary scale. 

The construction is absolute. Tiered terraces, step-wells, and massive causeways stack into the dark, forming near-touching walls and low, flat ceilings. The elevation is perpetually low, hemmed in by the dense saturation of the built environment. Massive columns, thick as ancient redwoods and carved with blunt, geometric grooves, march away into the black in every direction, a forest of milled stone. It is a city built to be the world, now serving as its tomb.

Against this endless, cold masonry, life has taken hold—insistent, patient, and overwhelming. The vegetation is a lush, impossible canopy of pale, fleshy growths. Without light, chlorophyll is a forgotten myth; these are organisms of the deep caldron, feeding on the slow chemical leaching of the stone and the mineral richness of the freezing water. Bioluminescence is absent; the flora is as blind as the world it inhabits. Thick, ribbony kelp-analogs wrap around the square pillars, their edges frilled and overlapping. Sponge-like masses, cold and slick to the touch, choke the corridors, growing in pale, calcified shelves that reach out into the passageways. Fungal towers, bloated and translucent, bleed slow, viscous sap that drifts upward in the sluggish current. The growth is layered six deep in places—a suffocating, beautiful necrosis of greenery reclaiming the hand-made earth. It rustles faintly as the immense, patient waters breathe.

There are no animals. No fish dart through the submerged archways; no whales sing in the dark. The fauna has vanished, leaving only the flora and the stone. The silence is staggering. Sound here is a rare, low-frequency event—the dull, distant groan of a megalithic block settling by a fraction of a millimeter under the oceanic pressure, or the slow, tear-like drip of condensate falling from a stalactite of calcified root. Mostly, there is only the tinnitus-hum of absolute quiet, pressed against the ears by the weight of the sea.

This is a world at the end of its dynamics. The erosion is primordial; it has reached its oldest stable state. The edges of the masonry are not sharp anymore—they have been worn to smooth, rounded contours by the patient, million-year lap of the water. The stones do not fall; they are held in a final, frozen equilibrium, locked in place by the hydrostatic pressure and their own incomprehensible mass. The lush vegetation does not rapidly decay or burst forth; it simply exists, growing a centimeter a century, locked in a sluggish, patient cycle of chemical exchange. 

It is a landscape of total immersion: submerged, frigid, and blind. A primitive, hand-built world drowned to its rooftops, now choked by a silent, pale jungle of deep-water flora, waiting out the final, infinite epoch in the crushing dark.
