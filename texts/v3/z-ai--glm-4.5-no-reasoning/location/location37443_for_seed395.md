# The Permeate

A world still forming. Not the kind of forming that involves cooling magma or tectonic shuffle — something earlier. Something underneath geology. The ground here hasn't decided what it is yet.

---

## What the Ground Is

Beneath everything lies the Substrate — a mineral expanse of crystalline lattice, warm to the touch, dense and interlocked at the molecular level so that nothing flows through it. No aquifer. No seep. No moisture held in stone pores or condensed in underground chambers. It is the driest substance imaginable: a salt-metal framework that binds water so tightly the molecule ceases to exist as water. What rises from below is not steam or vapor but a kind of mineral exhalation — volatile compounds that the heat draws upward in slow, continuous pulses.

The Substrate is low. Almost uniformly low. It spreads across the entirety of this place — continental in its reach, planar in its flatness — with only the gentlest corrugations. Ridges rise not in meters but in gentle swales, long undulations you feel in your legs before your eyes register them. The horizon doesn't break; it *thickens*.

And it is still forming. You can feel it. The lattice grows. At the leading edges — where the Substrate meets open air — crystalline filaments extend outward at a pace just faster than geological, just slower than biological. Millimeters per day. The world is accreting itself from below, writing its own foundation in real time. Where the lattice is newest, it has a translucent quality, a raw-nerve pinkness like tissue that hasn't keratinized. You can see the structure inside it — the repeating hexagonal packs, the interstitial voids that aren't quite voids, that hold something denser than air but refuse to be called fluid.

---

## What Grows

Everything here is lush, and nothing here drinks.

The vegetation — if that word fits — draws its moisture from air, from the mineral exhalation itself, from compounds that carry hydrogen and oxygen in arrangements no terrestrial plant would recognize. The predominant form is a kind of *mat*: not rooted, but *fused*. The flora's basal layer bonds directly to the Substrate's crystalline surface, interlocking at the molecular level. There is no soil. There is no humus. There is only lattice and the things that have learned to grow from it.

The mats are everywhere. They carpet the undulations in dense, overlapping sheets — burgundy, rust, deep amber, occasionally a black-green so saturated it seems to hold more color than light can account for. They are thick. Packed. Where one mat ends, another has already begun beneath it, layered three or six or twelve deep in places. The older layers compress into something like peat, though it crumbles to salt when broken, not to loam. The younger layers are soft, almost gelatinous, warm from the Substrate below and from their own slow metabolism.

From the mats rise the columns. They are the vertical expression of this place — stems, trunks, stalks, though those words imply a woodiness that isn't quite right. The columns are *chitinous-mineral*, grown from the same chemistry as the Substrate but organized along biological principles. They rise in clusters, in thickets, in forests that stretch to the haze. Some are slender and flex in the warm air currents; others are broad as silos, their surfaces ridged with growth lines that record the pulses of mineral exhalation like tree rings. They branch, and at their branch-tips they open *receptors* — not leaves, exactly, but broad, flat, translucent structures that catch light and extract what they need from the rising chemical breath.

The canopy — there is a canopy — closes. The columns grow densely enough that their receptors overlap, interlock, shade the mats below. Light filters through in a warm, amber wash. The interior of a column-stand is like the inside of a lung: humid with exhalation, warm, lit as if through membrane.

---

## What Lives

Fauna is abundant, and it follows the logic of the place.

The most numerous are the *crawlers* — low, flat, many-legged creatures that move across the mats in constant grazing patterns. Their bodies are armored in the same mineral-chitin as the columns; they crack softly when they move, like knuckles. They feed on the mats' upper growth, taking in the chemical-laden tissue and metabolizing it with a dry efficiency that produces no waste, only more mineral lattice in their shells. When a crawler dies, it doesn't rot. It *subsumes*. Its body fuses to the mat below and becomes substrate for new growth, its shell dissolving into the crystalline baseline over days. Nothing is lost. The packed density of this place depends on that compacted cycle.

Above the crawlers, in the column-stands, move the *climbers* — longer-limbed, more articulated, with gripping appendages that lock onto the columns' growth ridges. They are predator to the crawlers when they descend, and prey to nothing that anyone has observed. They navigate the dense vertical spaces with a quiet precision, their movements nearly soundless.

And then there are the *floaters*. This is the detail that makes the quiet feel intentional. The floaters are *bladder-organisms*, gas-filled and membrane-skinned, that drift between the column-canopies and the open air above. They are large — some of them, the oldest, are several meters across — and they rise and sink on thermal differentials, breathing in the mineral exhalation at altitude and exhaling something the columns need at canopy-level. They are the wind's fauna. They move with air currents, and when the air is still, they hover, and the world goes quiet.

---

## What Is Built

Here is the strangeness: this place has a city.

Or — it has *construction*. Organized construction. Infrastructure. Layered, dense, purposeful building that fills the space between columns and spreads across the mats in a complexity that exceeds organism-scale. But no tools made it. No hands held instruments. No technology shaped it.

The city is *grown*.

The builders are colonial organisms — something between coral and termite and fungus — that construct by secretion. Their structures rise from the Substrate in slow, accretive layers, each generation building on the last. The result is *urban*: streets at ground level, sheltered corridors between column-bases, vertical stacks that hollow out and become chambers, connecting passages that bridge between columns at height. The construction material is the same mineral-chitin as everything else here, but arranged with a density and regularity that can only be called architectural.

The streets aren't planned. They *emerge* — worn into existence by the crawlers' grazing patterns, then formalized by the builders who secrete walls along the established lines of movement. The chambers aren't designed. They *accrete* — started as shelter for a builder colony, extended by the next generation, linked to neighbors, grown upward and inward until the whole thing has the organic density of a medieval city grown over centuries, except it has no centuries. It has *process*. Constant, slow, packed process.

The city is everywhere. Not in the sense that the whole landscape is paved — the columns still rise through it, the mats still grow under it, the Substrate still forms beneath it — but in the sense that there is no place where the built environment doesn't *touch* the rest. It interleaves. The city is the ecosystem's urban phase.

---

## What It Feels Like

Warm. The warmth is constant. It rises from the Substrate and radiates from the mats' metabolism and collects under the canopy. It is not hot — it is the temperature of a body at rest, of a greenhouse in temperate latitude, of a place where energy moves through but doesn't escape.

Quiet. The quiet is the first thing you notice after the warmth. This is a place of massive biological activity — growth, feeding, construction, the floaters' slow respiration — but almost none of it produces sound. The crawlers' shells click faintly. The columns creak in air currents with a low, intermittent groan. The builders' secretion is a wet, soft sound, like very slow pouring. But there is no wind noise — the canopy and the density of growth break any air movement before it can gather. There is no water — no streams, no rain, no surf. There is no speech. The quiet isn't empty; it is *full*, the way a room full of sleeping people is full. Everything here is engaged in process, and the processes are silent.

The light is filtered, amber, steady. No harsh sun; no stars visible through the canopy's density. The receptors overhead glow faintly with transmitted light, and the floaters' membranes catch it and scatter it, and the mineral surfaces of the city reflect it in a low, warm sheen. It is always approximately the same brightness. The place has no night that anyone can find — only a deepening of the amber, a quieting of the thermal pulses, when the Substrate's exhalation slows and the floaters sink lower and the crawlers consolidate under the column-bases.

Vast. That is the final register. The city doesn't end. The columns don't thin. The mats don't break. The Substrate doesn't rise or fall. It goes on, and on, and on — across swales that take hours to cross, through stands that have no visible boundary, past city-districts that merge into each other without gates or margins. The scale defeats understanding. You could walk for days and the character of the place would not change. It is a world, not a landscape. A world that is still writing itself, one crystalline extension at a time, from the bottom up, in silence and warmth and the closest thing to total occupation that matter can manage.

---

It is not hostile. It is not alien in the way that alarms. It is simply *other* — a place where the familiar categories (city/forest/desert/reef) have collapsed into one continuous process, where everything is built and everything is grown and nothing is wasted and nothing is still and the only sound is the sound of the world becoming itself, slowly, under amber light, in dry warmth, in packed and patient silence.
