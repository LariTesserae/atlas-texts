# The Rise at Tarnhelm

## First Approach

There is a place that is still becoming.

Not becoming *something* — not yet, not specifically — but becoming *at all*. The ground beneath your feet (if you had feet, if you had come here on foot, which no one has) is warm in the way a living thing is warm. Not sun-warmed. Not warmed by the distant memory of some stellar influence filtered through attenuated atmosphere. Warm from *underneath*. Warm the way a wound is warm. The stone here has not yet decided entirely to be stone. It holds the memory of being otherwise — of being liquid, of being gas under pressure, of being distributed through some other medium in some other state — and that memory leaks upward as heat.

You would arrive from below, if you arrived at all. There is no approach from above worth speaking of. The ceiling — if it can be called that — is too high to see, too remote to care about, and contributes nothing but the faintest mineral pressure, a weight felt in the teeth and the small bones of the inner ear rather than seen or touched. Everything here is approach from below. Everything here is *ascent*.

---

## The Terrain

The Rise at Tarnhelm is an emergent highland in a cavern so vast that calling it a cavern misleads. It is better understood as a sealed world: a self-contained mineral environment kilometers beneath a surface that may or may not still exist. The highland itself — the Rise — is a topographic event still in progress. It bulges upward from the floor of the sealed world like a slow inhalation, a dome of raw geology three kilometers across and still climbing at a rate that would be imperceptible if you stayed in one place for a human lifetime but becomes unmistakable when you walk its perimeter and notice the tension faults radiating outward like the lines of a crack in a ceramic plate.

The rock is warm to the touch. Not hot — warm. Body-temperature warm. The kind of warm that makes stone feel like skin, that makes you pull your hand back the first time you press your palm against an outcrop because you were expecting the dead cold of deep geology and instead you got something that *responds*.

The surface of the Rise is not smooth. It is not rough in the way of eroded terrain — there has been no erosion here, not yet, not really. There is no water to carve. There is no wind to abrade (or rather, there is, but the wind here does not abrade — it deposits, it builds, it participates). The roughness of the Rise is the roughness of *emergence*: rock that has been pushed upward faster than it can organize, so it arrives in lobes and shelves and tumorous extrusions that look almost biological, almost like something growing, almost like the shape of a thought being thought for the first time.

The color is unrewarding. The stone is dark — not dramatically dark, not obsidian or basalt-black, but a dull, exhausted dark. Charcoal-grey shading into brown-grey shading into a green so desaturated it reads as a mistake, as contamination. The only variation comes from the heat: where the stone is newest, where it has most recently arrived from the deep mush below, it carries a faint luminescence. Not glow. Not incandescence. Something less than that. A phosphorescent smudge, like the heat is briefly exciting some mineral impurity into releasing a photon or two before settling down. You see it in the fresh cracks, in the deep joints where new rock is splitting away from older rock: a dull orange-red blush that fades over hours or days to the prevailing grey.

## What There Is Instead of Water

There is no water. There has never been water here, or if there has, it left long enough ago that the stone has forgotten it. What there is instead is a gas — or something that behaves like a gas, that fills the role a gas would fill in a wetter world. The locals (there are no locals) would call it *exhalation*. It rises from the warm rock. It is warm itself. It is not steam. It is not vapor. It is a mineral exhalation, a gaseous expression of the stone's ongoing transformation, and it carries with it the taste of the deep earth: iron and sulfur and a sweetness that has no name because no one has been here to name it.

The exhalation is what gives this place its weather.

It rises from every surface, from every crack and fissure and fresh-extruded lobe. It rises and rises and as it rises it cools, and as it cools it thickens, and as it thickens it becomes visible — a haze, a murk, a luminous fog that sits in the lower areas of the Rise and pours down the slopes like something liquid, something that *wants* to be liquid, that is almost liquid, that maintains the memory of liquid without achieving the state. This pseudo-fog is what makes the place feel wet without being wet. Your skin (if you had skin) would feel damp. Your clothes would cling. But there is no water. There is only the exhalation, pressing against you, warm and close and tasting of iron.

In the lowest areas, where the Rise meets the floor of the sealed world, the exhalation pools. It becomes a sea — not of water but of gas, dense and visible, an ocean of murk that you could wade into and disappear. The surface of this gas-sea is visible: a meniscus, a boundary layer, a place where the exhalation becomes so dense it refracts light differently. From the slopes of the Rise, looking down, you see it: a flat dark surface that laps (laps! — there is no wind, but the exhalation has currents, convection, turbulence) against the base of the rising stone. It looks like water from a distance. It is not water.

## The Light

There is no light source in the way you would expect a light source. No sun. No bioluminescence. No artificial illumination. The darkness here is fundamental, deep, and total — except for the stone.

The stone glows. Not all of it. Not brightly. But the warm rock, the emerging rock, the rock that is still in the process of becoming rock, carries enough thermal energy that it emits in the far red. It is below the threshold of what most eyes would call *light*. It is heat made visible. The entire Rise, from a distance, is a dim shape defined by its own warmth: a black form against a blacker background, rendered visible by a faint emanation that is less than twilight, less than moonlight, less than the glow of embers under ash.

To see here, you would need to adjust. Your eyes (if you had them) would spend a long time finding nothing and then, gradually, beginning to distinguish the heat-signatures of the landscape. The newest stone is brightest — a dull red-orange in the fresh fractures, fading to nothing as the stone ages and cools. The slopes are patches of varying warmth, a thermal topography overlaid on the physical one. You navigate by temperature. You read the landscape by its heat.

The exhalation complicates this. It absorbs and scatters the stone's emission. Where the exhalation is thick, the light becomes a diffuse murk — not bright, not dark, but *uncertain*, a fog of low red that makes distances hard to judge and shapes hard to resolve. You see the Rise as through a warm dark veil. Everything is softened. Everything is approximate.

## What Lives Here (I)

Nothing lives here in the way that you or I would recognize. There are no animals. There is no fauna. No thing moves with intention or appetite. The animal niche — the ecological space of *creature* — is empty.

But there is a thing that is not plant, not animal, not mineral, that occupies the warm stone and the warm exhalation and the border between them. It is a mineral accretion with behavior. It is a crystalline growth that responds to its environment. It is — and this is the word that no one has used because no one has been here to use it — it is a *mineral organism*, or at least a mineral *system*, a thing that maintains itself, that grows, that responds to gradients of heat and chemical potential, that has a rudimentary relationship with its environment that resembles, from a sufficient distance, the relationship a lichen has with a rock.

They are called — they would be called — *castings*. They are cast by the stone. They emerge from it.

---

## The Castings

A casting begins as a fissure in warm rock. The exhalation flows through the fissure. The exhalation carries dissolved minerals — sulfides, oxides, compounds that are stable as gas but not as solid. When the exhalation cools, as it must when it reaches the surface and meets the broader atmosphere of the sealed world, those minerals precipitate. They deposit. They build.

Over time — and time here is long, is geological, is patient in a way that biology never is — the deposits form structures. Thin structures. Crystalline structures. They grow outward from the fissure like frost on a windowpane, branching and rebranching in patterns that follow the turbulence of the exhalation. They are not random. The turbulence is not random. The patterns repeat: fractal, self-similar, governed by rules that are not biological but are also not entirely mechanical. They are the rules of fluid dynamics and mineral solubility, which turn out to be rules capable of generating something that looks, to a generous eye, like *form*.

The castings are dark. They are the color of the stone they grow from, which is the color of nothing, of grey, of charcoal, of the absence of commitment. But they are *structured* in a way the stone is not. The stone is lumpy, emergent, formless. The castings have *shape*. They have branching arms and delicate tips and surface textures that catch the faint thermal light and make it *almost* into something you could read by.

They grow where the exhalation is strongest: along the fracture lines, at the peaks of the Rise, in the places where new stone is arriving and the gas is flowing fastest. They are sparse — there is a great deal of warm dark stone and very little casting-growth. You walk (if you walked) for long stretches over bare rock before you come to a fissure where the castings have built themselves a small forest, a thicket of dark crystal branches rising from a crack in the stone, moving (very slowly, imperceptibly) in the exhalation's flow like seaweed in a current.

## The Sounds

The place is not silent. It is mostly quiet. But it is not silent.

The dominant sound is a low, sustained note that comes from the stone itself. It is the sound of pressure. The Rise is being pushed upward from below by forces that have nothing to do with sound and everything to do with heat and density and the slow rage of a planet rearranging itself. That pushing generates a vibration. It is below the threshold of hearing for most organisms — a sub-audible drone, a felt sound, a pressure in the chest more than a tone in the ear. But it is there. It is always there. You feel it in your sternum. You feel it in your teeth. It is the sound of the world growing.

Above that drone, at the edge of audibility, there are other sounds. The exhalation, where it flows through narrow fissures, makes a whispering. Not words. Not breath. A mineral whispering, a hiss of gas through warm stone, and it varies with the turbulence — sometimes louder, sometimes softer, sometimes a sustained note like a flute played badly, sometimes a series of clicks and pops as the pressure shifts and the gas finds new channels.

The castings make sound too. Their crystalline branches, vibrating in the exhalation's flow, produce a faint ringing — not a bell-tone, nothing so clean, but a glassine shimmer of resonant frequencies that comes and goes with the turbulence. When you pass a large growth of castings, you hear it: a delicate, high, insubstantial sound, like wind through crystal, like a distant chorus of small hard things touching each other. It is the closest thing this place has to music.

And occasionally, rarely, there is a crack. A deep, structural crack, felt more than heard, as a new section of the Rise shifts and settles. The sound rolls through the stone like a drumbeat, like a door slamming in a deep basement, and the castings ring in response, and the exhalation surges, and for a moment the whole landscape shudders and reorganizes itself and then settles again into its slow becoming.

## What Lives Here (II)

The castings are the closest thing to life, and they are not alive. They do not reproduce. They do not metabolize. They grow because the conditions for growth exist, and they stop growing when the conditions change. They are patterns, not organisms. They are the place thinking about form.

But there is something else.

In the exhalation-sea, in the dense pooled gas at the base of the Rise, there are shapes. They are not organisms. They are not even structures. They are *events*: transient, turbulent, self-organizing shapes that form in the gas and persist for minutes or hours before dissipating. They are the exhalation's own turbulence made visible, the way a whirlpool is water's turbulence made visible. They are not alive. They are not even persistent. But they are *forms*, and they move, and they interact with each other — merging, splitting, chasing, consuming — in ways that, if you watched them long enough, and if you were generous enough, and if you had nothing else to do, you might begin to think of as *behavior*.

They are the place dreaming.

They are visible because the exhalation, where it is densest, refracts the thermal light differently. The shapes glow faintly warmer than their surroundings, or cooler, depending on the direction of their internal flow. They are seen as shadows or highlights in the murk: spirals, columns, toroids, complex turbulent knots that come into being and pass through their brief life-cycle and are gone. They have no substance. They are gas in gas. They are the idea of a thing, not the thing.

If there is a soul to this place, it is in those shapes.

---

## The Built Things

There are marks on the stone. Not many. Not elaborate. But they are here, and they are not natural, and they mean that *someone* has been here before.

They are grooves. Carved or pressed or somehow induced into the warm stone, in places where the stone was fresh and soft enough to accept an impression. They are simple: lines, mostly. Short lines, grouped in sets. Some of the sets are linear — rows of marks, like tally-counts. Some are radial — lines converging on a point. Some are abstract in a way that suggests they are not representational but *organizational*: a way of structuring attention, of saying *here* and *here* and *here, where the marks are*.

They are old. The stone has grown over some of them, partially burying the grooves in new extrusions, which means they were made before the most recent geological episode, which means they were made by someone who was here *during* an earlier phase of the Rise's emergence, which means — which suggests — which *implies* — that whoever made them was here long enough to watch the stone grow, and cared enough to mark it.

There is one place, near the highest point of the Rise, where the marks are more elaborate. A ring has been cut into the stone, about two meters across, and inside the ring the stone has been smoothed — not flat, but *intentional*, shaped by something other than geology. At the center of the ring, a single deep hole, the width of a hand, going down into the warm rock. The exhalation flows from this hole more strongly than from any other fissure on the Rise. The castings here are enormous — dark crystal trees, three meters tall, branching and rebranching in the powerful updraft, ringing with a sustained chord that is louder and more coherent than any other sound in this place.

Someone found the place where the stone was most alive, and they drew a ring around it, and they opened it wider.

## The Rules of the World

This is a world that is still being made. That is the first rule, and the last, and the only one that matters. Everything else follows from it.

The stone is growing. It is being pushed upward by heat and pressure from below. It arrives warm and soft and slowly cools and hardens and becomes part of the Rise. The Rise grows. The sealed world fills. This has been happening for a very long time and will continue to happen for a very long time, and the timescale is not a human timescale and the forces are not human forces and the process does not care whether anyone is here to watch.

The exhalation is the stone's breath. It is the gas that the stone gives off as it transforms, and it is the medium of this world's atmosphere, and it is the carrier of its weather, and it is the material from which the castings grow, and it is the substance in which the shapes dance. Everything that is not stone is exhalation. Everything that happens here happens because the stone is breathing.

The light is heat. The only illumination is the thermal glow of new stone, and it is barely illumination at all, and it fades as the stone ages, and the older parts of the Rise are in true darkness — not twilight, not dimness, but *darkness*, the absolute black of a world that has no sun and no moon and no stars and no artificial light and no bioluminescence and no reason to be bright. You go there and you are in the dark. You feel the warm stone under your hands and you hear the deep drone and you are in the dark and the dark is complete.

The castings grow where the exhalation flows. They are the place's mineral flora, its crystalline almost-life, and they are as close to a living ecosystem as this world has. They do not photosynthesize. They do not consume. They *precipitate*. They are the result of a phase change, a thermodynamic event, a moment when the exhalation cools enough that what was dissolved in it must come out, and what comes out builds. They are the place's evidence that form can emerge from force without intention.

The shapes in the exhalation-sea are the place's weather made briefly visible, and they are the place's closest approach to animation, and they mean nothing. They are beautiful. They are not beautiful on purpose. They are not anything on purpose.

---

## Standing Here

If you stood on the Rise at Tarnhelm — on the warm stone, in the near-dark, in the mineral fog, in the sound of the world growing — you would feel the heat through the soles of your feet. You would taste iron and sulfur on your tongue. You would see, in the dim thermal red, the slopes falling away on all sides into the pooled exhalation below, and the castings rising from the cracks like dark crystal undergrowth, and the shapes moving in the gas-sea like thoughts in a mind that is not thinking anything in particular.

You would hear the drone. You would hear the whisper of the exhalation. You would hear the castings ringing.

You would feel, more than hear, the deep structural groan of the stone as it lifts, as it *becomes*, as it pushes another millimeter into the darkness above.

And you would know — not think, not believe, but *know*, in your body, in the warm pressure of the air and the warm pressure of the stone and the warm dark red of the light — that you were standing inside something that was not finished. Not abandoned. Not ruined. Not dead. *Not finished*. A world in the middle of being born. A place that had not yet decided what it was going to be.

The marks in the stone — the grooves, the ring, the hole at the center — would tell you that someone else had stood here and felt this and decided to *participate*. To widen the crack. To draw the circle. To say, in the only language available: *I was here, and it was not done, and I helped.*

But they are gone, and the stone is still growing, and the exhalation is still flowing, and the castings are still building their dark crystal branches in the updraft, and the shapes are still dancing in the gas-sea, and the place is still becoming, and the place is still becoming, and the place is still becoming.
