# The Photic Hollows

Beneath the skin of the world, there is a city that was never young.

---

## The First Impression

You come down through a fissure — a crack in the basalt that widened, over ages no one counted, into a ramp. The light finds you before you find the light. It comes from below. It comes from everywhere below. By the time your eyes adjust, you realize you are already inside something built, something shaped, something that has been waiting in its final form longer than most geologies.

The Photic Hollows.

A city carved from the underside of a plateau, inverted in its logic. Not built upward from a floor but hollowed downward from a ceiling, chamber after chamber descending in irregular terraces, connected by ramps and stairs and passages that someone cut with simple tools — chisels, maybe, or harder stones used as wedges — and then abandoned to their own persistence. The work is too even to be natural, too old to be deliberate in any way that matters now. The chisel marks have been softened by ten thousand years of mineral water running down the walls, leaving the surfaces smooth as skin, rippled, almost organic. Almost.

And it is bright. That is the disorienting thing.

Not bright like a lamp. Bright like limestone under noon sun, bright like a salt flat, bright like the inside of a geode when it cracks open. The light has no single source. It radiates from the stone itself — from veins of something crystalline threaded through the rock, pale amber and gray-white, pulsing with a luminescence that has no rhythm. Not fire. Not bioluminescence. Something mineral, something in the chemistry of the stone itself, activated by moisture and pressure and the slow decay of isotopes no one named. The light does not flicker. It shifts — sometimes brighter in one corridor, dimmer in another, responding to changes in the water that seeps through the rock, to the weight of the earth above, to nothing anyone can predict.

You can see for a hundred meters in any direction. The far walls glow. The floor under your feet glows, where the crystal veins breach the surface. Your own shadow stretches behind you, sharp-edged, then dissolves into the general brightness.

---

## The Shape of the Place

The Hollows descend in roughly concentric tiers. The uppermost — the one you enter through — is the most weathered, the most open. Walls have partially collapsed here, and the ceilings have sagged, so the rooms flow into each other without clear borders. You walk through what might have been a corridor and find yourself in what might have been a plaza and then in what might have been a cellar, all without passing through a door. The stonework is uniform in its vagueness. Whoever cut this place had a method but no aesthetic. Rooms are roughly rectangular. Ceilings are roughly flat. Pillars, where they exist, are roughly columns. Everything is *roughly* — as if the builders were working from memory of a city they'd once seen rather than from a plan.

Below the first tier, the structure firms up. Walls stand straighter. Corridors extend farther between intersections. The chisel work becomes visible again — small, regular dents in the stone, each one the width of a thumb, each one made by the same tool, the same hand, the same hundred hands over the same hundred years. You can trace the work gangs by the spacing of the marks. This section: tight, precise, the work of patient people. This section: wider, rougher, the work of people who were tired or hungry or dying.

The third tier down is the most intact and the deepest anyone has gone. The rooms here are larger — halls, maybe, or gathering spaces — and the crystal veins in the stone are thicker, brighter, so that the light in these deep chambers is almost uniform, almost shadowless. The walls glow. The floor glows. The ceiling, thirty meters up, glows. You stand in a sphere of soft amber radiance and you cannot tell where the light begins.

Below the third tier, the passages narrow and the crystal dims and the water rises and no one goes there.

The whole complex covers an area that would take half a day to cross on foot — a town, maybe, or a small city, carved entirely out of living rock, entirely underground, entirely lit by the stone itself. From the highest entrance to the lowest explored passage, it descends nearly two hundred meters. Not deep by mining standards. Deep enough that the earth's warmth is perceptible — a faint heat in the walls, as if the stone remembers fire.

---

## The Water

Water is everywhere and nowhere in the Hollows. It does not flow. It seeps.

You see it first as stains on the walls — dark patches where moisture has been wicked through the rock, mineral deposits left behind in pale trails. Then as drops, forming on the ceiling, falling with a regularity that is almost a clock. Then as thin films, spreading across the floors in the lower tiers, making the glowing stone slippery, reflecting the light upward so that the surfaces double themselves.

In the deeper chambers, the water pools. Not lakes — nothing so grand. Puddles, gathered in the low points of the uneven floors, shallow and still, warm from the stone. They reflect the amber light perfectly. When you step in one, the reflection breaks and reforms behind you. The water is clean — mineral clean, the way water is clean when it has been filtered through a hundred meters of limestone and has nothing organic left in it. It tastes of calcium and iron and nothing else.

After rain on the surface — and it rains sometimes, in this country, brief and cold and half-hearted — the seepage increases. The drops from the ceiling speed up. The puddles widen. The crystal veins brighten, as if the water activates them, pushing the luminescence to a higher register. For a day or two after a storm, the Hollows blaze. The light is almost white. The shadows almost vanish. The water on the floor becomes a mirror and the city doubles, ceiling reflected in floor, corridor reflected in corridor, and the whole place feels as if it is opening, as if it is about to become something else.

Then the seepage slows. The light dims. The puddles shrink. The Hollows settle back into their usual amber half-brightness and wait for the next rain.

---

## The Inhabitants

There are no plants in the Hollows. No moss, no fungi, no lichen, no algae. Nothing green, nothing growing, nothing that photosynthesizes or chemosynthesizes or does any of the quiet work of life. The stone is bare. The water is clear. The air smells of mineral and nothing else.

But the place is not empty.

The creatures here — the Hollows have their own name for them, or had, when there were people to name things — are not animals in any familiar sense. They are motion. They are pattern. They are the thing that happens in the crystal when the light and the water and the stone reach a certain agreement.

You see them first as distortions. A ripple in the surface of a puddle that has no cause. A brightening in one patch of wall that moves, slowly, from left to right, as if something is walking behind the stone. A shadow that shouldn't be there, cast by nothing, drifting across the floor with the deliberateness of something that has somewhere to be.

They are everywhere once you know how to look.

They move through the crystal veins the way fish move through water — slipping, flowing, changing direction without visible effort. When they reach a patch of crystal that breaks the surface, they emerge. Not fully. Not as bodies. As light. As a concentration of brightness that pulls free of the wall and hangs in the air, shapeless, shimmering, the size of a hand or a head or a torso, and then sinks back into the stone. Some of them are large. Some of them move in groups — three, five, a dozen bright spots traveling together through the same vein, coordinating their movement, adjusting their speed to match each other. Some are solitary and slow and seem to drift rather than travel.

They make no sound. They leave no trace. They do not respond to human presence in any way that anyone has been able to determine. They are not alive in any way that anyone has been able to determine, either. They have no bodies. They have no mass. They do not eat, do not reproduce, do not die. They are events. They are what the crystal does when it is excited.

But there are so *many* of them. In the lower tiers, where the crystal veins are thick and the light is strong, the walls are in constant motion — bright spots moving in every direction, emerging and submerging, clustering and dispersing, a traffic of light in the rock. The floor, where the crystal breaks through, is a stage. Shapes rise from it, hang for a moment, descend. The air above the puddles shimmers with them. They are the population of this city, and they are abundant, and they have been here longer than the city has, longer than the stone, maybe, longer than the light.

What they are, no one knows. The people who built the Hollows — or found them, or expanded them, or whatever they did — left no records that survived. Only the chisel marks. Only the rooms. Only the stone.

---

## The Turbulence

The Photic Hollows are not still.

This is the thing that surprises. The stone is ancient. The city is ancient. The light has been burning for longer than the concept of burning. Everything about the place suggests permanence, finality, the end-state of a process that ran its course eons ago and stopped.

But the light-creatures move. The water shifts. The crystal pulses. And every few hours — sometimes more often, sometimes less — the Hollows *change*.

A tremor runs through the stone. Not an earthquake. Something smaller, something local, something that seems to originate in the crystal itself. You feel it in your feet first — a vibration, low and sustained, like a note played on an instrument too large to see. The walls hum. The puddles shiver. The light-creatures accelerate.

Then the veins realign.

It happens slowly. You can watch it. The crystal in one wall brightens to an intense, hot white, while the crystal in an adjacent wall dims to almost nothing. The bright patch spreads, following the vein, branching at intersections, flowing like liquid into new channels. The light-creatures follow it — pouring into the bright vein, swarming, clustering, so that the illuminated path becomes a river of moving shapes. New veins open. Old veins close. The pattern of light in the room shifts, restructures itself, and a corridor that was dark a moment ago is now blazing, while a corridor that was bright is now dim.

It is not random. There is a logic to it — a pattern that almost resolves, almost becomes predictable, and then breaks. The realignment cascades through the Hollows, tier by tier, room by room, until the entire complex has reorganized its lighting. New paths appear. Old paths vanish. The city rearranges itself.

And then it stops. The tremor ends. The creatures slow. The light settles into its new configuration and holds.

Until the next time.

The people who come here — and people do come, now, from the surface settlements a day's walk away, with their chisels and their stone lamps and their ignorance — have learned to carry markers. Stacked stones. Scratches on the walls. The light-creatures ignore these. The water does not wash them away. They persist. And they are necessary, because after a realignment, the Hollows are not the same place you entered. The room you slept in is dark. The corridor you followed is closed. The passage to the surface has moved — not physically, the stone hasn't shifted, but the light has gone from it, and the dark passages in the Hollows are passages in rock, lit by nothing, and the crystal in them is dead, and no one walks them.

You find your way by the light. You find your way by the creatures, who always travel the living veins. You find your way by the water, which always seeps toward the active crystal. You follow the brightness and the moisture and the movement, and you come out.

Mostly.

## The Atmosphere

The air is cool. Not cold — cool, the way a cellar is cool, the way a cave is cool, the way the inside of the earth is cool when the sun has never touched it. You do not need a coat. You notice the temperature only when you stop moving, when you stand in one of the deep chambers and the stone's chill seeps through your boots and into your bones.

The air is damp. Not wet — damp, the way a morning is damp, the way a towel left hanging is damp. You feel it on your skin, a thin film of moisture that does not evaporate because the air is already full. It carries the smell of the stone — a flat, mineral smell, like wet chalk, like clay, like the inside of a kiln that has cooled. No rot. No growth. No organic scent of any kind. The Hollows smell like nothing that was ever alive.

The silence is not silence. The Hollows are too large for true silence, too full of water and stone and the small movements of the deep earth. But the sounds are so quiet, so sparse, so widely spaced, that they register less as sound and more as events. A drop falls from a ceiling thirty meters up and strikes a puddle and the sound travels through the chamber and dies in the walls and you wait for the next one. The crystal hums at a frequency below hearing — you feel it more than you hear it, a pressure in your chest, a fullness in your ears. The light-creatures make nothing. The water makes almost nothing. The stone makes nothing at all.

When the realignments come, there is sound — a low groan, a vibration that rises into audibility, that makes the walls sing in frequencies you feel in your teeth. It lasts ten seconds. Twenty. Then it drops below hearing again and the new pattern holds and the silence returns, and the silence is louder than the sound was.

---

## The World's Rules

The Photic Hollows operate on principles that are stable but not understood. This is the rule: you do not need to understand a thing to live with it. The people who use the Hollows do not understand them. They do not try. They have theories — the crystal is a kind of metal, the light-creatures are spirits, the realignments are the breathing of something vast and buried — but the theories are stories, not knowledge, and everyone knows the difference.

What they know is this:

The light shows you where to go. The dark shows you where not to go.
The water follows the light. Follow the water.
The creatures follow the water. Follow the creatures.
When the stone moves, stand still. When the stone stops, move.
Do not touch the bright crystal when it is bright. It burns — not with heat, but with light, a light that leaves marks on the skin that glow for days and do not fade.
Do not enter the dark passages. Not because something is in them. Because nothing is in them — no light, no water, no creatures, no air that moves. People who enter the dark passages do not come back, and no one has ever found their bodies, because no one has ever gone deep enough into the dark to look.

The Hollows give nothing and take nothing. They are not a mine — the crystal cannot be extracted. They are not a home — the damp and the cold and the realignments make permanent habitation impossible. They are a passage. A place you move through. A place you visit.

But people come. They come because the light is beautiful. They come because the creatures are mesmerizing. They come because there is nothing else like this place, because it is a city that builds itself and empties itself and rebuilds itself in a new shape every few hours, and because standing in the middle of it during a realignment, surrounded by moving light and shifting stone and the silence of a world that has no use for words, is an experience that does not translate into language and does not need to.

---

## The Last Thing

At the deepest explored point of the Hollows — the third tier, a hall with a ceiling so high it vanishes into brightness, a floor covered in still water that reflects the crystal veins so perfectly that the room seems to extend downward forever — there is a marking on the wall.

Not chisel marks. Not scratches. A pattern, cut into the stone with a tool no one has identified, filled with a dark mineral deposit that has hardened over the centuries into a surface like glass. The pattern is not decorative. It is not language. It is not a map. It is a shape — a branching, asymmetrical, organic shape that looks, if you squint, like the pattern of the crystal veins in the walls around it. As if someone, a long time ago, looked at the light moving through the stone and tried to draw what they saw.

The light-creatures pass through the marking sometimes. When they do, they brighten. For a moment — just a moment — the pattern on the wall glows with a light that is not the crystal's light, that is something else, something older, something that was put there before the city was carved, before the chisels, before the people.

Then the creature moves on. The pattern dims. The wall is just a wall again.

And the Hollows go on being what they are. A city of light in the body of the earth, inhabited by nothing, visited by no one who stays, changing and changing and never aging, settled into a final form that is not a form at all but a process, an endless rearrangement of the same stone and the same water and the same light, and it has been doing this since before the word for *ancient* was spoken, and it will be doing it long after the word is forgotten.

The Photic Hollows. Go and see. Follow the light. Do not follow the dark. Stand still when the world moves. Move when it stops.

It is enough.
