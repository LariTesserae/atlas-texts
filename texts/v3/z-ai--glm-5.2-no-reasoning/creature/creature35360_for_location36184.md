# What Lives at the Ascendant Veil

---

## The Obvious Living

The Tower-Growths live here. This goes without saying, though it cannot be said enough. They are the Veil's signature organism, its voice, its architecture. Thousands of them. Amber-barked, violet-tipped, hollow-stemmed, singing the sustained chord that defines the place. They are born from fissures, they grow toward light that does not truly arrive, they mature over centuries, they die in slow cracking collapses that take hours and add their descending wheeze to the chorus as a kind of funeral note before the fallen stalk dries and hollows and is absorbed by the creeping mats over years. They do not know they are singing. They are metabolizing. The sound is exhalation made structural. They are the largest things here and they are the loudest and they are, in the way that the word is usually used, the most alive.

The creeping mats live here. Dark green-black, spreading, patient, an inch per year, consuming stone and dead tissue with equal indifference, the oldest among them perhaps a thousand years old and still growing, still converting mineral to biology. They are the Veil's floor life, its understory, its janitorial corps. They are warm to the touch. They do not sing. They do not need to.

And there are the smaller organisms — the fissure-edge sulfur metabolizers, the micro-growths that cling to the warm stone where the gas vents are less intense, the things that have no common names because no one has spent enough time here to name them. They live in the crevices and the margins. They are the Veil's quiet majority.

These are the obvious living. They are not what I want to describe.

## The Ones Who Stayed

The people who built the six stone buildings at the eastern edge lived here, briefly. They were not the first to climb to the plateau but they were the first to attempt residence, and they are the ones whose presence is still material. Their tools. Their cordage. Their stone cup stained with Tower-Growth fluid. Their tally marks on interior walls.

What kind of people were they?

The oral histories — what fragments survive — suggest they were not pilgrims. They were not seekers or mystics or people drawn by the sound. They were practical people. Surveyors, perhaps, or explorers of the kind that climbs because the height is there and the knowledge is not. They came in a group. They came with supplies. They built shelters because the plateau offered no natural shelter and the wind, while not fierce, was constant and cold enough to make sleeping on bare stone miserable. They built with what they had — the plateau's own raw stone, fitted dry, walls rough but functional, roofs presumably of woven fiber, now gone.

They intended to stay. The buildings say this. No one builds six structures for a brief expedition. The tally marks say this — someone was counting something, tracking time perhaps, or recording observations, attempting the beginning of a systematic study. The stone cup says this — someone was collecting Tower-Growth fluid, testing it, exploring its properties. These were people doing work.

They did not stay.

The sound drove them out. Not quickly — they lasted long enough to build, to tally, to collect, to attempt. But the Veil-drift came faster than they expected, or it came in ways they did not expect. The buildings are at the eastern edge, the quietest part of the plateau, and even there the chorus is overwhelming by the standards of a mind that needs silence to think. They would have slept poorly. They would have woken with headaches, with disorientation, with the sense that their thoughts were not quite their own. They would have struggled to communicate — shouting over the sound, mishearing, eventually giving up and communicating by gesture, by touch, by the kind of grimaced shorthand that people develop in environments where speech is impossible.

And then one of them sat down and stopped speaking and stopped eating and listened until they died.

This is the person under the cairn. We do not know their name. We know they were part of the group. We know their companions could not carry them down and could not leave them exposed and could not burn them because nothing here burns. We know the cairn was built with some care — it is waist-high, which is more than a casual piling of stones. It is a marker. It is a grave. It is, perhaps, a warning, though the warning was not heeded because the people who might have heeded it did not come for centuries.

The others left. They climbed down. They carried with them the knowledge that the Veil was uninhabitable, and they carried the beginnings of the oral history, and they carried whatever the sound had done to them — the lingering drift, the persistent humming in the bones, the sense that some part of them had not fully come back down the mountain. They carried it down. They carried it home. They gave it to their children in the form of stories, and their children gave it to their children, and three generations of long-lived trees later, the stories are still being told, though they have changed, as stories do, and the Veil they describe is not quite the Veil that is, because the Veil that is has been rising and changing and growing louder while the stories remained fixed.

These people lived here. They do not live here now. They are present in their leavings — the stone walls, the tools, the marks, the cairn with its slow lean and its creeping colonization by the dark mats. They are present in the way that absences are present, in the shape of what is missing, in the silence where their voices should be, beneath the sound that took their voices away.

## The Ones Who Did Not Leave

The cairn person is not the only one.

Think about what the accounts say. The deep center of the Veil, where the oldest Tower-Growths stand and the chorus is densest, is a place that no one has entered and returned from with coherence intact. The question of whether anyone has entered and not returned at all is left open in the records because the people who might not have returned would not have left a record.

But they would have left something.

The Veil does not destroy. It un-invents. It loosens bonds. It returns form to formlessness. This is not a rapid process. A body — a human body, clothed, carrying tools, perhaps carrying a notebook — would not vanish. It would undergo a slow transformation. The fibers of the clothing would loosen. The leather of the boots would soften. The metal of the tools would become less rigid, more compliant. The skin, the hair, the fingernails — translucent first, then softer, then less distinct from the air around them. The bones would hold longest. Bone is dense, mineral, already halfway to stone. But even bone, given enough time in the deep center, in the place where the sound is not merely sound but the loosening of the story that things are made of — even bone would eventually become ambiguous.

Not gone. Not destroyed. Ambiguous. No longer clearly a body. No longer clearly not a body. Something that is in the process of becoming stone, of becoming mineral, of becoming part of the plateau that is still rising.

How many? We cannot know. The buildings were built by a group — perhaps six people, perhaps ten, perhaps more. One is under the cairn. The others left. But the buildings are old. Centuries old. Other visitors came after the builders left and before the surveyors stopped coming. The oral histories mention travelers, seekers, people who climbed to the Veil alone for reasons that the histories do not preserve because the people who came back down did not understand the reasons of the people who did not come back down.

Some of them went to the deep center. Some of them did not come back.

What remains of them is not a body. Not a skeleton. Not a pile of belongings. What remains is a stain in the stone, a discoloration, a patch of mineral that is slightly softer than the surrounding stone, slightly warmer, slightly more ambiguous in its structure. A shape, perhaps, if you know how to look. A suggestion of a shape. The impression that the stone here is not quite stone, that it is in a transitional state, that it remembers something it used to be.

The deep center of the Veil is full of these. Not many. Not a graveyard. But a few. A dozen, perhaps, over the centuries. Impressions in the rising stone. Shapes that are not shapes. Memories that the stone holds the way the stone holds everything here — not consciously, not intentionally, but thoroughly, in the way that a thing that has been made into stone holds the form of the thing it was before it was stone.

They live here, in a sense. They are not dead. The Veil does not kill. It un-makes. The difference is significant. Death is a boundary — a thing is alive, and then it is dead, and the line between is sharp. Un-making is not a boundary. It is a gradient. A slow loosening. The people who went to the deep center did not die. They became ambiguous. They entered a state where the categories of living and dead, of person and not-person, of self and environment, no longer apply.

Are they still there? In the stone? In the soft warm patches of mineral that hold the impression of a human shape?

Perhaps. Perhaps not. The question may not have an answer, because the Veil is a place where questions dissolve along with the things that ask them.

## The In-Between Things

There are things at the Veil that are not Tower-Growths and are not creeping mats and are not people and are not stone. They live in the spaces between categories, in the way that the Veil itself lives in the space between geological and biological, between the made and the un-made.

Consider the haze. The haze is not merely atmospheric. It is not merely moisture or mineral dust suspended in thin air. It has a composition. It contains the evaporated fluid of Tower-Growth stems — the luminous, heavy, not-water that drifts upward when a stem is cut or cracks or when the canopy sheds its finest overtones and the smallest twigs burst and release their contents. This fluid does not dissipate in the way that water vapor dissipates. It lingers. It accumulates. It joins the general haze and gives the air its density, its taste, its faint luminosity.

Is the haze alive? The question is wrong. The haze is not an organism. But it is not not-alive, either. It is a byproduct of life, a secretion, an exhalation — the way that oxygen is a byproduct of photosynthesis, the way that carbon dioxide is a byproduct of respiration. The haze is what the Veil breathes out. It fills the space between the Tower-Growths. It is the medium through which the sound travels. It is the thing that makes the light strange, scattering it, diffusing it, giving it no direction.

And the haze does something that purely physical haze would not do. It responds to the sound. When the chorus shifts — when a new growth adds its voice, when an old growth cracks and falls — the haze shifts too. It thickens in some places. It thins in others. It forms patterns that are not random, that have structure, that persist for hours or days before slowly dissipating. These patterns are not visible to the naked eye in the way that clouds are visible — they are too diffuse, too subtle. But they can be felt. A person walking through the Veil's interior moves through zones of thicker and thinner haze, and the thicker zones correspond to the denser parts of the chorus, and the thinner zones correspond to the quieter spaces, and the correspondence is not coincidence.

The haze is the sound, made material. Or the sound is the haze, made audible. Or they are the same thing, expressed in two different media.

This is an in-between thing. Not alive. Not dead. Not stone. Not gas. Something that the Veil produces because the Veil is a place where the boundaries between what things are and what they are not are themselves loosened, made permeable, made ambiguous.

---

Consider also the shadows.

The shadows at the Veil do not behave correctly. This is stated in the accounts. A standing person casts two shadows — one where expected, one at a right angle, fainter, as though cast by a sun that does not exist. The edges of shadows are too sharp in some places and absent in others.

Shadows are not alive. Shadows are the absence of light, which is not a thing but a condition. But at the Veil, where light itself is ambiguous — arriving from no clear source, scattered by the haze, emanating perhaps from the stone or the air or something else — the shadows take on a quality that shadows elsewhere do not have. They persist longer than they should. They have texture. They have weight, almost — the sense that a shadow is not merely an absence but a presence, a thing that is there, that has substance, that occupies space.

The second shadow — the one at the right angle, the one cast by a sun that does not exist — is the most troubling. It is always fainter than the primary shadow. It is always at the same angle, regardless of the time of day or the position of the apparent light source. It is cast by something. The question is what.

One possibility: the second shadow is cast by the deep sound. The fundamental note that comes from below the plateau, the note that has been sounding longer than the Tower-Growths have been growing. Sound is a pressure wave. Pressure waves interact with light. This is known. But the effect is infinitesimal under normal conditions — you cannot see a shadow cast by sound. At the Veil, conditions are not normal. The sound is not normal. The thing beneath the sound is not normal. If the deep note is doing to light what the chorus does to matter — loosening it, un-making it, creating gaps and absences where coherence should be — then the second shadow is the shadow of the sound itself, the place where the deep note has displaced light, has created an absence that has weight and angle and persistence.

This is speculation. No one has been able to study it. No one has been able to stay long enough.

But the shadows live here, in the sense that they are present, that they are not merely effects, that they have qualities that suggest something more than the simple blocking of photons by an opaque body. They are in-between things. Not light, not dark, not presence, not absence. Something the Veil produces in the space between categories.

## What Lives Deepest

Below the plateau, below the stone, below the fissures and the venting gas and the roots of the Tower-Growths — which are not roots but anchors, which are not anchors but extensions of the stem into the warm mineral substrate — below all of this, something is pushing upward.

This is stated plainly in the accounts. The plateau is rising. The crust is bulging. Forces beneath the surface, not fully understood, are driving the Veil upward at a rate of one finger-width per year. The deep note — the single low steady tone beneath the chorus, the sound of something arriving — comes from below.

What is it?

The accounts do not say. The surveyors did not know. The oral histories do not address it because the people who generated the oral histories did not know that the plateau was rising — they had no framework for measuring the movement of geological formations over decades. They knew the Veil was there. They knew it was strange. They knew it was dangerous. They did not know it was moving.

But it is moving. And the thing beneath it is moving. And the thing beneath it is not a geological process in the ordinary sense.

Consider: the plateau is not a volcanic uplift. There is no magma. The stone is warm but not molten. The gases venting from the fissures are not volcanic gases — they are stranger, denser, carrying compounds that exist nowhere else because the conditions that produce them exist nowhere else. The crust is not being pushed up by a plume of hot rock in the way that the crust of Hawaii is pushed up by a mantle plume. Something else is happening.

The deep note is the clue. It has been sounding for longer than the Tower-Growths have been growing. It will continue sounding after they are gone. It is steady. It is patient. It is not a byproduct of a process. It is the process. It is the sound of the thing itself, whatever the thing is, expressed as vibration, felt in the bone and the marrow and the fluid of the inner ear.

The people who stood at the eastern edge and listened — not with their ears but with the rest of them, with their bones and their blood and the spaces between their cells — heard it. They were warned not to listen long. They were told to go down.

What lives deepest at the Veil is the thing that is arriving. Not the Tower-Growths, which are its surface expression. Not the gases, which are its exhalation. Not the chorus, which is the sound of its surface processing. The thing itself. The thing beneath. The thing that has been arriving for longer than human memory and that will continue to arrive for longer than human futures.

It does not have a name. It does not need one. It is below the level at which names operate. It is below the level at which categories operate. It is the thing that pushes the stone upward and vents the gas and feeds the Tower-Growths and creates the conditions under which the chorus can form and the chorus can do what the chorus does, which is to un-make the made, to loosen the bonds, to return form to formlessness.

Is it alive? This is the wrong question. The question assumes a binary — alive or not alive — and the Veil is a place where binaries dissolve. The thing beneath is not alive in the way that a Tower-Growth is alive. It is not dead in the way that stone is dead. It is something else. Something that the word "alive" points toward but does not reach. Something that the word "process" points toward but does not reach. Something that the word "arriving" points toward and almost reaches, because "arriving" implies a destination, and the thing beneath may not have a destination. It may simply be arriving. It may be the act of arrival itself, without a thing that arrives and without a place that is arrived at.

This is not satisfying. This is not clear. This is not an answer.

But the Veil is not a place that provides answers. It is a place that dissolves them.

---

## What Lives Here, Finally

Everything I have described lives here. The Tower-Growths, with their amber stems and violet canopies and hollow pipes singing the chord that has been building for centuries. The creeping mats, dark and warm and patient, eating the stone one molecule at a time. The smaller organisms, the sulfur-metabolizers, the fissure-edge growths. The haze, which is not alive but which is not not-alive, which is the breath of the Veil made visible. The shadows, which are not alive but which have weight. The people who stayed — the builders, the cairn person, the ones who went to the deep center and did not come back and are now ambiguous stains in the rising stone. The thing beneath, which is arriving, which has always been arriving, which will always be arriving, which is the reason the plateau rises and the gases vent and the Tower-Growths grow and the chorus sounds and the bonds loosen and the forms return to formlessness.

And one more thing.

You live here, if you come. Not for long. Not permanently. But for the time you are present, you are part of the Veil. Your bones resonate. Your breath joins the haze. Your shadows fall at angles that should not be possible. Your body begins the slow process of becoming ambiguous — the fingernails translucent, the fibers loosened, the boundaries between self and sound and stone and haze beginning to blur.

The Veil does not want you. The Veil does not not-want you. The Veil is a process, and you, arriving, become part of the process. You become a shape the sound is making. You become a sentence in a language made of pitch. You become a note in the chord, briefly, for the duration of your visit, and when you leave, the note is subtracted, and the chord shifts, and the Veil goes on.

But part of you does not leave. Part of you stays. The part that the sound touched. The part that began to loosen. The part that became ambiguous. That part is still there, still in the stone, still in the haze, still in the chorus, a small contribution to the great sustained chord, a tiny alteration in the harmonic structure that persists after you are gone, that will persist as long as the Veil persists, which is to say as long as the thing beneath continues to arrive.

The Veil keeps everyone who visits. Not their bodies. Not their minds. A trace. A residue. An impression in the stone, in the haze, in the sound. Small. Imperceptible. But real.

This is what lives at the Ascendant Veil. Everything. Everything that has ever climbed to it. Everything that has ever grown from it. Everything that has ever sounded within it. Everything that is arriving beneath it.

And the sound goes on. And the plateau rises. And the thing beneath continues to come.

You should go down.
