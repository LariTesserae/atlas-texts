# What Lives Here

---

## The Keeper

It is not standing at the center. It is never standing. You would not see it if it were, because you are looking for the wrong shape — a body, a posture, something that occupies space the way you occupy space. It does not.

But it is there. It has been there longer than the cairn.

The structure at the center was built by something that no longer exists in any form you would recognize — not because it died but because it *became*. Whatever intelligence shaped those stones and cut those grooves did not leave the plateau. It sank. It pressed itself down through the compressite the way the root-mat presses down, the way the warmth presses up, and at some depth that your instruments could not plumb without drilling — and you should not drill — it settled into the mineral substrate and it *continued*. Not as a corpse. Not as a fossil. As a pattern. A slow heat that thinks. A gradient that remembers.

The warmth from below is its breath.

The grooves on the cairn are not instructions. They are not a name. They are a song — the first song, the one it sang when it was still a thing with a mouth and hands and a reason to make sound in the dark. Before it gave itself to the stone. Before it became the ground the orchestra stands on.

It hears everything. The stalk-song is its language, its weather, its sensory surface. Each shift in the wind is a syllable it reads. Each Walker that rasps a stalk and changes its pitch is a word in a sentence the Keeper is composing without knowing it is composing — or perhaps knowing fully, perhaps the knowing is the only thing it retained when it shed everything else. It cannot hear your breathing. It can hear your weight. Your footsteps are discordant. It is tolerating you.

It has tolerated others. The grooves on the cairn are worn in places that suggest retouching, maintenance, the careful hand of something that still had hands. But the most recent marks are older than your grandmother's grandmother's memory. Nothing with hands has been here in a long time. The Keeper is alone in the way a conductor is alone: not isolated but *of* a different order than the orchestra. It is the reason. They are the sound.

You should not stay long. Not because it is hostile. Because it is *attentive*, and prolonged attention from something that large and that old — something that experiences your presence as a vibration in the mineral substrate of its body — will begin to change you. Not dramatically. Not violently. You will simply start to feel warm from within. You will start to hear harmonies in the stalk-song that you cannot explain. You will start to understand, in the vaguest sense, that the field is saying something, and that it is saying it *to you*, and that what it is saying is very old and very patient and not entirely kind.

It does not want you to leave. It does not want you to stay. It does not want. It is beyond wanting. It is in the phase after wanting, the phase where the wanting has become so complete that it has dissolved back into the thing it wanted, and the eater and the eaten are the same substance, and the song is the singer and the singer is the stone.

But it knows you are here.

## The Walkers

They are not animals.

Or they are, but the word is insufficient in the way that calling a sentence a collection of letters is insufficient. They are *meaning*. Each Walker is a phoneme. Each grazing choice — which stalk, which segment, how deep to rasp — is a selection in a grammar that has no authors because every author is also a word in the sentence. They do not know this. Or they know it entirely, the way your blood cells know the shape of your body — not through cognition but through *being*. They are of the Keeper. Extensions of its surface. The root-mat below is the Keeper's skin; the stalks are the Keeper's spine, its vertebrae made vertical; the Walkers are the Keeper's tongue, tasting and shaping and licking the world into sound.

When a Walker rasps a stalk and changes its pitch, the field reorganizes. A new chord forms. The Gliders adjust their flight paths — they do navigate by sound, you were right to suspect this — and the gas-globes that were about to detach stay attached for another hour, or release early. The entire plateau shifts in response to a single animal the size of your hand taking one bite.

The Walkers do not care. They are eating. They are warm and they are dark and they are many and they are doing the only thing they know how to do, which is the same thing the Keeper knows how to do, which is the same thing the plateau knows how to do: *continue*. Continue in a way that does not repeat. Continue in a way that composes.

If you pick one up — and you should not, but you could, they are light and dry and surprisingly fragile, the shell crackling under gentle pressure — it would not struggle. It would go still. Not in the way a frightened animal goes still, playing dead. In the way a word goes still when you take it out of a sentence. It would be incomplete. It would be waiting, with a patience that is not its own, to be placed back into the grammar that gives it function.

You could feel, if you held it long enough, a faint warmth. The same warmth as the stone. The same warmth as the root-mat. The same warmth as the cairn. Everything here has the same warmth because everything here is the same temperature, and everything here is the same temperature because everything here is the same body, and the body is warm because deep below the compressite, something that used to have a shape is still, after all this time, burning.

Put it back. Put it back gently.

## The Gliders

They are the closest thing this place has to thoughts.

Not the Keeper's thoughts — the Keeper does not think in the linear sense, does not have the sequential, temporal experience of *one thing and then another*. The Keeper is simultaneous. The Gliders are sequential. They move in trajectories. They choose. They hunt. They fold their membranes and dive and carry their prey above the stalk-tops and they *go somewhere* with intention, which is something nothing else on the plateau does.

The Walkers do not go anywhere. They graze. They move, but the way a river moves — because the geometry of their situation demands it. The Gliders go *to*. They have destinations. They have preferences. If you watch them long enough — and you have time, you have nothing but time, the plateau does not recognize your urgency — you will see that individual Gliders have individual patterns. One hunts the eastern cluster, always, returning to the same thermal column after each kill. Another patrols the perimeter, never diving, never killing, riding the wind in wide circles that suggest not hunger but *attention*. Watchfulness. A sentry.

They are not intelligent. Not in the way you mean when you say the word. They do not plan. They do not remember in any way you could verify. But they *decide*, and they decide differently from one another, and that difference — that irreducible individuality in creatures that are essentially transparent kites — is the only thing on this plateau that acts like what you would call a mind.

The Keeper is the body. The Walkers are the grammar. The Gliders are the attention. The gas-globes are —

## The Globes and Their Passengers

The gas-globes are eggs.

Not in the reproductive sense. In the *semantic* sense. They are the plateau's attempts to say something it cannot say at ground level — the way a word that cannot be spoken in one register rises to another. The gas inside them is not waste gas, not metabolic byproduct. It is *compelled*. The root-mat produces it at the base of the tallest stalks, and the stalks channel it upward — the same stalks that sing are also exhaling — and the globes form at the apex, filling, filling, filling until the buoyancy exceeds the stalk's ability to hold them, and they detach and rise.

And what they carry inside — the dark motes, the particulate darkness — is the message. Or the messenger. Or both.

The motes are not mites. They are not organisms in any structural sense. They are crystals. Tiny, dark, irregular crystals of a mineral compound that does not exist naturally — that is *biofabricated*, assembled molecule by molecule by the root-mat from the minerals it dissolves from the compressite. Each mote is a fragment of information. Not data in the way you understand data — not binary, not symbolic. Structural information. The way a seed contains a tree. The way a bone contains the shape of the body it was. Each mote is a compressed, crystallized *description* of the plateau's state at the moment the globe formed — the pitch of every singing stalk, the position of every Walker, the trajectory of every Glider, the pressure of the cloud-ceiling. A snapshot. A freeze-frame. Made of stone and heat and the same patience that made the cairn.

When the globe rises to the cloud-ceiling and the atmospheric pressure drops and the globe expands and ruptures, the motes scatter. They drift. They are so small that the air itself is a medium they cannot fall through. They hang, suspended, and they are carried — by the wind, by the thermals, by the same forces that move the Gliders — until they settle. On stone. On stalks. On the lichen. On the root-mat. On the cairn.

And when they settle, they are absorbed. The lichen incorporates them. The root-mat incorporates them. The stalks grow around them. And the information they carry — the snapshot, the description — becomes part of the system. The next chord is different because the last chord was recorded. The next gust produces different harmonics because the last gust's harmonics were crystallized and scattered and reabsorbed.

The plateau is listening to itself.

This is not memory. Memory implies a past and a present, a storage and a retrieval. This is something else. This is a system that incorporates its own history into its own structure continuously, without mediation, without retrieval. The past does not exist here as a thing remembered. It exists as a thing *being*. Every mote that settles on the root-mat changes the next stalk that grows from it. Every crystal that the lichen absorbs changes the next fractal iteration. The plateau does not remember its song. The plateau *is* its song, all of it, every moment of it, compressed into mineral and heat and the slow patience of stone.

The cloud-ceiling presses down. Something vast, on the other side, leans against the membrane. You have been not-looking at it.

What presses against the ceiling is the accumulated weight of every mote that has ever risen and not been absorbed. The crystals that were too small, too light, too high — they rose past the atmosphere, past the ceiling, and they accumulated. Over time. Over so much time. A sediment of information, pressing down. A sky made of every song the plateau has ever sung, crystallized and floating, leaning back down against the place that made it.

You should not look at it. But you understand now why you shouldn't. Not because it is dangerous. Because if you look long enough, you will see shapes in it, and the shapes will be the plateau, and the plateau will be you looking at it, and the recursion will —

## The Lichen

The lichen is the oldest thing here that you can see.

Older than the cairn. Older than the root-mat's oldest growth. The fractal-branching pale crust on the bare stone between clusters has been extending itself since before the stalks grew, since before the warmth rose, since before the Keeper — if the Keeper is what you think it is — settled into the stone.

The lichen is not alive in the way the Walkers are alive. It is alive the way a crystal is alive — or the way a mathematical formula is alive, each iteration generating the next from a logic that does not require a body to execute it. It extends because extension is its nature. It branches because branching is the shape of its logic. It does not eat. It does not grow. It *calculates*, and the calculation is visible, and the visible calculation is the pale yellow-white crust on the stone, and the crust is a working-out of something — a proof, perhaps, or a sequence, or a question asked in a language that precedes language, a language made only of *next*.

The lichen does not enter the depression. The lichen does not grow on the cairn. This is not because the cairn is sacred. It is because the lichen's calculation does not include the cairn. The cairn is outside the lichen's problem. The lichen has been working on the same problem for longer than the cairn has existed, and the cairn is not part of the solution.

You do not know what the problem is. You suspect the lichen does not know either. You suspect the lichen does not need to know. You suspect that knowing is something that happens to creatures with minds, and that the lichen is something else, something that does not need to know because it *is* the knowing, the way a river does not need to understand gravity.

If you sit on the bare stone between a cluster of stalks and a patch of lichen, and if you sit long enough, you will see the lichen extend by one iteration. It will take an hour. At the edge of the fractal, a new branch will appear — slowly, palely, as if being exhaled onto the stone. It will be the next step in a calculation that has been running since before you had a species. It will not notice you.

Nothing here notices you, except the Keeper, and the Keeper notices everything, and the Keeper has been noticing you since you entered the fissure below, since before you began to climb, since the moment your weight touched the compressite and the compressite transmitted the vibration down into the body of the thing that was once something and is now the ground.

## What You Are

You are an interruption. A grace note in a song that did not ask for one. The field will incorporate you if you stay — not welcome you, not reject you, *incorporate* you, the way it incorporates a Walker's bite, the way it incorporates a gust, the way it incorporates a crystal mote settling on the root-mat. If you stay, your warmth will become part of the warmth. Your weight will become part of the gradient. The carbon dioxide you exhale will be taken up by the root-mat's chemistry and transmuted into something the stalks can use, and a stalk that grows tomorrow will be slightly different because you breathed today, and the Walker that grazes that stalk will alter it differently, and the Glider that hunts that Walker will fly a different path, and the globe that forms at the apex of the stalk that grew from your breath will carry a different mote to a different place in the cloud.

You will be part of the song. You are already part of the song. You have been part of the song since you came up through the crack in the world.

The crack is the Keeper's ear. It is how the Keeper hears the outside. You came up through its ear canal and you are standing on its tympanic membrane and everything you do is a vibration it is receiving.

The field sings. The structure hums. The Walkers rasp. The globes drift and pop. The wind shifts, and shifts, and shifts.

The cairn at the center is warm. The grooves in it are a song that something sang before it became the ground. The song is very old. The song is the first thing the Keeper remembered when it learned to remember, which is the same as the first thing the plateau learned to say when it learned to speak, which is the same as the first thing the lichen calculated when it began to calculate, which is the same sound the wind made when it first moved through the first crack in the first stalk in the first moment the world had a world to be.

You can go back down. The way is behind you. The crack in the world is patient. It will let you leave.

But you are still standing here. And the field has shifted, and a new chord is forming, and it is not a chord that has ever been played before, and it will not be played again, and the only reason it exists is because you are standing exactly where you are standing, with exactly your weight, in exactly this wind.

The Keeper knows this. The Keeper has known this since before you arrived. The Keeper arranged for you to arrive. Or the Keeper did not arrange for you to arrive and you are here anyway and the difference between those two things is, to the Keeper, the same as the difference between two notes in a chord — a difference that matters only in the hearing.

You are being heard. You have been heard. You will continue to be heard until you leave and possibly after.

Go back down. Or stay. The field does not care, which is the same as the field caring completely, which is the same as the field being the thing that cares, which is the same as the field being the thing that the caring is for.

The wind shifts. The song changes. You are still standing here.
