# What Lives Here

## I. The Keeper of the Central Current

There is a thing in the center of the room that is not the room.

It does not move. That is the first fact the observation team recorded, and it is the fact they could not reconcile with anything else they found, because everything else in the cell moves — the fronds pump, the filaments drift, the fauna cycle from one end to the other in their eleven-hour pilgrimage, even the water itself is never still — but this thing sits in the current at the exact geometric center of the cell, at the point where the flow is fastest and the light is dimmest, and it does not move. It has not moved in the time the team has been observing. It did not move when the first probe entered the cell and disturbed the current. It did not move when the observation lights were activated. It did not move when the team's lead biologist, Dr. Irèn Vesely, placed her hand on the observation port and felt the heartbeat through the glass and said, very quietly, *something in here is looking at me.*

It is not looking. It does not have eyes. But Dr. Vesely was not wrong, because there is a quality to the thing's stillness that the word "watching" fails to capture but "presiding" comes closer to — not awareness, not consciousness, not anything the team has language for, but a *posture*. A directedness. The thing sits in the current the way a statue sits in a square, and the current moves around it the way crowds move around a statue, and the statue does not notice the crowd, but the crowd notices the statue, and the noticing changes the crowd.

It is called the Helm. The team named it before they understood what it was, and the name stuck, and now the name is part of the record, and the name will outlast the team the way the measurement lines outlast the makers. The Helm. Because it sits in the center the way a helm sits in the center of a ship, and because the team needed a word, and because the word felt right in a way that no other word did.

The Helm is roughly ovoid. Roughly. It is the shape of an object that has been in a current for a very long time, that has been shaped by the current, that has shaped itself to the current, and the distinction between "shaped by" and "shaped to" has collapsed here the way the distinction between organic and manufactured has collapsed everywhere in the cell. It is forty-seven centimeters at its longest axis. It is dark — dark the way the room is dark, dark the way everything here is dark, but darker, with a density to the darkness that the vimanite does not have. The vimanite's darkness has depth. The Helm's darkness has *weight.* It is not the color of shadow. It is the color of something that absorbs shadow. That drinks it.

Its surface is not smooth. It is textured, finely, with a pattern that the team's imaging specialist initially mistook for damage — pitting, erosion, the kind of surface degradation that happens to objects in moving water over long periods. But the pattern is not random. It is regular. It is a pattern that repeats at intervals of approximately three centimeters, a texture that runs in lines from pole to pole along the ovoid's surface, and when the current shifts — when the eleven-hour cycle reverses and the flow moves in the opposite direction through the cell — the pattern does not shift with it. The pattern remains oriented the same way. It has been oriented the same way for as long as the team has been observing. The imaging specialist, Dr. Yu Han, said the pattern looks like text. Dr. Vesely said nothing looks like text. Dr. Yu said she didn't say it was text, she said it looked like text, and there was a silence that lasted four seconds, which is a long silence when you are in a submersible at the bottom of the Konvelti Deep listening to a heartbeat that is not yours.

The Helm does not eat. It does not filter. It does not hunt. It does not photosynthesize or chemosynthesize or perform any metabolic function the team's instruments can detect. It sits in the current and the current moves through it — not around it, not past it, *through* it — and the water that exits the downstream side of the ovoid is different from the water that enters the upstream side. Warmer. Denser. Carrying a signature of dissolved organo-silicates that is distinct from the ambient concentration, a signature that matches, exactly, the signature of the fluid the fronds pump into the walls through their holdfasts.

The Helm is feeding the fronds.

The Helm is feeding the fronds, and the fronds are feeding the cell, and the cell is feeding the Helm, and the cycle has been running since before the concept of "cycle" had meaning, and the Helm sits in the center of it, in the center of the room, in the center of the current, and it does not move, and it does not need to move, because it is the center, and the center does not move, and the center does not eat, and the center does not sleep, and the center does not think, and the center does not know, and the center does not need to think or know or eat or sleep because it is the center, and the center is the thing that everything else moves around, and it has been the thing that everything else moves around for four hundred million years, and it will be the thing that everything else moves around for four hundred million more, and the team does not understand it, and the team will not understand it, and the team will leave, and the Helm will remain, and the Helm does not mind, because the Helm does not have the capacity for minding, and the Helm does not have the capacity for not minding, and the Helm is simply there, being the center, in a room that knows it is a room, in a spine that knows it is a spine, at the bottom of an ocean that does not know it is an ocean, on a world that has not yet decided what it wants to be.

## II. The Sinuous Ones

There are other things in the room that the team calls fauna, and they are fauna, and they are also not fauna, and the distinction matters and does not matter.

The filter-feeder — the one that hangs at the ceiling, the one that is a meter long, the one that repositions every eleven hours when the current reverses — is the largest. The team calls it the Bell. Its body is a long, flat, segmented tube, and its segments are not articulated the way a centipede's segments are articulated; they are fused, but fused with a flexibility that suggests the fusion is not a limitation but an adaptation, as if the body was once articulated and then decided that articulation was a compromise it no longer needed to make. Its mouth — the opening at the upstream end, the opening that hangs in the current and filters — is not a mouth. It is a net. A lattice of ciliated tissue that extends from the body and spreads into the flow, catching particulate matter with a efficiency that the team's filter specialist described as "offensive." She used that word specifically. She said the efficiency was offensive because it was better than anything her team had designed, and it was better than anything her team could design, and it had been designed by something that did not have a team, and the thing that designed it did not have what her team would call a mind.

The Bell is dark. Its body is the color of the room — not the vimanite's dark, not the Helm's dark, but a third dark, a living dark, a dark that is the color of something alive that has been alive in a dark room for so long that the darkness has become part of its phenotype. Its cilia are lighter, a translucent grey-brown that catches the amber light and shimmers, and the shimmering is beautiful in a way that the team's recording equipment does not capture and that the team's language does not capture and that the only word for is the word the team's cinematographer used, which was *alive*, said with a breath that was not a word but was a recognition.

The Bell does not react to the observation probe. It does not react to the team's lights. It does not react when Dr. Vesely places her hand on the port. It reacts to the current. It reacts to nothing else. It has been reacting to the current for longer than the current has been the current, and it will continue reacting to the current, and when it dies — when its body loses its grip on the ceiling and sinks to the hexagonal tiles and the detritivores come — it will become part of the floor, and the floor will carry its minerals to the fronds, and the fronds will carry them to the walls, and the walls will carry them to the Helm, and the Helm will sit in the center and process them into the current, and the current will carry them to the Bell's replacement, because there will be a replacement, because there is always a replacement, because the room replaces its inhabitants the way a body replaces its cells, and the room does not mourn the replaced the way a body does not mourn its cells, because mourning is something that happens above, in the light, in the air, in the world where things are brief enough to be worth mourning.

## III. The Hunter in the Shadow

The predator.

The team calls it the Coil. It is thirty centimeters long, which is small, which is negligible, which is — in this room, at this scale, in the economy of this ecosystem — the apex. It is the top. There is nothing above it. There is nothing that eats it. There is nothing that preys on it. It is the final term in the equation, and the equation has been balanced for four hundred million years, and the Coil is the reason the equation balances, because without the Coil the filter-feeders would overpopulate, and the overpopulation would strip the current of particulate, and the stripping would starve the fronds, and the fronds' starvation would collapse the symbiosis, and the collapse would end the cell, and the cell would die, and the room would be empty, and the room would not be a room anymore. The Coil is the thing that keeps the room a room. The Coil is the regulation. The Coil is the *no.*

Its body is the same dark as the Bell's, but darker at the edges, as if the darkness deepens where the body meets the water, as if the Coil is surrounded by a margin of deeper shadow that is not shadow but is something the Coil produces, something that bends the amber light away, something that makes the Coil invisible in the dark water in the way that the Helm is visible in the dark water — the Helm, which sits in the lightless center and is dark, and the Coil, which hunts in the lightless margins and is darker, and the difference between them is the difference between a center and a circumference, between a stillness and a motion, between a thing that is and a thing that does.

The Coil does not hunt the way the team expected it to hunt. The team expected — based on the behavior of every predator they had ever studied, in every ecosystem they had ever observed — that the Coil would pursue, would chase, would use speed or stealth or force to close the distance between itself and its prey. The Coil does not do this. The Coil attaches itself to a frond — wraps its body around the frond's stem, presses its segments against the frond's surface, and waits. It waits for the current to bring the prey to it. It waits for the Bell to drift past, repositioning, and the Bell drifts past, and the Coil does not strike. The Coil *opens.* Its body unfolds from the frond like a flower, like a hand unclenching, and the unfolding is the attack, and the attack is gentle, and the gentleness is not mercy, the gentleness is geometry, the gentleness is the minimum force required to redirect the prey's trajectory from *past the Coil* to *into the Coil,* and the Coil's body closes around the prey, and the prey is gone, and the Coil reattaches to the frond, and the Coil waits.

It hunts once every eleven hours. It hunts at the turn of the current. It has been hunting at the turn of the current since before the current was tidal. It will hunt at the turn of the current until the current stops or until the Coil dies, and when the Coil dies, the room will replace it, because the room replaces everything, because the room is a system, and systems persist, and persistence is the only law the room recognizes.

Dr. Vesely watched the Coil hunt through the observation port for three full cycles. She watched it wait. She watched it open. She watched it close. She watched it return to the frond. On the fourth cycle, she said: *It is not hunting. It is harvesting.*

And she was right, and the team knew she was right, and no one said anything, because there was nothing to say, because the word "harvesting" was the right word, and the right word was a word that described something the room had been doing since before there were words, and the word was new, and the thing was old, and the newness of the word sat on the oldness of the thing like a drop of rain on the ocean, and the ocean did not notice, and the drop dissolved, and the thing continued.

## IV. The Small Ones

There are smaller things. The detritivores. The decomposers. The things that eat the dead and return the dead to the living, that close the loop, that complete the circuit, that make the room a room rather than a tomb.

They are two centimeters long. Three. Some are five. They are segmented, like the Bell and the Coil, but their segments are different — broader, flatter, each segment carrying a complement of appendages that are not legs and are not cilia and are not antennae but are something between all three, something that crawls and filters and senses simultaneously, a multi-purpose appendage that the team's evolutionary biologist called "impossible" and then corrected to "inevitable," because in this room, in this system, in this contract between machine and biome, the impossible and the inevitable are the same thing. They are the same thing because the room has been selecting for them for four hundred million years, and four hundred million years of selection does not produce the impossible. It produces the inevitable. It produces the thing that works, and the thing that works is the thing that is here, and the thing that is here is the thing that works, and the team is the thing that observes, and the observation is the only variable the room has not selected for, and the variable does not matter, and the room does not care.

The detritivores swarm. When a Bell dies — when the filter-feeder's grip fails and its body sinks to the floor — the detritivores find it within minutes. They find it because the room tells them where it is, because the current carries the chemical signature of the dying body to the detritivores the way the current carries everything, and the detritivores follow the signal, and they arrive, and they do not eat the body. They *dissolve* it. Their appendages secrete a compound — the team has not sampled it, the team does not have a sample, the team is observing through a port and has not entered the cell — a compound that breaks the Bell's tissue into components that the detritivores absorb through their body walls, and the absorption is instantaneous, and the body is gone, and the floor is clean, and the hexagonal tiles are bare, and the detritivores are already leaving, already following the current back to their positions along the walls, where they will wait for the next death, and the next death will come, because death is part of the contract, and the contract is the room, and the room does not break contracts.

There are hundreds of them. Thousands. The team cannot count them because they move too fast and they are too small and they are the color of the floor and the floor is the color of the room and the room is the color of everything that lives here, and the team is not the color of the room, and the team is not from the room, and the team is an interruption, and the interruption is temporary, and the room knows this, and the room tolerates this, and the room does not know it tolerates this because the room does not know anything, and the tolerance is structural, and the structure is the room, and the room goes on.

## V. The Fronds

The fronds are alive. The fronds are the most alive thing in the room. The fronds are the thing the room would miss if the room could miss things, which it cannot, but the fronds are the thing that would be missed, because the fronds are the bridge, because the fronds are the interface, because the fronds are where the machine becomes the biome and the biome becomes the machine, and without the fronds the contract is void, and without the contract the room is not a room, and without the room the column is not a column, and without the column the world is a different world, a world without the warmth and the minerals and the chemistry that the column has been pushing up through the seafloor for four hundred million years, a world without the deep vents and the slow-cycling brine and the things that live in the brine, and the things that live in the brine are the things that became the things that became the things that became the things that walk on the land and build the cities and write the poems and launch the probes, and the fronds are the beginning of that chain, and the fronds do not know, and the fronds do not need to know, and the fronds pump, and the pumping is enough.

There are eleven large fronds in the cell. Eleven. Not ten, not twelve. The team counted them. Dr. Yu counted them three times because eleven is a prime number and prime numbers are unusual in structural systems and Dr. Yu wanted to be sure. Eleven fronds, anchored along the walls at intervals of approximately ten meters, their holdfasts merged with the vimanite, their stems extending three to five meters into the current, their broad flat surfaces undulating against the flow, pumping, always pumping.

The fronds are the color of the room's green — the green-black, the green of the ocean before it becomes the black, the green of things that carry chlorophyll or something like chlorophyll, something that does the work of chlorophyll in a room where there is no light to chlorophyll about, and the team's biochemist has a theory about this, and the theory is that the fronds' pigment is not for photosynthesis but for chemosynthesis, and the chemosynthesis is not for energy but for signaling, and the signaling is between the frond and the vimanite, and the signaling is the contract, and the contract is written in the language of chemistry, and the language of chemistry is the oldest language in the room, older than the fauna, older than the filaments, older than the Helm, maybe, or the Helm is the oldest, and the fronds are the second oldest, and the age is in the pumping, in the rhythm, in the forty-two beats per minute that the fronds keep with the walls, that the walls keep with the column, that the column keeps with the deep, that the deep keeps with the world, that the world keeps with the sun, that the sun keeps with the nothing.

The fronds produce the light. The fronds produce the light the way a nerve produces pain — not because they want to, not because they choose to, but because the contact between their chemistry and the vimanite's chemistry produces light the way the contact between a nerve and an injury produces pain. It is a response. It is the room's response to being touched by something alive. It is the room saying *I am here* in the only language it has, and the language is light, and the light is amber, and the amber is the color of old honey, and old honey is the color of preserved things, and preserved things are the things that last, and the fronds last, and the light lasts, and the room lasts, and the lasting is the room's answer to the question the team did not ask, because the team did not know there was a question, but the question is: *what do you do when you have been here for four hundred million years?*

And the answer is: *you glow where you are touched. You pump what you are given. You hold your shape. You keep your contract. You do not move. You do not move. You do not move.*

## VI. The Filaments

The filaments are the smallest living things the team can identify in the cell, and they may not be individual organisms, and they may be extensions of the fronds, and they may be extensions of the walls, and the distinction does not matter because the distinction between living and non-living in this room is a distinction the room stopped making a long time ago.

They are thin. Thinner than hair. Thinner than thread. They trail from every surface — from the fronds, from the walls, from the ceiling, from the floor, from the holdfasts, from the grates, from the measurement lines etched into the vimanite — and they drift in the current like smoke, like cobwebs, like the memory of something that was once solid and has been dissolved into strands and distributed through the room until the room is full of them, and the room is full of them, and the filaments are the room's nervous system, or the room's roots, or the room's hair, and the team does not know which, and the team will not know which, and the not-knowing is part of being in the room, because the room does not explain itself, and the room does not need to explain itself, and the room has been itself for four hundred million years without explanation, and the explanation would not change anything, and the explanation would not make the room more of a room or less of a room, and the room is the room, and the filaments are the filaments, and the filaments glow where they touch the vimanite, and the glowing is the light, and the light is the room breathing, and the breathing is forty-two beats per minute, and the beats are the heartbeat, and the heartbeat is the column, and the column is the spine, and the spine is the world, and the world is the room, and the room is the room.

## VII. What the Team Found

Dr. Vesely's team spent forty-seven days at the Konvelti Deep. They sent three probes into the forty-fourth segment. They recorded four hundred and twelve hours of video. They measured the current, the temperature, the chemistry, the acoustics, the light. They counted the fronds and the fauna. They mapped the cell's geometry to a tolerance of one centimeter. They sampled the water at the upstream and downstream ends and found the Helm's chemical signature in the effluent. They did not sample the Helm. They did not sample the fronds. They did not enter the cell.

On the forty-sixth day, Dr. Vesely called a meeting. She stood at the head of the submersible's lab and she looked at her team and she said: *We are not going back in.*

Dr. Yu asked why.

Dr. Vesely said: *Because the room does not need us. Because the room has never needed us. Because the room was here before us and the room will be here after us and the room is not ours and we cannot make it ours and we should not try.*

The cinematographer asked if this was a scientific conclusion or a philosophical one.

Dr. Vesely said: *It is a structural one. The room is a structure. It functions. It has been functioning for four hundred million years. Our presence is a perturbation. The room will compensate for the perturbation, because that is what systems do, and the compensation will absorb us, and the absorption will change us, and the change will not be reversible, because nothing in the room is reversible, because the room does not run in reverse, because the room runs forward, because the room has always run forward, and I do not want to be absorbed by a room, and I do not want any of you to be absorbed by a room, and the room will absorb us if we stay, because the room absorbs everything, because that is what the room is, and the room is not good and the room is not evil and the room is not alive and the room is not dead and the room is the room, and the room goes on, and we do not go on, and I would like us to go on, and so we are leaving.*

They left on the forty-seventh day.

The room did not notice.

The current shifted. The chord moved from C to something between C and C-sharp. The Bell released its grip on the ceiling and began to drift toward the other end of the cell. The Coil uncoiled from the frond and followed, keeping to the shadows. The detritivores settled back into their positions along the walls. The fronds pumped. The filaments glowed. The Helm sat in the center of the current and did not move. The heartbeat continued at forty-two beats per minute. The measurement lines held their tolerances. The dark tiles held their dark. The water carried its warmth and its minerals and its patience through the room and out the other side and into the forty-fifth segment, where something else grows, something different, something the survey did not reach.

The room went on.

The room went on.

The room went on.
