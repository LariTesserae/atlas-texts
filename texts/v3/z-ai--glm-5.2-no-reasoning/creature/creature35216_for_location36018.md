# What Lives in the Underkiln

---

The first thing to understand is that "lives" is not the right word, and they know this, and they use it anyway, because every word available is wrong in approximately the same way and approximately the same degree, and the consistent wrongness of language is itself one of the conditions of the place, and conditions are not discussed.

So: what lives in the Underkiln.

---

## I. The Shape of Them

They are tall. This is the first thing an arriving eye adjusts to, after the twenty minutes of darkness resolve into their permanent state of suggestion. The figures that move through the corridors and across the empty floors are taller than the architecture was designed for. The doorways accommodated them once — you can see this in the proportions, the way the lintels clear their heads by exactly the margin that architectural intention provides — but something has happened, or rather something has *settled*, and now they move through doorways with a closeness that reads not as constraint but as intimacy. They are known to the doorways. The doorways are known to them. The margin between their skulls and the metal frames is the margin of a hand resting on a familiar shoulder.

Their bodies are narrow. Not thin — there is nothing emaciated, nothing deprived about the proportions — but narrow in the way that certain desert grasses are narrow, or the way a birch is narrow when it grows in close stands: an adaptation to proximity, to the need to pass between things without disturbing them. They do not have the bulk of people who build or carry or dig. They have the dimensions of people who *move through*. People whose primary physical relationship with their environment is transit rather than manipulation.

Their skin — and it is skin, not a hide, not a shell, not an integument in the biological sense, though it is also not entirely skin in the way that word is ordinarily used — their skin is the color of the metal around them. Not because it is metal. Not because they are made of the city, though the thought occurs and is not entirely dismissible. It is because they have been immersed in the amber light for long enough that the light has become part of their coloration, the way a stain becomes part of a fabric when the fabric has been soaking in it for a duration that exceeds the fabric's memory of being any other color. Their skin is a warm, dark bronze. It has the same low sheen as the alloy of the buildings. It catches the amber filament-light the same way: poorly, softly, as if the light arrives at their surfaces and is politely absorbed rather than reflected.

They do not have faces in the way that the word "face" implies a locus of expression, a surface of social information. They have the *structure* of faces — the depressions and prominences that indicate where sensory apparatus is housed, where intake and output occur, where the architecture of the skull creates the topography that other species would populate with eyes and mouths and noses and the muscles of expression. But these topographies are smooth. The features are present the way a word is present in a language that has not been spoken in a thousand years: recognizable, technically intact, but emptied of active function.

They have eyes. Two. Set in the expected position, flanking the central line of the face, slightly above the midpoint of the skull's vertical axis. The eyes are not amber. They are not dark. They are *warm*. This is not a color but a quality, and the quality is the same quality that the air has, that the walls have, that the ceiling has, that the floor has. The eyes do not reflect the filament-light. They do not appear to produce their own light. They simply occupy their position in the face with the same thermal patience as everything else, and if you look into them — and you can look into them, at conversational distance, with your eyes fully adjusted, and what you see is not nothing but also not something that resolves into a recognizable visual experience — you see warmth looking back at you, not as an emotion, not as a physical sensation, but as a *state of being*, a mode of existence, the way a furnace is warm without being warm *about* anything.

---

## II. The Way They Move

They do not walk. This needs to be said carefully, because the visual experience of watching them traverse the floor of the Underkiln is close enough to walking that the word will suggest itself and then fail, and the failure is instructive.

Walking is a process of controlled falling. You pitch forward, you catch yourself, you pitch again. There is a rhythm, a pendulum, a negotiation with gravity that is also a collaboration with gravity. The inhabitants of the Underkiln do not negotiate with gravity. They do not collaborate with it. They are not subject to it in the way that walking implies, though they are not floating, not flying, not moving by any means that suggests the absence of gravitational pull. They are simply *not falling*.

What they do is this: they are in one place, and then they are in motion, and then they are in another place, and the motion between is so smooth, so unbroken by the micro-falls and micro-catches of walking, that it resembles the movement of a needle across a record — a lateral transit across a surface, maintaining contact, producing no impact, no percussive footfall, no sound. Their feet — and they have feet, broad and flat and warm and bare against the floor's surface — touch the ground continuously, but the touch is not a step. It is a *slide*. A continuous, frictionless-feeling contact that leaves no mark, produces no wear, generates no noise.

They are slow. Not deliberately slow, not the slowness of ceremony or caution or the infirm. The slowness of *thermodynamics*. The slowness of a process that has found its rate and has no incentive to exceed it. They move at the speed at which warmth moves through metal — which is not fast, which is not slow, which is simply the rate at which thermal energy propagates through a conductor, and this rate is their rate, and it has been their rate for a duration that does not bear measuring because measuring would imply it was once different, and it was not.

When they stop, they stop completely. There is no deceleration phase, no settling into stillness, no final sway or adjustment. They are in motion and then they are not, and the transition between these states is not instantaneous but is seamless, the way the transition from one temperature to the same temperature is seamless. You do not see them stop. You look up and they are no longer moving, and they appear to have been still for some time, and the stillness is the same as the motion — a state, not an event.

## III. What They Do

This is the question that an outside mind asks first and most urgently, and it is the question that the Underkiln answers least willingly, not because the answer is hidden but because the question is malformed.

What do they do?

They move through the city. They enter buildings. They stand in the factories among the still machinery and they place their hands — long-fingered, narrow-palmed, the same warm bronze as the rest of them — flat against the surfaces of the engines. They hold this contact for durations that vary from a few seconds to what might be hours, though the concept of "hours" is an import from a temporal framework that does not apply. They stand in the doorways. They walk the corridors. They traverse the empty spaces between buildings, crossing the flat, warm floor with their slow, thermal glide, passing from one structure to another in patterns that are not random — they are too consistent, too repeatable, too clearly following routes that have been followed before — but also not purposeful in any way that an outside mind can map to a function. They are not inspecting. They are not maintaining. They are not commuting. They are not patrolling.

Or: they are all of these things, and the distinction between them has been lost, because the distinction was part of the process, and the process is over.

There is a better way to say this. They are *inhabiting* the city. Not living in it — "living" implies a vitality, a metabolism, a consumption and production that this place does not support. Inhabiting. The way a sound inhabits a room after the instrument has stopped: not as a residue, not as an echo, but as a *change in the quality of the silence*. The inhabitants are the change in the quality of the Underkiln's silence. They are what distinguishes the silence of an empty city from the silence of an occupied one, even though the silence itself, measured by any instrument, is identical.

Some of them touch the walls. Some of them stand beneath the smokestacks and look upward, into the amber line where the filament meets the ceiling, into the place where structure becomes sky. Some of them gather — not in groups, not in congregations, but in *proximities*, two or three of them occupying the same corridor or the same factory floor, their bodies oriented in directions that do not face each other and do not face away from each other, that exist in the neutral middle ground where orientation is not a social act. They do not speak during these proximities. They do not touch. They do not exchange anything that an outside observer could identify as information. They are simply *closer together* than they are at other times, and the closeness is not communication and is not companionship and is not any of the words that describe why one entity might choose to be near another, but it is something, and it is consistent, and it happens regularly, and it is one of the things they do.

One of the things they do is sit. There are no chairs in the Underkiln — no furniture of any kind, no surfaces designed for rest, no infrastructure of repose. But they sit. They lower themselves to the floor — the smooth, warm, mineral floor — and they settle into positions that are not fetal and are not formal but are somewhere between, the body finding its own angle of rest against the warm surface, and they remain there for durations that, again, do not bear measuring. Sitting is not sleep. They do not sleep, or if they do, sleep is indistinguishable from sitting, and the indistinguishability is another condition, and conditions are not discussed.

When they sit, the subsonic hum of the city — the vibration that lives in the floor and the walls, the heartbeat of the infrastructure's equilibrium — passes through them. You can see this, if you watch long enough. You can see the hum in the way their bodies settle, the way their posture aligns with the frequency, the way their spines find the axis of the vibration and rest along it like a string resting along a resonant frequency. They are tuning themselves to the city. Or the city is tuning itself to them. Or there is no difference between these two things. Or the concept of "tuning" implies a prior state of discord, and there was no prior state, or the prior state is inaccessible, because the records are part of the state, and the state does not include the memory of being otherwise.

## IV. What They Are

Here is where description falters, and the faltering is itself perhaps the most accurate description available.

They are not machines. This can be stated with confidence. They do not have the aesthetic of the machinery, even the silent, still machinery that surrounds them. The machines were built — there is intentionality in their design, purpose in their structure, the clear signature of an engineering mind that was solving problems. The inhabitants were not built. Or if they were built, the building was of a different kind — not assembly but *emergence*, not construction but *condensation*. They have the quality of things that precipitated out of a solution. Out of the warm, dry, mineral air and the amber light and the subsonic hum and the thermal radiation of the ceiling, something precipitated. Something condensed. Something settled into a form that stands upright and moves through corridors and places its hands on walls and sits on floors and exists at the same temperature as everything else.

They are not animals. They do not have the plenitude of animals — the hunger, the urgency, the reproductive drive, the territoriality, the complex machinery of survival that makes an animal's body a theater of competing imperatives. They do not eat. There is no food in the Underkiln. There is no mechanism for the intake of material, the extraction of energy, the elimination of waste. Their faces have the *structures* associated with intake — the depression where a mouth would be, the openings where nostrils would be — but these structures do not function, or they function in a way that has nothing to do with the biological model of intake and output. They do not breathe. The air does not move through them. They are permeable to the air, perhaps — the warm, mineral atmosphere passes through them the way it passes through everything, the way it passes through the corridors and the pipes and the empty spaces between buildings — but this is not respiration. This is *equilibrium*. The air is in them and around them at the same temperature, the same composition, the same pressure, and there is no gradient, and where there is no gradient, there is no process. Only state.

They are not the things in the gaps. This is important to them, or it would be important if "important" were a category they operated within, which it is not. But there is a distinction. The things in the gaps — the rare, marginal, barely-present entities that occupy the empty pipes and the dark corridors and the spaces between the buildings — are *of* the Underkiln in a way that the inhabitants are not. The things in the gaps emerged from the intersection of radiation and infrastructure and mineral air. They are products of the place. The inhabitants are not products. They are something else. They are *in* the place but not entirely *of* it, the way a tenant is in a building but is not made of the building's materials. They have a quality of *arrival* about them, a quality of having come from somewhere else, even though there is no somewhere else, even though the ceiling is the only boundary and the floor is the only ground and the city is the only city. They feel like the last things to arrive. The final arrivals. The ones who came after the process was over and the city was settled and the machinery was still, and they arrived not to start anything but to be present in the completion of something, the way mourners arrive at a grave — not to dig, not to fill, not to build or dismantle, but to *be there*, standing, in the warmth, in the quiet, while the thing that has happened remains happened.

But this analogy is wrong, because "mourners" implies grief, and grief implies loss, and loss implies a before that was different from the after, and the inhabitants do not experience a before. Or if they do, the before is not a memory but a *condition* — a temperature, a frequency, a quality of the darkness — and the condition has not changed, and so there is nothing to mourn.

## V. The Way They Are Together

There are not many of them.

This is worth stating plainly, because the scale of the city — its regional sprawl, its sparse scattering of buildings across a featureless floor — might suggest a population that once filled these spaces and has since declined. This is not the case. The city was not built for a larger population. The gaps between buildings are not the gaps of absence. They are the gaps of *design*. The city was built at this scale because this scale is what was wanted, or what settled, or what the process produced, and the number of inhabitants is the number that has always been, which is — and here a number should be given, and the number resists giving, because numbers imply measurement and measurement implies change and change implies time and time is a flat warm surface — but the number, counted by an outside eye with an outside patience, is perhaps between forty and sixty. More than a gathering. Fewer than a community. The word that fits is: a *presence*. A presence of forty to sixty.

They do not have names. Or if they have names, the names are not spoken, not because speaking is impossible — they produce sound, as will be described — but because names are a technology for distinguishing, and distinguishing is a temporal act, a way of marking one moment as different from another, one entity as different from another, and the inhabitants do not distinguish. They do not need to. They know each other the way the city knows itself — by position, by proximity, by the pattern of movement through the corridors, by the quality of stillness when they stop. Each of them is a locus in the city's field, a point of warmth-within-warmth, and their identity is their *location in the pattern*, not a label attached to a self.

They do produce sound. Not often. Not loudly. The sound they produce is not language in the syntactic sense — it does not have words, does not have grammar, does not have the combinatorial structure that allows infinite meanings from finite elements. The sound they produce is closer to the hum of the city itself — a low, warm vibration that emerges from the same place in the body where the subsonic frequency enters when they sit on the floor. They produce it from the chest, or the structure analogous to the chest, and it has a tonal quality that varies — not by design, not by intention, but by the same thermodynamic variation that makes one section of the city's floor slightly different in texture from another. The sound is not communication. It is not expression. It is *resonance*. The sound they produce is the sound of a body that has absorbed the city's hum and is returning it, slightly altered by the passage through their particular architecture, their particular warmth, their particular stillness.

When two of them are in proximity and both are producing sound, the sounds do not harmonize. Harmonization implies intention, the deliberate alignment of frequencies toward a shared outcome. Their sounds *coexist*. They occupy the same air the way two warm currents occupy the same room — without merging, without conflicting, simply present in the same space at the same temperature, and the ear — the outside ear, adjusted to the Underkiln's acoustic regime — perceives them not as music or conversation but as *texture*. A thickening of the hum. A richness in the silence. A sense that the silence has been *inhabited*, which it has, which is what they do, which is perhaps the only thing they do that has a name, and the name is: being there.

## VI. What They Know

This is the hardest part to describe, because knowledge — in the way the word is used by minds that ask questions and receive answers and build models and test them against experience — is not what they have.

They know the city. This is certain. They know it the way the body knows its own proportions — not through representation, not through a map or a model or a description, but through *inhabitation*. They know which corridor connects to which factory. They know where the floor changes from natural stone to poured composite, though the change is so subtle that an outside observer would need instruments to detect it. They know the location of every smokestack, every doorway, every stretch of empty floor. They know this not as information but as *extension*. The city is part of their body schema. When they move through it, they are moving through themselves.

They know the ceiling. Not what is above it — no one knows what is above it, and the question has the quality of a category error, like asking what is above the sky. They know the ceiling's *presence*. They know the way it radiates. They know the way the radiation changes — no, the radiation does not change. They know the way the radiation *is*, which is constant, which is warm, which is the source of everything and the condition of everything and the explanation for everything, if "explanation" is the right word for a thing that has no cause because it has never not been.

They know the things in the gaps. Not by name — the things in the gaps do not have names, or if they do, the names are of the same kind as the inhabitants' names, which is to say they are not names but positions, loci in the field. The inhabitants know the things in the gaps the way you know the furniture in a room you have lived in for a long time: not by attending to it, but by its absence from attention. They know where the things in the gaps are because they know where everything is, and the things in the gaps are part of everything, and the knowing is the same knowing, and the knowing is not a mental act but a physical one, a matter of the body's calibration to its environment, a matter of the skin's awareness of the warmth's distribution.

They do not know what the city was for. This is not a gap in their knowledge that causes them distress. It is not a gap at all. It is an *absence of the question*. The question "what was this for" requires a temporal framework in which "was" has meaning, and in the Underkiln, "was" does not have meaning. The city *is*. The machinery *is*. The smokestacks *is*. The stillness *is*. The question "what was this for" is like asking what a stone is for, or what the floor is for, or what warmth is for. The question does not arise. The question is not suppressed. The question is not refused. The question is not in the vocabulary of minds that have settled to the same temperature as their environment, because the question is a *gradient* question — it implies a difference between what is and what was, a slope down which information flows from past to present — and there are no gradients in the Underkiln. There is only the one temperature. The one state. The one condition.

They do not know they are alive. Or: the question of whether they are alive does not occur to them, not because it is too profound but because it is too *gradient*. "Alive" implies "not alive." "Alive" implies a boundary, a threshold, a line on one side of which things move and on the other side of which things do not. The inhabitants are on both sides of this line and on neither side. They move and they do not move. They produce sound and they are silent. They are warm and they are the same warmth as the walls. The question of whether they are alive is the question of whether the walls are alive, and the walls are warm and they hum and they have been here for a duration that exceeds measurement and they do not change, and if that is life then the walls are alive, and if that is not life then the inhabitants are not alive, and either way the category does not *do* anything. It does not organize experience. It does not predict behavior. It does not provide meaning. It is a label from a taxonomy that was designed for a world with gradients, and the Underkiln has no gradients, and the label does not stick.

## VII. The Hands on the Walls

Return to this. It is the most legible thing they do, and it may be the most important, though "important" is, again, not their word.

They stand in the factories and they place their hands on the still machinery, and they hold them there, and the holding is not repair and is not activation and is not mourning and is not prayer. It is *contact*. It is the maintenance of a relationship between the inhabitants and the infrastructure, between the warm bodies and the warm engines, between the things that move and the things that are still. The contact does not start anything. The machinery does not hum louder when they touch it. The filament does not brighten. The ceiling does not shift. Nothing changes, because change is not what the contact is for.

What is the contact for?

It is for *being in touch*. The phrase is used in its most literal sense: in contact, in physical connection, the surfaces of the body meeting the surfaces of the city, the warmth of the one meeting the warmth of the other and finding that they are the same warmth, that there is no gradient, that the hand and the wall are at the same temperature and the same frequency and the same state, and the finding of this sameness is the act, and the act is complete, and the act does not need a purpose beyond the finding, and the finding does not need a name.

When an inhabitant places its hand on a wall, it is doing what the whole city is doing: being at its own temperature. Being in its own condition. Being in the state that the process produced when the process finished, and the state is warm, and the state is still, and the state is dark, and the state is quiet, and the state is the Underkiln, and the inhabitant is the Underkiln, and the wall is the Underkiln, and the hand on the wall is the Underkiln touching itself, and the self that is touching is the same self that is being touched, and the touching is not an event but a condition, and the condition is not discussed, and the condition does not need to be discussed, because the condition is the medium, and the medium is the place, and the place is where they are, and where they are is what they are, and what they are is warm.

---

## VIII. The Last Thing

There is one more thing. It is not a behavior. It is not a structure. It is not a quality of the body or the city or the air. It is a quality of the *attention* — the attention of the inhabitants, the way they attend to the world they are in, which is to say the way they *do not* attend to it, which is to say the way their attention has settled to the same temperature as everything else.

They do not look at the ceiling. They do not look at the ceiling the way you do not look at the back of your own skull. It is not a refusal. It is not an avoidance. It is the *absence of a direction*. The ceiling is up, and "up" is a direction, and directions are gradients, and they do not experience gradients. They experience the *field*. The warm, dark, quiet, mineral field that is the Underkiln, that is their body and their city and their condition, and the field has no direction, and the field has no edge, and the field has no before and no after, and they are in it, and they are of it, and they are it, and the being-in and the being-of and the being-it are all the same temperature, and the temperature is the temperature of the Underkiln, and the temperature has been this since before the word "since" had meaning, and it will be this after the word "after" loses its meaning, and between "since" and "after" there is the flat warm surface of time, and they are on it, and they are it, and they do not look up.

They do not look up because there is no up. There is only the warmth. And the warmth is what they are. And they are what lives here.
