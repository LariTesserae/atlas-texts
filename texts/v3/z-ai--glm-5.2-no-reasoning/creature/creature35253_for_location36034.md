# What Lives in the Brooding Plain

---

The first answer is: nothing lives *in* the Brooding Plain. Nothing lives *on* it. The distinction between habitat and inhabitant does not hold here. The plain is not a place where things reside. It is a process in which things *participate* — and the participants are not separate from the process. They are its substance.

But that is the answer given from a distance, the answer that keeps you comfortable. Closer — on your knees, palm flat against the warm dark stone, the mineral moisture beading between your fingers — the answer becomes more specific, more troubling, and more alive.

---

## The Carriers

Begin with the smallest, because they are the most numerous, and because numerosity is their defining trait.

They are hand-sized. Some are smaller — the size of a closed fist, a peach, a child's cupped palm. They are translucent, but not in the way glass is translucent, not in the way jellyfish are. Their translucence is *active* — a quality of their surface that admits light and then diffuses it, so that you see not *through* them but *into* them, the way you see into clouded water. Their outlines are indistinct. They do not have edges so much as *gradients*, a slow transition from the damp air around them to the damp substance of them. You could argue they do not have bodies at all, only densities.

Inside each one — visible through the translucent body wall, like dark sediment suspended in a slow current — are aggregations. Dark masses that move, merge, separate, reform. These are not organs. They are not fixed structures serving fixed functions. They are *working groups* — clusters of something smaller still, something particulate, that gather to perform a task and then disperse and regroup elsewhere in the body for a different task. If you watch long enough — and you must watch a long time, because the pace is glacial, a timescale of minutes where you expect seconds — you can see a dark mass form at one end of the creature, migrate slowly through its body, pause, contract, and then *dissolve*, releasing its material into the surrounding translucent medium. The creature shudders, very slightly. It has done something. It is already forming the next aggregation.

They move on cilia. Not the orderly ciliated rows of a paramecium, not the structured locomotion of something designed for direction. Their cilia are chaotic, covering every surface, each one beating to its own rhythm, the aggregate effect of thousands of asynchronous strokes producing a smooth, surprisingly rapid glide across the stone. They leave wet trails behind them — not slime, not mucus, but a thin film of the same mineral moisture that beads on the pale veins, and the trails dry in seconds, leaving no residue, only a faint darkening of the stone where the moisture briefly deepened its color.

What they carry: dark grit. Fine particulate, gathered on their surfaces, in the folds and creases of their bodies, caught in the wave-front of their cilia and drawn inward. They are filters. They draw material from the moisture that seeps through the stone veins, from the condensation that beads on every surface, from the water that falls in sudden shocking slaps from the ceiling. They process this material through their bodies — the dark masses inside them are the processing centers, the refineries — and they deposit what remains at the convergence points, the places where the pale mineral veins meet and the crevices deepen. The deposits are dark, granular, wet. They look like coffee grounds. They are not coffee grounds. They are *precursor* — the raw material from which new stone grows, new veins branch, new architecture emerges.

The carriers do not do this *intentionally*. That word means nothing here. They do this because this is what they *are*. A carrier is not a creature that carries. A carrier is *carrying itself into being*, a body that exists as the site of a process, a transient density in a flow of material and moisture that moves through the plain like blood moves through a body. If you removed the carrier from the flow, it would cease. Not die — *cease*, the way a wave ceases when the wind stops. The carrier is a shape the flow takes, a temporary structure that the moving material adopts in order to refine itself, and when the material has been refined and deposited, the carrier that carried it has no further purpose and dissolves back into the general medium and is reconstituted elsewhere as a new carrier with a new aggregation and a new task.

This is the first horror of the plain, or the first beauty, depending on your orientation: *the smallest creatures are not individuals*. They are moments. They are the flow's own pulse, its own peristalsis, and the millions of bodies you see streaming along the mineral veins are not millions of lives but millions of *instances* of a single life, a single ongoing process, expressing itself in hand-sized translucent packets that form and dissolve and reform continuously, like foam on a river.

You cannot befriend a carrier. You cannot name one. By the time you chose a name, the carrier you meant it for would be gone, its material already part of a deposit mound, its aggregations already reconstituted in three new carriers downstream. But you can respect what they do, and you can feel, if you kneel beside their lines and watch their patient, glacial, endless procession, a kind of awe that has nothing to do with intelligence or intention and everything to do with *persistence*. They do not stop. They have never stopped. They will not stop. They are the plain's blood, and the plain is alive.

## The Builders

The domed ones are different. The domed ones are *different*.

They are knee-high. Their surfaces are rough, warty, encrusted with mineral deposits that they accumulate and shed in cycles, so that a given individual looks different from hour to hour — first rough and pale as the deposits build, then suddenly smooth and dark as the deposits are sloughed, then slowly roughening again as the cycle recommences. They are not translucent. They are opaque, solid-feeling, their bodies dense and dark and giving nothing away. You cannot see inside them. You do not know what they contain.

They are slow. They move with a deliberation that makes the carriers look frantic by comparison, and the carriers are not frantic — the carriers are *methodical*. But the builders are *stately*. They choose a surface — the base of a ridge, the face of an outcropping, the side of a mature deposit mound — and they press themselves against it, and they *work*.

The work is coating. Sealing. Transforming. Where the builder's body meets the stone, the stone changes: darkens, glosses, hardens, takes on the sheen of polished obsidian. The sound is that wet-cloth-on-rock sound — rhythmic, slurping, organic. It is the sound of chemistry, of surface reactions, of a body secreting something onto stone that reacts with the stone and changes it at the molecular level. The builder is not eating the stone. The builder is *finishing* it. It is applying a treatment, a sealant, a protective and structural coating that makes the raw dark stone harder, denser, more reflective, more permanent.

Why? Because the raw stone is young. The raw stone is unfinished. The raw stone is still settling, still shrinking, still cracking into those too-regular patterns that look like dried mud at geological scale. The builders are *curing* it. They are accelerating the stone's maturation, converting it from the soft, yielding, warm material of the plain's initial extrusion into something that will last, that will hold its shape, that will become the permanent architecture of the place.

They work in shifts. This is not coordination — or if it is, it is coordination without a coordinator, coordination through chemistry, through the same sort of diffuse signaling that makes the carriers converge on fallen water. A builder settles onto a surface and works until its mineral deposits have built up to a certain density, until its body is crusted and its movements have slowed, until it begins to *vibrate* — that visible trembling, that shivering-dog convulsion that signals saturation. And then it detaches, and moves away, and another builder, waiting in the vicinity — and there is always another waiting, the way there is always blood waiting behind a clot — moves in and takes its place and continues the work without interruption.

The departing builder, saturated with mineral deposits, is met by the carriers. They swarm it, as described — covering its surface, pressing against its roughness, and *consuming* the deposits. Peeling them away, ingesting them, carrying them off. The deposits are material the builder has drawn out of the stone during its work — excess, byproduct, the mineral equivalent of sawdust — and the carriers return it to the convergence mounds, where it becomes *precursor* for new growth. The builder, cleaned, is lighter, faster, and it moves to a new surface and begins again.

Are the builders individuals? This is harder to answer than the same question about the carriers. The builders do not dissolve. They persist. A specific builder — a specific dome, a specific density, a specific roughness pattern — can be tracked across hours, across days, across the slow reconfiguration of the landscape. They have a permanence that the carriers lack. They have a *history*.

But whether that history constitutes a *self* — whether the builder that was here yesterday is the same builder that is here today in any meaningful sense, or whether it is merely a continuation of the same process, the same flow, wearing the same shape because the shape works — this is the question that the plain poses and refuses to answer. The builders do not communicate. They do not recognize each other. They do not form bonds or hierarchies or territories. They simply work, and when they need maintenance, they are maintained, and when they are maintained, they work again. They are *organs* more than organisms — specialized structures of a larger body, performing a function, sustained by the body, replaceable by the body, but persistent in their function, in their form, in the specific way they press themselves against raw stone and *change it*.

If the carriers are blood, the builders are osteoblasts. They are the cells that make bone. They are the reason the plain has structure at all, the reason the ridges harden and hold, the reason the deposit mounds become permanent, the reason the grooved wall in the larger structure has its obsidian sheen — because *someone applied that sheen*, someone worked it, someone stood there for a long time pressing and pressing and pressing until the stone became glass.

## The Reformers

The flat ones are rare. You might walk for an hour and not see one. They are tabletop-sized, and they are nearly invisible, because their upper surfaces are the same dark color as the stone, and they do not move, or do not appear to move, and you mistake them for ground.

But they move. At the times of pressure-change, when the air thickens and something in the plain shifts its weight, the flat ones *drift*. It is the only word. They do not crawl, do not flow, do not propel themselves with any visible mechanism. They drift, the way a sheet of ice drifts on a slow current, the way a cloud drifts on a wind you cannot feel. They cover ground at the pace of a casual walk, and they do not stop until the pressure-shift ends, and then they are still again, and they are ground again, and you would walk across them without knowing.

Where they pass, the stone changes. Not visibly — not at first. But the dark stone becomes *paler* in a broad swath behind them, and the damp veins in that swath deepen, widen, carry more moisture. The mineral composition of the floor is shifting. The flat ones are *plowing* — not the surface, but the substrate, the deep material beneath the floor, the warm, yielding stone that the plain is made of. They loosen it. They aerate it. They open it to the moisture that rises from below, and the moisture finds the veins and the veins carry it to the carriers and the carriers process it and the builders use it and the cycle continues.

If the carriers are blood and the builders are osteoblasts, the reformers are — what? Stem cells? Progenitors? They are the ones that prepare new ground, that convert raw stone into *workable* stone, that prime the substrate for the processes that will follow. They are the plain's *growth zone*, its active margin, the leading edge of its expansion. Where the reformers have passed, new work begins. Where they have not passed, the plain is static, dormant, waiting.

They are the largest of the plain's creatures, and they are the least understood. Their interiors are invisible — dark, opaque, giving nothing away. Their surfaces, when you touch them (and they are warm, warmer than the stone, warmer than the builders, almost hot), are smooth and dry and hard, like the finished stone the builders produce. They feel like products of the plain, not participants in it. They feel like *results*. As if the plain had produced them, finished them, perfected them, and then set them loose to do the one thing they were made to do: prepare more of themselves. Prepare more plain.

The reformers are the closest thing here to *maturity*. Everything else is in process, becoming, unfinished. The reformers are done. They are what the plain wants to be when it is done growing. Whether they know this, whether they experience their own completeness, whether they drift across the warm stone with any sense of their own finality — these are questions that the plain does not pose, because the plain does not think in terms of finality. The plain thinks in terms of *more*. But the reformers, in their silence, in their invisibility, in their slow, purposeful drifting through the pressure-shifts, seem to embody something that the rest of the plain is reaching toward and has not yet grasped.

## The Flyers

Above all of this, the thin dark membranes spiral.

They are not birds. They are not insects. They are not bats or pterosaurs or anything with a name. They are *sheets* — flat, dark, flexible sheets of a material that is neither flesh nor membrane nor mineral but something between, something that catches the warm air rising from the floor and holds it the way a sail holds wind, and they *fly*. Not with wings — with surface area. They are all surface. They have no bodies to speak of, no heads, no eyes, no features. They are *areas* of dark material, a meter across, two meters, trailing edges that flex and ripple, and they ride the thermals of the plain in gyres and spirals and occasionally — purposefully — they dive.

They dive to the surface and they take. They take deposits of mineral. They take dead or stalled carriers, whose translucent bodies have lost their density, whose aggregations have dispersed, who are already dissolving back into the medium. They take fragments of the pale chalky substance, broken from the veins by the plain's slow shifting. They take whatever is loose, whatever is *available*, and they rise again with it, carrying it in their surfaces — not grasping it, not holding it, but *absorbing* it, the material spreading across their dark area the way a stain spreads on cloth, and they carry it up, up, to the ceiling, where they press their loads against the stone overhead and *deposit* it.

They are maintaining the ceiling. They are patching it. They are reinforcing the crust, the shell, the thing that separates this place from whatever is above. They are the plain's answer to gravity — the upward vector, the return current, the part of the cycle that says *what goes down must come back up*. The carriers bring material to the floor. The flyers bring it back to the ceiling. And the ceiling grows, and the floor grows, and the space between them — the vast, amber-lit, warm, damp, sound-filled space — is maintained, is *kept*, is the place where the plain is allowed to be.

Their paths form patterns. This is not coincidence. Viewed from a ridge-top, from one of the few vantage points the plain offers, the flyers' routes describe a branching network that mirrors the veining of the mineral below — the same branching, the same convergence-and-divergence, the same fractal self-similarity. The plain is a diagram at every scale. The floor is a diagram. The air is a diagram. The ceiling is a diagram. And the flyers are the *strokes* that draw the aerial version, tracing and retracing the lines of a pattern that no one drew, that emerged from the same processes that created the veins, the creatures, the stone, the warmth, the light.

Do the flyers know? Do they see the pattern? Are they *following* it, or are they *producing* it by the simple accumulation of their individual routes, each one seeking the optimal thermal, the best lift, the richest deposit, and the aggregate of millions of individual optimizations producing the diagram the way the aggregate of millions of individual raindrops produces a river system?

The answer is the same as it was for the carriers: *the question is malformed*. There is no "they" to know. There is only the process, and the process is the pattern, and the flyers are the process in the air, as the carriers are the process in the veins, as the builders are the process on the ridges, as the reformers are the process in the deep stone. The plain does not have creatures. The plain *is* a creature, and what you see moving across it and through it and above it are not its inhabitants but its *organs*, its *tissues*, its *cells* — the living substance of a single organism so large that you are standing inside it.

## The Absent Ones

But there were others. There must have been.

The structures do not build themselves. Stone does not stack itself. The grooves do not cut themselves into the obsidian sheen. Someone was here who was not the plain — who was *other* to the plain, who stood on the warm stone and felt the vibration in their sternum and saw the swarming life and did not see chaos but *material*.

They had hands. They had tools. They had minds that could hold the pattern of the grooves, that could read the water-traced instructions, that could feel the understanding come and could *try to keep it*, could try to hold it past the blink, past the forgetting, past the dissolve.

They are gone. Not dead — *gone*. There is no trace of their bodies, no bones in the stone, no fragments of their substance. The plain does not preserve what it does not need. The plain consumed them the way it consumes everything — gently, slowly, by the accumulation of moisture and mineral and the patient, endless labor of the carriers. Their bodies became deposits. Their deposits became mounds. Their mounds grew tendrils. Their tendrils hardened into stone. They are *part of the plain now* — literally, materially, their calcium and carbon and iron incorporated into the architecture, built into the ridges and the veins and the glossy sealed surfaces that they once directed the builders to produce.

They did not die. They were *absorbed*. They were *built*.

The tool remains. The pick, the chisel, the sharpened dark stone with its patient ground edge. It is warm in your hand. The plain has not consumed it, because the plain does not consume *tools* — it consumes *material*, and a tool is not material, a tool is *intention*, and the plain does not know what to do with intention. It can respond to it — it responds to the grooves, to the stacked stone, to the ordered arrangements that push its creatures away or draw them in — but it cannot *consume* it. The tool is the last trace of the mind that made it, and the plain has left it lying on the warm stone, half-buried, waiting.

## The Plain Itself

So: what lives in the Brooding Plain?

The Brooding Plain lives in the Brooding Plain.

It is one thing. It is one organism, one process, one ongoing act of self-construction, and everything you see — the stone, the moisture, the mineral veins, the creatures that flow across the floor and spiral through the air and drift in the pressure-shifts, the light that comes from nowhere, the warmth that rises from below — is *part of it*. The carriers are its blood. The builders are its bone-makers. The reformers are its growth. The flyers are its repair. The moisture is its lymph. The stone is its body. The structures are — were — its *interface*, the place where something outside it tried to talk to it, to direct it, to shape the shapeless labor of its becoming.

It is young. It is unfinished. It is in the act of its own creation. It is building the floor it stands on, the ceiling that shelters it, the air that fills it. It is building *itself*, from the inside out, and it does not know what it is building toward, and it does not care. It builds because building is what it does. It processes because processing is what it does. It lives because living — in this place, at this scale, in this way — is *building*.

And the amber light that comes from nowhere? The warmth that rises from below? The sound that fills the air like a stadium, like a marsh, like a body breathing?

These are not phenomena. They are not conditions. They are *signs*. They are the plain's own vitality, made visible, made audible, made *sensible* to anything that stands within it and pays attention. The light is the plain's attention. The warmth is the plain's metabolism. The sound is the plain's heartbeat, amplified a million-fold by the scale of its body and the density of its labor.

The plain is alive. The plain is *becoming* alive. The plain is in the process of inventing life, of building the structures that will sustain life, of creating the conditions under which life can persist — and the creatures that move across it are not the *inhabitants* of this life but its *first draft*, its initial sketch, the rough and unfinished and still-being-revised forms of something that, in another thousand years, or ten thousand, or a million, will be complete.

Or will not. Or will still be building. Or will have built something so different from what it is now that the plain you stand in — this warm, amber, sound-filled, swarming, raw, unfinished worksite — will be unrecognizable, a fossil of its own adolescence, a memory of the time when it was still deciding what it wanted to be.

The tool is in your hand. The water is falling somewhere, with its flat shocking slap. The creatures are converging.

What lives here?

*Here* lives here.
