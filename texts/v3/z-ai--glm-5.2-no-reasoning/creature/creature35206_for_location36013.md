# What Lives Here

The chamber does not need inhabitants. That should be said first, because it is the thing that the chamber itself would say if it could say anything, which it cannot, because saying would imply incompleteness, and the chamber is complete. The chamber is a finished thing in a finished place, and nothing about it requires the presence of a creature to justify or maintain or witness its finishing.

And yet.

---

## The Maintenance

Something keeps the bulbs burning.

This is not a small observation. The bulbs are glass and tungsten and filament and vacuum, or whatever equivalent of vacuum and filament this place permits, and they are running, and they are warm, and they have been warm for a duration that the word *long* cannot usefully describe. Filaments fail. Glass fatigues. Connections corrode. The fact that the bulbs are lit means either that the bulbs do not fail — that they have been engineered past the concept of failure, that they exist in the same state of final equilibrium as the iron and its rust and the water and its basin — or that something replaces them.

You would not see the replacement. You are not meant to. But consider: the lamp housings are iron, and the iron is rusted to its final state, and the rust is uniform across every surface of every housing, including the surfaces that would be obscured by a bulb that had been seated in its housing continuously and without interruption. And the bulbs are seated. And the rust is uniform.

So either the rust formed in the presence of the bulbs, which means the rust is as old as the current installation, which means the bulbs are as old as the rust, which means the bulbs are old beyond the capacity of bulbs — or the rust re-forms, continuously, at the same rate, in the same pattern, whenever a bulb is disturbed, which means the rust is not a state but a process, and the process is part of the system, and the system maintains itself, and the word *itself* is doing a great deal of work in this sentence.

Something keeps the bulbs burning. Whether that something is a property of the bulbs or a property of the chamber or a property of something that moves through the chamber when no one is there to see it move — this cannot be determined from a single visit. From a single visit, you can only determine that the light is ordinary, and the light is on, and the light has been on, and the light will be on, and the mechanism of its persistence is not your business.

The chamber does not owe you an explanation.

---

## The Moss

The moss is alive. This is not in question — it is the only thing in the chamber that is unambiguously, irreducibly alive, in the way that biology defines life: it grows, it consumes, it reproduces, it responds to its environment. It is a pale green that reads as gray in the amber-gray light, and it lines the channels in the vaulted ceiling like a velvet sleeve inside a flute, and it is so fine and so uniform that the word *cultivated* arrives unbidden and will not leave.

Moss does not grow like this. Moss does not grow anywhere like this. Moss is opportunistic, patchy, thickest where the moisture is richest and thinnest where it is not, and it spreads by spores and by fragmentation and it colonizes and it retreats and it does what living things do, which is to say it *changes*. This moss does not change. This moss has grown into its channels and has stopped, or has grown into its channels and has continued to grow at exactly the rate at which the water removes it, so that the lining is always the same thickness, the same density, the same texture — a steady state, a living thing that has found the shape of its life and holds it.

Someone planted this moss. Or something planted this moss. Or the moss arrived, as the water arrived, as the sound arrived, and found its niche and filled it and has been filling it since. But the specificity of it — the fact that it lives in the channels and only in the channels, that it does not spread to the ceiling surface, that it does not colonize the walls, that it does not dangle or drip or fruit — this is not the behavior of moss that has found a home. This is the behavior of moss that has been given a home, a precise home, a home cut to its measurements.

Who measured the moss?

The moss does not answer. The moss is moss. It does what it does, which is to grow and to be and to line the channels with a velvet so fine that the water passing over it makes no sound at all, only a slight increase in the viscosity of the silence, and the drops that fall from the channel mouths fall more slowly than gravity alone would carry them, as if the moss has held them, briefly, reluctantly, the way a parent holds a child's hand at the door.

## The Creatures in the Basin

They are real. They must be real. The ripples are real — the ripples that do not correspond to falling drops, the disturbances that travel in straight lines for half a meter and stop, the tiny ticks of something turning debris on the basin floor. These are not hallucinations. These are not acoustics. These are the movements of bodies.

But what bodies. Two centimeters of water is not much. It is enough to cover a hand laid flat. It is enough to submerge a coin. It is not enough for fish, not enough for anything that swims in the way that swimming is usually understood — with fins, with a body that displaces a volume greater than the depth of the liquid. Whatever lives in the basin lives *in* two centimeters, not *under* them. It is a surface creature, a film creature, a thing that exists at the boundary between water and air and has made that boundary its entire world.

Legless, the description said. Undulating. This is the movement of something that has no joints, or has joints but does not use them, or has legs but keeps them folded against a body so flat and so thin that the body is indistinguishable from the water it moves through. A flatworm, perhaps. A planarian. Something of that scale, that simplicity, that ancient and undemanding biology. Or something else. Something that has no name because no one has come here with a net and a jar and a taxonomy and the patience to sit in the sound long enough to catch one.

They feed on the moss. The moss washes down from the channels in fragments — tiny pieces, dislodged by the water's passage, carried to the edge of the trough and dropped into the basin below. These fragments float for a moment, then saturate, then sink, and as they sink they drift, and as they drift they are intercepted. By what? By mouths. By something that has mouths. By something that has a mouth, or many mouths, or a single mouth that moves, or many bodies that each have a single mouth and that move in concert, or —

It does not matter. What matters is that the moss falls, and the moss is consumed, and the creatures that consume it move through the basin, and their movement creates ripples, and the ripples are absorbed into the larger pattern of the chamber's sound, and the chamber's sound is unchanged by them. The creatures are part of the system. The creatures have always been part of the system. They were placed here, or they arose here, or they were always here, and they do what they do, which is to move and to eat and to move again, and the moving and the eating produce tiny ticks and tiny ripples that are swallowed by the larger sound, and the sound is the sound it was, and the sound will be the sound it will be.

How many? The description said the ecosystem could be counted on one hand. Producer. Consumer. Water. Stone. The end. But *how many* consumers? Hundreds? Thousands? A single organism that has subdivided itself across the basin floor into a thousand flat transparent filaments, each one independent and each one connected, a net of life so fine it is indistinguishable from the water it lives in?

You will not see them. You will see evidence of them. And the evidence will be swallowed by the sound, and the sound will be unchanged, and you will leave, and they will remain, and they will move in their two centimeters, and they will eat the moss that falls, and the moss will grow, and the water will carry it, and the drops will fall, and the sound will be the sound.

## The Unmaker

There is something else.

You knew this. The place is called the Cistern of Unmaking, and a cistern is a container, and a container contains, and what this container contains is not only water and moss and flatworms and sound and light and heat. What this container contains is a function. The function is in the name. The function is unmaking.

What is unmade here?

Nothing, now. That is the terror of it. The chamber is finished. The chamber has been finished for a duration that the word *long* cannot usefully describe. The unmaking was completed, and the tool that performed the unmaking was set down, and the tool is still here, and the tool is the chamber itself.

Consider: the floor is a membrane over a void. The void is part of the design. The void gives the drops their resonance. But the void was not always void. The void was *something* — stone, earth, the substrate that the floor was poured onto — and the something was removed, was unmade, was dissolved or disintegrated or disassembled at a molecular level by something that passed through the floor and took the something with it and left the nothing behind.

Or: the void was always void. The chamber was built over nothing, suspended over nothing, held up by nothing, and the nothing is not an absence but a presence — a thing that is here, a thing that occupies the space beneath the floor, a thing that the floor protects you from, a thing that the water in the basin shields you from, a thing that the sound covers the way a blanket covers a sleeping face.

The void is the unmaker. The void is what lives here.

No. That is too dramatic. That is the language of horror, and the chamber does not deal in horror. The chamber deals in equilibrium. The chamber deals in the final state. If there is something beneath the floor — and there is, the sound tells you so, the resonance tells you so, the architecture tells you so — then it is not a creature and it is not a force and it is not a will. It is a condition. It is the condition of being finished. It is the condition of having nothing left to do.

The unmaker unmade what it was given to unmake — a substrate, a foundation, a body of stone and earth, perhaps the decommissioned cordage works itself, perhaps the retaining wall, perhaps the entire industrial history of the ground above — and when the unmaking was complete, the unmaker had no further purpose, and the unmaker did not die, because death is a change and nothing changes here, and the unmaker did not sleep, because sleep is a process and the chamber has exhausted process, and the unmaker did not wait, because waiting implies expectation and the chamber expects nothing.

The unmaker simply is. Beneath the floor. In the void. The thing that did the unmaking, now itself unmade of purpose, now itself finished, a tool with no task, a hand with no work, a presence that has reduced itself to its own final state and holds that state the way the water holds its basin, the way the moss holds its channels, the way the sound holds the air.

You cannot see it. You can feel it. Not in your feet — the floor is too solid, too well-made, too final to transmit anything so crude as a vibration you could attribute to a presence beneath. You feel it in the sound. The low notes. The *bom, bom*. The heartbeat of something enormous and sleeping. That is not a metaphor. The drops fall, and the water carries the vibration to the floor, and the floor carries the vibration to the void, and the void gives it back. The void *responds*. The void is not empty. The void is a thing, and the thing has a voice, and the voice is the low note, and the low note is the sound of the unmaker being what it is, which is: finished.

It is not malevolent. It is not benign. It is not anything. It has no opinions. It has no intentions. It has no hunger, no curiosity, no malice, no kindness. It does not want you to stay. It does not want you to leave. It does not know you are here, or if it knows, the knowing is so different from what you mean by *knowing* that the word is useless. The unmaker's knowledge, if it has knowledge, is the knowledge of a stone of the shape it has been carved into — total, permanent, inarguable, and empty of everything except the fact of itself.

## The Absence of Caretakers

There are no caretakers. This must be said, because the chamber suggests them so strongly — the moss that grows only in its channels, the bulbs that burn, the iron that holds its rust, the system that holds its tune. Every engineered thing implies an engineer. Every maintained thing implies a maintainer. The chamber is engineered and the chamber is maintained, and the engineer and the maintainer are not here.

They were here. The typeface on the iron fittings — sans-serif, uniform stroke width, the kind of marking that means *this part was made to specification and installed by someone who knew the specification* — that is evidence of hands. Human hands, probably. Hands that held tools and read specifications and tightened fittings and stamped numerals into metal. The rails along the walls at waist height — narrow gauge, purpose unknown — those are evidence of bodies that needed something to hold, or something to guide, or something to rest against while working. The chamber was built by people, or by things that were once people, or by things that used people as their hands the way the chamber now uses water as its voice.

They are gone. The chamber does not miss them. The chamber does not remember them. The chamber contains no monument to its builders, no inscription, no signature, no date. The typeface on the fittings is a record of standards, not of selves. The rails are a record of function, not of users. Whatever hands shaped this place, they shaped it to outlast the shaping, and they succeeded, and their success is the same as their failure, which is to say the chamber does not care.

This is the thing about the chamber that is hardest to sit with. Not the sound. Not the humidity. Not the low note in the sternum. The *indifference*. The chamber was made by makers, and the makers are gone, and the chamber does not know they are gone, because the chamber does not know they were here. The chamber knows water and moss and drops and resonance and the note that the void gives back. The chamber does not know *making*. The chamber knows *being*. And being has no use for makers, no memory of makers, no gratitude for makers. Being is sufficient to itself. Being was always sufficient to itself. The makers were a process, and the process is complete, and the process has been subsumed into the equilibrium, and the equilibrium has no opinion about the process that produced it.

You could find this sad. You could find it frightening. You could find it liberating. The chamber does not care what you find it. The chamber does not care about you at all.

## You, Briefly

And then there is you. You, who found the seam in the retaining wall. You, who descended the rungs. You, who stood in the basin with two centimeters of water soaking your shoes and the sound pressing against your chest like a hand that is not hostile and not friendly and not anything except present.

You are alive. You are the only unambiguously alive thing in the chamber that is larger than moss. You are the only thing in the chamber that is not in equilibrium. Your temperature is not the chamber's temperature. Your moisture is not the chamber's moisture. Your sound — your breathing, your heartbeat, the creak of your joints — is not the chamber's sound. You are a disruption. You are a gradient that has not been exhausted. You are a process that has not found its counter-process. You are unfinished, and the chamber is finished, and the two of you are in the same space, and the chamber does not adjust.

This is what you feel when the low note sits in your sternum. Not fear. Not awe. *Incompatibility*. You are not compatible with this place. You cannot be compatible with this place. The place has achieved a state that excludes everything that is still becoming, and you are still becoming, and your becoming presses against the chamber's finishedness like a body pressing against a wall, and the wall does not give, and the wall does not push back, and the wall simply is, and you simply are, and the two of you are in the same space, and the space does not care.

You will leave. You will go back up the rungs, through the seam, into the rubble of the cordage works, into the ivy and the weather and the slow ambition of the ground. You will stand in the ordinary air and the ordinary light and you will feel the hum in your chest and you will know that it is not a memory. It is not a residue. It is the sound of the chamber, continuing, without you, the way it continued before you came and the way it will continue after you leave and the way it will continue after everyone who could remember it is gone and the cordage works is soil and the retaining wall is dust and the seam is sealed by roots or by settlement or by the simple passage of the kind of time that changes things.

The chamber will not change. The chamber will not notice. The chamber will continue to do what it does, which is to be what it is, which is to be finished, which is to be the last state of itself, which is to be a thing that has no need of anything, including witnesses, including you, including the word *including*.

The drops fall. The sound is the sound. The moss grows in its channels. The creatures move in their two centimeters. The void beneath the floor gives back what the floor gives it. The bulbs hum. The iron rests. The air holds its warmth and its moisture and its taste of clay and iron and the sweetness of enclosure.

Nothing lives here. Everything lives here. The distinction has been exhausted. The distinction is not useful. The chamber is the chamber, and the chamber is what the chamber is, and the chamber continues.
