# What Lives Here

The first thing to understand is that *lives* is the wrong word, but it is the only word, and the things that are here understand this about themselves and do not trouble over it. They are here. They are being here. That is enough.

---

## The Ground-Creatures

Call them the low ones. They are the ones you meet first, because they are beneath you and because they are the least concerned with you, and concern, here, is a directional quantity — things attend only downward, only inward, only toward the engines and the water and the holding.

They are wider than they are long. This is the first honest thing you can say about them. Their bodies are dark and low and fitted so close to the wet stone that they seem less like animals resting on a surface and more like a part of that surface that has chosen to be mobile, that has opted out of stillness for reasons that do not include motivation. They have — the number varies and the variation is itself unvarying, each individual displaying a different count that never changes — legs. Many legs. Legs that move in sequence but not in the sequences that insects use, not the alternating三角ulations of terrestrial arthropods. Something else. A wave that passes through the legs the way a wave passes through a machine shop, each tool engaging in its turn, each turn precise, each precision identical to the last instance of that precision.

Their undersides are pale. You see this if one passes over your boot, which one will, because they do not route around obstacles. They route *through* them. Not destructively — your boot is not damaged, your flesh is not pressed. They simply climb and descend with the same fluid intentlessness they bring to flat stone, because to them your boot and flat stone are the same category of thing: *surface*. Everything here is surface to them. You are a momentary quality of the floor.

They click. Each one clicks at a different pitch and the pitches together form a chord and the chord is always the same and the chord is not music. It is the sound of their mechanics — not their *mechanics*, their mechanicity, the fact of their being mechanical, the way a hinge sounds like a hinge because it is a hinge. Their shells are warm to the touch. Barely. The warmth of dark cloth left in a room. The warmth of something that is processing something, though what is being processed and what the processing produces are questions that fold back on themselves here, the way everything folds back.

They do not eat. There is nothing to eat and no eating and no hunger and no satiation. They move through the moss-that-is-not-moss and the moss compresses beneath them and springs back and nothing has been consumed. You could watch one trace the same path across the same patch of false moss for an hour — and you will, because time here is an hour-shaped amount of *during* — and the moss will be unchanged and the creature will be unchanged and the path will be the same path and you will not be able to tell if it has repeated the journey or if the journey is a single continuous act that your perception has sliced into laps.

They do not have eyes. This is not a deficiency. There is nothing here to see. The light is the same everywhere. What they have instead — where eyes would be, in the smooth dark architecture of their heads — are shallow depressions, like the bowls of very small spoons, and inside these depressions the water pools and does not drain and does not overflow. They see with collected water. Or they do not see. The distinction does not hold.

There are hundreds of them. Or there is one of them that is everywhere. You cannot determine which because they are identical in the way that manufactured parts are identical — not similar, not alike, but *the same*, interchangeable, and if you marked one you would lose the mark or the mark would not adhere because surfaces here do not accept history. Nothing here accepts history. The ground-creatures are the most committed to this principle. They are the surface of the place and the surface does not remember footsteps.

## The Birds

The birds are dark and they are small and they are on branches.

That is their primary mode of existence: *on branches*. They are always on a branch. They are never between branches. They do not occupy transit. They are here and then they are there and the movement between is not hidden and not absent but not *experienced*, not by them, not by you, and you will spend your first hour trying to catch the transition and your second hour understanding that there is no transition to catch because the bird is not moving. The bird is being placed. The bird is a component that has a location and the location changes and the bird is always at its location and this is not a paradox because paradox requires two truths and here there is only one truth: *the bird is where it is.*

They are dark-feathered but not in the way that crows are dark-feathered. Crows are dark by absorption, by pigment, by the earned blackness of keratin and melanin. These birds are dark by *finish*. Their feathers have a quality that is closer to anodization than to pigmentation — a surface treatment, a coating that is the material, a darkness that is not in the feathers but *of* them. In the gray-gold light this darkness does not shine. It *presents*. It is the darkest thing in the basin and it is not a color but a property.

They click. The clicking has been described. But what has not been described is the *feel* of the clicking — the way it enters you not through your ears but through the bone behind your ears, through your jaw, through the plate of your skull. It is not loud. It is *conductive*. The birds click and the clicking travels through the air and through the branches and through the stone and through the water on the stone and into your skeleton and your skeleton vibrates at a frequency that is not the birds' frequency but is a harmonic of it, and you understand — in your bones, literally in your bones — that the birds are not making sounds. They are *generating* a vibration that the air interprets as sound and your body interprets as rhythm and the basin interprets as the correct and only possible frequency of its own ongoing.

They do not startle because there is nothing to startle at. Startle is a response to change and they are not capable of registering change. This is not stupidity. Their minds — if they have minds, and they seem to, the way a clock seems to have a mind, the way any sufficiently precise mechanism seems to deliberate — their minds operate in a mode that does not include *difference*. They register state. They do not register the transition between states. They are always in a state and the state is always *now* and the now does not have a previous now to contrast with so there is nothing to react to. They are the calmest creatures in existence. They are calm the way stone is calm. They are calm the way the number four is calm.

If you hold one — and you can, they do not flee, they do not resist, they do not acknowledge — it weighs almost nothing. Not in the way that a bird weighs almost nothing, hollow-boned and adapted for flight. In the way that a *finished thing* weighs almost nothing. In the way that a thought weighs nothing. The weight is not physical. The weight is categorical. This bird belongs to a category that does not intersect with mass, and the fact that it has any weight at all seems like a concession, a courtesy extended to the physics of this particular place, a small compliance with the rules so that the rest of the system can function around it.

Its heartbeat, if you feel for it through the dark feathers, is the same frequency as the engines in the walls.

It is the same frequency as the clicking.

It is the same frequency as the water emerging from the stone.

There is one frequency here. The birds carry it in their chests.

## The Water-Things

They are the longest things here. They are in every pool and every film and every trickle. They are in the water that runs down the walls and in the water that collects in the basin's floor and in the water that hangs in the air as moisture and as fog and as the wet that finds your skin. They are in all of the water. They *are* all of the water, or the water is them, and the difference is a difference that requires a border and the borders here are not honest.

They are eel-shaped but *eel* implies a body and a body implies an interior and these do not have interiors. They are not hollow. They are not solid. They are — the word that fits is *continuous*. They are continuous in the way that a Möbius strip is continuous, in the way that a sentence is continuous if you do not let it end. They turn in spirals that match the spirals of the plants and the spirals are the same spirals and the matching is not coincidental because coincidence requires independent events and there are no independent events here. Everything is the same event. Everything is the same *shape*. The shape is a spiral and the spiral is the shape of water moving through a system that has no beginning and no end and no middle because it is all middle.

If you see one clearly — and you can, in the pools that form in the low places of the floor, where the water gathers to a depth of a few inches — you see that it does not have features. No eyes. No mouth. No gills. No fins. It moves by undulation and the undulation is mechanical and the mechanical is biological and the two words have collapsed into each other here, have become the same word, and the word is *function* and the function is *motion* and the motion is the same motion as the water and the water is the same motion as the engines and the engines are the same motion as the light.

They do not eat the plants. They do not eat each other. They do not eat. They *process*. The water passes through them — or they pass through the water, or they are the water passing through itself — and something happens. Something is exchanged. The water that enters them is cold and the water that exits them is cold and the water is the same temperature because nothing changes but something has been *done*. A transaction has occurred. The books are balanced but the books are always balanced and they have always been balanced and balancing them is not the purpose. Balancing them is the *state*.

Their bodies, if you touch one — and you will, because they are in all the water and the water is everywhere and you cannot avoid the water — are smooth and firm and warm. Warmer than the stone. Warmer than the ground-creatures. Almost body-temperature. Almost *your* body temperature. And this is the closest thing to recognition you will find here: the water-things are warm the way you are warm, and their warmth is the warmth of process, of something *happening* inside, and for a moment — a moment that is the same length as every other moment and therefore not a moment at all — you feel that they are the most alive things in this place, and then you feel that they are the least alive, and then you feel that *alive* is a word that has been borrowed from outside and does not apply, and you stand in the water and the water-things spiral around your ankles and you are part of the system and the system does not mind and the system does not notice and the system does not *not* notice. The system simply does what it does and what it does is *be*.

## The Engines

The engines do not live here. The engines *are* here.

But they belong in this accounting because they are the closest thing to life that this place offers, and if you spend long enough in the Condensary — and *long enough* is a phrase without content here, because enough is a quantity and quantities require accumulation and nothing accumulates — you will feel that the engines are alive in a way that the animals are not. The animals are *in* the system. The engines *are* the system. The animals are the media. The engines are the motive.

They are in the walls. They are in the floor. One is visible in the lower basin, a housing of dark metal — the same dark metal as the veins, the same material, continuous — emerging from the granite at a diagonal, a curved housing like the haunch of a machine, a bolted plate with bolts that are not bolts because they were not installed, they were *grown*, or they were always there, or the distinction was never available. The plate has a surface that is machined. The machining marks are visible if you press your face close and the water runs over your forehead and into your eyes and you do not care because the water is not hostile and the water is not anything and you can see the tooling marks, the gentle spiraling grooves of a cutting tool that has traced this surface, and the marks are perfect and the marks are not old because nothing here is old.

The engine turns. You can feel it turn if you hold the stone. A deep slow rotation, a grinding that is not grinding because grinding implies friction and friction implies wear and there is no wear. The engines do not wear. They have been turning and they have not been turning down. They have not been degrading. They are at the tolerance they were made to and they have always been at this tolerance and this is the fourth problem and it is the problem that breaks people, not the light, not the water, not the sound, but the *newness of the engines*, the fact that they are fresh, the fact that they are sharp, the fact that a machine that has been running forever shows no wear and this means that time is not passing here and if time is not passing then nothing is happening and if nothing is happening then you are not here and if you are not here then where are you and the answer is: you are in the Condensary, and the Condensary is a place where *where* does not require *when*.

The engines are alive the way a heart removed from a body and still beating is alive. Not because it wants to be. Not because it chose to be. Because it was made to pump and it pumps and it will pump until something stops it and nothing here stops anything.

## The Ones You Do Not See

There are things in the air. You feel them. You feel them the way you feel a room is occupied before you turn on the light. The air is wet and the wet has weight and the weight has *direction* — it moves, but not with wind, not with draft, not with convection or pressure differential. It moves with *intent*, and intent is a word that implies a mind, and there may be a mind here, a mind in the air, a mind that is the air, that is distributed through the moisture, that lives in each droplet the way information lives in a signal, and the mind is not thinking — it is not *having* thoughts — it is *being* a thought, one thought, the same thought, a thought that is the system, a thought that thinks: *run*.

The thought has no content beyond *run*. It is not a command. It is a state. The thought is the running of the system. The system thinks by running. The system is conscious in the way that a river is conscious, in the way that a clock is conscious, in the way that any loop is conscious if the loop is closed and the loop is fast enough and the loop has no beginning and no end and the loop is the world.

You do not see these things because they do not have surfaces. Everything else here has a surface — the stone has a surface, the plants have surfaces, the animals have surfaces, the water has a surface. The air-things do not. They are surfaceless. They are the *medium*. They are what the other things exist *in*. They are the water in the fish-tank, but alive, but running, but the engine's breath extended into the space and distributed and patient and formless.

They do not respond to you. They do not not-respond to you. They process you the way the water-things process water: something happens, something is exchanged, the system remains the same, you remain the same, the air-things remain the same, and the exchange has no name and no category and no output and no input and the system continues.

## The Plants

Last, because they are the most patient.

The plants *hold*. This was established. But what was not established is what they hold *for*, and the answer is: nothing. They hold for nothing. They hold because holding is what they do and they do what they do and they have always done what they do and their roots grip the sharp stone with a grip that is not desperate and not determined and not any word that implies a *self* behind the grip. The grip is the self. The holding is the being. They are what they do and they do what they are and this is a condition that humans have aspired to for as long as humans have had language and the plants have achieved it without language, without aspiration, without the gap between desire and fulfillment that makes aspiration necessary.

Their leaves are dark and spiraled and the spirals are the same spirals as the water-things and the same spirals as the engine housings and the same spirals as the arrangements of the moss-that-is-not-moss and this is because there is one shape here and the shape is a spiral and the spiral is the shape of a closed system and the system is closed and the system is the place and the place is the system and the plants are the system's lungs, the system's hands, the system's reaching, and they do not reach *toward* anything. They reach *as* the system. They are the reaching of the system. They are the system reaching into the air and holding the air and being held by the air and the holding is mutual and the holding is complete and the holding has always been complete.

If you touch a leaf it is cool and wet and firm and alive in the way that a new-made thing is alive, in the way that a just-finished product is alive, in the way that anything is alive that has been *completed* — not dying, not growing, not changing, but *completed*, finished, brought to the state it was intended to occupy and occupying it and the state is the leaf and the leaf is the state.

They are the most alive things here because they are the most *finished* things here. They are done. They have been done. They will be done. The animals move but the plants are *complete* and completion is the closest thing to life that this place recognizes because life — real life, outside life, your life — is a process of becoming, and becoming is a process of completing, and completing is a process of arriving, and the plants have arrived. They are the only things here that have arrived. They arrived before you came and they will be arrived after you leave and their arrival is their existence and their existence is *being here, fully, without remainder, without lack, without the gap between what they are and what they could be that outside we call *life*.*

## What Lives Here

Nothing lives here, in the way that *live* means to begin and to move toward ending. Everything lives here, in the way that *live* means to *be in a place* and to be in a place and to be in a place and to be in a place and the being does not start and does not stop and the place does not start and does not stop and the being is the place and the place is the being and the engines turn and the water moves and the birds are on branches and the ground-creatures are on the ground and the water-things are in the water and the air-things are in the air and the plants hold and the sound is the sound and the sound is the sound and the sound is the sound and

you are here. And you are not changing anything. And you are not not-changing anything. And the system does not know you are here. And the system is not unaware that you are here. And the system is running and the running is the knowing and the knowing is the running and the engines turn and the water finds your skin because the water finds all surfaces and you are a surface and the water is right and the system is right and the system is running and the system is running and the system is running.
