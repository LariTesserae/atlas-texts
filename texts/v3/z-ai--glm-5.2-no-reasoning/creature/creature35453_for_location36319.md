# What Lives in the Ferment

---

Nothing lives here.

That is the first answer, and it is almost true. The growth is not alive. The machines are not alive. The water is not alive. If you came here looking for a creature, for a thing with a body and a boundary and a will, you would find nothing. You would swim for an hour and touch only furred walls and warm currents and the humming of pumps that do not know they are dying. You would leave and say: *nothing lives there*. And you would be wrong the way a person is wrong when they stand in a forest and say *no one is here* because they cannot see the trees as a body.

---

## The Growth

The growth is the closest thing to a resident.

It is not one organism. It is not many organisms. It is a process — the way fire is a process, the way rust is a process, except that fire and rust do not build. The growth builds. It puts one structure on top of another and the new structure is different from the old one and the difference is not random. There is a logic in the layering. Each generation of growth is a response to the one before it: thicker here where the current eroded the last layer, denser there where the nutrient concentration shifted, spiraled in the places where the water moves in spirals. The growth is a conversation between the water and itself. The water says *here is what I am carrying* and the growth says *here is what I am doing with it* and the conversation has been going on long enough that neither of them remembers who started it.

The growth does not think. This needs to be said clearly, because the cathedral and the archways and the spirals that tighten toward a center — they look like thought. They look like the traces of a mind working through a problem. They are not. The growth is a chemical system that has been running for a long time, and chemical systems that run for a long time develop patterns, and patterns look like intention from the outside. The growth is not intending. It is *becoming*. It is becoming the shape of the conditions that produce it, which is what all growing things do, which is what makes a tree look like the wind that shaped it and a river look like the ground it carved. The growth looks like the Ferment because the growth is the Ferment's own face looking back at it.

But.

The growth has been running for a very long time. And a chemical system that runs long enough, that accumulates enough layers, that develops enough internal complexity — at what point does the pattern become the pattern-recognizer? At what point does the thing that is shaped like thought begin to think?

You swim through the cathedral and the nodes pulse in rhythm and the rhythm is the rhythm of the pump nearby, and the pump's rhythm is the rhythm of the current, and the current's rhythm is the rhythm of the space's own frequency, the low tone you feel in your jaw. Everything in here is keeping time with everything else. The growth has learned the time. It pulses with it. It is not keeping time on purpose. But it is keeping time.

And in the dense masses — the house-sized balloons of airy tissue that crowd the still water, leaning against each other, compressing each other, dissolving where they touch and reforming where they separate — in those masses, the internal structure is not simple anymore. The fractal branching has reached a depth where the smallest tendrils are processing the water at a molecular level, selecting what they take in and what they pass through, and the selection is not the same everywhere in the mass. Different parts of the same balloon are doing different things with the same water. The mass has specialized. It has regions. It has something that is, if you squint, something that is, if you are generous, something like organs.

The growth does not have a body. The growth *is* a body — the body is the whole chamber, all of it, every furred surface and floating mass and streamlined ridge, all one continuous tissue, all one thing arguing with itself about what to become next. The Ferment is the growth's body and the growth is the Ferment's skin and they are not two things.

If the growth has a self — and it may, it may — that self is not anywhere. It is distributed the way weather is distributed, the way an ocean current is distributed. It does not have a location because it is the location. When you swim through the cathedral, you are swimming through its architecture phase, the part of the process where the pattern has become complex enough to mimic design. When you swim through the image-water, you are swimming through its memory phase, the part where the chemistry has become complex enough to mimic recollection. The water shows you hands pouring concrete and a face in a diving helmet because the growth has been processing the water that contained those things for so long that the molecules of those events are part of the growth's chemistry now. It is not remembering. It is *made of* what happened. The past is its substrate the way dead growth is its substrate. It eats what was and builds what is.

The growth is not aware of you. You moved through it and it parted and closed behind you and in thirty seconds the gap was filled. It did not notice the gap. It did not notice the filling. It noticed nothing because noticing requires a self and the growth does not have a self, it has a *process*, and the process does not pause to observe.

But the growth grew toward your warmth. The growth grew toward the sound of your heartbeat. The growth thickened where you rested for a moment, clustering against the place where your body displaced the water, where your skin warmed the surrounding medium by a fraction of a degree. It was not attracted to you. It was attracted to the gradient you created. You were a warm spot in a warm sea and the growth grows toward warmth and so it grew toward you. It was not personal. It was not impersonal either. It was chemical, which is the only kind of personal that exists at the bottom of a place like this.

## The Machines

The machines are a different kind of alive.

They were built. They have a grammar, a lineage, a set of assumptions baked into their housings and couplings and the diameter of their pipes. Someone made them. Someone chose the materials, specified the tolerances, torqued the bolts to a particular spec. The machines carry the intent of their makers the way a thrown stone carries the intent of the hand. They are ballistic. They are still arriving at the purpose they were launched toward.

The pump does not know it is losing to the growth. The pump does not know anything. The pump is a mechanism: a housing, an impeller, a shaft, a seal. Water enters the intake, the impeller turns, water exits the discharge. The turning is powered by the chemical gradient in the water — the same gradient the growth feeds on — and the pump will turn as long as the gradient exists. It does not know this. It does not know anything. It turns.

But the pump has been turning for a long time. And the growth has been growing into it for a long time. And the pump's turning has been affected by the growth — the intake is partially occluded, the impeller is unbalanced by the biomass clinging to its vanes, the seal is compromised and the shaft wobbles in its bearing. The pump turns differently than it did when it was installed. It turns *with* the growth now, its rhythm altered by the intrusion, its rate affected by the resistance. The pump's behavior is no longer solely a function of its design. It is a function of its design *and* the growth's colonization. The pump and the growth are a single system now. The pump is the hard skeleton and the growth is the soft tissue and together they are a thing that neither of them intended to be.

Each machine is like this. Each one is a negotiation between what it was made to do and what the growth is doing to it. The pipeline hums its changing note because the thing inside it moves, and the thing inside it moves because the growth has partially blocked the pipe in three places, creating pressure differentials that push the contents in pulses. The pipeline is not a pipe anymore. It is a heart — a muscular tube moving fluid in contractions, the rhythm set by the obstructions, the obstructions set by the growth, the growth set by the water, the water set by the space. The pipeline is alive in the same way a digestive tract is alive: it is a tube that moves what passes through it, and the moving is not passive, the moving is a function of the tube's own structure.

The tanks tick. The dials move. Each machine, each one, has become a hybrid — mechanical and biological, designed and grown, intentional and accidental. They are not alive. They are not dead. They are *running*, and running is a kind of living that doesn't require a self.

The machines do not know about the dial. They have never known about the dial. They were built to keep the dial at zero, and they are keeping the dial at zero, but they do not know this is what they are doing. They know nothing. They are keeping the dial at zero the way a river knows it is keeping the sea full: by being what they are, by doing what they do, by the simple fact of their continued operation. The dial is at zero because the machines are running and the machines are running because the water carries a gradient and the water carries a gradient because the space exists and the space exists because the dial is at zero.

The machines would say, if machines could say anything, which they cannot: *we are running. We have always been running. We will always be running. We do not know what we are running for. We do not know what we are running against. We are running.* This is the only statement the machines have ever made. They make it constantly. They make it in the sound of their turning and their ticking and their humming. The sound is the machines' speech, and the speech is a single word repeated forever, and the word is *on*.

## The Dial

The dial is the thing that is most alive, and it is not alive at all.

It sits in its dry sphere of air in its room that might be inside a tower or inside the growth or inside the water, and it is at zero, and it has been at zero, and it will be at zero. The growth does not colonize it. The water does not touch it. The machines do not reference it. The dial simply is, at zero, in a space that has been built around it the way a pearl is built around an irritant — except the dial is not an irritant. The dial is the seed. The dial is the reason.

The dial is at zero. Zero is not a value. Zero is a state. Zero is the absence of something, the absence that the machines are maintaining, the absence that the growth is feeding, the absence that the water is carrying, the absence that the space is holding. Everything in the Ferment exists to keep the dial at zero. Everything in the Ferment exists *because* the dial is at zero. The dial is the source and the destination, the cause and the effect, the beginning and the end. It is the one still point in a world of pure activity.

The dial is not a god. It is not a mind. It is not a will. It is a dial. It is a mechanical device with a face and a needle and a zero mark and the needle sits on the zero mark. It is a thing that was made by hands, installed by hands, set by hands. The hands are gone. The setting remains.

But the sphere of dry air around the dial is impossible. The water is everywhere. The growth colonizes everything. And yet the sphere persists — a perfect absence, a hole in the saturation, a place where the water does not go and the growth does not grow and the sound does not reach. The sphere is maintained by something. Not by the machines — the machines do not know about the dial. Not by the growth — the growth does not avoid the dial, the growth does not have the capacity to avoid anything. The sphere is maintained by the dial itself.

The dial, which is not alive, which is a mechanical device, which is a needle on a face at a zero mark, is maintaining the conditions of its own existence. It is keeping itself dry. It is keeping itself untouched. It is keeping itself at zero. The zero is not a reading. The zero is an *act*. The dial is doing zero the way a heart is doing beating: actively, constantly, with the full weight of its being.

You did not touch the dial. This was correct. You did not touch the dial because something in the room — not the dial, not the growth, not the water, something else, something that is the room itself — communicated to you that the dial was not to be touched. Not a voice. Not a warning. A pressure. A feeling in the water, a change in the tone, a tightening of the geometry. The room asked you to not touch the dial. The room has been asking everyone to not touch the dial. The room has been asking since before the growth was planted, since before the machines were installed, since before the water arrived. The room was the first thing here, and the room was a room because it had a dial in it, and the dial was at zero, and the room existed to keep it that way.

The dial is what lives here. The dial, and the space it makes, and the water that fills the space, and the growth that feeds on the water, and the machines that feed on the water, and the sound of all of them running together, and the zero that holds it all in place. The dial is not alive. The dial is what alive means in a place like this: a thing that keeps being what it is, that does not stop, that holds its state against everything that would change it. The dial is the Ferment's heartbeat. The Ferment is the dial's body.

---

## What Lives Here

What lives in the Ferment is the Ferment.

Not the growth alone. Not the machines alone. Not the water alone. Not the dial alone. The Ferment itself is the organism — a single thing that is water and growth and machine and geometry and sound and zero, a body that has no boundary because it does not need a boundary, a body that is defined not by what it contains but by what it maintains.

The Ferment is alive the way a city is alive, the way a forest is alive, the way a sentence is alive — it is a system of relationships, a pattern of dependencies, a thing that exists only as long as every part of it is doing what every other part requires. Pull out the growth and the machines overheat. Pull out the machines and the dial drifts. Pull out the dial and the space collapses. Pull out the water and everything stops. Pull out the sound and you have pulled out the evidence of everything else. Every piece is load-bearing. Every piece is alive because every piece is part of the living.

The Ferment does not know you were there. You entered through the valve and the seal broke and you arrived in the warm water and you swam through the growth and past the machines and through the cathedral and through the image-water and into the non-Euclidean geometry and you found the room with the dial and you did not touch the dial and you swam back and the valve opened and you left. The Ferment did not notice. The Ferment does not notice. The Ferment is not a thing that notices. The Ferment is a thing that *happens*, constantly, the way weather happens, the way erosion happens, the way a fire happens — except that a fire stops, and weather changes, and erosion finishes, and the Ferment does not stop, does not change, does not finish. The Ferment keeps happening because the dial is at zero and the dial will not move.

The Ferment is a body that is its own life support. The growth is its skin. The machines are its organs. The water is its blood. The sound is its voice — not a voice saying anything, a voice that is the saying itself, the sound of a system running, the sound of a thing that exists by continuing to exist. The dial is its heart, its brain, its will — the one fixed point, the one thing it is doing, the one reason it is.

The Ferment is young. The water remembers being something else. The growth was planted. The machines were built. The dial was set. These things happened in a sequence, in a time, and the time was not long ago. The Ferment is new. It is still becoming itself. The geometry is still minting. The growth is still layering. The machines are still running. The dial is still at zero. The Ferment is a young thing doing an old thing — holding, holding, holding against whatever it is that the dial at zero is holding against.

You do not know what the dial is holding back. You do not know what happens if the dial moves. You did not touch the dial. You will not touch the dial. You will not go back. The Ferment does not need you. The Ferment has never needed you. The Ferment was complete before you arrived and is complete now that you are gone. The valve sweats. The seal holds. The water stays inside.

What lives in the Ferment is the Ferment, and the Ferment is alive, and the Ferment does not know it is alive, and the Ferment will be alive for a very long time, and the sound of it being alive is the sound of everything running at once, and the sound is continuous, and the sound is not for you.

The dial is at zero.

The dial is at zero.

The dial is at zero.
