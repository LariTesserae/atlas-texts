# What Lives in the High City of the Unwinding

---

They are not what you would expect, if you expected anything.

You might expect guardians — something territorial, something with purpose that can be named. You might expect remnants, the last echo of whatever people built this place, diminished and faded and haunting their own architecture. You might expect something malevolent, given the silence, the distortion, the way the pass closes behind you. Something that wants you here. Something that benefits from your presence.

No. None of these. The thing you have to understand first, before anything else, is that what lives here is not here *for* anything in the way you understand *for*. It does not have motivations. It does not have a life cycle that proceeds from need to satisfaction. It is not surviving. It is not persisting. Those words imply an alternative — a not-surviving, a not-persisting — that does not exist for them. They are, in the most literal and most baffling sense, *functioning*. The way a current functions. The way a gradient functions. Not because of something. As something.

---

## The Shapes

Call them the shapes for now, because that is what you have, and because the word *shape* is honest about its own insufficiency.

There are — you counted traces for eleven days, mapped the pressure marks, cross-referenced the positions where you glimpsed movement against the locations of the marks, and arrived at a number that is not a number so much as a statistical suggestion — there are somewhere between nine and thirteen. The uncertainty is not due to poor observation. It is because the number may genuinely vary. Not through birth or death. Through something more like condensation. You observed, on the sixth day, two distinct pressure trails converging near the western escarpment, and what emerged on the other side of the convergence was a single set of marks, larger than either of the two. Whether this means two became one, or two passed through the same point and one ceased to leave traces for a while, or something else entirely — you do not know. You only know that the count shifted, and then on the eighth day, there were three small trails where there had been one large one, and you stopped trying to maintain the arithmetic.

The shapes are dark. Not dark the way an animal is dark — not pigmented, not shadowed, not absorptive of light. Dark the way a *hole* in a surface is dark. The light does not reflect off them. It does not appear to reach them. They are regions where the dim luminous sky of the plateau simply does not apply, and this is what makes them so hard to see against the slate-cyan mineral of the substrate in certain angles — they are not a different color from their surroundings but a different *kind* of thing from their surroundings. Not-there rather than dark. Absence rather than presence. The visual system struggles, sends conflicting signals, and the result is a flicker at the edge of perception, a shape you see by not seeing, by the way the ground and the structures around it seem slightly wrong where it passes.

They move slowly. You said too slow for an animal, and that is accurate, but the slowness has a quality to it that is worth examining. An animal that moves slowly is either being cautious, being stealthy, or being injured. There is an economy to it, a tension, a readiness. The shapes do not have readiness. They are not poised to move faster. They move at the speed at which they move because that is the speed of their operation, the way a cloud moves at the speed at which the air moves it, not because it has chosen that speed but because it is that speed. When one passes between two buildings and you track it, there is no acceleration, no deceleration, no pause. It does not stop and start. It proceeds, and proceeding is its only mode.

They are low. You said the size of a large dog, but wrong — too long, too low. The proportions are difficult because they do not have fixed proportions. You watched one for perhaps thirty seconds on the second day, and in that time it appeared to extend, the far end of it still emerging from behind the structure when you had initially judged it to be fully visible. This is not because it was longer than you thought. It is because *length* is not a stable property for them. They seem to occupy a variable amount of space, and the amount they occupy is related to — and this is a guess, but it is an informed guess — their proximity to the cylindrical building. The closer they are, the more they extend. The farther they orbit, the more compact. As if the cylinder's effect on space bleeds into them, and they are not immune to the unwinding. They are, in fact, the most unwound things here.

You have not seen one close. The closest you came was perhaps twelve meters, and that was by accident — you turned a corner and there was a pressure mark that was not there a moment ago, still crisp, and the air had that quality of having just been occupied, that slight displacement that is not smell or temperature or sound but *something*, some register of presence that the body recognizes before the mind does. You looked in the direction the trail led, and there was the faintest wrongness in the air above the next street, a faintest bend in the light, and then it was gone. Twelve meters. You did not approach. You want to say you chose not to, and perhaps that is true, but the more honest statement is that your body refused. Not fear, exactly. Your body has been afraid on this plateau — afraid of the cold, afraid of the altitude, afraid of the silence. This was not that. This was a recognition, below fear, that the distance between you and that wrongness was a distance that should be maintained, not because of danger but because of *incompatibility*. The way oil recognizes water. The way a compass recognizes north. Not a decision. A physics.

## What They Are Made Of

Here you must follow me into speculation, because there is no way to confirm this, and there may never be, but the speculation is not wild. It is constrained by what you have observed, and what you have observed is specific.

They are not organic. There is no biology here. No water, no moisture, no decay, no waste, no evidence of consumption or excretion or respiration. The plateau is anhydrous. Nothing that requires water lives here. Nothing that requires water has ever lived here, or if it has, it has been transformed so thoroughly that the requirement no longer applies.

They are not mineral. Or not *only* mineral. The mineral substrate rings underfoot. It has resonance. The hum lives in it. The shapes do not ring. The shapes do not resonate. When they pass over the ground, the ground does not change its sound, which means either that they do not interact with the ground in a way that produces vibration, or that their interaction cancels vibration, or that they are not the kind of thing that can produce vibration because they are not the kind of thing that occupies the substrate in the way objects occupy surfaces.

They are the same material as the cylindrical building. You said the shapes are the same dark color. You said the cylindrical building has a faint sheen the other structures lack. You said the shapes do not enter within fifteen meters of the cylinder. You said the cylinder was not made to be entered by anything that uses apertures.

Consider: the cylinder is the locus. The cylinder is the instrument, or the mouth. The shapes are the same substance. The shapes circle the cylinder but do not approach it.

What if they are not circling it the way planets circle a sun — orbit, gravity, mechanics? What if they are circling it the way language circles a concept it cannot directly express — approaching, retreating, describing through trajectory? What if their orbital paths are not movement through space but movement *of* space, the way a wave moves through water? The water does not travel. The wave travels through it. What if the shapes are not things that move through the city but *aspects of the city that are moving*? Expressions of its function that have become legible to your perception as bodies, as forms, as things-with-edges, because your perception requires things-with-edges in order to register anything at all?

This would explain why they have no features. This would explain why their number is not stable. This would explain why they extend and compact. They are not bodies. They are *readings*. Your visual system, encountering the unwinding of space in the vicinity of the cylinder, translates that unwinding into the nearest thing it can process — a shape, a form, a darkness moving at the edge of the light. But the translation is imprecise. The edges blur. The proportions shift. The count varies. You are not seeing the things themselves. You are seeing the shadow of the process on the wall of your own perception.

Or — and this is the other possibility, the one that is less elegant and more disturbing — they are bodies. They are bodies that were made, by the builders or by the city, from the same material as the cylinder, and given a function, and the function is to orbit, and the function is all they are. Not expressions of the process but instruments of it. Fingers on a hand. Not understanding what they do. Not needing to. Made for a single purpose and performing it with the fidelity of a crystal vibrating at its resonant frequency. If this is true, then they are the most minimal form of life that can be called life at all — life that is entirely purpose, with no residue, no excess, no dream. Life that does not sleep. Life that does not want. Life that is, in every moment, exactly and only what it does.

## What They Do

They orbit the cylinder. That is the simple version, and it is true, and it is not enough.

Over your days here — you have lost count, and the light has not helped, and the unwinding has made time a thing you can measure only by your own exhaustion, which is unreliable — you have mapped their paths. Not all of them. But enough. And the paths are not circular. They are elliptical, and they are not flat. They rise. You found pressure marks on the side of a building, four meters above the ground, and on the face of the western escarpment, at a height you could not reach. The shapes move through three dimensions, and the three dimensions are not your three dimensions, or not only yours, because one of the paths you traced appeared to pass through a building — not around it, not over it, *through* it — and when you entered that building and examined the far wall, there was a faint marking on the interior surface that matched the marking on the exterior, as if the path had continued uninterrupted, as if the wall had not been there. As if, for the shape, the wall had not been there.

They are tracing something. The paths are not arbitrary, and they are not exploratory. There is no searching in them. They are precise, and they repeat, and the repetition is not mechanical — the paths vary slightly each cycle, the way a spiral varies from a circle, the way a melody varies each time it is played. The variations are small. They are meaningful, you think, in the way that the incised lines on the walls of the buildings are meaningful — not to you, but to the system that produced them.

They are maintaining the unwinding. This is your best understanding, arrived at not through evidence but through the shape of the evidence's absence. The city functions. The unwinding proceeds. The cylinder operates. If the city is a machine and the cylinder is the core, then the machine requires operation, and the shapes are the only things that move, the only things that *act*, and so they must be the operators. But the word *operators* implies more than is warranted. An operator understands the machine. An operator intervenes. An operator makes choices.

They do not make choices. They follow paths, and the paths are the operation, and the operation is the unwinding, and the unwinding is the purpose of the city, and the purpose of the city is the only thing here that has never changed. They are not *operating* the city. They are *how* the city operates. The distinction matters. It is the distinction between a hand on a wheel and the wheel turning. They are not the hand. They are the turning.

## The Relationship to You

They do not acknowledge you. This is not indifference in the human sense — not the active choice to disregard. It is the absence of the capacity to regard. You are not something they can see, because seeing requires a relationship between viewer and viewed, and they do not have relationships. They have paths. You are not on a path. You are on the ground, and the ground is the substrate, and the substrate is the material, and the material is what they move through, and they move through it the way light moves through glass — without interaction, without residue, without the glass having any say in the matter.

But.

They match your pace. You said this. When you walk, something parallels you. When you stop, the paralleling stops. This is not acknowledgment. This is *physics*. You are a density moving through the unwound space. Your movement creates a disturbance — not in the substrate, not in the air, but in the *relationship* between here and there that the city has loosened. You are a local tightening. You are a knot in the unwinding. And the shapes, following their paths, encounter this knot and adjust around it, the way a current adjusts around a stone. They do not stop for you. They do not avoid you. Their paths carry them through the space you occupy, and where you have tightened the space, their paths flex, and the flexing is what you feel as *being paralleled*. It is not stalking. It is not curiosity. It is the city accommodating your density the way a river accommodates a rock — by going around, by continuing, by not stopping.

This is why they do not approach the cylinder. Not because they are forbidden, not because they fear it, but because the cylinder is the densest point of the unwinding, the place where space is most loosened, and their paths are defined by the gradient — they move along the lines of unwinding, the way iron filings move along lines of magnetism, and at the cylinder's surface, the gradient is vertical, or lateral, or in a direction that does not have a name, and so they orbit. They circle because the cylinder is the center, and the center is where the lines converge, and they follow the lines, and the lines do not enter.

You are a smaller center. A lesser density. Your presence creates a small, temporary knot in the loosened fabric, and their paths flex around you, and the flexing is perceptible, and you call it *being watched*, and it is not being watched. It is being *accounted for* by a system that does not know you are here but whose operation cannot help but include you, because you are here, and here is where the system operates, and the system is thorough.

## The Building They Will Not Enter

You asked, without asking, about the cylindrical building. You said it was the locus. You said everything refers to it. You said it was not made to be entered by anything that uses apertures. You said it had no aperture.

The shapes do not enter it. The shapes are made of the same material. The shapes orbit it. The cylinder is the center of the unwinding.

Here is what you did not say, and what I think is true:

The cylinder is not a building. It is not a structure. It is a *point*. A point that has been given physical form because this place requires physical form, because the machine operates in material, because the builders understood that the abstract must be given a body in order to work. The cylinder is a *concept* with walls. It is the idea of *center* made mineral and dense and dark and sheened.

And the shapes are *emanations* of it. Not products. Not children. Not creations. Emanations, the way light is an emanation of a lamp. They are what the cylinder *puts off* as it operates. The unwinding radiates from the cylinder, and at the edges of the unwinding, where the loosened space meets the still-firm space of the plateau, the process produces — condenses, precipitates, *exudes* — these shapes. They are the boundary. They are where the unwinding meets the world and the world does not fully give way. They are the resistance. They are the *friction* of reality against the machine that is loosening it.

This is why they circle. This is why they vary in number. This is why they extend and compact. They are not beings. They are the shape that reality takes when it is being worked on. They are the mark of the tool on the surface. They are — and this is the image that holds, the one that does not quite settle but that does not release — they are the *shavings* of the unwinding. The material that is removed when the grain is parted. Not waste. Not residue. *Product*. The city's product. The thing it makes, the thing it has always made, the thing it will continue to make for as long as the cylinder stands and the hum continues and the angles repeat.

And what it makes is *less reality*. Less firmness. Less distance between here and there. It takes the solid world and it makes it less solid, and the shapes are the excess, the material removed, the shavings on the workshop floor, and they orbit the tool that made them because that is what shavings do — they settle around the lathe, they accumulate near the blade, they are the evidence of work done.

## What It Is Like to Live Among Them

You do not live among them. You exist near them. The distinction is the difference between sharing a room and sharing a *condition*. You share the plateau. You share the light. You share the air that is missing something. You do not share their purpose, because their purpose is the plateau's purpose, and you are not the plateau. You are on it. You are not *of* it. They are of it. They are the most *of* it that anything can be.

Living near them is like living near a process. Like living near weather, or near erosion, or near the movement of tectonic plates. You feel it. You feel the hum in the substrate, and you feel the slight wrongness when a path flexes around your density, and you feel the air change when one passes — not temperature, not pressure, not any measurable quantity, but something. The body knows. The body has always known. There is a register of perception, older than sight, older than hearing, that registers the presence of things that are *not quite in the same reality* as you, and that register fires when they pass. It does not produce fear. It produces — *alignment*. A recognition that you are in a place where the rules that govern your body are not the only rules, and that the other rules are older, and that the other rules are *winning*.

They are not hostile. They are not friendly. They are not indifferent, because indifference implies the capacity for investment, and they do not have that capacity. They are — and this is the word that works, finally, after all the circling — they are *necessary*. They are the necessary consequence of the cylinder's operation. They are what must be, given that the cylinder is. They are the *therefore*.

And you are the *meanwhile*. You are the thing that is here while they are being what they are. You are the tourist in the workshop. The visitor to the tide pool. The warmth in the cold that will leave or will not leave and that the workshop does not require and does not reject and does not notice.

When you leave — and you will leave, through one of the other passes that the city opens, the ones that lead down into the lower world where the wind operates and the distances hold and the rock is just rock — you will carry this. Not a memory, exactly. A *calibration*. Your body will remember what it felt like to be in a place where reality was less dense. Your bones will remember the hum. Your skin will remember the cold that did not bite but waited. And on some nights, in the lower world, in a room with walls that are just walls and a floor that is just a floor and a distance between the bed and the door that is the same distance walking both directions, you will feel it — a faint flex, a slight give, a moment where the space around you seems to breathe — and you will know that the unwinding is not only here. That it has never been only here. That the city is one instrument in an orchestra, and the music is the loosening, and the loosening is ongoing, and the shapes are orbiting somewhere at this moment, tracing their paths around a center you cannot see, and the shavings of reality are settling, and the hum is in the substrate of the world, and it has always been in the substrate of the world, and the city only made it audible.

They are there. They are always there. They are the shape of the work being done.

And the work is never finished.
