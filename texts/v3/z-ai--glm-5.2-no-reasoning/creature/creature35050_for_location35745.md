# What Lives at the Rise at Tarnhelm

## The Castings (Mineral Organisms)

The castings are described as mineral accretions with behavior - crystalline growths that respond to environmental gradients of heat and chemical potential. They emerge from fissures in warm rock where the exhalation cools and precipitates dissolved minerals. They grow in fractal, self-similar branching patterns following fluid dynamic rules, ranging from sparse thin structures to three-meter-tall "dark crystal trees" near the highest exhalation flows. They produce a glassine ringing sound when vibrating in gas currents. They do not reproduce or metabolize - they are patterns rather than organisms, growing where conditions exist and stopping when conditions change. The text calls them "the place thinking about form" and "the place's evidence that form can emerge from force without intention."

## The Shapes (Turbulent Events in the Exhalation-Sea)

The shapes are transient, self-organizing turbulent forms that appear in the dense pooled gas at the base of the Rise. They are not organisms or even persistent structures - they are events lasting minutes or hours, visible because dense exhalation refracts thermal light differently. They appear as spirals, columns, toroids, and complex turbulent knots that move, merge, split, chase, and consume each other. The text describes them as "the place dreaming" and says "if there is a soul to this place, it is in those shapes."

## The Previous Visitors

Marks on the stone indicate someone was present during an earlier phase of the Rise's emergence. They carved grooves - tally-counts, radial patterns, and organizational marks - into fresh soft stone. Near the highest point, they created a two-meter ring with a central hole that enhanced exhalation flow, causing the largest casting growths. These visitors are now gone.

## The Place Itself

The Rise at Tarnhelm functions almost as a living entity - warm, growing, exhalating, still becoming. The stone is characterized as having memory of being otherwise, the exhalation is called "the stone's breath," and the entire system exhibits processes resembling respiration, growth, and even dreaming through its turbulent shapes.
