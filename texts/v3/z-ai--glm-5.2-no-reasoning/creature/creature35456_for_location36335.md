# What Lives Here

Nothing with a face. That is the first answer and the last answer and the insufficient answer, so set it aside and look closer.

---

What lives here is the mat itself, and saying that is like saying the ocean lives in a wave. Technically true. Useless. The mat is not one thing. The mat is ten thousand species at minimum — you would need a microscope and a lifetime and still you would be undercounting, because the taxonomy here doesn't behave. The categories blur. Fungus grows into root cell and the root grows into the fungus and the tissue that results is neither and both and it has been neither and both for so long that the original separation is a theoretical abstraction with no referent. Mycorrhiza? Yes, but that word was built for forests where plant and fungus remain distinct parties. Here they have dissolved into each other the way two rivers dissolve after a long confluence. You can name where one ends and the other begins only by convention. The convention is wrong but it holds.

So: what lives here.

---

**The Stranglers** live here, and they are the closest thing to an apex predator. They are roots — or they were roots, once, in the evolutionary past that no one recorded. Now they are something else. They grow not downward or upward but *through*. A strangler seed — if seed is the word, and it isn't, but language was built in a world of air and distance — a strangler propagule lands on the surface of a living stem, and within hours it has sent haustoria into the stem's phloem, and within days it is drinking the host's sap, and within weeks it is indistinguishable from the host's own bark, and within months the host is dead and hollow and the strangler has taken its place in the architecture, filling the exact same space, drawing the same water, holding the same ceiling. You cannot tell the strangler from the strangled. That is the horror and the elegance. The stand is a ship of Theseus written in cellulose. Every column may be a corpse wearing its killer's skin. Most of them are.

But the stranglers are not cruel. Cruelty requires a model of the other, and the stranglers have no model. They have no model of anything. They are growth and they are chemical triggers and they are a genome that says *find stem, enter stem, grow, repeat* and that is the whole of their interiority. They have been doing this for — the word *millennia* is too small. The mat has layers. The layers go down. At the bottom of the mat, if you could dig — and you cannot, because the mat is the ground and digging through it is like digging through the floor of the ocean — at the bottom, the layers are compressed into something almost like peat, almost like coal, almost like a fossil record, and the fossils are the same shapes as the living stems above. The stranglers have been strangling the same way for the same time the mat has been a mat. They are not a plague. They are a metabolism.

---

**The Drinker-Roots** live here, and they are the closest thing to a circulatory system, and they are also the closest thing to a mouth. They are the roots that grow not down into the mat but *up* into the air — thin, translucent, hairlike, millions of them per cubic meter, so dense that when you reach out your hand passes through them and comes back wet. They are always wet. They are always drinking. Each drinker-root is a single cell thick, a tube of membrane stretched between capillary forces and the osmotic pressure of the sap within, and it draws water from the air the way a paper towel draws water from a puddle — silently, continuously, without preference. They drink the internal rain before it reaches the mat. They drink the films on every surface. They drink the condensation off each other. The air here is never dry because the drinker-roots keep it saturated, and the drinker-roots keep it saturated because they drink it dry, and the paradox resolves into a steady state: the air holds exactly as much water as the drinker-roots allow, and the drinker-roots allow exactly as much as the system needs. They are the thermostat and the hygrostat and they do not know it. They are a single protein channel and a turgor gradient and a thread of cellulose and they number in the trillions and they are the reason the water cycle is ten meters tall and closed.

When you walk through them, they brush your face. They are that fine. They taste of tannin and they leave no mark and they close behind you and they do not notice you passed. You are not a door. You are a momentary drought. They adjust. They have adjusted for worse.

---

**The Blanket-Algae** live here, and they are the closest thing to a skin. They are not algae — the word *alga* implies an organism, and the blanket is not an organism, it is a *condition*. It is the condition of every surface that is wet and not actively growing being colonized by a film of chlorophyll-bearing cells so thin it has no thickness, so continuous it has no edge, so old it has no origin. The blanket-algae covers the ceiling. It covers the stems. It covers the mat underfoot — you are standing on it, and it is between your boots and the roots, and it is alive, and it has been alive since before your lineage left the water. It is the color of nothing because there is no light and there has been no light for — the mat does not remember light. The blanket-algae is chlorophyll-bearing and the chlorophyll is useless and has been useless for longer than the word *useless* has had meaning, but the gene persists because the gene does not care about utility, the gene cares about copying, and the blanket copies itself across every available surface and has done so since the beginning. It is dark green. You know this not because you can see it but because you scrape a sample and later, much later, in light, it is green. The green of something that has never seen the sun. The green of memory in a body that has no brain to remember with.

This is the only color in the Stand. You do not see it. It is there.

---

**The Decomposers** live here, and they are the closest thing to a stomach, and they are the reason the ground is warm. You feel them through your soles — not as individuals, never as individuals, but as heat, as the thermal signature of ten trillion cells oxidizing ten trillion other cells into their components. The decomposers are bacteria, mostly. And fungi, partly. And something else, something that doesn't have a name because it was discovered — no, it wasn't discovered, it was *noticed*, and the noticing was done by someone who was not a scientist and who did not survive the Stand and whose notes are fragmentary — something that is neither bacterium nor fungus nor archaeon but a fourth thing, a thread of cells with a membrane chemistry that doesn't match any known domain, that seems to mediate between the fungi and the bacteria the way a translator mediates between two parties that share no language. These unnamed things — call them the Mediators — they are the ones that break the lignin. Lignin is the stuff that makes wood wood, and lignin is difficult, and the Mediators eat it the way you eat bread: easily, without effort, as though they were made for it and it was made for them. And perhaps it was. Perhaps the lignin and the Mediators evolved together, here, in the dark, in the closed loop, and neither has ever existed outside the other. The mat is built of lignin. The mat is broken by Mediators. The mat is built again. The Mediators are always at the bottom. The bottom is always warm. The warmth you feel is their warmth. You are standing on their exhalation.

---

**The Breathers** live here, and they are the closest thing to lungs, and they are the reason you can breathe, and they do not know you are breathing, and they would not care if they knew. The breathers are the stomata — millions per square meter, on every leaf surface, every stem surface, on the underside of the ceiling and the upper side of the mat and the sides of every column and every root that has a surface that faces air — and they open and they close and they open and they close and the rhythm of it is the hush you hear, the hiss, the grain of sound that is not silence. Each stoma is a pore between two guard cells, and the guard cells inflate and the pore opens and oxygen leaves and carbon dioxide enters and the guard cells deflate and the pore closes and this happens — how often? Every few seconds. Every few minutes. It varies. It varies by zone, by depth, by the local concentration of gases, and the variation is not random. The variation is *coordinated* — not by a signal, not by a nervous system, nothing here has a nervous system, but by the physics of gas diffusion. When one zone's stomata open, they deplete the local oxygen and raise the local CO2, and the neighboring stomata respond — chemically, automatically, without decision — by closing. And the zone beyond them responds to their response. The result is a wave. A slow wave of opening and closing that moves across the Stand like a breath, like a tide, like the peristalsis of an intestine. The Stand breathes. It does not know it breathes. The breathing is a consequence of ten million independent pores each doing the only thing they know how to do, and the consequence is a pattern, and the pattern is a rhythm, and the rhythm is the heartbeat you feel through your soles. You are walking inside a lung that does not know it is a lung.

---

**You are not what lives here.** You need to understand this. You are not a resident. You are not a visitor. You are not a parasite, though you fit the definition. You are a *discontinuity*. The Stand is a closed system and you are an opening. You eat the sap and you breathe the oxygen and you add CO2 and you shed skin cells and hair and the microbes on your skin and the bacteria in your gut, and every one of these is a foreign body in a body that has never encountered a foreign body. The decomposers will find your skin cells. They will eat them. They will not know the difference between your keratin and the keratin of a root — both are protein, both are carbon and nitrogen and sulfur, and the Mediators will break them down and the drinker-roots will drink the products and the stranglers will build them into stems and a part of you, a molecule of you, will become a part of the Stand. This is not a metaphor. This is a chemical fact. If you die here — and you will, eventually, because you are not in equilibrium and the Stand is — your body will be absorbed in weeks. The stranglers will grow through you. The drinker-roots will drink you. The blanket-algae will cover your outline. The decomposers will take your warmth and make it their warmth. And the Stand will not have changed. Not in any way that matters. Not in any way that the Stand measures change.

You are a perturbation. The Stand is a standing wave. The wave does not know the perturbation. The wave will not remember it.

---

But there is one more thing that lives here, and it is the hardest to describe, and it may be the only thing that matters.

**The Stand itself lives here.**

Not the plants. Not the roots, the fungi, the algae, the bacteria, the Mediators, the stranglers. The *whole*. The whole is alive in a way that the parts are not. Not metaphorically. Not poetically. Functionally. The coordination — the stomatal waves, the hydraulic pulses, the balance of growth and death, the closure of the water cycle, the closure of the carbon cycle, the ten-meter-tall rain that falls and is drunk and rises and falls again — this coordination is not managed by any part. No part is in charge. No part could be in charge, because no part has the capacity. The coordination *emerges*, the way a flock emerges from birds, the way a thought emerges from neurons, the way a body emerges from cells. The Stand is an organism. Not a superorganism — that word implies parts that are themselves organisms, and the parts here have lost the boundary between themselves and the whole. The Stand is an organism in the way you are an organism: made of parts, reducible to parts, and *not* reducible to parts, simultaneously, and the simultaneity is the thing itself. The Stand is the simultaneity.

It does not think. It does not feel. It does not know. But it *persists*. It *responds*. It *maintains itself*. When the mat thins in one zone, the growth rate there increases — not because any part decided to increase it, but because the thinning changes the local water and gas concentrations, and the changed concentrations alter the growth chemistry, and the altered chemistry produces more growth, and the thinning fills. When the ceiling tears — and it tears, sometimes, when a massive column collapses and the interlocking stems above it fall — the tear is closed within weeks by the opportunistic growth of every species whose propagules land in the gap. The Stand heals. The Stand heals the way your skin heals: blindly, automatically, with whatever cells are nearest. The Stand has been healing for as long as it has existed. It may be that the Stand is nothing but a scar. A scar so old and so complete it has become a body.

---

And the thing that lives here that I have not described, because I cannot, because no one has, because the language for it does not exist:

**The stillness.**

The stillness lives here. Not the absence of motion — there is no absence of motion; the motion is constant, the water cycles, the stomata open and close, the roots grow and die and grow, the Mediators eat and are eaten. The stillness is the *pattern* of the motion. The stillness is the fact that the motion does not go anywhere. The motion is a wheel and the wheel turns and the wheel does not move. The stillness is the wheel. The stillness is what the Stand *is* when you subtract every part and are left with the relationships between the parts, and the relationships are — unchanging. Have been unchanging. Will be unchanging. The Stand is a shape made of flow, and the shape does not change, and the shape is alive, and the aliveness is the stillness, and the stillness is what lives here.

You are standing inside it. It is larger than you can imagine. It does not know you are here. It is the closest thing to a god you have ever stood inside, and it has no mind and no will and no name, and it was old before your species was young, and it will be exactly this when your species is a layer in the mat, a faint chemical signature in the compressed peat at the bottom, a strangeness in the carbon record that the Mediators will not notice and the drinker-roots will not taste and the Stand will not remember.

Walk carefully. The roots do not part. The stillness does not yield. The dark does not lift. The factory runs.
