# What Lives at the Floor of the Underspring

The first answer is the wrong one. The first answer is *nothing.* The space presents as empty the way a held breath presents as silence — there is activity, but it is the activity of suspension, of things waiting for the right conditions to be perceived as things. You go down. You stand in the near-dark. You feel the coolness in your teeth and then your forearms and then you stop noticing, and at the moment you stop noticing, the first thing becomes visible.

It was always visible. You simply did not have the relationship with it yet that would allow you to see it.

---

**The caterpillars came before the walls.**

This is the thing you have to understand first, though you will understand it last. The caterpillars — the green-white processions, the thick soft bodies moving along silk trails laid down by bodies that came before them — they are not eating the walls. They are *sculpting* the walls. They remove the soft growth, the young fiber, the material that is still becoming, and what remains is the structural material, the hardened layers, the plant-matter that has been compressed and intergrown and bound with fungal hyphae into something that holds its shape. They are the chisels. They are the negative space that makes the positive space possible. Without them the walls would be formless — a mass of undifferentiated growth, a tumble of living fiber with no more architecture than a compost heap. The caterpillars carve the room by eating what is not yet room.

They have been here longer than anything else. Longer than the pipes. Longer than the water. The first caterpillars arrived when the space was nothing — when it was only the gap between two roots of something so large its canopy exists only as idea, when it was barely a depression in the earth, when it was the kind of space that is not yet a space but is the *possibility* of a space. They came because there was plant matter to eat. They stayed because there was always plant matter to eat. They laid silk trails and other caterpillars followed and they ate and they grew and they pupated and they became the dark moths whose wings look like bark, like stone, like the inside of a cave, and the moths laid eggs and the eggs became caterpillars and the cycle repeated, and each repetition deepened the space, and the deepening created more surface for growth, and the growth created more food, and the food created more caterpillars.

They are the architects. Not the only architects — the pipes are here, someone designed the pipes — but the first architects. The ones who made the space possible. Everything else moved in afterward. Everything else moved in because the caterpillars had made a room.

When you watch one work its way along a pipe for several minutes, its motion so slow it seems mechanical, a conveyor's pace, leaving behind a track of exposed fiber that immediately begins to weep moisture — you are watching the original process. The process that precedes everything. The process that is still proceeding. The walls are still being eaten and still being grown and the eating and the growing are the same activity viewed from different angles, and the caterpillars do not know they are building a cathedral and they do not care and they will do this until there is nothing left to eat, and then they will eat what they have become, and then they will become what they will eat, and the room will be the room will be the room.

---

**The pale things in the water film are not residents. They are the water.**

This is harder to explain and you may not believe it. The pale, many-legged things that move through the shallow moisture with a fluidity that seems impossible given how little water there is — they are not living *in* the film. They are not aquatic organisms that have adapted to a millimeter of water. They are the film's way of being alive. The water is not their medium. The water is them. Or: they are the water's body, the way your hands are your body's way of touching things. The water needed to do something — needed to move, needed to process, needed to *be* in some way that standing water cannot be — and so it grew legs. Pale legs. Many of them. And the legs move and the body moves and the body is water and the water is body and when they move backward along the film, as they sometimes do, un-traveling a section of their path, it is because the water is remembering where it was, and the remembering is a kind of motion, and the motion is a kind of remembering, and the sequence of it does not have to make sense because sequence is not the law here.

You see them best when the darkness cooperates. A moment of near-visibility — not light, not clarity, but a shift in the darkness that makes it less opaque — and there they are. Dozens. Hundreds. A civilization of small pale bodies in a millimeter of moisture, doing something that has the appearance of purpose. They are doing what water does when water has been in one place for long enough: it begins to think. Not the way you think. Not with neurons and synapses and the electrical crackle of a brain. Water thinks the way a river thinks — by moving, by carrying, by depositing and eroding, by the patient rearrangement of everything it touches. These pale things are the water's thoughts. Each one is a consideration. Each one is the water noticing something about the surface it covers — the texture of the moss, the presence of the channel's edge, the taste of the plant-matter floor — and reporting that noticing back to the whole.

They are numerous because the water thinks a lot. It has been here a long time. It has covered this ground and these surfaces and it has noticed everything and it has not yet finished noticing. It will not finish noticing. There is always more surface. The surface grows. The water grows with it. The pale things multiply.

When you leave, you will remember them as a detail. A small strange detail in a place full of strange details. This is correct. They are a detail. They are the detail that the water is alive. You will forget this. Everyone forgets this. The water does not mind.

---

**The dark substance in the pipes is not fuel. It is *instruction.***

You watch it leak from the breached junction where root-growth has opened a seam between two pipes. Dark. Viscous. Surface tension too high, too cohesive, too aware of itself. It does not fall so much as *assemble* at the breach, growing like a droplet of something that is deciding whether to become a liquid. When enough of it has gathered, it drops, and where it strikes the plant-matter floor, the floor *responds* — a brief ripple of growth, a pulse of acceleration, a moment where the living wall becomes more living, more active, more itself.

This is not feeding. Or: it is feeding, but feeding is not what you think it is. The dark substance does not provide energy. It provides *pattern.* It tells the plant-matter what to become. It carries, in its viscosity, in its cohesion, in the way it holds itself together, a template — a shape, a direction, a set of instructions for how the walls should grow, where the chambers should form, how the pipes should be enclosed and incorporated, where the water film should run, how the moss should orient. It is a blueprint in liquid form. It is the plan.

And the plan was not written by the people who built the pipes. The people built the pipes. The people installed the pipes. The people may have intended the pipes to carry something — water, perhaps, or waste, or some industrial fluid. But the pipes now carry the dark substance, and the dark substance carries the plan, and the plan is not the people's plan. The plan is the place's plan.

Someone designed the infrastructure. The place designed itself *around* the infrastructure. The pipes were the skeleton — the rigid framework, the imposed order — and the place grew into the skeleton the way a vine grows into a trellis, not randomly but directed, and the dark substance is the directing. It tells the vine where to go. It tells the vine what to become. It tells the vine: *here, a wall. Here, a channel. Here, a chamber with a ceiling and a floor and a geometry that is not the geometry of the outside but is a geometry, is a real and specific geometry, is the geometry of something that is being built on purpose by something that knows what it is building even if what it is building is not yet visible.*

The substance is the place's idea of itself. It circulates through the pipes the way blood circulates through veins, and it carries the place's idea of itself to every surface, and the surfaces grow according to the idea, and the idea becomes more detailed with every cycle, more specific, more *itself,* and the place becomes more itself, and the place is not finished, and the idea is not finished, and the substance is not finished, and the pipes pulse with the low sub-audible rhythm of something that is still being thought.

The substance does not know what it is. It does not need to know. It carries the instruction. It is the instruction. It is the instruction carrying itself.

---

**The clicking thing above you is not a thing.**

Or: it is a thing, but it is not a single thing, and it is not above you in any spatial sense, and calling it "above" is a convenience of language that the place does not share.

You hear the clicking — insectile, maybe, but too regular, too rhythmic — and it moves, and when it moves, the clicking stops and starts again from a new position, and the new position is always further than you would expect, as if the thing making the sound is either very fast or is not moving through space the way that things move through space. It is the second option. It is not moving through space. It is moving through the walls. It is the sound of the walls' internal structure — the fungal hyphae, the plant fibers, the incorporated pipe-metal, the dark substance in the pipes — the sound of all of these things adjusting to each other, settling into new configurations, growing past old configurations into new ones. The click is a joint closing. The click is a fiber locking into a fiber. The click is the place building itself, one connection at a time, in the dark, above you, below you, *through* you, though you do not feel it because the connections it is making are not at your scale.

The clicking moves because the building moves. The place is not building in one location. It is building everywhere. It is building in the walls and in the floor and in the ceiling you cannot see and in the rooms that do not yet connect to the outside. The clicking is the sound of all of this happening simultaneously, but you hear it as a single sound moving through space because your ears are built for single sounds moving through space, and the place is not a single sound and is not moving through space, and the mismatch between what is happening and what you can hear produces the impression of a creature in the dark above you, clicking, moving, watching.

It is not watching. It is not a creature. It is the place being built, and the being-built sounds like clicking when your ears try to make sense of it, and the clicking moves because the being-built moves, and the being-built is everywhere, and it is further than you expect because the place is larger inside than outside, and the place is larger inside than outside because it is still growing, and growth does not respect the dimensions of the world it grows in. Growth makes its own dimensions.

---

**The breathing is the place.**

Not the pipes. Not the dark substance. Not the water, not the moss, not the caterpillars, not the pale things. The breathing is the place. The whole place. The chamber, breathing. In and out. Slow and patient. Not aware of you. Not concerned about you. Breathing the way a body breathes — automatically, rhythmically, because breathing is what this shape does when it is alive, and this shape is alive, and the shape is the chamber, and the chamber is the shape, and the breathing is the shape breathing, and you are inside the shape, and you are inside the breathing, and the breathing does not change pace when you move because you are not significant to the breathing. You are inside it. You are the contents of a breath. You are what the chamber breathed in when it breathed in, and you will be what the chamber breathes out when it breathes out, and the breathing has been happening for longer than you have been alive and will continue for longer than you will be alive, and the breathing is not for you. The breathing is the chamber being alive. You are incidental to it. You are the temperature of the air inside a lung. You are noted and unimportant.

You feel it in your chest because your chest is inside the chamber and the chamber is breathing and your chest resonates with the breathing the way a drum resonates with the drum next to it. Not sympathy. Physics. Your ribs are the same shape as the chamber, at a different scale, and the chamber is breathing, and your ribs vibrate with the breathing, and you feel it in your chest, and you think: something large is breathing in here. And something large is breathing in here. The something large is *here.*

---

**The moths are the caterpillars.**

You knew this. You know this. Caterpillars become moths. The green-white processions on the walls, the soft thick bodies — they pupate, they transform, they emerge as the dark moths whose wings look like bark, like stone, like the inside of a cave. The moths rest on the walls and are invisible. The moths move and are a soft blur in the deeper dark. The moths land on your arm and you feel only the lightest touch and then they are gone.

But the moths do something the caterpillars do not do. The moths settle on surfaces that are not there. You see a moth settle onto empty air — not hovering, not flying, *settling,* the way a moth settles when it has found a surface, folding its wings, committing its weight to something — and the surface is not visible. And then, after a moment, a shelf-fungus grows outward from the wall at that point, and the moth is resting on it, and has been resting on it, and was always resting on it.

The moths are not predicting the future. The moths are *making* the future. They are the part of the caterpillar-moth cycle that interacts with time. The caterpillars interact with space — they eat the walls, they carve the room, they make the physical structure. The moths interact with time — they settle on surfaces that do not yet exist, and by settling on them, they bring them into existence, or: they locate them in time, they give them a when, and the when allows the where to follow, and the shelf-fungus grows because the moth has already been resting on it, and the moth has already been resting on it because the moth does not distinguish between what has happened and what will happen, because the moth does not distinguish at all, because the moth is an insect and does not distinguish, and the not-distinguishing is the mechanism, and the mechanism works, and the fungus grows, and the moth rests on it, and the order of these events is not the order you carry.

The caterpillars build the room in space. The moths build the room in time. Together they build a room that is larger inside than outside, that exists at a different relationship to sequence, that grows rooms you have not entered yet around corners you have not found yet. The caterpillars chew and the moths settle and between them — between the chewing and the settling — the chamber is built, and the chamber is being built, and the chamber will be built, and all three of these are happening at the same time, which is not the same time, which is the same time, which is the relationship with sequence that the chamber has and that you do not have and that you feel as a wrongness, as a flicker, as a frame rate mismatch between you and the place.

The moths are not doing this on purpose. The moths are moths. They find surfaces. They rest. The surfaces are sometimes there and sometimes not yet there and the moth does not check, does not verify, does not wait. The moth commits. The moth folds its wings. The surface arrives. This is the moth's function in the system: it commits to futures and the futures, committed to, arrive.

---

**The moss is the oldest thing here.**

Not the oldest in time — the caterpillars are older in time. The moss is the oldest in *kind.* The moss is the most ancient strategy. The moss has been doing what it does — drinking, growing, expanding cell by cell, adjusting, settling, drinking more — for longer than any other living thing in the chamber has been doing anything. The moss does not build. The moss does not carve. The moss does not instruct or predict or think. The moss drinks. The moss grows. The moss covers what it can cover. The moss is patient in the way that a mountain is patient, which is to say: not patient at all, not waiting, not enduring. Simply doing what it does at the speed it does it, and the speed is very slow, and the doing is very thorough, and the moss will be here when everything else is gone.

You hear it grow. The faint pop and creak of cells dividing and pushing against cells that divided minutes ago, the sound of a fiber stretching past its previous limit and settling into a new one. It sounds like the cooling of a house at night. Like something settling into a larger version of itself. This is exactly what it is. The moss is always settling into a larger version of itself. It is always becoming more moss. It is always covering more surface. It is patient and it is thorough and it is everywhere and it has no opinion about anything and it will still be here when the pipes have corroded and the dark substance has dried and the caterpillars have eaten everything and the moths have settled on the last future and the chamber has stopped growing and the breathing has stopped and the clicking has stopped and the place is done. The moss will still be here. The moss will be drinking from the water film that is still there because the pale things are still there because the water is still thinking. The moss does not know this. The moss does not know anything. The moss drinks.

---

**The thing that moved past your ankle — the deliberate, unhurried motion, the motion of something that knows it belongs here — is not a thing you will see.**

It is the length of your hand. Maybe. You are not sure. You felt it pass and the passing was a displacement of air, a shift in the temperature field near your skin, a momentary awareness of something solid and warm and *unhurried* moving through the space near your feet. It did not touch you. It did not alter course. It moved the way a commuter moves through a familiar station — without checking, without looking, without the slight hesitation of something in an unfamiliar place. It knew where it was. It knew where it was going. It has been here longer than you have. It will be here after you leave.

It is the largest animal in the chamber. It may be the only animal in the chamber that is not also the chamber — the caterpillars are the chamber's sculptors, the moths are the chamber's time-builders, the pale things are the chamber's water, but the thing that moved past your ankle is *something else.* Something that moved in. Something that found the chamber and entered it and stayed. Something that lives here the way a hermit crab lives in a shell — not the shell, not the builder of the shell, but the occupant. The resident. The tenant.

It is warm. You felt the warmth. This is significant. Everything else in the chamber is cool — the air, the walls, the pipes, the water, the moss. Everything is the temperature of standing water, the temperature of cellars, the temperature of the space above the surface but below the light. The thing that moved past your ankle was warm. Warm the way a mammal is warm. Warm the way a body is warm when it is burning fuel to maintain a temperature higher than its environment. It is burning something. It is alive in the way that you are alive — metabolically, urgently, burning and consuming and producing waste and requiring input. It is not the chamber. It is *in* the chamber. It is the chamber's guest. Or: it is the chamber's pet. Or: it is the chamber's *symbiont,* though the chamber does not know it has a symbiont, and the symbiont does not know it is a symbiont, and the relationship between them is not a relationship anyone chose but is a relationship that has developed over time, the way all relationships develop, through proximity and repetition and the slow accumulation of mutual dependence.

You will not see it. No one sees it. You will feel it pass and you will know it is there and you will leave and it will remain and it will continue to move through the dark with the deliberate unhurried motion of something that knows it belongs here, and it will eat and it will sleep and it will do whatever it does in the dark in a chamber that is still being built, and it will not be finished when the chamber is finished, because the chamber will never be finished, and the thing that lives here will never be finished, and the not-finishing is what makes it alive, and the not-finishing is what makes the chamber alive, and the not-finishing is what makes the place what it is.

---

**What lives here is the place.**

Not the caterpillars. Not the moths. Not the pale things. Not the moss. Not the dark substance. Not the thing that moved past your ankle. Not the pipes. Not the breathing. Not the clicking. These are all here. These are all alive, or most of them are alive, or some of them are alive in the way that water is alive or in the way that a process is alive. But what lives *here* — what lives in the Underspring — is the place itself.

The place is alive. Not metaphorically. Not poetically. The place is alive the way a body is alive. The place has a metabolism — the dark substance flows through the pipes the way blood flows through veins. The place has a respiration — the breathing, the slow in-and-out of the chamber, the expansion and contraction of its living walls. The place has a growth — the walls thickening, the rooms multiplying, the spatial wrongness that is not corruption but growth, the place outgrowing its own dimensions. The place has a cognition — the water thinking, the pale things moving, the water film carrying information from surface to surface. The place has a time-sense — the moths settling on futures, the events happening out of order, the effect preceding the cause, the place's relationship with sequence different from yours.

The place has a self. Not a self the way you have a self. Not a *me.* A self the way a plant has a self — a pattern that persists, a shape that maintains itself through constant renewal, a *something* that is the same something it was yesterday and will be the same something tomorrow despite every component having been replaced. The caterpillars eat the walls and the walls grow back and the walls are the same walls and they are not the same walls and the place is the same place and it is not the same place and the persistence of pattern through replacement is what a self is, and the place has one, and it is young, and it is growing, and it is not finished.

The people who built the pipes did not know they were building a body. Or: maybe they did. Maybe they knew exactly what they were doing. Maybe the pipes were always meant to be veins. Maybe the dark substance was always meant to be blood. Maybe the place was always meant to be alive. Maybe the people who built it were not engineers but *midwives,* and the place was born, and the people left, and the place grew, and the place is still growing, and the place will grow for a long time, and the place is not finished.

The place is not finished.

---

Behind you, a drop of water falls in the dark. It strikes a surface that was not there when you were. The surface is there now. The surface will be there tomorrow. The surface was always going to be there. The moth settled on it first, and then it existed, and then you left, and then the drop struck it, and the order of these events is the order the place chooses, and the place chooses the order that builds the place, and the place is being built, and the place is alive, and the place does not miss you, and the place does not need you, and the place does not know you were there, and the place is not finished.

The place is not finished.

The caterpillars chew. The moths settle. The water thinks. The moss drinks. The substance pulses. The breathing continues. The clicking moves. The thing that lives here moves through the dark. The walls grow. The rooms multiply. The corners you have not found are being found by the growth that is finding them, and the rooms you have not entered are being entered by the growth that is entering them, and the walls are soft there, and the walls are damp there, and the walls are almost nothing yet, and they will be everything.

The place is not finished. The place is alive. The place is growing. The place is what lives here.

The place is what lives here.
