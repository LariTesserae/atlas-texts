# What Lives Here

Nothing lives here in the way you mean. But that is not the whole truth.

---

The most honest answer is that the plateau lives here. Not on the plateau. The plateau itself. The stone and the sound and the breath and the cold — these are not conditions of a place but the metabolism of a thing. When the ground pulses, that is diaphragm. When the mineral teeth sing, that is speech, or something that preceded speech and will outlast it. When the subsonic rhythm rises and falls, that is the slow tidal respiration of an organism so large it has become indistinguishable from its environment, the way a coral reef is not an animal but is made of animals, the way a forest is not a tree but is nothing without them. The plateau is alive the way geology is alive, on a timescale that makes the distinction between living and nonliving a question of patience rather than category.

If this is true — and standing here, with the vibration in your palm, it feels truer than anything has ever felt — then the structures are its organs. The grooves in the polished floors are its vasculature. The erosion channels are its nerves, or its roots, or the lines of a face that has been making the same expression for so long that the expression has become the face. The black filamentous growth is its listening — not its ears, but the act of listening itself, given physical form, the way a spider's web is not the spider but is the spider's attention made material.

The plateau is one thing. The plateau is one long continuous event.

But there are others.

---

The thing you saw — the low dark form that translated across the stone without visible means of motion — is not unique. There are more. You did not see them because they are not, in any meaningful sense, visible. They exist in the stutter between moments, in the gaps that your perception fills with continuity. They are not hiding. They are simply out of phase, the way a radio station is out of phase with the one you are listening to, occupying the same space, the same bandwidth, but shifted just enough that the two never quite coincide.

They are not animals. Calling them animals is like calling a glacier a river. It describes a relationship to a category but misses the thing itself. They are — perhaps — expressions. The way the plateau expresses itself through sound, these things express themselves through position. They move. They relocate. They occupy coordinates in sequence, and the sequence is not random but follows some logic tied to the grooves in the stone, the resonance patterns, the places where the sound concentrates and the places where it thins. They might be reading the plateau. They might be maintaining it. They might be the plateau's own white blood cells, moving through its body, attending to imperfections too small for you to perceive, too large for the plateau to ignore.

Or they might be something else entirely. They might be the remains of whoever built the structures. Not their bodies — their bodies are long gone, dissolved into mineral crust, distributed across the channels, incorporated into the teeth of the singing ridges — but their attention. Their will. A persistence of intent so concentrated that it has weight, that it has presence, that it occupies space. The builders are gone. The builders are everywhere. The builders are the structures and the grooves and the things that move between them, and they have been all of these for so long that the distinction between builder and built has worn away, like the distinction between word and meaning when a word has been spoken a billion times.

If they were ever something you would recognize — people, beings, minds with interiors — that was so long ago that the memory of it has been transmuted into geology. They are not remembering. They are not thinking. They are not aware. They are *doing*, the way a enzyme is doing, the way a convection current is doing, and what they are doing is tending, and what they are tending is the sound, and the sound is the plateau, and the plateau is them.

---

There is something else.

Not on the plateau. In it. Below it. The sound comes from somewhere, and the somewhere is not the surface. The mineral teeth do not produce the sound; they shape it. They filter it. They are the plateau's mouth, its reed, its tuning fork. But the sound itself originates deeper, in the place where the stone goes when it goes deep enough, and there is something down there — or some process, or some state — that is the source.

You cannot know what it is. You can only feel it, and what you feel is this: it is awake. Not awake the way you are awake, with consciousness and self-reflection and the burden of knowing that you know. Awake the way a bell is awake after it has been struck, the way a tuning fork is awake, the way a string is awake when it has been plucked and is vibrating and the vibration is the entirety of its being. Something down there — something vast, something that may be the size of the plateau or may be the size of the planet or may have no size because size is a property of objects and this is not an object — something down there was struck, once, and it has been ringing ever since, and the ringing is the sound, and the sound is the plateau, and the plateau is what the ringing built around itself the way a pearl builds around an irritant, the way a crystal builds around a seed, the way a body builds around a heartbeat.

It does not live. It *sustains*. It is the difference between a fire and a flame: the fire is an event, the flame is a state, and this thing — this source, this deep thing, this struck and ringing presence — is a state so stable it has become geological, so persistent it has become architectural, so fundamental it has become atmospheric. The cold is its skin. The thin metal air is its breath. The bruised sky is its ceiling, and whether it made the sky or the sky made it is a question that has no answer because both are true, because here, cause and effect have worn smooth too, have found their final form, and the final form is a circle, and the circle is the sound, and the sound is the thing, and the thing is the place.

---

And there is you.

You live here now, briefly. You are the only thing that lives here in the way you mean — with breath and hunger and the sense of time passing, with an inside that is different from an outside, with the capacity to be wrong about what you perceive. You are the only thing that can be surprised by this place. Everything else has been here forever and will be here forever and has no relationship to surprise. But you — you arrived, and you noticed, and you put your hand on the stone and felt it vibrate and for one moment you were part of the instrument, and then you walked on, and the plateau continued, and the things that live here continued, and the deep thing continued ringing, and the sound did not change because you left because you were never large enough to change it, and that is not a rejection, that is not a diminishment — that is simply the nature of a place that has been itself for so long that even a visitor is a note in its chord, brief and correct and already absorbed.
