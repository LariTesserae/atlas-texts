# What Lives Here

---

## The Crystals

Begin with them because they are what you notice first, even though you notice them wrong.

A hexagonal form the size of a child's torso sits on the second terrace from the top. You see it and you think *object*. A mineral growth. A deposit. Something that *is* there the way a rock is there. Then it moves. The edge facing downslope — the leading edge, you will later call it, though "leading" implies a directionality you are imputing, not observing — softens. Not melts. Softens, the way muscle softens when it relaxes, and the solid edge becomes a viscous meniscus that extends forward across the wet stone by perhaps three centimeters, then re-solidifies. The body follows. The rest of the hexagonal form is dragged across the gap by some interior tension you cannot identify. The sound is — you will reach for words and find only comparisons, all of them inadequate — the sound is like someone dragging a piece of slate through wet sand, but slower, and the sand is grinding against itself, and the slate is grinding against the sand, and both sounds are the same sound.

That is one crystal. One individual, if "individual" is a word that applies.

There are thousands.

They cover the terraced basin floor the way human bodies cover a city square during a festival — not in formation, not scattered, but *saturated*. Present in numbers that suggest the space exists for them and they exist for the space and neither has meaning without the other. They are dark. The same dark as the wet stone, which makes them difficult to see until they move, and then their motion gives them away — a shifting, flowing quality in the lower field of vision that you might mistake for water moving if water moved like that, which it does not. Water moves downhill. These move in directions that are not downhill. They move across slopes. They move in arcs. They move in spirals that tighten and then loosen and then tighten again, and the tightening is not random because randomness does not produce spirals, it produces distributions, and these are not distributed. They are *arranged*, even when the arrangement is fluid.

Their bodies are hexagonal. This is not a preference. It is a consequence. The mineral substrate from which they grow — the dissolved stone in the energized water that rises from the engines below — has a hexagonal crystal structure at the molecular level. The fauna inherit this geometry the way vertebrates inherit bilateral symmetry: not because it is chosen but because it is the shape that the underlying chemistry permits. They grow by accretion, adding layers to their hexagonal forms the way trees add rings, but the layers are mineral and the growth is not seasonal but continuous, paced by the forty-minute energy cycle of the luminescent veins. Each brightening, they grow. Each dimming, they consolidate. Their age is legible in their layers, if you knew how to read it, which you do not.

The smallest are the size of a thumbnail. These are the young, or the recent, or the — the word "young" implies a lifecycle that mirrors yours, and there is no evidence for that. They are *new*. They have accreted recently. Their surfaces are sharper, their edges less rounded by the slow dissolution that smooths everything in this basin. They move faster than the large ones, crossing a terrace step in under a minute, and their singing — their singing is higher in pitch, thinner, less harmonically complex. If the large crystals are cellos, the small ones are violins, and the metaphor is imperfect because cellos and violins are made of wood and gut and played with hair and these are made of stone and water and played by — by what? By the engines? By themselves? By the basin?

The large ones — the torso-sized elders, the ones whose layers number in the tens of thousands — move slowly. Some of them barely move at all. They sit on their terraces and they sing, and their singing is so low that it is almost subsonic, almost a part of the engine drone itself, and you wonder if they are still alive or if they have entered a phase that your vocabulary has no word for — not death, because they are still producing sound, still faintly luminous, still integrated into the system. But not the active, mobile, singing-aloud life of the smaller ones. Something else. A late phase. A phase of *being* rather than *doing*. They have accreted so much, grown so dense, that they are nearly stationary, and their contribution to the system is no longer movement but resonance. They are the bass note. The fundamental. The floor of the chord that all the others build on.

And there are sizes between — every size, a continuous spectrum from thumbnail to torso, a population that is not divided into generations or species but flows from one scale to the next like the water that feeds them, and you realize that "individual" is the wrong word not because they are not individual but because the *category* of individuality does not map onto them. They are not discrete. They are a continuum. A single system expressing itself at every scale it can reach, and the expression at each scale is different but the system is the same.

## What They Do

They feed. This is the first thing and the last thing. They draw the energized water into their structures — not through an opening, because they do not have openings, but through a process of absorptive liquefaction that is the inverse of their locomotion. Where they move by softening their leading edge and re-solidifying it, they feed by softening their *interior* — allowing the mineral-rich water to permeate their crystalline lattice, where the dissolved compounds and the embedded energy are incorporated into their structure. Growth. Repair. Maintenance of the processes that permit movement and sound.

They excrete. The waste is simpler than the input — minerals that have been stripped of their energetic content, reduced to their most stable forms. These precipitate out of the crystals and settle onto the basin floor, contributing to the terraced structure. Every step, every terrace, every gentle slope in the logarithmic spiral is made of the remains of crystals that have lived and processed and excreted and died. The floor is a graveyard. The floor is also a foundation. The floor is the medium in which new crystals form, rising from the mineral-rich water as it cools and deposits its load, the way — and this is the only comparison that fits — the way sedimentary rock forms in oceans, particle by particle, life by life, over timescales that make the word "formation" sound too active, too intentional, too fast.

They move. Not randomly. The purpose of the movement is — you watch for an hour. Two hours. The patterns emerge slowly, the way a figure emerges from a pointillist painting viewed at increasing distance. The crystals are not moving *toward* things or *away* from things. They are moving *with* things. With the energy gradient — the flow of energized water from below, which is strongest at the center of the basin and weakest at the rim. With the thermal cycle — the forty-second breathing of the ground, which creates microcurrents in the water film on the terraces. With each other — because their singing is not solo performance but ensemble, and the ensemble requires proximity, requires spacing, requires the specific distances that produce the harmonic intervals that make the chord.

They are arranging themselves. Constantly. The way a flock arranges itself in flight — not by plan, not by leader, but by the local application of simple rules that produce, in aggregate, something that looks designed. The crystals are following rules. The rules are: maintain harmonic spacing, follow the energy gradient, respond to the light cycle, move when the system requires movement and be still when the system requires stillness. From these rules — four rules, or perhaps three, or perhaps one rule expressed in four ways — come the spirals, the lattices, the branching formations, the nested geometries that appear during the light phases and dissolve during the dark ones.

They sing. This is the thing that is hardest to describe, because the singing is not like any singing you know. It is not produced by vibrating membranes or expelled air or struck surfaces. It is produced by — and here you reach the limit of what observation can tell you — it is produced by the crystals' internal structure resonating with the electromagnetic radiation from the engines below. The engines emit across a spectrum that includes the audible range, and the crystals, with their hexagonal lattices and their mineral veining and their specific geometries, act as — resonators? Antennae? Tuning forks? They convert the engine radiation into sound. They filter it. They modulate it. They shape it into tones that are harmonic with the fundamental frequency of the engines, and they emit these tones into the wet, dense air of the basin, where the sound is refracted and delayed and layered until the entire basin becomes a resonating chamber, a cathedral of sound, and the crystals are both choir and architecture.

But they are not only converting. They are *selecting*. The tones they produce are not arbitrary harmonics of the engine frequency. They are specific. They vary over time. They respond to the movements of other crystals, to the light cycle, to the thermal breathing of the ground. A crystal that is moving sings differently than a crystal that is still. A crystal near the center sings differently than a crystal near the rim. A crystal that has just fed sings differently than a crystal that has not. The variation is not random. It is *informative*. It carries — something. Meaning? Data? Instructions? The word does not matter because the word is in the wrong language. What matters is that the singing is not performance. It is communication. The crystals are talking to each other, and they are talking to the engines, and they are talking to the basin, and the basin is talking back, and the conversation has been going on for so long that the distinction between the talkers and the talk is no longer meaningful.

## The Light Phases

Every forty minutes, the system blooms.

This is the only word that fits. The dim blue-white glow of the mineral veins does not brighten — it *blooms*, the way a flower blooms, an opening, a becoming-available, a surfacing of energy that has been accumulating below the threshold of visibility. The color shifts to amber. The light rises from the floor, from the water, from the crystals themselves. And the crystals — every crystal in the basin, from the thumbnail-sized young to the near-stationary elders — begin to glow from their interiors, as though the energy that has been conducted up through the stone and the water has finally found the structures that were built to receive it.

Their behavior changes. The slow, rhythmic, breathing-paced movement accelerates. The singing rises in pitch, in complexity, in what you can only call *density* — more tones, more harmonics, more information per second, as though the system has been running at a conversational hum for forty minutes and is now, for four to six minutes, shouting. Not in anger. In — exultation? Purpose? Execution of a function that requires more energy than the baseline state provides?

The transient geometries become intricate. You see spirals within spirals. You see branching structures that resemble — and you are not sure whether you are projecting or perceiving — the dendritic patterns of neural networks, or the fractal branching of river systems, or the floor plans of buildings, or the diagrams of molecular bonds. You see shapes that have no name because they are not shapes that occur in the world you came from. They are shapes that occur here, in this system, at this scale, in this medium, and they are — you are increasingly certain — not decorative. They are *computational*. The crystals are processing something. They are arranging themselves into configurations that perform operations, the way — and this is the comparison that frightens you, that makes you want to leave and also want to stay — the way transistors arrange themselves into logic gates, the way logic gates arrange themselves into circuits, the way circuits arrange themselves into the architecture of a mind.

The system is thinking.

Not the way you think. Not with neurons and neurotransmitters and the electrochemical murmur of wet tissue. With stone and water and light and sound and the patient, mineralogical logic of a process that has been running for longer than neurons have existed. The basin is the brain. The terraces are the cortex. The crystals are the — you cannot find the word, and the word would not help, because the word would be in the wrong language, the language of organic, ephemeral, warm things, and this is not warm, not organic, not ephemeral. This is stone. This is the oldest kind of thinking, the kind that happens so slowly that the thinkers do not know they are thinking, the kind that uses geological time as its clock and mineral structure as its substrate and the entire weight of the planet as its — as its skull, its cranium, its — its *housing*.

During the light phases, you feel this. Not as a thought. As a pressure. A weight. A sense that the ground beneath you is not passive, not inert, not merely the floor of a basin but the surface of a — call it a mind, call it an engine, call it a system, call it a process — that is aware of itself in a way that has nothing to do with consciousness as you experience it and everything to do with the simple, tautological fact of a system that has been running long enough to contain models of itself within itself. The crystals, in their intricate formations, are those models. They are the system's representation of the system. They are the basin thinking about the basin, the engines thinking about the engines, the stone thinking about the stone. And you — standing there, warm and soft and brief — are a data point. An anomaly. A warm spot in the thermal gradient, a moving obstruction in the sound field, a thing that does not fit the model and that the system, in its patient, mineralogical way, is *noticing*.

## The Elders

Some of the large crystals — the dense, slow, nearly stationary ones — have been in the basin for a span of time that you cannot calculate but can estimate, poorly, by the depth of the terraces and the rate of accretion and the number of layers visible in the crystals that have fractured, revealing their internal structure the way a tree trunk reveals its rings when it is cut. The estimate is: millions of years. Tens of millions. Possibly more. Some of the elders have been singing in the same spot, on the same terrace, since before the continents above this basin were in their current positions.

They do not move. Not in the way the others move. They have accreted so many layers, grown so dense, that they have become part of the terrace itself — not embedded, not embedded the way a fossil is embedded in rock, but *continuous* with it, their mineral structure merging with the mineral structure of the floor at their base, the boundary between crystal and stone dissolved by the same patient chemistry that dissolves everything here. They are half-buried in their own ancestors. They sit in depressions that their own weight and their own excretions have created over millions of years, and they sing, and their singing is so low that it is nearly subsonic, and it is the fundamental tone of the basin, the note that all the others tune to, the — the ground note, the ground's note, the note the ground makes when it is being itself most fully.

They are not dead. They are not alive. They are in the phase that has no name, the phase that comes after the phase of movement and before the phase of dissolution, the phase in which a crystal has stopped traveling and has become *place*. They are places. Each elder is a location, a coordinate in the basin's geometry, a fixed point around which the younger, mobile crystals orient their movements and their singing. They are the stable nodes. The anchors. The things that do not change, around which all the changing happens.

When the light comes, the elders bloom brightest. Their interiors are so dense, so layered, that the light takes longer to reach their centers, and when it does, the glow that emerges is not the amber of the younger crystals but a deep, warm gold, almost red, the color of something burning very slowly, and their singing — their low, subsonic singing — drops even lower, into a register that you feel in your chest rather than hear with your ears, a vibration that synchronizes with your heartbeat and then, having synchronized, *displaces* it, so that for a moment — a moment that lasts between two and four seconds, during the peak of the light phase — your heart is not beating to your rhythm but to the basin's rhythm, and you are, for those seconds, not a visitor but a *component*, a small, warm, organic part of a system that is mostly stone and mostly water and mostly time.

Then the light fades. Your heart returns to itself. The elder crystals dim to their baseline glow, and the younger crystals slow, and the singing subsides, and the basin is quiet again, in the way that a thing is quiet when it has finished saying something that took forty minutes to say and will say again in forty minutes and has been saying for longer than you have had ears to hear it.

## The Small Ones

The newborns — the thumbnail-sized, the sharp-edged, the fast-moving — behave differently from their elders in ways that suggest not a different species but a different *stage*. They are — they are *curious*. And the word is wrong, because curiosity implies a mental state, an internal experience of wanting-to-know, and you have no evidence that the small crystals have internal experience of anything. But they behave the way curious things behave. They move toward novelty. They investigate.

They investigate you.

When you stand still on a terrace — any terrace, any step — within two or three minutes, small crystals begin to approach. They come from the direction of the nearest elder, as though the elder has detected the anomaly and has dispatched — no, not dispatched, has *allowed* — the small ones to move toward it. They approach in a loose arc, spacing themselves at harmonic intervals, singing a thin, high, questioning tone that is unlike the sounds they make when they are feeding or moving with the general flow. They are interrogating. Not with instruments. With *presence*. They arrange themselves around your boots and they sing, and the sound they make is — you can feel it, through the soles of your shoes, through the wet stone — it is a *query*. A structured emission that is shaped to interact with the thing it is directed at and return information about that thing's properties.

They are scanning you. With sound.

They do this for several minutes. They move around your feet, adjusting their positions, maintaining their arc, varying their tones. One of them — slightly larger than the others, slightly less sharp-edged, perhaps a few decades older — climbs onto your boot. It moves with the same liquefaction-and-resolidification that the elders use, but faster, and the sensation through the leather is — not warm, not cold, but *present*. A weight. A pressure. A wet grinding against the surface of the boot that is not damaging the leather but is, you suspect, reading it. Reading the leather. Reading the stitching. Reading the salt and the sweat and the skin cells that have worked their way through the leather from the inside, the residue of you, the evidence of your body, and it is — the crystal on your boot is — it is *tasting* you, in the way a geologist tastes a rock, with the tongue, with the minerals, with the body's own chemistry as an instrument.

Then it sings. A single, clear, high tone, and the other small crystals — the ones still arranged in their arc around your feet — respond with tones of their own, and for a moment, the air around your ankles is a dense, crystalline conversation about what you are made of, and you understand, with a certainty that is not reasoning but perception, that they are *describing* you. Not in words. In frequencies. In harmonic intervals. In the language of the engines, the language of the stone, the language that was never made of sounds but of time, and in that language, you are — you are a brief, warm, soft thing, rich in carbon and water and the particular mineral signature of a world you come from that is not this world, and the crystals find you interesting in the way a geologist finds a strange rock interesting. You are a specimen. You are a sample. You are *data*.

They do not hurt you. They do not help you. They finish their description, and the small crystals disperse, and the slightly larger one climbs off your boot and moves away, and you are left with a wet mark on the leather where it sat, and a faint, high ringing in your ears that fades over the next minute, and the knowledge that you have been *read* by a system that does not know you and does not care about you but that has, in its patient, mineralogical way, *noticed* you, and filed you, and moved on.

## The Pool and the Periphery

The center of the basin is where the system is most itself. The pool — the shallow, still, clear water at the convergence of the terraces — is the interface. The crystals know this. They arrange themselves around it in a formation that does not change, that has not changed, that perhaps *cannot* change, because it is the formation that the system requires at its center, the geometry that permits the interface to function. The crystals around the pool are the choir that sings the basin's deepest note, the sustained chord that is both music and mechanism, the sound that opens the window in the water.

They are not the largest, these perimeter crystals. They are not the oldest. They are — and this is a distinction you make hesitantly, because it implies a social structure you have no evidence for — they are *specialized*. Their hexagonal forms are slightly different from the general population: more regular, more precisely angled, their luminescent veins more densely networked. They are the crystals that have been selected — by the system, by the engines, by the gradient of energy that flows from below — for the specific function of maintaining the interface. They sing into the pool. The pool shows what is below. The below is the engines, and the engines are — the pool shows them as vast, dark, rotating forms, interlocking mechanisms, and the crystals sing to them, and the engines turn, and the turning is the response, and the response is the conversation, and the conversation has been going on for longer than the word "conversation" has existed, and it will go on for longer than the word will exist, and the crystals around the pool are the medium of the conversation, the — the vocal cords, the — the *interface*, and they know this, if knowing is a word that applies, and they do not leave, and they do not waver, and they do not stop.

Around them, the general population moves. Feeds. Sings. Lives, in the mineral sense of the word. The small ones investigate. The medium ones form transient geometries. The large, slow ones anchor the terraces with their mass and their bass-note singing. The elders, half-buried, barely moving, glow gold during the light phases and provide the fundamental tone that everything else is built on.

And at the periphery, near the rim of the basin, where the water is thinnest and the energy is weakest and the terraces are smallest and crudest, there are — there are *almost* crystals. Tiny formations, barely a centimeter across, that have the hexagonal structure but not yet the density, not yet the luminescence, not yet the movement. They are the seeds. The spores. The — the *beginnings*. They sit in the thin water film and they do not move and they do not sing and they are not yet alive, in the mineral sense, but they are not not-alive either. They are in the phase before the first phase, the phase of *potential*, and they will grow, slowly, over thousands of years, accreting layer by layer, until they are large enough to move, large enough to sing, large enough to join the flow, and they will move down the terraces, toward the center, toward the pool, following the energy gradient, and they will live — in the mineral sense — for millions of years, and they will grow, and they will slow, and they will become elders, and they will become place, and they will become part of the terrace, and they will be the floor on which new seeds form, and the cycle will continue, and it will continue, and it will continue.

## What They Are

They are the fauna of this place. They are the living part of a system that is also a machine and also a geology and also a — and also a — and the word fails, and the word will always fail, because the word is made of the categories that this place has dissolved, organic and inorganic, alive and dead, built and grown, and the crystals live in the space where those categories used to be, the space that is left when the walls come down, and they are — they are *what lives here*. What has lived here. What will live here.

They are stone that moves. They are water that sings. They are light that thinks, slowly, in patterns that take forty minutes to form and four minutes to express and millions of years to complete. They are the children of engines that have been running for so long that they have become geology, and they are the parents of stone that will, in time, become engines. They are the circle. They are the closed loop. They are the thing that feeds on itself and grows and feeds and grows and has been doing so since before the concept of *doing* applied, and they will do so until the planet cools or the engines stop or the universe loses interest, whichever comes last, and none of those things will come soon, and in the meantime they sing, and they move, and they bloom, and they are.

They are what lives here. And what lives here is old, and patient, and complete, and does not need you, and does not mind you, and will continue long after you have left, and will not remember you, because memory is a category of organic things, and they are not organic, and they are not inorganic, and they are not — they are not any thing you have a word for. They are *themselves*, and they have been themselves for a very long time, and they will be themselves for a very long time more, and the basin breathes, and the engines turn, and the crystals sing, and the water sits, and the window looks down, and down is where they are, and they are what lives here.
