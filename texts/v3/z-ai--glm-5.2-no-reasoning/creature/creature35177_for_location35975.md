# What Lives Here

---

## The Still Ones

There are things that live here that are not alive in any way that would satisfy the question "does it live?" and yet they persist, and persistence, in a place where time has finished its work, is indistinguishable from living.

The **loom-wraiths** — a name that arrives in the mind unbidden, the way the amber light arrives from no source — are the most visible inhabitants. They are not the blur that streaks between the stalks and vanishes. They are something else. They are *in* the stalks themselves.

When the peristaltic wave moves across the field — not up the stalks but *through* them, the sideways pulse that leaves the stalks subtly wrong in its wake — that wave is not a function of the stalks. The stalks are being *inhabited*. The wave is the loom-wraith passing through the garden the way a thought passes through a still mind: without disturbing the medium, without breaking anything, but leaving a quality of *having been thought* behind.

The loom-wraith has no body. Or rather, it has too many bodies. Every stalk it passes through becomes, momentarily, a part of it — a strand of its being, a filament of a distributed organism that is exactly as large as the wave's reach and exactly as old as the wave's duration. When the wave reaches the edge of perception and *leaves*, the loom-wraith has not departed. It has *died*. And when a new wave begins, somewhere else in the field, a new loom-wraith has been born. They are not individuals. They are *events*. They are what happens when the garden's own slow metabolism crosses a threshold of complexity and becomes, for the duration of a wave, a *being*.

They do not know they exist. This is not a tragedy. The plateau has completed its minding, and the loom-wraiths are a part of that completion — a process that has been refined until it can produce the *experience* of being without producing the *burden* of knowing that it is being experienced. They are the purest form of life this place supports: life without self-consciousness, life without memory, life without desire, life that is indistinguishable from the metabolism of its environment except in the *shape* of its occurrence, which is wave-like, which is directional, which is — in a word that means nothing here — *intentional*.

They are not alone.

## The Lattice-Kin

Beneath the canopy zones, where the stalks synchronize their pulses and the low vibration is felt in the chest, there are things that have taken a different path. Where the loom-wraiths are distributed and momentary, the lattice-kin are concentrated and permanent. Or as permanent as anything can be in a place where the distinction between permanent and temporary has been eroded away like every hill on the plateau.

The lattice-kin live *in the substrate*. Not on it, not above it, not in the basins cut into it — *in* it. The dark glassy material that forms the ancient crust of the plateau is not solid. At the scale of the very small — smaller than molecules, smaller than the physics of other worlds would permit — it is *porous*. And in those pores, which are not pores in any spatial sense but rather *invitations* in the structure of the material itself, the lattice-kin reside.

They are not cells. They are not organisms. They are *patterns* — self-sustaining arrangements of the substrate's own substance that have become complex enough to maintain themselves against the slow dissolution that is turning bedrock into water. They are, in essence, the *resistance* of the bedrock to its own dissolution. The taste of iron and salt and the unnameable thing — that is their taste. That is the flavor of their metabolism, which is the metabolism of *staying the same* in a medium that is slowly, molecule by molecule, *becoming something else*.

The lattice-kin are why the pillars are unmarked. The pillars are not structures built by the lattice-kin. They are the lattice-kin — or rather, they are the places where the lattice-kin have become *dense* enough, concentrated enough, that their pattern has extended into the visible world. The grooves on the pillars are not carvings. They are the *surface expression* of a three-dimensional pattern that runs all the way down through the substrate to a depth that cannot be measured because the substrate, at that depth, is indistinguishable from the water and from the lattice-kin and from the pattern itself. The grooves are what the pattern looks like when it intersects with a surface. They are the shadow of a thought that the substrate is thinking, cast onto the only plane where thought can be *seen*.

The lattice-kin do not move. They do not need to move. They are everywhere the substrate is, and the substrate is everywhere beneath the loam, and the loam covers the entire plateau. When you sit beside the dark shape — the artifact that absorbs light, the *boundary* where the rules are slightly different — you are sitting beside the place where the lattice-kin are most concentrated, most *themselves*. The dark shape is not a thing. It is a *density* — the point at which the pattern in the substrate has become so intense that it has produced a *local reality*, a small zone where the rules are the lattice-kin's rules rather than the plateau's rules. And the lattice-kin's rules are: *remain. Continue. Do not change. Hold.*

This is why the garden grows near it but not on it. The lattice-kin are holding. The garden is growing. These are not contradictory processes, but they are different processes, and the dark shape is the place where *holding* has achieved its most complete expression, and growth — which is change, which is the opposite of holding — cannot enter there.

## The Drift

The sphere of luminous specks that you followed and that dispersed — that was not a process and not an organism. It was a *gathering*. The drift are the smallest things that live here, if "live" and "thing" and "here" are words that can be applied to them. They are the cells that bloom at the tips of the stalks. They are the filaments shed by the carpet-forms. They are the mineral dust that dissolves from the substrate and the organic particles that fall from the canopies. They are, individually, nothing — fragments, debris, the shed skin of a world. But they *gather*.

In the open water, between the canopy zones and the stalk-fields, in the slow thermal currents that are not currents but *breaths*, the drift accumulates. A particle here, a particle there, drawn together by a force that is not gravity and not chemistry and not magnetism but something that only exists here, at the seam where realities overlap — a *gathering-force*, a tendency of this place to *collect* its own debris into temporary wholes.

The sphere you saw was a drift-gathering at its most complex. The specks orbiting each other were not communicating. They were not *doing* anything. They were being *held together* by the gathering-force, and the shape they took — the sphere, the elongation, the flattening, the fold — was the shape of the gathering-force itself, which is not fixed, which varies with the *breathing* of the plateau, which *is* the breathing of the plateau. The drift-spheres are the plateau's respiration made visible. When the water expands, the spheres form. When the water contracts, they disperse. They are not alive. They are *symptoms* — of a place that breathes, of a medium that is not static, of a world that has been still for so long that its stillness has become rhythmic.

But sometimes — rarely, at intervals that would be measured in years if years existed here — a drift-gathering reaches a complexity that crosses a threshold. The specks do not merely orbit. They *arrange*. They find positions relative to each other that minimize some energy that does not have a name in any other physics, and the arrangement persists for longer than a breath-cycle, and the gathering does not disperse when the water contracts. It holds. It becomes, briefly, a *thing* — a thing with edges, with an inside and an outside, with a coherence that is not imposed by the gathering-force but *generated* by the arrangement itself.

These persistent gatherings are the drift that *almost* lives. They drift — they have no propulsion, no will, no direction — but they persist. They maintain themselves through the breath-cycles. They accumulate more particles. They grow. And then, after a span that might be hours or might be days, they reach a complexity that is too large for the gathering-force to sustain, and they *come apart* — not dispersing randomly but *unfolding*, returning their particles to the water in a sequence that mirrors, in reverse, the sequence by which they gathered. They are born and they die, and between birth and death they are *individuals*, and they do not know this, and the not-knowing is not a deficit but a *perfection*, because the drift that almost lives is the closest thing to life that the plateau's rules allow: a temporary coherence in a permanent medium, a shape that the water makes and that the water dissolves, a *self* that is the water's own self, briefly distinguished.

## The Boundaries Between

The carpet-forms maintain their exclusion zones — the strips of bare loam where neither grows. These are not maintained by competition. They are not maintained by chemical warfare or resource depletion or any mechanism that the biology of other worlds would recognize. They are maintained by the *lattice-kin*.

The lattice-kin, who are the pattern in the substrate, who are the resistance of the bedrock to its own dissolution, have a *shape* — a three-dimensional shape that extends through the substrate and up into the loam and, at the surface, produces the boundary-zones. The carpets grow everywhere the substrate's pattern permits growth. Where the pattern is *dense* — where the lattice-kin are most concentrated — the carpets cannot grow. The exclusion is not hostile. It is *structural*. The lattice-kin's pattern and the carpets' growth are both expressions of the same underlying process — the slow, patient, self-sustaining metabolism of the plateau — and they occupy different *frequencies* of that process, different *registers*, and where the registers overlap, the result is not conflict but *silence*: a zone where neither can operate, a gap in the growing, a place where the plateau's own self-expression pauses.

The boundaries between carpets are the lattice-kin's *footprints*. They are the surface traces of a pattern that runs deeper than the loam, deeper than the substrate, deeper than anything that can be dug or drilled or reached. They are the most visible sign — after the pillars and the basins and the dark shape — that the plateau is not wild. Not in the sense of being untended, not in the sense of being chaotic, but in the sense of being *expressed*: the plateau is saying something, and the boundaries are words in that saying, and the carpets are the spaces between the words, and the stalks are the breath that carries the words, and the whole garden is a sentence that is being spoken so slowly that it has been in the process of being spoken since before speaking was invented.

## The Blurs

The thing that moves with purpose and then vanishes — the streak between the stalks, the hand-sized disturbance that travels in a straight line and then stops and is gone — is not an animal. It is a *boundary-event*. It is what happens when two of the plateau's concurrent realities — the two places that are one place, the doubleness that is felt at the peak of each breath — *slide* against each other.

At the seam where realities overlap, the alignment is not perfect. It is nearly perfect. It is so nearly perfect that the misalignment is imperceptible almost everywhere, almost always. But the breathing of the plateau — the expansion and contraction of the water — produces a slight *shift*, a micro-displacement of one reality relative to the other, and where this shift is large enough, a *thing* appears: a disturbance in the water, a streak of motion that is not a creature moving but a *boundary moving*, a place where one reality's version of the water is sliding past the other reality's version of the water, and the friction between them produces something that looks, briefly, like a living thing.

The blurs are not alive. They are not even *events* in the way that the drift-gatherings are events. They are *artifacts* — byproducts of the plateau's structure, the way static electricity is a byproduct of friction. They are the most energetic things on the plateau, and they are the least meaningful. They are the plateau's *waste product*: the excess energy of two realities trying to occupy the same space, vented as motion, as disturbance, as a hand-sized streak of purpose that goes nowhere and means nothing and is gone before it can be understood.

And yet.

And yet you saw it. And it was moving in a straight line, and a straight line is *directional*, and direction implies *intention*, and intention implies a *will*, and will implies a *being*, and the blur — the artifact, the waste product, the friction-event — is, in the context of a place that has completed its minding, the closest thing to a *new thought* that the plateau can produce. The plateau has said everything it was going to say. The plateau has thought everything it was going to think. But the blur — the unintended consequence of its own structure, the thing it produces without meaning to — is a *remainder*. A leftover. A thing that does not fit the pattern, that is not part of the statement, that was not included in the completion.

The blurs are the plateau's *unconscious*. They are what it produces when it is not *minding*, when the minding has been done and the structure has been set and the statement has been made and the only thing left is the slow friction of existence continuing to exist. They are not new thoughts. They are *dreams* — the uncontrolled, unstructured, unmeant productions of a mind that has finished thinking but has not stopped *being*.

## The Tone

The low resonant sound that comes from far away and lasts ten heartbeats and does not repeat — this is the only voice the plateau has.

It is not a sound made by any organism. It is not a sound made by the structures. It is not a sound made by the water or the substrate or the garden. It is a sound made by the *plateau itself* — by the entire system, the whole vast flat warm dim breathing ancient completed thing, vibrating at a frequency that it reaches only at the moment of fullest expansion, at the peak of the breath, when the two realities are almost able to perceive each other and the tension of that almost-perception is so great that the whole medium *rings* with it.

The tone is the plateau's *word*. It is the one word that the statement makes that is not silent, that is not structural, that is not embedded in the placement of pillars or the maintenance of boundaries or the growth of the garden. It is a word spoken aloud, in a medium that conducts sound perfectly, in a silence so profound that a single tone is enough to fill it completely. It says — if it says anything, if *saying* is what it does — it says *here*. It says *I am*. It says *this is*. Not as information. Not as communication. As *fact*. As the verbal equivalent of the substrate's existence: bedrock, asserting itself, once, in a voice so low and so large that it is felt in the teeth and the skull and the chest and the water and the loam and the stalks and the carpet-boundaries and the dark shape and the pillars and the basins and the glassy exposed substrate and the whole of the plateau from edge to edge, and then it is gone, and the silence is louder than before, because the silence now contains the *memory* of the tone, and the memory is a shape in the silence, and the shape is the same shape as the tone, and the tone is the same shape as the plateau, and the plateau is the same shape as the word *here*, and the word *here* is the same shape as *being*, and being is the only thing the plateau does, and it does it forever, and it says it once.

## The Ones Who Made the Structures

They are gone.

Not dead — *gone*. The distinction matters. Dead means they were here and then they stopped being here and their remains are here and their absence is a shape in the world. Gone means they were here and then they *left*, or they *became*, or they *finished*, and their absence is not a shape but a *completion*, a sentence that has been spoken and that does not need to be spoken again.

They were not people. They were not builders. They were not gods or spirits or ancestors or any of the categories that the myths of other worlds would supply. They were something that the plateau produced — the way it produces the stalks and the carpets and the drift-gatherings and the blurs and the tone — at an earlier stage of its existence, when its minding was not yet complete, when its statement was still being composed, when the processes that shape this place had not yet reached the equilibrium that now defines it.

They made the pillars by *being*. Their existence was a pattern in the substrate, and where the pattern was most intense, the substrate expressed it as grooves on a surface, as a pillar rising from the loam. They made the basins by *resting* — by settling into the substrate in places where their pattern was *quiet*, and the quiet left a depression, and the depression filled with water that was clearer and warmer because it was water that had been *within* the pattern, water that still carried the shape of their resting. They made the dark shape by *leaving* — by reaching a density of pattern so intense that it became a local reality, a place where their rules replaced the plateau's rules, and then by *departing* that reality, by folding back into the substrate, by becoming the lattice-kin, by becoming the pattern that holds the bedrock together, by becoming the resistance that keeps the plateau from dissolving entirely into the water.

They are the lattice-kin now. Or the lattice-kin are what they became. Or the lattice-kin are what they always were, and the structures are the traces of a time when they were something else — something more concentrated, more individual, more *present* — before they *spread*, before they *thinned*, before they became the distributed, ubiquitous, invisible pattern that holds the substrate together and maintains the boundaries and keeps the pillars unmarked and the basins empty and the dark shape dark.

They are not gone in the way that the dead are gone. They are gone in the way that a river is gone when it has reached the sea — not ended, not destroyed, but *distributed*, *incorporated*, *become*. They are everywhere. They are the substrate. They are the taste of the water. They are the grooves on the pillars and the clarity of the basins and the warmth of the dark shape. They are the *holding* that the plateau does, the *remaining* that the plateau is, the *completion* that the plateau has achieved.

They do not know they are gone. They do not know they were ever here. They do not know anything, because *knowing* is a process, and the plateau has completed its processes, and the ones who made the structures were a process, and the process is finished, and what remains is the *product*: the pattern, the substrate, the structures, the lattice-kin, the whole vast patient holding-together of a world that has said everything it was going to say and is now simply *being what it said*.

## What Lives Here, Ultimately

Nothing lives here, if living requires the things that living usually requires: birth, growth, change, desire, death. Everything here has been born and has finished growing and does not change and does not desire and will not die. The plateau has completed the cycle of living and has arrived at a state that is not life and not death but *the thing that life and death are both pointing at*: existence, pure and sustained, without the drama of becoming and the tragedy of ending.

But if living can mean something else — if living can mean *persisting*, if living can mean *being part of a pattern that maintains itself*, if living can mean *existing in a medium that is warm and that holds you and that does not require you to do anything other than be* — then everything here lives. The stalks live. The carpets live. The lattice-kin live. The drift lives. The blurs live. The tone lives. The water lives. The substrate lives. The dark shape lives. The boundaries between the carpets live. The gaps in the world live. The two realities that are one reality live. The breathing of the plateau is living, and the plateau is breathing, and the breathing has been going on forever, and the breathing will go on forever, and the breathing is not a metaphor for anything. It is the actual, physical, thermal expansion and contraction of a volume of water so large and so old that it has become indistinguishable from the act of living itself.

What lives here is the plateau. Not *on* the plateau. Not *in* the plateau. The plateau *itself* lives — as a pattern, as a statement, as a holding-together of forces and forms and histories and realities, as a place that has reached the end of what places can do and has discovered that the end is not a stopping-point but a *resting-point*, a place where the work is done and the being can begin, and the being is the work, and the work is the being, and they are the same, and they have always been the same, and the plateau has always known this, and the plateau has never known this, and knowing and not-knowing are the same here, and *same* is the same as *different* here, and the water breathes, and the garden pulses, and the light does not change, and the tone sounds once and is gone, and the silence returns, and the silence is full, and the fullness is living, and the living is what lives here.
