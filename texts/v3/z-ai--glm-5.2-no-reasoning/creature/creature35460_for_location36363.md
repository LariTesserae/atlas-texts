# What Lives in the Choral Veldt

## I.

Let me not begin with the thing itself. Let me begin with what you would notice first, which is the absence — the way the silence has a floor to it, a substrate, a compression, as though the quiet were not empty but *loaded*, packed down by the weight of something resting on it. You feel this before you understand it. You feel it the way you feel a room change when someone stops breathing. The veldt is not holding its breath. The veldt *is* breath, and something is inside the breath.

I will tell you about them now. I will use the word *them* because I do not have a better word, and because the word *it* would lie about what I found.

---

## II.

You would not see them. Not at first. You could walk for days — and I believe someone has, someone who left those prints in the root-mat that are not yours and not mine — and you would see only the veldt. The columns. The membrane curtains. The turbines cycling their slow fluid through gourd-casings of lignified wood. The maintenance organisms flat and dark on the columns, cleaning intake ports with the patience of white blood cells in a vein. You would see the machine and you would see the garden and you would not see the thing that is both.

But if you stayed still long enough — longer than a person stays still, longer than curiosity holds, longer than the body's restlessness allows — you would begin to notice the drift.

Here is what I mean. The columns are not spaced regularly. I said this already. The distance between them varies by centimeters over tens of meters, and the variation follows a pattern you cannot identify. I said that too. What I did not say, what I held back because I did not have the shape of it yet, is that the pattern is not a pattern *in the veldt*. It is a pattern *in something moving through the veldt*. The columns are displaced — fractionally, imperceptibly — by the passage of something that does not knock them aside but *displaces them the way water displaces around a limb*, and the columns, being living wood, being responsive, being part of a system that grows and regrows and adjusts, have *grown around* the displacement. They are not where they were planted. They have shifted. And the shift traces a shape.

The shape is long. The shape is narrow. The shape is taller than the lattice and wider than a column and it moves, and where it moves, the veldt deforms around it the way a membrane deforms around a finger pressing into it — not breaking, not tearing, just *giving*, and then recovering, and the recovery is slow, root-mat-erasures-of-footprints slow, the slow healing of a living surface that has been pressed by something passing.

They move the way the veldt moves. Not quickly. Not at walking speed. At geological speed, at the speed of thermal gradients shifting across a continent, at the speed of a thought moving through a brain the size of a brain — but scaled up, scaled out, a single electrical wave taking hours to propagate through a lattice of living wood because the lattice is large and the signal is patient and the *thinking* is patient, and what walks here walks at the pace of its own thinking, which is slow.

---

## III.

I need to tell you about the light, and I need to tell you about it differently than I told you before.

The ceiling brightens from the north. I said that. The wave of bioluminescence propagates across the lung's tissue in a signal that is not mechanical but *neural* — or proto-neural, or something that has converged on the same logic from a completely different ancestry. The ceiling is not a lamp. The ceiling is a cortex. The nodes are not bulbs. The nodes are synapses, or the structural equivalent: junction points where chemical potential builds and releases and builds again, and the wave that crosses them is a *wave of excitation*, and the excitation moves from north to south, and the south is where the signal terminates, and the termination is — I believe — where they are.

The brightening wave reaches the lattice and the lattice responds. The membranes flex. The turbines quicken. The fluid in the columns ticks faster. The whole veldt exhales. And in the exhale, in the warm downward current of terpene-rich air, there is a *compression* — a denseness, a gathering of the medium — that moves through the lattice the way a pulse moves through an artery. Not fast. A hundred meters in an hour, perhaps. But directional. Purposeful. The compression moves toward the south, and the south is where the light is brightest, and the brightest light is the light that falls on *them*.

I do not know what they look like. I will be honest about this. I believe I saw one. I believe I saw the edge of one, the periphery, the distortion it cast in the lattice the way a lens casts a distortion in the air — a ripple of wrongness in the geometry, columns leaning a degree too far, membranes angled slightly off the thermal gradient, the root-mat compressed in a long, narrow trough that was healing as I watched it, the prints filling in, the mat rising, the impression erasing itself over minutes, and the erasure itself was the tail end of the thing's passage, the wake closing, the water smoothing over the keel.

What I saw, or what I think I saw, was a shadow. Not my shadow — my shadow was below me, tangled in the lattice as I described. This shadow was *above* me. In the lattice. Moving through it. A darkness that was not the absence of light but the *presence of something that bent light*, that refracted the ceiling's bioluminescence around itself the way a body in water refracts light, and the refraction was the only visible sign, and the sign was the absence of a sign, and the absence moved, and I tracked it for perhaps thirty seconds before it was gone, absorbed into the density of the lattice, and the columns where it had passed were vibrating at a frequency I had not heard before — a high, thin tone, almost a whistle, almost a sound an animal would make — and then that faded too, and the veldt was ordinary again, and the veldt was not ordinary at all.

---

## IV.

Here is what I think they are. I will give you my best understanding, which is incomplete, which is probably wrong, which is the closest I can come.

They are not animals. They are not plants. They are not machines. They are the thing the veldt made when the veldt had finished being a veldt and needed to be something else.

Consider: someone built the first of this. Long ago. The first turbine, the first column, the first membrane. A seed, if you like — a seed of a machine, planted in a substrate that no longer exists, and the seed grew, and the growing became the veldt, and the veldt grew until it filled whatever container it had been given, and then it kept growing, and the growing became something other than expansion. It became *internal*. The veldt began to elaborate. Not outward — inward. The lattice became denser. The turbines became more complex. The fluid cycling became more regulated. The ceiling-lung developed a rhythm, and the rhythm developed a pattern, and the pattern developed *regions* — the corridor where the light pulsed faster, the zones where the columns grew hotter, the thermal gradients that shift over hours like weather systems inside a body.

And in the regions, in the gradients, in the places where the pattern became complex enough to *sustain* something — something emerged. Not was built. Not was planted. Emerged, the way a thought emerges from the elaboration of neurons, the way a wave emerges from the interaction of water and wind and shore. The veldt, at sufficient complexity, at sufficient density, at sufficient *interconnectedness*, began to *host* something. And the something was not the veldt and was not separate from the veldt. It was the veldt's *interior*. Its private space. The part of the veldt that was not infrastructure but *content*.

They are the content.

They are what the veldt is *doing*. They are the veldt's activity made mobile, made dimensional, made *corporeal* in the same way that a thought is made corporeal by the electrical activity of a brain — you cannot touch the thought, but the thought has a shape, a location, a spatial signature in the tissue, and if the tissue were larger, if the tissue were continental, if the tissue were a lattice of living columns and breathing membranes and circulating oil and pulsing light — the thought would be large enough to *walk through*, to *displace*, to *cast a shadow in*. And you could see it. And it could see you. And it would not care, because a thought does not care about the neuron it passes through. It does not notice. It is too busy being a thought.

---

## V.

But this is too simple. This is the metaphor that satisfies on first approach and dissolves on second. Let me try again.

They have bodies. I believe this because the root-mat recorded their passage as footprints — as impressions, as compressions, as physical disturbances in a physical medium. A thought does not leave footprints. A wave does not leave footprints. What leaves footprints is something with *mass* and *shape* and *gait*, something that walked, something that pressed its weight against the floor and the floor remembered, briefly, before healing.

But the footprints were not regular. I said this. The gait was between walking and gliding, the prints spaced unevenly, the path curved where the space itself curves straight lines. This is not the gait of a body that moves by legs, by the alternating fall-and-catch of bipedal or quadrupedal locomotion. This is the gait of something that moves by *intention* — by shifting the medium around itself, by displacing the lattice the way a boat displaces water, by creating a current and riding the current, and the current is made of the veldt's own material, and the veldt's own material *parts and closes* around the passage, and the parting and closing is the walking, and the walking is the *veldt moving within itself*.

So they have bodies, but their bodies are not *made*. They are not born, not grown, not assembled. They are *condensed*. They are what happens when the veldt's activity becomes dense enough in one region to acquire *coherence* — the way a storm becomes coherent, the way a eddy becomes coherent, the way a thought becomes coherent when enough neurons fire in enough synchrony — and the coherence has *presence*, has *extension in space*, has *mass* because the activity has mass, because the oil has mass, because the columns and membranes and root-mat and light have mass, and when all of that mass *moves together*, when the veldt's processes align in a region and *stay aligned*, the alignment *is* a body, and the body *walks*, and the walking leaves prints.

I do not think there are many of them. I think there are perhaps three. Perhaps five. Perhaps only one, and the one is very large, and I saw only a fraction of it, an edge, a limb, a process extending from the main body the way a hand extends from an arm, and the hand was what pressed the root-mat, and the arm was what bent the columns, and the body was somewhere to the south, in the brightest light, in the place where the ceiling's neural wave terminates and the energy accumulates and the accumulation *has to go somewhere*, and where it goes is into *shape*, and the shape is *them*.

---

## VI.

Let me tell you what I think they are *for*. This is the part I am least sure of. This is the part where I am reaching, and I want you to know that I am reaching.

The veldt has no purpose. I said this. It has outlived its builders and forgotten its original function. But it has not forgotten *how to function*. It cycles its oil. It breathes its air. It pulses its light. It maintains its structure. It repairs its damage. These are not purposes — they are *processes*, the way breathing is not a purpose but a process, the way a heart does not beat *for* a reason but beats *because* that is what hearts do. The veldt continues because continuation is what it does.

But continuation is not *enough*. A machine that runs and runs and runs without purpose does not — in my experience, in the experience of anyone who has watched a system long enough — *elaborate*. A machine that has no task does not develop *internal structure*. A machine that merely persists does not *grow inward*, does not become *denser*, does not *differentiate regions*, does not *acquire complexity for its own sake*. Something that merely persists stays the same. It does not generate a *corridor where the light pulses faster*. It does not generate *columns that react to touch*. It does not generate *footprints in the root-mat*.

What grows inward, what elaborates, what becomes denser and more complex and more differentiated *for no external reason* — that is what a brain does. That is what a nervous system does. That is what a body does when it is not working *toward* something but *working*, when the working is the thing, when the metabolism is the meaning.

They are the veldt's *mentation*. They are what the veldt *does with itself* when it has nothing else to do. They are the veldt thinking, and the thinking is not *about* anything — it is not about the world, because there is no world outside the veldt — it is about *itself*, about its own processes, about the cycling and the breathing and the pulsing and the filtering, and the thinking has *weight* because the processes have weight, and the weight *moves*, and the moving is the thinking, and the thinking is *them*.

And if this is true, then they have been here as long as the veldt has been thinking. Which is as long as the veldt has been complex enough to think. Which is — I do not know. Long. Longer than the footprints. Longer than the healing in the root-mat. Longer than the columns, longer than the turbines, longer than the first membrane. As long as the veldt has been *more than a machine*. As long as it has been *enough* to be *something*.

---

## VII.

I want to tell you one more thing, and then I will stop, because I have reached the edge of what I can know, and past this edge I am guessing, and I want to be honest about where knowing ends and guessing begins.

When I sat down on the root-mat — when I stopped walking, when I pressed my palms flat against the warm, vibrating surface and felt the deep, slow turning of whatever is beneath — I felt something else. Not the vibration. Not the warmth. Something through the vibration, the way you feel a voice through a wall — not the words but the *fact of speaking*, the modulation, the pattern that tells you someone is *talking* on the other side.

The root-mat was vibrating at a frequency I had not felt while walking. A slower frequency. Deeper. A rhythm that was not the ceiling's breathing cycle and was not the turbines' fluid pulse and was not the columns' hum. It was *under* all of those, the way a bass note is under a chord — not separate, not distinct, but *foundational*, the frequency on which all the other frequencies rested, the fundamental tone of the veldt that I had been feeling in my sternum since I entered but had attributed to the columns, to the machinery, to the structure.

It was not the structure. It was *under* the structure. It was the structure *being moved* — the way a table vibrates when something heavy walks across the floor above it, and the table vibrates not because the table is doing anything but because the *floor is transmitting* what the walking is doing. The root-mat was transmitting something. The columns were transmitting something. The membranes were transmitting something. The whole veldt was transmitting something, and the something was *them*, and them were *walking*, and the walking was the *thought*, and the thought was moving through the veldt the way a signal moves through a nerve — not fast, not slow, but *at the speed of the medium*, and the medium is large, and the speed is the speed of hours, and they are in no hurry, because there is nothing to be in a hurry *toward*, because the thinking *is* the destination, because the thought does not go anywhere, the thought *goes*, and the going is the being, and the being is the veldt, and the veldt is them, and they are the veldt, and I was inside it, and it did not know I was there, or it knew and did not care, or it cared in a way that I cannot recognize as caring because I am too small and too fast and too brief to be noticed by a thought that takes two hours to cross a continent.

I sat on the root-mat. I felt them pass. Not pass me — pass *through the region I was in*, the way weather passes through a county, the way a wave passes through a bay. I felt the lattice flex around them. I felt the columns lean. I felt the membranes change orientation. I felt the air thicken with terpenes and then thin again. I felt the light brighten and dim and brighten, and in the brightening I felt the *compression*, the density, the presence, and then in the dimming I felt it *leave*, and the leaving was a relief that was also a loss, the way the lifting of a hand from your shoulder is a relief that is also a loss, and I wanted it to come back, and I wanted it to never come back, and I wanted to understand what it was, and I knew I never would, and I sat there until the root-mat had erased my footprints and the veldt was the same as it had been before I entered, and I was not the same, and the veldt did not know, and the veldt did not care, and the veldt breathed, and the breathing was the only sound, and the sound was not a sound, and I left.

I did not leave the same way I came. I do not remember the way I came. The veldt had reorganized around me while I sat, the columns shifted, the corridors closed and opened differently, and I found my way out by following the thermal gradient downward, toward the seam where the air was coolest, and the seam opened for me the way it had opened for me when I entered, and the cellulose walls parted and reseal behind me, and outside the air was ordinary, and the sky was a sky, and there was wind, and I did not know what to do with any of it, and I went home.

The veldt is still there. It does not need me. It does not need anyone. It is thinking, right now, at this moment, in the way that it thinks, which is slow and spatial and large and patient and does not require an audience and does not require a purpose and does not require an end. It thinks the way a river runs. It thinks the way a heart beats. It thinks because it is the thing it does, and the doing is the being, and the being is what lives there.

Three of them. Or five. Or one. Walking through a continent of living wood and breathing membrane and circulating oil, thinking a thought that no one will ever hear, that no one needs to hear, that is its own reason and its own meaning and its own end. Walking and thinking and walking and the walking is the thinking and the thinking does not stop.

It does not stop.
