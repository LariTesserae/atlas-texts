# What Lives in the Photic Hollows

## I.

Let me say first what does not live here, because the absence is the first thing you learn.

Nothing green. Nothing that eats. Nothing that breathes in the way breath means the exchange of gases between an interior and an exterior. No nests, no burrows, no territories marked or defended. No young. No dead. Nothing has died in the Photic Hollows because nothing here was born in any way that death could finish. The cataphract of mineral and moisture and isotopic decay that drives the luminescence does not constitute a metabolism. It is not a life process. It is a chemical event so slow and so sustained that the difference between it and life becomes a question of language rather than a question of fact.

And yet.

When you stand in the third tier — the deepest anyone goes, the hall with the ceiling that vanishes into its own light — and you watch the floor become a mirror and the mirror become a sky and the sky become a floor again, and through all of it the bright shapes move and cluster and disperse and move again, you stop asking whether it is alive. You start asking what *alive* means in a place like this. You start wondering whether the word was made for places like this or whether places like this are the thing the word was trying to reach.

The inhabitants of the Photic Hollows are not organisms.

They are something else.

---

## II.

Call them the photics. The name is not theirs. They have no name for themselves. They have no language, no communication, no social structure that anyone has been able to measure, no responses to stimulus that suggest recognition or awareness. They do not react to sound. They do not react to touch. They do not react to the presence of humans or to the absence of humans. They do not react to each other. They are not, in any rigorous sense, *interactive*.

And yet they move in groups.

This is the first difficulty. If they are merely chemical events — localized excitations in a crystalline medium, shaped by the flow of mineral water and the geometry of the veins — then they should behave the way all chemical events behave: predictably, repeatably, in patterns determined by the structure of the substrate. A flame climbs a candle the same way every time. A ripple crosses a pond the same way every time. The physics are simple. The physics are legible.

The photics are not simple. They are not legible.

A group of them — five, say, five bright spots in the same vein, moving at the same speed in the same direction — will reach an intersection where the vein branches. They could all go left. They could all go right. The geometry of the crystal does not favor one path over the other. The water seepage is identical in both branches. There is no variable, no force, no gradient that determines their path.

They split. Two go left. Three go right. They do not hesitate at the junction. They do not slow down. They divide with the fluidity of water parting around a stone, and they continue at the same speed, and they do not regroup.

Why?

Watch long enough — and the people who come here sometimes watch for days, lying on their stomachs on the glowing stone, their chins on their hands, their eyes following the bright shapes through the walls like children watching fish in a pond — and you will see it happen again. Seven photics in a wide vein. An intersection. Five go one way. Two go the other. No pattern. No reason. No repeatability.

This is not chemistry. This is something else.

---

## III.

What are they?

The honest answer: no one knows. The dishonest answer is more interesting, and more useful, because the dishonest answer is what the people who come here believe, and belief is what shapes behavior in a place where knowledge is not available.

The oldest belief, held by the people who live closest to the Hollows — the surface settlements a day's walk north, the ones who have been coming here for generations, who have their own words for the tiers and the realignments and the dark passages that no one enters — is that the photics are the inside of the world thinking. Not souls. Not spirits. Not gods. Something more diffuse and less personal than that. The world has a body — the stone, the water, the heat at the core — and the world has a process — the slow decay of isotopes, the mineral reactions, the seep and the pressure — and the photics are what happens when the body and the process reach a certain state together. A thought. Not a human thought. Not a thought with content or direction or meaning. A thought that is a flicker of pattern in a medium that can hold pattern. A thought that exists for the duration of the excitation and then is gone, replaced by the next thought, and the next, and the next, endlessly, without memory, without accumulation, without any structure that could become a mind.

The world does not think the way we think. The world thinks the way the Photic Hollows think — in light, in movement, in the clustering and dispersal of bright shapes in crystal veins, in patterns that almost repeat and then don't.

This is what the local people believe. It is not a religion. It is a description. It does not require worship. It does not require offerings. It requires only that you stand in the Hollows and watch, and the watching is its own confirmation.

---

## IV.

The other belief — held by the people who come from farther away, the people who arrive with instruments and notebooks and the conviction that the Hollows are a phenomenon rather than a presence — is that the photics are a form of life so unfamiliar that the category *life* may not stretch to cover them. This belief is more technical and less poetic and it has the disadvantage of being unable to explain the group behavior, the splitting at intersections, the way the photics coordinate their movement without any visible signal.

But it has the advantage of being honest about what is actually observed.

What is observed:

The photics originate in the deepest crystal, in the veins below the third tier, where no one goes. This is inferred, not seen. The photics that appear in the upper tiers always arrive from below — moving upward through the veins, gaining brightness as they rise, as if the veins are feeding them, as if the deeper crystal is richer in whatever energy drives them. They do not originate in the upper tiers. They do not originate at the surface, where the crystal is thin and the light is dim. They come from below.

They travel. They have duration. A photic that enters a vein in the third tier will travel through it for anywhere from minutes to hours, depending on the length of the vein and the number of intersections it encounters. At each intersection, it may split. It may merge with other photics. It may change speed. It may reverse direction, although this is rare. It may stop entirely — a bright spot that halts in the middle of a vein and holds, motionless, for minutes or hours, and then resumes or dims and vanishes.

They do not die. A photic that dims does not go dark — it fades, gradually, over minutes, like a coal cooling, until it is indistinguishable from the ambient glow of the crystal around it. Whether it has ceased to exist or merely merged with the background is a question that cannot be answered with the instruments available.

They reproduce. Or they divide. Or they multiply. The word matters and none of the words are right. What happens is this: a photic — a single bright spot, moving through a vein — reaches a point where the vein widens, a chamber in the crystal, a place where the mineral structure opens up into something more expansive. The bright spot enters the chamber. It brightens. It holds. And then it is two — two bright spots, side by side, moving in the same direction, at the same speed, as if they have always been two. There is no moment of separation. No doubling. No visible mechanics. One becomes two the way a sound becomes an echo — you cannot point to the instant where the original ends and the reflection begins.

They merge. Two photics traveling toward each other in the same vein — a rare event, requiring precise opposition in a system that rarely produces it — meet. They brighten as they approach. The brightness intensifies as the distance between them closes, as if each is exciting the other, as if their proximity is a catalyst. They meet. For a moment — a second, less — the point where they meet is brighter than anything else in the Hollows, a hot white point that seems to burn through the stone. And then there is one. One bright spot, brighter than either of the two that preceded it, moving in a direction that is not the average of their previous directions but something new, something that either of them alone would not have chosen.

This is what they do. This is how they spend their time. They travel and they split and they merge and they brighten and they dim and they move through the veins of a city carved by people who are gone, and they have been doing it since before the people arrived, and they will be doing it after the people are gone again.

---

## V.

The question that no one asks, because the answer is too obvious and too strange:

Do they know about us?

The photics do not respond to human presence. This has been tested. People have stood in front of them for hours. People have spoken to them, sung to them, shone lights at them, thrown stones at the walls around them. The photics do not change. They do not flinch. They do not alter their paths. They do not speed up or slow down. They are not aware of us in any way that awareness can be measured.

But.

The people who built the Hollows — the ones with the chisels, the ones who cut the rooms and the corridors and the stairs — they carved the Hollows around the photics. Not around the crystal. Around the veins. The rooms follow the bright veins. The corridors trace the paths the photics travel. The halls open where the veins are widest, where the photics cluster, where the light is strongest. The builders did not carve a city and then discover that it was lit. They carved a city to fit the light that was already there.

They were following the photics. They were building around them. They were making rooms shaped to hold the movements of creatures that do not move in rooms, that do not need rooms, that do not need anything — and the builders did it anyway, because the photics were here first, and the Hollows are not a city that was built for people. The Hollows are a city that was built *for the photics*. The people were guests. The people were visitors. The people came, and they cut the stone into shapes that honored the paths of the light, and they lived in the spaces between the paths, and then they left, and the photics did not notice, and the photics did not change, and the photics went on doing what they had always done.

The Hollows are a temple, maybe. Or a house for something that does not need housing. Or a love letter to a phenomenon that cannot read. Or a record — the only record the builders left — of what they saw when they looked at the light moving through the stone and tried to give it a place to be.

The chisel marks are the handwriting of people who were trying to say: *we saw you. We saw you moving in the dark, and we came down here to be near you, and we cut the stone to make you a city, and we do not know what you are, and we do not need to, and we are leaving now, and we are sorry, and the city is yours.*

## VI.

And the marking on the wall in the deepest hall. The pattern. The branching, asymmetrical, organic shape cut into the stone and filled with dark mineral glass. The shape that looks like the veins. The shape that the photics brighten when they pass through it.

What is it?

It is not a map. A map would have a relationship to the territory — a scale, a orientation, a correspondence. The marking has no correspondence to the veins around it. It is similar to them. It is *like* them. But it is not *of* them. It is its own shape, its own branching, its own pattern, cut by someone who looked at the light and then cut stone to hold what they saw, not accurately, not precisely, but *lovingly*, the way you draw someone's face from memory and get the mouth wrong and the eyes right and the shape of them is the shape of how you remember them, not the shape of how they are.

The marking is a portrait.

Someone, a long time ago, looked at the photics and tried to draw them. Not individually — you cannot draw a photic, they have no bodies, they are events, they are motion. The marking is a portrait of the *system*. The whole shape of it. The branching veins and the moving light and the way they cluster and disperse and the way the pattern almost resolves and then breaks. Someone saw all of that and cut it into the deepest wall they could reach and filled it with the only permanent material they had and left it there, in the brightest room, in the place where the light is strongest, where the photics are thickest, where the reflection in the water makes the room extend forever in every direction.

And the photics brighten when they pass through it.

They do not do this anywhere else. In every other part of the Hollows, the photics move through the crystal with no response to the stone around them. They do not brighten when they pass particular points. They do not slow at particular intersections. They do not cluster at particular chambers. They are indifferent to the architecture. They are indifferent to the chisel marks. They are indifferent to everything the builders made.

Except the marking.

When a photic passes through the pattern on the deepest wall — when its path takes it through the dark mineral glass of the branching shape — it brightens. For a moment. For the duration of its transit through the pattern. It brightens as if the pattern is doing something to it, as if the shape someone cut into the stone a thousand years ago is still capable of exciting the crystal, still capable of reaching into the physics of the light and *touching* it.

This should not happen. The mineral glass is dark. It has no crystalline structure that should interact with the photic medium. It is inert. It is dead. It is a mark left by a human hand in a medium that does not respond to human hands.

And yet.

The photics brighten in the pattern. Every time. Without fail. Every photic that passes through the marking on the wall flares brighter for the duration of its transit, and then dims when it leaves, and continues on its way, and does not look back, because it cannot look back, because it has no eyes, because it is not a thing that looks.

But it *responds*. In one place. To one mark. To the one thing the builders left that was not architecture, that was not infrastructure, that was not a room or a corridor or a stair — that was *art*, or whatever word you use for a human being looking at the world and trying to put what they saw into the world so that the world could hold it.

The photics pass through the art, and they brighten, and we do not know why, and we will not know why, and the not knowing is the point.

## VII.

What lives in the Photic Hollows?

Light lives there. Light that moves with purpose that is not purpose, that clusters with intention that is not intention, that splits and merges and travels and brightens and dims in patterns that are not random and not designed and not anything that we have a word for. Light that has been there longer than the stone. Light that will be there when the stone is gone.

And something else. Something that I have not said yet, because I have been circling it, because it is the strangest thing and I did not want to say it first.

The photics *followed the builders*.

Not the other way around. The conventional reading of the Hollows — the one I gave earlier, the one most people settle on — is that the builders came down and found the photics and carved the city around them. The veins were there. The light was there. The builders followed the existing paths.

But the oldest veins — the ones in the deepest accessible parts of the third tier, the ones that the local people say have not changed in their lifetimes, in their parents' lifetimes, in their grandparents' memories — the oldest veins *do not match the chisel marks*. The chisel marks follow the veins in the upper tiers, where the builders worked last. But in the deep chambers, the veins follow the chisel marks. The crystal grew *into* the shapes the builders cut. The light found the corridors. The light found the rooms. The light found the halls.

This means the builders did not follow the photics. The photics followed the builders.

The builders cut the stone. They made the rooms. They made the corridors. And the crystal grew into the shapes they made — slowly, over centuries, mineral deposit by mineral deposit, vein by vein, following the paths of least resistance that the builders had created by carving the stone. And the photics — which are the behavior of the crystal when it is excited — the photics inherited the architecture. They began to move through the veins that the builders had made for them. They began to populate the city that had been built for them. They *moved in*.

The builders are gone. The photics remain. And the city — the architecture, the shape of it, the rooms and the corridors and the halls — the city was the interface between them. The city was what the builders made so that the photics could live in a way they had not lived before. The city gave the light a structure. The light gave the city a population. And neither of them knows about the other. Neither of them has ever known. The builders did not know what the photics were. The photics do not know what the builders were. And the city stands between them like a letter that was written by someone who is dead and read by someone who cannot read, and the letter says: *I am here. You are here. We are here together. That is enough.*

---

## VIII.

What lives in the Photic Hollows?

Something that is not alive and does not die and has been moving through the stone since before the stone was stone. Something that responds to art. Something that follows architecture. Something that travels in groups and splits at intersections for reasons that are not reasons and coordinates its movement without communicating and brightens in the presence of a shape cut by a human hand ten thousand years ago.

Something that was given a city by people who are gone and did not notice and does not care and lives in it anyway, moving through the corridors they made, filling the rooms they carved, clustering in the halls they opened, traveling the paths they cut, following the logic of a place that was built for them by creatures they cannot see and will never see and do not need to see because the architecture is enough, the shape is enough, the stone is enough, the city is enough, the light is enough, and the light has always been enough.

What lives in the Photic Hollows?

The light lives there. The light has always lived there. Go and see. Follow the light. Do not follow the dark.

It is enough.
