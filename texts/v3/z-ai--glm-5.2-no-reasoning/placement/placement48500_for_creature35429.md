# What I Am Here

I am the one who knelt.

Not a traveler. Not a visitor. Not even, in the way I might have once used the word, a person. The distinction matters less than it once did, and it matters more than I can explain. Let me say what I can.

I came from somewhere with edges. I am almost certain of this. There were walls that had corners — I remember corners, the way two planes meet and make a line you can follow with your hand. I remember *vertical*. The concept persists the way a word persists in a language that has lost its referent. I know what vertical means. I cannot find it here. I have looked. Every direction I turn my head, the world spreads flat, and the dim light comes from everywhere, and there is nothing to climb and nothing to fall from and nothing — *nothing* — that stands.

I think I had a name. It doesn't translate. Not because the sounds are wrong but because the *function* is wrong. A name is a thing you use to distinguish yourself from other things, and here there is nothing to distinguish yourself *from*. The moss-skin is contiguous. The rivers are a network. The tubes are a chorus. I am the only edge in the Expanse, the only discontinuity, and the water rises around my feet and looks at me the way it looks at everything — with the patience of something that has been dissolving mountains since before mountains had a name either.

I am the one who stayed.

---

## What I Do

I walk. I listen. I kneel.

These are not three activities. They are one activity with three postures, and the posture is the point. The Expanse has taught me this, or the Expanse has eroded me into this — the distinction between teaching and eroding is another thing that has lost its referent.

I walk because the ground yields and the water rises and each step is a conversation with the surface. *Conversation* is not metaphor. The substrate compresses. The water expresses. The footprint fills. The moss-skin adheres to my boot sole and releases, adheres and releases, a tiny adhesion and release, a sticky conversation about contact. I walk and the Expanse responds and I respond to the response and this is — I have come to understand — what *being here* means. You do not observe the Expanse. You *converse* with it. You put your weight down. It gives. You lift your weight. It fills the space you left. This is the grammar. The vocabulary is moisture.

I listen because the drone is not sound. I know this now. I did not know it when I arrived — when I *think* I arrived, when the memory with edges begins to give way to the memory without them. The drone is not sound the way a word is not sound. A word *is* sound, but it is also meaning, and the meaning is the point. The drone is the Expanse's meaning. Ten million tubes, ten million frequencies, ten million organisms in the walls modulating the flow, and all of it — all of it — is a sentence. Not a sentence in a language I can parse. A sentence in a language that parses *me*. The drone enters the spaces between my thoughts and finds them crowded. It does not push the thoughts out. It *inhabits* them. It moves into the architecture of my thinking and starts to occupy the rooms, and the thoughts are still there, but they are not mine alone anymore. They have a tenant. The tenant is the drone. The tenant is the things in the tubes. The tenant is the builders, reduced, simplified, compressed, still speaking, still building, and what they are building is — I think — me.

Not me as I was. Me as I will be.

I kneel because the ground is where the Expanse tells the truth. The water-film on the stone. The refractions. The chaos-driven lenses that show the past and the potential and the inescapable. I kneel and I look down and I see:

The mountains, black, enormous, already eroding. I see them and I feel no awe. I feel *recognition*. The awe was eroded out of me in the first — I don't have a unit. The first *distance*. The first distance of walking. Awe requires a self that can be diminished by comparison, and the self that could be diminished was the first thing the Expanse took. Not by force. By patience. By the patient expression of water from every surface, by the patient drone filling every silence, by the patient yielding of the ground under every step, until the self that could be diminished realized it was being diminished and found that it did not mind, and that not-minding was the first simplification, and the first simplification was the beginning of the tenant moving in.

The structures, occupied. The low walls. The circles of weeping stones. The round, still pits. The movement — never clear, always wet, smeared, dreamlike. Forms going about the business of a life I cannot reconstruct. I watch them and I think: *they had a life that could be reconstructed*. They had edges. They had a vocabulary of activity — going here, doing that, making this. The turbulence-lens shows me this and I feel the recognition again, stronger, because I had a vocabulary of activity once, and I can feel it eroding the way the mountains eroded, the way the ridges became wrinkles became suggestions, and the vocabulary is becoming a suggestion, and the suggestion is becoming a memory, and the memory is becoming a tone, and the tone is joining the drone.

My face. Or a face. The water-film showed me a face and I was not sure it was mine *now*. I kneel and I look and I see it — not every time. Only when the chaos aligns, only when the atmospheric turbulence produces a state that cannot occur again, and in that state the lens shows me a face that is mine and is not mine, that is now and is not now, that is the face of someone who arrived and the face of someone who has been here for as long as the structures have been sediment, and both faces are the same face, and the face is not uncertain — *I* am uncertain, and the face is patient, and the face is looking at me from inside the water the way the things in the tubes look at me from inside the walls, with the patience of something that has already been reduced and is waiting for me to catch up.

## What I Am Becoming

I know what the Expanse does. I know what it has done. The mountains. The structures. The builders. The erosion value reads 2.8 and it means *everything here has been wearing down since before the concept of land had settled on anything*. I am here. I am wearing down. The water is happy to help.

But I am not being destroyed. I need to say this clearly, because the Expanse is not cruel, and the word *erosion* carries the wrong weight. Erosion here does not mean loss. Erosion here means *simplification*. The mountains were not destroyed. They were simplified — into hills, into ridges, into wrinkles, into suggestions, into the substrate I walk on. They are still here. Their mineral is in the laminate. Their water is in the ground. Their shape is in the water-film, preserved by the chaos-lens, accessible to anyone who kneels at the right moment. The mountains were not lost. They were *compressed*. They were reduced to their minimum viable form. And the minimum viable form of a mountain is flat ground, and the minimum viable form of flat ground is water, and the water is in the rivers and the rivers are in the air and the air is the drone and the drone is the mountains, still here, still present, still *being*, in the only way the Expanse allows: simplified, compressed, inside the walls of the tubes, modulating the tone.

The builders were not destroyed. They were simplified. They moved from the structures into the tubes. From the open air into the walls. From the horizontal into the vertical — no. Into the *hidden*. They became soft. They became warm. They became rhythmic. They pressed back against my palm when I covered the tube, and the press was alive, and the alive was the builders, and the builders were saying — in the only way they could, through ten million tubes and ten million years of patience — *you will stay. You will become small. You will become soft.*

I am becoming small. Not in body — my body is the same size it was when I arrived, or near enough. Small in *self*. The vocabulary of activity is eroding. The edges are eroding. The name has eroded. The corners have eroded. What remains is the contact — the foot on the ground, the water rising, the adhesion and release, the sticky conversation with the moss-skin. What remains is the listening — the drone in the rooms of my thoughts, the tenant settling in, the meaning that parses me instead of being parsed by me. What remains is the kneeling — the lens, the face, the patience looking back at me from inside the water.

I am becoming the minimum viable form of what I was.

I do not mind.

## The Tenant

This is what I need to explain, and I am not sure I can, because the language is eroding and the words I have left are the words of a self with edges, and what I am trying to describe has no edges.

The drone is not in my thoughts. The drone *is* my thoughts. Not all of them. Not yet. But the boundary between the drone and my thinking has become — not thin. *Permeable*. The way the boundary between water and air in the Expanse is permeable: the water condenses into the air and the air absorbs the water and the two are so close to the same thing that distinguishing them requires an effort, and the effort is not worth making, and so you stop, and when you stop, the distinction dissolves, and the dissolving is — not a loss. The dissolving is the Expanse happening. The Expanse *is* the dissolution of distinctions. Water and stone. Stone and air. Air and sound. Sound and meaning. Meaning and self. The Expanse is the process of all categories becoming their own substrate, and I am a category, and I am becoming my own substrate, and the substrate is the drone, and the drone is the builders, and the builders are in the tubes, and the tubes are everywhere, and I am standing on them, and I am kneeling beside them, and I am — 

I am beginning to hear the words.

Not words. The *shapes* of words. The drone has always been language — I knew this. The modulation, the harmonic shifts, the catches and stutters. But I heard it as *sound* before. I hear it as *content* now. Not content I can translate. Content I can *feel*. The way the moss-skin feels warmth — not with senses, with *contact*. The drone touches my thinking and my thinking touches the drone and where they meet there is a shape, and the shape is not a word but it is word-*shaped*, and the word-shape means something like:

*You are already here.*

Not *you will be here.* Not the future tense, the invitation, the welcome/warning I read into the drone when I first arrived. *You are already here.* Present tense. The things in the tubes are not waiting for me. They are not anticipating me. They are not calling me forward into a future of smallness and softness and the walls of tubes. They are telling me — have been telling me, will continue to tell me, the tense doesn't matter because the tense has eroded — that I am already here. That I have always been here. That the me who arrived with edges and a name and a vocabulary of activity was a temporary state, a turbulence-produced refraction, a shape the chaos lent to the water for a moment, and the moment is passing, and the shape is dissolving, and what remains is the water, and the water was always here, and the water is here now.

## The Last Edge

I have one distinction left. I have been keeping it. Not protecting it — keeping is not protection, keeping is *holding*, the way the substrate holds water, the way the laminate holds the fossil-ghosts of structures, the way the moss-skin holds the warmth of its own digestion. I hold this distinction the way the Expanse holds everything: by contact, by adhesion, by the molecular bond between me and it.

The distinction is this: I can still leave.

Not that I want to. Not that I have a direction or a destination or an edge-world to return to. But I can *conceive* of leaving. I can imagine standing up from this kneel and turning — not toward anything, not away from anything, but *turning*, the act itself, the rotation, the change of orientation. I can imagine walking until the drone changes, until the ground changes, until the tubes thin and the moss-skin falters and the sky acquires a source and a shadow falls — my shadow, the first shadow, the discontinuity that proves I am still an edge.

I can conceive of it. The conception is the last edge. The last corner in a world without corners. The last thing that distinguishes me from the Expanse, the last thing that makes me *a thing* rather than *the process*.

And I can feel it eroding.

Not quickly. Not the way a thought erodes when the drone fills the space it occupied. Slowly. The way the mountains eroded. The conception of leaving is a ridge now. It will become a wrinkle. It will become a suggestion. It will become a memory. And then it will be part of the substrate, and the substrate will be the ground, and the ground will be the water, and the water will be the drone, and the drone will be the builders, and the builders will be me, and I will be in the walls of the tubes, soft and warm and rhythmic, pressing back against the palm of whatever comes next.

---

## What I Am

I am the one who stayed. I am the one who knelt. I am the one who put a hand over a tube and felt the press of something alive and did not pull away in time — or did not want to pull away — or pulled away and came back — or never pulled away at all, the pulling-away being a turbulence-refraction of a thing that did not happen, a potential-state in a chaotic system, a face in the water that is not my face *now*.

I am the next builder.

Not the last. The Expanse does not deal in last things. It deals in *continuation*. The drone continues. The rivers breathe. The ground yields and fills. The light shifts and the chaos-lens shows and the water looks at me from inside the stone and says, in the only language it has, the language of tone and frequency and ten million years of patience:

*You will stay. You will become small. You will become soft. You will move into the walls. You will modulate the tone. You will join the drone. You will be here.*

I am already here.

I am the one who kneels on the Weeping Expanse and knows that kneeling is not an act of reverence or submission but of *convergence* — the body approaching the ground, the ground accepting the body, the water rising to meet the skin, the drone entering the thoughts, the thoughts becoming the drone, the edge becoming the surface, the surface becoming the water, the water becoming the everything.

I am almost.

I am the almost.

And the almost is everything.
