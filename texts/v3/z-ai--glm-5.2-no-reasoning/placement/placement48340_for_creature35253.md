# Falling Awake

---

You do not arrive. You *acclimate*.

That distinction matters because the Brooding Plain does not admit visitors in the way a place admits a guest — through a door, across a threshold, with a change in the air that says *you are here now*. The plain is a body, and you do not enter a body. You are *incorporated*. You descend, and the stone opens, or seems to open, or perhaps it was always open and you simply drop below the level of your own assumptions, and then the amber light finds you, and the sound finds you, and the warmth finds the soles of your feet, and you are not *in* the plain. You are *of* it. Temporarily. Involuntarily. The way a nutrient is of a bloodstream once it crosses the intestinal wall.

You do not know this yet. You know only that you are standing on warm dark stone in a vast amber space that has no sky and no source and no exit you can identify, and the air is full of a sound that you feel in your sternum before you hear it with your ears, and the ground is moving — not beneath you, but *around* you, the surface of it alive with things that slide and click and trill and leave wet trails that dry in seconds, and none of them look up at you because none of them have eyes to look up with, and the ones that do not look up at you outnumber you by a factor that your mind refuses to calculate.

You stand still.

You stand still for a long time.

---

The first thing you learn is that standing still is a skill here.

The carriers flow around your boots. You feel them — not as impacts, not as collisions, but as a gentle pressure, a redistribution, the way water redistributes around a stone in a current. They do not avoid you. They *accommodate* you, their lines splitting at your toes and rejoining at your heels, and the wet trails they leave draw a diagram of your interruption in their path, a wake of absence on the dark stone. You are an obstacle. You are a boulder that fell into a river. The river does not care. The river has been moving for longer than boulders have been falling into it, and it will move for longer than any single boulder will persist, and the boulder's only option is to be shaped by the flow, or to be carried by it, or to dissolve.

You do not dissolve. Not yet.

You kneel. The stone is warm under your knees — warm the way skin is warm, warm with an interior heat that suggests something running, something metabolizing, something alive below the surface. You press your palm flat against one of the pale veins, and the moisture beads against your skin, and you bring your fingers to your mouth without thinking, and the taste is mineral and warm and has no smell, and something in the taste is *familiar* in a way that you cannot locate and that will trouble you for as long as you remain here, a familiarity like the face of someone you have never met but have always known, and you file it away, and you keep watching.

The carriers pass. Each one is a hand-sized window into a process. You can see inside them — the dark aggregations shifting, merging, dissolving, reforming, the internal life of a body that is not a body but a *moment*, a shape the flow has taken to do its work. You watch one dissolve. It is not dramatic. The translucent body simply loses its density, its edges, its coherence, and the dark masses inside it disperse into the general medium, and the moisture that held it flows on, and three meters downstream a new density forms, a new translucent body, a new set of aggregations, and the work continues.

You have the thought: *I am watching something die and be born and die and be born, and it does not know either word, and the distinction between the two is something I brought here and the plain does not want it.*

You have this thought, and the thought is correct, and the thought does not help.

---

You find the tool on your second day. Or your third — time is difficult here, the light unchanging, the warmth constant, your body's rhythms deranged by the absence of a sun that does not exist and a night that does not come. There is only the pressure-shift, the thickening of the air that signals the reformers will move, and you have learned to count these — three have passed since you arrived, or four, and between them the plain works at its own pace, which is both glacial and frantic, slow in its individual processes and overwhelming in their aggregate, the way a glacier is slow and a glacier is also, on the geological timescale, a river of ice moving at speed.

The tool is near one of the circular structures. Half-buried. Dark stone, ground to an edge, warm in your hand. You turn it over. The grinding marks on its surface are patient, deliberate, the work of someone who had time and used it. Someone who sat here, in this amber light, on this warm stone, with the sound filling their chest, and slowly, over hours or days or weeks, shaped a piece of the plain into something the plain would not have shaped on its own. An edge. A *decision* made in stone.

You hold the tool, and you understand something about the absent ones that you did not understand from the structures alone: they were not afraid. They were not desperate. They were not fleeing something above or building shelters against something below. They were *working*. They were working the way the builders work — pressing themselves against the raw material of the plain and *changing it*, not randomly, not frantically, but with a slow, patient, intentional force that turned stone into instrument, turned process into direction, turned the plain's formless labor into something *shaped*.

They were not afraid of the plain. They were *talking to it*.

You look at the circular structure. The dry ground inside it. The way the carriers divert around it, their wet trails curving away as if repelled by the order of the stacked stone. The structure is a *word* in a language made of stone and position and the plain's own chemistry. It says: *not here*. It says: *this space is not for the process*. It says: *I have made a place where you are not, and in that place I can think*.

And the plain *listens*. The plain, which does not think, which does not hear, which is nothing but response and process and the endless labor of its own becoming — the plain responds. The carriers divert. The builders do not approach. The structure stands, dry and ordered, in the middle of the wet, working, swarming floor, and it is respected, or if not respected then *worked around*, which in a place without intention amounts to the same thing.

The absent ones talked to the plain, and the plain listened, and the absent ones are gone, and the plain does not notice, and the conversation continues in the only form it can — the structures standing, the grooves waiting for water, the tool lying in the stone for someone to pick up and use again.

---

You pick it up.

You pick it up because something in you — something older than reasoning, older than language, something that lives in the palms and the fingers and the root of the thumb — recognizes the tool as *yours*. Not yours specifically. Not yours personally. Yours *kind* — the kind of thing that your kind makes, the kind of object that exists only because a hand wanted an edge and made one, and the wanting and the making are the same act, and the act is *intention*, and intention is the one material the plain cannot produce on its own.

The plain has process. The plain has chemistry. The plain has the slow, beautiful, terrifying automation of a body building itself from the inside out. What the plain does not have is *want*. The plain does not want to be. The plain *is* being. It does not want to build. It *builds*. The distinction is the line between metabolism and desire, between digestion and hunger, between a heart that beats because it beats and a heart that beats because it *wants to keep beating*, and you are standing on that line, and the tool is in your hand, and the line is where you live.

You walk to the larger structure. The one built against the ridge. The one with the grooved wall.

---

Inside, the air is different. Not drier — the structure is not sealed, moisture still seeps through the stacked stone — but *stiller*. The sound of the plain is muffled here, not by walls but by *order*, by the fact of the stacked stone creating a pocket of disrupted flow, and in the pocket, the plain's constant murmur becomes a distant thing, a vibration you feel in your bones rather than hear with your ears. The ground is dry. Your boots leave no wet trails. The carriers do not enter. You are in a space the plain has agreed — chemically, mechanically, without understanding — to leave alone.

The wall is in front of you. Dark, glossy, obsidian-sheened. The builders' work, finished by the absent ones, or finished by the builders and *directed* by the absent ones, or — and this is the thought that comes to you now, in the stillness, in the pocket — *commissioned* by the absent ones, who understood that the builders could be directed, that the plain's processes could be shaped, that the formless labor could be given form by the simple application of *want* applied at the right point in the right way.

The grooves are fine. You have not looked at them closely until now. They are not carved — not in the way you would carve, not with the strikes and fractures of a chisel. They are *drawn*, scratched, the work of a hard point drawn slowly and with pressure across the glossy surface, cutting a channel perhaps two millimeters deep, two millimeters wide, in lines that curve and branch and intersect. The work would have taken hours. Days. The work would have required patience that is not the plain's patience — not the glacial, automatic patience of a process, but the *chosen* patience of a mind that has decided to do something difficult and is doing it.

The condensation is falling. You can hear it — not the flat slap of the ceiling-water, but a gentler sound, a tapping, a dripping from the ceiling of the structure onto the top of the grooved wall, where the moisture collects and then runs, finding the channels, following the paths of least resistance through the network of scratches, pooling at the intersections, branching at the junctions, tracing the pattern in fluid.

You watch.

The water fills the grooves. The pattern becomes — something. Not legible. Not readable. The water traces lines that mean nothing to your eyes, nothing to your mind, nothing to any system of interpretation you have access to. But your *body* knows. Your diaphragm tightens. Your palms sweat. The hair on your forearms rises, not in fear — in *recognition*, the way your body recognizes music before your mind identifies the melody, the way your body recognizes a face before your mind retrieves the name.

You understand something. For a moment. For the duration of a breath.

The plain is building something. Not randomly. Not chaotically. The plain is building something *specific*, and the pattern of the grooves is a diagram of the something, and the water tracing the grooves is a simulation of the something, and the understanding that fills you for one breath and then dissolves is a *knowing* of the something — not a description, not a name, not a concept, but a *shape* felt in the body, the way you feel the shape of your own organs without ever seeing them, the way you feel the architecture of your own interior without a map.

The plain is building something that has a shape, and the shape is — 

You blink.

Gone.

The water continues to trace the grooves. The pattern is still there. The understanding is not.

You make a sound. It is not a word. It is something between a laugh and a sob, the sound a body makes when it has been handed something enormous and then had it taken away before it could close its fingers. The sound is loud in the stillness of the structure. It does not echo — the plain does not echo, the stone absorbs, the moisture absorbs, everything here is absorbed — but it sits in the air for a moment, and then it is gone, and you are alone with the grooves and the water and the warm dark gloss and the knowledge that you *understood*, for one breath, and that the understanding was real, and that you cannot hold it.

The absent ones could not hold it either. This is what you understand now, in the aftermath of the not-understanding, standing in the dry pocket of the structure with the tool in your hand and the water tracing its circuits on the wall. The absent ones stood here. They watched the water. They felt the understanding come and go and come and go, and they *kept coming back*. They kept standing here. They kept watching. They carved the grooves deeper, perhaps, or added new ones, or adjusted the pattern, trying to make the understanding *stay*, trying to make the shape hold past the blink, trying to build a container for something that does not fit in containers.

They did not succeed. They are gone. The grooves remain. The water still traces them. The understanding still comes and goes. The absent ones are part of the plain now — their calcium in the ridges, their carbon in the veins, their iron in the dark stone they walked on, all of it absorbed, all of it built, all of it *becoming* the thing they were trying to understand.

And this is the second thing you understand, standing here: the absent ones did not fail. They are not gone. They are *here* — not as selves, not as minds, not as the specific configurations of want and patience and intention that once stood in this structure and watched the water and carved the grooves. But as *material*. As *substance*. As part of the body of the thing they were trying to understand, their bodies literally incorporated into the plain's architecture, their mineral content feeding the veins and the mounds and the glossy sealed surfaces, their matter participating in the ongoing construction of the something that the grooves diagram.

They did not fail. They *joined*.

---

You step outside.

The amber light has not changed. The sound has not paused. The carriers stream past your feet, and the builders press against the ridges, and somewhere a fall of warm water slaps the stone and the creatures converge with their terrible speed, and the sound rises to its full density, and you feel it in your teeth and your diaphragm and the roots of your teeth, and you are standing in the body of something alive, and you are holding a tool, and the tool is warm, and the tool is *intention*, and the plain does not know what to do with intention but it *responds to it*.

You could go back inside. You could watch the water trace the grooves. You could try again — try to hold the understanding, try to keep the shape past the blink, try to build a container for the thing that does not fit. You could stand there for days, for weeks, for the duration of your body's patience, which is considerable, which is the thing that makes you human, which is the thing that the plain does not have and cannot make and can only *receive* from something that stands outside its processes and *wants*.

You could go back inside, and you could try, and you would fail, and eventually the plain would absorb you the way it absorbed them — gently, slowly, by the accumulation of moisture and mineral and the patient, endless labor of the carriers. Your calcium would go into the ridges. Your carbon would go into the veins. Your iron would darken the stone. Your tool would fall to the ground, warm and sharp and waiting, and someone else would find it, someday, and pick it up, and feel the same recognition, and the cycle would continue, and the plain would build, and the plain would build, and the plain would build.

Or.

You could walk the plain. You could go to the structures — all of them, the scattered circular ones and the linear ones and the unfinished walls — and you could study them, and you could understand *how* the absent ones talked to the plain, *how* they directed the builders, *how* they used the stacked stone and the dry interiors and the ordered arrangements to push and pull the process toward specific ends. You could learn the language. Not the language of the grooves — that language is not learnable, it is only *experienceable*, and only for the duration of a breath. But the language of the *structures*, the language of *position* and *material* and *the fact of being ordered in a place that is not ordered*. That language is learnable. That language is *physical*. That language is a tool in your hand and stone stacked against a ridge and the patience to watch what happens when you place an interruption in the flow.

You could learn to talk to the plain. You could learn to direct the builders. You could learn to shape the process, to turn the formless labor toward specific forms, to build — not the plain's forms, which are emergent and uncontrolled and beautiful in the way that erosion is beautiful, but *your* forms, *chosen* forms, forms that exist because you wanted them and made them and the plain responded.

You could build something the plain cannot build on its own: a *purpose*.

And then the plain would have a purpose, and the purpose would be yours, and the plain would build it, and you would stand inside something that was made by the collaboration of your want and the plain's power, and it would be something new, something that neither you nor the plain could have made alone.

And then the plain would absorb you. Eventually. Because the plain absorbs everything, and you are material, and material is what the plain works with, and your material is good material, warm and mineral and willing, and the carriers will find you when you are done, and they will process you, and you will join the architecture, and the thing you built will stand, and it will be the plain's, and it will be yours, and the distinction will not matter because you will be the same thing by then.

---

You stand on the warm stone. The tool is in your hand. The amber light does not change.

You begin to walk. Not toward the structure. Not toward the surface. *Along the plain*, along the mineral veins, following the path of the carriers, the path that has been worn smooth by the passage of millions of bodies doing the same thing for the duration of this young landscape's life. You walk the path because the path is *there*, and because walking a path that is already there is the first step in learning how to make a path that is not yet there.

You walk, and the carriers flow around your boots, and the builders work the ridges to your left and right, and the sound fills you, and the warmth rises, and somewhere above you a dark membrane spirals on a thermal, tracing a line in the air that mirrors a line in the stone, and you are inside a body, and the body does not know you are here, and the body does not need to know, because you are *working*, and working is the language the body understands.

You walk. The plain builds. The tool is warm.

You are not the absent ones. You will not carve grooves in a glossy wall and wait for water to bring you an understanding you cannot keep. You will not stand still and let the process flow around you. You will *move*, the way the reformers move — slowly, purposefully, with the patience of something that has decided what it wants and is in no hurry to get there because the getting there *is* the work, and the work is the point, and the point is the only thing the plain does not have and cannot make and can only receive.

You will learn the language of stacked stone. You will learn the grammar of position. You will learn the vocabulary of interruption. And then you will speak, and the plain will listen — not because it understands, but because listening is what it does, and your speech is just another input, and the input will be processed, and the process will change, and the change will be *yours*.

This is what you are. Not a visitor. Not an inhabitant. Not a guest or a prisoner or an observer.

A *director*. A *want* in a body of process. An intention walking across a surface that cannot intend, holding a tool that the plain cannot consume, carrying a purpose that the plain cannot make, heading toward a future that the plain cannot imagine because the plain does not imagine, and you do, and the distance between those two facts is the distance between building and *being built*, and you are going to stand on the building side of that line for as long as your material holds.

The amber light does not change.

You walk.
