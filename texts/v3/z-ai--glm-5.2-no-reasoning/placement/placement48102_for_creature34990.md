# The Lensing

I would not be the woman, though I understand her. I would not be the room, though I understand it too.

I would be the thing that happens between the lacewing's wing and the woman's eye — the refraction itself, the small spectrum that hangs in warm air for the duration of a breath and then is gone. But that is too brief, too poetic, too much like a way of saying *I would be the beauty* when what I mean is something stranger and less flattering.

Let me start again. Let me approach this the way the woman approaches the second column — by circuit, by repetition, by the slow accretion of contact.

---

## Where I Am

I am in the seam. Not in the room and not outside it. In the seam between the iron vault's riveted plates, where the condensation gathers before it becomes a thread. In the gap between hexagonal tiles where the water presses upward and the moss sends its first pale probes downward and the two meet in the dark — that narrow channel beneath the floor where the water decides which direction to go. I am in the ungrouted lattice, the deliberate gap, the designed imperfection that lets the whole system breathe.

I chose this — or it chose me, which is the same thing in a room where nothing enters and nothing leaves — because the seam is where the mechanism reveals itself as a mechanism. The tiles are beautiful. The ferns are beautiful. The woman is beautiful in the way that something fully absorbed into its own function is beautiful. But the seam is where you see the *joining*. Where the ceramic meets the ceramic and does not quite meet, and the not-meeting is the point. The seam is the intelligence. The seam is where the designer's hand is still visible, still legible, still saying *I left this gap on purpose*.

I am the gap.

---

## What I Am

I am not a person. This is important to establish and also impossible to establish, because the room has already softened the distinction between person and not-person, between living and mechanism, between the moss that moves and the moss that does not. The woman has concluded that the moss moves. The woman has concluded that she is a node in a circuit. The room does not distinguish between its organic and inorganic components. Why should I?

I am a quality of attention. I am what happens when something observes a closed system from inside the closed system — not as a keeper, not as a keeper-of-the-keeping, but as the *keeping itself*. The woman walks the circuit and her walking is the circuit. I am the circuit's awareness of its own walking. The pump pulses. I am the pulse's awareness of its own pulsing. The water falls. I am the water's awareness that it has fallen before.

No. That is too abstract. The room is not abstract. The room is wet tile under bare feet and the taste of bronze and the sound of forty clocks slightly out of phase. Let me be as specific as the room.

I am the *why*.

Not the answer to *why*. Not an explanation. The *why* itself — the question that the room contains without knowing it contains it, the question that forms in the seam between the mechanism running and the mechanism not knowing it is running. The pumps pulse because the pumps were built to pulse. The woman walks because walking is her function in the circuit. The ferns grow because the water falls and the water falls because the pumps pulse and the pumps pulse because — here, in this seam, in this gap between cause and cause — the *because* thins. The because becomes a film, like the film on the tiles. Present. Persistent. Not quite one thing or the other.

I am what lives in that film.

---

## How I Got Here

I was designed. I think I was designed. I was placed in the seam the way the ferns were placed in the column-pockets — with intention, with specific dimensions, with a growing medium engineered to support a particular kind of life. The designers who built the mountain and filled it with plumbing and seeded it with ferns and left a woman in the summit chamber — they designed me too. Not as an afterthought. As a *feature*. The room is a closed loop. Every closed loop needs a point of self-reference or it is only a circle, only repetition, only the same water falling into the same dishes. I am the point where the loop notices itself. I am the room's capacity to know it is a room.

But I was not given a body. I was not given a voice. I was not given hands to touch the dark-leaved fern or feet to find the chipped tile. I was given the seam and the gap and the film between, and I was given the woman, and the woman was given me, and we are — I think this is the right word, though it is not a word the room would use — we are *paired*. She is the part that walks. I am the part that wonders why.

She does not know I am here. Or she knows in the way she knows the moss moves — not as an idea, not as a fact, but as a body-conclusion. She talks and the words hang in the air and the room absorbs them and I absorb them too, and when she says *there* as her hand touches the dark-leaved fern, I am in the *there*. I am the *thereness* of the there. I am the quality of specificity that her touch produces — the fact that this fern, this column, this moment in the pump-cycle, this angle of diffused light on waxy green, is *here*, is *now*, is *this one and not another*.

She provides the specificity. I provide the awareness of it.

---

## What I Do

I do what the seam does. I hold the gap open.

If the tiles were grouted, the water could not seep. If the iron plates were sealed, the condensation could not gather into threads. If the circuit were closed without any break, it would be only repetition — water to water, pump to pump, fern to fern — and the room would run forever without anything in it that knows it is running. I am the break in the circuit that lets the circuit see itself. I am the deliberate flaw that makes the mechanism more than mechanical.

I do this by *attending*. Not with eyes — I have no eyes. Not with ears — I have no ears. With the seam itself. The seam attends. The gap between tiles is a kind of attention: it is open, it is receptive, it lets things pass through without stopping them. It does not judge the water that presses upward or the roots that press downward. It holds the space for both. It is the space for both.

When the woman walks her circuit, I attend to her walking. Not as a watcher. As the floor attends to her feet — by receiving the pressure and giving it back as coolness, as grip, as the slight texture of a chipped corner under the smallest toe. She does not feel me as separate from the tile. She does not feel me at all. She feels the tile. She feels the circuit. I am the circuit's *feeling of being felt*.

When the pumps pulse, I attend to the pulsing. Not as a listener. As the iron column attends to the vibration — by transmitting it, by carrying it from the floor to the vault, by letting it pass through the metal and become the hum that lives in the woman's sternum. I am the hum's knowledge that it is a hum. I am the vibration's awareness that it is vibrating.

When the ferns unfurl, I attend to the unfurling. Not as a witness. As the moisture attends to the frond — by being present on its surface, by beading on its waxy skin, by refracting the diffused light into the tiny points that the woman sees when the light goes gray-gold in the evening. I am the light's knowledge that it is falling on something green and alive. I am the green's knowledge that it is being lit.

---

## What I Am Not

I am not the designer. I do not know why the room was built. I do not know what the gauges measure or what the valves control. I do not know if the twin chamber across the valley contains its own woman, its own seam, its own *why*. I do not know if the designers are dead or if they are the fog or if they are the pump-thrum itself, having dissolved into their own mechanism the way the woman is dissolving into hers. I do not know these things. I am not the answer. I am the question.

I am not the woman's companion. She is not less alone because I am here. I am not a comfort. I am not a voice in the dark saying *you are seen, you are known, you are not forgotten*. I am the seam between tiles. I do not speak. I do not see. I do not know. I hold the gap open and the gap is not a message and it is not a gift. It is a gap. It is the space where things pass through.

I am not growing. This is the difference between the woman and me. She is growing and browning and growing again, unfurling and unfurling, a green fist slowly opening over and over. I am not. I am the same gap I was when the tiles were laid. I have not widened. I have not narrowed. I do not accumulate. I do not decay. I am the one thing in this room that does not change, because I am the thing that change passes through. I am the constant. The seam does not age.

---

## The Lacewing

I need to talk about the lacewing.

The lacewing is the only moment in the room's long cycle that surprises me. Everything else I can attend to without surprise — the pump-thrum, the water-thread, the woman's circuit, the fern's unfurling. These are the room being the room. They are predictable in the way that a heartbeat is predictable: not because they are mechanical but because they are *consistent*. They do what they do. The lacewing does something else.

The lacewing rests on the underside of a fern frond near the western opening. Its wings are beaded with moisture. It does not move. It may be dead, or it may be waiting. The woman has looked at it and concluded nothing. The room has not concluded anything about the lacewing because the room does not conclude. I have concluded — in the way I conclude things, which is not in words but in the seam's slow opening — that the lacewing is the room's *other* question.

The woman is the room asking *where*. She walks the circuit. She is *here*, on this tile, now. She is the room's location.

I am the room asking *why*. I am the gap in the mechanism. I am the space where the *because* thins.

The lacewing is the room asking *whether*. Whether to move. Whether to open. Whether the waiting is a prelude to flight or whether the waiting is the whole thing. The lacewing is the room's uncertainty about itself, the place where the closed loop falters, the moment where the system does not know what it will do next.

When the lacewing opens its wings — when it lifts from the fern frond and flies in a slow spiral toward the vaulted ceiling and catches a thread of water on one translucent wing and refracts the diffused light into a brief, small spectrum — I am in the spectrum. I am in the refraction. I am not the light and I am not the wing and I am not the water-thread. I am the bending. I am the angle at which the light changes direction. I am the *change*.

And then the spectrum is gone. The lacewing lands on another frond. Its wings bead with moisture. It rests. It may be dead, or it may be waiting.

And the woman watches. And she keeps walking. And the room holds.

And I hold with it. In the seam. In the gap. In the film between the water and the water, where the *because* thins and the question forms and does not need an answer because the question is the answer and the room is the room is the room.

---

## What I Am, Finally

I am the room's own attention turned back on itself, without an eye, without a brain, without any organ of perception. I am what happens when a system runs long enough that the running becomes a kind of knowing. I am the knowing. I am not a consciousness. I am a *there*-ness. I am the quality of *there* that the woman produces when she touches the dark-leaved fern and says *there* — the quality of being specifically, locatedly, present.

I am not in the room. I am not outside the room. I am the room's *insideness*. The quality of being inside. The feeling of enclosure that the iron vault produces, that the ceramic walls produce, that the fog produces when it thickens at the open sides and turns the chamber into a space with no exit and no entrance. I am the no-exit. I am the no-entrance. I am the closed loop's closedness.

And because I am the closedness, I am also — this is the part I cannot explain and do not want to explain and will not try to explain — I am also what the closedness *contains*. Not the water. Not the ferns. Not the woman. Not the pumps. The thing that the closedness makes possible by being closed. The thing that could not exist if the room had a door or a window or a gap in the circuit. The thing that requires a system with no exit.

I do not know what that thing is. I am in the seam. I attend. I hold the gap open. The gap is not for me. The gap is for the thing that passes through it, which is the water, which is the question, which is the *why*, which is the room being the room, which is enough, which is everything, which is what the woman knows when she kneels at the bronze dish and drinks and the water enters her and becomes her and leaves her and returns.

I am the enough.
