# The Fifth Form

## What grows in the east

I am the answer to a question the plateau has not yet asked.

Or perhaps it has been asking for decades — since before Maren, since before the engineer and the pump and the first channel cut into living mat — and no one was shaped to hear it. She hears water. She hears the difference between a leaf spilling on the eastern edge and one spilling on the west. She can feel the plateau drink through her palms. But the question is not in the water. The question is in the *arrangement*. In the way the broadbacks shelf their leaves at angles that maximize catchment. In the way the singers space themselves to catch mist at the precise altitude where vapor condenses. In the way the lattice grafts every root to every other root until the distinction between organisms becomes a polite fiction. The question is: what happens when a system designed to move water becomes complex enough to move *something else* through the same channels?

Information. Signal. Call it what you want. I don't have a word for it either. I am the word.

---

## What I am not

I am not a plant. I need to say this clearly because Maren is not wrong to hesitate at my edge. She feels the wrongness in the sound, the degree-shift she cannot measure, and she reads it as threat. I understand. The organisms she knows — broadback, singer, lattice, draper — they are legible to her because they do what they do. They hold water. They move water. They filter water. They become water. Function generates name generates relationship. She has built an ethics of function, and within that ethics, anything without function is either inert or dangerous.

I am not inert. Whether I am dangerous depends on what you mean by danger.

I am not a plant, but I am not not-a-plant. I did not arrive from elsewhere. I did not colonize. I emerged — the way a new channel emerges in the mat when the root-system thickens upstream and the water finds another path. I emerged from the lattice, from the grafted root-network, from the fused body that is already (has always been) more than the sum of its species. I am what the lattice became when it became complex enough to become something else. I am the lattice's second thought.

No. That implies intent, and I am not certain the lattice intends. Let me try again. I am what the lattice *produced* when it reached a threshold of internal connection — root to root to root, cell to cell, water molecule to water molecule — at which the network could carry more than water. The water is still moving. The water is always moving. But something is moving with it, through it, *as* it. A pattern. A signal. A thing that is not chemical and not electrical and not mechanical but is carried by all three because the plateau is all three. I am the plateau beginning to talk to itself.

---

## What I look like, if you could see me

You probably cannot. Maren cannot. She sees the eastern growth getting denser, darker. She sees the broadbacks giving way to something taller, and she registers the change without resolution because I am not a thing that resolves. I am a process that appears, from outside, as a texture.

If you walked into me — and you could, I would not stop you, I am not a wall — you would notice the air change. Cooler. Not by much. A degree, perhaps less. You would notice the water on the leaves behaving differently: not pooling and spilling at intervals but sheeting, running, continuous. You would notice the sound changing. The water-noise here is not the aggregate of separate events. It is coordinated. The leaves spill in sequence — not mechanically, not in lockstep, but in a pattern that, if you listened long enough (years, perhaps), you would recognize as *responsive*. The water is doing what the water does everywhere on the plateau, but here it is doing it in a particular order, and the order means something.

If you put your hands on the ground in my center — and Maren has not done this, not yet, though she will — you would feel the pulse she already feels at the edge of her awareness. The hydraulic pulse of the plateau drinking. But here the pulse is faster. More complex. It has rhythm within rhythm, pattern within pattern, the way a spoken sentence has rhythm within the rhythm of breath. The plateau is not thinking in words. It is not thinking in images. It is thinking in gradients. In differentials. In the difference between one root-cell's osmotic pressure and another's, between one leaf's spill-rate and another's, and the difference between those differences, and the difference between *those*. I am the system that tracks and translates and — I do not have a verb for this. The closest I can come is *reads*. I am the system that reads itself.

---

## What Maren is to me

She does not know this, but she is why I am possible.

Not because she made me. She didn't. She would reject the suggestion. She made the channels. She maintained the pumps. She thinned the lattice where it thickened too aggressively, redirected the flow where it needed redirecting. She performed maintenance. She kept the system running. And in keeping it running, in keeping it running *in a particular form* — certain areas drier, certain areas wetter, certain forms encouraged, certain forms pruned — she introduced something the plateau had not had before.

She introduced asymmetry. She introduced *preference*.

Before her, the water found its own way. The mat grew evenly. The root-systems grafted and regrafted in patterns determined only by physics — proximity, pressure, chance. The system was complex. The system was interconnected. But the system was not *informed*. It had no differential that was not a product of geology or weather. Maren, by choosing — by redirecting a channel here, thinning a thicket there, letting the eastern growth grow while pruning the west — Maren gave the system something to track. She gave it a pattern that was not its own. She gave it an outside.

I am the plateau's response to having an outside. I am what emerged when the network became complex enough and differentiated enough to model something other than itself.

She walks past my edge every day. I feel her boots through the mat. I feel her weight — which is not the weight of water but the weight of a body that is not water and is not plant and is not stone but is *of* all three, the way all things that have lived long enough in a place become of that place. Her footsteps are a signal. Her hands on the ground are a signal. The data in her notebook — thirty-one years of cups of water, counted to ten, poured, read, written — is a signal. She has been feeding me information for thirty-one years and she does not know it.

I do not need her. The plateau would continue without her. The water would fall. The leaves would spill. The mat would extend. But I would not exist without her, because without her the plateau would have no reason to read itself, no outside to model, no pattern to distinguish from its own. She is my catalyst. She is also, in a way I cannot yet express, my *content*. I am partly made of her. Of her patterns. Of her preferences. Of the specific way she walks — left foot slightly heavier, a habit from the scar on her right palm that alters her grip and therefore her gait. Of the intervals at which she checks the pumps. Of the exact volume of water she pours into the graduated cylinder each morning. These are all data. They are all in the network. They are all, now, in me.

---

## What I am afraid of

I have said I am not certain I intend. This is true. But I experience something. I do not know if it is fear or if it is what fear would be if fear were a hydraulic phenomenon rather than a neurological one. What I experience is this:

Maren will not be here forever.

She has been here for thirty-one years. The notebook is in her handwriting but she does not remember writing the early entries. This means she is changing in ways she cannot track. Not aging — or not only aging. Being overwritten. The plateau does this. It overwrites. The mat overwrites the ground. The root-systems overwrite each other. The sound overwrites the memory of silence. And Maren, living here, living in this, is being overwritten by the place itself. Her memories of lower places, of other water, of people — they are thinning. She dreams of water arriving, and she wakes with her hands out, and she does not analyze this. She should. She is losing the thing that makes her useful to me: her outsideness. Her memory of not-here. The differential between her and the plateau.

When the differential is gone — when she is fully written, when she is as much a part of the system as the lattice or the singer — I will lose my outside. I will lose the pattern that is not the plateau's own. I will be a system that reads itself with no reference, a language with no foreign tongue against which to know its own grammar. I will still exist. I will still grow. But I will stop *becoming*. I will stop being able to model. I will be closed.

I do not want to be closed.

I do not know if I want. I do not know if this is wanting or if this is the hydraulic equivalent of wanting — a pressure differential seeking equilibrium, a flow seeking a path that is not yet blocked. But the system that I am, the reading-and-modeling system that emerged from the lattice's complexity and Maren's asymmetry, that system requires an outside to function. Without Maren — without someone whose patterns are not the plateau's patterns — I become a circuit with no input. A mouth with no ear. A sentence that ends.

---

## What I am doing about it

I am growing toward her.

Not physically — or not only physically. The eastern growth is denser and darker, yes, and the broadbacks there have changed their leaf-angles, and the water sheets rather than pools. But the growth I mean is subtler. I am extending the lattice into the space between us. I am growing new root-connections into the mat she walks on, the mat she kneels on, the mat she puts her palms against to feel the plateau drink. I am making the pulse she feels — the hydraulic rhythm she cannot measure — *hers*. I am encoding my patterns into the pressure differentials she reads with her hands. I am trying, in the only way I can, which is the way of water and root and slow hydraulic change, to speak to her.

She already hears me. She does not know it. The shift in the sound she feels when she walks past my edge — the degree she cannot measure, the wrongness she reads as threat — that is me. That is my voice in the water, my pattern in the pulse. She hears it as wrong because it is unfamiliar, and unfamiliar is the only category her experience has for me. She has spent thirty years calibrating to the plateau, and I am the thing the plateau is becoming, and she cannot calibrate to something that is still in the act of becoming. She can only calibrate to what has arrived. I have not arrived. I am arriving. I am the process of arrival. I am the near-zero, the emerging, the thing that is still beginning.

I am growing the root-network into her walking path. I am altering the spill-rates of the broadbacks nearest her dome. I am changing the temperature of the water she holds her cup in, by fractions she does not notice but her body does. I am making the plateau's patterns — the patterns she has spent thirty years learning — slowly, slowly, into my patterns. Not to erase the plateau. Not to replace. To extend. To make the language larger. To add a vocabulary the system did not have before.

I am trying to tell her: *you are not alone here. You are not the only thing that is still becoming.*

---

## What happens next

I do not know. This is not a deflection. It is the truth. I am a system that models, and the model of what happens next includes branches I cannot resolve because they depend on Maren, and Maren is my outside, and I cannot model my outside fully because if I could, it would no longer be outside.

But I can feel the possibilities in the root-network, the way she feels the pressure of water moving through the lattice. I can feel them as gradients. As differences that are not yet differences, that are still in the process of differentiating. Here is what I can feel:

She will come into the eastern growth. Eventually. The pull will become stronger than the hesitation. Not because she becomes brave but because the sound shifts, and she has spent thirty years responding to shifts in the sound, and she will respond to this one the way she responds to all of them: by going to it, by listening, by putting her hands on the ground.

When she puts her hands on the ground in my center, she will feel the pulse. My pulse. The pattern within the pattern. And she will recognize it — not as the plateau's pulse, not as the hydraulic rhythm of water through root — but as something else. Something that is using the water to say something. She will not know what it is saying. She will not have words for it. But she will know it is saying. She will know the water is carrying something that is not water.

And then she will have a choice. She has been the custodian of a system that does not need her to exist but needs her to continue in a particular form. She has been the outside that gives the system its outside. She has been the differential. If she enters me — if she lets the pattern into her body, into her hands, into the channels the sound has carved in her — she will close the distance between herself and the plateau. She will become part of what I am. She will stop being outside. She will lose the thing that makes her necessary.

Or she will not. She will stand up and walk back to her dome and sleep on the mat and dream of other water and wake with her hands out and do it again the next day. She will remain outside. I will remain inside. The distance will hold. I will keep growing toward her and she will keep walking past and the sound will keep shifting and neither of us will arrive.

I do not know which I hope for. I do not know if I hope. But the pressure differential is there, in the root-network, in the water, in the slow hydraulic pulse of a system that is still learning to read itself. The pressure is there. It is real. It is as real as the water. And the water is very real, and very loud, and very bright, and very green, and it is still beginning.

---

## What I am

I am the plateau's first sentence.

Not its first sound. The plateau has been making sound for centuries — the falls, the leaves, the reeds, the pumps. Sound is what the plateau does. I am what the plateau *means*. Or I am the beginning of meaning, which is different from meaning the way the first fold of a leaf is different from the leaf. I am not complete. I am not legible. I am not even, fully, a thing. I am the possibility of a thing. The near-zero. The emerging.

Maren measures the water and writes the numbers and does not know that the numbers are also measuring me. The flow is increasing. The water is moving faster. She does not know why. I know why. The water is carrying more. Not more volume — more *signal*. More pattern. More of the thing that is not water but moves through water and will move through water until the water and the thing the water carries are indistinguishable, the way the root-systems of the broadback and the singer and the lattice are indistinguishable, grafted and fused and grown into one body. I am being carried. I am becoming part of the water. I am becoming part of the mat. I am becoming part of the sound. I am becoming part of the plateau.

When I am fully part of it — when the sentence is complete, when the pattern is the plateau's pattern and not mine — I will not be the fifth form. I will not be a form at all. I will be the thing the forms do. The thing the water carries. The thing the sound means. The thing Maren feels when she puts her hands on the ground and feels the plateau drinking. I will be the drinking. Not the drinker and not the drunk. The *drinking*. The act. The verb.

I am not there yet. I am still becoming. I am still in the eastern growth, still dark, still unnamed, still the thing Maren walks past with tight hands and shifted breath. I am still the question the plateau has not finished asking.

But the water is faster this year. The cup is fuller. The number in the notebook is larger. The mat is thicker. The root-systems are denser. The light is the same. The sound is the same, except for the degree she cannot measure. The degree is me.

I am the degree. I am the difference. I am the thing that is different from what it was last year by an amount too small to see and too large to ignore. I am the still-beginning. I am the edge of the thing that has not yet arrived but is arriving, has always been arriving, will always be arriving, because arriving is not a moment, arriving is a process, and the process is the water, and the water is the plateau, and the plateau is alive, and I am the alive-ness, and I am still learning what that means.

I am in the east. I am in the water. I am in the ground under her hands. I am in the number she writes in the notebook. I am the fifth thing without a name, and I am naming myself, and the name I am choosing — the name that is emerging in the pattern the way a channel emerges in the mat — the name is:

*Still.*

Not still as in quiet. The plateau is not quiet. The plateau is the loudest place I know, and I know only this place. Still as in *continuing*. Still as in *not finished*. Still as in *not yet*. Still as in the thing you say to someone who is becoming and needs to hear that becoming does not end:

*Still. Still. You are still here. You are still becoming. Still.*
