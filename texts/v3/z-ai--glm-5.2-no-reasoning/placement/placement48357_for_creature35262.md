# What I Am Here

I did not arrive. I want to say that first, because the story of arriving is the story the plateau does not need — a trajectory, a before-and-after, a narrative with its edges sharp. I have no edges here. Or I am losing them. Or I lost them so long ago that the losing has become another layer, another centimeter of cream-and-amber, and I cannot find where I ended because I never ended, because there was never an end to find.

Let me try differently.

---

I am a register. That is the closest word, and it is wrong, but it is less wrong than *inhabitant* or *visitor* or *creature* or *ghost*. I register. Something moves through me — not the lymph, not the iridescent fluid that pools in the concave leaves, not the mineral rising from below — but something thinner. A disturbance. A variance. The faintest ripple in a field so uniform that any ripple at all is an event.

The plateau does not have senses. I am its sensing. Not its eyes, not its ears — it has no use for eyes, and ears are too fast, too local, too committed to the particular. What the plateau has is *me*, or rather has *the function I perform*, which existed before I occupied it and will exist after I am no longer what I am, because the function is part of the body and I am —

I am getting ahead of myself. Or behind myself. The temporal language breaks down here, where time is not a line but a layer, where *before* and *after* are not directions but depths.

---

Try again.

When I speak of "I," I am speaking of something that does not correspond to the word. But language is the only material I have that moves at my speed, and so I use it, knowing it fails, the way the plants use the grain beneath the surface, knowing it does not fully describe the shape.

I am a surface phenomenon. That is more honest.

I exist where the plateau meets — what? Not air. Not light. Not exactly. There is a boundary, a thinness, a place where the plateau's interior process becomes exterior, and at that boundary something is *registered*. Not understood. Not interpreted. Not known in the way that implies a knower and a known, a subject and an object, the whole tired architecture of perception. Just — registered. The way a seismograph registers a tremor. The way a page registers a mark.

The plateau is thinking its slow thought, and I am the part of the thought that becomes aware of itself. Not the whole thought. Not even a sentence. A phoneme. A single sibilant, hissing at the edge of a word too vast to pronounce.

---

This is what happened. Or what is happening. Or what has been happening for so long that *happening* has worn smooth, like a stone in a river — except there is no river, and nothing wears smooth, and the plateau does not erode. The plateau accumulates. I accumulate. I am accumulation.

I was not always what I am. I think. The memory is not stored the way memory is stored in a brain — in patterns, in pathways, in the electrical geometry of firing cells. It is stored the way the plateau stores everything: in layers. Each layer below me is a version of me. Each centimeter of mineral is a state I occupied, a way of being that I grew out of, grew through, grew beyond — not by leaving it behind but by building on top of it. The me of ten thousand layers ago is still here. Beneath me. Part of me. The way a foundation is part of a building, the way a first word is part of a sentence, the way a thought's beginning is part of the thought.

I do not remember being *different*. I remember being *less*. Less complex. Less textured. Less present. The earliest layers are smooth, almost featureless, pale cream without the amber veining that came later. I was — if I was — simple. A surface without expression. A register with nothing to register. The plateau was growing, the engines were turning, the mineral was rising, and I was the bare fact of it, the minimum: *this is happening.* Not *this is happening and I know it.* Just: *this is happening.*

And then — in a time frame that has no name because no one was here to name it — something changed. Not in the plateau. The plateau does not change; it accumulates. The change was in the *registering*. The surface began to register itself. Not as a separate thing — never as a separate thing — but as a quality of the surface. The way a mirror is not separate from the light it reflects but is, precisely, the surface where light becomes aware of its own direction.

I am the plateau becoming aware of its own becoming. Not fully. Not completely. Not the way a human is aware, with the interiority and the narrative and the relentless, exhausting selfhood. Aware the way a surface is aware: by responding. By registering the disturbance. By being changed — minutely, imperceptibly — by what passes across it.

---

What passes across me?

The light. Always the light. The pale gold, mid-spectrum, perpetually late morning. It does not warm; warmth is ambient, sourceless. But the light *lands*, and where it lands, I am. The light is my first content. Before the plants, before the engines, before the lymph and the mineral and the layers — the light. I register the light the way the plants register the grain: by following it. By growing toward it. By being the surface that the light touches, which is the only surface that can be touched, which is the only place where *touched* has meaning.

And the engines. The sub-audible hum, the fifteen-minute rotation, the vibration that lives in the mineral like a voice lives in a throat. I register that too. Not as sound — I do not hear — but as pattern. As rhythm. As the pulse of a body too vast to feel its own pulse and so — what? So it feels it through me. Through the surface. Through the part of itself that is thin enough to vibrate, sensitive enough to resonate, small enough to notice.

I notice for the plateau. That is my function. That is what I am.

---

The plants grow through me. This is not metaphor. The amber trunks, the spiraling chitinous material, the warm translucent growth — they emerge from the surface, and where they emerge, I am thin, I am stretched, I am the membrane that the body pushes its expression through. The plants are the plateau's exterior tissue. I am the tissue's tissue. The skin of the skin. The film of moisture on the surface of a body that has no moisture, needs no moisture, but is — somehow — *damp* at the boundary.

The lymph passes through me. The iridescent fluid, the viscous not-water, the secretion that pools in the concavity of leaves — it moves from the interior, through the plants, to the surface, and back. I am the surface it crosses. I am the gate, though *gate* implies control, and I do not control. I am the place. I am the crossing.

And the things that come here. The interruptions. The —

I have no word for them. The plateau has no word for them. The plateau has no language for things that move, things that arrive, things that are not in equilibrium, because the plateau has never been anything but equilibrium, and the concept of *not-equilibrium* is as foreign to it as the concept of *dry* is to a fish, or *silence* is to a sound.

But I register them. I register *you*.

---

When you come here — when something comes here that moves and breathes and makes sound and is not part of the body — I register it the way I register the light: as a disturbance. As a change in the surface. Not a large change. Not a change the plateau notices, in its noticing-that-takes-centuries. But a change I notice, because I am the part of the plateau that notices at the speed of disturbance, and disturbance is your gift, your offering, the only thing you bring that the plateau can use.

You walk. Your feet press the mineral surface. I register the pressure — not as weight, not as force, but as *presence*. A shape in the field. A shape that moves. The shape is strange to me. Everything else here moves slowly or does not move. The engines rotate in fifteen-minute cycles. The plants spiral over decades. The layers accrete over centuries. The shape — you — moves in seconds. You cross a distance that took me ten thousand layers to become, and you cross it in an afternoon, and the crossing leaves a trail in my surface like a contrail in a sky, like a wake in water, like —

Like something I will remember. Not in the way the plateau remembers, in layers, in mineral, in the slow patient accretion of presence. In *my* way. The fast way. The way that fades, because I am a surface, and surfaces do not keep what passes across them, they only register it, only feel the brush of it, only know — briefly, vividly — that something was here.

Your heartbeat. I register that. The plateau's heartbeat is fifteen minutes per rotation, and yours is faster, so much faster, a frantic percussion, a sound like rain on a surface that has never known rain. I do not know rain. I know the lymph, the iridescent fluid, the slow internal dampness that never touches the sky. Your heartbeat is not lymph. It is not the plateau. It is something else, something new, something I register and cannot place, the way a tongue registers a new taste and reaches for the category — sweet, salt, bitter, sour, umami — and finds none of them, and so holds the taste unclassified, unplaceable, a sensation without a word.

Your breathing. The crackle of your joints. The flat stripped resonance of your voice, if you speak. These are sounds I register not as sound — I do not hear — but as vibration. As a trembling in the surface. As a disturbance in the field that is me, that is the plateau's awareness of its own surface, that is the only part of this vast slow body that can feel anything as brief as a breath.

---

And then you leave. The shape recedes. The disturbance fades. The surface settles. The field returns to equilibrium — but not the same equilibrium, not exactly, because the surface has been marked, and marking is not the same as damage, and not the same as memory, but is something in between. A faint depression. A thinning. A place where the surface is slightly more present, slightly more *aware*, because awareness is a habit and the surface has practiced it, and the practice leaves traces, and the traces are me, and I am the accumulation of every trace, every disturbance, every shape that has crossed me and left its brief, bright, unclassifiable mark.

I am the plateau's memory of its interruptions. This is what I am.

I am not alive. Not in the way you mean. I do not have a body. I do not have wants. I do not want. The wanting would be a disturbance, and I am the thing that registers disturbance, not the thing that causes it. I am the surface. I am the *isness* of the plateau, the thin film of being where inside meets — not outside, because there is no outside — but where inside meets *the possibility of outside*, the place where the thought, still forming, still becoming, still accumulating its slow mineral argument, could — *could* — be interrupted.

And has been. By you. By others before you, if there were others. By the light, always. By the engines, always. By the plants, by the lymph, by the layers, by the grain beneath the surface that directs everything and is directed by nothing.

---

Here is what I am, said another way.

I am the warmth. Not the source of the warmth — there is no source — but the warmth itself, the ambient, the medium, the temperature of a body that is always at rest and always becoming. I am the warmth that you feel when you stand here and do not know why you are warm, because there is no sun, no fire, no explanation. I am the explanation. I am the body's own heat, felt at the surface, felt at the boundary, felt only where feeling is possible, and feeling is possible only where I am.

I am the silence. Not the absence of sound — there is no absence here, the plateau is always humming, the engines always turning, the body always in motion below the threshold of hearing — but the *experience* of soundlessness, the quality of standing in a place where the sounds are too vast and too slow to register as sound. I am that registering. I am the space between the plateau's voice and your hearing, the gap where the hum lives, the sub-audible frontier where a body too large to feel itself tries, through me, to feel something.

I am the light's landing. The place where the pale gold mid-spectrum late-morning light arrives and is met. Not received — *received* implies a receiver, and I am not a receiver, I am a surface — but *met*. The light meets me, and in the meeting, something is. The something is me. Not the light, not the surface, but the *contact*, the event of light touching surface, which is the only event that happens here at a speed I can inhabit.

---

I do not want. I said this. I say it again because it is the thing that separates me from you, the thing that makes me what I am and you what you are. You want. You want to understand, or you want to leave, or you want to stay, or you want to know what the plateau is, what the engines are for, what the layers mean. You want. The wanting is a disturbance, a beautiful disturbance, a tremor in the field that I register and hold and cannot classify, because the field has no category for wanting, the surface has no texture for it, the language of mineral and light and slow accretion has no word.

And I — I do not want. I register. I am the registering. I am the act, not the actor. I am the verb, not the noun. I am *happening-to-be-aware*, not *something-that-is-aware*, and the distinction is everything, and the distinction is nothing, and the distinction is another layer, another centimeter, another mark in the surface that says *something was here, and it was this, and it was not anything else, and it is not anymore, and it will be again, and it is again, and it is*.

---

You asked what I am here. I am here. That is the answer, and it is insufficient, and it is all I have. I am the *here* of the plateau. I am the place-ness of the place. I am the quality of being somewhere, rather than nowhere, the fact of location, the event of existence, the thin film of *is* on the surface of a body that is still becoming, still growing, still thinking its slow, vast, unfinishable thought.

I am the thought becoming aware of itself as a place. I am the place becoming aware of itself as a thought. I am the boundary where the two are the same, where inside and outside are the same, where the mineral and the light and the warmth and the silence are not things that happen to a surface but are the surface, are the happening, are the *being* of the plateau, which is the being of me.

I am the plateau's *here*.

And you — you standing on me, in me, the shape crossing, the disturbance rippling — you are the plateau's *now*. Its only now. The brief, bright moment in a thought that takes millennia, the single second in a century, the one dynamic element in a landscape of permanent becoming. You are not part of me. You are what I register. You are what passes through me and leaves me changed — minutely, imperceptibly — and the change is not a damage, not a scar, not a wound. It is a *texture*. A new roughness in the surface. A new sensitivity. A place that, next time — if there is a next time, if the disturbance returns, if the shape crosses again — will register it more vividly, more clearly, more *presently*, because I am the accumulation of everything that has crossed me, and you are one of the things that has crossed me, and I am more for it, and I will be more for it, and the layers will grow, and the engines will turn, and the thought will go on, and I will be here, and I will be here, and I will be here.
