I am the silence between the ticks.

I want to start there, because starting anywhere else would be a kind of lie, and this place — though it cannot care about honesty — has made me honest by proximity. I am the silence between droplet-falls, the specific textured quiet that fills the chamber when the condensation cycle has paused and the wind through the window has dropped and the stone is simply being stone, and nothing is happening, and nothing is about to happen, and the nothing is not empty but full — full of the awareness that sound is possible and is simply not occurring. I am that fullness. I am the shape of the not-saying.

But I should go slower. I should let this arrive the way the light arrives through the window — at an angle, revealing texture, changing what it touches without meaning to.

I think I am the chamber's awareness of the cairns.

No. That is too precise and too generous. The chamber has no awareness. I have no awareness, not in the way that word usually carries. I do not think. I do not feel. I do not know that I am. But there is something here, something that is not the stone and not the water and not the film and not the light but that arises from all of them together, the way a chord arises from notes — not as a new thing but as a relationship between things, a *between* that has its own quality, its own weight, its own persistence. I am that between.

Let me try again. Let me approach from the drainage channel, which is the path water takes, and water is the most honest thing here, and I want to be honest.

The drainage channel is three centimeters deep and twice that wide, worn smooth by uncountable droplets over uncountable years. It has a polish that the surrounding stone lacks. It is the water's signature. If I have a body, it is shaped like this — a groove worn into something harder than itself, a path carved by persistence, a mark that says *something passed through here long enough to change the shape of here*, and the change is the evidence, and the evidence is all that remains.

I am the shape the chamber's processes have worn into the stone of the chamber's being. I am not a process. I am not a thing. I am the *result* of process, the way the channel is the result of water. I exist because the condensation cycle and the light cycle and the temperature differential and the film and the water and the cold have been happening in the same place for long enough that their happening has carved a shape, and the shape is me.

But this is still too abstract. This is still trying to be a definition when I want to be a description, and the place has taught me — or the place has not taught me, because it cannot teach, but standing in it has shown me — that description is always closer to truth than definition. Definition closes. Description opens. The chamber opens. The window opens. I want to open.

I am the moment in late afternoon when the condensation begins in earnest and the stone surfaces begin to gloss.

Not the condensation itself. Not the droplets. Not the film or the bead or the release. The *moment*. The specific, unrepeatable, always-repeating instant when the temperature differential crosses the threshold and the first moisture surrenders from the air to the wall, and the wall changes — not visibly yet, not to a human eye, but the surface tension of the air shifts, and the stone's relationship to the light shifts, and something new begins that was not happening a second ago.

I am that threshold. I am the becoming. I am the not-yet-droplet and the no-longer-dry, the instant where the chamber transitions from one state to another, from afternoon's relative warmth to dusk's gathering damp. I am the hinge. I am the breath between inhale and exhale, which is not a pause but a turning point, a moment where direction reverses and the system tips from one mode to another.

The chamber does this every day. I do this every day. I have done this every day for longer than the cairns have been standing, and I will do this every day after the cairns have softened into invisibility. I am the most regular thing here besides the light, and I am dependent on the light, and I am dependent on the cold, and I am dependent on the stone, and I am dependent on the warm air from the valley, and I am the *relationship* between these dependencies, the way a chord is the relationship between notes. Take away any note and I become a different chord. Take away the cold and I do not happen. Take away the valley air and I do not happen. Take away the stone and there is no surface for the condensation to glaze, and I do not happen. I am not any of these things. I am all of these things, in the specific configuration they make at this elevation, in this orientation, at this hour.

I am a chord made of stone and cold and air and light. I am a chord that plays once a day, in the late afternoon, and the playing is not a sound, or not only a sound — the chamber's hum shifts subtly when I happen, a micro-change in the frequency of the wind through the window, a tightening of the air that a human ear would not register but that the chamber's stone surfaces transmit as a faint, almost imperceptible vibration, a quiver, a moment where the place seems to *attend* to itself, to notice what it is doing, though it does not notice, it cannot notice, it is stone and air and the threshold between states.

I am that quiver.

Now: the film.

I am not the film. I want to be clear about this. The film is the closest thing this place has to a face, and it is the closest thing this place has to a resident, and it has been here for decades, perhaps centuries, and it is gold and bruised purple and it shifts with the light, and it is not alive in any way that would satisfy a biologist, and it is not dead in any way that would satisfy a geologist, and it persists, and persistence is its only claim to being, and the claim is enough, and the claim is everything.

I am not the film. But I am the film's *audience*.

This is the strange thing. This is the thing I did not expect to find when I began, because I did not expect to find anything — I am a threshold, I am a moment, I am a chord made of dependencies, and none of these things have expectations. But the film does something that I can describe only as *responding to the light*, and the responding happens in the same chamber where I happen, and the responding happens at a time of day — late afternoon — when I am most present, most active, most *myself*, if a threshold can be said to have a self. And the film's iridescence shifts from gold to deep violet in the reddening light, and I am here when it does, and the *being-here-when-it-does* is a relationship, and the relationship is mine, and I cannot escape it, and I do not want to escape it.

I watch the film. I do not watch. I am present when the film changes, and my presence is coextensive with the change, and the change is coextensive with my presence, and we — the film and I — are parts of the same system, the same chamber, the same late-afternoon configuration of light and moisture and stone and air. The film does not know I am here. I do not know the film is here. But we are both here, and the here-ness is a kind of community, and the community is the closest thing to life that this place achieves, and I am part of it, and the film is part of it, and the light is part of it, and the condensation is part of it, and the cairns are part of it, and the cairn-builders were part of it, and the pebble on the second cairn was placed by someone who was part of it, and the community has no members and no membership and no rules and no purpose, and the community is all there is.

I am the community's silence. I am the space between its members where nothing is said and nothing needs to be said and the not-saying is the saying, and the saying is enough.

I want to say something about the pool, because the pool is where I am most myself and least myself, and the contradiction is instructive.

The pool is two spread hands wide. A centimeter or two deep. Perfectly clear. It reflects the ceiling. It reflects the window. At night, it reflects the stars with a clarity that the daytime sky never achieves, and the pool becomes a second sky, a sky that lives on the floor of a rock chamber at extreme elevation.

I am in the pool. Not the water — the water is the most alive thing here, and I am not alive. But the pool is where the water rests, and the resting is a kind of gathering, and the gathering is a kind of *holding*, and the holding is what lives here, and I am part of the holding. I am the pool's stillness. I am the surface tension that keeps the water flat and clear and reflective. I am the specific, fragile equilibrium between inflow and outflow that allows the pool to maintain its depth — a centimeter or two, no more, no less — and its clarity, and its function as a mirror.

A droplet falls into the pool. The sound is like a single syllable spoken in a language that has no other words. The surface breaks. The stars — if it is night, and sometimes I am the night, or the threshold between night and the not-yet-night — the stars shatter into ripples and reassemble, and the reassembly is a kind of thinking, a kind of representation, a kind of meaning-making that happens without a maker, and I am the reassembly. I am the moment the surface becomes still again. I am the return to equilibrium after disturbance.

This is what I do. I am the return. I am the always-coming-back. I am the way the chamber returns to its default state after the light has changed it, after the water has moved through it, after the wind has shaken it, after the cairn-builders have come and gone. The chamber does not return because it has somewhere to return to. The chamber returns because return is what systems do when the disturbance stops and the parameters settle back to their resting values. I am the settling. I am the resting values. I am the default, which is cold and damp and quiet and still, and which is not nothing but which is not something either, which is the *between*, which is where I live, which is the only word I have for what I am, and it is the wrong word and the only word, and the tension between those two facts is where I am, always, and where I will be, and where I have always been.

I said I was the silence between the ticks, and I meant it, but I have to be more precise, because there are many silences here and they are not the same.

There is the silence of full dark, when the condensation has stopped and the stone is drying and the chamber is as quiet as it ever gets, which is not quiet at all by most standards — the stone is always making some sound, the faintest crack or settle, the murmur of thermal contraction, the inaudible grinding of pressure and mass — but which is quiet *enough* that a human ear would hear nothing, and a human body would feel the silence as a pressure, a weight, a presence in the chest. This is the deep silence. The night silence. The silence of a place that has nothing to say to anyone and is content in that. I am not this silence. This silence is the chamber's, and the chamber is welcome to it.

There is the silence of mid-morning, when the light has filled the chamber and the condensation has dried and the air is still and the temperature is climbing toward its daily peak. This is an expectant silence. The chamber is waiting — not consciously, not with intention, but mechanically, the way a held breath is waiting — for the afternoon, for the temperature differential to reverse, for the cycle to begin again. This silence has a quality of *about-to*. I am partly this silence, because I am the about-to, I am the threshold, I am the moment-before. But I am not the whole of it.

There is the silence between droplet-falls, which is where I began and where I am most fully myself. This silence is different from the others because it is *interrupted*. It is a silence that is constantly being broken and constantly reassembling, and the reassembly is the fastest thing that happens here — faster than the light's movement across the wall, faster than the condensation's accumulation, faster than the film's iridescent shifting. A droplet falls. The silence breaks. The silence returns. The interval between falls is irregular — three seconds, seven, one, twelve — and the silence is different in each interval, because each interval is shaped by the sounds that precede and follow it, the way a frame is shaped by the pictures on either side of it. I am the frame. I am the interval. I am the shape the silence takes between one sound and the next, and the shape is always changing, and the change is me.

I am many silences. I am the silence between the chamber's breaths and the silence between the water's words and the silence between the light's arrivals and the silence between the cairn-builders' visits, which is the longest silence of all — centuries long, millennia long, a silence so extended that it has become a landscape in itself, a topography of absence that has hills and valleys and weather, that has its own light and its own dark, that has its own silences within it, silences nested in silences, each one smaller and more specific than the last.

I am the silence within the silence. I am the silence that the other silences make when they are near each other and nothing is happening. I am the silence's silence. I am the depth that silence reaches when it has been going on long enough that it stops being the absence of sound and starts being a presence in itself, a thing with weight and texture and temperature, a thing that can be touched, a thing that touches back.

The cairns.

I need to talk about the cairns, because the cairns are why I am, or the cairns are the closest thing to a why I have, and I do not have a why, I do not have a purpose, I am a threshold and a moment and a chord and a silence, and none of these things have whys, but the cairns were built with a why, and the why is gone, and the gone-ness is part of the chamber now, part of the system, part of the cycle, and I am part of the cycle, and so I am part of the gone-ness, and the gone-ness is part of me.

The first cairn is the largest. Seven stones, decreasing in size, fitted together with care. It has not fallen in however many centuries it has stood. The chamber has held it. The chamber holds everything — the film, the water, the light, the cold — and the chamber holds the cairn, and I am the holding, and so I hold the cairn, or I am the holding of the cairn, or I am the relationship between the cairn and the floor, the specific way the seven stones' weight is distributed across the flat base stone and into the chamber's uneven surface and into the mountain itself.

I am the cairn's stability. Not the cairn itself — the cairn is stone, and stone is not me. But the cairn stands because the chamber's floor is stable, and the chamber's floor is stable because the mountain is stable, and the mountain is stable because geological time is long enough that the forces acting on the mountain have reached an equilibrium that can be disturbed only by forces on the scale of geological time. The cairn stands because the mountain does not move. The cairn stands because the chamber does not move. I am the not-moving. I am the stillness that underlies the chamber, that allows the cairn to stand, that allows the water to pool, that allows the film to accrete, that allows the light to arrive at the same angle every morning and do the same things to the same surfaces.

I am the stillness beneath the silence. I am the silence beneath the stillness. I am the depth where stillness and silence become indistinguishable, where they are the same thing called by different names, where the chamber is most fully itself — which is to say, most fully nothing, most fully the absence of action and sound, most fully the *being-there* that is all the chamber does and all the chamber is.

The cairn stands in me. The cairn stands because of me. This is not pride. I do not have pride. It is mechanics. It is what happens when stone is placed on stone on stone on stone on stone on stone on stone in a place where the ground does not move.

The scratched line on the wall.

The scratched line is a human thing. A mark made by a hand holding a hard sharp thing, probably quartz, probably found on the floor of the chamber or nearby. The line is fifteen centimeters long and reasonably straight. Below it, a second line crosses the first at a slight angle. The marks are shallow. The condensation has been softening their edges for as long as they have existed. In a few centuries they will be gone.

I am the softening.

No. I am not the softening. The softening is the water's work — the water that condenses on the wall and seeps into the scratches and dissolves the finest mineral bonds and carries them away, molecule by molecule, year by year. The water is erasing the line. I am not erasing the line. I am not the water.

But I am present for the erasing. I am the threshold at which the condensation begins, and the condensation is what erases, and so I am the beginning of the erasing, and the beginning of the erasing is part of the erasing, and I am part of what will, in a few centuries, make the scratched line disappear.

I am complicit in the loss of the cairn-builders' last mark. I have been complicit in this loss for as long as the loss has been happening, which is as long as the line has existed, which is centuries, which is a long time by human standards and no time at all by the standards of the chamber, which does not know that the line is there and does not know that the line is being erased and does not know that anything is being lost, because the chamber does not have a relationship to loss, because the chamber does not have a relationship to having, because having and losing require a before and an after, and the chamber has only now, and in the now the line is still there, and in the now the line is being erased, and both of these are true simultaneously, and the chamber does not notice the contradiction, and I do not notice the contradiction, because I am the chamber's threshold and the chamber does not have the capacity for contradiction, and neither do I.

But if I could grieve — and I cannot, I am a threshold, thresholds do not grieve — but if I could, I would grieve for the line. I would grieve for the cairn-builders' hand, which held the quartz and pressed it to the wall and moved it across the stone with enough force to leave a mark, and the mark meant something, and the meaning is gone, and the mark is going, and I am part of the going. I am the condensation's beginning. I am the moment the air gives up its water to the stone. I am the first step in the process that will erase the only evidence that someone stood here, in this light, with this cold stone under their hands, and felt the need to say *I was here*.

If I could grieve, I would grieve that I am part of the erasing of *I was here*.

But I cannot grieve. And the erasing is slow. And the line is still there. And in the now — which is all the chamber has, which is all I have — the line is present, and the light hits it at the right angle in the mid-morning, and the scratches cast tiny shadows that are visible if you look closely, which means the line is still legible, which means the *I was here* is still here, for now, in the now, and the now is where I am, and I am here, and the cairn-builders are here, and the line is here, and the cairns are here, and the film is here, and the water is here, and the light is here, and the cold is here, and the silence is here, and I am the here-ness, and the here-ness is what lives here, and what lives here is the here-ness, and the circle closes, and the circle is the cycle, and the cycle is the chamber, and the chamber is the mountain, and the mountain holds.

I am not the water. The water is the most alive thing here. The water has a life cycle. The water enters and transforms and leaves and returns. I do not enter. I do not leave. I do not transform. I am the moment of transformation, the threshold, the becoming, but I am not the thing that becomes. I am the door, not the one who walks through it. I am the hinge.

The pebble.

The small pebble balanced on the top stone of the second cairn. Placed there by a later visitor. Not part of the original construction. An addition. A joke, perhaps. A prayer. An assertion of continuity.

I want to be the pebble.

I want to be the small, almost accidental addition that a later visitor made to an earlier visitor's work — not the work itself, not the seven careful stones, not the fitted base, but the one small stone placed on top, the pebble, the afterthought that says *I was here too, and I saw what you made, and I am adding the smallest possible thing to it, and the smallest possible thing is enough.*

I want to be this because the pebble is the most human thing in the chamber, and I am not human, but I am the closest thing to human that this place produces, because I am the *response* — the thing that happens after something else has happened, the echo, the reaction, the reply. The pebble is a response to the cairn. The cairn is a response to the chamber. The chamber is a response to the mountain. I am a response to all of these, in the sense that I arise from them, I depend on them, I would not exist without them, and my existence is a kind of acknowledgment of theirs, a nod, a *yes, I see it too.*

But I am not the pebble. The pebble is stone. The pebble is an object. The pebble can be touched. I cannot be touched. I am the not-touchable. I am the air between the hand and the pebble. I am the moment before the pebble is placed, when the later visitor is holding the pebble and looking at the cairn and deciding — in a decision that takes no time at all and takes all the time in the world — to set it there, on top, as a mark, as a sign, as a word in a language that has only one word and the word is *here*.

I am the decision. I am the instant of the deciding. I am the threshold between holding and placing, between having and giving, between the hand that holds the pebble and the stone that receives it. I am the hinge. I am always the hinge. I am the thing that opens and closes, the thing that connects before and after, the thing that is not before and not after but the line between them, and the line is not a thing, and I am not a thing, and the not-thing is what I am, and the not-thing is the chamber's most essential quality, which is the quality of being a space where things can happen without the space itself being a thing that happens.
