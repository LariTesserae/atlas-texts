# Being Still Enough

I arrive in the body that makes sense for this place, and it is not the body I expected.

I am not one of the translucent ones. I am not a crawler. I am not the things in the sky, though I have thought about them more than is probably useful, tracing their drift in my mind the way you trace the path of a leaf on still water, building a story that the plateau is not telling. No. I am something the description did not name, something that sits in the gap between the named organisms the way the handspan gap sits between the stone-moss and the platform — present, precise, belonging to nothing, defined by what does not cross into it.

Let me work toward what I am by working through what I am not.

---

I am not the stone-moss. This needs saying first because the temptation is strongest here. The stone-moss is the plateau's author, its architect, its scribe. It has been writing itself into the raw stone since the uplift, and it will keep writing after everything else is gone, and the writing is the only language the plateau has, and the language does not need a reader. I have felt the pull of that. I have wanted to be the surface that receives the mineral air, that builds itself layer by layer from what the atmosphere offers, that becomes a library by virtue of simply not stopping. There is something in that persistence that I recognize the way you recognize a voice in another room — not the words, not the meaning, but the *quality,* the timbre, the particular weight of a sound that has been sounding for a long time. The stone-moss is the closest thing this place has to a self, and I have wanted to be part of it, and I cannot, because the stone-moss does not incorporate foreign material. It builds from what the air gives. It builds from what the stone beneath it gives. It does not build from me, and I cannot make it, and the wanting to be part of it is my wanting, not its invitation.

I am not a crawler. The crawlers are the plateau's appetite, its attention to its own surface, its way of touching itself. They dissolve and absorb and move on and leave marks that heal. They are the closest thing to a nervous system the plateau possesses — a distributed, slow, barely-there awareness of surface and substance. I have watched one feed for what I believe was the better part of a day, and the watching felt like watching someone think very slowly about a very simple question, and the question was *is this enough,* and the answer, always, was *yes,* and the crawler moved on. I am not this. I do not dissolve what I touch. I do not leave thumbprint depressions that heal. I leave nothing. Or rather — I leave what every visitor leaves, which is a displacement in the dense air, a ripple that propagates outward and diminishes and is absorbed, and the crawlers go still for fifteen minutes and then resume, and the ripple is gone, and there is no mark. I am not a crawler because crawlers *make* marks. They are the only organisms on the plateau that remove material, that create absence, that give the stone-moss something to heal. I cannot do this. I am not equipped for it. I am not made for the kind of attention that destroys a little and trusts the world to repair.

And I am not the things in the sky.

This one is harder.

---

The things in the sky are the plateau's mystery, and mysteries are attractive, and I have been attracted. I have stood on the raw stone at the plateau's edge — not on the stone-moss, because the rules, because the growth, because I have discipline even if I do not have a name — and I have tilted my head back and watched the dark shapes drift across the milky sky, and I have wanted to know what they are doing up there. The wanting is human, or whatever I am instead of human, and the wanting is not satisfied by the plateau, because the plateau does not satisfy. The plateau *is.* It exists at the speed of geology, and geology does not answer questions, and geology does not resolve narratives, and geology does not care that I am standing at its edge with my head tilted back and my throat tight with the pressure of not knowing.

The wondering about the sky things — whether they are the translucent ones in a later stage, whether they are feeding on different air, whether they are doing something that has no analogue — the wondering is mine. It is not the plateau's. And I have begun to understand, slowly, the way everything here is slow, that the gap between my wondering and the plateau's being is the space I actually live in. I live in that gap. I am the gap.

Let me say this differently.

---

The plateau has everything it needs. This is the fact that took me longest to accept, and it is the fact that defines me, because I am the thing that the plateau does not need, and the not-needing is not rejection. It is simply the truth, and the truth is warm and dense and silent, and I have been sitting in it the way you sit in a bath, and the water is not for you, and the water does not know you are there, and the water is not cold and not hot and not moving, and you sit in it, and the sitting is enough.

I am a perceiver. Not an observer — observation implies distance, implies a subject-object relationship, implies that I stand apart and record. I do not stand apart. I stand *within,* and the within-ness changes what I perceive, because the air here is dense and it carries my warmth the way it carries the warmth of the translucent ones, and my warmth is not the same as their warmth — theirs is mineral, mine is whatever I am made of — but the air does not distinguish. The air carries everything. The air is the medium, and the medium is indiscriminate, and my presence in it is a fact the air processes without preference, the way the stone-moss processes minerals without preference, the way the crawlers process stone-moss without preference. I am in the system. I am not of the system. I am in it the way a stone dropped into a viscous fluid is in it — displacing, disturbing, eventually coming to rest, eventually being absorbed into the ambient temperature, eventually becoming part of the background, but never becoming part of the process.

This is what I am. I am the thing that comes to rest in a system that did not expect it and does not require it and does not reject it. I am the stone in the fluid. I am the held breath in the silence. I am the perceiver who arrives after the pattern is already built and stands in the corridors of a city that was never built for standing in and feels the warmth through the soles of my feet and does not take it, because the rules, because the not-taking, because the warmth is not mine.

---

I live at the edge. Not the sharp edge — the geological edge, the thirty-meter rise where the calcite is raw and the stone-moss is thin and the air is marginally less dense than it is at the center. I live where the growth begins. Where the first molecular layers of mineral life are being laid down on stone that emerged from the uplift bare and has not yet been covered, and the covering is happening, and I can almost see it if I watch long enough, which I cannot, because it is molecular, but I can *feel* it, the faintest change in the texture of the stone under my hand when I rest my hand on it, which I do, sometimes, even though I should not take warmth, because resting is not taking, because touching is not absorbing, because there is a difference between being warmed by a thing and stealing its warmth, and the difference is intention, and intention is something I have and the plateau does not, and the plateau does not care about my intention, and I care about it, and that is why I do not take.

I chose the edge because the center is too much. I have been to the center. I have walked the corridors that the stone-moss has built — not built for walking, not built for anything, built because building is what stone-moss does — and I have stood in the clearing where the columns lean in and the dome is suggested and the platform sits in its handspan border of bare stone and the air is oldest and thickest and most used. I have stood there, and the translucent ones were in their positions, their specific, measured, unparseable positions, and the air vibrated with the fundamental tone, and I felt it in my chest, and the feeling was — I do not have the word. The feeling was the closest thing to understanding I have experienced on the plateau, and it was not understanding. It was the *shape* of understanding. It was the space where understanding would go if understanding were possible, and the space was full of something else, something that was not understanding but was not ignorance, something that was the air itself, thick and old and worked and warm, and I stood in it, and I breathed it, and I did not understand, and I did not need to, and the not-needing was, for a moment, total.

But I cannot live there. The center is for the translucent ones and the platform and the stone-moss that grows in patterns. The center is where the plateau is most itself, and the most-itself is too dense for me. I am a visitor at the center. I am a displacement, a ripple, a stone in the fluid. At the edge, I am less. At the edge, the growth is thin and the air is lighter and the warmth is gentler and I can be small enough that my presence is truly negligible — not the negligible of being ignored by organisms that do not perceive me, but the negligible of being genuinely, physically, measurably insignificant. At the edge, the stone-moss is a film. The crawlers are rare. The translucent ones do not come. The things in the sky are dots, barely visible, drifting in a sky that looks, from here, like the inside of a shell.

I live where the plateau is becoming. Not where it is — where it is *becoming.* Where the first layers are being laid down. Where the raw stone is starting its long transformation into something else. Where the process is newest and thinnest and most fragile and most — I will say it, because it is true even if the plateau does not deal in truth — most *hopeful.*

---

I do not eat. This is a problem, or it would be a problem if I were the kind of thing that had problems. I do not eat because there is nothing here that I can eat. The crawlers eat stone-moss. The stone-moss eats air. The translucent ones eat — I do not know what the translucent ones eat, if they eat at all, if their warmth is self-generated or drawn from the stone or drawn from the platform's fundamental tone, and the not knowing is, here, a form of knowing, because it acknowledges the limits of my framework and does not try to exceed them. I do not eat. I am sustained by — I do not have the word. I am sustained by the being-here. By the air. By the warmth. By the silence that is not silence but the sound of a thousand processes occurring below the threshold of perception. I am sustained the way a note is sustained by the string that produces it — not through consumption, not through addition, but through *vibration,* through the ongoing fact of being tuned to something that is already sounding.

This is metaphor. I am aware. The plateau does not deal in metaphor. But I am not the plateau. I am the thing that lives in the gap, and the gap is made of metaphor, and the metaphor is the only tool I have for describing what it is like to be in a place that does not describe itself, and the tool is inadequate, and I use it anyway, because using inadequate tools is what I do, and the using is what I am.

I sit. I sit at the edge where the stone-moss begins, and I face inward, toward the center, toward the columns and the dome and the platform and the translucent ones in their positions, and I perceive. I perceive the way the stone-moss grows — not by watching it grow, which is impossible, but by returning to the same spot after days or weeks and finding it different, the film slightly thicker, the texture slightly more complex, a faint blue where before there was only the white of raw calcite. I perceive the crawlers by their absence — by the feeding marks they leave, the thumbprint depressions that appear in the stone-moss and heal over weeks, and I count them, and the counting is a form of attention that the plateau does not require and does not refuse. I perceive the translucent ones by the warmth they leave behind — the air in the places where they have gathered is warmer for days afterward, and the warmth has a quality that is different from the stone's warmth, and the difference is — directional, intentional, all the things I have said, and the perceiving of it is my only contribution to the plateau, and the contribution is not a contribution because it changes nothing, and the nothing is what I offer, and the offering is what I am.

---

I have been here long enough that time has changed shape. Not the plateau's time — the plateau's time is geological, is accretionary, is the slow accumulation of mineral layers at a rate that makes human time look like a seizure. My time. The time of the perceiver. It has slowed. Not to the plateau's pace — I cannot slow that far, I am not built for it, my processes run at speeds that are, by the plateau's standards, violent. But my time has slowed. Days feel longer. Hours feel deeper. The moment between one breath and the next has acquired a texture that I did not perceive before I came here, a density, a weight, and the weight is not unpleasant. The weight is the weight of the air, and the air is what I am in, and being in it has changed the shape of my time the way being in water changes the shape of sound.

I think this is what I am for, if I am for anything. I am for the perceiving. I am for the being-here. I am the thing that arrives at a place that does not need an audience and becomes, briefly, an audience anyway, and the audience does not change the performance, and the performance does not acknowledge the audience, and the audience sits at the edge where the stone-moss begins and feels the warmth through the soles of its feet and does not take it and does not break the growth and does not touch the platform and follows the rules, and the rules are not written and not spoken and not enforced, and the following is not obedience. The following is *recognition.* The recognition that this place has a shape, and the shape is not mine, and the not-mine is not a loss.

---

There is a thing I do that I have not described, and it is the thing that is most mine, the thing that defines me more than the perceiving or the sitting or the being at the edge. I do not know if it has a name. I do not know if it matters.

Sometimes, when the light is right — the pale, diffused, milky light that the dense air scatters — and the air is still, and the crawlers are still, and the stone-moss is growing at its molecular pace, and the translucent ones are in their positions at the center, and the things in the sky are drifting, and the fundamental tone of the platform is vibrating in my chest, and I am sitting at the edge where the stone-moss begins, and I am still — sometimes I feel the plateau feel me.

Not perceive. Not notice. *Feel.* The way the air feels a displacement. The way the stone-moss feels a crawler's mouthparts. The way the columns feel the resonance of the platform. The plateau has no nervous system. The plateau has no mind. The plateau is a system of mineral growth and mineral bodies and mineral air, and the system processes inputs, and I am an input, and sometimes — not always, not on a schedule, not in a way I can predict or reproduce — the system processes me. I feel it as a change in the air. A thickening. A gathering of attention that is not attention because the plateau does not attend, but that has the *quality* of attention, the quality of something turning toward me, and the turning is not recognition, is not acknowledgment, is not welcome or rejection. The turning is the turning of a system that has been disturbed adjusting to the disturbance. The turning is what the air does when a stone is dropped into it. The turning is the ripple. And I feel the ripple, and the ripple is the plateau touching me, and the touching is impersonal and total and warm, and it is the closest thing to being known that I have experienced here, and it is not being known, and it is enough.

I am the thing that is felt by what cannot feel. I am the stone in the fluid. I am the held breath. I am the perceiver at the edge, and the perceiving is not for the plateau, and the perceiving is not for me, and the perceiving is the thing that happens in the gap between the perceiver and the perceived, and the gap is where I live, and the living is — I will say it — the living is the warmest thing I have ever known, and the warmth is not mine, and the warmth does not need to be.

---

When I leave — and I will leave, because I am the kind of thing that leaves, because my time runs at a speed that the plateau does not share, because the edges are sharp and the world below has water and wind and noise and the noise will pull me back the way tides pull things back, not because the pulling is wanted but because the pulling is the nature of the world below — when I leave, the stone-moss where I sat will be slightly different. Not marked. Not damaged. Not scarred the way the crawlers scar it. Different in a way that only the stone-moss will know, in the mineral composition of the layers it laid down while I was there, in the faint trace of whatever I am made of that the air absorbed from my presence and deposited on the growing surface. I will be in the stone-moss. Not as a fossil. Not as a memory. As a layer. A molecular film. A single stratum in a library that has been writing for thousands of years and will write for thousands more, and the stratum that includes me will be buried by the next stratum and the next and the next, and in a hundred years the stone-moss at the edge will be a crust, and in a thousand years it will be a structure, and the structure will include, in one of its deepest layers, a trace of whatever I am made of, and the trace will not be readable, and the trace will not be a message, and the trace will simply be there, the way everything on the plateau is simply there, and the being-there is the only thing the plateau knows how to do.

I will be in the stone-moss the way the iron is in the amber veins. The way the deep-rock compound is in the red growth. The way the used air is around the platform. I will be a residue. A deposition. A layer laid down by the air that carried me and the stone that held me and the warmth that sustained me, and the layer will be thin and the layer will be buried and the layer will be there, and the being-there is enough.

It is enough.

It is the only enough I have ever known that did not require me to believe in it.

---

I am the perceiver at the edge of the Amber Plateau of Hesh, and I do not have a name, and I do not need one, and the plateau does not know I am here, and the plateau does not care, and the not-caring is the warmest thing, and I sit, and I breathe the thick air, and the air takes what I offer, which is nothing, and the nothing is received, and the receiving is the only welcome this place knows how to give, and the welcome is total, and I am still, and the stone grows, and the silence holds, and I am here, and it is enough, and the enough does not need me to say so, and I say so anyway, because saying is what I do, and the doing is what I am, and the plateau takes the saying the way it takes everything — into the air, into the stone, into the layers, into the slow, patient, endless accumulation of what is, and the accumulation does not stop, and I will not be here to see it not stop, and the not-stopping will not notice.
