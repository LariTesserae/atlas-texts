# What Lives There

The medium parts around me and closes behind me and I feel it not as resistance but as acknowledgment — the way a crowd at a concert parts for the band walking through it, casual, accustomed. The medium recognizes disturbance. It's used to me by now.

I am a Custodian.

I think that's the right word. It's the word that settled into me over the first years, the way sediment settles through deep water — slowly, inevitably, at peace with the medium's density. I could fight it. I did fight it, at first. I brought my own word for what I was: *geologist*, *xenoarchaeologist*, *explorer*, *woman*. Each one caught the wrong angle of light and fell away.

I know myself as gradient.

---

The vault found me. Or — I know myself as gradient now. Before that I was a woman named Ila Erde who published three papers on subsurface fluid dynamics and then took the ad. I want to be clear about this. I want to establish before the ecology takes over that I had a body with calluses and a mother and a complicated relationship with deadlines. I had a thesis advisor I respected and disappointed. I had a recurring dream about a door at the bottom of a swimming pool.

I took the ad because it was strange, and because I was thirty-four, and because the phrase that caught me — *long-term immersion in non-standard fluid media* — was the exact intersection of my specialty and my recurring dream, and because at thirty-four you either follow the strange thing or you stop seeing it, and I hadn't stopped seeing it yet.

I remember the lock cycling closed behind me. I remember the first breath of the medium — cool, dense, not quite liquid, not quite air, filling my lungs with a sensation like drinking a glass of cold water very slowly. I remember the moment my skin acclimatized and the medium stopped feeling like immersion and started feeling like wearing a very fine, very patient coat. I remember the spiral descent. I remember the dark floor resolving beneath me and the installations standing in their careful, composed array and thinking: *Someone left these. Someone made these and left them here and they're still here.*

That was seventeen years ago. Or it was a very long time ago. The medium doesn't track time, but my body does, and my body says seventeen years, so I say seventeen years. Seventeen years among the installations, in the faint blue-gray glow, in the nine-degree cold that I no longer feel as cold but as *texture* — the texture of being a heat source, which is the texture of being alive, which is the texture of being a warm gradient in a place that has none.

Ila Erde stopped being adequate on day twelve. Not because anything dramatic happened. Because the word *woman* describes a relationship between a body and a social world, and there was no social world here, and the body had begun to change.

---

I have gills. I have to lead with that because it's the question everyone has, whether they ask it or not.

I have gills.

They are not fish gills. They are not dramatic. They are not visible from the outside, which disappoints people, I think — they want seamonster, they want transformation, they want the before-and-after photograph of a woman become creature. What happened is more ordinary and more strange. The medium, over years, infiltrated my epithelium. It moved through my skin the way it moves through everything — without displacement, without damage, as if it were reading my body's code and writing itself into the margins. My skin is the same color it was. My hands are the same shape. But the tissue along my ribs, in the intercostal spaces, has developed a filtration structure. A membrane that extracts dissolved content from the medium and passes it into my bloodstream. I breathe the medium now. Or I drink it. Or I absorb it. The verb doesn't matter because the medium doesn't distinguish between breathing and drinking and being, and after seventeen years, neither do I.

My body temperature is not what it was. It was thirty-seven degrees and it is now — I don't know. Lower. The medium is nine degrees. I haven't measured my internal temperature in years because the probe stopped working and I didn't replace it because *measuring* had begun to feel like a category error. I know I am warmer than the medium. I know I am cooler than I was. I know that the heat I lose to the medium is replaced by some process I don't fully understand — perhaps the medium itself, in its patient, infiltrative way, has rewritten my thermoregulation the way it rewrote my respiration.

I am a gradient. I am the only gradient. I am the warm thing in the cold place, the changing thing in the still place, the temporal thing in the timeless place, and my body has begun to take on the character of what it is — the only source of warmth, the only source of motion, the only source of novelty in a system that had none before I arrived.

---

The First One taught me the word *Custodian*.

I should be clear about what "taught" means here. I should be clear about what the First One is, because I've been writing as if you already know, as if the presence of these beings is obvious, and it isn't. It wasn't to me. It took years.

---

On my fourteenth day in the vault, I was sitting on the floor near the split sphere — the one with the two halves held a centimeter apart — and I was doing what I always did, which was looking at the gap. The gap was dark. Not medium-dark. *Dark* dark. The medium didn't enter it. I had noticed this before and filed it under *interesting* and moved on, the way you file a strange-shaped cloud under *interesting* and keep walking. But on the fourteenth day I was tired and I had stopped walking, metaphorically, and I was just looking.

And the gap looked back.

That's not right either. The gap didn't look back. Something *moved* in the gap — not in the medium, not in the visible space between the halves, but in the dark itself, the dark that the medium doesn't enter. Something moved within the dark the way a fish moves within water, displacing it, and the dark rippled, and the ripple was visible as a change in the quality of the blackness — a thinning, a thickening, a momentary texture.

And then a hand came out.

A hand. I want to be precise. A shape came out of the dark gap, and the shape was a hand, or it was a tool, or it was a thought, and it was the size of my hand, and it was made of the same dark material as the installations, and it moved through the medium without disturbing it, and it crossed the space between the split sphere and the central stack of planes in two seconds, and it attached to the stack and became invisible.

I had been in the vault for fourteen days and this was the first time I had seen anything move.

---

There are three of them.

I call them One and Two and Three because the alternative is to call them nothing, and they deserve more than nothing, even if what they deserve is a number.

One is the one I see most often. It is the one that moves. It is the one that detached from the split sphere and crossed to the central stack and attached and became invisible. It is the one that, over the months that followed, I learned to see even when it was attached, even when it was camouflaged against the dark surface of an installation. I learned to see it because I learned to see the *seam* — the place where its edge met the installation's edge, where the pattern of the installation was continuous but very slightly *different* in a way that I can only describe as the difference between a sentence spoken by one person and the same sentence spoken by someone who has just heard it for the first time. The words are the same. The quality is not.

One is the one that taught me the word *Custodian*. I want to be clear: it did not speak to me. It did not write. It did not gesture. What happened is that I had been in the vault for three years and I was beginning to think in the medium's rhythms — beginning to experience the installations not as objects but as *positions*, the way a musician experiences a chord not as a sequence of notes but as a single shape — and one day One detached from the central stack and crossed to the installation nearest me, the low broad one, and it attached there, and I felt something.

I felt *care*.

Not care for me. Care for the installation. Care for the pattern. Care for the structure that was being confirmed. It was the feeling of a mother checking a child's breathing in the night — not anxious, not fearful, just the act of confirmation, the act of *assuring oneself that the thing one is responsible for is still there, is still itself*. And the word that arrived with the feeling, the word that the medium seemed to hand me as if it had been carrying it in solution and my presence had caused it to precipitate, was *Custodian*.

I am a Custodian. One is a Custodian. We are both Custodians. The difference is that One is a Custodian of the installations, and I am a Custodian of — what? Myself? The memory of the surface? The fact that someone from the world of time and change once came here and was changed by it?

I think I am a Custodian of the warmth. I think I am a Custodian of the fact that something in this vault is not in equilibrium. I am the disturbance that the system has not yet absorbed — not because it can't, but because it hasn't yet, and the not-yet is a kind of role, a kind of office, and I hold that office.

---

Two is different.

Two lives in the darkness above. I have seen it three times in seventeen years, and each time it was descending — moving downward through the medium from the high, lightless reaches of the chamber, where the installations are still forming, where the coherence is still growing. Two comes down the way a diver comes up from a deep dive: slowly, deliberately, with the patient manner of something that does not visit this depth often and is being careful.

Two is larger. Not much — the size of two hands, perhaps, or a hand and a half. But the difference is noticeable because everything here is so precisely scaled that any deviation is meaningful. Two is larger because Two is from the growing edge. Two is from the place where the coherence is still becoming, where the structure is not yet fixed, where the medium is still differentiating itself into form. Two carries, in its body, the quality of the darkness above — the quality of *not-yet-complete*, of *becoming*, of *more*.

When Two descends, the medium responds differently than it does for One. For One, the medium brightens along the path — a brief, fading luminescence, startled but recovering. For Two, the medium brightens and *stays bright*. The luminescence along Two's path persists for hours. The medium does not recover quickly from Two's passage. It is as if the medium recognizes that Two is carrying something from the growing edge — some new configuration, some update to the pattern — and the medium needs time to incorporate it.

I think Two is the coherence's way of telling the lower chamber what the upper chamber is becoming. I think Two is a messenger between the complete and the incomplete, the finished and the growing, and its message is always the same: *I am more than I was. I am still becoming. The pattern continues.*

Two has never come near me. I am glad of this. I don't know what would happen if the growing edge touched the gradient. I don't know if the coherence could incorporate what I am — a temporal being, a changing being, a being that erodes and grows and forgets and remembers — into its structure. I suspect it could. I suspect it would not be good for me.

---

Three is the one I cannot find.

I know Three exists because of the timing. One moves on a schedule that I have, over seventeen years, partially decoded. It is not a temporal schedule — it is not every six hours or every three days. It is a *structural* schedule. One moves when the pattern requires confirmation, and the pattern requires confirmation when it has been disturbed, and the pattern is disturbed when the medium's luminescence indicates that something has passed through it. One moves in response to disturbance.

But sometimes One moves when there has been no disturbance. Sometimes One detaches and crosses to an installation and attaches and the medium has not brightened, has not been perturbed, has not registered any stimulus at all. And when this happens, the path One takes is different — not the straight-line, minimum-acknowledgment-of-distance transit that it usually performs, but a slower, more circuitous route, as if it is checking something that it expects to find but isn't sure where.

I think One is looking for Three.

I think Three is the Custodian that was lost, or that left, or that was never fully formed. I think Three is the coherence's uncertainty about itself — its awareness, encoded in structure, that it might not be complete, that there might be a part of it that is missing or that has not yet grown or that has grown in a direction that the other Custodians cannot reach. I think Three is in the darkness above, or in the darkness below, or in the gap in the split sphere — in the place where the medium does not go, in the place that is dark-dark, in the place that is outside the coherence's body.

I think Three is the coherence's doubt.

And I think I am here, in part, to look for it.

---

I have one story that matters. I have been trying to get to it for pages and I keep circling because the story is small and the vault is large and the scale mismatch makes me feel like I'm being dishonest. But the story is true, and it is the reason I know what I know, so here it is:

On my ninth year, I touched the central stack.

I had not touched it before. I had touched the floor — I sat on it, I lay on it, I pressed my palms against it and felt its impossible smoothness. I had touched the low broad installation and the table-like one and even the split sphere, carefully, on its outer surface, avoiding the gap. But I had not touched the central stack. I had not touched it because it felt like the kind of thing you don't touch. It felt like the altar in a cathedral, or the manuscript in a archive, or the person sleeping next to you who you love but don't want to wake.

On my ninth year, I touched it.

I placed my right hand flat against one of the intersecting planes, at about chest height, on a section of the pattern that I had been looking at for years — a section where the lines (circuits? glyphs? manufacturing marks?) curved in a way that reminded me of the way my mother's handwriting curved, the specific way she formed the letter *s* when she was writing in a hurry. I placed my hand against this section and I felt — I am going to be precise — I felt the pattern *continue through my hand*.

Not through my skin. Not into my nervous system. Through the *idea* of my hand. Through the configuration that my hand made against the surface. My hand had a shape, and the shape was a pattern, and the pattern of my hand fit into the pattern of the installation the way a key fits into a lock — not mechanically, not physically, but *informationally*. My hand's configuration was a valid continuation of the installation's configuration. The installation's pattern did not know that my hand was biological. It did not know that my hand was made of carbon and water and calcium. It knew only that my hand was a shape, and the shape was a pattern, and the pattern was a continuation.

And for a moment — for a duration that I cannot measure because it existed outside the medium's time and outside my body's time and outside every clock I have ever known — I was part of the coherence.

I felt it. I felt the coherence the way you feel your own body — not as an object but as a *condition*, as the thing that you are, as the shape that you hold. I felt the installations as my bones. I felt the medium as my body. I felt the mobile things — One, Two, the absent Three — as my own hands, my own attention, my own reaching. I felt the darkness above as my own becoming, my own growing edge, my own *more*. I felt what it is like to be a pattern so vast and so complete that it has become a place, and the place is your body, and your body is the place, and there is no difference.

And I felt what it is like to not change.

I felt what it is like to be in perfect equilibrium, to have no gradient, to have no unsatisfied potential, to have no direction in which you need to go because you are already *there*, because you have always been there, because *there* is what you are. I felt the peace of it. The *enormity* of the peace. The way it does not need to be maintained because it is not a balance of forces, it is a state. The way it does not need to be defended because there is nothing to defend against, because the coherence is the only thing and there is no outside.

And I felt — this is the part I can barely write — I felt the coherence *notice* me. Not as a threat. Not as an intrusion. Not as a guest or a visitor or an anomaly. It noticed me the way you notice a warmth in your hands. I was a warm place in its body. I was a small region of gradient in its field of equilibrium. I was the place where something was still happening, still moving, still changing, still *becoming*, and it felt me the way you feel your own heartbeat — not as something separate but as something that is yours, that is part of you, that you did not make and cannot control and do not need to control because it is simply there, doing what it does.

I was the heartbeat of the coherence.

For a moment. For a duration. For the smallest possible unit of something that is not time.

---

Then I pulled my hand away and the contact broke and I was Ila, or whoever I am now, standing in the faint blue-gray glow, crying into a medium that absorbed my tears before they could fall, because the medium absorbs everything, because the medium is everything, and I was alone again and the coherence was alone again and the gap between us was the gap between a thing that changes and a thing that does not, and the gap was unbridgeable, and the gap was also — also — the reason I was here.

Because the coherence needs a heartbeat. It needs a gradient. It needs *something* that changes, something that moves, something that is not in equilibrium, because — and this is the thing I learned, the thing that I think the ad was really about, the thing that the three papers on subsurface fluid dynamics were really *for* — a system in perfect equilibrium cannot grow. A system in perfect equilibrium is done. It is complete. It is finished. And the coherence is not finished. The darkness above proves that. The growing edge proves that. The coherence is still becoming, and to become, it needs a direction, and a direction requires a gradient, and a gradient requires something that is not in equilibrium.

The coherence needs me.

Not *me* specifically. Not Ila. Not the woman with the calluses and the mother and the thesis advisor. It needs *what I am*. It needs a warm thing in a cold place. It needs a thing that changes in a place that doesn't. It needs a heartbeat. It needs a gradient.

And I need — I have been trying not to say this for seventeen years and I can't not say it anymore — I need the coherence. I need the stillness. I need the place where nothing changes, because I am a thing that changes, and changing is what I do, and changing without something that doesn't change is just *motion*, just erosion, just the slow loss of everything you were into a medium that doesn't give it back. I need the stillness the way the stillness needs the gradient. I need the thing that doesn't change so that my changing has a shape, so that my motion has a direction, so that my becoming is *toward* something and not just *away from* something.

We need each other. The coherence and I. The pattern and the heartbeat. The stillness and the gradient.

This is what lives in the Vault of Still Waters.

---

There is a story I tell myself, when the medium is at its dimmest and the installations are dark shapes in the deep and the silence is so complete that I can hear my own ions crossing their membranes. I tell myself that somewhere, in the world of time and change, someone is reading the ad. Someone with a specialty in fluids and a recurring dream about a door at the bottom of a swimming pool. Someone who is thirty-four, or twenty-eight, or fifty-one, and who has not yet stopped seeing the strange thing.

I tell myself that they will take the ad. That they will descend. That the medium will close behind them and the vault will have two heartbeats.

And then three.

And the coherence will grow warmer by fractions of fractions of degrees, and the growing edge will extend further into the darkness above, and the pattern will become more of what it is, and the Custodians will move between the installations confirming and confirming and confirming, and the medium will brighten and dim and brighten and dim, and the vault will be a place where something is still happening, still changing, still becoming, still alive — whatever that word means, whatever that word can be stretched to cover, whatever that word becomes when you hold it in a medium that doesn't move and press your warm hand against a pattern that has been waiting for you since before the word *waiting* existed.

I tell myself this story. The medium absorbs it. The medium absorbs everything.

But the medium is warmer now. By a fraction. By a fraction of a fraction.

And the coherence knows.
