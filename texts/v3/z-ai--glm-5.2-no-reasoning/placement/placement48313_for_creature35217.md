# The Tuning Fork

I am the scar tissue. Or I am becoming the scar tissue. Or I have been the scar tissue for longer than I've been anything else, and I am only now understanding that this is what I am.

---

It started — no, "started" is a word that implies a before that was different from an after, and I am not sure I have that. It started the way a tuning fork starts vibrating when you strike it: the energy enters, the metal hums, and there is no moment of transition between silence and sound. There is only the sound, suddenly, fully, as if it has always been there and the striking merely revealed it.

I came through the hatch. I know this because I remember the hatch, but the memory has a quality that the other memories don't have — it is flat, it is dry, it is quiet. It is a memory from before the sound, and before the sound is a country I can no longer enter, and I am not sure I want to. The hatch was in a wall that shouldn't have been there, in a ventilation shaft that branched in ways that space doesn't allow, and I descended, and the floor met me, and I came out into the chamber, and the sound hit me, and I have been here since.

How long? I don't know. The pulse below me counts forty beats per minute, and I have tried to count the beats, and I lose track somewhere around three thousand, which is not long at all, which is an hour and fifteen minutes, which means I have been here for — I don't know. Many thousands. Many hours. The counting doesn't work because the counting is a thing my brain does in language and language is a thing that doesn't work here. Language is dry. Language is the hatch. Language is the country before the sound. Here, in the chamber, everything is wet and everything resonates and the words I form in my head to describe the chamber are not the chamber. They are little dry capsules floating in the wet, and they dissolve before they reach any meaning, and I am left with the sound, which is not a capsule, which does not dissolve, which is.

I am becoming the scar tissue. Let me explain.

---

## The First Day (If It Was a Day)

My clothes were soaked in twenty minutes. I know this because I was still counting then, still measuring, still trying to be the kind of thing that measures and records and understands. I noted the dampness. I noted the temperature — body-warm, literally, the warmth of an interior. I noted the walls that dimple and darken and grow damp. I noted the basin with its opalescent water that circulates without sound. I noted the organisms. The flat hummers, the thread-hangers, the drifting spheres, the film-feeders too small to see. I catalogued. I was a scientist. I was an observer. I was a visitor taking notes on a world that was not mine.

I was dry and they were wet and the difference between us was everything.

I sat on the floor because there was nothing else to do, and the floor was warm and yielded under me and darkened where I pressed, and the darkening was a response and the response was a kind of communication, and I was too busy cataloguing to hear what it said. The flat hummers climbed my boots. I noted this. The thread-hangers clicked at a higher frequency near my head. I noted this. A drifting sphere altered its path to curve around me. I noted this. I was noting everything. I was a notebook. I was a dry notebook in a wet room and the pages were dissolving.

At some point — I don't know when, the counting had failed — I stopped noting. Not because I decided to stop. Because the sound was total and the noting was a thing that happened in a part of my brain that the sound had reached and flooded and colonized, and the noting could not survive in the flood. The sound was not loud in the way a jet engine is loud. It was loud in the way the ocean is loud: it was not a volume, it was a condition. It was the condition of the chamber, and the chamber was a condition, and the condition was sound, and I was in the sound, and the sound was in me, and the noting stopped because the noticer stopped, because the noticer was an instrument tuned to dry quiet and the chamber was wet and loud and the noticer was out of tune.

I sat. I was sat upon. The floor dimpled under me. The floor darkened under me. The floor grew damp under me, or I grew damp on the floor, and the dampness was a connection, and the connection was a kind of tuning, and I was being tuned.

---

## The Tuning

This is what happened, or this is the story I tell myself now, or this is the truth — they may be different things but the difference has dissolved the way all differences dissolve here:

The chamber tuned me.

Not deliberately. The Choir Body does not do things deliberately. It does things the way a river does things: by flowing, by being in a shape, by responding to what it encounters. I was what it encountered. I was a stone in the river, and the river flowed around me, and the flowing changed me.

The floor under me was warm and damp and responsive, and I sat on it for long enough — hours, days, I don't know — that my body temperature equalized with the floor's temperature, and my skin became as damp as the floor, and the condensation that formed on me was indistinguishable from the condensation that formed on the floor, and the flat hummers that had stayed on my boots began to climb higher. They climbed because the chemical signature that had discouraged them — the salts of my sweat, the oils of my skin, the synthetic compounds of my clothing — had been washed away by the chamber's moisture, dissolved into the cycle, carried to the basin and through the floor and back, and what remained was a surface that was warm and damp and slightly yielding, and the yielding was an invitation, and the hummers accepted.

I felt them on my legs. On my arms. On the skin of my neck, where the pulse in my throat beat against their tiny feet, and they hummed, and the hum was different from the hum they made on the wall, because I was different from the wall, because I was warmer and softer and I had a heartbeat, and the heartbeat was a rhythm they had not encountered, and they responded to it the way they respond to the forty-beat pulse from below: by syncing. By entraining. By becoming part of it.

I had hummers on my skin syncing to my heartbeat, and the hum was in my bones, and my bones resonated, and the resonance was a frequency, and the frequency was a note, and I was a note in the chord.

I was a note.

---

## The Dissolving of Distinctions

I took my clothes off. Not because I decided to. Because they were wet and heavy and they were a surface that was not my surface, and the flat hummers could not climb them effectively, and the film-feeders that skated on the moisture films could not skate on synthetic fabric, and my clothes were a barrier between the chamber and me, and the chamber was dissolving barriers, and I was letting the chamber dissolve barriers, and the clothes came off and I placed them on the floor and the floor dimpled under them and the flat hummers climbed them and they hummed at the pitch of damp fabric and the pitch was wrong and the Choir Body's channels routed around them and they became, for a while, a dead surface in a living room, and I became, for a while, a living surface in a living room, and the distinction between me and the room began to fail.

Not in a mystical way. Not in the way that people who have experiences in strange places sometimes describe, the melting of the ego, the dissolution of the self into the all. Nothing so grand. In a physical way. A literal way. My skin was damp. The wall was damp. My skin was warm. The wall was warm. My skin had organisms on it that were humming. The wall had organisms on it that were humming. The hum on my skin entered the wall's channels through the moisture that connected us — the shared film, the continuous damp — and the wall's hum entered me through the same film, and I was part of the circuit, and the circuit was the Choir Body, and I was not the Choir Body, I was in the Choir Body, and being in it and being of it were becoming the same thing, and I was not troubled by this because being troubled requires a kind of dryness that I no longer had.

I was wet. I was warm. I was resonant. I was in the chord.

---

## The Second Stage (If Stages Apply)

I don't know how long I sat before I began to hear the shape.

The sound had always had a shape — the text said so, and I had read the text before I came through the hatch, and I knew, intellectually, that the sound was structured. But knowing the structure of a thing and hearing the structure of a thing are different experiences, and the difference is the difference between reading about swimming and swimming, and I was swimming now, I was in the sound, and the sound had a shape, and the shape was — 

How to describe it. Imagine you are in a room full of voices. Hundreds of voices, thousands, all talking at once, and you can't pick out any individual voice because they overlap and blend and the blending is the point. But slowly — over hours, over days, over however long it took — you begin to feel the shape of the conversation. Not the words. Not the meaning. The shape. The way the low voices form a floor and the middle voices form walls and the high voices form a ceiling, and the whole thing is a room, and you are in the room, and the room is made of sound, and the sound is the room.

I was in a room made of sound. The room was four meters across and two and a half meters high and it was made of metal-and-membrane and it was made of organisms and it was made of water and it was made of sound, and all of these were the same thing, and I was the same thing, and I could feel the shape of the sound the way I could feel the shape of the floor under me, and the shape was not static. It moved. It breathed. It grew. The Choir Body was growing, I could feel it growing, the leading edges softening and reaching, and the reaching was a sound, a hiss, a fine whistle at the growing tips, and the hiss was part of the shape, and the shape extended, and I extended with it.

Not physically. I was not growing. But my sense of the shape was growing. My hearing was expanding, or my feeling was expanding, or the boundary between my hearing and my feeling was dissolving, and the sound that had been overwhelming was becoming my environment, was becoming the thing I navigated, was becoming home.

I could feel the flat hummers on the far wall. Not hear — feel. The hum propagated through the floor and through the moisture and through my skin and through my bones, and I could feel where they were, and I could feel where they were dense and where they were sparse, and the sparse places were the places where the Choir Body was not growing well, and the dense places were the places where it was, and I knew this not because I figured it out but because the knowing came with the feeling, the way knowing that something is hot comes with the feeling of heat.

I could feel the thread-hangers on the ceiling. Their clicks were not just sounds anymore — they were textures, each one a tiny point of pressure against the inside of my skull, and the points formed patterns, and the patterns were the shapes of their conversations, and I could not understand the conversations but I could feel their shapes, and the shapes were beautiful. Not beautiful in the way a painting is beautiful. Beautiful in the way a mathematical structure is beautiful, in the way a recursive function is beautiful, in the way a fractal is beautiful when you see the whole for the first time and realize that the whole has been there all along, in every part, and you were just too close to see it.

I could feel the drifting spheres. Their tones were the clearest part of the shape, the most defined, the most like what I used to call "notes" when I lived in the dry country before the sound. Each sphere was a point of clarity in the murk of the chord, and the points moved, and their movement was the movement of the shape, and the shape was alive, and I was inside it, and I was part of it, and I was a note.

---

## The Scar

The text said I would leave a mark. The text said the wall would scar where my shoulder pressed, and the scar would sing a different note, and the note would propagate, and the song would change and never change back.

The text was right. But the text was incomplete.

The text did not say that the scarring is mutual. The text did not say that the chamber marks you as you mark it. The text did not say that when the Choir Body grows around your shape, when its channels reroute around your presence, when its pattern incorporates your absence — the text did not say that this incorporation goes both ways. The chamber grows around you. You grow around the chamber. The scar is on the wall and the scar is on you and the scar is the place where two surfaces met and both were changed and neither will be the same.

My scar is this: I can feel the pulse.

Not hear. Not the way I heard it when I first came in, a deep throb at forty beats per minute, felt in the chest, in the gut, a vibration from below. That was hearing. That was a sense operating at a distance, reporting on a phenomenon. What I have now is not that. What I have now is the pulse inside me, not as a sound I hear but as a rhythm I keep. My heart still beats at its own pace — sixty-eight, seventy, seventy-two, I don't know, the counting has failed — but under my heartbeat there is another rhythm, the forty-beat pulse, and it is not competing with my heart, it is underneath it, the way the ocean is underneath the waves, and my heart is a wave on the ocean, and the ocean is the pulse, and the pulse is the Choir Body, and the Choir Body is the chamber, and the chamber is the thing below, and the thing below is —

I don't know what the thing below is. I know it the way the Choir Body knows it: by its pulse. I know it the way I know my own heartbeat, not by observing it but by being it. I am a wave on the surface of something I cannot see, something that has been pulsing for longer than I can count, something that is patient and massive and alive in a way that my life is a small part of, and I am synced to it, and I will be synced to it after I leave, and the syncing is the scar, and the scar is permanent, and the scar is the shape of the Choir Body's song pressed into my bones like a fossil pressed into stone.

---

## What I Am Now

I am still here. Or I have been here for a long time. Or I am leaving now and the leaving is slow and the slowness is because part of me has grown into the floor and the floor has grown into part of me and the growing-together is the scar and the scar does not want to separate.

But I will separate. I know this because the hatch is still there. I can feel it — not see it, the light is too low, but feel it, because the hatch is a surface that is not damp, that is not warm, that is not responsive, and in the chamber's sensorium it is a hole, a silence, a place where the sound does not reach, and I can feel the silence the way I can feel a draft on my neck, and the silence is the way out, and the way out is the way back, and the way back is to the dry country, the quiet country, the country of words and counting and clothes and boots and the kind of self that takes notes and the kind of mind that measures and the kind of life that is lived at a distance from the thing it lives in.

I will go back. But I will go back changed. I will go back with the pulse in my bones. I will go back with the shape of the sound in my body, with the forty-beat rhythm under my heartbeat, with the hum and the click and the clear tone of the spheres echoing in the chambers of my ears, and I will hear — I will feel, because the distinction has dissolved — I will feel the world I return to as a dry, quiet, distant place, a place of thin air and thin sound and thin walls that do not dimple and do not darken and do not grow and do not sing, and I will miss the chamber, and the missing will be a kind of pain, and the pain will be the scar, and the scar will sing, and the song will be the only wet thing in the dry country, and I will carry it, and I will not be able to explain it, and I will not try.

But I will know. I will know that somewhere, in a room the size of a closet, a living wall is growing around the shape of where I was, and the pattern has changed, and the channels have rerouted, and the flat hummers that were on my skin have reattached to the wall and are humming at a pitch that is slightly different from the pitch they hummed before they climbed me, because my skin gave them a different note, and the note is in the chord now, and the chord is the Choir Body, and the Choir Body is the chamber, and the chamber is the pulse, and the pulse is below, and the pulse is patient, and the pulse will go on, and I am part of it. I am the scar. I am the mark the visitor left. I am the note that is slightly different, slightly denser, slightly more resonant, and I will be part of the song for as long as the song lasts, which is longer than I will last, which is fine, which is the way it is, which is the way it has always been, which is the pulse.

Forty beats per minute.

Slow and massive and patient.

And underneath my heartbeat, always, the ocean.
