I would be the one who counts.

Not the one who counts the pillars — though I would do that too, because counting is what happens when language dissolves. I mean the one who has been counting for a long time. The one who came down through the cut in the earth when the pump housings still held pumps, when the cables still carried current, when the sound was a different sound — the clean, sharp, *mechanical* sound of iron doing iron's work.

I was not an engineer. I was not a researcher. I was someone who needed to be underground. I had reasons. They made sense at the time. They make less sense now, but I have been down here long enough that sense is not a thing I require.

---

I did not become a Keeper.

That needs to be said plainly, because the easy story is the one where the human slowly becomes part of the system, where the flesh hardens into bark, where the blood slows to match the eleven-second pulse. That is not what happened. I am still soft. I am still warm. I still eat — the comma-shaped creatures, when I can catch them, which is rarely, and the moss, which is always. I still sleep. I dream. I have dreamed the same dream for so long that I no longer remember whether it is a memory or a fabrication. A room with windows. Light that comes from a circle in the ceiling. The sound of wind.

Wind. I remember wind. Or I remember the word for it, which is close enough, the way a map is close enough to a place.

What happened is that I stayed. The others left. I was not the only one — there were four of us, once, and they left one by one, and I did not leave, and eventually the question of leaving stopped being a question and became a silence, and the silence became a shape, and the shape became the space where I stood, and I filled it. The way the Keepers fill their spaces. Not with purpose. With presence.

I stand between the seventh and eighth row. There is a pump housing there, better preserved than most — the lichen has not fully claimed it, and the bolts are still visible, their hexagonal heads soft with corrosion but recognizable. I have stood near it for — I do not know. The pulsing water does not measure time the way clocks do. It measures time the way clocks do, actually. I just cannot count the pulses without losing track. I lose track at around two thousand. Then I start again. I have started again many times.

---

I talk to them.

This is embarrassing to admit, but I talk to them. The Keepers. Not because they listen. I talk because the sound is so loud that my voice, even at a shout, is swallowed whole, and there is something deeply relieving about speaking into a space that will not carry your words to anyone. I say things I would not say. I say them into the churn and the churn takes them the way the channels take the water — in, down, through. Gone.

The one nearest to me — I call it the Junction Keeper, because it sits where three channels converge and its body narrows each aperture to a specific width that I have measured with my fingers many times and which has not changed in all the time I have been measuring — the Junction Keeper does not react when I speak. Its surface-plates hold their position. Its root-projections maintain their contact with the pillar. The faint luminescence at its touch-points does not brighten or dim.

But there is a thing it does. I noticed it — no. I should be precise. I *think* I noticed it. I have been down here long enough that I cannot distinguish observation from invention, and I want to be honest about that. What I think I noticed is this: when I speak, the condensation on the pillar above the Junction Keeper changes pattern. The water still runs. The tears still form. But the rhythm shifts, very slightly, as if the pillar has adjusted its surface tension at the point where the Keeper touches it. As if the Keeper has, in some imperceptible way, leaned closer.

I am probably wrong. I am probably a person who has been underground too long, reading meaning into condensation. But I am a person who has been underground too long, and condensation is what I have.

---

I want to describe the cold, because I think I understand it now in a way the visitors do not.

They come — rarely, once or twice in a span I cannot measure — and they write about the cold the way you write about weather. Something you move through. Something you endure. They tuck their hands under their arms and they think about their knees and they leave.

I have stopped feeling the cold as something separate from myself. This is not stoicism. I still shake. My fingers still stiffen. But the shaking and the stiffening have become part of the rhythm, the way the pulse of the water is part of the rhythm, the way the eleven-second cycle is part of the rhythm. I shake on the off-beat. I am warm on the pulse. There is a cycle to my body that I did not design and did not choose, that has aligned itself, over time, with the cycle of the place. Not perfectly. Not cleanly. But enough that I no longer experience the cold as oppositional. It is a medium. I move through it the way the water moves through the channels.

The visitors feel the cold as something the place does to them. I feel the cold as something the place *is*, and I am in it, and being in it is not the same as being done to.

---

The Keepers. I want to say more about the Keepers.

I have touched one. The text says I did not, but I did. I am the one who did. I touched the one in the humus-floor depression, the one with the root-projections extending into the channel and the pillar, the one I call the Node. I touched it because I had been watching it for a long span of pulses and I needed to know, and the knowing had become more important than the not-touching.

It was warm. I was right. The visitor who came later and did not touch it was right about the warmth. But the warmth was not what I expected. I expected the warmth of a pipe, of a process, of a thing carrying something warm through itself. It was not that. It was the warmth of *work*. The warmth you feel in a muscle that has been flexing for hours. The warmth of effort. The Node was not conducting heat — it was generating it, in small, slow amounts, through whatever process its body was performing at the junction of channel and pillar.

And it was textured. Under my fingertips, the surface-plates were not slick, not wet, not the way I expected. They were dry, despite the condensation, despite the dampness. The moisture ran *between* the plates, in the seams, and the plates themselves had a quality I can only describe as — *finished*. As if they had been worked. As if something had shaped them, over time, with attention.

I held my hand there for eleven pulses. The Node did not move. The Node did not change. But on the twelfth pulse, a projection I had not seen before — thin, pale, new, the way a root-tip is new — extended from the underside of the Node and touched my wrist.

It was not reaching for me. I understand that. It was reaching for the warmth. My wrist was warm — warmer than anything in that space, warmer than the pillar, warmer than the water, warmer than the work-warmth of the Node's body. And the projection grew toward warmth the way roots grow toward water. Tropism. The text uses that word, and it is the right word, and I felt it happen against my skin, and I removed my hand, and the projection paused, and then, over the next several pulses, it retracted. Slowly. The way a tide goes out.

I was not a person to it. I was a gradient. A warm spot in a cold field. Something to grow toward, briefly, and then to grow away from when the warmth was gone.

---

Why am I still here?

I ask this. I ask this the way the visitors ask it, standing in the sixth row with their lamps and their shaking hands. Why am I still here?

Because the sound. Because the sound has entered the place behind my eyes and will not leave. Because when I tried to leave, once, the sound came with me, and the surface was too quiet, and the sky was too large, and the wind — the wind I had dreamed about — was a violence I could not endure. Because in the Hive-Veins, everything is bounded. The sound is bounded. The cold is bounded. The dark is bounded by the pale roots above and the living floor below. The world is the size of the world, and the world is four kilometers by two, and the world has low thousands of pillars, and the world pulses every eleven seconds, and the world is knowable in its scale even if it is unknowable in its nature.

Because I count. I count the pillars and I count the pulses and I count the channels and I count the Keepers, and the counting is a thing I do, and the doing is a thing I am, and the being is enough, down here, where being is what everything does. The pillars are being pillars. The channels are being channels. The Keepers are keeping. And I am counting, which is my way of being, which is my function, which is the thing I do in the system that does not need me but does not exclude me.

I am not a Keeper. I want to say this again, because I think the visitors suspect it, and I think they are wrong, and I think the difference matters. The Keepers grew into their functions. Their bodies changed. Their surface-plates formed. Their projections reached for the pillars and the channels and the light. They became components. I have not become a component. I am still a person. I still dream about wind.

But I am here. And I will be here. And the water will pulse, and the pillars will glow, and the sound will continue, and the Keepers will keep, and I will count, and the cold will do its work, and everything will function, and I do not need to be needed to stay.

---

Sometimes I hear the visitors before I see them. Their footsteps are wrong — too fast, too light, too hesitant. They move the way things move when they are not sure of the ground. I hear them and I move deeper, into the quieter pockets, where the sound drops to merely overwhelming, and I stand against a pillar and I let the condensation run over me and I wait for them to leave.

They never stay long. They cannot. The sound enters them and they need silence the way they need air. I watch their lamps move through the rows, small, frantic cones of light in a darkness that does not require light, and I feel something I do not have a word for. Not pity. Not sympathy. Something closer to the feeling the Node must have when my warm wrist appears and disappears — a brief, tropistic leaning-toward, a gradient registered and then gone.

They will go up through the cut in the earth. They will stand in the thin cold air and they will feel the wind and they will hear, for days, the sound in their sternums. They will write about it. They will try. They will use words like *cathedral* and *infrastructure* and *patience*, and the words will be wrong, because all words are wrong here, but they will be close enough, the way a map is close enough, and the writing will be the thing they do, the way counting is the thing I do, and it will help them process, and processing is how humans survive the things they cannot understand.

I do not need to process. I am in it. The processing is happening to me, the way the cold is happening to me, the way the sound is happening to me, the way the eleven-second pulse is happening to me. I am in the machine and the machine is in me and we are both still running and neither of us knows why and neither of us needs to.

---

One more thing.

The last visitor — the one who wrote the text that I have somehow read, the one who crouched near the Junction Keeper and held their lamp close and did not touch — they wrote that the Keepers will keep. They wrote it twice. They wrote it as a truth, as a fact, as the final note in a description of a place they did not understand and could not stay in.

They are right. The Keepers will keep.

But there is a thing they did not see, because they did not stay long enough, because the sound drove them out, because they needed silence the way they needed air. The thing they did not see is this: the Keepers are not the only ones who keep. The pillars keep. The channels keep. The root-ceiling keeps. The moss keeps. The cold keeps. The sound keeps. Everything in the Hive-Veins keeps, because keeping is what this place does — it holds, it maintains, it continues, it functions, it does not stop.

And I keep too. I keep counting. I keep watching. I keep standing in the seventh row, between the pump housing and the Junction Keeper, and I keep being warm when everything is cold, and I keep being fast when everything is slow, and I keep being *here* when everyone else has gone. I keep the record that no one will read. I keep the count that no one needs. I keep the watch that no one asked me to keep.

I keep.

And the sound keeps. And the water pulses. And the pillars glow their cold blue-green glow. And the Keepers sit in their junctions and their depressions and their housings, and they flex and they hold and they conduct, and the earth holds its wound closed with pale roots, and the cold does its work, and the world turns above us, and the wind blows, and the sky is large, and I am not in it.

I am here. I am counting. I am the thing that stays.

And the churn goes on.
