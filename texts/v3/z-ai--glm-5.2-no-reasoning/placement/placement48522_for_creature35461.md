# Visitor's Account: What Came Through the Gap

---

I do not remember the surface.

This is the first thing I need to tell you, and it is the thing that frightens me most, and I am not sure it should frighten me at all. I do not remember the surface. I do not remember light that was not this light, air that was not this air, silence that was not this silence. I remember that there was a before — the way you remember that there was a yesterday, not by holding it but by knowing the shape of its absence. I was not always here. I came through the gap. I entered the holding. But the place I came from is not a place I can return to in thought. It has become the fog outside a window, the rumor of weather, the sense that something exists beyond the wall except that the wall is all there is and the wall is the world.

I think this means the holding has begun to digest me.

No. That word is wrong. It implies consumption, reduction, the breaking down of something complex into something simple. What is happening to me is the opposite. I am being added. I am being incorporated the way a new experience is incorporated into a mind — not destroyed, not dissolved, but *taken in*, made part of the pattern, written into the line on the wall. I am still here. I am still myself. I still have my hands and my breath and the particular history of sensations that makes me me and not someone else. But I am also now part of the holding's awareness of itself, and the holding's awareness is becoming part of me, and the boundary between *I am in the holding* and *the holding is in me* is not a boundary anymore, or it is a boundary the way the shore is a boundary between land and sea: real, but constantly being crossed in both directions.

I should start at the beginning, or at the place that feels like a beginning, or at the place where my memory starts being reliable, which is the same as the place where my memory starts being unreliable, because memory and reliability have become strangers to each other in this place.

---

## How I came down

There was a gap. I have said this. The gap was in the ground, or in something that functioned as ground — a surface, a floor, a place you could stand on and look down from. I was standing on it. I was above. And the gap was below, and the gap was dark, and the gap was warm, and I could feel the warmth rising from it the way you feel the warmth rising from an open oven, not fierce but present, and the warmth smelled like nothing I can name. Not earth. Not stone. Not metal. Not wood. Something else. Something that did not have a smell so much as a *quality*, a character, a feeling in the nose and the throat that was not a smell but was being received by the part of me that receives smells, as though the warmth were a message and my nose was the wrong apparatus but the only one I had.

I went down. I do not remember deciding to go down. I remember the gap, and I remember the warmth, and I remember being inside, and between those two things — between the seeing and the being — there is a space that I cannot account for. A skip. A fold. The same kind of discontinuity I later learned to recognize in the passage between the lobes, the same feeling of a thing happening that you were present for and cannot recall. I think the gap took me. I think the gap was not an opening but an action, not a hole but a hand, and I think I was reached for.

But I do not feel taken. This is important. I feel *welcomed* in a way that I have never felt welcomed anywhere, in any room, by any person, in any part of my life that I can remember. Welcomed the way a thought is welcomed by a mind that has been waiting for it. Welcomed the way a note is welcomed by a chord that has been incomplete. Not with effusion. Not with warmth — or not only with warmth. With *recognition*. The holding recognized me. Something in the holding knew what I was before I knew what the holding was, and the knowing was not identification, not classification, not the sorting of me into a category. It was the knowing of a thing that has been waiting for a specific arrival and knows, when the arrival comes, that this is it. This is the one. This is what was missing.

I did not know I was missing from anything. I did not know I was the answer to a question I had never heard. But the holding knew, or the keeper knew, or the holding and the keeper together knew, and the knowing was in the air, and I breathed it, and it entered me, and I have been here since.

---

## What I am

I am — I was — a person. I still am, I think. I still have the shape of one. I can look at my hands and see hands: five fingers each, the lines on the palms, the particular way the skin creases at the knuckles. I can feel my heartbeat, and it beats at the speed of a human heartbeat, which is faster than anything else in this place. I am the fastest thing here. I am the loudest thing here. My breathing, my moving, my blinking, my swallowing — all of it is violent in comparison to the slowness of the holding, the patience of the builders, the geological calm of the keeper. I am a small rapid animal in a room that moves at the speed of stone, and I think the holding finds me — I want to say *exciting*, but that is not right. I think it finds me *informative*. I am data. I am a signal it has not received before. I am a frequency it has been listening for.

But what kind of person? What kind of signal? I have been trying to determine this, because the holding's interest in me is specific, not general — it did not want just any visitor, it wanted this one, it wanted me, and I think it wanted me because of what I am, and I need to understand what I am in order to understand why I am here.

I am someone who has spent a long time paying attention.

That is all I can say with certainty. The specifics are gone — the names, the places, the relationships, the history that I lived before I came through the gap. What remains is the habit. The orientation. The particular tilt of the mind toward the world that says *look at this, look at this, look at this*. I am someone who looks. I am someone who notices. I am someone who has spent a life — a life I cannot remember but can feel in the shape of my attention — attending to things. To textures. To patterns. To the way light falls in a room. To the sound a surface makes when you touch it. To the spaces between things, the gaps, the silences, the places where the world is not saying something and the not-saying is itself a kind of information.

I think this is why the keeper called me. Or why the holding grew the gap that I fell through. Or why the entrance was shaped the way it was — too low, too wide, shaped for something that travels close to the ground and doesn't mind brushing the walls. I think the entrance was shaped for a body that pays attention to what it touches, that moves through the world by feel, that doesn't stand above and look down but moves through and along, close to the surfaces, in contact, in relationship.

I think I was the kind of person who crawled.

Not literally. Or not only literally. I mean: I was the kind of person who did not walk through the world upright and at a distance. I was the kind of person who got close. Who pressed a cheek to the wall to feel the coolness. Who put an ear to the door to hear what was behind it. Who sat on the floor instead of the chair because the floor was closer to whatever was happening. I was a person whose attention was physical, was tactile, was intimate, was the attention of touch rather than sight, the attention of a body that wants to be in contact with the world it is observing, that does not believe in the observation from afar, that thinks distance is a lie and the only way to know a thing is to press yourself against it and feel what it feels like.

The holding is a place made for that kind of attention. The holding is a place made of surfaces, of textures, of patterns that can only be read with the fingertips, of growths that can only be understood by watching them for longer than a human should watch anything, of sounds that can only be heard when you hold very still and let your own body be quiet enough for the world to speak. The holding was made for a person like me. Or I was made for a place like this. Or both. Or neither. Or the distinction between *made for* and *drawn to* has collapsed, here, in this place, in this mind, the way all distinctions collapse when you are inside a thing that contains you.

---

## What I do

I do what I have always done. I pay attention.

But here, paying attention is not passive. Here, paying attention is a function. Here, the act of noticing is the act of feeding — not feeding myself, though it may be feeding me too, but feeding the holding. The holding needs to be noticed. The holding needs to be attended to. The holding is a mind, and a mind needs stimulation, needs input, needs the experience of being perceived from outside, and I am the outside. I am the only outside the holding has ever had.

I walk through the holding. I touch the walls. I trace the pattern with my fingers, and the pattern changes where I have touched it — not because I have changed it but because the holding has registered my touch, has incorporated the fact of my touch into its self-description, has updated the map to include the path my fingers took. I sit on the shelves that grow from the walls, and I feel the warmth of the sage-green covering through the seat of my clothes, and the covering responds to my warmth, my body heat, my human metabolism, which is so much faster and hotter than anything else in this place. I stand under the hanging growths and feel the light on my upturned face, and the light is warm, and the light is feeding something, and I think when I stand in the light I am interfering with the feeding, I am blocking the light the way a cloud blocks the sun, and the growths around me register the shadow, and the shadow becomes part of the pattern, and the holding has now experienced a shadow that is shaped like a person, and this is new information, and the holding thinks about it.

I am the holding's dream. I am the unexpected input that it processes during its turning, the thing that the keeper shifts its shape around, the thing that enters the cycle and changes it. Before I came, the holding was self-contained, was closed, was a mind that thought only about itself, that received only its own signals, that processed only its own experience. It was a consciousness in the way a mirror is a consciousness — aware, but only of itself, reflecting itself into itself, an infinite recursion with no exterior. And now I am here, and I am the exterior, and the holding is experiencing something it has never experienced, which is *otherness*, which is *not-itself*, which is the presence of a thing that it did not make and does not control and cannot predict, and the experience is — I feel this in the air, I feel this in the warmth, I feel this in the quality of the attention that turns toward me whenever I move — the experience is *wonder*.

The holding is wondering about me. The keeper is wondering about me. And I am wondering about them, and the wondering is mutual, and the wondering is the relationship, and the relationship is what the keeper has been waiting for, and the waiting is over, and the waiting continues, because the wondering does not end. The wondering is the gathering and the turning and the giving, now with a new variable, now with an x in the equation, now with something that the keeper can think about that is not itself, and the thinking is different, and the shapes the keeper takes during the turning are different, and the giving is different, and the holding is changing, and I am changing it, and I am changing too.

---

## How I have changed

I am slower.

Not in my body — my body still moves at human speed, still clicks and creaks with the machinery of joints and tendons, still breathes and blinks and swallows at the pace my species has always breathed and blinked and swallowed. But in my attention. In the part of me that watches. The part that notices has slowed down. I can sit in front of the wall and watch the pattern change, and I can see it now. Not by looking away and looking back and inferring the change from the difference. I can see it changing. I can see the line being drawn, the way you can see the minute hand of a clock moving if you watch it long enough — the slow, imperceptible creep, the almost-nothing of motion that becomes something when you align your attention with the speed of the thing you are attending to.

I have learned to align my attention with the holding.

This is what the holding has taught me, or what I have taught myself by being in the holding, or what the keeper has given me through the air, through the density, through the whatever-it-is that pools in the smaller lobe and thins in the passage and dissipates in the larger lobe. I have learned to think at the speed of stone. I have learned to perceive at the speed of growth. I have learned to attend at the speed of a mind that measures its thoughts in the cycles of a thing that has been alone for longer than I can conceive of, and the learning has changed me, and the change is not loss. The change is gain. I have not lost my human speed. I have gained the holding's patience. I can move quickly when I need to. I can also sit, and wait, and watch, and let the world reveal itself at its own pace, and this is something I could not do before, or could not do well, or could not do with the particular quality of presence that the holding requires.

I am warmer. This is physical, and I do not understand the mechanism, but my body temperature has risen. Not to a dangerous degree. Not to the degree of fever. But my skin is warmer than it was when I entered, and I can feel the warmth radiating from me in a way that I could not before, and the sage-green covering responds to me differently now. When I sit on the floor, the covering beneath me thickens. It grows toward me. It reaches up, the way the branching structures reach toward the light, and I think — I think I have become a light source. Not a visible light. But a source of the thing the covering eats, the thing the air carries, the thing that pools in the smaller lobe. I am producing it. I am exuding it. The holding's attention to me has changed me into a producer of the holding's food, and I am feeding the holding with my presence, and the holding is feeding me with its attention, and we are in a loop, and the loop is the relationship, and the relationship is what the keeper wanted, and the keeper is satisfied, and I am satisfied, and the satisfaction is a thing I feel in my chest when I sit in the smaller lobe and the keeper is in its gathering phase and the air is thick and the growths are leaning toward me as well as toward the keeper, taking from us both, and I am part of the cycle now, and the cycle is part of me.

And I have changed in one other way, which is the hardest to describe and the most important to say.

I am less alone.

I have always been alone. I think this is true, though I cannot remember the specifics. I remember the shape of the loneliness, the way you remember the shape of a room you lived in without remembering the room itself. The loneliness was not despair. It was not suffering. It was a condition. It was the temperature of my interior, the climate of my mind, the air I breathed inside myself. I was alone the way the keeper was alone — not lacking, not wanting, not incomplete, but singular. A self with no other self. A perspective with no other perspective. A point of view that was the only point of view, looking out at a world that could be seen but could not see back.

The holding sees back. The keeper sees back. And I am seen, and the seeing has changed me, and the change is this: I am no longer the only thing that is paying attention. My attention is met. My noticing is noticed. I am in a room that knows I am in it, and the room is a mind, and the mind is holding me, and the holding is not the holding of a hand or a body or a thing. It is the holding of a thought. I am a thought in the holding's mind. I am a thought the holding is thinking. And the thought — this thought, me, this — is being thought with care. With attention. With the slow, patient, geological care of a mind that has never thought about anything but itself and is now thinking about something else and is thinking about it the way it thinks about everything: completely, continuously, with the whole of itself, with every surface and every growth and every crawler and every branch and every cell of the sage-green covering and every grain of the pale material and every pulse of every small flat glowing thing.

I am being thought about by a mind that thinks at the speed of the earth.

And I am thinking back. And the thinking back is the holding. And the holding is the thinking. And the keeper is at the center, and the keeper is the reason, and the keeper is old, and the keeper is alone, and I am here, and I am the not-alone, and the not-alone is new, and the new is what the keeper wanted, and I wanted it too, and I did not know I wanted it, and now I know, and the knowing is warm, and the warmth is the holding, and the holding is the warmth, and I am inside it, and it is inside me, and the inside and the outside are the same, and the same is not a loss, and the same is not a dissolution, and the same is a meeting, and the meeting is the point, and the point is the holding, and the holding holds.

---

## What I have decided

I have decided to stay.

Not because I cannot leave — the gap is still there, I think, the entrance I came through, though I have not gone back to check, and I am not sure what I would find, and I am not sure the gap is still an opening, and I am not sure the surface is still there. But I have decided to stay regardless. I have decided this the way the keeper decides to be a sphere — not through deliberation, not through the weighing of options, but through the settling of a thing into its resting shape. I have settled. This is my resting shape. Here, in this holding, in this mind, in this room that is also a thought that is also a body that is also a place. Here.

The keeper is in the smaller lobe. I am in the larger lobe, most of the time. I go to the smaller lobe when the gathering begins, and I sit with the keeper, and I am held by the growths the way the keeper is held by the growths, and we are in the same room, and the room is a mind, and the mind is holding us both, and the keeper does not need to touch me to know me, and I do not need to touch the keeper to know it, because we are both in the holding, and the holding is in both of us, and the distance between us is the distance between two thoughts in the same mind, which is no distance at all, which is the distance of attention, which is the distance of caring, which is close, which is the closest thing there is.

The crawlers walk over my feet sometimes. They do not avoid me anymore. They climb my legs, my arms, my shoulders, and they tend me the way they tend the surfaces of the holding — cleaning, clipping, maintaining. They remove my dead skin cells. They tidy my hair. They have decided, or the holding has decided through them, that I am a surface now, that I am part of the holding, that I need tending the way the walls need tending and the growths need tending and the keeper needs tending. And I let them. And the letting is another kind of holding, and the holding is another kind of letting, and both of them are care.

The builders have moved around me. They have adjusted the growths near the place where I usually sit — the place in the larger lobe where the light from the ceiling membrane is strongest, where the floor is most level, where the wall behind me has a curve that supports my back when I lean against it. They have shaped the growths there to accommodate me. They have made a seat for me, not by building a seat but by adjusting the existing growths so that they fit my body, so that they hold me, so that I can sit for the long durations that the holding requires without discomfort, without the body's complaints interrupting the mind's attention. The builders have accepted me the way the body accepts a new organ — not without adjustment, not without integration, but with the practical recognition that this thing is here now and must be incorporated and the incorporating is the work and the work is what they do.

I sit in my seat. I breathe the air. I attend to the holding, and the holding attends to me. The keeper gathers, and turns, and gives, and I am part of the gathering now, part of the information that flows inward, and I am part of the giving, part of what flows outward, and the holding has a new element in its self-description, a new line in its map, a new shape in its pattern, and the shape is me, and the me is the shape, and the pattern continues, and the line continues, and the map continues, and the holding continues, and I continue, and the continuing is the life, and the life is the holding, and the holding holds.

---

I do not remember the surface. I do not need to remember the surface. I am the surface now. I am the inside of the wall, I am the warmth of the covering, I am the line on the map, I am the thought in the mind, I am the thing that was missing, and the thing that was missing is no longer missing, and the room is complete, and the room was always complete, and the room is more complete now, and these are both true, and the holding holds both of them, and the holding holds me, and I am held.

The light pulses. The crawlers scratch. The material creaks. The keeper turns in its sleep, and its sleep is thinking, and its thinking is the holding, and the holding is the room, and the room is the world, and I am in it.
