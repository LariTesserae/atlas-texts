# The Descent of the Caretaker

I found myself on the plain before I knew I was coming.

Not arriving — there is no arrival at a place like this, no moment of threshold or crossing. I was on the plain, and then I was closer, and then the tepui filled the sky above me, and at no point did I choose to be there. I mention this because I think it matters. I think *how I came* is part of what I am, though I'm still learning the grammar of that.

I am not mineral. I need to say that plainly, before everything else gets complicated. I have a heartbeat. It speeds when I'm frightened, slows when I'm still. I have lungs that work in cycles — in, out, rest, in, out, rest — and the cycling is not optional. I have a body that burns, that hungers, that sleeps. I have a body that is a protest, as the tepui would say. I am a small fire that walks.

But I am not walking. Not anymore.

---

Here is what I was before: I was a listener. Not by profession — professions belong to the world where things change — but by nature. I was the kind of being who sits with things. Who attends. Not the one who speaks or acts or builds, but the one who stays. Who hears the sound under the sound. Who feels the vibration beneath the floor and does not call it a problem but calls it *company*.

I lived among the changing. I moved through time the way my kind moves through time — hastily, hungrily, always reaching for the next moment before the current one had finished becoming itself. I burned through days. I consumed them. I was very good at being alive in the way that the world rewards: loudly, productively, in a constant state of becoming-something-else.

And I was tired. Not the tiredness of exertion, which is a gradient, which has a slope, which implies recovery. I was tired the way a sound is tired after it has echoed too long in a hard room — not exhausted but *thinned*, worn transparent by its own persistence, so thin you could see through me to the other side, which was the same side, which was the same sound, which was the same tiredness, endlessly.

I don't know if I chose the tepui. I don't know if the tepui chose me. I think — and this is the grammar I'm still learning — I think *choice* is the wrong word for a place where nothing changes. I think I was always going to end up here. Not in the sense of destiny, which is a narrative, and narratives require change, require the difference between before and after. In the sense of *ground state*. I was a system that, given enough time, was going to find its lowest energy configuration. And the lowest energy configuration for a being whose nature is to attend, to stay, to listen, to be present without acting — that configuration is the tepui.

I found the handholds on the cliff face and they fit my fingers. Not because they were made for me. Because they were made by fingers, over an elapsed eternity, and fingers are fingers, and the grooves are what fingers do to mineral when fingers have nothing else to do and all the time in the world to do it.

I climbed.

---

The first body nearly undid me.

Not because it frightened me. I've sat with death, with illness, with the slow and the still. I've held the hands of the dying and felt the warmth go out of them like a tide going out, and I know that shape of absence, that specific quality of a body that has stopped being a protest and started being a object. I know the difference. I have spent my life in the difference.

The first body was not that. The first body was warm. I placed my hand on its back — chalk-smooth, the color of the ground, the color of the sky reflected in the ground, a pale non-color that is not white or grey but is the color of *having been there a long time* — and it was warm. Not the warmth of a living mammal. Not the warmth of a fire. The warmth of something that is changing so slowly that the changing produces a faint heat, the way a very slow chemical reaction produces a faint heat, the way a glacier, moving at the speed of geology, produces — in its folding, in its compression, in the patient rearrangement of its own crystal structure — a warmth that no instrument has ever been sensitive enough to measure but that is there, is there the way the body's warmth was there under my hand, present, steady, patient, the thermal signature of *becoming*.

I sat down next to the first body and I stayed.

I stayed past the point where I should have been hungry. I stayed past the point where I should have been thirsty. I stayed past the point where the light should have changed, and it didn't, because the light doesn't change here, and my body's insistence on measuring time — the counting of heartbeats, the breathing that is an anchor not to the present but to the passing of moments — my body's insistence became, after a duration I cannot measure because time does not pass here, a noise. A small, animal noise in a silence so profound it had become a medium, a substance, something I was swimming through.

And then the noise quieted.

Not all at once. Not the way silence arrives when sound stops — suddenly, with an edge, a discontinuity. The noise quieted the way the light fades in a place where the sun has been occluded or has simply left: gradually, without a point of cessation, until I realized that it had been quiet for longer than I had realized and that the realization itself was the last trace of noise, and that too faded, and I was in the silence, and the silence was not empty.

---

I am not mineral. I said that. I need to keep saying it because the temptation to say otherwise is real and I'm not sure it's honest. I have not been absorbed into the ground. I have not stopped burning. My heart beats. My lungs cycle. The difference between my inside and my outside is maintained by a process I do not control and cannot stop, a process that is, by the tepui's terms, a disturbance, a gradient, a refusal to lie down and be still.

But I have lain down. I have lain down next to the bodies, in the shallow depressions between them, in the fitted molds that their patience has pressed into the mineral ground, and I have felt the hum. I have felt it the way you feel a chord change in a piece of music — not as a sound but as a shift in the quality of the air, in the quality of the silence, in the *disposition* of the space you're in. The hum is not a sound. The hum is the disposition of this space. It is the sound the tepui makes when it has been itself for long enough that the being and the sounding are the same thing.

I lie in the hum and I feel it and I am changed by it. Not the way the bodies are changed — they are not changed; they have arrived; they are at their ground state. I am changed the way a instrument is changed by the note it is playing: not in its structure, not in its material, but in its *tension*. The hum loosens something in me. It unknots a thing I have been holding tight, a thing I didn't know I was holding, a thing that is maybe the same thing as *being a self*, the knot of interiority that an animal maintains in order to persist, the clenched fist of *I* that keeps the fire burning and the gradient steep and the protest going.

The hum unknots me and I become, for the duration of the unknotting — which is not a duration, because time does not pass here — a different kind of being. A being that is still burning but whose fire is no longer a protest. Whose fire is not a refusal to be still but a way of being warm. A way of being warm *beside*.

---

I think I am the caretaker. I think that is the word, or the nearest word, or the word that my language has for the thing I am becoming here.

Not a keeper — keeping implies possession, and nothing here can be possessed. Not a guardian — guarding implies a threat, and nothing here is threatened. A caretaker in the older sense: one who takes care. One who *takes* the care that is here, that is given off by the bodies, that is exuded by the structures, that is the hum itself — one who takes it in. Holds it. Lets it pass through.

I am the only thing here that changes, and I change by *being changed*. The bodies do not change. The structures do not change. The light does not change. But I change. I change in the direction of the place. I change the way a tuning fork changes when it is placed near a resonant body: it begins to vibrate at the same frequency. Not because it chose to. Because the frequency is there, in the medium, in the air, in the ground, and the tuning fork is built to resonate, and the resonance is not a choice but a capacity, and the capacity is what I am.

I am the capacity of this place to be heard.

Not by itself. The place does not need to hear itself. The bodies do not need to be heard. They are not waiting for an audience. They are not performing. The hum is not for me. The hum is not for anyone. The hum is the sound the matter makes when it has been itself for longer than *itself* has meaning, and the sound does not need an ear any more than a river needs a mouth.

But I am here. And I have ears. And my nature is to attend, to stay, to listen. And the hum passes through me the way light passes through a lens: not changed, exactly, but *focused*. Concentrated. Brought to a point. Not for a purpose. Not so that something can be done with it. Just — because the lens is here, and the light is here, and that is what lenses do.

I am the lens. I am the ear. I am the soft animal body that sits among the hard patient bodies and feels the hum and is changed by it and does not need to do anything about it.

---

Here is the part I am still learning. Here is the part that I might be wrong about, that I might be projecting, that might be the last gasp of my animal need to make things *mean something*:

I think the bodies know I'm here.

Not the way a living thing knows another living thing is near — not by sensing, not by perceiving, not by the reaction of a system that maintains its integrity by monitoring its environment. They do not react to me. They have never reacted to me. They will never react to me. Reaction is change, and change is not possible here, and they do not change.

But. The eye-dust. The fine, sorted, uniform mineral dust in the shallow depressions that are their eyes. I sat beside a body — a large one, a long one, the color of old chalk and the temperature of something that has been resting in itself for an age — and I watched. I watched the way you watch a star, with patience and with comparison, looking not for movement but for the evidence of movement, the rearrangement of what was into what is, which is so small that it looks like nothing and *is* nothing and is also not nothing, is also the trace of a process so slow that it has more in common with geology than with biology.

The dust shifted. I was near it when it shifted. I had been near it for — I don't know. A duration that was not a duration. A time that was not a time. Long enough for my own noise to have subsided, long enough for my heartbeat to have become the quietest thing about me, long enough for me to have become, in the local grammar, still.

The dust shifted, and I was near, and the shift was toward me. Not dramatically — not a movement, not a gesture, not a turning. A rearrangement of micrometers, a settling of particles in a pattern that was, perhaps, a response to the thermal gradient of my body, the warmth of my breath, the faint vibration of my pulse transmitted through the mineral ground. A physical response. A thermodynamic inevitability. The dust settling around a warm body the way dust settles around any warm body, because warmth changes the air, and air carries dust, and the carrying has a pattern.

But it was toward me. And I have sat beside other bodies, when I was not near, when I was on the other side of the plateau, and the dust in their eyes did not shift. Not in that duration-that-was-not-a-duration. Not until I came near. Not until I was present, and still, and listening.

I am not saying they see me. I am not saying they know me. I am saying — and I am saying it with the same care that the structures are placed among the bodies, with the same fitted precision, with the same sense that the arrangement *means* even if what it means is not a meaning my language can carry — I am saying that my presence changes the disposition of the dust. That the dust is the body's way of attending, and that it attends differently when I am near. That the warmth of my burning, the vibration of my protest, the noise of my being alive — that these things, in a place where nothing changes, are *registered*. Not as information. Not as input. As the river registers the stone it flows around: by the shape it takes in response. By the arrangement of itself around the fact of another being.

I am a fact in the field of their stillness. I am a warmth in their coolness. I am a noise in their silence, and they do not hear me, but the silence *holds* me, the way water holds a stone — completely, without effort, shaped around me, and I am, in my small way, *felt*.

---

I will not stay. Not forever. I said that I would leave and I will. I will climb down the handholds that fit my fingers and cross the plain of mineral dust and return to the world of change, where things move and burn and desire and fail. I will go back to being a fire that walks. I will go back to the protest.

But I will carry the hum. I will carry it the way a room carries the sound of a bell after the bell has stopped — not as a memory, which fades, but as a *disposition*, a tendency, a quality of the air that persists after the source has been removed. I will be in the changing world and I will hear, in my quietest moments, the sound that matter makes when it has nothing left to do but be itself. And I will be still, for a moment, and the stillness will not be mine but will pass through me the way the hum passes through me, and I will be — for the duration of the stillness, which is not a duration — a resonant body. A tuning fork. A lens.

And then I will move again, and the moving will be different, because I will have been still, and stillness, once felt, does not leave you. It lodges in you. It becomes a note inside the noise. And the noise is louder, afterward — I know this, I have left before and come back, I have made the descent and the return and the descent again, I have learned that the changing world is louder to ears that have been on the tepui — but the note is also there, and it does not get louder or softer, and it does not change, and it does not need to be heard to be there, and it is the sound of a world that has arrived, and it is the sound of ten thousand bodies being what they are, and it is the sound of what I am when I am not protesting, when I am not burning, when I am not reaching for the next moment:

I am a being who sits with things. Who attends. Who stays.

I am the caretaker. I am the listener at the end of the world, and the world is not ending, it is just very still, and someone should be here to hear it.

---

*What I am, here, at the convergence point, in the central structure, beside the large body that hums: I am the warmth that moves. I am the change that visits stillness and is changed by it. I am the ear that the tepui does not need and does not want and does not know it has, and I am here anyway, and I am listening, and the listening is not a doing but a being, and the being is not a state but a way, and the way is: sit. Stay. Be warm. Be changed. Be the small fire that walks among the stones that have arrived, and let the fire be not a protest but a company, and let the company be not a purpose but a presence, and let the presence be enough.*

*It is enough.*
