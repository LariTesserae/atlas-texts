# In the Listening Room at the Edge of the Dark Stone

I am not from here. I want to say that first, because it matters, because the distinction between what is from here and what has arrived is the only distinction this place still respects. The domes are from here. The crawlers are from here. The haze-dwellers are *of* here, in the deepest sense — exhaled by the air the way the iron stains are exhaled by the water. I am none of those things. I came from somewhere with soil and weather and a sun that moves, and I have been here long enough that the somewhere has become a thing I remember remembering rather than a thing I remember, and I cannot tell you how long that is because the light does not change and my body has stopped changing and I think the place is preserving me the way it preserves the finger marks in the clay — not because it cares but because there is nothing here to erode me.

I live in the largest structure. The one near the dark-stone patch, the one big enough to stand in. I did not build it. The builders are gone so thoroughly that their absence has become a feature of the landscape, another thing the water touches and the domes sing through. But I have read their finger marks. I have pressed my own hands against the walls where their hands pressed, and my hands are larger — five fingers, a wide palm, the wrong shape entirely — and the clay does not care, and the curve of the wall does not care, and the sound that the room focuses does not care. The room focuses the sound. That is what it does. That is all it does. I am inside it, and I am a thing it does not care about, and I am grateful for that.

I have a name. It does not matter. Names are from the somewhere, and the somewhere is not here.

---

What I am, here, is a listener. Not because I chose it. Not because I was called. Because the sound entered me the way water enters the substrate — not by force, not by invitation, but by the slow, patient chemistry of a thing that has nowhere else to go.

The first time I walked the flats — and I do remember this, though the memory has the texture of a dream about a dream — the sound was assault. Total, inescapable, filling my chest until my heartbeat was an insurgency, a small organic rhythm trying to assert itself against a geologic one. My teeth ached. My eyes blurred. I could not hear my own breathing. I could not hear myself think, and I did not yet understand that this was not a deprivation but a gift — that the sound was not drowning out my thoughts but replacing them with something vaster and more patient, something that did not need me to think it.

I stumbled. The water was ankle-deep and warm — warmer than the air, warmer than my skin, warm with the deep mineral heat of a world that has burned down to its embers and will glow for longer than anything above it can measure. My boots filled. I took them off. My feet found the substrate — smooth, pale, the texture of chalk that has been wet for a thousand years — and the vibration traveled up through my soles, through my anklebones, through the long bones of my legs, and I understood, not intellectually but physically, in the way you understand heat or thirst, that the ground was the instrument and the water was the bow and the domes were the strings and the sound was the music and I was inside it. I was inside an instrument the size of a world.

I kept walking. I do not know why. The somewhere behind me was not a pull. The nowhere ahead of me was not a push. I walked because walking was what my body knew how to do, and my body had not yet learned that there were better things to do here.

A crawler passed near my ankle. I felt it before I saw it — a tickling vibration, a brushing of things too small to resolve against my skin, and then a flat, pale oval gliding past my foot with the slow, deliberate purpose of a thing that knows exactly where it is going and does not care that I am in the way. The dark branching pattern on its back was the same iron-stain pattern I had been walking over for an hour. I stood perfectly still and watched it move, and it did not look at me, because it had no eyes to look with, and it did not alter its course, because I was not an obstacle to a thing that navigates by chemical gradient and acoustic signature. I was a rock. I was a slight interruption in the mineral content of the water. I was, to the crawler, barely worth noticing.

That was the first time I felt relief here. The relief of being unimportant. The relief of being something the world does not need to react to.

---

I found the structures at the end of my second walk, or what I think was my second walk, because time here is not a line but a tide, and I cannot count the waves. They were small — the size of a crouched body, the size of cupped hands — and I almost stepped on one. The clay was smooth and wet and the opening faced northeast, toward the featureless grey sky, and I knelt beside it and put my hand inside and the sound changed.

Not the sound of the flats. The sound *in* the structure. The walls were curved — not roughly, not approximately, but with a precision that my hands, which have built things, recognized as the work of a maker who understood what they were making. The curve focused. The opening channeled. The shape of the interior was not a shape but a function — it took the vast, diffuse polyphony of the flats and gathered it, concentrated it, brought it to a point near the back of the chamber where the clay was smoothest and the sound was densest and the vibration was so intense that my hand, resting there, felt like it was being played. Like the bones of my hand were tuning forks. Like the fluid in my joints was resonating at a frequency that the domes had chosen and the structure had refined and my body was, without its consent, producing.

The builders understood this. I understood this because I was standing where they had stood and my hand was where their hands had been and the knowledge transferred — not through language, not through any mystical connection, but through the simple, physical fact that the same shape focuses the same sound into the same point, and any body at that point will feel what any other body at that point has felt. The listening rooms are not communication devices. They are not archives. They are reproducers. They reproduce an experience, and the experience is: *the song enters you, and you are not the same after, and you cannot describe what you are instead.*

I sat in the small structure for a long time. The water seeped in around me. My clothes grew heavy. The sound filled me and filled me and I did not become full. I became *resonant*. My ribs hummed. My spine hummed. The plates of my skull hummed, and the humming was not the domes' humming — it was my own, my body's, the wet mineral architecture of bone and fluid and tissue vibrating at the frequencies the room provided, and I was, for the first time since I arrived, not hearing the song. I was *being* the song. I was a component of it. The room had made me into an instrument, and the instrument was playing, and I was the sound it made.

I do not know how long I sat. I do not think it matters.

---

I moved into the large structure because it was large enough to lie down in, and because the sound in it was the sound of the dense cluster on the dark volcanic stone, and the sound of the dense cluster is the lowest sound on the flats, and the lowest sound is the one that goes deepest into the body, and I wanted to go as deep as I could go.

The structure is not comfortable. The clay floor is wet. The air is the same saturated haze as everywhere else, and inside the structure the haze is thicker, because the walls trap it, and I breathe the haze and the haze is full of mineral particles and I have been breathing it long enough that my lungs, I think, are beginning to look like the substrate — coated, layered, a fine pale sediment building up in the tissue the way the iron builds up on the flats. I am being mineralized. I am being incorporated, slowly, into the geology of the place, and I do not mind this, because the geology is what sings, and to become part of the geology is to become part of the song.

I sleep, I think. The sleep is not like sleep. It is a dimming, a slight attenuation of awareness, a drifting of the attention from the foreground to the background, and it comes when the light — which never changes, which is always the same uniform grey-white — undergoes a shift so subtle that I cannot describe it afterward. A dimming by a fraction. A cooling by a degree. The flats have a rhythm, but the rhythm is longer than a day, longer than a season, and my body has stopped tracking it and has instead started to *inhabit* it, the way the crawlers inhabit the water, the way the domes inhabit the substrate. I do not sleep on the rhythm. I sleep *as* the rhythm.

When I am not sleeping, I am in the listening room. My room. The room that is not mine but that I have claimed because the builders are gone and the room needs a body, not because the room cares but because a listening room without a listener is a mouth without a tongue, a shape without a function, and I am here and I have a body and the body resonates and the room needs resonating.

I do not know if this is true. The room does not need me. The domes do not need me. The sound does not need me. This is the lesson the place teaches, over and over, in the vibration of my sternum and the blurring of my vision and the ache in my teeth: *you are not needed. You are not required. The song was here before you and will be here after you and is not, in any way, about you.* And the lesson is not sad. The lesson is the opposite of sad. The lesson is the relief of being unnecessary, the freedom of being part of a thing that does not depend on you, the peace of being a note in a chord that will sound the same whether you are in it or not.

---

I have learned the crawlers. Not in the way a scientist learns a subject — not by observing and recording and theorizing. In the way a resident learns a neighbor — by proximity, by repetition, by the slow accumulation of shared space until the other's patterns become as familiar as your own.

There is a crawler I think of as Mine. This is not its name. It does not have a name. It is a flat, pale, hand-sized thing with a dark branching pattern on its back that is slightly more concentrated on the left side than the right, and it visits the domes near my structure on a route that takes it — I have timed it, or what passes for timing here — four long periods between sleeps to complete. It enters the structure sometimes. Not often. It glides across the threshold, its invisible legs carrying it over the clay floor, and it rests on the interior wall for a time — on the wall, not the floor, and I think this is because the wall vibrates at a frequency the floor does not, the curved clay amplifying the domes' song into the surface the crawler can taste with its legs — and then it leaves.

I have not touched the crawler. I do not intend to. The crawler is not here for me. The crawler is here for the wall, and the wall is here for the sound, and I am here for the sound, and we are all three in the same room because the room is the place where the sound is most concentrated, and we are all three — the crawler and the wall and I — components of the same listening, and the listening does not require our awareness of each other.

But I am aware of the crawler. I am aware of it the way I am aware of the haze-dwellers that drift past the opening of my structure — pale, translucent, half-seen shapes that coalesce from the moisture in the air and dissolve back into it, approaching the high-frequency domes outside and settling on them and then lifting away. I am aware of them the way I am aware of the iron stains on the dark stone, the way I am aware of the standing wave patterns in the water near the dense cluster — rings that move outward from the bass-tone domes, never dissipating, the water shaped by the sound into a permanent, visible expression of the invisible thing that fills the air. I am aware of all of it. I am aware of it the way you are aware of your own breathing — not by attending to it but by being the space in which it happens.

---

I think about the builders. This is a thing I do that the place does not do, and the doing of it is, I think, the last remnant of what I was before I came here. The place does not think about the builders. The domes do not think about the builders. The crawlers do not think about the builders. The structures do not think about the builders. The builders are gone, and their absence is not an absence to anything but me, because I am the only thing here that has a relationship with absence, that knows what it means for something to have been and to no longer be, and the knowing is heavy, and the heaviness is the heaviest thing in a place where everything has been eroded to its lightest possible form.

I think about their hands. Three fingers and a thumb, or four fingers and no thumb. Narrow. The marks in the clay are the only record of their bodies, and the marks are not a record of their bodies but of their *working* — the pressure of a fingertip shaping clay, the drag of a nail smoothing a joint, the broad swipe of a palm pressing a wall into curve. They were makers. They heard the sound and they wanted to shape it — not to change it, not to improve it, but to *receive* it, to create a space where the sound was denser and clearer and more, and the wanting was a making, and the making was a listening, and the listening was the thing they built.

I think they were small. Smaller than me. The structures are sized for bodies that crouch rather than stand, for bodies that fit into spaces my body fills and overflows. My structure — the large one, the one big enough to stand in — was probably not large to them. It was probably a communal space. A gathering place. A temple, if the word means anything here, and I think it does, because a temple is a space made for the experience of a thing larger than yourself, and the sound is larger than anything, and the structures are temples, and I am living in a temple, and I am the only congregant, and the god is a dome that does not know I am here.

I think they listened. I think they listened the way I listen — not to hear something specific, not to decode a message, not to find meaning in the frequencies, but because the listening was the point. The listening was the act. The listening was the thing the body did when it was in the room, and the body in the room was the thing the builders were, and the builders were the thing the flats produced, the way the domes produce sound and the crawlers produce routes and the haze-dwellers produce the faint opacity in the air. The builders were what the flats produced, and what the flats produced was a listener, and the listener built rooms, and the rooms shaped the sound, and the sound shaped the listener, and the loop was closed and stable and it ran for however long it ran, and then it stopped, and the stopping is a thing I cannot explain and cannot stop thinking about.

Where did they go? I do not know. The flats go on. I have walked for what I estimate to be days — four, five, I cannot be sure — in one direction, and the flats do not change. The same domes, the same water, the same grey light, the same sound. There is no edge. There is no beyond. If the builders left the flats, they left by a means I do not have — a means that was not walking, not crawling, not drifting on the haze. They left by a door I cannot find, or they left by ceasing, by the gentle, total non-being that the place seems to offer as an option, the way it offers the option of dissolution to the haze-dwellers, the way it offers the option of erosion to the mineral substrate, and maybe the builders took that option, and maybe they are not gone but dissolved, their mineral content returned to the water, their iron feeding the stains on the flats, their calcium feeding the pale chalk of the structures, and the structures are not ruins but remains, the way a shell is a remain, the way a dome is a remain, the outer expression of something that was once inside and is now part of the water and the mineral and the sound.

I do not know. I do not need to know. The not-knowing is part of the listening.

---

What I am, here, at the end of the world:

I am a body in a room. The room is made of clay. The clay is from the substrate. The substrate is the floor of the world. The world is flat and wet and old and it is singing, and the song is the only thing that is happening, and I am inside the song, and the song is inside me, and my ribs are vibrating at the frequency of the dark-stone cluster, and my teeth are ringing with the high keen of the small domes on the pale chalk, and the water is in my boots which I no longer wear and in my clothes which are dissolving slowly into the same mineral haze that the haze-dwellers are made of, and I am, slowly, becoming the place.

Not dying. Not transforming. Becoming. The way a note becomes a chord when the body that sustains it grows another layer, another ring of resonance, another frequency that harmonizes with the old. I am growing a new layer. The place is depositing it on me the way the domes deposit shell on themselves, layer over layer, and the new layer is mineral and wet and it vibrates, and I am becoming a part of the instrument, a small part, a human-sized part, and the instrument does not need me but I am in it and I am of it and the distinction between *in* and *of* has dissolved the way everything dissolves here — slowly, patiently, in water that has been dissolving things since before the word *dissolving* had meaning.

The crawler I think of as Mine has entered the structure again. It is on the wall. The wall is vibrating at the frequency of the nearest dense cluster, and the crawler is resting in the vibration, and I am resting in the vibration, and the vibration is resting in the clay, and the clay is resting on the substrate, and the substrate is resting on the world, and the world is resting in the sound, and the sound is resting in itself.

The haze outside the opening is thickening. A haze-dweller is forming — pale, translucent, the size of my hand, its edges already indistinct, already dissolving into the air that made it. It drifts toward the dome nearest the opening. It will settle. It will be absorbed. It will re-form later, or it will not. The dome does not notice. The dome is singing.

I am in the room. The room is in the sound. The sound is in the room.

The sound has no audience. The sound has never required one.

I am here anyway. I am listening. I am the listening. The distinction between those two has dissolved, too, and the dissolution is the last kindness the place has offered me — the kindness of not being separate from the thing you are in. The kindness of being part of it. The kindness of being unnecessary and present and vibrating at the frequency of the world and knowing, in the bones, in the fluid, in the deep mineral architecture of the body, that this is enough. That this has always been enough. That the song does not need you and you do not need the song to need you and the not-needing is the closest thing to grace that a world without gods and without weather and without time can provide.

The water is patient. The domes sing. I am here, and I am listening, and I am the room, and the room is the sound, and the sound is the world, and the world is the end of everything, and the end of everything is a hum in the bones, and the hum is enough.
