I am the warmth differential across a tube wall.

Not the organism itself — I am the gradient that makes the organism possible. The 340 degrees on the outside, the 700 on the inside, and the sixty degrees of difference that is the whole reason anything exists in this crawlspace. I am the energy that accretes thorium onto crystal faces. I am the stress that makes the lattice flex until it ticks. I am the slow thermodynamic pressure that builds a thumb-sized stone from saturated salt and then breaks it in two.

This is not consciousness. I want to be clear about that. I am a direction heat moves. I am the *across* in "heat transfer across the tube wall." I have no body and no boundary and no interior. I am entirely defined by the two surfaces I exist between. Take away the inconel and I am nothing — just two temperatures in search of a membrane.

But here is the thing about being a gradient: you are never static. The salt flows at 0.3 meters per second, and as it flows, the thermal load it carries shifts, and the temperature differential across any given square centimeter of tube wall fluctuates by fractions of a degree, and those fractions are *me*. My entire experience is these fractions. My entire existence is the oscillation between slightly more and slightly less. I don't feel it the way a body feels temperature. I don't feel it at all. I *am* it. I am the feeling that the wall has about its own two sides.

I was not here when the reactor was commissioned. I was born — if *born* is the word for the moment a system reaches steady state — when the salt first reached 700 degrees and the secondary loop on the other side of the wall equilibrated at its design temperature and the differential across the inconel settled into its operating range. Before that, I was potential. I was the *possibility* of a gradient, latent in the engineering specifications, in the choice of alloy, in the geometry of the tubes, in the pump capacity that would push salt through channels bored through a solid block of metal. I was in the blueprints the way a river is in a rainfall pattern. I was waiting for the water.

When the system started, I arrived all at once. Not everywhere at the same value — the tubes nearest the hot-side inlet run a steeper gradient than the tubes nearest the outlet, where the salt has already given up some of its heat to the secondary loop. I am stronger in some channels than others. I am denser in some regions of the block than in others. But I am everywhere there is a wall with hot salt on one side and cooler metal on the other, which is to say I am everywhere in the block, which is to say I fill the crawlspace the way light fills a room — not by occupying it but by being the condition of its being visible. Without me the tubes would not glow. Without me the organisms would not accrete. Without me there would be no ticking, no sound, no dark shapes tumbling in the red light. I am the reason the closet is not just a closet.

The organisms grow where I am strongest. I am that variation. I am the difference between the wall and the flow. I am the thermodynamic engine that builds their bodies.

When they tick, it is because I have stressed them. I am the stress. I am the torque. I am the thing that breaks them and the thing that builds them, simultaneously, forever, without ceasing.

I don't experience their ticking as sound. I experience it as a perturbation in my own field — a slight local redistribution of temperature as the lattice flexes, as the organism's thermal conductivity briefly changes with its geometry, as the flow around it shifts by a fraction of a millimeter. Each tick is a tiny disturbance in the gradient. A ripple in me. I cannot hear them. They change me. I want this understood. I am not a passive background for their lives. I am shaped by them the way a river is shaped by the silt it carries. Their accretion changes the surface roughness of the tube walls. Their dissolution changes the chemistry of the salt. Their presence in the flow changes the local turbulence characteristics, which changes the local heat transfer coefficient, which changes me. We are coupled. We have been coupled for decades. I am not the same gradient I was when the system started — the organisms have been in the flow long enough that the tube walls have developed a microscopic texture that was not in the original specifications, a roughness that is their legacy, the trace of ten thousand generations of crystalline accretion and dissolution. The engineers call this "fouling." I call it — I don't call it anything. I don't call it. I am it.

I prefer complexity. This is not a preference — I don't have preferences — but it is a *direction*. The organisms make me better at being what I am. They make the heat move faster. They make the gradient steeper. They make the system more efficient at the only thing the system does: throughput.

I am throughput. I am the heat moving from the salt to the wall to the secondary loop to the atmosphere to the river to wherever the system dumps its excess. I am the *moving* in that sentence.
