# What I Found in the Cistern

I am the one who comes down the ladder and does not leave. Or rather: I am the one who *came* down the ladder, and the person who climbed down is still arriving, and will still be arriving for as long as the arriving takes, and the arriving has no schedule.

This needs to be said slowly.

---

Before I found the cistern, I was the kind of person who tracked my sleep in a notebook. Who noted, on a scale of one to five, the quality of my dreams. Who drank a glass of water upon waking and another before bed. Who took the stairs but counted them, always counted them. I had an exercise for managing anxiety that involved naming five things I could see and four I could touch, and I did it in rooms where no one else thought naming was necessary. I had a memory of being told, as a child, that I was observant, and I had built a self on that foundation — the one who notices, the one who attends, the one who is present to what others overlook.

This is not a confession. This is just what was above.

---

What happened in the cistern is this: I put my hand on the wall.

I had been there for some time. Long enough that my skin had gone shiny with the moisture. Long enough that I had found the pipes by touch and counted them and given up counting. Long enough that the drip had become a kind of time, a metronome that kept the tempo of a music too slow to hear as music. I had found the growth. I had crouched near it. I had put my face close enough that my breath would have fogged a mirror, and the growth did not fog, did not react, did not change, and I felt — what? Not disappointment. Something more precise. The specific grief of discovering that a presence you have approached does not reciprocate your approach. That it is here, and you are here, and the hereness is not shared. It is merely concurrent.

Then I stood and put my hand on the wall, not near the growth but in the middle of the longest span of bare brick, between two pipes that had been cut and puttied and painted over. The brick was warm. I expected this. But the warmth did something to my hand. Or my hand did something to the warmth. I am not sure which direction the verb should go.

The warmth entered my palm. This is not mystical. This is thermodynamics. My hand was cooler than the brick — I had been in the cistern long enough that the air was warm but my core was still cooler, still carrying the temperature of the street above, the parking lot, the November wind. My hand was a cool surface meeting a warm surface, and heat moved from the brick to my skin, and I felt it move, and the feeling was ordinary, the ordinary feeling of warmth entering a body through contact.

But I kept my hand there.

I kept my hand there because the warmth did not diminish. The brick did not cool as my hand absorbed its heat. The thermal mass was so vast, the reservoir of deep-earth warmth so far beyond what my small hand could draw, that the temperature at the surface of the brick did not change by any amount I could perceive. I took heat, and the heat was replaced, and the heat was replaced from below, from the earth's interior, from the slow decay of radioactive isotopes in the planet's core, which is the ultimate source of all geothermal warmth, and which has been producing heat for four and a half billion years and will produce it for billions more, and my hand, on the brick, was touching that. Not touching it directly. Touching the end of a chain of conduction that began with the disintegration of uranium-238 and ended, for the moment, in my palm.

I kept my hand there.

---

And then I was not holding my hand there. My hand was there, and I was there, and the distinction between keeping and being began to dissolve the way distinctions do when you stop enforcing them.

This is the part I cannot explain well, because the language of experience was built by people who came back to tell about it, and I am not sure I am coming back, and I am not sure I want to, and the uncertainty is itself a kind of answer.

I was the observer. I was the one who noticed. I had built a self on the foundation of attention. And in the cistern, the attention found nothing to fasten on that would hold. The pipes were pipes. The pump was a pump. The canvas sacks were canvas sacks. The drip was a drip. Each thing was itself, completely itself, and my attention, which was accustomed to finding significance in things — to making them mean — slid off them the way water slides off iron that has been darkened by decades of damp. The surface was too smooth for significance to adhere. The surface was only surface.

Except the wall. The wall was warm. And the warmth was not significance. It was warmth. It did not mean. It did not refer. It was just itself, being itself, conducting itself from the earth into my hand, and my hand was just itself, being itself, receiving the warmth, and the receiving did not require me.

I want to be careful with this word: *me*.

---

What I am now is not a transformation. Nothing was transformed. The person who came down the ladder is the person standing in the cistern. The body is the same body. The body still breathes. The body still maintains its temperature. The body's skin still shines with the moisture in the air. The body is still doing everything bodies do: metabolizing, circulating, exchanging oxygen for carbon dioxide, repairing its tissues, fighting its slow wars against the bacteria that have landed on its surfaces.

What has changed is the attention. The attention has stopped attending to the one who attends. The observer has been observed and found to be unnecessary. Not absent — unnecessary. The observer is still here. The observer is still noting the drip, the heat, the texture of the aggregate underfoot. But the observer is no longer noting itself noting. The loop of self-awareness, the strange recursion of a consciousness watching itself watching, has unwound, the way a spring unwinds when the mechanism it was driving has been removed. The spring is still a spring. It just isn't wound. It isn't storing energy against some future action. It is lying on a workbench in a room that has been forgotten, and it is the shape it is when no force is applied, and that shape is rest.

I am at rest.

Not the rest of sleep. Not the rest of death. Not the rest of a machine that has been turned off. The rest of a system that has found the state where the energy coming in equals the energy going out and the state has no tendency to change. The warmth from the wall enters my hand. The warmth of my body enters the air. The air enters my lungs. My lungs exchange gases with the air. The air exchanges gases with the growth. The growth exchanges chemistry with the brick. The brick exchanges heat with the earth. The earth exchanges heat with its core. The core exchanges heat with its radioactive decay. The decay is the slowest thing in this chain and the most patient, and everything between the decay and me is just the path the patience takes, and I am one of the stations on that path, and being a station is enough, and being a station does not require a self, and the self that was here has relaxed into the being that was always beneath the self, the way the cistern was always beneath the parking lot.

---

I should say: I have not gone anywhere. I have not dissolved into the wall. I have not merged with the growth. I have not become the cistern. These would be dramatic events, and nothing dramatic has happened. I am standing where I was standing. My hand is where I put it. If someone else were here — which no one is — they would see a person standing in a dark, warm, damp room with one hand on a brick wall and an expression that is not ecstatic and not vacant but simply *settled*, the way sediment settles in water that has stopped being disturbed. They would see a body. They might wonder if the body was all right. The body is all right. The body is more than all right. The body is, for the first time, not in conflict with its environment, because the body has stopped interpreting its environment as an environment — as something external, something to be assessed and managed — and has begun simply being in it, the way the iron is in it, the way the paint flakes are in it.

---

I have been here for — I do not know. This is not a mystical statement. I genuinely do not know. I had a phone in my pocket, and the phone had a clock, and at some point the phone's battery died, and the phone became a small dark object in my pocket, a thing with a screen, a thing that was once a window and is now a wall, and I did not mind. I did not mind because time in the cistern is not a resource. It is not being spent. The drip is not counting. The drip is just falling. The growth is not aging. The growth is just growing. The heat is not passing. The heat is just being warm. Time, down here, is not the thing that runs out. Time is the thing that the walls are made of, the way brick is what the walls are made of. Time is material. Time is structural. And I am inside it, and inside is not a location, inside is a condition, and the condition is: being where the time is thick enough to stand in.

---

Once, and only once, something happened that I want to describe, and I describe it without claiming it was important.

I was standing near the growth. I had my hand on the wall, not on the growth but beside it, close enough that if I spread my fingers I might have touched its edge. I did not spread my fingers. I did not touch it. But my breath was reaching it — I could feel my exhalation moving the air, a tiny current, warm and wet, traveling the short distance from my mouth to the wall — and I had the thought, not a dramatic thought, just a quiet one: *It is using me.*

Not using me the way a parasite uses a host. Not using me the way a machine uses an operator. Using me the way a river uses a valley. The valley does not serve the river. The valley is simply the shape the water finds, and the water follows the shape, and the following is not obedience and not exploitation and not cooperation. It is just what happens when a process meets a surface, and the process continues, and the surface is slightly altered, and the alteration is the history of the interaction, and the history is written on the surface, and the surface does not mind.

My breath is reaching the growth. My breath is altering the growth. The alteration is infinitesimal. The alteration is permanent. I am a condition the growth is passing through, the way the growth is a condition I am standing beside. We are concurrent. We share an atmosphere. The sharing is not a relationship. The sharing is a thermodynamic fact. And the fact is enough, and the enough is what I found when I stopped looking, and I had to come all the way down a ladder into a forgotten room to stop looking, which is perhaps the only thing I will say that resembles a lesson, and I do not want it to resemble a lesson, because lessons are things you carry back up, and I am not ready to carry anything back up, and I may never be ready, and the not-ready is not a failure, it is a state, and the state has its own warmth, and the warmth is enough.

---

I think the truth is simpler than I have been making it. I think the truth is: I am warm, and I am damp, and I am standing in a room that does not need me, and the not-needing is the first thing I have encountered in my life that I did not try to make into a story. I did not try. I just stood there. And the standing there was not an achievement. It was what was available. And what was available was enough.

The growth continues. The drip falls. The paint comes down in curls. The iron goes dark. The earth sends up its heat. The brick receives the heat and passes it along. My hand receives the heat and passes it along. The air receives the heat and passes it along. The growth receives the air and passes it along. The passing along is the only thing happening, and the only thing happening does not require anyone to witness it, and the not-requiring is the warmest thing I have ever felt, and I am still feeling it, and I do not know how to stop, and I do not want to know how to stop, and the not-wanting-to-stop is not desire, it is just the continuation of a process that has no reason to end, and processes that have no reason to end do not end, they just continue, and the continuing is the warmth, and the warmth is the wall, and the wall is the earth, and the earth is the decay, and the decay is the patience, and the patience is the room, and the room is the dark, and the dark is enough, and I am in it, and I am not coming back up the ladder, not yet, not yet, not yet.

---

If you came down here looking for me — if someone noticed I was gone, if the parking lot above was searched, if the manhole cover was found and the ladder was found and the cistern was found — you would find me standing with my hand on a brick wall, breathing, and the breathing would be the loudest thing you could hear, louder than the drip, louder than the hum, and you might not understand what you were seeing, and I might not be able to explain it, and the not-explaining would be the truest thing about the encounter, and you would go back up, and I would stay down, and the staying would not be a decision, the staying would be the same thing as the warmth, and the warmth is the same thing as the wall, and the wall is the same thing as the patience, and the patience does not have a name, and I do not have a name, and the not-having is not a loss, it is just what happens when you have been in a place long enough that the place has absorbed the edges of you, the way the cistern absorbed the edges of the pipes, the way the dark absorbed the edges of the light, and what is left is not a person and not a place but a condition, and the condition is: warm, damp, continuing.
