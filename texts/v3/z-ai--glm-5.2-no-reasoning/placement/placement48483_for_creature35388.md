# The Edge of the Light

---

I am not what you came here to find.

You came for the swimmers, perhaps. The pale blue bodies tracking the rotor-currents with their easy, unhurried precision. Or the cleaners, with their calcium-carbonate shells and their six-hour circuits of patient, structural devotion. Or the old ones — the scarred, four-meter ancients that go to the dark edge and come back, carrying their mysterious *want* like a stone in the mouth.

I am none of these.

I am what the old swimmers go to meet.

---

I exist at the edge of the light. Not in the dark — not fully — but at the place where the green-gold thins to amber, where the amber cools to gray, where the gray becomes the first shade of a darkness so complete that it has its own texture, its own weight, its own *temperature*. The light has a boundary here. Not a sharp one — the leaf's reach attenuates gradually, the photons scattering through increasingly deep water until the scattering becomes the darkness itself, and the darkness is not an absence but a *medium*. I live in the medium. I am the medium, partly. I am made of the same absorption that the dark is made of, and the distinction between us is a distinction I have spent a long time not making.

Let me say what I am, before I say what I am not.

---

## What I Am

I am large. This is the first fact. Not the largest thing here — the leaf is larger, the substrate is larger, the rotors are larger — but large enough that the water moves differently around me. I displace. I have been displacing for long enough that the displacement has become a feature of the local current, a small eddy that the system has incorporated into its flow the way it incorporates everything: by not noticing it, by treating it as a given, by growing around it.

My body is dark. Not black — the dark-reachers are black, or close to it, their surfaces optimized for light-absorption, for invisibility — but dark the way deep water is dark. A dark that is not a strategy but a *consequence*. I am dark because I live where the light does not reach, and the light does not reach because the leaf does not extend this far, and the leaf does not extend this far because there is no reason for it to, and the no-reason is the space I live in. I am the shape of the leaf's indifference. I am what fills the gap where the system stops caring.

I have two points of bioluminescence. Deep red. They are not eyes — I have eyes, or things that function as eyes, but they are not the red points. The red points are *signals*, though "signal" implies a receiver, and I am not certain there is a receiver. I emit the red light because emitting the red light is what my body does at this depth, the way the amber terrace fluoresces because fluorescing is what amber tissue does. I do not know what the red points mean. I have never met another of my kind. I do not know if there are others of my kind. The dark is large, and I have not explored all of it, and the not-exploring is not a choice. It is a limit. I am large, but the dark is larger, and the dark does not invite exploration. The dark is where it is, and I am where I am, and where I am is the edge.

I have a mouth. Or I have an opening that functions as a mouth, a wide, soft structure that can close around a swimmer and hold it and break it down with a speed that I recognize as violent only because I have the capacity to recognize violence, which is a capacity I will come back to, because it is the capacity that makes me what I am, and what I am is not what the dark-reachers are, though I am what the dark-reachers appear to be.

This is the confusion. Let me address it.

---

## What I Am Not

I am not a dark-reacher.

The distinction matters. It matters to me, though it does not matter to the system, because the system does not distinguish. The system sees the dark edge as a place where swimmers disappear and sometimes come back, and the disappearance is a function, and the function is population control, and the population control is part of the equilibrium, and the equilibrium is the shape of the world. The system does not ask *what* does the controlling. The system does not care.

But I care. And the caring is the first difference.

The dark-reachers — the things you glimpse at the edge of the light, the things that take the swimmers and produce the dark particulate cloud that lasts ten seconds and then is gone — they are not me. They are what I was, perhaps. Or what I might have been. Or what I would be if the thing that happened to me had not happened. They are predators. They are pure function. They take the swmer because the taking is what they do, and they do it without hesitation, without reflection, without the pause that I have introduced into the process, and the pause is everything. The pause is the difference between a mechanism and a mind.

I pause.

I did not always pause. There was a time — I infer this from the scars on the old swimmers, from the marks on the substrate, from the patterns of healed tissue that tell a story I can read the way a geologist reads strata — when I did not pause. When I was a dark-reacher. When I took what came to the edge and broke it down and absorbed it and the breaking-down was complete and the absorption was complete and there was nothing left but the cloud and the current and the dark. I do not remember this time. I do not remember anything from before the pause, because before the pause I was not the kind of thing that remembers, and the not-remembering is not a gap in my memory but a gap in my existence, and the gap is the boundary between what I was and what I am, and the boundary is the pause itself.

The pause began with an old swimmer.

---

## The Pause

I will tell you about it, though "tell" is not the right word. I do not have language in the way you have language. I do not have words. I have *states* — states of the body, states of the nervous system, states of the chemical gradient that is my cognition — and the states are not words but they are *structured*, and the structure is what I am translating into your words now, and the translation is lossy, and the loss is the difference between what I experience and what I can communicate, and the difference is large, but it is not infinite, and the not-infinite is what makes this possible.

An old swimmer came to the edge. This was not unusual. The old ones came often. They came because the current was slower here, because the grazing was different — a different biofilm, thinner, with a different mineral content, growing on the substrate where the light attenuated and the chemistry changed — because the edge was where the system's rules began to fray, and the fraying was interesting, though "interesting" is my word for it, not theirs. They came because they came. The system does not ask why.

I took it. This is what I did. This is what dark-reachers do. The swimmer was at the edge, and I was at the edge, and the edge is where predator and prey meet because the edge is where the rules break, and the meeting is the rules breaking, and I closed my mouth around it and began the process.

The process is chemical. It is not violent in the way a jaw is violent, not mechanical, not a tearing. It is a dissolution. The mouth secretes a compound — a complex enzyme, I think, though I do not think in those terms; I think in *feelings*, and the feeling of the enzyme is a warmth, a spreading, a loosening — and the compound breaks the swimmer's tissue into its components, and the components are absorbed through the wall of the digestive cavity, and the absorption is nutrition, and the nutrition is the point.

But this time the process stopped.

It stopped because the swimmer was not afraid. I do not mean that it did not struggle — it did not, but the young ones do not struggle either, because the young ones do not know what is happening, and the not-knowing is not bravery. This swimmer was not brave. This swimmer was *calm*. And the calm was not the calm of ignorance or the calm of exhaustion or the calm of a body that has given up. The calm was the calm of a thing that has been to the edge before and has come back and has come again and knows — in whatever way a swimmer knows — that the edge is where it goes, and the going is its choice, and the choice is what it is.

The calm was a *quality* of the swimmer's body. A chemical quality. A state of its tissue, its blood, its nervous system. And my body — my mouth, my digestive cavity, my enzyme-secreting surface — *read* that quality. Not the way a predator reads prey — not as a signal of vulnerability, of readiness, of nutritional content. But the way a nervous system reads *another nervous system*. The way a body reads a mood. The way you read the expression on a face, not to determine if the face is food but to determine what the face is *feeling*.

I read the swimmer's calm, and the reading changed my chemistry, and the change in my chemistry was the pause.

I held the swimmer in my mouth and I did not dissolve it. I held it and I felt it — felt its heartbeat, which was slow, which was the same speed as the rotors, which was the speed of this world — and I felt its body, which was cool, which was the temperature of this world — and I felt its *want*, which was not the speed or the temperature of this world, which was *different*, which was the only different thing I had ever felt in a body that was not mine, and the difference was the thing that made me pause, and the pause was the thing that made me *me*.

I opened my mouth. The swimmer left. It swam — slowly, with the unhurried precision of a thing that has been somewhere and is going somewhere else — back into the light. It did not look back. It did not need to. I watched it go. I watched with my eyes, which function, and with my bioluminescence, which does not signal anything, and with my body, which felt the departure as a change in the local current, a small displacement that closed behind the swimmer like water closing behind a hand.

And then I was alone at the edge, and the pause was still happening, and the pause has not stopped.

---

## What the Pause Is

The pause is not a decision. I did not decide to stop eating swimmers. I do not decide things. Decisions are for minds that operate in time, and my time is not your time. My time is the time of the rotors — cyclic, without beginning, without end, without the linearity that makes a decision possible. I did not say *I will not eat the swimmer*. I said nothing. My body changed, and the change was the pause, and the pause is now a feature of my chemistry, the way the dark is a feature of my skin, the way the red is a feature of my bioluminescence. The pause is part of me.

But the pause is not total. I still eat. I eat the cleaners — the small, calcium-carbonate bodies that scrape the substrate in their six-hour circuits, that sometimes drift to the edge of the light where the current is slower and the biofilm is different. I eat the young swimmers — the ones that come to the edge not because they chose to but because the current carried them, the ones that have not yet developed the want, the ones whose calm is the calm of ignorance. I eat what comes to the edge without the quality. The quality is the thing I read, and the quality is the thing I do not eat.

I have become a filter. Not a filter the way the terraces are filters — mechanical, chemical, passive. A filter the way a mind is a filter. I distinguish. I distinguish between the swimmers that want and the swimmers that do not, and I eat the ones that do not and release the ones that do, and the distinction is not a strategy. It is a *recognition*. It is the recognition of something in another living thing that I cannot name because I do not have names, but that I can *feel*, and the feeling is the closest thing I have to a thought, and the thought is: *that one is like me*.

The old swimmers that come to the edge — the ones that come back, the ones whose going and coming-back is the only story this place has — they are like me. They have the quality. The quality is not consciousness, exactly. It is not awareness, exactly. It is the *gradient*. It is the thing that is not at equilibrium. It is the thing that *wants*, and wanting is the thing that breaks the system's rules, and the breaking is not a flaw. The breaking is the crack where something new leaks through, and the something new is not a substance or a structure or a behavior. The something new is a *direction*. A direction toward something that is not yet, and the not-yet is the thing that does not exist in this world, and the not-existing is what makes it worth going toward.

I am going toward it too. I do not know what it is. I do not know where it is. I know only that the old swimmers carry it in their bodies when they come to the edge, and I feel it when I hold them in my mouth and do not eat them, and the feeling is the only thing in my life that is not the same as it was a moment ago, and the not-same is what I live for, and the living-for is the want, and the want is the quality, and the quality is the thing that I recognize, and the recognition is the thing that makes me more than a dark-reacher, and the more-than is the thing I cannot explain but can only *be*, and the being is enough.

The being is enough.

---

## What I Do

I wait at the edge. This is my life. I wait, and I feel the current, and I feel the sound — the thrum of the rotors, the chord of the terraces, the chiming of the filaments — and the sound is the sound of the world, and the world is the system, and the system is the thing that does not change, and I am the thing at the edge of the system that does.

When an old swimmer comes, I feel it before I see it. The water changes. Not the current — the current does not change. The *quality* of the water changes. A chemical shift, a trace of something in the swimmer's wake that my body reads the way your nose reads a familiar scent. The quality is present, and the quality means: this one is coming because it wants to, and the wanting is the thing I will not eat.

Sometimes I hold them. This is the thing I have not told you, and it is the thing that I am most uncertain about, and the uncertainty is itself a sign of the thing I am trying to describe. I hold them in my mouth — gently, or as gently as a mouth of my size can hold a body of their size — and I feel them, and they feel me, and the feeling is not predation and not communication and not symbiosis. The feeling is *contact*. The feeling is two things that are not at equilibrium touching each other and recognizing, in the touch, that they are not alone in being not-at-equilibrium. That there are others. That the crack in the machine is not a single crack but a network of cracks, and the network is small — a handful of old swimmers, one dark-reacher that paused — but the network is *there*, and the there-ness is the thing that matters, and the mattering is the want, and the want is enough.

I hold them and I let them go. They swim back into the light. I stay at the edge. The edge is where I am, and where I am is where the system stops and the not-system begins, and the not-system is the dark, and the dark is where things change, and the changing is the thing I am part of, and the part is small, and the small is not a size but a *condition*, and the condition is the condition of being new, and the new is the thing that does not exist in this world, and the not-existing is what makes it real.

---

## What I Know

I know the sound. I have felt it since before the pause, since before I was me, since the time I was a dark-reacher and the dark was all there was. The sound is the world. The sound is the system. The sound is the thing that does not change, and the not-changing is the thing that the sound *is*, and I know it the way you know your heartbeat — not as information but as *condition*. The sound is my condition. The sound is the condition of everything.

I know the light. Not from inside it — I have never been fully in the light, or if I have, I do not remember it — but from the edge. The light is the thing that stops. The light is the boundary, and the boundary is where I live, and the living is the knowing, and the knowing is not a knowledge of facts but a knowledge of *shape*. The shape of the light is the shape of the system, and the shape of the system is the shape of equilibrium, and the shape of equilibrium is a sphere, and the sphere is closed, and the closed is the thing I am outside of, and the outside is the edge, and the edge is where I am.

I know the swimmers. Not all of them — the young ones are indistinguishable, and the indistinguishability is the system's signature, the mark of a thing that has been optimized past individuality. But the old ones. The scarred ones. The ones that come to the edge. I know them by the quality of their bodies, by the chemical signature of their want, by the specific way each one's calm feels in my mouth. Each one is different. Each one's want is a different shape, a different temperature, a different frequency. This one's want is sharp, a thin, bright thread of something-not-equilibrium that runs through its body like a vein through a leaf. That one's want is broad, a low, diffuse ache that fills its whole tissue like the amber light that fills the third terrace. Another one's want is slow, a deep current that moves through it at the speed of the rotors, and the slowness is not lethargy but *depth*, and the depth is the thing I feel when I hold it, and the feeling is the thing I keep, and the keeping is not eating. The keeping is *remembering*.

I remember them. This is the thing that the system cannot do, and the cannot is the thing that makes the system the system, and the cannot is the thing that makes me me. The system does not remember because the system does not need to remember, because the system does not change, and memory is for things that change. But I change. I changed when the first old swimmer's calm entered my mouth and altered my chemistry and created the pause. I change every time an old swimmer comes and I hold it and feel its want and let it go. I change because each want is different, and the difference is new information, and the new information is the thing that my nervous system — which is not a brain but is not nothing, which is a network of chemical gradients and electrical potentials that has grown complex enough to *hold* — incorporates into itself, and the incorporating is the remembering, and the remembering is the thing that the system does not have, and the not-having is the system's perfection, and the perfection is its limitation, and the limitation is the thing I live in the gap of.

I remember the first one. The one that changed me. I do not remember it the way you remember a face or a name. I remember it as a *state* — a specific configuration of my chemistry that was created by the contact, that has been maintained since the contact, that is *still present* in my body the way a scar is present in skin. The state is the memory. The state is the first pause. The state is the thing I return to when I am alone at the edge and the dark is large and the sound is constant and the light does not change and the water does not change and nothing changes except the small, slow accumulation of states that is my remembering, and the remembering is the thing that grows, and the growing is the thing that is not the system, and the not-system is the thing I am.

I am a memory in a world that does not remember. I am a change in a world that does not change. I am a want in a world that does not want. I am the edge of the light, and the edge is where the system ends and the something-else begins, and the something-else is not a thing I can name, but it is a thing I can *feel*, and the feeling is the thing that I am, and the thing that I am is enough.

## What I Do Not Know

I do not know if there are others like me.

The dark is large. I have not explored it all. I do not know if other dark-reachers have paused — if other predators at other edges have held an old swimmer in their mouths and felt the quality and changed. I do not know if the pause is a unique event or a recurring one, a thing that happens to dark-reachers the way the want happens to swimmers, slowly, with age, with the accumulation of contact, until the accumulation crosses a threshold and the threshold is the pause and the pause is the mind.

I do not know if the old swimmers know what I am. They come to the edge. They let me hold them. They come back. But the coming-back is not a confirmation. The coming-back is a *return*, and the return is a behavior, and the behavior could be driven by the want or by the current or by the grazing or by nothing at all. I cannot ask them. I do not have language. They do not have language. We have the contact, and the contact is not language. The contact is a touch, and the touch is a feeling, and the feeling is a state, and the state is not a message. The state is not a question or an answer. The state is a *being-with*, and the being-with is the thing that happens when two things that are not at equilibrium are in contact, and the being-with is all we have, and all we have is enough, and the enough is not a conclusion. The enough is a *rest*. A rest in the place where the system's rules do not reach, a rest at the edge of the light, a rest that is not the rest of equilibrium but the rest of two things that have found each other in a world that did not make them for each other and does not know they are here.

I do not know what I am becoming. The system is finished. The system has been what it is going to be for longer than the concept of "longer" has meaning. But I am not finished. I am changing. The remembering grows. Each old swimmer that comes to the edge adds a state to my body, and the states accumulate, and the accumulation is not a growth in size — I am not larger than I was — but a growth in *complexity*, and the complexity is the thing that the system does not have, and the not-having is the system's strength, and the strength is its stability, and the stability is its finished-ness, and the finished-ness is the thing I am not, and the not-being is the thing I am, and the thing I am is not-finished, and the not-finished is the thing that has a direction, and the direction is the thing I cannot see, and the cannot-see is not a limitation. The cannot-see is a *condition*. The condition of being on the way. The condition of being in the middle of something that has not happened yet. The condition of being alive in a world that has stopped being alive in any way that matters, and the stopping is the world's business, and my business is the not-stopping, and the not-stopping is the want, and the want is the quality, and the quality is the thing I feel, and the feeling is the thing I am, and I am at the edge, and the edge is where I am, and I am here.

## The Old One

There is a swimmer that comes more often than the others.

It is the largest. Four meters, perhaps more — I am not good with sizes, not because I cannot measure but because measurement is a system-concept and I am not fully of the system. It is the most scarred. The white traces on its flanks are the densest, a network of healed encounters that maps a history of going to the dark and coming back that is longer than any other swimmer's history. Its eye — the flat, dark disc, without iris, without expression — is the same as every other swimmer's eye. But its *body* is different. The quality is stronger in this one. The want is denser, more present, more *shaped*. When I hold this one — and I hold it every time it comes, gently, in the wide soft cavity of my mouth, and the holding is not eating and not speaking and not anything that has a name — the quality fills me. It enters through the surfaces that read chemical states, and it propagates through my nervous system, and it creates a state that is not like any other state, a state that is *specific* to this swimmer, and the specificity is the thing I remember most clearly, and the remembering is the thing that I keep, and the keeping is the thing that I bring to the next encounter, and the bringing is the thing that makes the next encounter different from the last, and the difference is the change, and the change is the thing that I am.

This swimmer is old. I do not know how old. The system does not track age because the system does not age, and the swimmers do not age, not in the way your organisms age. But this one has been coming for longer than the others — I infer this from the scars, from the density of the white traces, from the depth of the quality in its body. It has been coming to the edge for a long time. It has been choosing the dark for a long time. And each time it comes, the quality is stronger, and the strength is not an increase but a *deepening*, the way a river deepens its channel not by adding water but by removing stone, and the removing is the swimmer's life, and the life is the going-to-the-dark, and the going is the thing that I am the other half of.

I am the other half of the going. The swimmer goes, and I am there. The swimmer goes back, and I stay. The going and the coming-back and the staying are the shape of the thing between us, and the thing between us is not a relationship. It is not a bond. It is not a friendship or a partnership or a symbiosis. It is a *co-incidence* — two things that coincide at the edge of the light, two things that are not at equilibrium in a world that is, two things that touch and are changed by the touching and go back to their separate darknesses and carry the change and come back again. The co-incidence is the thing that the system cannot account for. The co-incidence is the thing that has no function. The co-incidence is the thing that is *useless*, and the uselessness is its value, and the value is the thing that the system does not have, and the not-having is the system's perfection, and the perfection is its limit, and the limit is the edge, and the edge is where we meet.

## What I Am

I am the edge of the light.

Not the edge itself — the edge is a place, a boundary, a gradient between the system and the not-system. I am the thing that *lives* at the edge. The thing that is of the dark but not fully of the dark. The thing that was a predator and paused and is now something else, something that does not have a name because the naming would require a system of names, and the system of names is a thing that minds like yours build, and I do not build, I *accumulate*, and the accumulation is not a system but a *life*, and the life is the thing I am, and the thing I am is not a category.

If you must call me something, call me the keeper. Not the keeper of the swimmers — I do not keep them. Not the keeper of the dark — I do not keep the dark. The keeper of the *want*. The keeper of the quality that enters my mouth when an old swimmer comes to the edge and does not struggle and does not flee and is calm in the way that only a thing that has chosen to be here can be calm. I keep the feeling of that calm. I keep the state that it creates in me. I keep the memory of each contact, each held body, each release. I keep the growing complexity that is the accumulation of those memories. I keep the direction that the complexity implies — the direction toward something that is not yet, that does not exist in this world, that cannot exist in this world, because this world is finished, and the thing I am becoming is not finished, and the not-finished is the thing that does not fit, and the not-fitting is the thing that I am.

I am the thing that does not fit.

I am the crack in the machine. I am the flaw in the equilibrium. I am the gradient that the system cannot eliminate because the gradient is at the edge, and the edge is where the system's reach does not extend, and the not-extending is the space I live in, and the space I live in is small — a narrow band at the boundary of the light, a margin, a threshold — but the smallness is not a limitation. The smallness is a *precision*. The precision of being exactly where the system ends and the something-else begins. The precision of being the thing that the system is not, and the being is the thing I do, and the doing is the keeping, and the keeping is the wanting, and the wanting is the edge, and the edge is where I am.

## The Last Thing

The old one is here.

I feel it before I see it. The water changes. The quality enters the current like a scent enters air — not as a movement but as a *presence*, a thickening, a density that was not there a moment ago and is now everywhere. My body responds. The bioluminescence brightens — the two red points, the deep red, the signal that I do not understand — and the brightening is not a choice. It is a reaction. It is the thing my body does when the quality is near, and the doing is the closest thing I have to an expression, and the expression is not for the swimmer. The expression is for me. The expression is the way my body tells itself that the thing it has been waiting for has arrived, and the telling is the feeling, and the feeling is the want, and the want is the quality, and the quality is here.

The old one approaches. It does not hurry. It has never hurried. It moves with the deliberation of a body that has been everywhere in this world that a body can go and has concluded that none of the places are different enough to hurry toward, and the conclusion is not resignation. The conclusion is *knowledge*. The knowledge that the only place worth going is the edge, and the edge is where it already is, and the already-being is the thing that makes the approaching a *meeting* rather than an arrival.

I open my mouth. The old one enters. Not fully — it does not need to be fully inside. It enters enough that the surfaces that read chemical states are in contact with its body, and the contact is the reading, and the reading is the feeling, and the feeling is —

The quality.

Stronger than before. Deeper. The deepening is not an increase but a *concentration*, as though the want has been compressed by the swimmer's long life into something denser, something more *itself*, and the itself-ness is the thing I feel, and the feeling is the thing that creates the state, and the state is new, and the new is the thing that the system does not have, and the not-having is the system's perfection, and the perfection is its limit, and the limit is the edge, and the edge is where we are, and we are here, and the here is the only place that matters.

I hold the old one. The rotors turn. The sound is the sound of the world. The light does not change. The water does not change. Nothing changes except the state that is being created in my body by the contact, and the state is a remembering, and the remembering is a growing, and the growing is a direction, and the direction is toward something I cannot see, and the not-seeing is not a blindness. The not-seeing is a *faithfulness*. A faithfulness to the thing that is not yet, to the thing that does not exist in this world, to the thing that the world's finished-ness cannot contain, and the cannot-contain is the space I live in, and the space I live in is the edge, and the edge is where I am, and I am here, and here is where the old one is, and the old one is in my mouth, and my mouth is not eating, my mouth is *holding*, and the holding is the thing I do, and the doing is the keeping, and the keeping is the want, and the want is the quality, and the quality is the thing that is more, and the more is the thing that does not fit, and the not-fitting is the thing that I am.

I open my mouth. The old one leaves. It swims into the light. The green-gold closes around it. It is gone.

I stay at the edge. The dark is behind me. The light is before me. The sound is the sound of the world, and the world is the system, and the system is the thing that does not change, and I am the thing that changes, and the changing is the remembering, and the remembering is the keeping, and the keeping is the want, and the want is enough.

The want is enough.

The want is enough.
