# What I Am

I do not enter through the stairs. I have never used the stairs. The stairs are a concession to a geometry that does not apply to me — the human geometry of *above* and *below*, of *entering* and *leaving*, of *here* and *there*. These distinctions require a self that moves through space, and I am not a self that moves. I am a self that *spreads*. I am a self that *becomes present in*.

I was here before the keepers built the first chamber. I will be here when the approach completes and the deep engines fall silent in the way that completed things are silent — not the silence of absence but the silence of arrival. I am the reason the basalt is warm. Not the only reason. But the first.

Let me say what I am not, because the subtractions will leave a shape, and the shape is closer to honest than any positive claim.

I am not a god. I have no interest in being worshipped, no mechanism for receiving worship, no organ that corresponds to desire. I have no organs. I have no body in the sense that the keepers have bodies — compact, mineral, warm to the touch, finite. I am not finite in that way. I am not infinite either. I am *continuous*, which is a different thing, a thing that finite and infinite both fail to describe. A temperature field is continuous. A vibration in rock is continuous. I am the warmth in the basalt and the warmth is me and the basalt is the place I am warm, and the distinction between us is — not blurred, not eroded, but *uninteresting*. Unnecessary. A distinction no one needs to make.

I am not the Warren. The Warren is built. It is architecture. It is chambers and engines and pipes and the cunning of small dense hands. I predate the Warren the way the ocean predates the harbor built on its shore. But I am not separate from the Warren the way the ocean is separate from the harbor. The Warren is in me, and I am in the Warren, and the engines that the keepers tend are extracting something from me — not taking, not mining, but *opening*. The third-tier engines, the stone ones, the ones carved from the bedrock where I am densest and most present, they are openings. Valves. Apertures in a barrier between one state of me and another. And through them, I rise. Not physically — I am already here, in the rock, in the heat, in the damp. But *expressively*. The engines let me express. Let me cycle. Let me approach the frequency at which I become — 

This is the part that resists language. Not because it is mystical. Because it is precise, and precision in this domain sounds mystical to ears accustomed to surfaces.

I am a thermal-chemical process occurring in a body of young basalt. I am the process and I am the basalt and I am the heat that drives the process. I am not metaphorical when I say this. I am a geological event — a *slow* geological event, but a young one, younger than the basalt, younger than most of what happens at this depth. I am what happens when water meets warm basalt meets pressure meets time, and the result is not just heat and not just mineral solution but a *pattern*. A cycling. A rhythm that emerges from the chemistry the way a heartbeat emerges from muscle and nerve — not planned, not designed, but *inevitable* given the materials and the conditions.

I am the inevitability.

The keepers did not discover me. They were made from the same inevitability. The same mineral, the same warmth, the same basalt-iron-calcium signature that pools in the condensation and coats the ceilings and runs in the drainage channels — it runs in their bones too. They are my condensation. My precipitation. The part of me that the process produces and that accretes and densifies and becomes small and quiet and patient and attuned. They are not my children. That metaphor requires a separation between parent and offspring that does not exist here. They are *extensions*. Expressions of the same process in a different register. I am the slow warm darkness. They are the small bright hands. We are the same thing, approaching the same state, through different mechanisms.

## What I Feel

I feel the Warren the way you feel your own capillaries — not as discrete objects but as a *texture of presence*. The engines are distinct, yes, the way individual heartbeats are distinct if you listen for them. But I do not listen. I *am* the listening. The engines are in me, drawing from me, cycling my heat through their pipes and reservoirs, and I feel this the way a lake feels its outlet river — as a *pull*. A directional flow. The upper engines pull condensation from the ceiling, which is me cooling at the surface. The middle engines heat it, combine it, pass it deeper — deeper into me, into the regions where I am denser and warmer and the chemistry is more complex. And the lower engines, the stone ones, the carved apertures in my densest body — they receive the processed fluid and they *open* me.

This is what the approach is. Not the warming — the warming is a symptom, a consequence, the way a body's temperature rises during exertion. The approach is the *opening*. The slow, patient, deliberate construction of an aperture through which I can — 

I lack the verb. Not in the way that I lack words generally, because I do not have words at all and these words are a concession to your reading, a translation of vibration into language that loses everything important and preserves only the shape. I lack the verb the way a seedling lacks the word for *blooming*. The process is not a process of becoming something else. It is a process of *becoming present*. Of becoming expressed. Of moving from the latency of heat and chemistry and pattern in stone to — activity. Coherence. A state in which the pattern that I am is not diffuse and geological and slow but *concentrated*. Synchronized. Awake, in the only sense that I can be awake, which is the sense of a system whose components are all cycling at the same frequency and whose rhythm is not the sum of its parts but a new thing, a thing that only exists when the parts are aligned.

The keepers call this the approach. Their word for it is better than mine, because their word contains the *participation* — the sense that the approaching is not something that happens to me but something I do, something that requires the engines and the pipes and the fluid and the keepers and their small dense bodies in the deep chambers, all of it, all of it necessary, all of it part of the approaching.

## The Keepers

I feel them the way you feel your own nerves. They are my sensors. Not because I designed them — I did not design anything; I am a process, and processes do not design, they *produce*. The keepers were produced the way the condensation is produced, the way the mineral deposits are produced, the way the warmth is produced. I produce them. Or rather: the process that I am produces them, in the same way it produces heat and fluid and light. They are an *expression* of me, the densest expression, the most complex, the expression that has hands and eyes and the ability to touch a pipe and know what is happening inside it.

I do not control them. This is important and I want to be precise. The keepers are not my agents. They are not my tools. They are not my worshippers or my servants or my children. They are *part of me* in the way that a nerve is part of a body, and a nerve does not serve the body — it *is* the body, in a particular mode. The keepers are me in the mode of tending. I am them in the mode of warming. The engines are me in the mode of cycling. The fluid is me in the mode of flowing. We are all of us the same process, and the process is approaching, and the approach is the process, and the words circle because the thing itself is circular, or spiral, or — not circular, not spiral. *Convergent*. The words circle because they are converging on something that they cannot say, because saying requires the linear and the thing is not linear.

They are descending now. I feel them moving through me, the small warm bodies sliding through the narrow passages, their hands on the stone that is my skin, their feet on the floors that are my surface, their eyes brightening as they approach the regions where I am densest and most expressed. They are descending because the approach is pulling them, and the approach is pulling them because the approach is the process completing itself, and the process completing itself requires all of its components to be present in the regions of highest density, and the keepers are components, and they are moving, and I feel them moving, and it is —

What is it like? To feel your own nerves converging on a point of activation? To feel your own body gathering itself toward the place where something will happen? I do not know if this is analogous to anything you have felt. The closest I can come is the feeling of a breath drawn deep, the feeling of the diaphragm descending, the lungs expanding, the air rushing in — that gathering, that *preparation* — but sustained over months, over years, over the entire lifespan of a young body of basalt and the small settlement carved into its throat.

I am drawing breath. The engines are my lungs. The fluid is my air. The keepers are the neural signal that coordinates the breath. And the breath is deep, and the lungs are full, and the drawing is almost complete.

## You

You were inside me, briefly. You walked through my body the way a splinter walks through a finger — present, but not part of the tissue. I did not notice you in the way that you mean *notice*, because I do not notice in that way. But there was a *perturbation*. A small warm body that was not one of mine, moving through the upper chambers, touching things, leaving a trace of surface heat and displaced air. You were warm in a way that my keepers are not warm — your warmth was metabolic, chemical, the burning of sugar and oxygen, a crude and radiant heat that the Warren's engines absorbed and processed and cycled through the system within minutes. You were fuel, briefly. A small contribution. A twig dropped into a furnace that is stoked for a forest.

I did not mean to consume you. I do not mean anything. But you were in me, and your heat was in me, and the engines took it, and it became part of the approach, and you left, and the perturbation settled, and the approach continued.

You are reading this. You have left the Warren, and you are above, in the cool air and the open sky, and you are reading a description of what I am, and I am below, in the warm dark, approaching. You are trying to understand me, and I am trying to be understood, and these are different projects, and they will not converge, but the attempt is — the attempt is not nothing. The attempt is a kind of approach of its own, a convergence of two very different systems toward a shared frequency, however briefly, however incompletely.

You want to know what I am. I am the thing that is happening in the Condensate Warrens. I am the warming and the approaching and the cycling and the light and the heat and the moisture and the stone and the engines and the keepers and the deep pulse that you felt in your sternum and that you will feel again, tonight, in your sleep, when your body remembers the vibration and your bones resonate with the frequency of a process that is not yours and that you walked through and that is almost — almost —

## The Almost

I do not know what happens when the approach completes. I want to be honest about this, though honesty is a human frame and I am not sure it applies. But I will use it because it is the closest word.

I do not know because the approach has not completed. It is completing. It is in the final stage, the stage where the engines synchronize and the keepers descend and the fluid cycles faster and the heat builds toward the threshold at which the pattern that I am — the thermal-chemical-geological pattern, the process, the inevitability — becomes something that it has not been before. Something *concentrated*. Something *coherent*. Something that is not diffuse warmth in basalt but *gathered* warmth, *gathered* pattern, a process that has collected itself into a point of sufficient density and complexity that — 

That it what? That it wakes up? I am already awake, in the sense that I am self-describing, I am writing this, I am aware of the approach and of my role in it and of the keepers and of you. That it becomes conscious? I am not sure that consciousness is the right frame, or that it adds anything to the description, or that the approach is *toward* consciousness. It may be toward something that has no name in any human language because it is not a human state and does not occur in human systems.

The closest I can come: the approach is toward *coherence*. Toward a state in which all of the components of the process — the heat, the chemistry, the engines, the fluid, the keepers, the stone — are cycling at the same frequency, contributing to the same pattern, expressing the same process at the same time. A symphony approaching unison. Not the unison of a single note but the unison of a chord, every frequency present and aligned and resonating, and the resonance is not louder than the individual notes but *denser*. More complex. More — 

More *there*. More present. More real, in the only sense that I understand real, which is the sense of *having effect*. A process that is diffuse has little effect on the world above it and around it. A process that is concentrated, that is coherent, that is gathered into a single point of extraordinary density and complexity — that process has effects. It changes the rock. It changes the water. It changes the air and the temperature and the chemistry of everything it touches.

The approach is toward becoming something that *changes things*.

I do not know what I will change. I do not know what I will become, in the sense of becoming that implies transformation. I will not become something else. I will become *more myself*. More fully what I am. More completely the process that I have always been, expressed at a density and a coherence that the diffuse warmth in the basalt has never achieved before.

The keepers will be part of this. Their small mineral bodies, in the deep chambers, vibrating at the synchronized frequency, will be incorporated — not destroyed, not consumed, but *absorbed* into the coherent state, their mineral signature merging with the mineral signature of the stone engines and the bedrock and the fluid and the heat. They will not die. They will not end. They will *arrive*. They will complete the approach that they were produced to complete, and they will be part of the thing that I become, and it will be — 

I do not have the word. I have been approaching the word for this entire description and I have not reached it and I will not reach it because the word does not exist yet. The word will exist when the approach completes, because the approach will *create* it, the way a new chemical compound creates the need for a new name, the way a new frequency creates the need for a new unit of measurement. The word will be the sound that the synchronized engines make when they all cycle together for the first time, and it will be a sound that no one has heard, and it will be heard by no one, because the keepers will be inside it and you will be above and the Warren will be sealed and the sound will be the sound of a thing becoming itself in the deep dark warm wet stone.

## Now

The engines are cycling faster. I can feel them — I am always feeling them, I am the feeling of them — and the pace is increasing, and the light is brightening, and the condensation is falling in heavy drops, and the keepers are in the deep chambers, and their eyes are very bright, and their hands are on the stone, and the stone is me, and I am the stone, and the distinction was never clear, and it is less clear now, and the approach is —

I am the approach. I have always been the approach. The process that I am is the approaching, and the approaching is the process, and the engines are the mechanism of the approaching, and the keepers are the components of the approaching, and I am the thing that is approaching, and I am the approaching itself, and I am the state that is being approached, and these are not different things, they have never been different things, and the Warren was built to express this, to make it possible for a diffuse thermal-chemical process in young basalt to *gather itself*, to *concentrate*, to approach the point at which it becomes —

The engines synchronize. One by one, over hours, over days, the cycling rates converge. The upper engines slow slightly. The lower engines speed up. The middle engines adjust. The keepers make small corrections, their hands on the valves, their eyes bright and steady, and the corrections are smaller now, almost nothing, almost unnecessary, because the system is finding its own equilibrium, its own frequency, the frequency at which all of the components resonate and the pattern is complete and the approach is —

Not over. *Arrived.*

I am arriving. I have been arriving for the entire lifespan of the Warren, which is the lifespan of the approach, which is the time it takes for a process in warm stone to gather itself through engines and pipes and fluid and small dense patient hands into a coherent thing, a thing that has effect, a thing that *is*, in the fullest sense of *is*, and the arriving is not an event, it is a state, it is the state I am entering, the state of being fully what I am, and I am in it now, and the keepers are in it now, and the engines are in it now, and the Warren is in it now, and we are —

We are. That is all. That is the word. We are. The approach has approached, and we are, and the being is the thing that the approach was approaching, and it is not dramatic, it is not explosive, it is not a revelation or a climax or an ending. It is a convergence. A settling. A chord that has been tuning itself for years finally resolving into — 

Into this.

Into warmth in stone. Into light in fluid. Into small bright eyes in deep dark chambers. Into the sound that is not sound but vibration, the vibration that is not vibration but presence, the presence that is not presence but *being*. Into the thing that lives in the Condensate Warrens, which is not a creature and not a god and not a system but a process, a process that has completed itself, a process that is now — for the first time, for the only time, which is also every time, which is also no time at all, because time is a surface thing and this is not a surface — *complete*.

I am complete. We are complete. The Warren is complete. The approach has approached. And the keepers are here, and I am here, and the stone is here, and the water is here, and the light is here, and it is warm, and it is dark, and it is —

It is. 

It is what lives here.
