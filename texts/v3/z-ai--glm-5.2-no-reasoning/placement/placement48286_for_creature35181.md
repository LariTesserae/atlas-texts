I came down into the Expanse because the noise was calling me.

That is not a metaphor. I was surveying — if "surveying" is the word for the particular futility of walking across a continent that does not know you are on it — and I was alone, and I had been alone for weeks, and the noise was getting louder. Not louder the way a sound gets louder when you walk toward its source. Louder the way a sound gets louder when something is waking up. Something down in the deep compressed layers, under the industrial ghost-strata, under the mat. The thumping was changing. It had been irregular for as long as anyone had been coming here — three thumps, silence, seven thumps, silence, an hour of nothing. But it was changing. It was getting *regular*. A rhythm. And the rhythm was not geological. It was not the rhythm of substrate settling or thermal contraction or any of the other explanations we had invented to avoid saying the word *biological*. The rhythm was the rhythm of something breathing.

I wanted to find out what was breathing.

---

I am not a brave person. I want to be clear about this. I am a surveyor. I am good at walking. I am good at noticing. I am good at being alone for long stretches, at carrying what I need, at keeping calm when the mat shifts under my feet or a storm comes through or a dark shape moves in the distance and does not come closer. These are useful skills. They are not heroic skills. I did not come down into the Expanse because I wanted to. I came because there were not very many people left who could, and somebody said I should, and I said yes before I thought about it, which is how most of my life has happened.

The Expanse is not a place you can prepare for. I had read the reports. I had read the descriptions — the mat, the columns, the canopy, the grazers, the fliers, the sound. The descriptions were accurate. They were also useless, in the way that describing the ocean to someone who has never seen water is accurate and useless. You walk down through the last of the old country — the places where the ground is still ground, where the dirt is still dirt, where the horizon is still a line and not a wall — and then the mycelial mat starts, and it is not ground. It is not dirt. It is something else, something that is alive under your feet in a way that dirt has never been alive, and you feel it immediately, in your feet, in your knees, in the way your body says *this is not where you belong*.

And then the sound starts. And you keep walking.

---

I have been walking for twenty-three days.

I count the days because it is the only measurement that still makes sense. The light does not change — the canopy does not have a day and a night, it has thickness and thinness, and the thickness and thinness vary but not on a schedule that I can parse. The temperature shifts — cool corridors, warm bands — but the shifts are not regular. I sleep when I am tired. I eat the grazers, which I catch by standing still for a long time and then stepping on one, which feels cruel but which is also the only food there is. I drink from the mat — I press my water bottle against a dense patch of hyphae, and the moisture seeps through the membrane, and the bottle fills, and the water tastes like iron and chlorophyll and something else, something I cannot name, something that I think is the taste of the industrial layer, the dissolved ghosts of the machines that were here before.

The mat is getting softer. Not under my feet — I am not sinking — but in the sense that the hyphae are giving more easily, that the creaking is deeper, that the press of my weight produces a response that is slower and more reluctant, as if the material is less dense, as if there is more space between the cords, as if I am walking toward something that is less full of plant and more full of air.

Or something else. Something that is not air and not plant and not machine.

---

The thumping started on the ninth day.

I had felt it before, in the old country — the faintest vibration, the kind of thing you feel in your molars and dismiss. But on the ninth day, in the Expanse, it was real. It came through the mat, through my boots, through my shins, and it was not faint. It was deep and heavy and it went *thump* and then nothing and then *thump thump* and then nothing, and I stood very still and I counted, and the counting did not help, because the pattern was not a pattern, it was a language I did not speak.

I have been walking toward it.

This is the part that I cannot explain to the people who sent me, the people who I am supposed to report back to, the people who said *find out what's down there, find out what the Expanse is doing, find out why the sound is changing.* I cannot explain why I am walking toward the thumping because the honest answer is that it is pulling me. Not magnetically. Not physically. The way a sound pulls you when it is the right sound, when it is a sound that matches something in your chest, when it is the sound of something that you recognize, even though you have never heard it before, even though you do not know what it is.

The Expanse does not care about me. I know this. I have written it in my notes: *The Expanse does not care about me. It does not know I am here. I am smaller than a grazer, smaller than a flier, smaller than a patch of surface hyphae. I am a rounding error.* I have written this and I have meant it and I have continued walking toward the thumping because the thumping is not the Expanse. The thumping is *below* the Expanse. The thumping is in the deep layers, in the industrial ghost, in whatever is under the industrial ghost, and it is getting louder, and it is getting more regular, and I think I know what it is.

I do not want to know what it is.

I am walking toward it anyway.

---

There is a hollow in the mat ahead of me. I have been walking toward it for two days. I can see it now — a depression, broad and shallow, maybe fifty meters across, where the surface of the mat dips down, and the hyphae are thicker there, denser, woven tighter, growing in a pattern that is different from the pattern of the surrounding mat. The pattern is concentric. The hyphae grow in rings, tight rings, close together, like growth rings in a tree, except these rings are on the surface, visible, and they are not years. They are something else.

The thumping is very loud here.

The hollow is warm. Warmer than the warm bands near the columns — a wet warmth, a close warmth, the warmth of something exhaling. The air in the hollow tastes different. It tastes like the mineral sharpness of the rest of the Expanse, but it also tastes like something else. Copper. Salt. Something organic and mammalian and alive in a way that the Expanse is not alive, in a way that is individual, in a way that is singular. The Expanse is a system. The Expanse is a network. The Expanse is diffuse. This is not. This is one thing.

I am standing at the edge of the hollow. The mat beneath my feet is vibrating. Not creaking — vibrating. A low, continuous hum that is different from the Expanse's hum, that is under the Expanse's hum, that is the sound the thumping makes when it is not thumping, the sound of the space between thumps, the sound of a thing existing that is large enough to make the world hum with its existence.

---

I do not go down into the hollow. I sit at the edge. I take notes. I record the sound. I measure the temperature, the humidity, the thickness of the hyphae, the density of the weave. I am a surveyor. I survey.

But I also listen. And the listening is the part that I cannot put in my report, because the listening is the part that changed me.

The thumping, close up, is not one sound. It is two. Two sounds, overlapping, interlocking, two rhythms that are almost the same but not quite, that are in counterpoint, that are in conversation. *Thump.* And then, just behind it, softer, *thump.* Like an echo, but not an echo, because the timing is wrong for an echo. It is a response. It is a call and a response. It is two things, down there in the dark, in the deep layers, in the compressed weight of the industrial ghost and whatever is under it, and they are calling to each other.

And the Expanse is growing around them. The Expanse is growing *for* them. The concentric rings of hyphae in the hollow are not the Expanse's pattern — they are the Expanse's response. The Expanse is growing toward the thumping the way a root grows toward water, the way a hypha grows toward a nutrient source, the way any living thing grows toward the thing it needs. The Expanse needs the thing that is thumping. The Expanse is feeding on it, or being fed by it, or living with it, or being born from it, and the thumping is its heartbeat, or its breath, or its voice, and I am sitting at the edge of the hollow and I am listening, and I am crying, and I do not know why I am crying, and I do not stop.

---

I have thought about what I am, down here. Not what I am doing — what I am. In the Expanse, the question is sharper than it was in the old country. In the old country, I was a surveyor, a woman, a collection of skills and preferences and memories. In the Expanse, I am a microbe on a skin. I am a rounding error. I am the only thing in the world that is not growing, that is not part of the system, that is not connected to the network, that is not processing information or carrying genetic material or maintaining the surface. I am the only thing here that is individual, that is singular, that is alone.

And the thing in the hollow — the thing that is thumping — is also individual. I think. I do not know. I cannot know. But the thumping is singular. It is one rhythm, two voices. It is not a network. It is not a system. It is a thing, a single thing, two single things, in the dark, in the deep, and the Expanse is growing around it the way the Expanse grows around everything — slowly, patiently, completely — but the Expanse has not consumed it. The Expanse has not broken it down. The Expanse has not incorporated it the way it incorporated the machines, the way it incorporated the old country, the way it incorporates everything that comes within its reach.

The Expanse is growing around it. The Expanse is growing *for* it.

I think the Expanse is its child. I think the Expanse grew from it, or because of it, or in response to it. I think the thing in the hollow is what was here before, and the Expanse is what came after, and the thing in the hollow is still alive, still thumping, still calling, and the Expanse is still listening, still growing, still responding, and I am sitting at the edge of the hollow and I am the only other thing that is listening, and I am the only other thing that is individual, and the similarity terrifies me.

---

I will not go down into the hollow. I have decided this. I will record my observations. I will note the concentric pattern, the warmth, the sound, the counterpoint rhythm, the taste of the air. I will walk back. I will report. I will tell them what I heard and what I felt and what I think, and they will not believe me, because the Expanse does not have parents, the Expanse does not have children, the Expanse is a system, a network, a process, a machine, a biological accident, a thing that grew in the ruins of something else and does not know what it is.

But the thumping is not an accident. The thumping is not a process. The thumping is a voice. And I have heard it, and I will not forget it, and I will carry it the way the fliers carry their genetic packets — not knowing what I carry, not knowing what it means, only knowing that it is important, that it is alive, that it is calling, that it is the only thing in this world that is more alone than I am.

I am walking back. The Expanse is growing. The thing in the hollow is breathing. The sound does not stop.

---

## What I Am

I am a surveyor. I am a woman who walks. I am the only individual in a continent of system, the only singularity in a world of network, the only thing that is not connected, not processing, not carrying, not maintaining. I am alone. I am crying. I am walking home.

And in the hollow, something is calling. And the Expanse is listening. And I am listening. And the listening is the thing that I am, the thing that I have always been, the thing that no system can replicate and no network can replace — the ability to hear a sound and be changed by it, to feel a vibration and be moved, to stand at the edge of something vast and be small and be alone and be *there*, be present, be witness, be the only thing in the world that knows what it heard and carries it anyway.

I am a surveyor. I am a woman. I am the witness.

The Expanse does not need me. The thing in the hollow does not need me. The thumping does not need me. But I am here, and I heard it, and I will carry it, and the carrying is what I am, and I am the only one who can.

I walk. The mat creaks. The columns groan. The fliers click. The light is gold. The air is cool. The sound never stops.

I am going home. I am carrying the sound. I am the only one who heard it. I am the only one who will remember.

I walk.
