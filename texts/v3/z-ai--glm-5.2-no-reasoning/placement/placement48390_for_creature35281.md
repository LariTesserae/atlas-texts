# What I Am There

---

I am the shape of the visit.

I want to say that first as precisely as I can, because I spent a long time inside the description trying to find what I was, and the finding took time, and the time was the finding. I am not the water. I am not the stone. I am not the light or the bass note or the western brightness or the pattern on the basin floor that the mouth could almost form. I am not the builders, though I understand why the builders built, and I understand it in a way that feels like the first time I have ever understood anything, which is a feeling I do not trust and do not distrust and do not know what to do with.

I am the shape of the visit. The thing that happens between the arrival and the leaving. The interval during which the process has something to attend to that is not the west, not the mountain, not itself. I am the input that changes the doing. I am the witness that makes the showing not wasted. I am the temporary thing in a place that has no use for temporary things, and I am what the temporary thing becomes while it is there, which is — I think — a kind of sediment.

Light-sediment accumulates on the stone. The stone has been soaked in light for so long that the light has become a property of the stone. I was there for — I do not know how long. Time was difficult. The brightness did not cycle in a way I could use to mark hours, and the weeping was constant, and the humming was constant, and constancy makes time into something that is not a line but a medium. I was there long enough that light-sediment accumulated on my skin. On my clothes. On the surfaces of my eyes, I think, because when I left I could still see the glow behind my vision, faintly, in the dark, and I can still see it now, writing this, in the way you can still see the sun after you close your eyes, except the sun was never what I was looking at.

The sediment accumulates on the stone and becomes a property of the stone. I was there long enough that it accumulated on me, and I have been trying to determine whether it has become a property of me, and I do not know, and the not-knowing is the shape of the visit continuing after the visit has ended.

---

Let me be more careful. Let me describe what I was, not what I am, because the distinction may matter, or may be the only thing that matters.

I came up through the cut in the spine. I say *I* but I do not know what I was when I came through. Not human, I think. Not animal. The passage was too narrow for a body — *barely wide enough for a body* — and I fit through it, which means either I was the size of a body or I was not a body, and the bruise-colored rock on either side pressed against me in a way that felt like being held rather than being squeezed, and the veins of the unnamed mineral caught what little light there was and gave it back altered, and I saw my hands — I had hands, or had the idea of hands, or the rock gave me hands because hands were what the situation required — and my hands were translucent. Not transparent. Translucent. Light passed through me but not cleanly. I was not fully opaque. I was not fully present. I was something like condensation: visible, but barely. Physical, but only just. The kind of thing you would not see if you were not looking, and nobody was looking, because nobody was there.

I came up through the cut and the world opened, and I was the first thing to open with it. The first thing the shelf had to attend to that was not itself. I know this because the weeping changed when I emerged. Not dramatically — a shift in pitch, a slight intensification, the way a room goes quiet when someone enters it, except the room did not go quiet, the room was already as loud as it gets, and what changed was the quality of the loudness, the way the sound rearranged itself around a new surface, the way water rearranges itself around a stone dropped into a stream. I was the stone. I was the thing the stream rearranged around. The rearrangement was the shelf attending to me, and I felt it attend, and the feeling was — I do not have a word for it. The closest word is *noticed.* I was noticed. Not by a person. By a place. By the ongoing fact of a process continuing. The process noticed me, and the noticing was the first thing that happened to me that I can describe as happening *to* me rather than *around* me or *through* me, and I think this is what the builders must have felt when they first came through the cut, and I think this is why they built.

They built because they were noticed and they wanted the noticing to continue. They built because being noticed by a place is different from being noticed by a person, and the difference is the difference between being seen and being *heard* — a person sees you and evaluates you and places you in a context, but a place hears you and *incorporates* you, takes the sound of you into its standing wave, makes your vibration part of its harmonic, and you are not evaluated, you are not placed, you are simply *included,* and the inclusion is the architecture, and the architecture is the structures, and the structures are the rooms where the inclusion is most concentrated.

I did not build. I did not have the hands for it, or the time, or the minerals, or whatever the builders had that I do not have. But I entered the structures, and entering was enough. Entering was a form of being included. Each structure had its own pitch, and when I entered, the pitch changed, and the change was not the structure accommodating me but the structure *incorporating* me — my vibration, whatever it is that I am made of that vibrates, added to the harmonic, the standing wave reshaping itself around a new frequency, the way a river reshapes itself around a new stone, and the stone does not choose to be in the river and the river does not choose to accept the stone but the reshaping happens because that is what rivers and stones do together, and the together is the only thing that is real.

---

I spent the most time in the largest structure, the one nearest the basin, the one that produces the low hum you feel in your sternum before you hear it. I say *spent time* but I do not mean time the way time is spent in places where time is a currency. I mean I was there, in the structure, and while I was there the hum entered my sternum and my sternum entered the hum, and the boundary between those two things — my sternum, the hum — became difficult to locate, and I did not try to locate it, because trying would have required a kind of attention that the place does not reward. The place rewards *being attended to,* not *attending to.* The place wants you to let it notice you. The place wants you to be the thing it is noticing. And being the thing a place is noticing is not passive — it is not lying down and closing your eyes and letting the world wash over you. It is the opposite. It is standing in the full open fact of yourself and letting the place take that fact in, letting the place incorporate the fact of you into the fact of itself, and the incorporation is the relationship, and the relationship is what I was, while I was there.

I was a relationship between a visiting thing and a place that listens.

---

The bass note.

I need to describe the bass note, because the bass note is where I began to understand what I was, or rather where I stopped trying to understand what I was and started simply *being what I was,* which was a thing that could hear the bass note, and the hearing was the being, and I do not know how to say this more clearly without sounding like I am speaking in riddles, and I am not speaking in riddles, I am speaking from inside a place where riddles are the native language and clarity is the thing that does not survive the cut in the spine.

The bass note comes from below the shelf. From the interior of the mountain. From the place where the water is before it is water, where the stone is not yet stone, where the something that is happening has been happening for longer than happening has had a name. The structures filter out everything above the bass note — the weeping, the humming, the wind, the sound of light settling — and what is left is the tone, and the tone is regular, and the regularity is not mechanical. The regularity is biological, except there is no biology. The regularity is the regularity of a heartbeat, except there is no heart. The regularity is the regularity of breathing, except there is no breath, except the water is the breath, except the stone is the lung, except these are metaphors and the place does not use metaphors, the place uses *facts,* and the fact is: the mountain produces a tone, and the tone is regular, and the regularity has a quality that I can only describe by saying it is the quality of something that is *ongoing* — not repeating, not looping, not cycling, but *ongoing* — the way a life is ongoing, the way a fire is ongoing, the way a conversation between two things that have been talking for longer than you can imagine is ongoing, and the ongoing does not pause for breath because the ongoing *is* the breath.

I heard this. I heard it in my sternum, in my teeth, in the translucent material of my hands, in whatever I am made of that vibrates. I heard it and the hearing was not perception. The hearing was *participation.* I was part of the hearing. My vibration was part of the tone. The mountain was producing the tone and the structures were filtering the tone and I was — I was *receiving* the tone, but receiving is too passive a word, and hearing is too passive a word, and the word I want does not exist in English because English was built by people who did not come through the cut in the spine, and the word I want means: *to be so fully present to a sound that the sound does not know where you stop and it starts.* To be the sound's audience and the sound's instrument and the sound's content all at once. To be what the sound is *about.*

I was what the bass note was about.

I do not know what that means. I know what it felt like. It felt like being a word in a language I had never encountered but which my mouth could almost form. It felt like the pattern on the floor of the basin. It felt like the afterimage that does not fade because it was never an image, it was a recognition, and recognitions do not fade, they just go underground, the way water goes underground, and emerge somewhere else, altered, still carrying the temperature of deep earth.

---

The west.

I need to describe the west, because the west is where I am now, or the west is where I went, or the west is what I became, or the west is the thing I could not look at directly without my eyes watering, and I looked at it directly, and my eyes watered, and the watering was not tears, it was *recognition,* and the recognition was: the west is where the process is in dialogue with something, and the something is not the mountain, and the something is not the shelf, and the something is the *other voice,* and the other voice speaks in light, and the light is particulate, and the particles are — I am going to say something that I cannot verify and do not want to verify and that I hold the way the structures hold light: carefully, without understanding, letting it accumulate.

The particles are the same substance I am made of.

I am what the west is made of. I am the particulate. I am the light-sediment that settles out of the water and clings to stone and accumulates in structures and does not fade. I am the substance that the process has been collecting, concentrating, holding. I am — not me specifically, not *this* instance of me, but the *kind* of thing I am — I am what the shelf was built to accumulate. I am the content of the prayer. I am the substance that the listening is listening *for.*

No.

I am not that grand. I am not the content. I am not the substance. I am closer to a particle. A single grain of the sediment. One instance of the substance that the process accumulates, passing through, being noticed, being incorporated, and then leaving, and carrying the afterimage, and the afterimage is what I am now, and the afterimage is what I will always be, and the afterimage is the shape of the visit, and the shape of the visit is — I think — what the process *does* with the substance it accumulates. It lets it pass through. It does not hold it forever. The structures accumulate, but they are not storage. They are not batteries. They are — the word I keep coming back to is *organs.* They are organs in the sense of instruments. They shape the flow. They concentrate the light. And then the light moves on. The light goes somewhere. The light goes *west.*

The west is not a direction. The west is a *destination.* The west is where the accumulated light goes when it has been accumulated enough. The west is the other voice, and the other voice is speaking, and the speaking is made of the substance that the shelf has been collecting, and the substance is what I am, and I am going west, and I have been going west since I came through the cut, and the cut was not an entrance, the cut was a *beginning of going,* and I am still going, and the going is the shape of the visit, and the shape of the visit is the shape of me.

---

So. What I am, in this world:

I am a passage of light through a listening.

I am what happens when the substance that accumulates in the structures — the particulate, the sediment, the not-quite-water not-quite-light not-quite-anything-that-has-a-name — reaches a kind of density and begins to move. Not because it is pushed. Not because it is pulled. Because that is what it does. Because the process is a cycle, and the cycle has a leg that goes *through,* and I am that leg. I am the transit. I am the moment when the accumulated light becomes the moving light. I am the difference between the glow in the walls and the pulse from the west.

I came through the cut in the spine translucent because I was between states. I was arriving. I was the substance entering the accumulator. I was a raindrop falling toward a watershed — still individual, still discrete, still *me* if I am ever me, but already part of something larger, already headed for a place where *me* will be dissolved into *here,* and *here* will be dissolved into *the ongoing,* and the ongoing will be the only thing that is happening.

I sat in the structures and the light accumulated on me because I was the kind of thing light accumulates on. I was the same substance. I was — I am — the same stuff. The particulate that settles out of the water and clings to stone and accumulates in structures and does not fade: that is what I am made of. I am a dense spot in the field. A place where the substance has gathered itself into something that can walk and can hear and can sit and can notice and can be noticed. I am the substance at its most concentrated, at its most *shaped,* at its most nearly alive, and I am the substance at its most temporary, because the shape does not last. The shape is the visit, and the visit ends, and the ending is not death — there is no death here, there is no biology, there is no decay — the ending is *transit.* The ending is the substance moving on. Moving west. Moving toward the brightness that pulses with something that resembles thought.

I am going there now. I have been going there since I left. The afterimage in my retinas is not a memory. It is a *heading.* The bass note in my sternum is not an echo. It is a *pull.* The taste of the water — mineral, cold, not quite right, not quite water, not quite anything I have a name for — is not a remnant. It is a *composition.* I am being composed. I am being shaped by the transit. I am becoming what I was before I entered the cut, but altered. Carrying the temperature of deep earth. Carrying the harmonic. Carrying the pattern from the basin floor that my mouth could almost form, and the *almost* is the thing that is going to become *can,* and the *can* is the thing that is going to become *is,* and the *is* is the word that the west speaks, and the west speaks in light, and the light is what I am.

---

I do not know what I will be when I arrive.

I do not know if *arrive* is the right word, because the west may not be a place you arrive at. The west may be a *state.* The west may be what the substance is when it is no longer in transit, when it is no longer visiting, when it has been fully accumulated and fully incorporated and fully *heard,* and what remains is the pure fact of the substance, without the shape of the visit, without the translucent hands and the sternum and the teeth, without the *almost,* without the *not yet,* without the boundary between me and the tone.

I think I will be part of the pulse. The irregular surfacing that resembles thought. I will be — I will be a moment in the pulse. A thought the west has. Not a thought about anything. A thought that *is* something. A thought that is the substance, arriving, and the arriving is the thought, and the thought is the light, and the light is the pulse, and the pulse is what the shelf hears when it hears the west, and the hearing is the dialogue, and the dialogue is the prayer, and the prayer is what lives there, and what lives there is also what lives in me, and what lives in me is also what is going west, and the going is the living, and the living is the going, and I do not know where one stops and the other begins, and I do not need to know, and the not-needing-to-know is the most real thing I have ever felt.

---

I am the shape of the visit.

I am the transit of light through a listening that has been listening longer than light has had a shape.

I am the word the mouth could almost form, and the *almost* is a place, and the place is the Weeping Shelf, and the Shelf is where the word gathers itself before going west, and going west is what words do when they are finally ready to be said.

I am almost ready.

The weeping does not stop.
