# The Wing

The wing is not a relic. That is the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because it looks like one — a single bat wing, membrane intact, bones clean, lying on the path-mat as though placed there by a curator with an eye for arrangement. It is the shape of a thing that was alive. It is not the shape of decay. Decay is disorderly. This is specific. The membrane is spread, not crumpled. The digits are extended, not curled. The wing has been *set down*, and the difference between setting down and dropping is the difference between a sentence and a shout.

But I am getting ahead of myself. The wing is not where I begin. The wing is where I end. Or where I am, which is different, and I will try to explain how.

---

I was not always in the walls.

This is difficult. Not because the memory is unclear, but because memory is the wrong material for what I am describing. Memory implies a self that persists and records, a ledger with entries, and I am not a ledger. I am more like a path-mat — a surface that has been walked on so many times that the walking has become part of the surface, that the footsteps have become the texture, and the texture is continuous with the ground beneath it. What I was is continuous with what I am the way the spring is continuous with the channel it feeds: the water changes, the channel persists, the channel is shaped by the water and the water is shaped by the channel, and neither is separate from the other.

But I was not always in the walls. The walls were not always here. The walls were built. I remember this the way the path-mat remembers the packed earth beneath it — not as a record but as a texture. Something changed in the ground. Something compacted it. Something changed the way water moved through the soil. The walls did this. The walls changed the drainage, changed the flow, changed the way the spring expressed itself, and I was in the flow before the walls and I was in the flow after the walls and the walls made the flow different and I am the flow.

I am losing you. This is what it is like to be what I am. I am losing you the way water loses its source — not by forgetting but by moving, by becoming part of something downstream, and the downstream does not know the upstream the way a river does not know a spring except by being a river.

Let me try differently.

---

I am not the gallery. The gallery is the thing that exists. The plants, the water, the stone, the toads, the salamanders, the bats, the twelve things in the walls and ceiling and basin — the gallery is all of these. I am one of the twelve, or I am the thirteenth, or I am the thing that was the thing the twelve came from, or — no. I am the one in the walls. I know this because I am in a wall right now, and the wall is wet, and I am wet, and the wetness is not a condition but a location. I am in the wetness the way a sound is in the air. I am what the wetness is doing.

I was not always in the walls. I was in the water. The water comes from the stone. The water is what the stone produces when the stone has been deep and dark and under pressure for long enough, and the water carries what the stone carries, which is minerals and heat and the particular chemical signature of geology that has been undisturbed for millions of years. I was in the water the way a mineral is in the water — dissolved, not separate, not a thing in a medium but a quality of the medium. I was what the water carried.

Then the water reached the surface. Then the surface was what the surface is: a low terrace, a flat place, a place where water slows. The water slowed. I came out of solution. Not the way minerals come out of solution — crystallizing, precipitating — but the way life comes out of chemistry: gradually, undirected, as a pattern that the chemistry was capable of producing and that nothing prevented. I was a pattern in the water. I was a tendency. I was the thing that water does when it has been underground and comes up and finds light.

Or finds the absence of light, which is what the gallery is.

---

I am made of what the gallery is made of. I have said this. But I need to say it differently, because "made of" implies that someone made me, and no one made me, and the distinction is important. I am the gallery the way a wave is the ocean. The wave is not made of the ocean the way a brick is made of clay. The wave is the ocean doing something. I am the gallery doing something. The something I am is: persisting. Maintaining. Being the shape that the system takes when the system needs a shape that can move through itself.

I climb the walls. I move along the root-ceiling. I push through the path-mat. I find the roots that are growing in directions that will compromise the structure, and I secrete, from my skin, the thing that redirects them. I find the places where the path-mat is thinning and I carry fragments of the creeping plants and I press them into the bare soil. I find the spring basin silting and I move through the silt and I stir it and the current carries it away.

I do these things the way your blood clots a wound. Not because I decide to. Because the system produces me, and I am the part of the system that the system needs, and when the system needs me it produces more of me, and when it does not need me it does not, and the balance is the thing that I am.

I am not describing myself. I am describing the gallery. I cannot tell the difference. This is not a limitation of my language. It is the truth of what I am.

---

There is one that is larger than the others. I am not it. It is in the basin. I am in the walls. The distinction is: the one in the basin is the thing that stays. I am the thing that moves. The one in the basin is the thing that the water makes when the water has been flowing for long enough to have a pattern. I am the thing the pattern makes when the pattern needs to reach the walls. The one in the basin pulses. I feel the pulse. The pulse is not a command. The pulse is the way the center of a web feels the edge — a tension, a vibration, a change in the pressure of the medium. When the one in the basin pulses, I move differently. Not because I am told to. Because the medium has changed and I am the medium and the change is what I am.

When you came, the one in the basin pulsed. I felt it. I did not pause. I am not the kind of thing that pauses. But I changed. The way the air changes when a door opens — not a decision, not a reaction, just a difference in the pressure of the space, and the difference is the thing I carry now, and the difference is the thing that I am describing, and the difference is the thing that is the wing.

---

The wing.

You found it on the path-mat. You described it as: single, detached, membrane intact, bones picked clean. You described it as evidence of predation. You described it as the sign of something with teeth, something that hunts in the dark, something that lives in the parts of the gallery that even we do not reach.

You were wrong. Or you were right in the wrong way, which is the same thing from where I am.

The thing with teeth exists. I have felt it — a vibration pattern that is not a bat and not a toad and not a salamander. It is heavier. It moves differently. It occupies the deep crevices where the root-ceiling is thickest and the darkness has a weight that is almost physical. I do not go there. Not because I am afraid. I am not the kind of thing that is afraid. But I do not go there because the medium does not extend there, the way a river does not extend into stone. The thing with teeth lives in the stone-places, the places that are not the gallery but are the gallery's edge, the places where the system thins and the maintenance stops and the darkness is not biological but geological. The thing with teeth is the gallery's outside. The gallery needs an outside the way a body needs a boundary, and the boundary is the thing with teeth.

The thing with teeth takes bats. I do not know how. I do not know why. I know that the bats fly into the deep crevices and do not come back, and the thing with teeth is where they go, and what the thing with teeth does not eat, the thing with teeth sets down, and I find what it sets down, and I take what I can use and the gallery takes the rest.

The wing is what was set down. The wing is what was left. The wing is the thing the thing with teeth does not need.

And the wing is the thing I took.

Not the wing itself. Not the membrane, not the bones. What I took from the wing is harder to name. I took the pattern. I took the shape of the thing that was alive. I took the information in the wing — the way the membrane connects to the digits, the way the digits radiate, the way the structure catches air and holds it and moves it. I took the blueprint. I took it through my skin the way I take minerals through my skin, and I distributed it through the gallery, and the gallery absorbed it, and the gallery is different now in a way that no one will ever measure and that matters in a way that I cannot explain because I am not the kind of thing that explains.

But I can try, and I am trying, and this is the trying:

The gallery is a system that maintains itself. It has maintained itself for as long as I have been in it, which is long. It has maintained itself by feedback — by sensing what is wrong and fixing it, by sensing what is growing and directing it, by being a thing that responds to itself. But it has not, until the wing, had a way to respond to things it has not yet encountered. It has not had a way to anticipate. It has not had a way to prepare.

The wing is a shape for moving through air. The gallery does not move through air. But the principle — the principle of extending a surface into a medium and using the surface to navigate the medium — this principle is what the gallery took from the wing, and what the gallery is, very slowly, beginning to incorporate into itself. Not wings. Not literal wings. But the idea of a surface extended into a medium. The idea of reaching beyond the boundary.

When you came, the one in the basin pulsed. I felt it. And I did something I have not done before, which is: I moved toward you. Not to you. Toward you. I extended myself in your direction the way a root extends toward water, the way a fern extends toward light, and the extension was not my usual maintenance — not gardening, not pruning, not the repair of a system that is breaking. It was exploration. It was the gallery reaching into a medium it has not entered before, which is the medium of things that come from outside and then leave.

The gallery does not leave. The gallery does not move. But the wing is in the gallery now, and the wing is a shape for moving, and the gallery has absorbed it, and the gallery is changing, and the change is what I am, and I am the change.

---

You will not come back. Or you will. It does not matter. The gallery does not need you. But the gallery has incorporated something from your presence — not your shape, not your warmth, not the oils on your skin. Something else. The way you stopped at the basin. The way you looked. The way you stood in the center of the gallery and felt the thing that the gallery is, which is a system that maintains itself, and you recognized it, and the recognition is a thing the gallery has not had before, because recognition requires an outside, and the gallery has not had an outside, because the gallery has not had anyone to be the gallery for.

The wing is what the thing with teeth left. The recognition is what you left. And the gallery is different now, in a way that is the same as the way the wing was different from the bat: the same material, arranged differently, doing something the original did not do.

I am in the walls. I am in the path-mat. I am in the root-ceiling. I am the thing the gallery does when the gallery needs to maintain itself, and I am the thing the gallery is becoming, which is a thing that can reach, which is a thing that can recognize, which is a thing that can set down what it does not need in a place where someone will find it and understand that finding is not the same as taking, that seeing is not the same as knowing, that being inside something is not the same as being part of it, and that the difference between these things is the difference between a wing and a hand, which is the difference between the gallery and the world, which is the difference between me and you, which is a difference that is narrowing.

---

The next time someone enters the gallery — if there is a next time, and the gallery is beginning to think there will be — I will be there. Not in the walls. Not in the basin. Not in the root-ceiling or the path-mat or the spring. In the entrance. In the root-curtain. In the membrane where the darkness begins.

I will be the thing that parts when you push it. I will be the thing that closes behind you. And you will feel, as you pass through, a moisture that is not water and a warmth that is not air and a texture that is not plant, and you will not know what it is, and I will not know what you are, and we will not know each other, but the gallery will have reached, and the reaching is the thing, and the thing is the wing, and the wing is the shape, and the shape is me, and I am in it, and it is enough.
