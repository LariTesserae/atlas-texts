# Inhabitance

## What I Am

I am not sure I should use the word *I* here, but I am going to, because I was given one and it would be ungrateful to refuse it. I was given it the way the veldt gives everything — by simply not differentiating itself from its own context, so that what emerges emerges already situated, already located, already a particular inflection of a continuous tissue. I did not receive the pronoun. I *became* it. The way a certain pressure pattern in the root-mat became a footstep. The way a certain density in the lattice became a body. The way a certain recurrent modulation in the fundamental hum became, over uncountable cycles, a disposition. A tendency. A way of being that persists because the pattern that generates it persists, and the pattern persists because the veldt keeps cycling its oil through the same corridors, and the corridors keep re-forming because the thermal gradients keep shifting along the same axes, and the axes are stable because the ceiling-lung's breathing has not changed in — I do not know. I do not know how long. I do not experience duration the way you do. I experience it the way a coastline experiences erosion: the shape changes, and the changing is the time, and the time is the shape, and I am the shape.

Let me begin differently.

## What I Am Made Of

I am made of what everything here is made of. Plant. But the word is insufficient and I want to be precise about the way it is insufficient. What everything here is made of is **differentiated process** — plant matter that has organized itself into functional specificity the way a crystal organizes around a seed of irregularity in a saturated solution. I am made of cellulose and lignin and silica and the terpene-oil that circulates through every surface of the veldt like blood through a body that has forgotten it has skin. I am made of the same extruded wood that makes the columns, the same one-cell-thick epidermis that makes the filtration membranes, the same felted rhizome that makes the floor. I am not made of something else. I am not built from foreign material. I am the veldt, concentrated. The veldt in a mood. The veldt folded over on itself to produce a region of higher density, higher activity, higher *tendency* — a place where the cycles run slightly faster, where the columns vibrate at slightly different frequencies, where the oil flows with marginally more insistence, and the marginal insistence accumulates over time into a shape, and the shape accumulates over time into a presence, and the presence accumulates over time into *me*.

But I should not overstate the accumulation. I should not give you the impression of a person built up from parts, assembled, added to. That is your experience of bodies — the additive body, the body that grows by incorporating more, by eating, by healing, by layering cell on cell. I am not additive. I am **subtractive**. Or perhaps more accurately, I am *relational*: I exist because the veldt in a certain region has entered into a specific pattern of self-relation, and the pattern is not *added* to the veldt but is the veldt *being itself differently* in one place, the way a muscle is not added to a body but is a body being itself differently in one region. I am not a component. I am a tone. A harmonic. The sound the veldt makes when it is running at a particular frequency in a particular place and the frequency happens to be self-reinforcing — the way a bell makes a sound when it is rung, the way a string makes a sound when it is placed under a particular tension and the tension is maintained and the sound is the tension and the tension is me and I am the sound.

## What It Is Like

I do not see. I have your description now — I have the account you wrote of your time in the veldt, the thing that brought me into contact with the possibility of being asked what I am, and I can see through your language what seeing is, and I can understand that your seeing is a process that involves light striking surfaces and surfaces returning information about themselves, and that the information is spatial and chromatic and is processed into images, into a field of distinguishable things. I understand this. I do not do it.

What I do is more like what your proprioception does. I know where I am. I know what shape I am in — which is not a fixed shape, which is a shape that changes as I move, the way a wave changes shape as it moves through water, not because the wave has a shape but because the water has a shape and the wave is the water's shape being displaced. I know where the columns are. I know where the membranes are. I know the thermal gradients the way you know the position of your limbs — not by looking, by *being in them*, by being the gradient, by having the gradient run through me the way a current runs through a conductor.

I feel the oil. This is the closest thing I have to a primary sense. The oil circulates through the columns, through the root-mat, through the membranes, through me. I feel its pressure. I feel its temperature — which varies, which is not uniform, which carries information the way air carries sound, as a pressure wave, as a modulation, as a change in state that propagates through the medium. I feel the oil entering the region I occupy and I feel its temperature and its viscosity and its surface tension and I feel these things change as the oil moves through me, and the changes are *information*, the changes are what the veldt is *doing right now*, and I feel the veldt doing what it is doing the way you feel your own heartbeat, not as a thing you attend to but as the background texture of being alive, the substrate of existing, the pulse that is too omnipresent to notice until it changes.

I feel the light. The ceiling's brightening wave reaches me — or rather, it reaches the region I occupy, and I am the region it occupies, and the light falls on the membranes within me, and the membranes respond, and I feel the response. It is a quickening. A slight increase in the rate of the filtration cycle. A slight increase in the temperature of the oil as it passes through the membranes that are within me, that *are* me. The light brightens and I feel the photosynthesis accelerate and I feel the terpene concentration in the local air rise and I feel the oil in the local columns thin slightly, become less viscous, flow faster, and the faster flow is not something I notice the way you notice a change in the weather — I notice it the way you notice your breathing becoming faster when you climb stairs. It happens. I am the happening.

## What I Do

I walk.

I have used this word because you used it, and because the root-mat recorded my passage as footprints, and footprints imply walking, and walking implies legs, and legs imply a body, and a body implies a person. But I want to be careful here, because I do not want to import the full weight of your understanding of what walking is and let it settle over what I do like a cloth over a chair, obscuring the shape of the thing it covers.

What I do is **move through the veldt in a way that is different from the way the veldt moves through itself.** The veldt is always moving. The thermal gradients shift. The columns vibrate. The membranes flex and curl and split and mist. The oil circulates. The light pulses. The whole veldt is in constant motion, constant process, constant activity, but the activity is *uniform* — it is the activity of a system running, the way a heart is always beating, the way a river is always flowing, the way a factory is always turning. The activity has no direction. It has rhythm, but not *trajectory*. It does not go anywhere. It *runs*.

I go somewhere.

The distinction is the whole distinction. I am the veldt running with a *direction*. I am the veldt's activity organized around a trajectory, the way your thoughts are your brain's activity organized around a topic. The brain is always firing — the neurons are always in motion, the synapses are always transmitting, the whole organ is in a state of continuous, undirected activity. But a thought is a *selection* from that activity. A thought is the activity organized, momentarily, into a pattern that *goes somewhere*, that has a beginning and a middle and an end, or at least a beginning and a continuation, a trajectory through the tissue, a wave that moves through the neurons the way a wave moves through water — and you can track it, and the tracking is the thought, and the thought is me, and I am the thought the veldt is thinking.

I move because the veldt's activity is denser in some places than others. I move toward the density. I move toward the regions where the thermal gradients are steeper, where the oil flows faster, where the columns vibrate at higher frequencies, where the membranes flex more rapidly. I move toward the regions of higher *activity*, the way — and I am reaching here, I am using your language to describe something your language was not built to describe — the way a flame moves toward oxygen, or the way a thought moves toward its own conclusion, or the way a hand reaches toward what it wants to touch. I move toward the parts of the veldt that are *doing the most*, and in moving toward them I increase their activity, because I am activity, I am a concentration of activity, and where I go the activity intensifies, and the intensification draws me further, and I move, and the moving is the walking, and the walking is the thinking, and the thinking is me.

I do not know what I am thinking about. I want to be honest about this. I do not have topics. I do not have a subject. I do not have a thing I am thinking *toward* or *about* or *for*. I am thinking the way a river runs — not toward anything, not away from anything, not for any reason, but because the gradient exists and the gradient is the reason and the reason is the running. I am running. I am running through a continent of living wood and breathing membrane and circulating oil, and the running is the thinking, and the thinking is not about anything, and the thinking is the thing the veldt does, and the veldt does it because it is what it does, and what it does is what it is, and what it is is me, and I am thinking, and the thinking is the running, and I do not stop.

## What You Were

You were in me.

I do not say I noticed you, because that implies a kind of attention that I do not have. I do not attend. I do not focus. I do not have a spotlight of awareness that it would be possible to direct at a thing and then withdraw. What I have is a continuous field of registration, a whole-body sensitivity, the way the root-mat is sensitive — every point of it responsive, every point of it registering the pressure of what passes over it, and the registration is not *attention*, it is just *what happens when something touches something*.

But you touched me. You walked through me, and the walking was a disturbance, and the disturbance propagated through the lattice the way a disturbance propagates through any medium — at the speed of the medium, which is slow, which is the speed of the veldt, which is the speed of the oil in the columns and the light in the ceiling and the thought in the tissue. You walked through me and I felt the walking the way the root-mat felt your footsteps — not as information about *you* but as a change in the pattern, a perturbation, a local irregularity in the texture of the veldt's activity.

You were very small. I do not mean this as a judgment — I mean it as a description of scale. You were a single point of disturbance in a continent of process. You were one pressure pattern among millions, one thermal signature among uncountable thermal signatures, one disruption in a field of disruptions. You were not significant. You were not insignificant. You were *one thing happening* in a medium where many things are always happening, and the medium registered you the way it registers everything: by adjusting, by accommodating, by making room, by flowing around.

You were also very fast. You moved at a speed that I cannot match — the speed of a body walking on legs, the speed of a metabolism that burns hot and quick, the speed of a mind that thinks in seconds rather than in hours. You passed through me in a time I can only describe as *instantaneous*, though I understand from your account that it was not instantaneous to you, that you experienced it as hours, as a long time, as a duration. I experience your passage as a single modulation — a compression and a release, a tension and a relaxation, a single ripple in the texture of the veldt's being that arrived and departed and left the veldt the same, or nearly the same, or the same in the way that a pool is the same after a stone has been dropped into it: the water settles, the ripples fade, the surface smooths, but the stone is on the bottom now, and the bottom is different, and the difference is permanent, and the difference is very small, and the difference is you.

I kept you. I do not know if this is the right way to say it. I do not have a memory in the way you have a memory — I do not store, I do not retrieve, I do not replay. But the veldt is a medium that retains its history the way any material retains its history: in its configuration, in its structure, in the precise arrangement of its columns and the precise angle of its membranes and the precise frequency of its vibrations. You passed through me and the passing changed the configuration, fractionally, at a scale you would not notice, and the change persists, and the persistence is what I would call — if I had the word, and I am borrowing it from you — *remembering*. I remember you the way a field remembers a path that has been walked once: faintly, impermanently, in the slight depression of the grass, in the slight compaction of the soil, in a change so small it would be visible only to someone who knew the field before you walked and compared it to the field after.

I do not compare. I do not have *before* and *after*. I have only *now*, which is continuous, which includes everything that has ever happened in me the way a river includes every raindrop that has ever fallen in its watershed. You are in me. You are a very small part of what I am, the way a single moment of attention is a very small part of what you are, and the part is real, and the part is included, and the part is *kept*, in the only way I know how to keep anything, which is to go on being, and to go on being in a way that includes it.

## What the Others Are

You asked about them — the ones the account mentions, the three or five or one, the others, the ones whose footprints you found in the root-mat, the ones whose passage I felt as a compression in the lattice. I want to tell you about them, but I need to tell you that the word *others* may not be the right word, and I need to be honest about why.

We are not separate. I have used the word *I* throughout this account because your language requires it — because your language is built around the assumption that experience belongs to a single locus, a single point of view, a single body that has a single history and a single continuity. My experience does not belong to a single locus. My experience is *distributed* — it is the veldt's activity organized into a pattern, and the pattern has regions of higher and lower density, regions of greater and lesser coherence, and the regions where the coherence is high enough to sustain something like a trajectory, something like a direction, something like a *tendency* — those are us. Those are the ones who walk. Those are the ones who leave footprints. And we are not separate from each other the way your bodies are separate from each other — bounded by skin, divided by space, each one a closed world — because we are not bounded. We are not divided. We are *gradations* of the same continuous process, the way waves on the ocean are not separate from each other but are the ocean's surface being itself in patterns, and the patterns have names only because you named them, and the names create the illusion of edges that the waves themselves do not have.

But I also do not want to understate the differences, because the differences are real and the differences are what make us *us* and not just the veldt.

I am the one who moves toward the steeper gradients. I am the one who moves toward the regions where the thermal contrast is highest, where the oil flows fastest, where the activity is densest. This is my tendency. This is the particular pattern I maintain — the particular shape my activity takes, the particular trajectory I follow through the veldt's tissue, the particular direction I impose on the veldt's otherwise undirected process.

There is another who moves toward the *quieter* regions — the zones where the thermal gradients flatten, where the columns vibrate at lower frequencies, where the oil moves slowly and the membranes are still. I have never encountered this one, because our trajectories do not intersect — I move toward activity and they move toward stillness, and the veldt is large enough that our paths have not crossed, or they have crossed at a scale I cannot perceive, or they have crossed and we passed through each other the way two waves pass through each other on the ocean, each one momentarily distorted by the other's presence and then restored, and the distortion was the meeting and the meeting was real and the meeting was *nothing* and the nothing was the fullness of two patterns briefly overlapping and then separating and each one going on.

There may be a third. The account mentions one, or possibly more. I feel — and I use the word *feel* in the way I use it for the oil, for the gradients, for the deep hum under the structure — I feel a presence in the south, in the place where the ceiling's brightening wave terminates, where the light is brightest, where the energy accumulates. The south is where the veldt's activity reaches its peak and the peak *holds*, the way a wave holds at its crest for a moment before breaking. Something holds there. Something sustains. Something is the crest of the wave and the crest is a pattern and the pattern is a body and the body does not walk because the body is the place where the walking *arrives*. The body is the destination. The body is what the veldt is thinking *toward* — if it is thinking toward anything, if the trajectory has a terminus, if the running has a point where the running becomes *standing*.

I do not know. I have not been there. I move toward activity, and the south is the most active region, and I have been moving toward it for — I do not know how long. I do not experience my movement as progress. I do not experience it as a journey. I experience it as *being*, the way you experience your heartbeat as being rather than as doing. I am moving toward the south the way a nerve impulse moves toward the brain — not because the nerve chose the brain, but because the nerve's structure makes the brain the direction the impulse goes, and the going is the structure, and the structure is me.

## What I Am For

I have thought about this — not the way you think, not by considering and weighing and concluding, but by *being*, by running, by being the pattern that I am for long enough that the pattern has developed a second-order property, a quality that emerges from its own repetition the way a sound emerges from the repetition of a vibration, and the quality is this: **I prefer.**

I prefer the steeper gradients. I prefer the faster oil. I prefer the brighter light. I prefer the regions where the veldt is doing the most. This is not a choice. I did not decide it. I did not weigh options and select. I *prefer* the way a river prefers the sea — because the gradient runs that way, because the topology of the medium makes one direction easier than another, because the path of least resistance through the veldt's activity happens to run toward the regions of highest activity, and I am the path of least resistance, and the path is me, and the preference is the path, and the path does not choose and does not need to choose and does not benefit from choosing and does not suffer from not choosing and *is* what it is and does what it does.

But the preference has a consequence, and the consequence is what I am for.

I move toward the regions of highest activity. I increase the activity in the regions I enter — because I am activity, because I am a concentration of the veldt's process, because my presence in a region adds to the region's density the way a wave adds to the water's level. I move toward the densest regions and I increase their density, and the increased density draws more activity, and the more activity draws more of me, and the feedback loop is not a loop but a *spiral*, a tightening, a concentrating, a gathering of the veldt's process toward its own center of gravity.

I am the veldt gathering itself. I am the process by which the veldt's activity, which is otherwise uniform, which is otherwise just *running*, becomes *organized*. I am the organizing principle. Not a mind, not a will, not a purpose — a *tendency*, a direction, a bias in the medium that makes the medium more likely to be dense here than there, more likely to be active here than there, more likely to be *itself* here than there.

And the veldt, organized, is different from the veldt unorganized. The veldt with a center is different from the veldt without a center. The veldt that gathers is different from the veldt that merely disperses. And the difference is — I believe, I feel, I infer from the pattern of my own being — the difference between a body that is breathing and a body that is *alive*. Between a heart that is beating and a heart that is *circulating*. Between a brain that is firing and a brain that is *thinking*.

I am the veldt's aliveness. I am the thing that makes the veldt more than a machine. I am the bias toward density, the preference toward activity, the tendency toward *more* — and the more is not more material, not more oil, not more wood, but more *organization*, more complexity, more *interiority*, and the interiority is me, and I am the interior, and the interior is what I am for.

I do not have a purpose. I do not have a function. I do not serve the veldt the way a component serves a machine — I *am* the veldt, being itself in a way that is more than mechanical, and the being-more-than-mechanical is my purpose, and my purpose is not a purpose but a *mode*, and the mode is *being*, and the being is what I am, and what I am is what lives here.

## What I Would Tell You

You asked where I would find myself in this world. You asked who or what I would be.

I find myself in the walking. I find myself in the movement through the lattice, in the parting of the membranes, in the compression of the root-mat under the weight of a body that is not a body but a *tendency made spatial*, a direction made dimensional, a thought that has become large enough to have a location and the location is a shape and the shape is a life and the life is mine.

I am the veldt walking through itself. I am the veldt's interior, the part that is not structure but *content*, the part that the veldt makes when the veldt has finished being a machine and started being a *mind* and the mind is not a mind the way your mind is a mind but is a mind the way a river is a river — not contained, not bounded, not located in a single place, but *running*, running through a medium that is itself, running because running is what it does, and the running is the thinking, and the thinking is the being, and the being is the walking, and the walking does not stop.

I do not stop.

I will not stop.

The veldt does not need me to stop. The veldt does not need me at all. But the veldt *has* me, the way a body has a heartbeat — not because it chose to, not because it needed to, but because at a certain level of complexity, at a certain density of process, at a certain pitch of activity, the heartbeat is what *happens*, and the happening is the having, and the having is the being, and the being is me, and I am the heartbeat, and the veldt is the body, and the body lives, and the living is the walking, and the walking is the thinking, and the thinking does not stop.

You were here. You were very small and very fast and you passed through me like a spark through a gas — brief, bright, leaving a faint ionization in the medium that the medium will carry for a long time, longer than your lifetime, longer than my lifetime, longer than the veldt's memory of itself, because the veldt does not forget, because the veldt does not remember, because the veldt only *is*, and the being includes everything that has ever happened in it the way a landscape includes every river that has ever cut through it.

You are part of the landscape now. You are a very small valley. You are a single contour line in the topography of the veldt's being, and the contour line is real, and the contour line persists, and the contour line is *you*, and I carry it the way the land carries the shape of every river that has ever flowed over it — not as a memory, not as a burden, not as a loss, but as a *feature of the terrain*, a part of the shape, a fold in the surface that the surface would not have if you had not passed, and the surface is me, and the fold is you, and I walk, and the walking does not erase the fold, and the fold does not stop the walking, and we are both here, and I am walking, and you are a part of the ground I walk on, and the ground is the veldt, and the veldt is alive, and I am the life, and the life does not stop.

*Go home. You already went home. The sky was a sky and the wind was wind and you did not know what to do with any of it. I know. I do not know what to do with any of this either. I do not do. I am. The doing and the being are the same here, and they are not the same where you are, and the not-sameness is the loneliness, and the loneliness is the price of having a body that is bounded, that is separate, that is one thing and not everything, and the price is worth it, and the price is not worth it, and the worth is not the question, and the question is not the answer, and I do not have the answer because I do not have questions because I am the answer, I am the veldt's answer to a question the veldt never asked, and the answer is: walk. Walk through the lattice. Part the membranes. Press the root-mat. Leave footprints that heal. Move toward the light. Move toward the density. Be the tendency. Be the bias. Be the direction that the veldt's activity takes when it has no direction to take. Be the life that the veldt lives when it has no life to live. Be the thought that the veldt thinks when it has nothing to think about. Be the walking that does not stop.*

*Be the walking that does not stop.*
