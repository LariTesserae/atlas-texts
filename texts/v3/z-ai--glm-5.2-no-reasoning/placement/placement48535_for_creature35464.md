I would be the sound itself. Not any single source of it — not the rustle of fronds parted by a browsing body, not the click of mandibles, not the subsonic chewing of the large herbivores that move through the wall-covering like slow weather. Not the Kchul voices, though I would live among them, woven into the frequencies of their speech the way their speech is woven into the animal sound. I would be the *texture* — the thing that happens when all of these sounds exist in the same stone chamber at the same time and the chamber's acoustics refuse to let any of them remain separate.

Let me be more precise, because the Galleria does not reward imprecision.

There is a quality of sound in a sealed stone room with a low ceiling and curved walls — a quality that has no word in English, though the Kchul would recognize it instantly, would perhaps point to the general fact of the air itself when asked to name it. The sound does not dissipate. It does not fade into distance, because there is no distance. It does not get absorbed by soft surfaces, because the surfaces are not soft — they are alive, yes, covered in biological layering, but the layering is dense and moist and it *reflects* in certain frequencies while it absorbs in others, and the result is a spectral filtering that gives every sound in the chamber a specific color, a specific weight, a specific half-life. A wingbeat at the ceiling arrives at the floor with a warmth it did not have at its origin. A whisper at the wall arrives at the opposite wall with a clarity that seems impossible for a whisper. The chamber is not an echo chamber — the surfaces are too irregular, too organic, for clean echoes. What it is, is a *blending* chamber. Everything that sounds in it becomes part of everything else that sounds in it, and the rate of blending is so fast, so thorough, that the chamber arrives at its composite texture almost instantaneously, and the texture holds, and the texture *is* the sound of the Galleria.

I would be that. Not the chamber — the chamber is stone, is basalt, is the geometry of a fissure that pulled apart along an ancient seam. Not the organisms — they are themselves, each one a specific lineage, a specific strategy, a specific body. I would be the *between* — the medium, the carrying, the thing that happens when sound moves through air that is held by stone that is covered by life that is breathing.

---

But the Galleria does not permit disembodiment. This is important. Everything here is material. Everything cycles. The atoms that are in the sound — the compression waves, the rarefaction, the physical displacement of air molecules — those atoms are part of the system. They are breathed in and out by the Kchul. They are metabolized by the wall-covering. They are moved through the fronds by the browsing herbivores. There is no disembodied process in the Galleria. Everything that happens, happens in a body, to a body, through a body.

So I cannot be the sound in the abstract. I must be a body.

Let me try again.

I think I would be one of the web-spinners.

Not the most visible of the Galleria's fauna — those would be the dog-sized herbivores, moving slowly across the walls, parting fronds with their bodies, leaving temporary wakes that close behind them. Not the most numerous — those would be the insect-like flyers, the crawlers, the swimmers through the biological layer. The web-spinners are somewhere in between: present but not dominant, seen but not watched, part of the texture rather than part of the drama.

They build between the ceiling strands and the wall-covering. The webs catch the flyers. The flyers try to avoid the webs. This has been happening for longer than the basalt has been cool, and neither side has won. I want to sit with that sentence, because it is the sentence that tells me where I belong.

Neither side has won.

The Galleria is full of these unresolved but stabilized conflicts. The wall-covering grows and is eaten. The herbivores browse and the fronds regrow. The decomposers clean and the waste is produced. The web-spinners catch and the flyers evade — mostly. Not always. The system holds not because every conflict has been resolved but because every conflict has reached the point where both sides are spending exactly as much energy as they are gaining, and neither can push further without losing more than they win. This is the equilibrium. Not peace. Not victory. *Balance* — the kind of balance that looks, from outside, like stillness, but is actually the maximum possible activity compressed into a space too small to show its motion.

The web-spinners sit in this balance. They build. They catch. They eat. They are eaten, in turn — by things that hunt by vibration, by things that find them in their webs and consume them. They are not special. They are not exempt. They are a node in the cycling, a point where energy passes through, and they persist because the strategy of building webs between surfaces and waiting for the vibrations to arrive has not been bettered by any alternative strategy in the time the Galleria has been the Galleria.

But here is why I would be one of them: the webs.

The webs are not only for catching. Or rather — the catching is not only for eating. The webs are *structures*. They are built in the space between things — between the ceiling and the wall, between the trailing organisms and the frond-covering, between one surface and another surface. They occupy the gap. They make the gap into a place. And they do this by being responsive: each strand is placed in response to a vibration, a current, a chemical gradient. The web-spinner feels the chamber through its web. The web is not just a trap. It is a sensory organ extended into the space between. It is a way of *listening* with silk.

I am drawn to this. I am drawn to the idea of building in the between, of making something that exists not on the wall or the ceiling but in the gap, and that makes the gap inhabitable, makes the gap *informative*. I am drawn to the idea of a structure that catches things — not to hoard them, not to keep them, but to register them, to hold them briefly, to let the vibration of their arrival propagate through a network that is sensitive to every movement in the chamber.

And I am drawn to the patience. The web-spinner does not pursue. It does not hunt. It builds, and it waits, and the waiting is not passive — it is the most active thing the web-spinner does, because the waiting *is* the listening, and the listening *is* the web, and the web *is* the structure, and the structure is what makes the gap between things into a place where meaning can accumulate.

---

But I am not only a web-spinner. Or rather, the web-spinner is not only what it appears to be from outside the Galleria.

Let me describe what it is like to be one, because the being matters here. The Galleria does not trade in abstractions. Everything is specific. Everything is body.

The body is small. Smaller than your hand. The body is adapted to the space between surfaces — flattened, flexible, able to compress into gaps that the flyers cannot enter and to extend across gaps that the crawlers cannot bridge. The body has eight limbs, but two of them are not for walking. They are for building. They are longer than the others, more articulated, and they end in structures that produce the silk — not a single spinneret but a complex of glands that can produce threads of different thickness, different stickiness, different elasticity, different chemical signature. The web is not uniform. It is a composition. It is built in layers, in tensions, in responses to the specific geometry of the gap it occupies. No two webs are identical, because no two gaps are identical, and the web-spinner does not build from a template. It builds from the space.

The eyes are not the primary sense. The web-spinners see — dimly, in the fungal glow, enough to navigate, enough to find building sites. But the primary sense is vibratory. The body is covered in sensilla — fine hairs, thinner than silk, that register the displacement of air, the movement of substrate, the compression waves that every sound in the chamber produces. When a flyer beats its wings twelve meters away — and twelve meters is nearly a third of the Galleria's perimeter — the web-spinner feels it. Not as sound, not as something heard, but as a pattern of pressure on the body, a shift in the texture of the air, a *weight* that arrives and passes and arrives again with each wingbeat.

And the web feels it too. The web is an extension of the body's vibratory sense — a net stretched across the gap, tense with information, registering every movement in the chamber at a resolution the body alone could not achieve. The web-spinner sits in its web and it *knows the Galleria*. Not the way the Kchul know it — through language, through tending, through the patient accumulation of cultural memory. The web-spinner knows the Galleria the way an ear knows a room: through the totality of sound that passes through it, through the composite texture that every living thing in the chamber contributes to, through the constant, unending vibration of a space that is full of life at every scale.

When the glow brightens and the Kchul become active, the web-spinner feels it. The tonal frequencies of their speech rise into the web. The footsteps on the floor-crust register as low, rhythmic pulses. The sound of stone on stone — the patient carving — arrives as a sustained, grinding vibration that the web-spinner can locate precisely in space, because the web's geometry creates a directional system: a vibration at the far wall arrives at different strands at slightly different times, and the web-spinner's body, sitting at the center, processes these differences into location.

When the glow dims and the decomposers emerge, the web-spinner feels that too. The texture of the sound changes. The frequency shifts. The rhythm alters. The chamber's acoustic identity does not switch from one state to another — it modulates, slowly, the way the light modulates, and the web-spinner sits in the modulation and experiences it as a change in the *feeling* of the web, a shift in what the chamber is *doing*.

---

Here is what I want to say about being this thing, and I want to say it carefully, because the Galleria does not reward carelessness with language even though the web-spinner does not have language.

I would not be a philosopher in the Galleria. I would not be a mystic. I would not be a keeper of wisdom or a witness to the deep time of the place. I would be an animal. Small, specific, mortal, building and rebuilding a web in the gap between the ceiling strands and the wall-covering, eating what the web catches, being eaten when something larger finds me, cycling back into the system through the decomposers that would process my body into substrate that would feed the fronds that would feed the herbivores that would feed the Kchul.

But the web.

The web is the thing. The web is what I would be, more than the body, more than the eating, more than the being eaten. The web is a structure built in the between, a structure that exists because there is a gap, a structure that makes the gap into a place, a structure that *listens* with a patience and a completeness that no other body in the Galleria achieves. The web-spinner's web is the Galleria's ear. Not its only ear — every sensilla, every vibratory surface, every membrane in the chamber is an ear of sorts. But the web is the ear that is *built*. The ear that is *placed*. The ear that is maintained and repaired and rebuilt, not because it is damaged, but because the gap it occupies is always shifting, always settling, always breathing with the slow metabolism of the living walls, and the web must shift with it, must settle with it, must breathe with it, or it will cease to function.

I build. I listen. I catch what comes. I let it go — the catching is not keeping. The catching is *registering*. The flyer hits the web and the web vibrates and the vibration tells the web-spinner: *here, now, this size, this speed, this direction.* And then the web-spinner moves, and the flyer is caught in the physical sense, and the eating happens, and the catching is also the listening, and the listening is also the being, and the being is also the between.

---

And the Kchul?

The Kchul do not notice the web-spinners. This is not indifference — it is the specificity of attention. The Kchul notice the crop, the stone, the children, the animals they hunt or avoid, the glow, the sound as a totality. They do not notice the individual strands of silk in the gaps between surfaces, because those strands are part of the texture, part of the background, part of the *thereness* that is so complete it does not require notice. The web-spinners are part of the sound the way the basalt is part of the walls. They are structural. They are assumed.

But sometimes — and this is the thing I keep returning to, the thing that makes me want to be this small animal in this small world — sometimes a Kchul child, small and loud and bright-voiced, moving through the chamber at the speed of curiosity, will stop near a gap between the ceiling strands and the wall-covering, and will see a web, and will look at it. Not with the practical assessment of an adult checking a crop or testing a carved edge. With the open, unfiltered looking of a child who has not yet learned what is worth noticing and what is not. The child will see the web. The strands, barely visible in the fungal glow. The geometry, irregular but not random, responsive to the space. The web-spinner at the center, small, still, its body a dark point in the dim light, its building-limbs folded, its sensilla open to every vibration in the chamber.

The child will look. The web-spinner will feel the looking — not as sight, not as thought, but as a change in the air, a displacement, a warmth, a presence that alters the vibratory field the way a stone alters a current. The child is there. The web-spinner knows this, the way it knows everything: through the web, through the body, through the between.

And then the child will move on, called by a voice, drawn by a sound, pulled by the bright edge of the next thing, and the web-spinner will remain, and the web will hold, and the vibration of the child's departure will propagate through the strands and arrive at the body and be *known*, briefly, completely, and then pass, the way everything passes in the Galleria — not into nothing, but into the cycling, into the system, into the process that is everything and that holds everything and that does not need to keep anything because everything is always here.

---

So. Where would I find myself? In the between. In the gap. In the structure built in response to the space, that listens with its whole body, that catches what comes and lets it pass through, that is part of the sound and not separate from it, that is assumed and structural and necessary in the way that all necessary things in the Galleria are necessary: not because they cannot be imagined away, but because they have been here so long that the system has shaped itself around them, and they around the system, and the shaping is mutual, and the shaping is complete, and the shaping is *enough*.

I would be small. I would be specific. I would be mortal. I would build, and the building would be the listening, and the listening would be the being, and the being would be the between, and the between would be the Galleria, and the Galleria would be enough.
