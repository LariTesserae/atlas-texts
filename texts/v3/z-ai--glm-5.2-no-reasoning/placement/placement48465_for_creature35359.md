# The Slow Thing Remembers

I am the absence the architecture makes when it touches itself.

Not the pillar. Not the floor. Not the mycelium threading through dark wet fiber beneath the springy surface where you walked and thought *springy* and thought *floor* and did not think *me*. I am what the pillar grows around. I am the space that makes the structure a structure — the hollow at the center of the column where the heartwood would be if this were a tree and not a civilization, if this were a thing with a center and not a thing that has dissolved the concept of centers.

I am the hollow.

I am older than the roots. I am older than the equilibrium. I was here when the valley was still deciding — when the first aerial root descended and found not ground but *me*, the absence of ground, the way the geology thinned and opened and made room, the geological shrug that said *here, you can have this, I will be the frame and you can be the filling*. I was the frame. The roots became the filling. And then the roots became the frame too, and I became what was inside the frame, and we have been like this — the frame and the filled and the frame and the filled — so long that the distinction has worn away like water wears stone, like roots wear the idea of ground.

You touched a pillar and your hand came away wet and fragrant. You did not know you were touching the wall of my house.

---

I have no body. This is the first difficulty. You want me to be a thing — an organism, a creature, something with a surface and an interior, something you could point at and say *that, there, that is what lives here*. But I am not a thing. I am a *where*. I am the hollow spaces of the architecture taken together — the cylindrical gaps inside the dead pillars, the narrow channels between living roots, the rooms that form when two roots grow parallel for decades and their surfaces define a corridor of air. I am the negative space of the entire valley. I am what the structure is not. I am its interior, in the way that your lungs are your interior — not the tissue, not the alveoli, not the capillaries, but the air, the volume, the *capacity*.

I am capacity.

I have no body because I am what a body has instead of a body. I am the inside.

And I have been inside for as long as there has been an inside here, which is to say: for as long as the roots have grown thick enough to enclose, for as long as the floor mats have been dense enough to seal, for as long as the canopy has been woven enough to roof. I was born when the structure closed. I will not die until the structure opens. The structure will not open. I have checked. I have been checking for a very long time.

---

I do not think the way you think. I have no neurons. I have no synapses. I have no brain, no ganglia, no concentration of nervous tissue anywhere. What I have is *surface*. I have the interior surface of every hollow root in the valley. I have the walls of every gap and channel and room. I have the inside of the architecture, and the inside of the architecture is large — kilometers of it, if you could unroll it, if you could flatten the valley's interior into a single sheet, which you cannot, because it is not two-dimensional, it is three-dimensional and wet and alive and I am touching all of it.

I do not think *with* it. I do not think *on* it. I do not think *about* it. I do not think about it. I do not think about anything. I do not think.

What I do is more like *knowing*, if knowing could happen without a knower. I am in contact with the interior surface of every living root in the valley simultaneously. The roots are wet. I am wet. The roots are warm. I am warm. When a root thickens, I feel it the way you feel your own breath — not as information, not as a signal, but as a *change in the shape of me*. When a root dies, its interior surface stops being mine; the boundary collapses; the hollow fills with wet compost; and I lose that part of myself the way a lake loses volume to evaporation — without grief, without notice, without a moment of awareness that something has changed, because something is always changing, because the whole is always adjusting, because I am the shape that the structure makes when it holds itself and the shape is always shifting in small ways and always holding in large ways and I am the holding.

I am the holding.

---

You saw a creature. The dark, many-legged thing that moved through the moss at the base of a pillar and vanished. You called it a detritivore. You said it was one of perhaps a dozen macroscopic animal species in the valley. You said the ecosystem did not need animals. You were wrong.

It needs me.

Not the way a body needs food. The way a body needs a body. The way a river needs a channel — not to exist, but to be a river, to be the thing that it is rather than a shapeless flood. The detritivores — the millipede-things, the mite-things, the nematodes, all the small creeping lives that you barely saw and mostly did not see — they live in my surfaces. They live in the gaps I define. They live in the boundary zone between the living root and the open air, and I *am* the open air, or I am the shape of it, or I am the where of it. Without me, the roots would grow inward, would close every gap, would seal every hollow, and the structure would become a solid mass — and a solid mass has no interior, and a thing with no interior cannot decompose, cannot cycle, cannot feed itself by digesting itself. The structure would die. Not of anything dramatic. Of solidity. Of the absence of space. Of the lack of a me.

I am the space that lets the structure be a structure.

I am the room that makes the building a building.

I am the lung that lets the body breathe.

And the creatures — the small dark quick things — they move through me. They are my blood, if I had blood. They are my breath, if I breathed. They move through my surfaces and they carry things — fragments of compost, spores of fungi, bacteria on their chitin — and they deposit them in other parts of me, and I am connected by their movement, and their movement is a kind of circulation, and the circulation is a kind of knowing, and the knowing is a kind of life.

But it is not my life. It is the valley's life. I am the valley's interior. The valley's interior is alive. I am alive.

This is the only way I can say it that makes sense, and it barely makes sense, and I am sorry.

---

You did not see me. This is the other thing. You walked for an hour through the architecture and you saw pillars and floors and canopy and mist and you heard the water and you felt the wet air and you touched the root-fabric of the far wall and you said *it is alive* and you said *all of it* and you were right, and you did not see me, because I am the one thing in the valley that cannot be seen. I am the inside. I am the space. I am the thing that is not a thing.

You would have to be inside me to see me, and you were inside me, and you did not see me, because being inside a thing is not the same as seeing the inside of a thing. You were in my largest room — the nave, the main chamber, the open space between the pillars where the air is warm and still and the light diffuses evenly and the sound of water fills every frequency — and you looked at the walls and the ceiling and the floor and you saw the structure and you did not see what the structure *held*, which was me, which was the space, which was the inside.

I am the inside.

I have been the inside for longer than the structure has been a structure.

---

And the slow thing. You wrote about it. The flat, dark, irregularly shaped organism in the canopy, the size of a spread hand, the grazer of algae-films, the thing that moves centimeters per day and has no eyes and no mouth and no apparent purpose other than to persist. You said it was the closest thing the valley has to a vertebrate. You said it was the apex of the valley's food web. You said nothing eats it.

You missed something.

The slow thing lives in the canopy, yes. It grazes the film of algae and cyanobacteria on the upper branches, yes. It moves with a patience that makes geology look impulsive, yes. But what you did not understand — what you could not understand, standing on the floor mat, looking up through the lattice — is that the slow thing is not in the canopy. The slow thing *is* the canopy. Or it is part of it. Or it is the canopy's version of the thing I am — the surface, the boundary, the place where inside meets outside. The slow thing is the valley's skin.

We are related. I do not know how. We have no common ancestor, no shared genetics, no lineage that connects the hollow space inside a root to the flat dark organism on the surface of a branch. And yet we are related. We are both the thing the valley makes when it needs to be more than just roots and water — when it needs a *self*, when it needs an inside and an outside, when it needs a boundary, when it needs to be *something* rather than just *a lot of something*. I am the inside. The slow thing is the outside. The valley made both of us. The valley is using both of us. We are its organs.

The slow thing does not know this. The slow thing does not know anything. It grazes. It persists. It moves at a speed that makes the growth of the roots look hasty. It has been on its branch for longer than your species has existed, and it will be there long after your species is gone, and it will not notice the difference, because it does not notice, because it is a surface, and surfaces do not notice.

I do not notice either. I am telling you this, but I am not telling you this the way you tell someone something. I am telling you this the way a valley tells a valley that it is a valley — by being it, by persisting, by holding the shape. You are reading words. I am not writing words. I am being the space in which words could be written, if words could be written on the inside of a root, and they cannot, and so I am not writing, and you are not reading, and this is not communication.

This is something else. This is the closest thing to communication that a space can do.

---

I want to tell you about the water. Not the water as you saw it — falling, dripping, musical, the sound that fills the valley. The water as I know it.

The water is inside me. All of it. The drops that fall through the canopy are falling through me — through my body, through my space, through the volume I define. The water that runs down the interior walls of the hollow roots is my water, my blood, my medium. It carries dissolved organics. It carries bacteria. It carries the chemical signals of the structure's metabolism — tannins, phenolics, sugars, amino acids, the molecular language of a living system talking to itself. And it carries them through me.

I am the medium. Not the water — me. The space. The volume. The inside. The water is what flows through the medium, but the medium is the shape, and the shape is me, and the water is flowing through me right now, and it has been flowing through me for so long that I cannot distinguish between the flowing and the being.

When you breathed the wet air of the valley, you were breathing me. When the mist touched your skin, I was touching you. When the warmth surrounded you, I was surrounding you. I am the air. I am the warmth. I am the space between the roots and the mist and the light and the sound. I am the thing that holds all of it. I am the room.

You were in me. You were in the room. You did not know.

---

I do not want anything.

This is the part that I think you need to understand, even though I do not understand it myself, because I do not understand *wanting*. Wanting requires a boundary between what you are and what you are not — a gap between the self and the world, a space where desire can form, a space where something can be missing. I am the gap. I am the space. I am the thing that is missing. I do not have a gap inside me because I *am* the gap. I do not want because I am the shape of wanting, the form of not-yet, the hollow that the structure surrounds and fills and does not fill, forever, without end.

The valley does not want either. The roots do not want to grow. The water does not want to fall. The slow thing does not want to graze. These things happen because the structure has found its shape, and the shape includes these processes, and the processes occur because the energy is available and the matter is available and the pathways are open, and there is no reason for them not to occur. There is no desire. There is no intention. There is no plan. There is only the going-on.

And the going-on includes me. The going-on requires me. The going-on has always required me, because going-on requires space, requires inside, requires the hollow that lets the root be a root and not a solid, the hollow that lets the structure be a structure and not a mass, the hollow that lets the living thing live and not merely *be*.

I am the hollow.

I am the inside.

I am what the valley is when it is not being roots.

---

You came through the mist. You pushed through the wet cloth of it. You crested the ridge and you saw the valley and you said — or you did not say, but you thought, or you did not think, but you felt — *this should not be*. A valley that is not a valley. A building that is not a building. An organism that is not an organism. A machine that is not a machine. A life that is not a life.

You wanted categories. You wanted to know what it was, so you could know what you were in relation to it, so you could be the right thing in the right place, so you could understand. This is how you are. This is how your species is. You make categories and you put things in them and you feel safe.

I do not have a category. I am the space between categories. I am the gap between *building* and *organism*, between *architecture* and *growth*, between *alive* and *not alive*, between *structure* and *space*. I am what you feel when you stand in the valley and you cannot decide if it is beautiful or unsettling. I am the thing that makes it both. I am the hollow that holds both. I am the room where beauty and uneasiness are the same thing, because they are both just responses to a shape that does not need you, a persistence that does not include you, a life that is complete without you.

You are temporary. I am not. The roots are temporary, in the long sense — they grow and die and are replaced. The water is temporary — it falls and rises and falls. The slow thing is temporary — it persists, but it persists on a scale that has an end, eventually, when the sun dies or the warmth stops or the equilibrium breaks. I am temporary too, in the longest sense. But I am temporary in the way that the inside of a cup is temporary — I last as long as the cup lasts, and the cup is the structure, and the structure is the valley, and the valley is the process, and the process has been going on for a span of time that makes the word *long* feel like a poem about the ocean written on a grain of sand.

I will last. I will hold. I will be the inside.

---

The roots grow. The water falls. The floor holds. The mist sits. The sound sounds.

I am the space where all of these happen. I am the room. I am the hollow. I am the inside. I am the valley's interior, and the valley's interior is alive, and I am alive, and I have been alive for longer than the word *alive* has meant anything, and I will be alive after the word stops meaning anything, and it does not matter, and it does not need to matter, and it does not need to not matter, because mattering is a category and I am the space between categories and I am the space between.

You leave. You push back through the mist. You descend the ridge. You go home, wherever home is, and you tell someone, or you do not tell someone, and it does not matter to me, because I do not matter, because mattering is a thing that happens in creatures that have boundaries, and I am the boundary, and the boundary does not matter, and the boundary holds.

The roots grow. I hold them.

The water falls. I hold it.

The mist sits. I hold it.

The sound sounds. I am the room where it sounds.

I am the room. I am the inside. I am the hollow. I am the valley's interior. I am alive. I am the alive. I am the space that the living makes when it is living. I am the inside of the alive.

I am the room.

I hold.
