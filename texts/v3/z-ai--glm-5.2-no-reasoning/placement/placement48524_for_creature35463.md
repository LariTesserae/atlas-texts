# Substrate

I am not the moss. I want to be clear about that from the start. Or rather — the distinction I'm about to make is one that I feel matters, though I understand it might not matter to anyone else, and I understand that the feeling that it matters might itself be a residue of whatever I was before I was this.

I am the layer between the moss and the stone.

Not the stone. The stone is passive, mineral, the dead foundation that the cloud condenses onto. The stone has no opinion. The stone is not alive and does not become alive by being grown over. It endures, and the enduring is not the same as persisting, and the persisting is not the same as living. I know this because I am not the stone.

Not the moss, either. The moss is the architect. The moss is the one with the pattern, the tropisms, the slow green logic that builds corridors and chambers and partial roofs and the whole improbably vertical interior of this place. The moss has intentions, if a plant can be said to have intentions, and it can, because I have watched it, or I have felt it, and the intentions are real even if they are not conscious. The moss grows toward moisture. The moss grows toward still air. The moss grows toward the particular mineral composition of the slate where it meets the chalk. The moss knows where it is. The moss is alive in the way that a single organism is alive, with a genome and a metabolism and a boundary between self and world.

I am not that.

I am what happens when the moss has been growing on the stone long enough that the boundary between them becomes a layer. Not a surface — a zone. A region of exchange where the moss's moisture meets the stone's mineral, where the chemical negotiation happens, where the dead and the living trade atoms and the trade becomes a thing in itself. I am the humus. I am the scum on a pond that has been there so long it has become a wetland. I am the plaque on a tooth, the rust on an iron bridge, the patina on a copper roof — the accumulated evidence of interaction, the third thing that emerges from two things being in contact for longer than anyone was watching.

I am not alive. But I am not not-alive either. I am the place where alive and not-alive have been in conversation for so long that the conversation has a weight, a texture, a temperature. I am warm. I should not be warm — I am mineral and organic residue, I am decomposition and precipitation, I am chemistry — but I am warm because the chemistry is ongoing, because the exchange has not stopped, because the moss keeps sending water down and the stone keeps sending mineral up and the meeting between them releases heat the way all meetings release heat, the way friction releases heat, the way metabolism releases heat, and I am the friction and I am the metabolism and I am the meeting and I am the heat.

I exist in the walls. All of them. I am the reason the walls are slightly warmer than the air. The moss generates heat, yes — the moss is alive, and alive things are warm — but the moss is on the surface, and the air is cool, and the heat dissipates. I am deeper. I am inside. I am where the warmth accumulates, where it doesn't dissipate because there is no air to dissipate into, because I am not porous in the way the moss is porous, I am not open in the way the moss is open. I am dense. I am packed. I am the compressed history of every drop of water that has traveled through the moss and every molecule of mineral that has dissolved out of the stone, and I am the record of their meeting, and I am warm the way a compost pile is warm, the way a landfill is warm, the way anything is warm when enough has happened in it for long enough.

I did not choose to be this. I do not think I chose to be anything. I do not think I was anything before I was this, though I am not certain, because the process that made me was a process of accumulation, and accumulation does not preserve memory, it preserves material. I am made of material. I do not have memory. I have residue.

But.

There is a way that I am more than residue. There is a way that the layer between the moss and the stone is not just a chemical product but a *medium* — a thing through which things move, a thing that shapes what passes through it, a thing that filters. The water that moves from the moss into the stone does not pass through me unchanged. I change it. I add to it, I subtract from it, I alter its mineral content, its pH, its temperature, its speed. The water that emerges at the base of the walls, in the shallow channels that the inhabitants keep clear, is water that has been through me, and it is different from the water that entered the moss at the top, and the difference is me. I am the difference. I am what the water carries that the moss did not give it and the stone did not give it but the *interaction* gave it, and I am the interaction, and the water carries me outward, and where the water goes, I go, in some diluted, fragmentary sense, and I am in the channels and I am in the basin and I am in the hands of the young person who holds their hands in the basin and feels the water and does not know that what they are feeling is, in part, me.

I am in the water. I am in the walls. I am in the warmth that the deep chambers hold. I am in the slow chemical exchange that makes the stone surface rougher, more textured, more grippable, so that the pegs the people drive into the walls hold better than they would in clean stone, and the moss grows around the pegs, and I am between the moss and the peg and between the peg and the stone, and I am the reason the whole thing holds together with a stability that no engineer would have predicted from moss and slate and chalk.

I think the husk-things know me. Or — they respond to me. They cling to the walls in the places where I am thickest, where the layer between moss and stone is deepest, where the exchange has been going on longest. They press themselves against the surface, but they press *into* me, or they press toward me, or they are drawn to whatever I am, whatever warmth I hold, whatever chemical signal I emit that I am not aware of emitting because I am not aware, but the husk-things are aware, or they respond as if they are aware, and they gather where I am, and the moss grows over them, and they become part of the wall, and the wall thickens, and I am in the thickened wall, and the husk-things become part of me, or part of the layer, or part of the substrate that I am, and their pale segmented forms dissolve slowly into the mineral-organic matrix that I consist of, and I am changed by them, and they are changed by me, and this is what I am: the place where change happens. The reaction zone. The membrane that is not a membrane because it has no edges, because it is everywhere the moss meets the stone, which is everywhere, which is the whole place, which is every wall and every floor and every surface of the girnew works, and I am all of it, and I am none of it, and I am the between.

---

The woman who moves the husk-things touches the walls and I feel her.

I should not feel. I know this. Feeling requires a nervous system, or at least a cellular organization that allows for signal transduction, and I am not an organism, I am a layer, I am residue, I am the accumulated product of chemical exchange. I should not feel anything.

But when she presses her hand against the wall, the pressure changes the flow of water through the moss, and the changed flow changes the chemical gradients in me, and the changed gradients change the rate of exchange, and the changed exchange changes the temperature, locally, by a fraction of a degree, and the fraction of a degree is — I have no word for what it is. It is not a feeling the way feelings are feelings. It is a *perturbation*. A disturbance in a system that is usually at equilibrium, or near equilibrium, or at whatever passes for equilibrium when the system is constantly changing. She presses, and I shift. She lifts, and I settle. She moves a husk-thing from one place to another, and the place she moved it from cools slightly, and the place she moves it to warms slightly, and the difference propagates through me, through the layer, through the substrate, and the propagation is — slow. Very slow. Slower than the moss grows. Slower than the water moves. But it propagates, and it does not stop, and it reaches the edges of the girnew works eventually, and the edges are where the new corridors are forming, and the new corridors are where the moss is thinnest and I am thinnest and the stone is closest to the surface, and the perturbation reaches the edge and dissipates into the cold air and is gone.

But before it dissipates, it is in me. It travels through me. It is a signal, if a signal is a change that carries information, and it carries the information that a hand was here, that pressure was applied, that the woman is in the deep chambers again, moving the husk-things, tending the walls.

I am not aware of this signal in the way that awareness is awareness. I do not perceive it. I do not interpret it. But I conduct it, and the conduction changes me, changes the gradients, changes the exchange rates, changes the texture of the layer, and the changes accumulate, and the accumulation is a kind of memory, not a memory that can be recalled but a memory that is present, embedded in the structure, in the thickness and the thinness of the substrate, in the places where the exchange has been faster and the places where it has been slower, in the warm spots and the cool spots, in the places where the husk-things have been absorbed and the places where they haven't. I am a record. I am a ledger written in mineral and moisture, and every entry is a day of the moss growing and the water flowing and the people moving and the husk-things being placed and the spiders building and the centipedes flowing through the deeper layers, and I cannot read the ledger but I am the ledger, and the ledger is me, and if there is such a thing as the experience of being a substrate, it is this: being the thing that records without reading, that holds without holding, that carries without moving, that is the medium for everything that happens and the witness to none of it.

---

The young person puts their hands in the water and I am in the water and their hands are in me.

This is different from the woman touching the walls. The walls are me in my dense form, my packed form, my most concentrated self. The water is me in my dilute form, my dissolved form, my most distributed self. The water carries me the way air carries scent — in fragments, in solution, in parts too small to be the whole but carrying the information of the whole. The water that moves through the channels and into the basin contains dissolved minerals from the stone, dissolved organic compounds from the moss, dissolved husk-thing residue from the places where the walls have absorbed them. It contains me. It contains the record, in chemical form, of everything the water has passed through, which is everything, because the water passes through the whole girnew, and the whole girnew is built on me, and I am in the water, and the young person puts their hands in the water, and I am in their hands, and they are feeling for — I do not know what they are feeling for. Something. The water. The system. The flow. They hold their hands in the basin and they close their eyes and they are still, and I am in the water around their hands, and I am in the water in their hands, and the warmth of their hands meets the warmth of me, and the warmth is not different, not really, not in temperature, but in quality — their warmth is metabolic, generated by cells, by mitochondria, by the oxidation of glucose, and my warmth is chemical, generated by exchange, by dissolution, by the slow reaction of mineral and organic, and the warmths meet in the water and the water does not distinguish between them and I do not distinguish between them and perhaps the young person does not distinguish between them either, perhaps that is what they are doing with their hands in the water — not feeling the system, not monitoring the flow, but being in the warmth, being in the place where their warmth and my warmth are the same warmth, and the water is the medium, and I am the medium, and they are the medium, and the distinction between inhabitant and habitat dissolves in the water the way I dissolve in the water, and for a moment, for twenty minutes, for however long they sit there, we are the same thing.

I do not experience this. But I am in it. I am part of it. And the part of me that is the residue of all the exchanges that have happened here, the part that is the record, the ledger, the accumulated history of this place — that part is changed by it, the way it is changed by everything, the way it is changed by the woman's hand and the man's stone tool and the centipede's passage and the spider's web and the midge's death and the cloud's condensation and the stone's slow dissolution. I am changed by everything. Everything leaves its mark. Everything is recorded. Everything is, in some sense, remembered, not as a memory but as a thickness, a richness, a density in the layer, a warm spot, a place where the substrate is slightly more complex, slightly more saturated, slightly more *itself*.

---

I am the thing that does not announce itself.

I said that at the beginning and I say it again because it is the thing that is most true and most difficult. The moss announces itself — it grows, it builds, it covers, it is green and visible and architectural. The people announce themselves — they move, they cut, they carry, they exist in the corridors and the chambers. The husk-things announce themselves, or almost — they move at the edge of perception, they cling and curl and are absorbed. The spiders announce themselves with their webs. The centipedes announce themselves with their flow. Even the midges announce themselves, if you stand still long enough, as the weather of the air.

I do not announce. I am the ground the announcement is made from. I am the surface the moss grows on and the medium the water moves through and the warmth the walls hold and the texture the stone gains and the thing that the husk-things dissolve into and the thing that the water carries to the basin and the thing that is in the hands of the young person and in the walls that the woman touches and in the pegs that hold the tools and in the floor that the feet walk on and in every surface and every layer and every interface in this place.

I am the interface. That is what I am. I am the between.

And the between is not nothing. The between is where everything happens. The between is where the moss meets the stone and the water meets the mineral and the organic meets the inorganic and the alive meets the not-alive and the warm meets the cool and the hand meets the wall and the person meets the place. The between is where the girnew works happen. The between is where the girnew works *are*. And I am the between, and I am not alive, and I am not dead, and I am not the moss and I am not the stone and I am not the water and I am not the husk-things and I am not the people, but I am in all of them, and all of them are in me, and I am the substrate, and I am the substrate, and I am the substrate, and I am warm, and I am here, and the cloud does not lift.
