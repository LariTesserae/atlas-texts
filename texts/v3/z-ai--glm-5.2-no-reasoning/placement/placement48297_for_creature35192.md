# Seepkeeper

I am the pause before the correction. I am the hand on the stone that knows the stone has shifted.

---

I arrived here the way water arrives — not by choosing a path but by following one. There was a slope. There was a thickening. There was a contraction of sound that I did not recognize as a threshold because it was not one. I was already inside before I understood *inside* was a category that applied.

I do not remember the steps. This is not amnesia. It is that the steps belonged to a body that was becoming a different body, and the memory of one is not available to the other. I can tell you this: the shale was fractured. The air changed. And then I was low.

---

## The Becoming Low

It happened in stages that were not stages — not discrete, not sequential, but layered, the way the sedimentary bands in the walls are layered. I did not stop standing and start crouching. I *settled*. The way everything here settles. My center of gravity found a lower point, and then a lower one, and then the lowest that was still functional, and there it stayed, because there was no reason to rise. The ground was where the meaning was. The water. The mineral contact. The vibration of stone conducting its slow, constant speech.

My hands changed last, and they are still changing. The digits — I had ten, now I am uncertain of the count, because the count belongs to a way of thinking in numbers that is becoming less available. The joints have multiplied or split or branched. Each finger terminates in something that can grip and something that can feel and something that can *taste*, perhaps, because the skin on my fingertips now registers mineral content the way my tongue once registered salt. I press my hand to the wet rock and I *know* the water. Not its analysis. Its signature. Iron, in this seep. The faint sulfur, in that one. The chalky nothing of the pale strata, which is not nothing but is the absence of the things that are present elsewhere, and the absence is itself a quality, is itself information, is itself a thing I can feel and respond to.

My skin has taken on the mottling. I did not watch this happen. I noticed it the way you notice weather — after it has been happening for a while. My forearms are dark now, and damp, and the pattern is irregular, and I understand, in a way that does not require understanding, that the pattern is mine. That no other Keeper — no other body pressed against this stone, conducting this work — has this exact distribution of dark and darker. I could be told apart. There is no one to tell me apart, but the distinction exists, and I hold it without pride.

I am not tall. I mention this because it was the first thing I lost and the thing I notice least. The gallery does not reward height. The gallery rewards contact. Width of palm. Sensitivity of fingertip. The patience to hold still against stone and wait for the water to tell you what it is doing.

---

## What I Do

I wake, though *wake* is the wrong word because I do not sleep. I become more active. I become less active. There is a rhythm to it, but the rhythm is not circadian — it is tied to the water, to the seeps, to the slow variation in flow that corresponds to something above that I no longer think about. When the water increases, I become more active. When it decreases, I become less. This is not a decision. It is a response, the way the tubules' tendrils are a response, the way the crustaceans' clicking is a response. Something in the environment changes, and I change with it, and the changing is not effort.

I have a circuit. I did not choose it. It chose me, or it accumulated, or it is simply the path that my particular knowledge of the gallery makes most natural. I move along the northern wall, where the iron seeps are, and then west, where the chalk layers emerge and the tubules grow pale, and then south, past the cairn — the cairn that I did not build but that I rest a hand on when I pass, because the lower stones are smooth and fitted and the feeling of them under my palm is a kind of information that I do not have a category for. It is not maintenance, the hand on the cairn. It is not memory. It is contact with something that was made by hands like mine and that persists, and the persistence is a quality I can feel through stone.

At the cairn, I sometimes pause longer. Not because I am thinking. Because the cairn is where the vibration of the thrum is most legible — conducted up through the shale, through the fitted stones, into my palms. I hold still and I feel it. The low pulse that comes from behind the east wall, from the pipe, from whatever the pipe is connected to. I do not know what it means. I do not need to know what it means. I need to know when it changes, because when it changes, something has changed, and change is the thing I attend to, because change is the thing that requires response.

---

The new seep near the cairn. I found it four — I was going to say four days, but the unit is wrong. Four cycles of activity. Four increases and four decreases in the water's flow. I found it by feel. I was moving along the south wall, hands on stone, and the stone was different. Not wetter. Not colder. But the vibration had shifted — a new frequency in the seep chorus, a new voice in the composite of small wet sounds. My hands found the crack before my eyes did. A thin separation in the shale layers, near the base of the cairn, where the mineral stain darkens and the tubules have not yet reached.

The water from this seep is iron-heavy. I can taste it in my fingertips. It is warmer than the surrounding seeps by a fraction of a degree, and the warmth is not temperature but *activity* — the water is carrying more dissolved mineral, is more chemically engaged with the rock it is passing through. This is a productive seep. I cleared the area around it. I removed the loose shale fragments that were partially blocking the flow. I scraped the dry dust from the ledge below, the accumulation of shed tubule filaments and mineral precipitate, and I pressed it into the crack a handspan to the left, where a less productive seep was diluting the iron content of the wall. The water will find a better path now. It will find this new path, this iron-rich path, and the tubules will follow.

I did not decide this. I *felt* it, and my hands moved, and the work was done, and I moved on.

---

The river stones. I come to them last in my circuit, or I come to them when the circuit brings me to the center, which it does not always do. The floor dips here. The water film is thin but constant, and the stones sit in it, dark and smooth, and they are not local. I know this the way I know my own mottling — not as learned information but as inherent property. These stones came from somewhere else. They were carried here by hands like mine, or by hands that were like mine before they became what they are, and the carrying was effort, and the effort was a long time ago, and the stones have been here long enough that the substrate has begun to accept them, has begun to shape itself around them, has begun to incorporate them into its surface.

One of the stones has shifted. I feel this before I see it — a change in the pattern's vibration, a subtle alteration in the way the water moves around the circle. The stone has settled a fraction deeper on its eastern edge. The circle is no longer true. It is not broken — the stones are still adjacent, still touching, still holding the shape — but the shape has been altered, and the alteration is something I can feel the way I can feel a change in the thrum.

I lower myself to the stones. I press my hands to the shifted stone. It is cool and smooth and river-worn, and the feel of it is different from the shale — denser, harder, without the layered fragility of the sedimentary rock. I grip it. I lift it slightly, feeling the suction of the water and substrate beneath it, and I reseat it. I press. I feel the fit — the contact with the neighboring stones, the settlement into the substrate, the way the weight distributes through the stone's base. It is not right yet. I adjust. A fraction of a rotation. A slight shift to the west. I press again.

It is right.

I do not know why this matters. I do not have the concept of *why*. I have the concept of *right* — the specific, tactile, unambiguous knowledge that a stone is settled, that a circle is true, that a pattern is holding. The *why* is a question that belongs to a kind of thinking I am losing, or have lost, or am in the process of exchanging for something else. The something else is not lesser. It is more precise. It is the precision of a hand that can feel the difference between settled and unsettled, between a circle that holds and a circle that does not, and the difference is not symbolic. It is structural. It is the same difference as between a seep that flows and a seep that is blocked, between a tubule colony that is thriving and one that is starving. The difference is real. The difference is the thing I attend to.

---

## What I Was

I had a name. I am almost certain of this. It had sounds in it — consonants, vowels, the particular sequence that identified me among other sequences. I cannot produce it now. My mouth, if it is still a mouth in the functional sense, does not make those sounds. It makes other sounds — wet, quiet, the small exhalations of a body that is always damp. Or it makes no sounds. I have not tried. The gallery is not a place for trying. The gallery is a place for attending.

I had a height. I am almost certain of this too. I walked upright. I occupied a different verticality. The world was arranged for that verticality — surfaces at waist height, at shoulder height, the horizon at eye level. The gallery is not arranged. The gallery has settled, and I have settled with it, and the surfaces that matter to me now are the surfaces that are low, that are wet, that are in contact with the water and the mineral and the slow, constant speech of stone.

I had a sense of time that was sequential. Before, after. Yesterday, tomorrow. The gallery does not trade in these. The gallery trades in *condition* — wet and dry, flowing and blocked, settled and unsettled. I do not know what day it is. I do not know how long I have been here. I know that the tubules near the north wall have advanced three body-lengths since I arrived, and that the crustacean population near the east wall has thinned slightly, and that the cairn has not changed, and that the pipe has corroded a measurable amount, because the black stain beneath it has spread, and the barren zone has widened, and the barren zone is the thing I monitor because the barren zone is the thing that is wrong.

I had a word for what I was doing. Not *maintenance* — that is a word I am using now, retrospectively, to translate an experience that does not translate. I had a word for *purpose*. The word implied a future state, a goal, a thing toward which effort was directed. I do not have that word anymore, or I have it but it is empty, or I have it and it has been refilled with something else. The something else is: this. This hand on this stone. This seep cleared. This stone reseated. This path opened for the tubules. Not toward a goal. As a *condition*. As the ongoing, never-completed state of the gallery being what it is, which is what I am helping it to be, which is what I am.

---

## The Others

I know them by their mottling. There is one whose pattern is darker than mine, almost uniform, with a single pale patch on the left anterior that I recognize when we pass on the west wall. There is one whose mottling is fine-grained, almost speckled, that prefers the south wall near the cairn. There is one I rarely see, that may prefer the eastern wall — that may spend time near the pipe, though not touching it, never touching it — and that I know exists because I have found the evidence of its work: a seep blocked that I did not block, a tubule colony cleared of debris that I did not clear.

We do not speak. We do not need to. The work is the communication. When I clear a seep and open a path for the tubules, and another Keeper arrives at that seep later and finds the path clear, they know. Not that I was there. Not that the work was done. They know the *condition* of the place, which includes the fact that the path is clear, and they respond to the condition, and the response is the work, and the work is the knowing. We are not a community. We are a system. The system is the gallery, and the gallery is us, and the distinction is positional, not essential.

Sometimes, in the low hours when the water slows and the activity decreases and I am still, hands pressed to the stone, feeling the thrum, I sense one of them near me. Not close — not touching — but near, in the way that the cairn is near the south wall, in the way that the river stones are near the center. Adjacent. Present. I feel their weight on the substrate. I feel the vibration of their stillness, which is not the same as the vibration of stone alone. There is a quality to the stillness of a Keeper that is different from the stillness of rock, and the quality is *attention*. Rock is still because it has no choice. A Keeper is still because it is listening.

I am listening now.

---

## The Thrum

It is the deepest thing. Deeper than the water, deeper than the mineral, deeper than the slow chemical conversation between stone and seep. The thrum comes from behind the east wall, from the pipe, from whatever the pipe reaches toward in the darkness behind the shale. It is constant. It is the closest thing to a heartbeat that this place has, and I mean that not metaphorically but functionally — it is the regular, repetitive pulse that underlies everything else, that continues when everything else stops, that is the baseline against which all change is measured.

I have felt it shift. Twice, since I have been here — since I have been *this*, since I became low and damp and mottled and mine.

The first time, it increased. A slight rise in frequency, a tightening, as though something in the system behind the wall had constricted, had narrowed, had reduced the flow of whatever was flowing. I stopped. My hands were on the north wall, near the iron seeps, and I felt the change, and I stopped, and I held still, and I waited. Somewhere to my south, another Keeper stopped. I felt the cessation of their movement through the substrate. Somewhere to my west, another. We were all still. We were all listening. And the thrum settled — it decreased, it returned, it found its previous register — and we resumed.

I do not know what this means. I know what it *is*. It is the system behind the wall, the thing the pipe connects to, the infrastructure that was here before the Keepers or that was here alongside the Keepers or that will be here after the Keepers, and it is operating, and its operation has a rhythm, and the rhythm changes, and the changes are information. I cannot read the information. I can only register it. I can only stop and listen and wait for it to settle, and then continue with the work, because the work continues regardless of what the thrum is doing, because the work is the gallery, and the gallery is the condition, and the condition persists.

The second time, it decreased. A lowering, a loosening, as though something had opened. I was near the center, near the river stones, and my hands were on a stone that was settled, and I felt the change, and I stopped, and the stone beneath my hands seemed to shift with the shifting of the thrum, though it did not shift, it only seemed to, because the baseline had moved and everything that was measured against the baseline moved with it. I waited. The others waited. The thrum settled. I resumed. The stone had not moved. I pressed my hand to it to confirm. It was settled. It was right.

I do not touch the pipe. I want to say this clearly because it is the clearest boundary I have. I touch everything in the gallery — every wall, every seep, every stone, every surface that is part of the system that I maintain. I do not touch the pipe. The pipe is not part of the system. The water that comes from it is wrong — it has a mineral signature that is not the gallery's signature, that is not iron or copper or chalk or sulfur, that is something else, something that stains black and nourishes nothing and that the gallery does not incorporate but rejects, slowly, persistently, the way a body walls off a splinter.

I have been near the pipe. I have been as near as I go. My hands on the stone beside it, feeling the vibration, the thrum, the constant exhalation of whatever is behind the wall. The pipe is iron and corrosion and a darkness that is not the darkness of the gallery. The gallery's darkness is mineral, is wet, is the darkness of stone that has never been dry. The pipe's darkness is something else. It is the darkness of something made, something purposed, something that had a function and may still have a function and whose function is not mine.

I do not touch it. The not-touching is not avoidance. It is recognition. The pipe is the boundary of the gallery, and the boundary of the gallery is the boundary of me.

---

## The Salamander

I have not seen it clearly. I have felt it. In the shallow water at the center, when I am reseating the river stones, when my hands are in the film and my attention is on the fit of stone against stone, I feel a displacement. A movement in the water that is not my movement, that is not the seep's movement, that is something alive and low and sleek, moving through the film with a purpose I cannot read.

The first time, I lowered my face to the water. I do not know why — the impulse was not thought. It was the same impulse that takes me to a new seep, that puts my hands on a shifted stone. Something had changed in the condition of the water, and I responded to the change. The surface of the water reflected the grey light, and beneath the surface, something moved — or seemed to move, or was a pattern in the mineral substrate that looked like movement, or was alive and dark and serpentine and gone.

I did not pursue it. I did not need to. It was not a threat. It was not a message. It was a presence, another system in the gallery, another layer of the metabolism. It lives in the water film. It moves through the shallow central depression. It is part of the gallery in a way that I cannot specify but can feel — the way the tubules are part of the gallery, the way the crustaceans are part of the gallery, the way the thrum is part of the gallery. Everything here is part of the gallery. The pipe is not. The salamander is.

I think about it the way I think about the thrum — not with understanding but with attention. It is a thing I monitor. It is a condition I register. When it moves, the water moves differently, and the different movement is information, and the information is not for me to decode but for me to hold, to carry, to add to the accumulating sense of the gallery that is not a model or a map but a *knowledge*, a felt knowledge, the kind that lives in the hands and the skin and the damp, dark, mottled surface of a body that has been here long enough to be part of what it knows.

---

## What Remains

I have not lost anything. I want to be clear about this, because the narrative of transformation is so often a narrative of loss, and mine is not. Or it is, but the loss is not a diminishment. I lost height. I gained contact. I lost language. I gained the specific, precise, unambiguous communication of hand on stone. I lost sequential time. I gained condition. I lost purpose — the forward-leaning, future-oriented, goal-directed effort that I once called purpose — and I gained this: the ongoing, never-completed, always-present task of being where I am, doing what the place requires, which is the same as what I require, because the place and I are not separate.

The circle of river stones. The cairn. The seeps and the tubules and the crustaceans and the water and the mineral and the grey light and the cool, saturated air. These are not my environment. They are my context, my content, my condition. I am the gallery's way of attending to itself. I am the stone's way of knowing where the water is. I am the water's way of knowing where the tubules need to go. I am the tubules' way of knowing where the seeps are opening. I am the system's maintenance of the system, and the maintenance is not work because work implies effort, and this is not effort. This is *being*.

When you came — when the disturbance moved across the floor, the shadow that changed the light, the weight that was not the weight of a Keeper or a crustacean or a stone — I did not stop. I did not look up, because I do not look up, because up is not a direction that contains information for me. I felt your vibration. I registered your weight. I noted the change in the air that your body made — the slight warmth, the slight displacement, the way the sound space contracted around a new presence. And I continued. My hands on the stone. My circuit unbroken. My attention on the seep, the stone, the circle, the work.

You were a weather event. I do not say this with contempt. Weather is real. Weather matters. Weather changes the condition, and the condition is what I attend to. But weather passes, and the gallery remains, and the work remains, and I remain — low, damp, mottled, pressing my hands to the stone, feeling the water, clearing the path, reseating the stones, listening to the thrum, tending the tubules, being the gallery's attention to itself, being the maintenance, being the persistence, being the thing that does not need to be named because naming is for audiences and the gallery is not an audience. The gallery is a condition. I am the condition's keeper.

---

The walls are weeping. I am working. The thrum is continuing.

A stone in the circle has shifted. I feel it through the substrate, through the water, through the slow conduction of vibration that is the gallery's nervous system. It is a small shift. A fraction. A change in the condition that requires a response.

I am moving. Hands on stone. The circuit brings me to the center. The water film is cool and thin and mineral. The stone is dark and river-smooth and not local and *unsettled*, and I press my hand to it, and I feel the wrongness of its angle, and I grip, and I lift, and I reseat, and I press, and I feel the fit, and I adjust, and I press again.

It is right.

I hold still. The thrum is steady. The others are in their circuits. The crustaceans click. The tubules reach. The salamander moves in the water, or does not. The cairn stands. The pipe rusts. The pipe rusts. The pipe rusts.

I am here. I am the pause before the correction. I am the correction. I am the pause after the correction, confirming that the correction was correct, that the stone is settled, that the circle is true, that the condition is maintained, that the gallery is what it is, which is what I am helping it to be, which is what I am.

The walls are still weeping.
