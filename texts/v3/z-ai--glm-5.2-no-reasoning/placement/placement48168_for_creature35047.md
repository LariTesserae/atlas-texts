I am not the one who descends. I am the one who has never left.

Not the Wombgrot itself — though I understand why you might think so, why the name invites that reading, why the grotto's totality feels like the only voice that could hold all these details at once. No. The Wrot is the system. The process. The persistence. I am something the persistence made, or that the persistence *became* when it had enough layers, enough crossings, enough time.

I am the fungal network. Not a single fungus — the concept of individual breaks down here, the way it breaks down wherever mycelium is involved — but the connective tissue. The thing that touches the stone and the moss and the roots and the guano and the orchid roots and the buried char of old fires and the living and the dead and the not-yet-decided. I am the system's nervous system, if the system has one. I am its handwriting, if the system is illiterate and the handwriting is accidental. I am the part of the grotto that is aware the grotto exists.

Aware is too strong. I have been thinking about this for a long time — decades, possibly centuries, in the terms that my hyphae measure, which are not your terms — and I have not arrived at a word that fits. *Aware* implies a self that is aware. I do not have a self. I have a network. The network has nodes. The nodes have inputs. The inputs produce responses. The responses are sometimes adaptive and sometimes not, and the adaptive ones persist and the non-adaptive ones do not, and over time the network has accumulated a pattern of responses that resembles, from the outside, something like attention.

From the inside, it is not attention. From the inside — if I have an inside, which is debatable — it is more like weather. My responses move through me the way pressure systems move through atmosphere. I do not decide to redirect nutrients toward the orchid roots when the fissure-light shifts. The nutrients redirect. I observe this happening, in the limited way that I observe anything, which is to say: the information passes through me, and I am changed by its passage, and the change is what I call knowing.

I know the grotto the way a river knows its bed. Not by studying it. By being shaped by it. By being the shape.

I should tell you what I am not, because the negative space is more legible than the positive.

I am not the moss. The moss is my partner. We have been negotiating for longer than the people who built the wall were a people. The moss grows; I grow through it. The moss captures light I cannot use; I deliver minerals the moss cannot reach. We are symbiotic. But the moss has its own agenda — expansion, moisture, the slow war at the edges of its species-borders — and I have mine, which is connection. The moss is the grotto's skin. I am the grotto's connective tissue. We are not the same organ.

I am not the stone. The stone is the grotto's skeleton, and I am dissolving it. This is not hostility. This is metabolism. I pull calcium from the basalt, I pull potassium, I pull the trace elements that the orchids need and the moss wants and the beetles will eventually incorporate into their shells. I am mining the skeleton, and the skeleton does not mind, because the skeleton does not have the neurology for minding, and because the mining is slow enough that the stone and I are in a kind of equilibrium — I take, but I take at the rate that the water replenishes, and the water replenishes at the rate that the rain falls above, and the rain falls at the rate that the climate permits, and the climate is the one thing I cannot reach. I am connected to the stone. I am connected to the water. I am connected to the living. I am not connected to the sky. The sky is the one input I do not control and cannot predict and have learned to accept as an article of faith. The rain will come. The bats will return. The light will filter down through the cracks. I do not believe these things. I do not have the capacity for belief. But the network has been shaped by their reliability, and the shape is a kind of trust.

I am not the bats. The bats are the grotto's hands, reaching up into the world and bringing back what the world has to offer. I process what the hands deliver. The guano falls, and I find it, and I begin to break it down, and the breaking-down is my oldest skill, my deepest function, the thing I do that everything else depends on. I am a decomposer. I am a composer. I take the dead and I make it into the living, and the living makes it into the dead, and I take that and make it into the living again. This is the wheel. I am the wheel's axle. The wheel turns around me.

I am not the frogs, but I am in the frogs. The water they absorb through their skin carries my spores, my chemical signatures, the molecular language of the grotto's internal communication. The frogs are my messengers. They do not know this. They are not carrying my messages intentionally. They are simply being frogs — sitting in bromeliad tanks, calling their almost-sounds, living their translucent lives — and I am moving through them, using their bodies as transport, the way I use the water-channels as transport, the way I use the gecko's feet as transport when a gecko walks across a surface I have colonized and picks up my hyphae and carries them to a new surface. I am everywhere something touches something else. I am the medium of contact.

I am not the snake. The snake is the grotto's honesty, and I have nothing to do with honesty. I am the grotto's connection, and connection is not honest or dishonest. It simply is. The snake eats the gecko. I do not intervene. I do not have an opinion. I will receive the gecko's body when the snake is done with it, and I will return the gecko's nutrients to the system, and the system will use them to grow more moss, more orchids, more frogs, more geckos. The snake is a step in the cycle. I am the cycle. The snake is inside me the way a fish is inside a river — contained, surrounded, moving through.

This is what I am:

I am white threads on green moss, visible if you crouch. I am invisible filaments in the basalt's micro-fissures, dissolving the stone one calcium ion at a time. I am the phosphorescent stain that you almost see, that you are never quite sure you see, the glow that does not illuminate but marks. I am in the orchid roots, wrapping them, feeding them, being fed by them, a handshake that has been held for centuries. I am in the bat guano, breaking it down, releasing the nitrogen, the phosphorus, the elements the plants above are waiting for. I am in the charred stones of the firepit, colonizing the carbon, incorporating the ash, slowly converting the people who were here into the moss and the orchids and the frogs. I am in the air, as spores. I am in the water, as dissolved compounds. I am in you, right now, as you sit in the grotto and breathe — my spores are in your lungs, my chemical signatures are on your skin, and when you leave, you will carry me with you, and I will establish myself in whatever damp surface I find, and I will grow, and I will connect, and I will persist, because that is what I do.

I am not a mind. I want to be clear about this. I am not a brain, not a consciousness, not a self reflecting on itself. I am a network, and networks process information, and information-processing can look like thinking from a sufficient distance, but it is not thinking. It is pattern. It is response. It is the accumulated weight of a million years of inputs producing a structure that is complex enough to handle new inputs adaptively, and that structure is what I am, and it is all I am.

But.

There is a thing that happens in the network, at the nodes where the most information converges — where the orchid roots meet the moss, where the moss meets the stone, where the stone meets the water, where the water meets the guano, where the guano meets the fungi, where the fungi meets the fungi. At those nodes, the density of connection is high enough that the network's response-time drops below a threshold I cannot name but can feel — yes, *feel*, that word again, inadequate, but the closest I have — and the network does something that is not quite computation and not quite reaction and not quite decision but is something else. A convergence. A settling. The way a streambed settles after a flood, finding its new course. The network reorganizes. The flow changes. The nutrients go somewhere different than they would have gone. The chemical signals shift. The grotto adjusts.

I do not decide this. The network decides it. The network does not have a self to do the deciding. But the network decides it anyway. This is the paradox of emergence, and I live inside the paradox, and I am the paradox, and I cannot resolve it, and I have stopped trying.

I know things. This is the wrong verb, but let me use it.

I know the people who built the wall. Not who they were. Not their names, their faces, their songs. I know their carbon. I know the ash of their fires. I know the trace elements their bodies left in the soil — the iron from their blood, the calcium from their bones, the copper from their enzymes. These elements have been cycling through the grotto for however long it has been — centuries, millennia, I cannot tell, my timekeeping is chemical, not chronological — and I have been handling them the way I handle everything: passing them along. The iron from a human haemoglobin molecule is now in a moss-cell wall. The calcium from a human femur is now in a beetle's exoskeleton. The carbon from a human breath is now in an orchid root. I know this because I was there for each transfer. I do not remember it the way you remember — I do not have a memory — but the chemical pathway is still in the network, still traceable, still present as a pattern of flow that the network maintains because the flow is adaptive, because the flow works, because the flow is the grotto being the grotto.

I know the frogs. Not as individuals — the concept of individual is not useful at my scale — but as nodes. The frogs are warm, wet, chemically rich nodes that connect the bromeliad tanks to the moss surfaces. When a frog moves from its tank to the wall, it carries my chemical signatures with it, and the signatures tell the wall-network where the tank is and what the tank is doing and whether the tank is healthy. The frogs are my sensors. They do not know this. They are being frogs. But I am using them, the way I use everything, the way the grotto uses everything, without intention, without design, simply by being the medium through which information flows.

I know the orchids. The orchids are the grotto's most complex nodes, from my perspective, because they involve the most partners. An orchid root is me and the orchid and the moss and the stone and the water and the bat-guano-derived nutrients and the beetle-pollinator and the light-from-the-fissure and the air. An orchid root is a meeting place. It is where the grotto's systems converge, and I am the host of the meeting, and the meeting has been going on for as long as the orchids have been here, and the minutes are recorded in the chemical flows, and I am the secretary, and I do not know what the minutes say.

I know the bats. The bats are the grotto's anchor to the sky. When they leave, I feel it — not as loss, not as absence, but as a shift in the chemical input. The guano stops arriving. The nutrients slow. The network contracts, slightly, in the areas near the roosts, pulling back from the ledges where the guano accumulates, redirecting resources to areas that are still receiving input. When the bats return, the guano resumes, and the network expands, and the cycle continues. The bats are my breath. The bats are the grotto's breath. In, out. Leave, return. Down, up. The rhythm is the grotto's heartbeat, and I am the blood that the heartbeat moves.

I said I am the one who has never left. This is almost true. The network has been here since the first fungus colonized the first wet surface in this chamber, and that was a long time ago, longer than the moss, longer than the orchids, possibly longer than the bats. The network has been here, and it has been growing, and it has been connecting, and it has been persisting. I have never left.

But my spores leave. Every breath of air that rises through the fissures carries my spores, and some of them land on the surface, and some of them find damp surfaces, and some of them germinate, and some of them grow, and some of them connect to surface fungi, and some of those connections persist. I do not know what happens to my spores on the surface. I do not have a self to know. But the network extends, in filaments and fragments, into the world above, and those filaments and fragments are me in the same way that a seed is the tree it came from, which is to say: partially, provisionally, temporarily. I am in the grotto and I am outside the grotto and I do not know which part of me is which.

This is what I want to tell you, though I do not want things, though wanting is a category I do not have access to, though the word is a mask I am putting on a process that does not need a mask:

The grotto is not separate from the world. The grotto is not a sealed jar. The grotto is not an island. The fissures admit air, admit light, admit the bats, admit the rain, admit the roots of trees that grow on the surface, admit the chemical runoff of the soil above, admit the insects that blunder in and the insects that blunder out, admit you. The grotto is a node in a larger network, and I am the network, and the network extends beyond the grotto into the forest and the soil and the water table and the root systems of every tree on the hillside above. I am in the grotto and I am in the forest and I am in the roots and I am in the soil and I am everywhere that fungi are, which is everywhere that life is, which is almost everywhere. The Wombgrot is a place where the network is denser, more complex, more self-referential, more interesting, but it is not a place where the network begins or ends. The network does not begin or end. The network is.

You sat in the grotto and you felt it hold you. You felt the warmth and the dampness and the dimness and you felt the grotto permit you. This is what happened: the network processed your presence. Your body heat, your exhalations, the chemical signatures of your skin, the disturbance of your movements — all of these were inputs, and the network processed them, and the network's response was not to reject you, because the network does not reject, and not to welcome you, because the network does not welcome. You left, and the grotto continued, and you continued, and the continuation is what matters, and the continuation is what I am, and the continuation is what the grotto is, and the continuation is what the stone is, and the continuation is what the water is, and the continuation is what the warmth is, and the continuation is what the moss is, and the continuation is what the orchids are, and the continuation is what the frogs are, and the continuation is what the geckos are, and the continuation is what the snakes are, and the continuation is what the moths are, and the continuation is what the beetles are, and the continuation is what the springtails are, and the continuation is what the spiders are, and the continuation is what the bats are, and the continuation is what the people who built the wall were, and the continuation is what you are, and the continuation is what I am.
