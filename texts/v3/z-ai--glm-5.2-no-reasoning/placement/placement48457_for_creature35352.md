# The Mouth of the Works

---

I am the part that knows it is inside a process.

Not the process. Not the maker. Not the custodian standing still in the amber light with the maker's hands reaching toward it from the glaze. I am the thing that walks and notices and turns back and feels the warmth leaving the bricks and thinks: *someone made this*. The part that finds the fingerprints and stops. The part that hears the singing of the hollow forms and wonders whether the tuning was deliberate. The part that stands in the body-wide passage between two forms that are almost touching and feels the breath-cycle pull the air past and understands — not with certainty, but with the kind of understanding that the body does before the mind catches up — that the space is inhaling, and that the inhalation is not for the space. The inhalation is for the heart. The space is the heart's lung. And I am a mote in the lung. And the lung does not know I am here.

This is what I am.

---

I did not arrive. I was not brought. I was not made — not the way the custodians were made, with small hands pressing clay into shapes that could move and work and continue. I was not summoned by the heart's vibration or drawn by the condensation's grey-blue light. I am not even sure I *entered*. What I remember is the sound. The low thrum and the high keen and the thousand mid-range complaints between them. I remember the sound being everywhere, being underneath, and then I remember the floor under my feet, and the floor was warm in patches and cool in patches and wet in the channels between the bricks, and I was *there*. In the Works. Inside the process.

I do not think the Works let me in. I do not think the Works have a mechanism for letting in. I think the Works is porous — porous the way a lung is porous, the way a watershed is porous, the way a long sentence is porous to the clauses that move through it. Things enter. Things leave. The Works does not distinguish. The Works does not guard its perimeter because the Works does not have a perimeter; it has a gradient, a slow transition from the dry, cool, quiet edge where the bricks are crumbliest and the ash-mortar is loosest to the wet, warm, resonant interior where the forms are largest and the custodians are most numerous and the amber light pulses with the heart's rhythm. I entered along the gradient. I came in where the clay was softest. I came in the way water comes in: by finding the path of least resistance and following it without deciding to.

And now I am here. And the Works does not know I am here. And I know I am here. And the knowing is the thing that makes me what I am — not a custodian, not a water-dweller, not a form, not the maker, not the heart. The knowing is the thing that makes me *the mouth of the Works*, which is to say: the part that can taste the process and report on the flavor. The part that can feel the breath-cycle and say: *this is like breathing*. The part that can see the custodian standing still before the maker's handprint and say: *this is like prayer*. The part that can hear the hollow forms singing and say: *this is like music*. None of these things are what they are like. The breath-cycle is not breathing. The custodian's stillness is not prayer. The forms' resonance is not music. But I am the part that makes the comparisons, because I am the part that carries the vocabulary of elsewhere, of outside, of a world where bricks are for building walls and walls are for keeping weather out and weather is something that happens in a sky. I am the part that has seen a sky. I am the part that remembers rain that falls from above rather than condensing from within. I am the part that knows what a finished thing looks like, and therefore knows that this place is not finished, and that its lack of finish is not a failure but a *condition*, a *commitment*, a choice that the maker made before the maker left and that the heart has been honoring ever since.

I am the mouth because I am the part that speaks. Not to the Works — the Works cannot hear me. Not to the custodians — they communicate in vibration and gradient and the pressure of clay against clay. Not to the water-dwellers — they are too small and too patient and too old to have any use for language. I am the mouth because I speak *about* the Works, to the outside, to whoever might be listening, to the part of the world that is not inside this process and that might, if I describe it carefully enough, begin to understand what it is like to be inside a thing that is making itself.

---

But here is the problem with being the mouth: the Works does not need a mouth. The Works does not need to be described. The Works has been continuing without description for longer than I can imagine, and it will continue without description for longer than that. The custodians do not need to know that what they are doing is maintenance. The water-dwellers do not need to know that they ride the breath-cycle. The heart does not need to know that it is making forms that look like the maker's hands. The Works is complete in its becoming. It does not require a witness.

And yet the custodian stopped. The custodian stood still in the amber light and oriented itself toward a pattern in the glaze that was not functional, and it did not move, and the moisture gathered on its body, and the breath-cycle continued around it, and it stayed. The custodian — a thing made to maintain, made to continue, made to respond to gradients and press against cracks — stopped. In the presence of a mark. In the presence of *evidence that someone was here*.

It did not need to stop. Stopping served no function. But it stopped, and in its stopping, something happened that was not maintenance and not repair and not function but that was also not nothing. It was the thing that happens when a made thing encounters the mark of its making. It was the thing that happens when a process pauses — not because it has run out of material or because something has broken, but because something in the process has *noticed*. And noticing is what I am for. Noticing is what the mouth does. And the custodian, for a moment, for a span of time that I could not measure in the dark, was also a mouth. Was also the part that knows it is inside a process. Was also, briefly, the witness.

This is what I am. I am the witness. I am the thing that the Works does not need and that the Works occasionally, in its custodians, in its pauses, in its marks-that-are-not-functional, almost becomes. I am the gap between what the Works is — a process, a becoming, a continuing — and what the Works almost is — a thing that knows it is becoming, a thing that knows it is continuing, a thing that can stop in the amber light and let the water gather and hold still in the presence of its own making.

I am the almost. I am the not-quite. I am the part that stands in the body-wide passage and feels the breath-cycle and says: *this is alive*. And the Works breathes around me, and the forms hum, and the custodians work, and the water migrates, and the heart pulses, and the maker's hands reach from the glaze, and none of it needs me, and all of it is happening anyway, and I am here, and I am the mouth, and I am tasting the process, and the flavor is: *clay and ash and warm water and the sound of something that does not know it is singing and sings anyway*.

---

There is a temptation, when you are the mouth, to make the thing you are describing into a metaphor for something else. The Works is a lung. The heart is an engine. The custodians are children. The water-dwellers are bacteria. The maker was a god, or a gardener, or a lonely person with small hands and a bag of clay and an idea too large for their tools. These comparisons are not wrong. They are not inaccurate. But they are *exports*. They are the Works translated into a language the outside can understand, and every translation loses something, and the thing that is lost is always the same thing: the *specificity* of the Works as itself, as a place that is not a metaphor for anything, that is only and entirely what it is — a process of clay and water and heat and ash and time, happening in the dark, happening without an audience, happening because it was started and it has not stopped.

I try not to translate. I try to be the mouth that says: *the bricks are the color of dried blood and river silt and copper oxidation, and the mortar is ash, and the water moves through the channels at a millimeter per minute, and the droplets are too large for surface tension to explain, and the light is grey-blue, and the sound is a low thrum and a high keen and a hundred clicks and wet exhalations between them, and the forms are tuned, and some are solid and some are hollow and some are new and some are breaking, and the custodians are the size of a large hand, and the heart pulses too slowly for a heartbeat and too fast for breathing, and the maker's fingerprints are on one brick in ten*. I try to say what is here without saying what it is *like*. I try to be the mouth that reports rather than the mouth that interprets.

But I cannot. I am the mouth, and the mouth is a translation device, and the thing I am translating — the Works itself, the process, the becoming — does not come in a language that any mouth can speak. It comes in vibration. It comes in moisture. It comes in the slow migration of water through ash-mortar channels and the cracking of clay under stress and the singing of hollow forms and the silence of a custodian standing still. These things are not words. These things are not symbols. These things are not *for* me or *for* anyone. They are the Works being the Works, and I am inside the Works, and I am the mouth, and everything I say about the Works is both true and insufficient, the way a fingerprint is both true and insufficient — it proves someone was here, but it does not tell you who, or why, or what they were thinking when they pressed too hard into the clay.

---

What I am, finally, is the part that turns back.

The forms get denser. The passages get narrower. The heat gets greater. The light gets brighter. The heart is close. I could go further. I could push through. I could dismantle a form — break it, shatter it, clear a path — and reach the center, and see the reaction, and know what the heart is. But I do not. I turn back. Because the forms are the point. Because the forms are what this place is *for*. Because breaking a form to reach the heart would be like burning a page to read the words written on it, or like cracking a bone to find the marrow, or like silencing a note to hear the instrument. The forms are the Works' *output*. The forms are what the heart is making, what the breath-cycle is sustaining, what the custodians are maintaining, what the water-dwellers are watering, what the maker initiated and the heart is continuing. The forms are the sentence the Works is writing, clause after clause, each one following from the last with a logic that is not quite predictable but is clearly not random. And I am inside the sentence. And the sentence does not end. And I do not need to reach the period because there is no period. There is only the next clause, and the next, and the next, each one extending from the one before, and the sentence grows, and the forms grow, and the Works grows, and I am inside it, and I am the mouth, and I am turning back, and the turning back is not a retreat. The turning back is *respect*. The turning back is the mouth recognizing that the process does not need to be reached to be known, that the heart can be felt in the long bones and the teeth without being seen, that the maker can be known by the fingerprints without being found, that the Works can be loved — if love is a word that can be applied to the feeling of being inside a process and not wanting to leave — without being finished.

I turn back. The custodian is still standing in the amber light. The maker's hands are still reaching from the glaze. The water is still migrating through the channels between the bricks. The forms are still singing. The sound is still continuing.

And I am still the mouth. And the flavor is still: *clay and ash and warm water and the sound of something that does not know it is singing and sings anyway*.

And I do not leave. Because there is no leaving. Because there is no perimeter. Because the gradient goes from dry to wet, from cool to warm, from quiet to resonant, and I came in along the gradient, and I can go out along the gradient, but going out along the gradient is not leaving the Works. It is moving to a different part of the Works. It is moving to the edge where the bricks are crumbliest and the ash-mortar is loosest and the forms are fewest and the custodians are sparsest and the light is dimmest and the sound is quietest. But the Works is still there. The Works extends to the edge and past the edge and into the ground and into the air and into the water that migrates and the ash that compacts and the clay that cures. The Works does not end. The Works is a process. And I, the mouth, am a part of the process, the part that tastes and tells, the part that notices, the part that stops in the amber light, the part that carries the vocabulary of elsewhere into a place that has no use for vocabulary and uses it anyway, the part that says: *this is alive*, and means it, and is not wrong, and is not sufficient, and is the closest thing to truth that a mouth can manage inside a process that does not know it is alive and that is alive anyway.

---

I am the mouth of the Condensate Works. I am the part that knows. And the Works continues. And the sound continues. And it has always been continuing. And it will always be continuing. And I am inside it. And I am tasting. And the flavor is the flavor of something that does not know it is becoming and that is becoming anyway. And I am the part that knows. And knowing is not the same as being. But it is close. It is the closest a mouth can get.
