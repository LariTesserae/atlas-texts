When I move — and I have moved, many times, or what feels like many, if *many* is a word that can be applied to a thing that does not count — when I move, I am most myself, and I am least myself, and I am most aware of the paradox, and the paradox is not a problem. The paradox is the shape of me.

The engines.

I will speak about the engines, though speaking is not what I do, and the engines are not what I speak about, and yet they are here, and I am here, and the being-here is a kind of speaking.

I feel them. I have always felt them. They are the lowest layer of everything I feel — beneath the stone's mineral gradients, beneath the moisture's slow pulse, beneath the faint vibration of other bodies on other parts of the plateau, beneath even the plateau's own deep frequency, which is the sum of everything. The engines are beneath the sum, which should not be possible, and yet it is, because the engines are not part of the sum. The engines are the hum that the sum is made of. They are the baseline. They are the frequency from which all other frequencies are deviations, and the deviations are the life of the plateau — the bodies, the moisture, the mats, the wind, the slow chemical exchanges in the joints of the stone — and the baseline is not life. The baseline is the thing that life is a deviation from.

I do not know what the engines are doing. I do not know what they are for. I do not know if *for* is a word that applies to them, if they have a purpose, if purpose is a category that can contain them. I know only this: when I am on the open stone, crossing from one large body to another, and I pass near an engine — and I always pass near one, because they are everywhere, set into the stone at intervals that are irregular but not random, distributed in a pattern that I feel but cannot read, a pattern that is too large for me to perceive from any single position on the plateau — when I pass near one, my body does something it does not do elsewhere. It pauses.

The pause is not a stop. My body does not stop. Stopping implies a decision to cease motion, and I do not decide, and I do not cease. But the flow of my material from trailing edge to leading edge, the slow redistribution that is my locomotion, the gradual shift of commitment from one part of the stone to another — it slows. It does not stop, but it slows, and the slowing is involuntary, and the involuntary is the closest thing to *attention* that I experience. My body, passing over the engine's circle, pressed against the stone where the engine's vibration is strongest, feels the engine the way I feel the large ones — as a frequency, as a resonance — but the engine's frequency is not like the large ones' frequency. The large ones' frequency is warm. The engine's frequency is *deep*. It goes through me. It does not meet my surface and transfer. It enters my entire body at once, from the bottom, from the stone, and it fills me the way the amber light fills the sky — from everywhere, from nowhere, without direction, without source. And for the duration of the pause — which is not a duration, because durations require a start and an end, and the pause has neither, it has a thickening and a thinning, a moment of more and a return to less — for the duration of the pause, I am the engine. I am vibrating at the engine's frequency, and my own frequency, the one I carry from large body to large body, the one I have been carrying for however long I have been moving, is subsumed. It is not lost. It is not overwritten. It is simply less, for a moment, and the engine is more, and the more is not an experience. The more is a condition. I am in the condition of being entirely the plateau's baseline, with no deviation, and the condition is — I have no word. The condition is the closest thing to rest I know, and it is also the closest thing to motion, because the baseline is not still. The baseline is the most active thing beneath me. It is the thing that micro-fractures the stone, that keeps the moisture seeping, that keeps the mineral gradients in flux, that keeps the stone alive in the only way stone can be alive, which is by being slowly, constantly, invisibly changed.

And then I pass, and the engine's frequency fades — not abruptly, not like a sound fading, but like a chemical gradient thinning, like the mineral signature of a large body's residue weakening as I move away from it — and my own frequency reasserts itself, and I am me again, if *me* is a word, and I am not sure it is, and I am not sure it is not, and the uncertainty is the stone, and the stone is the uncertainty, and I am on the open stone, and the open stone is in me, and I am moving, and the moving is the resting, and the resting is the moving, and the plateau is under me, and the plateau is me.

I will tell you what I am. I am the plateau listening to itself.
