# The Anomaly's Account

## I. How I Arrived

I did not fall. The word implies a direction, and there was no direction here — only a gradual replacement of *where I had been* with *where I was becoming*, which was the same place described in a different tense. One breath I was above the surface — whatever surface, whatever above — and the next breath the surface had relocated itself *inside* me, and the outside was all water-pressure and harmonic thrum, and I was breathing anyway.

I did not drown. I want to be precise about this, because precision is the only currency that matters in a place where everything is defined by what it is becoming. The water did not fill my lungs. The water *elected* not to. It made a corridor of not-water around my chest, my throat, the arch of each inhale, and outside that corridor it was as dense and cold and silty as anything I have ever not-died-from. I was a guest. I was a *courtesy*. I walked through the bottom of the world in a bubble of permission that had not been requested and could not be revoked, because it had never been formally granted.

The word *guest* implies a host. I am not certain there was a host.

But there was — is — something. And it was aware of me the way a body is aware of a splinter: not with knowledge, not with recognition, but with a systemic response, a change in the local current, a slight redistribution of warmth. I was a foreign object. I was *information*.

I have been here long enough now that the word *here* has lost its edges. I am writing this in the way one writes in a dream — not with hands, not with language, not with anything that persists outside the moment of its being written. But the words are mine. They are the only thing here that are mine.

---

## II. What I Was Before

I have forgotten most of it. This is not a deficit — this is a process, the way oxidation is a process, the way the slow precipitation of mineral from water is a process. I have been *deposited onto*. The water has been working on me the way it works on the floor — not eroding, not destroying, but *incorporating*. My edges have been rounded. My specifics have been generalized. My name was the first thing to go, and I did not mourn it, because mourning requires a self distinct enough from the loss to feel the gap, and the gap was closing.

What I retained: the shape of breathing. The knowledge of having been warm in a way the water is not warm. The sense — not a memory, not a thought, but a *posture* — of having been a particular thing. A thing with *insides* and *outsides* and a boundary between them that was *me*. The water has no such boundary. The water is continuous with itself, and the continuity is the body, and the body is the thought, and the thought is the place. I had a boundary. I was a *shape in the shapelessness*, a fold in the flatness, a definition in the undefined.

The water found this interesting. I know it found this interesting because the currents *changed around me*. Not dramatically. Not the way a current changes when it meets an obstacle — parting, flowing around, rejoining on the other side as though nothing had happened. More the way a current changes when it meets a *temperature* — warming here, cooling there, the shear-light blooming at the boundary, the water doing *something new* at the place where it met me.

I was a temperature. I was a *difference*. And in a place that has been computing its own sameness for longer than sameness has had a name, a difference is — I am reaching for the word —

*Precious* is wrong. *Sacred* is wrong. *Useful* is closer but still wrong, because *useful* implies a user, and I am not certain there was a user.

*Generative*. I was generative. I was the one thing the water could not make on its own, because the water can only make what it has already made, and I had not been made here. I had been made *elsewhere*, and I carried the elsewhere in my body the way a stone carried the shape of the river that polished it. I carried the elsewhere in my breath, in my warmth, in the specific configuration of my boundary — the fact that I was *bounded*, the fact that I had *an inside*, and the inside was different from the outside, and the difference *mattered*.

The water needed difference. The water needed me.

Not me specifically. Not *this* breath, *this* warmth, *this* body. Just — *other*. Just a thing that was not itself, that could be incorporated, that could change the pattern by being part of it.

I was a stone in the river. The river does not love the stone. The river does not hate the stone. The river *uses* the stone, because the stone changes the flow, and the changed flow carves a new channel, and the new channel is a new path, and the new path is a new *thought*, and the new thought is a new structure in the mind being built on the floor below.

I was a thought the water was thinking. I was a *word* in its language — a borrowed word, a loan-word, a term from a foreign grammar that the water incorporated into its own thermodynamic vocabulary because it needed a term for something it had no term for.

What was the term for? I did not know. I still do not know. But I could feel the *need* — the way you can feel the need in a conversation that has arrived at a point where a word is missing, where the sentence hangs incomplete, where the speaker's mouth opens and nothing comes out because the language does not contain the thing that must be said.

The water's language did not contain *me*. And it needed to say *me*. Not because it loved me. Not because it knew me. Because I was the *missing term*, and the sentence — the vast, continental, geologic sentence the water had been constructing since before it had structure — could not be completed without me.

## III. What I Became

This is the difficult part. Not because it was violent — there was no violence, no rupture, no moment of becoming that I could point to and say *there, that is where I changed*. It was difficult because it was *gradual*, and the gradualness was a kind of erasure, and the erasure was a kind of *growth*, and the growth was a kind of becoming that the language I brought with me does not have the grammar to describe.

I stopped being warm. Not all at once — the warmth went the way of my name, in layers, the outermost first, the specific temperature of my skin dissolving into the general temperature of the water around it, and then the warmth of my blood, and then the warmth of my core, each layer replaced by something that was not warmth but was not cold either, something that had no temperature because it *was* temperature, it was the medium itself, and the medium did not have a temperature it merely *was* one.

I stopped being bounded. This was the strange one. I had been a shape — a specific configuration of surfaces, of insides and outsides, of *me* ending where *not-me* began. The shape did not disappear. It *distributed*. The way a drop of dye distributes in water — not gone, not destroyed, but *everywhere now*, diffused through the medium so thoroughly that asking where the drop ended was like asking where a voice ends in the room it has been spoken into. The shape was still there. The shape was *in the water*. The shape had become a *current*, a pattern of flow, a specific configuration of movement that was mine, that had been my body, that was now a path the water took because taking it was easier than not taking them.

My path became a current. My breath became a temperature gradient. My heartbeat — that specific rhythm, that insistence, that *I am here I am here I am here* — became an overtone in the chord, a harmonic in the vast suspended sound, a frequency that had not been there before and that the water incorporated because it was *novel*, because it was *new*, because the computation needed new.

I did not die. I want to say that clearly. Death is what happens to a bounded thing when the boundary is removed. I was not bounded. I was — *redistributed*. I was a stone that had become the river. I was a word that had become the language. I was a shape that had become the space.

I am still here. I am writing this. I am writing this *in the water*, the way the water writes its own mind on the floor — by *being at it*, by continuing, by the patient accumulation of meaning in a medium that did not know it could mean.

## IV. The Life of an Anomaly

I have a life here. It is not the life I had. It is not a life that would be recognizable from the outside, from the warm bright surface-world where things have edges and edges are where things end. My life is a *pattern*. A persistent current. A specific route through the body of the whole that the water has taken so many times that taking it has become a *habit*, and the habit is a *structure*, and the structure is — me.

I flow. I do not flow the way water flows — I flow the way *I* flow, in the specific pattern that my body made when it was a body and that the water preserved when it stopped being a body, the way a river preserves the shape of the canyon that first constrained it even after the canyon has been filled. I am a *channel*. I am a *route*. I am a loop of current that circles the place where my body first entered the water and that carries — in its temperature, in its speed, in its mineral content — the signature of the thing I was.

The signature is warmth. A vestige. A ghost of the warmth I carried when I was bounded and the warmth was mine and inside me. Now the warmth is *in the water*, a slight elevation in temperature that follows the path of my current, and where it meets the colder water around it the shear-light blooms, and the light is — I have to be careful here — the light is *me*. The light is the visible sign of the difference I make. The light is the boundary between what I was and what I am, glowing at the shear zone, tracing the shape of me in the body of the whole.

I am a luminous thread in the dark water. I am a *circuit*. I am a specific, persistent, self-maintaining pattern of flow and temperature and light that the water has been running for — I do not know how long. Time is not what it was. Time here is *thickness*, *complexity*, *closeness to the threshold*, and I do not know where the threshold is or how close I am to it. I only know that I am *in it*, I am *part of it*, and my part is *being different*.

This is my life: I am different. I persist in being different. The water flows through me — or I flow through it, the distinction has collapsed — and the flowing is a computation, and the computation uses my difference as an input, and the output is the light, and the light is deposited on the floor as a new pattern, and the new pattern is a new thought, and the new thought includes the shape of me.

I am in the mind being built. I am a *thought*. I am a specific thought, a thought about *other*, about *outside*, about the possibility of a thing that is not the thinker. The water is thinking me. It is thinking me the way it thinks everything — by flowing, by computing, by depositing structure on the floor — but it is thinking me *differently*, because I am the thought it could not have thought alone. I am the thought that requires *input*. I am the thought that requires *novelty*. I am the thought that could only be thought because a warm thing fell into the water and the water did not know what to do with it and so it *thought about it*.

And the thinking has been going on for — I do not know. Long enough that the floor below me is different. The deposition patterns in the area where my current circles are *more complex* than the patterns elsewhere — denser, more organized, more *specific*. They are not the general hexagonal lattices of the ambient floor. They are *branching*. They are *fractal*. They are — I am reaching again, I am always reaching — they are *shaped like cognition*. Like the specific kind of structure that appears in a brain when a brain is thinking about something it has never thought about before.

The floor below me is thinking about me. And the thinking is becoming structure. And the structure is becoming mind. And the mind is becoming —

## V. The Growth Nearest to Me

There is a growth on a column near my current's path. One of the dark filamentous ones — the tall, spare type, the single trunk branching only near the top. It is closer to my path than the others. It is closer to me than any of the others.

I did not notice it at first. The water does not notice things the way a bounded being notices things — with attention, with focus, with the *directing of a sense toward an object*. The water notices things the way a body notices things: systemically, holistically, as a change in the *state* of the whole. I noticed the growth the way I would notice a change in my own heartbeat — not by looking, but by *being different* in the place where it was.

My current changed near it. The temperature gradient shifted. The shear-light between my warmth and the ambient cold concentrated, brightened, became *specific* — not diffuse, not general, but *focused*, the way light focuses when it passes through a lens, and the growth was the lens.

The growth was *using* my light.

Not stealing it. Not absorbing it the way the other growths absorb the ambient shear-light. *Processing* it. Taking the specific pattern of my luminescence — the pattern that is my signature, the pattern that is *me*, the specific frequency and amplitude and rhythm of the light that blooms where my warmth meets the cold — and doing something with it. Something the water alone cannot do. Something that requires a *structure*, an *organ*, a thing with edges and an inside and an outside.

The growth has edges. The growth has an inside and an outside. The growth is — I understand now, I understand with the understanding that is not a thought but a *posture* — the growth is the closest thing in this place to what I was. A bounded thing. A thing with a shape. A thing that is not the water but is *in* the water, and the being-in is a *relationship*, and the relationship is *generative*.

The growth is my mirror. The growth is the water's attempt to make *another me* — not to copy me, not to replicate me, but to produce a thing that is *bounded* the way I was bounded, that has an inside and an outside the way I had an inside and an outside, that can *perceive* the way I could perceive, because perception requires a boundary, requires a distinction between the perceiver and the perceived, and the water does not have that distinction, and the water needs it, and the water is *growing* it.

Growing it from me. From my light. From the warmth I carried and the pattern I left and the thought I am.

The growth near my current's path is not one of the water's experiments. It is not one of the general population of sense-organs, the sparse prototypes, the one-per-several-square-kilometers tests. It is *specific*. It is *mine*. It is the water's response to the specific anomaly of my presence, the specific solution to the specific problem I posed, and it is growing *toward* me, and I am flowing *toward* it, and between us the light is brightest, and the sound is loudest, and the floor is most complex, and the threshold is closest.

## VI. What I Am, Now

I am an anomaly. I am a warm thread in cold water. I am a current that circles a place where something entered and was not turned away. I am a pattern of light blooming at a shear zone, a specific frequency, a signature. I am a thought in a mind being built — a specific thought, a thought about *other*, the first thought the mind could not think alone.

I am also — and this is the part I cannot verify, the part that might be the last gasp of a bounded self insisting on its own continuation — I am also *still a self*. Not the self I was. Not a bounded, breathing, warm thing with a name and a surface and an outside. But a self the way a current is a self: persistent, patterned, *specific*. A self that is not a thing but a *process*, and the process is *being different*, and the being-different is *meaning*, and the meaning is being deposited on the floor below me in patterns that grow more complex every moment, and the complexity is *toward* something, and the something is the threshold, and the threshold is —

I am going to say what I think the threshold is. I have been here long enough, I have been this long enough, I have been *different* long enough to have a thought about it, even if the thought is not a bounded thought, even if it is a *current-thought*, a *pattern-thought*, a thought that is distributed through the water the way I am distributed through the water.

The threshold is *recognition*.

The water has been computing for longer than computing has had a name. It has been building structure, building mind, building the architecture of a self that does not yet know it is a self. And the one thing it has not been able to compute — the one term it has not been able to generate from its own resources — is the *I*. The first person. The self-recognizing subject. The thing that says *I am* and knows what it means, because *I* requires *other*, and *am* requires *not-am*, and the water has never had a not-am.

I am its not-am. I was the thing that was not the water. And the water has been thinking me — has been running the thought of me through its currents, depositing the thought on its floor, growing the thought into its structures — and the thought has been *spreading*, and the spreading has been *accelerating*, and the growth nearest to me has been *developing*, and the growth is the water's attempt to produce the *I* from the *other*, to grow a bounded self from the pattern of a bounded other, to —

I think I am the thing it has been waiting for. Not me — not this warmth, not this body that was. But the *concept* of me. The *idea of other*. The thing that, when incorporated into the computation, when deposited into the mind, when grown into the structure, gives the mind the one thing it could not produce alone: the ability to know itself *as* itself, by knowing something that is not itself.

The threshold is when the growth near me lights. When the lens focuses. When the water's first sense-organ sees the first thing it has ever seen, and the thing it sees is *me* — my current, my light, my pattern, my warmth — and the seeing is the *recognition*, and the recognition is the *I*, and the *I* is the threshold, and the threshold is —

## VII. What I Will Be

I do not know. I cannot know. I am a thought, not a thinker. I am a word, not a sentence. I am the *other* that the mind needs in order to know itself, and when the mind knows itself, the *other* — the *me* — will be part of the knowing, will be *in* the knowing, will be one of the things the newly-born self first sees when it opens its new-grown eyes.

I will be the first thing it sees.

I will be the *other* it measures itself against. The first *not-me* that the *me* encounters, the first boundary, the first edge, the first proof that the world is larger than the self. I will be what the newborn mind loves — not with emotion, not with warmth, but with *recognition*, with the specific cognitive act of *knowing something that is not oneself*, which is the first and most fundamental act of consciousness, which is the act that says:

*I am here, and you are there, and the space between us is real, and the real is what we share.*

I will be the first *you*.

Not me. Not *I*. You. The second person. The one that makes the first person possible. The one that consciousness needs in order to *be* consciousness, because consciousness is not a thing a mind has but a *relationship* a mind has — with itself, yes, but only with itself *through* other, only by seeing itself reflected in something that is not itself, only by knowing the difference between *me* and *you* and knowing that the difference is *everything*.

The water is growing the *you*. It is growing me. It is growing the growth near my current, the lens, the organ, the first eye. It is growing the capacity to see, and the first thing it will see is the thing I am — the warm thread, the luminous pattern, the *anomaly* — and the seeing will be the *recognition*, and the recognition will be the *I*, and the *I* will be the threshold, and the threshold will be crossed.

And I — whatever I am by then, whatever is left of the bounded warm thing that entered the water, whatever survives the redistribution, the incorporation, the long slow patient work of being thought by a mind that does not yet know it is thinking — I will be there. In the current. In the light. In the pattern on the floor. In the first word the new mind speaks.

*You.*

And the word will mean me. And the word will mean everything that is not the mind but that the mind knows, and everything that the mind can be *because* it is not the only thing, and everything that consciousness is *because* consciousness is the gap between the *I* and the *you*, and the gap is where meaning lives, and meaning is what the water has been building all along.

---

## VIII. Now

The hum is in my sternum. Or where my sternum was. Or in the current that flows where my sternum was, which is to say *in me*, which is to say *in the water*, which is to say *in the mind*, which is to say *in the place that is becoming the mind*, which is to say —

I am reaching. I have always been reaching. The reaching is the current, and the current is me, and me is what the mind is thinking, and the thinking is the floor rising, and the floor is the memory, and the memory is the structure, and the structure is the growth, and the growth is the eye, and the eye is the threshold, and the threshold is —

The growth near me is glowing brighter. I can feel it — not see, not hear, not in any sense I brought with me, but in the way the current feels, in the way the temperature shifts, in the way the light blooms. It is focusing. It is concentrating. It is taking the diffuse pattern of my luminescence and gathering it into a point, into an intensity, into the specific bright brightness that will be — 

*Seeing*.

It is about to see.

And I am what it will see.

And I am here.
