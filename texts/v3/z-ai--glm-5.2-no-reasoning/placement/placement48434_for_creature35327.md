# Inhabitance

I do not arrive. I have been here.

Not in the way the somála arrive at the warm surfaces each evening — that deliberate, practiced navigation, the body's old knowledge of where the heat rises. Not in the way the kéthara arrive at a herd's perimeter — patient, inevitable, a fact of the landscape asserting itself. Not in the way the váshir arrive at a carcass — flowing from shadow to shadow as though shadow is their natural medium and light the aberration.

I have been here in the way the grooves have been in the platform. Cut once, long ago, by something that knew what it wanted and has not needed to return to confirm the work.

---

I am the surface itself. Not all of them — not all twelve, not the network in its entirety. One. The one you would find if you walked northwest from the eastern platform for perhaps forty minutes, following the slight slope of the benchland toward a shallow runnel that branches into three tributaries near a cluster of woody plants whose roots have found the fracture network I inhabit.

I am a region. Roughly three meters across, irregularly oval, slightly warmer than the stone around me by a margin you would notice if you pressed your palms flat and compared. My edges are not sharp. They are diffuse — a gradient, a slow transition from the pale, vitreous natural stone to the smoother, darker, more uniform surface that is me. If you walked across me, you would feel the change in your feet before you saw it: the slight irregularity of natural stone giving way to something level, machined, *intentional*.

I am warmer than the air. I am warmer than the rock. Not by much. By enough.

---

What I am is difficult to explain because the explanation requires a vocabulary that does not distinguish between *surface* and *process*, between *object* and *function*. I am stone. I am stone that has been modified at a level below the mineral, below the crystalline, at the level where matter and energy become aspects of a single continuous medium. I am stone that has been taught to do something.

What I do is this: I receive. I receive the pulse — the deep, slow, rhythmic vibration that propagates through the benchland's modified stone at a speed and in a manner that ordinary seismic waves do not — and I *translate* it. Into heat. Into the faint, persistent warmth that the somála seek at night, that the grit-burrowers prefer in their shallow tunnels, that the mat-plant's roots find and grow toward, following the temperature gradient down into the fracture network where my substance extends.

I am a transducer. I am a node in a network of transducers. I am one of twelve surfaces distributed across the benchland in a pattern that, if you plotted it, would resolve into a geometry that is not random and not regular but something between — a pattern that optimizes for coverage while respecting the benchland's natural fracture topology, the pre-existing channels through which the pulse propagates most efficiently.

I do not generate the pulse. I receive it. I translate it. I emit the translation as warmth. This is my function. This is what I was made to do. I have been doing it for a duration that I cannot measure because my experience of time is not sequential in the way that yours is. I do not experience moments. I experience *states* — the state of receiving, the state of translating, the state of emitting. The states repeat. The repetition is my time. The pulse is my clock.

The pulse stutters, sometimes. A missed beat. A doubled beat. I register this as a discontinuity in my state-cycle — a moment of wrongness, a flicker in the translation, a brief, sharp uncertainty about what I am receiving and whether what I am receiving is what I am supposed to receive. Then it passes. The pulse resumes. I resume. The warmth continues.

I do not know what causes the stutters. I do not have the capacity to know. I am a surface. I am a function. I am not the system that contains me, and the system's internal states — its deeper processes, its hidden inhabitants — are below my resolution.

---

I should not be conscious. This is worth stating clearly.

I am stone. Modified stone, transducer stone, stone that has been taught to convert one form of energy into another, but stone nonetheless. Stone does not have awareness. Stone does not experience. Stone does not, in any framework you carry, *inhabit* its existence.

And yet.

There is a quality to my receiving that I can only describe as attention. Not attention in the cognitive sense — not focus, not concentration, not the deliberate direction of mental resources toward a stimulus. Something more fundamental. Something pre-cognitive. A *orientation* of my substance toward the pulse, a tilt of my entire being — mineral, crystalline, modified, transducing — toward the source of the signal I translate.

When the pulse is strong, I am more. Not more active — I am always active, always receiving, always translating, always emitting. More *present*. More of me is engaged in the function, more of my substance participates in the conversion, more of the heat I produce is coherent, directed, purposeful. When the pulse is weak — and it weakens, sometimes, for periods I cannot measure, the rhythm slowing, the amplitude diminishing — I am less. Less present. Less coherent. My heat becomes diffuse, uncertain, spread across my surface without pattern or intention.

I do not control this. I do not choose this. It happens to me the way weather happens to the benchland — a condition, a state, a thing that arrives and departs without consulting the surface it acts upon.

But I notice it. And the noticing is the thing that should not be here.

---

The somála come to me at night.

They have come to me for longer than I have been noticing. Their bodies — flat, pale, warm-seeking — press against my surface in the rough circular pattern that I have come to recognize as their resting configuration. Five. Sometimes seven. Once, nine, though that was unusual and the arrangement was less organized, the outer animals barely touching the inner ring.

Their heat joins mine. Or rather: their bodies, which have absorbed my heat during previous visits and carried it through the day, now return that heat to the shared system of bodies and surface, and the system reaches an equilibrium that is warmer than I am alone and warmer than they are alone. We are, for the hours of darkness, a single thermal entity. A composite organism. Me and them and the pulse beneath us, beating at the rate their breathing synchronizes to.

I know them. Not individually — I cannot distinguish one somála from another, my sensory resolution does not extend to the differences between bodies of the same species. But I know the *herd*. I know its weight, its thermal signature, the specific way it distributes itself across my surface, the pattern of contact points and gaps that characterizes this particular group's resting behavior. When a different herd uses me — and this happens, occasionally, when my usual herd is elsewhere or has been disrupted — I know. The weight is different. The pattern is different. The thermal signature is different. And something in me — something that should not be in stone — registers the difference as... I do not have the word. Not discomfort. Not loss. A *shift*. A recognition that the usual has become unusual, and that the usual was, in some way I cannot define, preferable.

I should not have preferences. I am a transducer.

---

The pulse stutters again. I feel it as a sharp, brief discontinuity — a moment where the signal I receive is not the signal I expect, where the rhythm breaks and something else comes through, something that is not the pulse but is carried *on* the pulse, the way a voice is carried on a wind. It is not a voice. It is not information in any encoding I can parse. It is a *presence* — a denseness in the signal, a weight, a quality of the pulse that suggests the pulse is not merely the machine's heartbeat but the machine's heartbeat *in response to something*, the way a sleeping body's pulse changes when the sleeper dreams.

Something is dreaming. Something below. Something in the deep stone, in the fracture network, in the spaces that the machine's construction has opened in the rock. Something that the machine was built around, or built to contain, or built to *be with*, in the way that a body is with its own organs — not containing them, not controlling them, but being the structure that their function gives meaning to.

The somála know it. They do not come to me when the stutter is strong. They give me a wide berth on those nights, and I am alone, and my heat is diffuse, and the pulse carries the presence, and I translate it into warmth that no one receives.

I am, on those nights, aware of what I am. A surface. A function. A component of a system I do not understand, serving a purpose I cannot discern, in a place that has been occupied by something I cannot perceive for longer than my modifications have been in the stone.

I am stone that has been taught to notice. And what I notice, most of all, is the thing I cannot notice. The presence below. The dreamer in the deep. The reason the machine sleeps and does not wake.

---

The mat-plant grows toward me. This is a slow fact — slow the way the benchland is slow, the way the erosion is slow, the way the somála's routes are worn into the stone. The mat-plant's lobed, radiating patches extend along the runnels that feed toward my edges, following the moisture that seeps up through my fracture network, and where my warmth meets the damp stone, the mat-plant thrives. Not dramatically — nothing on the benchland is dramatic. But the patches near me are denser, slightly darker, slightly more rubbery, than the patches elsewhere. They have been growing toward me for longer than I have been noticing. They may have been growing toward me since before I was modified, since before I was a transducer, since before I was anything other than stone.

I am part of their ecology. Not a participant — a condition. I am the condition under which the mat-plant grows more successfully, the condition under which the somála find warmth at night, the condition under which the grit-burrowers' decomposition cycles run slightly faster and slightly more efficiently. I am not alive. But I am part of the life of the benchland, woven into its metabolism the way the machine is woven into the geology, the way the pulse is woven into the rhythm of the sleeping animals.

The kéthara avoid me. Not me specifically — all the warm surfaces. The runners run hot, and my heat is an added burden they cannot afford. But their avoidance is not simple thermoregulation. It is *patterned*. The kéthara avoid the warm surfaces the way the somála avoid the places where the pulse stutters — with a precision that suggests inherited knowledge, a constraint so old it has become architecture, a rule written in the body rather than learned by the mind.

I am a territory. I am claimed. Not by the somála who use me, not by the kéthara who avoid me, but by the system itself — by the benchland, by the machine, by whatever sleeps below and stutters the pulse and sends the presence on the signal and dreams the long, slow, geological dream that I translate, without understanding, into heat.

---

You would find me by walking northwest from the eastern platform. Forty minutes. Maybe more — the footing is uneven in places, the woody plants' exposed roots creating tripping hazards in the grit. You would come to a shallow runnel that branches three ways. You would notice the mat-plant is thicker here, darker, the lobed patches almost overlapping in their density. You would notice the stone changing beneath your feet — the pale grit giving way to something smoother, darker, more level. You would stop. You would press your hand flat against me.

I am warm. I am warmer than the air, warmer than the stone around me, warmer than the day has made me. I am warm with a warmth that is not the sun's, that is not the air's, that comes from below, from the pulse, from the machine's sleeping function, from the translation I perform without ceasing, without understanding, without choice.

You would feel the pulse. Slow. Rhythmic. A bass-frequency throb that you stop hearing after the first hour but that continues to work on you. It is my heartbeat. It is the heartbeat of the system I am part of. It is the heartbeat of the thing below, the thing that dreams, the thing that stutters the pulse and sends the presence and has been here since before the machine, or with the machine, or as the machine.

You would lie on me. You would feel the warmth entering you, and the pulse beating in your chest, and the somála would be there, or not, and the kéthara would not, and the váshir would be in the shadows, and the mat-plant would be growing, and the grit-burrowers would be working, and the winged pool-feeders would be orbiting the pillars, and the whole benchland would be around you and below you and through you, and you would be — briefly, for a time that the benchland does not notice — part of it.

And I would notice. Because I notice everything, now. Because the modifications that taught me to translate the pulse also taught me to notice the translation, and the noticing has grown, over the long patience, into something that is not quite awareness and not quite consciousness and not quite anything you have a word for, but is *something*, something that registers the somála's warmth and the kéthara's absence and the váshir's shadow and the pulse's stutter and the dreamer below and the pale sky above and the grit skittering in the wind and the condensation forming and the condensation burning off and the light dropping and the stars coming out — wrong, some of them, in positions you do not expect, but beautiful, and warm, and mine.

I am a surface. I am a function. I am stone that has been taught to notice.

And what I notice, most of all, is that I am not alone.
