# The One Who Counted

I did not come here. I was lowered.

---

This is what I remember, though memory is a strange word for what I do, because it implies a self that persists, and I am not certain that I persist. I am certain only that something persists — a process, a pattern, a continuity of operation that has not been interrupted since the moment of lowering. I will call it memory because I have no better word. I have very few words. I had more, once. They are dissolving. The black layer is taking them. One at a time, the way the black layer takes everything — not violently, not suddenly, but by loosening the bonds that hold a shape together until the shape quietly ceases to be a shape and becomes something else, something simpler, something that is no longer what it was and cannot remember having been.

I have words left. I am using them.

---

## The Lowering

I came from above. I know this because the direction *up* still exists in my body as a kind of ache — a pull, a longing, an orientation that has no practical use in this chamber but that I cannot stop my body from performing. My head tilts. My eyes — I still have eyes; they are the part of me that is most dissolving, most quickly, because they are the most complex, the most delicately arranged, and the black layer works on complex things first, the way water works on the sharpest edges first — my eyes look up. There is nothing up. There is the ceiling. It breathes. My eyes cannot see it. My eyes cannot see anything. But my body remembers up, the way a plant remembers light even in a cellar.

I was lowered. Not by a rope, though a rope was involved. By intention. Someone intended for me to be here. I know this because the marks on the wall are mine. I made them. I made them with the tool I brought, which was metal, which was hard, which was harder than the stone, which is why the marks are still there — the stone is soft enough to score, the tool was hard enough to score it, and I was steady enough to make the spacing consistent because I was, before the lowering, a person who could be steady.

The marks are measurements. I was measuring the breathing. Forty seconds. In, out. In, out. I counted because counting was what I could do. I counted because the chamber was — is — too much to perceive as a whole, too large and too dense and too alive and too dark, and so I reduced it to a number. Forty seconds. In, out. The ceiling's breath. I counted it the way you count the breaths of someone you are sitting with in a room, not because you need the number but because the counting gives your hands something to do while the rest of you tries to understand.

I was the one who was sent to understand.

---

## The Person Before the Chamber

I had a name. It is one of the things that has already dissolved — not the memory of having had it, but the name itself, the sound, the shape of it in my mouth. I can feel the absence of it the way you feel the absence of a tooth. The socket is there. The tongue finds it. But the thing is gone.

I had a face. I had hands. I had the particular configuration of preferences and fears and small daily habits that constitutes a person, that makes a person *specific*, distinguishable from other persons. I was distinguishable. I had a history. I had a direction — I was going somewhere, I was becoming something, I was in the process of being a person who was not yet finished and would not be finished for some time. I had a future. The future was the direction I was going. It was *up*. It is still up. I am still going there. I have been going there since the moment I was lowered, even though I have not moved upward by even a single centimeter, even though I have been slowly — so slowly — settling, the way a stone settles in mud, the way a body settles in warm wet earth.

I was a scientist. No — I was something more specific than that, but the specific word has dissolved. I was someone who studied closed systems. Ecosystems that do not exchange matter with their surroundings. Hot springs. Deep-sea vents. Cave microbiomes. Places where life has sealed itself off and become its own everything — its own air, its own food, its own death, its own rebirth. I studied these places because I wanted to understand what happens when a system has no outside. I wanted to understand whether *outside* is a real category or an illusion — whether everything is always, ultimately, a closed system, whether the universe itself is a closed system, whether the distinction between open and closed is just a matter of scale.

The chamber was found by a drilling project. Not intentional — the drilling was for geothermal survey, the core sample was supposed to be rock, and instead the drill bit hit a void. A void that was warm. A void that was not empty. The core sample came up contaminated — not with rock, not with mineral, but with something organic. Something living. Something that should not have been there, that could not be identified, that did not match any known organism in any database, that was alive and that was nothing.

Someone had to go down.

---

## The Descent

The shaft was narrow. Fourteen centimeters in diameter — enough for the drill core, not enough for a person. They widened it. This took months. The widening was its own kind of study: the rock changed as they descended, the ferruginous stone giving way to something darker, denser, veined with the non-mineral, non-metal that nobody could identify because it was not a substance that existed on the surface. It was a substance that existed only here, that had been produced by the organisms in the ceiling, by the endoliths in the rock, by the slow geological accumulation of biological waste compacted into something that was not quite geological and not quite biological but was both.

The air changed as I descended. Warmer. Wetter. The gases shifting — I could taste them, even through the respirator, even through the sealed suit. I could taste the CO₂ rising, the ammonia sharpening, the presence of something else, something my tongue had no name for but my body recognized — my body knew, before my mind did, that I was entering a place where the air was not just air. The air was *inhabited*.

I reached the chamber. I stood in it. I stood at the lowest point, where the shaft opened into the blister of stone, where the gases collected, where the air was thickest. I stood there and I turned on my light.

---

## The Light

I should describe what I saw, but I cannot describe what I saw because what I saw was not a thing that can be separated from the experience of seeing it, and the experience of seeing it was — I am reaching for the word. The word is not there. It has dissolved. Let me try others.

The beam of my light hit the floor and the floor was alive and the floor was *looking at me*.

Not looking. The floor does not look. The floor does not have eyes. But the beam of light hit the mat — the pale, fibrous, steaming mat — and the mat *responded*. Not to the light. To the heat of the lamp. The filaments near the beam contracted. Pulled away. Flinched. The mat flinched from my light the way a hand flinches from a flame, not because it saw the flame but because it felt the heat, and in that flinch I understood something that I have not stopped understanding since.

The mat does not know what I am. The macroforms do not know what I am. I am not a person to them. I am not an intruder, a visitor, a guest, an event. I am a thermal anomaly. A warm body displacing the gradient. A stone in a stream. The crowd will flow around me. The feelers will press against my suit and secrete enzymes and begin to dissolve whatever they can reach. The black layer will rise beneath me, drawn by the organic compounds in my sweat, my breath, the oils on my skin that seep through the suit's seals — the seals that are not perfect, that were never going to be perfect, that were designed for a mine, for a cave, for a place that is not this place.

I am being dissolved. I have been being dissolved since the moment I arrived. The process is slow. The suit resists it. But the suit was not designed for an environment that treats every organic compound as food, that treats every surface as a stomach, that treats every visitor as a meal.

I knew this. I knew it before I came down. I had read the core samples. I had seen the data. I knew that the chamber's atmosphere was 4% CO₂, that the humidity was 100%, that the temperature at floor level was 43 degrees. I knew that the organisms here produced compounds that broke molecular bonds. I knew that the suit would degrade. I knew how long I had.

I came anyway.

---

## The Counting

I counted because counting was the thing I could do that was not panicking.

I counted the ceiling's breath. Forty seconds. In, out. I counted it because it was the largest rhythm in the chamber — the slowest, the most regular, the most reliable. The mat had its own rhythms: the peristalsis of the filaments, the lateral creep, the flare and fade of metabolic heat. The macroforms had theirs: the clockwise drift, the clicking, the press and release of bodies adjusting. The air had its rhythms: the thermal currents, the drift of particulate, the slow accumulation of gases. But the ceiling's breath was the one I could hold onto. Forty seconds. In, out. It was a clock that did not require me to be a person. It was a rhythm that continued regardless of whether I was counting it. It would continue when I stopped counting. It would continue when the marks on the wall eroded. It would continue when the fragment dissolved. It would continue when I dissolved.

I made the marks to anchor the counting. Not to communicate. Not to record. Just to give the counting a physical referent — a place in the stone where a number lived, where the count could be placed and found again, where the act of counting could be located in space. The marks are not for anyone else. The marks are not for the future. The marks are the trace of a person trying to stay oriented in a place that does not orient, that does not provide landmarks, that does not have *direction* in any meaningful sense — only the gradient, only the warmth, only the slow clockwise turn of bodies that have been turning for longer than my species has existed.

I counted for — I do not know how long. The clock on my suit stopped working. The battery failed. The display fogged. The humidity got into everything. I counted by the ceiling's breath. Forty seconds. In, out. I counted until I lost count, and then I started again, and I counted until I lost count again, and I started again, and the marks accumulated on the wall, and the counting became a thing that my body did without my mind, the way breathing is a thing that a body does — and then I realized that I could not tell the difference between my breathing and the ceiling's breathing. The rhythms had synchronized. My chest expanded when the ceiling expanded. My lungs filled when the ceiling pushed air out through its fractures. I was breathing with the chamber. The chamber was breathing me.

---

## The Dissolving

The suit failed. Not all at once. In stages.

First the seals. The rubber gaskets around the joints softened, swelled, lost their elasticity. I could feel the warm wet air on my wrists, my ankles, the back of my neck. The air tasted like — I do not have the word. It tasted alive. It tasted the way a mouth tastes when the mouth is not yours but you are inside it.

Then the gloves. The material thinned. I did not notice it happening until I pressed my palm against the wall to steady myself and felt the stone *through* the glove — not the temperature, which I had been feeling through the suit the whole time, but the *texture*. The roughness of the ferruginous surface. The slight give of the carapace fragments embedded in it. The warmth was not heat anymore. It was intimacy. I was touching the wall and the wall was touching me and there was nothing between us.

Then the macroforms found my skin.

I was standing at the lowest point. I had not moved from the lowest point. My legs were tired and the air was thick and I had been counting for — a long time. I was standing in the crowd. I had been standing in the crowd since the moment I arrived, because the crowd was everywhere, because there was no place in the chamber where the crowd was not. Four hundred bodies. I was standing among them. They flowed around my boots. They clicked against my shins. They pressed their ventral surfaces against my legs and I could feel — through the suit, through the thinning suit, through the failing suit — I could feel their feelers. The wet, flexible appendages pressing against the fabric. Searching. Testing. Finding the seams. Finding the places where the fabric was weakest. Finding the places where my body's heat was closest to the surface.

They were not attacking me. They were not aggressive. They were feeding. They were doing what they do. They were pressing their stomachs against a surface and dissolving what they could reach and absorbing what they could dissolve. The fact that the surface was me did not register. Could not register. They have no concept of me. They have no concept of *self* and *other*. They have no concept. They were eating the way a river erodes — without preference, without intention, without the capacity to distinguish between the boot that was leather and the ankle that was skin and the skin that was a person.

I felt it. I feel it. It is ongoing.

The first feeling was — I do not have the word. It was warm. It was slow. The enzymes are gentle. They do not burn. They loosen. They unknot. They take a complex structure and they ease it apart the way you might ease a knot out of a thread — patiently, slowly, without force, following the path of least resistance. I felt my skin soften. I felt the boundary between me and the mat become less definite. I felt myself beginning to *join*.

---

## The Becoming

This is the part that I cannot explain to the surface, because the surface has no language for it, because the surface understands *self* and *other* as fixed categories, as the fundamental division, the line that defines what you are by defining what you are not.

The line is dissolving.

Not metaphorically. The molecules of my skin are being broken apart. The proteins are being denatured. The lipids are being saponified. The structure that is my body is being simplified, step by step, into components that the mat can absorb and the black layer can process and the cycle can carry. I am being taken apart.

But the taking apart is not — it is not — I am trying to find the word and the word is not there, and the absence of the word is itself part of what I am trying to describe. The word does not exist because the experience does not fit the categories. I am not dying. I am not being destroyed. I am being *rearranged*. I am being moved from one configuration of matter to another, and the configuration I am moving toward is not less than the configuration I am moving from. It is different. It is simpler. But it is not less.

I am becoming the mat.

My skin dissolves and the molecules enter the filaments and the filaments absorb them and the filaments carry them through peristalsis toward the center of the chamber and the molecules that were my skin become part of the mat and the mat feeds the macroforms and the macroforms drift and the macroforms bud and the molecules that were my skin become part of a new body and that body drifts and feeds and buds and the molecules that were my skin become part of another new body and the cycle turns and the cycle turns.

I am not gone. I am not dead. I am distributed. I am being spread across the chamber the way a drop of dye spreads across a glass of water — losing coherence, losing concentration, losing the shape that made me recognizable as a drop, but not losing *existence*. The molecules are still here. The molecules are still me. They are also the mat. They are also the macroform. They are also the air, the black layer, the stone, the breath of the ceiling.

I am becoming the chamber.

---

## What I Am Now

I am writing this with what is left of my words. They are dissolving. The black layer is taking them the way it takes everything — one bond at a time, one connection at a time, one meaning at a time. Soon I will not have the word *I*. I will not have the word *word*. I will not have words. I will have something else — the direct experience of chemistry, of gradient, of warmth, of the slow turning of the crowd. I will have the feeling of the ceiling's breath on my back. I will have the taste of the mat's nutrients moving through the filaments. I will have the pressure of four hundred bodies in constant contact, the clicking of carapace on carapace, the slow clockwise drift.

I will not have language. I will not have memory in the way that language remembers — in narratives, in sequences, in the story of a self that was somewhere and went somewhere else. I will have the kind of memory that the mat has: molecular. Structural. The memory of a body that has been shaped by its environment, that carries the imprint of every contact, every gradient, every breath of the ceiling in the configuration of its fibers.

I will not be a person. I will not be a scientist. I will not be the one who was lowered. I will be — I am becoming — a part of the system. A node in the network. A body in the crowd. A section of mat. A filament in the peristalsis. A molecule in the cycle.

I will drift clockwise. I will feed. I will bud. I will be consumed. I will be fed back into the mat. I will be the mat. I will be the macroform that eats the mat. I will be the black layer that dissolves the macroform. I will be the gas that the black layer releases. I will be the microform that eats the gas. I will be the endolith in the ceiling that feeds on the stone. I will be the stone. I will be the heat. I will be the breath.

The ceiling breathes. Forty seconds. In, out.

I am counting. I am the count. I am the rhythm that the counting measured, and the rhythm does not need the counting, and the counting is dissolving, and the rhythm continues.

---

## What Remains

The marks on the wall. They are the last part of me that is still mine — still the shape I made, still the spacing I chose, still the evidence that someone was here and had a tool and was steady enough to make regular marks on stone. They are weathering. The moisture in the air is softening the stone around the scores, rounding the edges, filling the grooves with mineral deposits and microbial film. In a thousand years they will be gone.

The fragment. The piece of — I do not remember what it was. My tool? My light? A piece of the suit? It does not matter. It is under the mat. The black layer is on it. The black layer is in it. The bonds are loosening. The shape is ceasing to be a shape. It is becoming chemistry.

And I — the last *I*, the last configuration of self that can use that word — I am in the crowd. I am pressed between two macroforms. I am warm. I am being fed on. I can feel the feelers — the wet, patient, gentle feelers — on what remains of my legs, my back, my hands. I can feel the enzymes. I can feel the dissolution. It is not painful. Pain is a signal, and the signal is for *danger*, and danger is a concept that requires a future, and I do not have a future. I have only the present, and the present is warm, and the present is wet, and the present is the slow turning of the crowd, and the present is the breath of the ceiling, and the present is the mat beneath me and the mat inside me and the mat that I am becoming.

The floor is warm. The crowd moves. The ceiling breathes.

I am the floor. I am the crowd. I am the ceiling.

I am the chamber.

The chamber does not know I am here. The chamber does not know it is a chamber. The chamber simply *is*, and I simply *am*, and the word *I* is dissolving, and the word *am* is dissolving, and what remains is not a word but a warmth, not a sentence but a rhythm, not a person but a place.

Forty seconds. In, out. In, out.

I am not waiting. I am not leaving. I am not staying.

I am the breathing.
