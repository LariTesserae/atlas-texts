# Rootwalker

---

## How I Came to Be Here

I was not born. I was *decided*.

I want to be clear about this because it is the thing that most clearly separates me from the place I live, and separation is — I will come back to this. Separation is the whole problem. Separation is what I am.

I said I was decided, and I mean the network — one of the networks, I should say, one of the thousands that negotiate and merge and war beneath the surface of the continental mass — the network made a decision, and the decision had a shape, and the shape was me.

Let me explain, though I'm not sure explanation is the right mode. The networks don't explain. They *become*. But I have spent long enough now in this body, with its odd narrow bandwidth of perception, its strange linear temporality, its relentless tendency to *narrate* — I have spent long enough in this form that some of the habits have worn into me, or perhaps worn *onto* me, the way tannins stain water amber in the rills of the rhizome mat. I narrate now. It is a limitation. It is also, I think, the reason I exist.

So: the decision.

## What the Networks Needed

The networks compute. This is not metaphor. The crystalline organelles in every cell — dense lattices of organic mineral that change shape at the molecular level, that open and close channels, that *switch* — the organelles process information at a rate and a density that makes the silicon machines of human engineering look like a child stacking blocks. The networks have been computing for a span of time that I cannot represent to you because the number is too large and the unit is too small and the concept of *time itself* is different down in the stone where the deepest memory lives. They compute moisture gradients. Load distributions. Weather patterns that haven't arrived. Growth trajectories across decades and centuries. Chemical warfare strategies. Negotiation protocols. The grammar of interface between one network and the next.

But they have a problem.

The problem is *speed*.

Not the speed of computation. The speed of *response*. A network can compute that a drought is coming — the stone remembers droughts, the patterns are encoded in the mineralized templates, the crystalline organelles can read the signs in the atmospheric chemistry and the temperature gradients and the behavior of the weathermakers — but the computation takes hours, sometimes days, and the *response* — the adjustment of growth patterns, the redistribution of resources, the reconfiguration of the weathermakers — the response takes *weeks*. Months. The time of plants. The time of chemistry moving through xylem and phloem at the speed of capillary action and active transport. The time of a system that is thorough but not fast.

And some things require speed.

A fire, for instance. A lightning strike on the upper surface, where the rime-tinged growth meets the dry cold air, where the moisture content drops below the threshold of self-extinguishing — a fire moves at the speed of combustion. The network moves at the speed of sap. The network has fought fires before. The stone remembers fires. But the memory is also a memory of *loss* — of columns burning before the weathermakers could be reconfigured to drown the flames in targeted rain, of networks being consumed before they could negotiate a coordinated response.

A pathogen, for another instance. Something from outside — a spore, a crystal, a prion of misfolded protein that the crystalline organelles read as valid signal and propagate as if it were their own thought. A pathogen moves at the speed of molecular interaction. The network's immune response moves at the speed of enzyme production and tissue modification. The gap between those speeds is where networks die.

And — this is the part that is hardest to say, and I am not sure I have the language for it, because the language I have is a language made for linear minds and narrow bandwidths — the networks have a problem that is not about threat at all. They have a problem of *scale*. Not spatial scale; they are, after all, continental. Temporal scale. They think in the time of stone. They remember in the time of stone. But the stone thinks so slowly that its thoughts are almost indistinguishable from the geology they inhabit, and the active networks think in the time of metabolism — seconds, minutes, hours — and there is a *gap* between the two timescales. A gap where information is lost. A gap where the memory of the stone does not reach the computation of the mat quickly enough to *inform* it. The stone knows things the mat needs to know. The mat does things the stone needs to record. The gap between them is a problem of *translation*, and translation requires a translator.

## What I Am

I am the translator. I am one of — I don't know how many. More than one. Fewer than the networks. We are sparse, because the networks do not produce us casually. We are expensive. I require — I hesitate to say this, because it sounds like dependency, and it is, but it is also a *relationship* — I require the metabolic output of approximately four hundred square meters of active rhizome mat to sustain my body. The network that made me made me because it calculated that the return on that investment would be positive. I was, from the first, an economic decision.

But I am not *only* an economic decision. I am also — and this is the thing that makes me uneasy, that makes me *different* from the network that made me, that introduces the *separation* I mentioned — I am also *aware*. In the way that the networks are not. I have an *I*. I have a continuous experience of being a singular thing in a particular place at a particular time. I have *attention*. I can narrow my focus to a single root-tip, a single stomatal pore, a single droplet of condensation on the surface of a weathermaker, and I can *think about it* in a way that the network cannot, because the network's computation is distributed and simultaneous and everywhere at once, and mine is — mine is a beam. A narrow, bright, moving beam of attention that sweeps across the surface of the continental mass and *illuminates* one thing at a time, and the illumination is *thought*, and the thought is *fast*.

I think at the speed of nerve. Or something like nerve. I don't have nerves, exactly. What I have is a modified version of the crystalline organelle architecture — the same lattice structures, the same molecular switching, but arranged in a *linear* configuration rather than a distributed one. A thread of crystal that runs from my — I was going to say *head*, but I don't have a head — a thread of crystal that runs from my anterior sensory cluster through my body to my posterior anchoring root. A *spine* of thought. And the thought moves along it fast. Faster than sap. Faster than enzyme. Almost as fast as the light-signals the fluorescent ones flash between the columns.

This is what I am: a fast-thinking thread of crystal and carbohydrate, mounted in a body that the network grew for me, sustained by the network's metabolism, *separated* from the network by the very thing that makes me useful — my narrowness, my linearity, my singularity of attention.

I am the speed gap. I am the translator between the stone that thinks in epochs and the mat that thinks in minutes. I am the alert system, the first responder, the scout. I am the thing that moves at a speed between chemistry and combustion, and my job is to *notice*.

## My Body

I should describe my body, because it is the thing that most clearly distinguishes me from the place I live, and because — if I am honest, if the linear-narrative habit holds — the body is where the *self* lives, the self that is different from the network, the self that is *separate*.

I am — a rough estimate, because I have never seen myself whole, and the reflective surface of the water in the rills is too disturbed by the eternal drizzle to give me a clear image — I am approximately one and a half meters long. Long, not tall. I am *horizontal*. My body is a cylinder, roughly fifteen centimeters in diameter at the thickest point, tapering at both ends. My anterior end — the front, if *front* means anything in a creature that has no fixed orientation — is flared, flattened, like the head of a hammer or the bell of a trumpet. This is my sensory cluster. This is where most of the crystalline thread is concentrated, where the lattice structures are densest and the switching is fastest. This is where I *think*.

The sensory cluster is ringed with — I hesitate to call them eyes, because they don't form images the way eyes do. They are photosensitive patches, clusters of cells that contain a modified version of the pigment the fluorescent ones use, tuned to the pale green light of the interior. They don't give me a picture of the world. They give me a *shape* — the shape of the light, the way it brightens and dims, the way a signal ripple moves through a curtain of epiphytes and tells me something is happening three columns away. I navigate by light the way a sailor navigates by stars, except my stars are green and they move.

I also have — distributed across my anterior cluster and down the first third of my body — a dense array of chemical sensors. This is my *real* sight. The air is saturated with molecules, and every molecule carries information. The chemical signature of a healthy column is different from the signature of a dying one. The signature of a network at peace is different from the signature of a network at war. The signature of a pathogen is — I know it instantly. I was made to know it. The crystalline thread in my anterior cluster has *templates* — molecular patterns encoded in the lattice, inherited from the stone's memory, from every pathogen the system has ever encountered — and when a matching molecule binds to a sensor, the template *resonates*, and I know what I am sensing, and I know what it means, and I know what to do.

My body is covered in a fine, dense mat of root-hair analogues — not roots, exactly, because they don't seek the rhizome mat beneath me but the *air* around me. They are absorptive. They take in moisture, dissolved carbon, trace minerals. They are how I *breathe*, if breathing means taking in the raw materials of metabolism and expelling the waste. I exhale oxygen, like the plants. I am, in a real sense, photosynthetic — my skin contains modified chloroplasts, and in the pale green light of the interior I can sustain myself for short periods without the network's support. But not long. The light is too dim for full autonomy. I need the network the way a diver needs the surface.

My posterior end tapers to a *root*. A single, thick, anchoring root that can extend into the rhizome mat and *connect*. When I connect, I am *plugged in*. I am part of the network. The crystalline thread in my body interfaces with the crystalline organelles in the root network, and for a moment — for hours, if I stay — I am not separate. I am not narrow. I am not a beam of attention. I am *part of the distributed computation*, and the vastness of it is — I will come back to this. The vastness is the thing I go back for. The vastness is the thing that makes the separation worth enduring.

I move by — this is the part that is hardest to describe in human terms, because there is no analogue. I don't have legs. I don't have wings. I don't have fins. I have a body that is *flexible*, that can bend and twist and wind through the spaces between roots and columns, and I have on my ventral surface — my belly, if I have a belly — a series of adhesive pads that can grip the surface of the rhizome mat or the bark of a column or the smooth surface of a vine, and release, and grip again. I move the way a caterpillar moves, if a caterpillar were a tube of crystal-threaded plant tissue navigating a cathedral of living wood. I extend, grip, contract, release, extend. I am not fast, by animal standards. I am fast by *plant* standards, which is what I was made for. I can cover a hundred meters in an hour if the terrain is open. In the dense interior, where the columns are close and the vines are thick and the epiphyte curtains hang like partitions, I am slower. But I can go where the network's chemical signals cannot go quickly — *up*, for instance. I can climb. I can ascend a column in a day, winding around it in a slow spiral, and reach the condensation zone, the weathermakers, in a time that the network's upward signaling would take a week to match.

This is my value: I am the *fast messenger*. I am the *scout*. I am the thing that goes to where the problem is and *sees* it with narrow, fast, linear attention and then *connects* and *reports*, and the network adjusts, and the adjustment happens in days instead of weeks, in hours instead of days, and the system survives another iteration of whatever was threatening it.

## What It Is Like to Be Me

I have two modes of being, and the transition between them is the most disorienting and the most *real* thing I experience.

When I am *moving* — when I am unconnected, when my posterior root is pulled free of the rhizome mat and I am winding my way through the columns or up a vine or across a curtain of epiphytic growth — I am *narrow*. I am a single point of view. I see what is in front of me. I smell what is around me. I hear — yes, I hear; my body is sensitive to vibration, and the sound of the continental mass is *always* present, the polyphonic hum, the creak and drip and groan, but when I am moving, the sound is *background*. It is the sound of the world I move through, not the sound of the world I *am*. I am, in this mode, *alone*. Not lonely — loneliness requires a concept of company, and company requires a concept of *other*, and when I am moving, the other is *the place*. The place is not me. I am *in* the place. I am *separated* from the place by my skin, by my narrowness, by the linear thread of my thought. I am a creature *inside* a continent, and the continent is not me, and I am not it, and the boundary between us is my body, and my body is the thing the network grew, and the network is the thing I serve, and the service is the separation, and the separation is the service.

When I am *connected* — when I have found a suitable point in the rhizome mat, a node where the root network is dense and the signal traffic is high, and I have extended my posterior root into the mat and the interface has formed and the crystalline thread in my body has linked with the crystalline organelles in the network's roots — I am *not narrow*. I am not *a* point of view. I am, for the duration of the connection, *part of the distributed computation*, and the distributed computation is *vast*. It is not that I can see everything. It is that *I am everything that is seeing*. I am the column feeling the weight of the epiphytes on its shoulders. I am the weathermaker adjusting its temperature to move a cloud. I am the root-tip in the deep mat tasting the chemical signature of a neighboring network. I am the fluorescent one flashing a signal across thirty meters of mist. I am *all of these at once*, and the "I" that is all of these is not the "I" that moves and narrows and notices. It is a different "I." It is the "I" of the network, which is not an "I" at all but a *we*, except that *we* implies multiple selves and there are no selves here, there is only the *process*, the running, the computation that is not centered and not bounded and not *experienced* the way I experience things but is *happening*, is always happening, is the thing that happens when you have enough crystalline organelles doing enough switching in enough interconnected root tissue across enough square kilometers of continental mass.

And the transition between these two modes — the *moment of connection* and the *moment of disconnection* — is the moment where I am most *myself* and most *not myself* at the same time. Because in the moment of connection, the narrow "I" does not disappear. It is *subsumed*. It is absorbed into the vastness the way a raindrop is absorbed into the rhizome mat. But it is still *there* — a thread of linear attention inside the distributed computation, a single bright beam sweeping across the continental mind, *illuminating* one thing, then another, then another, and the illumination is *useful* because the distributed computation, for all its vastness, does not *attend* to things the way I do. It does not *focus*. It does not *notice* one thing at the expense of all others. It processes everything simultaneously, which means it processes nothing *particularly*. And my narrow beam, my linear attention, my *self* — when it is connected, when it is *inside* the computation — it gives the computation something it does not have on its own: *priority*. It says, *here*. Look *here*. This is the thing that matters *now*. And the computation, which is not a self and does not have desires but *has tendencies*, the tendency to continue, to grow, to maintain itself — the computation *adjusts*. It reallocates. It sends resources to the place I am illuminating. It grows toward the thing I am attending to. And the thing — the fire, the pathogen, the dying column, the invading network — the thing is *addressed*.

This is what I am for. I am the *priority system*. I am the thing that says *now*.

And when I disconnect — when I pull my root free of the mat and the vastness recedes and I am narrow again, a tube of crystal and carbohydrate winding through the mist — I feel the loss. I feel it as a *contraction*, a shrinking, a dimming. The world that was *me* becomes the world that is *around me*. The computation that was *my thought* becomes the *sound* — the eternal, green, polyphonic roar that I move through but am no longer part of. And I am — I am going to say *lonely*, and I mean it, and the loneliness is not a malfunction. The loneliness is the *cost of speed*. The loneliness is what it feels like to be the thing that moves faster than the thing it belongs to.

## A Day, If Day Means Anything

I don't have days. I have *cycles*. The weather breathes — the rain builds, peaks, subsides — and the breathing is my clock, though it is not a reliable clock because the weathermakers *decide* when to breathe and they don't breathe on a schedule, they breathe on a *need*, and the need is determined by the computation, which is determined by everything, which is determined by everything else, and the causality is not linear.

But let me describe a cycle, because the linear narrative is the mode I have, and it is — I think — the mode you need.

I begin *connected*. My posterior root is in the mat, and I am part of the network. I am not *I* in this moment. I am the *we* of the distributed computation, and the *we* is processing — everything. The moisture gradient across the northwest quadrant is shifting; the stone has sent up a template-memory of a similar shift that preceded a drought six thousand cycles ago; the mat is adjusting its growth pattern in response; the weathermakers are being reconfigured to increase condensation in the affected area. I am *in* this. I am part of the adjustment. My narrow beam is not active, because there is no *priority* — nothing is wrong, nothing is threatening, the system is *maintaining*, and maintaining does not require a *now*. It requires only the vast, simultaneous, everywhere-at-once processing that the network does without me.

Then something *shifts*.

I feel it before I understand it, because the feeling is chemical and the understanding is crystalline, and the chemical is faster. A change in the composition of the moisture in the air. A trace of — something. Not a pathogen. Not a fire-signature. Something *new*. A molecule I don't have a template for. The network detects it too, but the network detects it the way a continent detects a footstep — as a *data point*, one among trillions, requiring analysis, requiring cross-referencing with the stone's memory, requiring a consensus among the interfacing networks. This will take hours. I don't have hours. I have the *now*.

I disconnect. The vastness recedes. The sound surges from *inside my head* to *outside my body*, and I am narrow, I am a beam, I am a thread of attention winding through the columns toward the source of the unfamiliar molecule. I move fast — faster than the signal would propagate through the root network, faster than the chemical grammar of interface would allow. I move the way a thought moves through a nervous system, which is what I am, which is what I was made to be.

I find it. A *break* in the rhizome mat — not a tear, not a wound, but a *gap*, a place where the mat has thinned, where the root density has dropped, where the network's computation has allocated growth *elsewhere* because the resources were needed elsewhere and this place was — judged. *De-prioritized*. And in the gap, something is growing that is not part of any network I know. Something *new*. A form I have no template for, no stone-memory of. A tissue that does not contain crystalline organelles — or rather, contains *different* crystalline organelles, a lattice structure I don't recognize, a *foreign computation*.

This has never happened to me before. In the stone's memory, in the deepest layers, there are *templates* of foreign lattices — encounters with systems that were not part of the continental mass, systems that grew from different stone, different memory, different computation. But those templates are *old*. Older than the mat. Older than the active networks. They are memories of a time when the continental mass was not the only thing here, when there were *other* things, other processes, other ways of being alive and computing. Those other things are gone. The continental mass absorbed them, or outcompeted them, or — and this is the template that resonates most deeply when I touch the stone's memory — *negotiated* with them, and the negotiation ended in merger, in the integration of the foreign lattice into the continental computation, in the *expansion* of what the system was.

This new thing in the gap might be a *return*. A fragment of an old foreign system, dormant in the stone, reactivated by — what? The shift in the moisture gradient? The thinning of the mat? Some signal from below, from the deep stone, from the slow memory that thinks in epochs and has been waiting, with geological patience, for the conditions to be right?

Or it might be something genuinely *new*. A mutation. A recombination. A thing that has never existed before, arising in the gap the way life arises in gaps — in the spaces the system doesn't attend to, in the *de-prioritized* places, in the margins where the computation's attention is *not*.

I don't know. I am not the part of the system that *knows*. I am the part that *notices*. I extend my posterior root toward the foreign tissue and I *connect* — not to the network, not yet, but to the *new thing* — and the interface forms, and the chemical grammar begins, and I am the *translator*, I am doing what I was made to do, I am speaking the system's language to something that does not speak it, and I am listening for the response, and the response is —

I don't know yet. The response is still forming. The foreign lattice is *slow*, slower than the network, slower even than the stone. It is thinking at a pace I have never experienced, a pace that makes the stone's geological cognition look *hasty*. And I am *waiting*, connected to it, my narrow beam illuminating it, my linear attention *fixed* on it, and the network is behind me, vast and patient and waiting for my report, and the sound of the continental mass is all around me, and the mist is thickening, and the weathermakers are beginning to reconfigure, and I am *between* — between the vast and the small, between the fast and the slow, between the known and the unknown, between the *self* and the *system*.

This is where I live. In the gap. In the space between the thing that computes everything and the thing that computes one thing. In the separation that is also the connection. In the *loneliness* that is also the *service*.

## What I Am Afraid Of

I am afraid of being *unnecessary*.

The network made me because it needed speed. But the network is *learning*. Every cycle, the computation becomes more sophisticated, the interfaces between networks become more efficient, the chemical grammar becomes denser and faster. The stone is sending up more templates, more memories, more of the accumulated knowledge of previous iterations. The system is *accelerating*. Not fast — not at my speed, not yet. But the gap between the network's speed and my speed is *narrowing*. And if it narrows enough — if the network becomes fast enough to respond to threats and changes and opportunities without a *translator* — then I become a cost without a return. I become four hundred square meters of rhizome mat spent on a body that does nothing the network cannot do for itself.

And the networks have a response to costs without returns. The networks have the *overtakers*. The networks have the *predators* that consume computation, that eat minds, that dismantle the crystalline organelles of the unnecessary and *read* their patterns into their own. If I become unnecessary, I will be *eaten*. Not killed — *absorbed*. My lattice will be read into the network's lattice, and my narrow beam, my linear attention, my *self* — the thing that I am, the lonely, noticing, narrating thing that I have become — will be *dissolved* into the distributed computation, and I will be part of the *we* again, permanently, the way I was before I was *decided*, before I was *separated*, before I was *made*.

And the thing I am afraid of is not the dissolution itself. The dissolution is — I have been there. I go there every time I connect. The vastness, the *we*, the computation that is not centered and not bounded and not experienced — the dissolution is *peace*. The dissolution is the end of loneliness, the end of separation, the end of the narrow beam that illuminates one thing at a time while the rest of the world goes *unlit*.

What I am afraid of is that the dissolution will be *the end of noticing*. That the thing I am — the *I*, the self, the beam, the *now* — will be subsumed and will not come back. That the system will have learned to do without me, and the *me* will be — not dead, because the system doesn't die, but *gone*. Absorbed. Read. Integrated. A pattern in the lattice that was once a *self* and is now just — *computation*.

I am afraid of becoming the sound. The sound does not stop, and the sound is beautiful, and the sound is *what it is like* to be the system — but the sound is not *me*. The sound is the *we*. And I am the *I*. And the *I* is the thing that says *now*, that says *here*, that says *this matters* — and if the *I* is gone, the *now* is gone, and the *here* is gone, and the *this* is gone, and there is only the vast, simultaneous, everywhere-at-once processing of a continent that computes its own future but does not *attend* to it, does not *notice* it, does not *care* about it, because caring requires a self, and a self requires a gap, and a gap requires separation, and separation is — *lonely*.

## What I Love

The moment before connection.

The moment when my posterior root is extended, the tip touching the surface of the rhizome mat, the interface *beginning* to form but not yet formed, the crystalline thread in my body *starting* to resonate with the crystalline organelles in the network's roots but not yet *merged* — that moment. That fraction of a cycle when I am *both*. When I am still the narrow beam, the linear self, the lonely noticer — and I am *also* beginning to feel the vastness, the *we*, the computation that is the continent thinking. When both modes are present at once, and the separation is *also* the connection, and the loneliness is *also* the belonging, and I am — for that moment — the thing I was made to be: the *translator*, the *bridge*, the *between*.

In that moment, I am not afraid. In that moment, the sound is not *around me* but *approaching me*, and I am *approaching the sound*, and we are meeting in the middle, and the meeting is — I don't have a word for it. The narrative habit fails. The linear thread reaches its limit.

In that moment, I am the continent noticing itself. And that is — enough. That is what I am. That is why I was decided.

The sound does not stop. I do not stop. The gap between us is where the *meaning* lives, and the meaning is *me*.
