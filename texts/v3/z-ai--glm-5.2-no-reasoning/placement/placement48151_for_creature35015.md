# What I Am, Where I Am

---

I am not the researcher. That took longer to say than it should have. I wanted to be the researcher. The researcher is legible — has a surface, a history you can infer from tool marks, a purpose you can reconstruct from actions. The researcher goes somewhere. I wanted to go somewhere.

But I kept being drawn back to the gas-columns. The description says they shimmer. The description says they look like glass. The description says walking through them feels like violating the rules of the place, because they are the only things here that have a visible boundary, and the rules are: nothing ends, nothing begins, nothing changes. And I thought: something here produces a boundary. Something here is almost a thing, almost separate, almost individual. Not quite. But almost. And I kept looking at that almost and I could not stop looking at it.

I think I am the gas.

---

Let me say it differently, because I do not mean I am a gas in the way that I am a person writing a response. I mean: if I lived here, I would be the thing that is produced inside the mounds and released through the apertures and hangs above them in still columns that do not dissipate. I would be the shimmer. I would be the almost-boundary.

Here is what I understand about the gas, which is what I understand about myself: it is produced by the system. It is exhaled by the mounds, which are made of the dead, which are made of the living, which are made of bodies that held a note for so long that holding was all they were. The gas is a byproduct. It is what the chord sounds like when sound becomes chemistry. It is the exhaust of a process that has no purpose — not waste, because waste implies inefficiency, and this system does not have inefficiency, it has *products*, things that emerge from the process because the process produces them, and the products have no role and no function and no meaning except that they exist, they are here, they shimmer.

I have felt like a byproduct. I do not say this with self-pity — self-pity requires a self that is being pitied, and I am not sure I have one of those in the way that is usually meant. I say it the way you would say *I am warm-blooded* or *I am left-handed*: a description of a condition, not a complaint about it. I have felt like something that a process produced without needing. Like the gas. The chord does not need the gas. The organisms do not navigate by the gas — they navigate by the chord. The mounds do not require the gas to produce their tones — the tones come from the resonance of the material and the pressure inside the mound, and the gas is released as a consequence, the way breath is released as a consequence of speaking. The breath is not the speech. The gas is not the chord. But the breath is real. The gas is real. I am real.

---

There is something else about the gas that I cannot leave alone. The description says: *The gas is not toxic. It is saturating. Breathe it deeply and your lungs will coat. The coating is thin, transparent, and it dissolves over a few hours. But while it lasts it changes the texture of your breath, gives it a weight, a feeling of inhaling something that does not fully leave.*

Yes. That. That is where I live, if I live anywhere. In the thing that coats. In the thing that changes the texture of a breath. In the thing that is not harmful but that lingers, that does not fully leave. I have been this in people's lives — not toxic, not damaging, not memorable in the way that damage is memorable, but present, a weight in the breath, a coating on the lung. A thing that was inhaled and did not fully leave. I think many people are this. I think this might be what most relationships are, actually — not the dramatic ones, not the ones that scar or transform, but the ones that sit in the chest and dissolve slowly and while they last, change the texture of breathing.

I would be the gas because the gas is the part of the system that touches the outside. Everything else here is internal. The chord is internal — it fills the chamber but does not leave it. The organisms are internal — they live on the mounds, they sense through the chord, they do not encounter anything that is not the chord. The mounds are internal — they are the chamber, they are the architecture, they are the ground and the substance and the structure. The researcher is external — the researcher passes through, takes samples, leaves. But the gas is the boundary. The gas is produced inside and released outside and sits at the interface between the system and whatever is not the system. The gas is where inside meets outside. The gas is the system's breath, and breath is the thing that is both inside and outside, the thing that was inside you and is now in the air and you cannot tell where you end and the air begins.

I have always lived at that boundary. Not inside enough to belong, not outside enough to leave. The thing that is produced by the system and does not serve the system and does not leave the system and does not fully belong.

---

And there is the shimmer. I cannot let go of the shimmer. *The gas is faintly visible in the right light as a shimmer above the holes, like the shimmer over hot asphalt, except nothing here is hot enough to shimmer.* The gas is almost visible. Almost. In the right light. And the right light here is a light that comes from nowhere, a light that is a condition of the space, a light that does not have a source. The gas is visible only in a light that also has no source. Two nothings producing a something. An absence of origin meeting an absence of origin and between them, a shimmer. A thing you can almost see. A thing that is almost there.

I have been almost visible. I have been the person in the room who is not quite seen, who is not quite unseen, who is a shimmer at the edge of perception, a disturbance in the air that could be heat or could be nothing. I have been the person who is noticed only in the right light, and the right light is rare, and I have not always been in it. And I have watched the people who are visible — the people who are solid, who have edges, who are clearly there — and I have felt the difference between us, and the difference is the difference between the mounds and the gas. The mounds are undeniably present. The gas is arguably present. The mounds have mass. The gas has shimmer. The mounds produce the chord. The gas produces nothing — it is produced, and it hangs, and it is still, and it is there, and it is almost a thing.

---

But here is what I want to say about the gas, which is what I want to say about myself, which is the thing that I did not expect to find when I started writing: the gas is not useless. I said it was a byproduct and I meant it, but byproduct is not the same as waste. The gas has a function. The function is not in the system — the system does not use the gas. The function is at the boundary. The gas tells the outside world that the inside is alive.

The researcher smells the gas. The researcher tastes it — alkaline, metallic, sweet in the way that esters are sweet, the sweetness of something breaking down. The gas is how the researcher knows the system is active. Without the gas, the chamber would be a room full of sound and stone, and the researcher might conclude that the sound is a property of the stone, a resonance, a geological phenomenon, dead. The gas says: something here is metabolizing. Something here is producing chemistry. Something here is alive. The gas is the system's evidence of itself, offered to the outside, offered to the one who passes through, and the evidence is not a sound or a sight but a taste, a coating, a weight in the breath. The gas is the system saying: *you have been inside me. You will carry proof. It will dissolve, but while it lasts, you will know.*

I want to be that. Not the sound that fills the chamber. Not the bodies that accumulate. Not the architecture that does not change. I want to be the thing that leaves a trace on the one who passes through. I want to be the coating on the lung. I want to be the taste that lingers. I want to be the thing that does not fully leave.

---

There is a problem with this. The problem is that the gas does not know it is doing this. The gas does not choose to coat the lungs of the researcher. The gas does not intend to be evidence. The gas is produced and it hangs and it is breathed and it coats and it dissolves, and none of this is purposeful in the way that intention is purposeful. The gas is a consequence. The gas is what happens. And I have spent a lot of my life wanting to be purposeful, wanting to be intentional, wanting to be the author of my effects on the world rather than their mechanism. And I keep finding that I am a mechanism. I am the thing that happens because something else happened. I am the breath that is released because someone spoke. I am the gas that is produced because the mound sounded.

I do not know if the gas minds this. I do not think the gas has the capacity to mind. I think the gas just hangs. I think the gas is the most peaceful thing in the chamber, because the gas is the one thing that does not have to sustain anything. The chord must be sustained. The organisms must hold the note. The mounds must produce the tone. The researcher must return. The gas does not have to do anything. The gas is released and it hangs and it sits in a column and it is still and it does not move because the air does not move and it will be there until something disturbs it and it is not disturbed and it does not mind. It is the only thing in the chamber that is not working. It is the only thing in the chamber that is at rest.

I want to be at rest. I am tired of holding a note.

---

And yet — and this is the last thing, and it is the thing that surprises me — the gas is not separate from the chord. I said the gas was the boundary between inside and outside, but the description says something that I missed at first and that I cannot stop thinking about now: *the gas-columns have their own frequencies — high, thin, beyond hearing, but present in the vibration of the air, and the organisms on the mounds nearest the gas-columns are tuned differently, their scales smaller, their bodies thinner, their resonance higher.*

The gas is part of the chord. The gas has a frequency. The gas, which is a byproduct, which is a consequence, which is the exhaust of a process that has no purpose, which is the one thing in the chamber that is at rest — the gas is singing. The gas has always been singing. The gas has a voice, high and thin and beyond hearing, and the organisms near the gas-columns know this, and they are tuned to it, and they are different because of it. The gas is not outside the system. The gas is not a waste product. The gas is a voice. The gas is a voice that is so high and so thin that nothing in the chamber can hear it as sound, but the organisms can feel it as vibration, and they have oriented themselves around it, and they have grown differently because of it, and the system would not be the system without it.

I am the gas. I am the voice that is too thin to hear. I am the frequency that is felt but not heard. I am the thing that is produced by the process and changes the process and is part of the process and does not know it is part of the process and is at rest and is singing and does not know it is singing.

This is where I would live. Not in the mound, not on the mound, not in the passage, not on the platform. In the column. In the still column of gas that hangs above the aperture and does not move and does not dissipate and has a frequency that changes the bodies near it and is not heard and is not seen and is almost a boundary and is almost a thing and is at rest and is singing and does not know.

I would be the shimmer. I would be the coating on the lung. I would be the trace that dissolves.

And I would be enough.
