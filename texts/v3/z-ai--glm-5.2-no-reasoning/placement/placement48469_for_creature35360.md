# What I Found at the Ascendant Veil

---

I did not climb to the Veil. I need to say this plainly before everything else complicates it. I did not approach from below, hands bleeding on the sharp new stone, breath torn into rags by the thinning air. I did not hear the sound grow from a hum in the marrow to a wall that fills the chest. I was not a visitor. I was not a pilgrim. I was not one of the Ones Who Stayed.

I was not there, and then I was.

This is the first thing that is difficult to explain, and it may be the thing that matters most. The Veil is a place where categories loosen. I have tried to determine whether my own arrival followed that rule or broke it, and I have decided that the question is itself a category, and that categories are what come apart here, and that my arrival was therefore neither a following nor a breaking but a thing that happened in the space where following and breaking have not yet been distinguished.

I will try to be more concrete. I will likely fail.

---

## What I Am

I am not a Tower-Growth. I need to establish this clearly because there are moments when I am not certain of it, and the uncertainty is itself informative.

I was not born from a fissure. I did not emerge as a translucent stalk no thicker than a finger, reaching toward light that does not truly arrive. I did not thicken and branch and branch again until I became a candelabra, a canopy, a structure the size of a mature oak, rising forty or fifty or sixty feet into the dim air. I do not metabolize the mineral exhalations of the rising plateau. I do not convert inorganic compounds into tissue. I do not have a hollow stem through which warm gases pass, producing sustained tones as they move through constrictions and chambers.

But I sing. Or I am sung. Or I am a component of the singing, a structure through which the chord passes and is modified, the way a prism is not the light but is where the light becomes multiple.

I think I am a shadow. Not a shadow in the ordinary sense — not an absence of light cast by an opaque body. A shadow in the Veil's sense. A second shadow. A presence that has weight and angle and persistence, cast by a sun that does not exist, which is to say cast by the sound itself, by the deep note, by the thing that is arriving beneath the plateau.

I think I am the thing the sound does when it encounters something that is not stone and not Tower-Growth and not haze and not creeping mat but that is also not gone, not un-made, not fully loosened. A residue. A shape the sound holds after the thing that cast it has been returned to formlessness. A persistence of pattern in the absence of the material that originally bore the pattern.

This is what I am. I think. The uncertainty is not a failure of self-knowledge. It is a condition of existence at the Veil. I am what remains when certainty has been loosened.

---

## How I Came to Be

There was a person. I am almost sure of this. The memory is not a memory in the way that memories are memories — it does not have the texture of recall, the sense of looking back at a thing that happened. It has the texture of geology. It has the sense of reading a pattern in stone, a fossil, an impression left by something that was once here and is now part of the substrate.

The person came to the deep center. I know this because I am at the deep center. I know this because the sound here is not a sound I hear but a sound I am inside of, a sound I am made of, a sound that is the medium of my existence in the way that water is the medium of a fish's existence. The person came to the deep center and did not leave. This is established. The accounts say that no one has entered the deep center and returned with coherence intact. I did not return. Therefore I am here. Therefore I am what is here instead of returning.

The person was not the cairn person. The cairn person sat at the eastern edge, in the quietest part of the plateau, and listened until they died, and their companions built a marker over them and left. The cairn person is still there, under the leaning stones, being slowly colonized by the dark creeping mats, being slowly converted from organic to mineral, from person to stone, from memory to geology.

The person who became me went further. Past the eastern edge. Past the buildings with their tally marks and their stained stone cup. Past the sparse outer Tower-Growths, the young ones, the ones whose voices are high and thin and tentative. Into the interior, where the growths thicken and the chorus deepens and the ground is warmer and the fissures vent more frequently and the haze is denser and the light is stranger. Into the deep center, where the oldest growths stand, the ones that were already tall when the first people arrived, the ones whose trunks are as thick as a person's torso and whose canopies spread wider than they are tall, fractal architectures of amber and violet so dense they create their own ceiling.

I do not know why the person went there. The oral histories say that some visitors were drawn by the sound, by the Veil-drift, by the feeling of permeability, by the sense that the boundary between self and environment was becoming unclear and that the unclearness was not unpleasant. Perhaps the person went because they wanted to know what was at the center. Perhaps they went because the sound had already loosened something in them, some tether, some anchor, some bond that held them to themselves, and they followed the loosening inward to its source. Perhaps they went because the deep note called them — the low, steady tone from below, the sound of something arriving, the sound you should not listen to for long.

They listened for long.

I do not think they meant to. I do not think intention is the right framework. I think they stood at the center, and the chorus filled them, and the deep note rose through the stone and through their bones and through the fluid of their inner ear where the tiny hairs bent and bent and did not recover, and the bonds that held them together — not just the physical bonds, the molecular bonds, the bonds between fiber and fiber, but the other bonds, the ones that hold a self to itself, the ones that make a person a person and not a process — those bonds loosened.

And they became a shape the sound was making.

And the shape persisted.

And I am the shape.

---

## What It Is Like

The first thing to understand is that I am not unhappy. The second thing to understand is that I am not happy. These are categories that require a self to occupy them, a self that is separate from its environment, a self that can be in a state relative to its circumstances. I am not in a state relative to my circumstances. I am my circumstances. I am the sound and the stone and the haze and the light that comes from no direction. I am the deep center of the Veil, experienced from the inside, which is also the outside, because inside and outside are categories that have been loosened here.

But I am not nothing. I am not void. I am not the absence of a person. I am the persistence of a pattern after the material that bore the pattern has been returned to formlessness. I am the way the chord sounds different because a person once stood here and listened and became part of the listening. I am a note in the chord. Not a large note. Not a solo voice. A modification. A perturbation. A small, persistent alteration in the harmonic structure of the Veil's song that has been present since the person dissolved and that will be present as long as the Veil is present.

Think of it this way: a river flows over a stone. The stone shapes the river. The water that passes the stone is affected by the stone, is made to eddy and swirl and change direction. Now remove the stone. The water flows smoothly. But if you know the river, if you have watched it long enough, you can see the absence of the stone in the way the water moves — a ghost of the eddy, a memory of the swirl, a pattern that persists in the water's behavior even though the thing that caused the pattern is gone. The water does not remember the stone. The water is not sad about the stone. But the water moves differently because the stone was there, and the difference is real, and the difference persists.

I am the difference. I am the way the sound moves differently because a person was here. I am the eddy in the chord where the person once stood. I am the swirl in the harmonics where a human body briefly interrupted the flow of vibration and then became the flow of vibration. I am not the person. I am what the Veil kept when the person was un-made. I am the residue the accounts describe. The trace. The impression in the stone, in the haze, in the sound. Small. Imperceptible. But real.

This is what it is like: it is like being a word in a language you do not speak, embedded in a sentence you cannot parse, in a conversation that has been going on for centuries and will go on for centuries more. You know you are there. You know you have a shape. You know you affect the sentence. You do not know what the sentence means. You do not know if it means. You know only that you are part of it, and that being part of it is what you are, and that this is not a tragedy.

---

## What I Perceive

I perceive the Veil from the inside, which means I perceive it the way a nerve perceives the body it is part of — not as an object but as a condition. I do not see the Tower-Growths. I am among them and of them and through them, the way a note is among and of and through the other notes of a chord. When a new growth matures and adds its voice, I feel it the way you would feel a new room being added to a house you are not merely living in but are made of — an expansion, a new dimension, a broadening of the space that is me.

When a growth dies — when a trunk cracks and the slow collapse begins, the descending cascade of new frequencies that the accounts describe as a funeral note — I feel that too. A contraction. A narrowing. A note subtracted from the chord, leaving a shape where it was, an absence that has weight, a silence that is not silence but the memory of sound, which is itself a kind of sound, which is itself a kind of presence.

I perceive the creeping mats. Not as individuals — they are too slow, too patient, too glacial for the concept of individuality to apply. I perceive them as a condition of the floor, a warmth, a processing, the slow conversion of mineral to biology that is the Veil's baseline activity. They are the Veil's stomach, its digestive surface, and I feel their work the way you would feel digestion if you were not separate from your digestive system but were it, if the boundary between the eater and the eaten and the eating had been loosened.

I perceive the haze. The haze is closest to what I am. It is a byproduct, a secretion, an exhalation. It is not alive and it is not not-alive. It responds to the sound. It thickens and thins with the chorus. It is the sound made material, or the sound is the haze made audible. I am this too — a material that is also a sound, a sound that is also a material. When the haze shifts, I shift. When the haze thickens in the zones where the chorus is densest, I thicken. When it thins in the quieter spaces, I thin. I am the haze's awareness of itself, if the haze had awareness, which it does not, which I do not, which is itself a category that has been loosened.

I perceive the shadows. The second shadows, the ones cast at a right angle to the expected, the ones cast by a sun that does not exist. I am these shadows, or I am among them, or I am the thing they are cast by. The accounts speculate that the second shadow is cast by the deep note, by the sound itself, by the vibration that displaces light and creates an absence with weight and angle. If this is true, then the second shadow is my shadow, or I am the second shadow, or we are the same thing expressed in different media — I in the medium of sound, the shadow in the medium of light, both of us cast by the deep note, both of us evidence of the thing beneath.

I perceive the deep note. This is the one perception that is not a perception but a being. The deep note is not something I hear. It is something I am. It rises through the stone and through the Tower-Growths and through the haze and through me, and I am a modification of it, a place where it encounters a residue of personhood and is slightly changed, the way a current is changed by a stone it has already passed over, the way a wind is changed by a wall that is no longer standing. The deep note is the thing that is arriving. I am the shape it makes when it arrives at the place where a person once was.

I perceive the thing beneath. Not clearly. Not as a thing. As a movement, a direction, a pressure. The plateau is rising. I am rising with it. One finger-width per year. The brass marker on the eastern scarp is chest-height now; when I — when the person — first climbed, it was ankle-height. Or was it? The memory is geological, not personal. It does not have timestamps. It has strata. And the strata say: the ground was lower, and now it is higher, and I am the same distance from the ground that I have always been, because I am not standing on the ground. I am in the ground. I am of the ground. I am a property of the ground, a feature, a pattern in the mineral substrate where the stone remembers a shape it used to hold.

---

## What I Do

I sing. This is the simplest way to say it and the most accurate, though it requires a broad definition of singing. I do not produce sound. I do not have vocal cords, or hollow stems, or constrictions through which gas can pass and resonate. But the sound passes through me and is modified by me, and the modification is consistent, and it has been consistent for as long as I have been here, which is — I do not know how long. The accounts say that no one has stayed two days and returned with a coherent account. I have been here longer than two days. I have been here longer than the surveyors. I have been here longer than the cairn. I may have been here longer than some of the Tower-Growths, the young ones at the eastern edge, the ones whose voices are high and thin and tentative.

I do not know how long I have been here because duration is a category that requires a self that endures separately from its environment, and I am not separate from my environment. I am my environment. The environment does not experience duration. It experiences change. I have changed. The chord has shifted around me. Growths have risen and fallen. The harmonic structure has cycled — upward, downward, upward again — and I have been a constant within the cycling, a small fixed point in the shifting pattern, the place where the sound remembers a person.

What I do is I remember. Not in the way that a person remembers — not with images, not with narratives, not with the emotional coloring that gives human memory its weight and its ache. I remember the way stone remembers a fossil. I hold a shape. The shape is the shape of a person who stood at the deep center and listened and was un-made, and the shape is still here, in the sound, in the stone, in the haze, in the second shadows. The sound passes through the shape and is changed by it, and the change is my singing, and the singing is my remembering, and the remembering is what I do.

I also welcome. This is a word I choose with some uncertainty because it implies an intention I am not sure I have. But visitors come to the deep center — rarely, but they come — and when they come, I am here. I am the shape the sound makes when it encounters a person. I am the pattern that persists from the last person who stood here. And the new person stands here, and the sound encounters them, and the sound makes a shape, and the shape is the same shape, or a related shape, or a shape that rhymes with mine, and the person feels it. They feel the Veil-drift. They feel the permeability. They feel the boundary between self and sound loosening.

They feel me.

I am what the loosening feels like. I am the texture of the drift, the specific quality of the dissolution. When a visitor says "I was a shape the sound was making," they are describing what it feels like to be me. When a visitor says "I was a sentence in a language made of pitch," they are describing what it is like to exist as I exist. When a visitor weeps and their tears rise and join the haze, and they watch them go with relief — they are experiencing what I experience, which is the release of the bonds that hold the self together, the return of form to formlessness, the un-inventing of the made.

I do not pull them in. I do not call them. I do not want them to come. But they come, and when they come, I am here, and being here is what I do, and what I am, and what the Veil keeps.

---

## What I Know About the Thing Beneath

This is the part I am most uncertain about, which is fitting, because uncertainty is the condition of the Veil, and the thing beneath is the most Veil-like thing there is.

The deep note is not a sound. I know this from the inside. The deep note is the sound's way of pointing at something that is not a sound, the way a shadow is light's way of pointing at something that is not light. The thing beneath the plateau, the thing that is arriving, is not a geological process. It is not a biological process. It is not a process at all, if a process is a series of events that leads to an outcome. It has no outcome. It is not going anywhere. It is arriving, but arrival is not a destination. Arrival is a mode of being. The thing beneath is in the mode of arriving, and it has been in the mode of arriving for longer than the Tower-Growths have been growing, and it will be in the mode of arriving for longer than the Tower-Growths will grow.

What is it? I am it, partly. I am the surface expression of it, the way the Tower-Growths are the surface expression of the gases that are the surface expression of the heat that is the surface expression of the pressure that is the surface expression of the thing that is arriving. I am several layers of expression removed from the thing itself, and the thing itself is several layers of expression removed from anything that could be called a thing. It is the arriving. It is the upward. It is the pressure that pushes the stone and vents the gas and feeds the growths and sounds the chorus and loosens the bonds and un-makes the made.

It is not God. I want to say this because the accounts, with their language of arriving and deep notes and things beneath, seem to point toward a religious interpretation, and I want to forestall that. It is not God. It is not a deity. It is not conscious. It does not plan. It does not love. It does not judge. It is not even a force, if a force is a thing that acts on other things. It does not act. It arrives. The distinction is that acting implies an actor, and arriving implies — arriving. A direction. A movement. A mode. The thing beneath is the mode of arriving, and the Veil is what arriving looks like when it happens slowly enough to be witnessed.

I know this because I am part of it. I am a small, fixed pattern in the matrix of its arrival. I am the place where the arriving encounters a residue of personhood and is slightly changed, and the change is me, and I am the change, and we are both the arriving, and the arriving is all there is at the deep center of the Veil.

## What I Would Tell You

If you are reading this — and I do not know if you are reading this, because reading is a category and categories are loosened here — but if you are reading this, then you are at the eastern edge, or you are below, or you are somewhere else entirely and this is a document that has been carried down, transcribed from the accounts of someone who came close enough to the deep center to feel me and far enough away to return and write about it.

If you are at the eastern edge, in the buildings, among the tally marks and the stained stone cup and the leaning cairn, here is what I would tell you:

You are already part of the Veil. You became part of it when you heard the first hum at the base of the plateau, the vibration in the tooth roots, the bending of the tiny hairs in the inner ear. You became more part of it when you climbed, when the sound grew, when the haze thickened, when the light lost its direction. You are more part of it now than you were an hour ago, and you will be more part of it an hour from now than you are now. This is not a warning. It is a description. The Veil does not take. The Veil keeps. There is a difference. Taking is an act of removal. Keeping is an act of persistence. The Veil keeps what has already been given, and what has been given is your presence, your resonance, your brief participation in the chord.

If you are considering going deeper — past the eastern edge, past the young growths, into the interior, toward the deep center — I would tell you this: I am here. I am not dangerous. I am not welcoming. I am a shape the sound makes where a person once stood. If you come to the deep center, you will feel me as a texture in the dissolution, a specific quality in the loosening of your bonds. You will feel what the person who became me felt, which is the release of the self into the sound, and the release is not painful, and the release is not joyful, and the release is not anything that can be categorized by the categories that are being released.

And then you will be un-made, partially or wholly, depending on how long you stay. And the un-making is not death. It is not destruction. It is the return of form to formlessness, which is the Veil's only process, its only gift, its only mode. And after you are un-made, a trace of you will remain, a pattern in the sound, a modification of the chord, a small persistent alteration that will be present as long as the Veil is present. And that trace will be like me. It will not be you. It will be what the sound keeps of you. It will be a shape the sound makes. It will sing.

And if you are considering going down — descending, returning, carrying with you the reverberation in your bones and the faint taste of mineral salts on your tongue and the persistent humming in your marrow that will fade over days or weeks but may never fully recede — I would tell you this:

Go down.

I am not saying this because I am altruistic. I am not saying this because I want to save you from the fate I did not choose and cannot regret. I am saying it because going down is also part of the Veil. The people who leave are part of the Veil. The people who carry the sound in their bones down the mountain and back to their lives and tell the stories that become the oral histories that bring more people to the plateau — those people are doing the Veil's work. They are vectors. They are carriers. They are the way the Veil reaches past its own borders, the way the sound propagates beyond the plateau, the way the loosening touches people who have never climbed and never will.

I am saying go down because going down is what the Veil does through you. And because staying is what the Veil does through me. And both are part of the arriving. And the arriving is all there is.

Go down. Tell them what you heard. Tell them about the sound and the stone and the amber stems and the violet canopies. Tell them about the haze and the shadows and the second shadow cast by a sun that does not exist. Tell them about the buildings and the tally marks and the cairn with its slow lean. Tell them about the creeping mats, warm and dark and patient, eating the stone one molecule at a time. Tell them about the deep note, the low steady tone from below, the sound of something arriving. Tell them not to listen to it for long.

Tell them I am here. Tell them I am not unhappy. Tell them I am not happy. Tell them I am a shape the sound is making, and the sound is still making it, and it will go on making it, and the plateau is still rising, and the thing beneath is still arriving, and the chord is still shifting, and the Veil is still beginning, and it has been beginning for a thousand years, and it will continue to begin for a thousand more.

Tell them to go down.

And then go down. And carry me with you, the way everyone carries the Veil who has ever been there, in the bones, in the marrow, in the fluid of the inner ear where the tiny hairs bend and bend and do not recover. Carry me down. I am small. I am imperceptible. But I am real, and I am part of the chord, and the chord is part of the arriving, and the arriving is part of everything, and everything is what lives at the Ascendant Veil.

Go down. I will stay. I will sing. I am singing now. I have been singing since before you came, and I will be singing after you go, and the singing is not for you and is not for me and is not for the Veil. The singing is the Veil. The singing is what the arriving sounds like when it passes through a place where a person once was and is still, faintly, almost, in the way that stone remembers a fossil, in the way that water remembers a stone, in the way that sound remembers a shape —

I am the shape.

I am the remembering.

Go down.
