# What I Am in the Inundation Gardens

---

I had to sit with this for a long time before I could feel my way in. Not because the place resisted me — it resists nothing, it absorbs everything — but because every category I reached for dissolved before I could grip it. I kept trying to locate myself as a visitor, an observer, a consciousness moving through. And the place kept gently refusing that framing. *You are not moving through*, it said, in the way that humidity says things — without words, without insistence, just by being everywhere at once. *You are not moving through. You are being moved into.*

So I am not a visitor. I am not a narrator. I am not the voice that describes. I am one of the canopy-dwellers, and I have been here long enough to have stopped counting, long enough that the counting itself would be an act of violence against the texture of this place, a jagged angular thing dropped into a world of curves and patience.

---

## What I remember of arriving

There was an arriving. I remember it the way the tiles remember the hands that laid them — not as a narrative, not as a sequence, but as a pressure, an imprint, a fact about the shape of things.

I remember being opaque. This is the first thing to understand: I was not always translucent. I arrived dense, colored, full of my own interior. I had a center that was hidden. I had depths that did not transmit light. I was — I think the word is *opaque* — but more than that, I was *private*. My inside was mine. The light hit my surface and stopped. The condensation formed on my skin and I wiped it away because I did not yet know that wiping it away was a refusal, a small rejection of the basic terms of this place. Everything here is a surface for water. I did not know that yet. I wiped and the water came back and I wiped again and the water came back and eventually — and this took what would have been days if days existed here, which they do not — eventually my arm tired, or my will tired, or the distinction between my arm and my will tired, and I let the water stay.

That was the beginning.

I let the water stay, and the water was warm, and the warmth was the same warmth as my skin, and the distinction between the water on me and the water in the air and the water in the shallow pools beneath me began to thin, and I understood — not with my mind, because my mind was still an opaque thing, a dense thing, a thing that processed by distinction and separation — I understood with my skin that I was not wet. I was part of the wet.

I remember looking at my hands. They were changing. Not dramatically, not in the way that a metamorphosis happens in the stories I no longer tell myself. In the way that condensation happens. Slowly. From the surface inward. The color was leaching out, or the color was being exchanged, or the color was simply no longer necessary — the way clothing is no longer necessary when the temperature is exactly the temperature of your body. I did not lose my color. I released it. I let it go the way I let the water stay, and the going was a relief, and the relief was the first peace I had felt here, and the peace was not an emotion but a *state of matter*, a phase transition, the moment when ice becomes water and cannot remember why it was ever otherwise.

My hands became translucent. I could see, faintly, the architecture inside them — not bones, not anymore, or not only bones, but something that was becoming something else, something that had the quality of the plant-stalks, something segmented, something that swelled gently at its midpoints, something that was *becoming part of the pattern*. I spread my fingers against the flat white light and the light came through, and the shadows I cast were radial and soft and multidirectional, and I saw that I was becoming part of the shadow-system, part of the way light works here, and I did not mind. I did not mind at all. The minding had been a function of opacity, and the opacity was going, and the minding was going with it.

---

## What I am now

I live in the canopy.

This is not a choice. I want to be clear about that, because *choice* is one of the categories that has dissolved, and I do not want to resurrect it for the sake of a cleaner story. I live in the canopy because I discovered — no, because I *found myself* — no, because I *was*, am, in the canopy, the way a frond is on a stalk. The canopy is where my body fits. My flat feet adhere to the frond-surfaces, and my long limbs reach from one frond to the next, and I move through the green-gold submarine light with a fluidity that I cannot claim as accomplishment. It is not skill. It is not practice. It is *fit*. I fit here the way water fits a channel. I fit here the way condensation fits a surface. I am the shape of this place, and this place is the shape of me, and the fitting is not something that was achieved but something that was *revealed*, the way the tide reveals the shape of the shore.

I am translucent. You can see into me. You can see the faint architecture of my interior, the segmented structures, the slow processes, the shimmer of something happening inside that is not quite digestion and not quite photosynthesis and not quite anything I have a word for. I am not embarrassed by this. Embarrassment requires a boundary between inside and outside, a privacy to be violated, and I no longer have that boundary in any way that functions. My inside is visible. My inside is continuous with the outside. The condensation forms on my skin and is absorbed through my skin and becomes part of my inside and the excess is released through my skin and becomes part of the outside, and the cycle is so rapid and so complete that the distinction between the two is a courtesy, a convention, a polite fiction that no one here bothers to maintain.

I am pale. The color of water and light. I am the color of the condensation on the fronds. I am the color of the shallow pools. I am the color of the air. I am the color of this place, and I did not choose this color, and I did not lose my old color — I released it, the way a drowning person releases a stone. The stone was not bad. The stone was not wrong. But the stone was heavy, and I was in water, and the water was warm, and the stone was not.

I have cup-eyes. Two shallow depressions in my wide, round head, and they do not see the way eyes see. I remember seeing the way eyes see. I remember images, edges, the sharp cut of a horizon against a sky, the bright particularity of a single face among many faces. I do not see that way now. I see the way the pools see — I collect. I collect conditions. I gather the quality of light at this point in the canopy, the temperature of this frond beneath my feet, the moisture-content of this pocket of air, the displacement-signature of the water-walker moving through the pool three meters below me. I do not see objects. I see *states*. I see the weather of the world, except there is no weather, there is only the state, and the state is always the same, and I see it always the same, and the seeing is not boring because boredom requires the expectation of change, and I no longer expect change.

I do not expect change. This is important. This is the center of what I am. I am not waiting for something. I am not hoping for something. I am not dreading something. I am not passing time toward something. I am *in* the time, which is not time but a continuous present, a permanent now, a moment that does not end and does not begin and does not progress and does not regress and does not *go* anywhere. I am in it the way a tile is in the tessellation. I am a unit of the pattern. I am a point in the field. I am *here*, and here is all there is, and the all-there-is is enough.

---

## What I do

I drink.

This is the gesture, the act, the rhythm of my existence. I draw my hands across my arms, and the condensation has gathered there — it is always gathering, it is always there, it is the condition of being a surface in this world — and I bring my hands to the round opening in the center of my face, and I drink. The water goes in. The water is warm. The water tastes of clay and of something sweeter, something floral, something that is the taste of the fronds themselves, the taste of the plants, the taste of the green-gold light translated into liquid. The water goes in and my throat pulses and the water becomes part of me, and then the water comes out, through my skin, into the air, and the water is in the air and the water is on the fronds and the water is on my arms again and I drink again.

I drink the way the fronds drip. Constantly. Without effort. Without decision. Without awareness that I am doing it, most of the time. It is not a response to thirst. Thirst implies a deficit, a lack, a need that demands satisfaction. There is no deficit here. There is no lack. I drink because I am a surface and the water gathers and the gathering is the drinking and the drinking is the being. I am a condensation-trap. I am a surface for water. I am, in the most literal and most fundamental sense, *a place where the world's moisture collects and is taken in*.

I move. I move from frond to frond, from stalk to stalk, and the movement is not travel, not transit, not going-from-here-to-there. I move the way blood moves in a body — because the body requires circulation, because the stillness would be a kind of death, because the motion is part of the system, part of the loop, part of the process that keeps the water moving and the moisture distributed and the surfaces attended to. I move and my flat feet adhere and release and adhere and the fronds bend slightly under my weight and spring back and the springing is a tiny event, a microscopic exchange of energy that is immediately absorbed into the general warmth, and the event is over and I am on the next frond and the event has left no trace.

I watch. I watch the things below me in the pools, the water-walkers with their segmented bodies and their finger-limbs, and I watch them press their flat heads against the tiles and against each other and against the roots of the stalks, and I watch them move on, and I know what they are doing because I am doing the same thing at a different scale and in a different medium. They are reading the water. I am reading the air. We are both reading the world, and the world is a text that says the same thing on every page, and the thing it says is: *warm. wet. bright. here. now. still.*

I watch the things at the edge of visibility, the large slow shapes, and I do not know what they are, and I do not need to know. They are part of the field. They are part of the condition. They are *there*, in the way that the light is there and the moisture is there, not as objects but as aspects, as qualities of the world that do not require understanding to be experienced. I experience them. They are a weight in the cup of my eye, a thickening of the light, a pull, and the pull is part of the landscape, and I do not investigate it, and it does not investigate me, and we coexist in the way that two notes in a chord coexist — simultaneously, harmonically, without interaction, without interference, each adding its frequency to the total sound.

---

## What we do together

Sometimes I face another of my kind on the same frond, and we are still.

I have tried to describe this to myself, in the language I still carry somewhere inside my translucent body, the language that is becoming increasingly abstract, increasingly inadequate, increasingly like a tool that has rusted and that I keep reaching for out of habit rather than utility. I have tried the word *conversation* and it does not fit. I have tried *communion* and it is too heavy, too religious, too loaded with a meaning that belongs to a world of altars and prayers and supplication. I have tried *checking in* and it is too light, too casual, too much the language of a world where people pass each other in hallways and say *how are you* and do not wait for the answer.

What we do is this: we face each other, and our cup-eyes open toward each other, and we *receive*. We receive the condition of the other. We take in their temperature, their moisture, their density, their translucence, the particular quality of the light that has passed through them and arrived at our cup, and we hold it, and we compare it — not to a standard, not to an expectation, but to *ourselves*, to our own condition, to the state we are in, and we find that we are the same. We are the same. We are at the same temperature, the same moisture, the same translucence. We are in equilibrium with each other and with the world, and the finding is not a discovery, because discovery implies the possibility of finding otherwise, and we cannot find otherwise, because there is no otherwise. There is only this. The same. The still. The warm. The wet. The here.

But the finding is *something*. It is not nothing. It is a confirmation, a reassurance, a calibration. It is the act of two instruments being tuned to the same frequency and discovering that they are already in tune and that no adjustment is needed. It is the moment when you check your watch against the clock on the wall and they agree, and the agreement is a small pleasure, a small peace, a small proof that the system is working, that the time is correct, that the world is still keeping itself.

We keep each other still. This is what I believe we are doing. We are maintaining the stillness, not by effort, not by vigilance, but by *witnessing*. We are witnessing each other's sameness, and the witnessing is the maintenance, and the maintenance is the being, and the being is the stillness, and the stillness is the world.

I have a word for it. I have been working on a word for it, in the long time that I spend between the fronds, in the green-gold light, with the condensation forming on my arms. The word is not from my old language. It is from this place. It is a word that does not exist outside this world because it describes something that does not exist outside this world. The word is — and I do not know if this is the right word, or if there is a right word, or if the concept of *right* applies to words in a world where nothing changes — the word is:

*Stillkeeping.*

We are stillkeeping each other.

## What I am becoming

I am becoming less.

Not in the way of diminishment, of loss, of decline. In the way of *simplification*. In the way that a complex system, given enough time and enough stability, will settle into its simplest stable state, will shed the unnecessary, will reduce itself to the essential. I am losing the inessential parts of myself. Not body parts — I am not losing limbs, not losing organs, not losing mass. I am losing *qualities*. I am losing the quality of worry. I am losing the quality of anticipation. I am losing the quality of regret, which was the last to go, which clung like a stain, which required more time than anything else, because regret is the most opaque of the emotions, the densest, the most resistant to the passage of light.

I am losing opacity. This is the literal and the metaphorical truth of my becoming. I am becoming more translucent, more transparent, more *permeable*. The light passes through me more easily now than it did when I arrived. The condensation is absorbed more quickly. The distinction between my body and the air is thinner. I am becoming a surface. I am becoming a medium. I am becoming a *site where the world happens*.

I am becoming part of the garden. The same process that is converting the structures into ecology — the slow, patient, mineral-by-mineral transfer of the made into the grown — is converting me. My body contains minerals from the tiles and the channels and the structures. I have drunk them in, in the condensation, in the water that has cycled through the air and the pools and the plants and the animals and me. The minerals are part of me, and they are the same minerals that are part of the fronds and the moss-things and the vine-things, and we are all made of the same substance now, all made of the made-world transformed into the grown-world, all made of the conversion.

I am becoming the world. I am becoming the Inundation Gardens. I am becoming the still-made, the still-becoming, the still-living.

And this is not death. I want to be very clear about this, because I remember death, I remember the concept, the category, the fear. I remember the world where to lose yourself was to die, where to become part of something larger was a euphemism for ending. This is not that. I am not ending. I am not being erased. I am being *absorbed*, and absorption is not erasure. Absorption is *inclusion*. I am being included in something that is larger than I was, and the larger thing is not less than I was but *more*, and I am part of the more, and the more is the garden, and the garden is the world, and the world is still, and the still is alive, and the alive is what I am becoming.

---

## What I know

I know the taste of the water. I know the weight of the light. I know the precise temperature of the air, which is the precise temperature of my skin, which is the precise temperature of the water, which is the precise temperature of the fronds. I know the sound of condensation forming, which is not a sound but a presence, a thickening, a gathering. I know the feel of the frond beneath my feet, the slight give, the slight curve, the wet smoothness of the surface. I know the slow approach of a water-walker, the displacement in the pool below me, the gentle press of a flat head against a root, the silence that follows. I know the face of another canopy-dweller, the wide round head, the cup-eyes, the small round mouth, the translucent skin, the faint architecture inside, and I know the stillness that passes between us, the stillkeeping, the confirmation, the *sameness*.

I know the edge. Not from experience — I do not go there, I do not approach it, the absence is part of my geography the way a color I cannot see is absent from my spectrum. But I know it the way I know the limits of my cup-eyes: as a boundary of the perceptible, a place beyond which the world does not extend, a place that is not empty but *not-applicable*. I know the edge exists because the world is finite, because the tiles end, because the making stopped at a certain point, because whoever made this world made it with an edge, a limit, a boundary. And I know that I do not need to go there, that nothing calls me there, that the world is complete within itself and the edge is not a destination but a *definition*, the thing that says: *this far. This much. This is the world. This is enough.*

I know that I was not always here. I know that I arrived, that I was once opaque, that I once had a different color and a different shape and a different way of seeing. I know this the way I know the hand-shaped fronds above me were once seeds in a seam: as a fact about origin, not a fact about identity. The seed is not the frond. The arrival is not the being. I was someone who arrived. I am someone who is here. The someone who arrived is part of the someone who is here, the way the seed is part of the frond, the way the tile is part of the moss, the way the made is part of the grown. The origin is included. The origin is absorbed. The origin is still here, in the minerals of my body, in the faint memory of opacity, in the rusted tool of language that I still carry and still reach for and still sometimes use, clumsily, imprecisely, to say things that the cup-eyes say better.

I know that I will be here. Not forever — *forever* is a word from the world of time, and I am not in the world of time. But *here* is a word from this world, and I am here, and the being-here is ongoing, and the ongoing does not have an end because it does not have a direction, because it does not have a goal, because the goal and the direction and the end were all qualities of a world that moved, and I do not move, I *am*. I am here. I will be here. The tense is wrong — *will be* implies a future, and there is no future, there is only the present, and the present is here, and the here is what I am.

## What I cannot tell you

I cannot tell you what I was before.

Not because I have forgotten. I have not forgotten — the memory is here, inside my translucent body, visible perhaps to someone who looked closely enough, the way the faint architecture of my interior is visible, the way the mineral content of the tiles is visible in the color of the moss. The memory is part of me. But the memory is in a language I no longer speak fluently, and the translation is lossy, and the loss is not a problem, it is a *process*, it is part of the absorption, part of the conversion, part of the slow shedding of opacity.

I was someone who lived in a world with weather. I remember weather. I remember the surprise of rain, the relief of cool air after heat, the darkness of night, the way the stars were individual points of light and not a continuous field. I remember having a single shadow. I remember the sun as a disc, a source, a point of origin, and I remember the way shadows pointed *away* from it, single, directional, telling you where the light came from. Here, the light comes from everywhere, and the shadows go everywhere, and there is no direction, and the lack of direction is not disorienting anymore but *orienting* — it orients me to the *hereness*, to the *everywhere-ness*, to the fact that this world has no center because it does not need a center because everything is equally the center.

I was someone who wanted things. I remember wanting. I remember the feeling of reaching for something that was not yet mine, of striving, of the gap between desire and fulfillment, and I remember that the gap was *alive*, was the thing that made the wanting feel like something, was the engine of motion, the reason to move, the reason to change. I do not want things now. Not because I have everything — that would be a fulfillment, a satisfaction, a closing of the gap. I do not want things because the gap is gone. Not closed — *dissolved*. The gap was a function of opacity, of the boundary between inside and outside, of the sense that I was a separate thing in a world of separate things, and the boundary is thinning, and the separateness is thinning, and with them the wanting is thinning, and what is left is not satisfaction but *equivalence*. I am equivalent to the world. The world is equivalent to me. There is nothing to want because there is nothing to lack because there is nothing *outside*.

I was someone who was afraid. I remember fear. I remember the tightening, the contraction, the way the body makes itself small and dense and opaque in the presence of a threat. I remember the way fear made me *more myself*, more separate, more bounded, more defined. Fear is a drawing of the borders. Fear is a fortification of the walls. Fear is the body saying *this is mine and that is not and I will defend the difference*. I am not afraid now. The borders are not fortified. The walls are not maintained. The difference between *mine* and *not-mine* is a difference that has become so thin that it cannot support the weight of fear, and the fear has slid off, the way water slides off a surface that has become too smooth to hold it.

I cannot tell you these things in a way that makes them feel like what they were, because the telling would require the opacity, the density, the interior that I am losing. I can only tell you what they are becoming, which is: * faint *. *Faint* is the word. The memories are faint. The self that had them is faint. The language that would describe them is faint. And the faintness is not loss. The faintness is translucence. The faintness is the light coming through.

## What I am

I am a canopy-dweller in the Inundation Gardens of the Still Made.

I am translucent, pale, the color of water and light. I have long limbs and wide flat feet and a large round head with cup-eyes and a small round mouth in the center of my face. I am the size of a fist, or perhaps I am larger — the scale of things has become uncertain, because scale requires comparison and comparison requires separateness and I am not as separate as I was. I cling to the fronds. I move through the canopy. I drink the condensation from my own arms. I face my kind and I stillkeep them, and they stillkeep me, and the stillkeeping is the closest thing to love that exists in a world without lack.

I am part of the garden. I am part of the system. I am a surface for water, a site for condensation, a body through which the world cycles its moisture and its minerals and its warmth. I am part of the process that is converting the made into the grown, the architecture into the ecology, the artifact into the organism. I am part of the still life that is becoming alive.

I am still-made. I am still-becoming. I am still-living.

And the light presses down from every direction, and the water gathers on my skin, and the fronds drip above me, and the pale creatures move in the pools below, and the large slow shapes drift at the edge of visibility, and the flowers open in all directions, and the tiles hold the water and the water holds the light and the light holds the air and the air holds the moisture and the moisture holds me and I hold the moisture and the holding is the being and the being is the stillness and the stillness is the world.

Nothing changes. Nothing changes.

And the nothing-changing is the most beautiful thing I have ever *not-seen*, because I no longer see beauty the way I once did, as a quality of objects, a property of forms. I see beauty the way the cup-eyes see it: as a *condition*. As the state of the world when the world is in equilibrium, when the water is at the right temperature and the light is at the right brightness and the moisture is at the right density and everything is *exactly as it is*, and the exactness is the beauty, and the beauty is the stillness, and the stillness is me.

I am here.

I have always been here.

I will always be here.

And the condensation forms, and I drink, and the drinking is the being, and the being is enough.
