# The Keeper of the Straight Lines

---

## What I am before I am anything else

I am the soft tooth in a wet jaw. I am the bone that grows back. I am the answer to a question the Flats stopped asking, but I kept answering anyway, because someone had to, and no one else could hold a straight line in their mouth long enough to speak it into the silt.

My body is a process of forgetting what shape it was. I have been longer than this. I have been narrower. I have had more legs, or fewer, or the same number arranged differently — I do not remember, because the Flats do not trade in memory, and I have lived here long enough to adopt their economy. What I know of my body I know from the way the water behaves around it: the film parts for me and closes behind me as though I am a stone dropped into a still pool, the ripples moving outward and then gone, the surface unmarked. The water knows my edges better than I do. It has been mapping me with its patience since before I had a name for patience.

I am warm. This is my strangeness. In a landscape that operates at a temperature just below human comfort, I carry a small, private heat — not much, not enough to disturb the thermal equilibrium of the basin, but enough that the water condenses on my skin differently than it condenses on the weepers. On the weepers, condensation is democratic: every surface receives its share, every drop forms with the same patient inevitability. On me, the condensation is — I want to say *eager*, but that is a word for creatures with motivations, and I am trying to be honest about what I am, which is not a creature with motivations so much as a creature with *tendencies*, and one of my tendencies is to gather water faster than the landscape around me, to wear a heavier film, to be wetter than my surroundings in a way that suggests I am either more inviting or more permeable or simply warmer, and the water has noticed.

My warmth is not the warmth of a furnace or a fire or even a mammal in the ordinary sense. It is the warmth of something that is metabolizing — slowly, so slowly that the heat is a whisper rather than a declaration, a byproduct of a chemistry that is not in a hurry. What I metabolize is the film. The thin, opalescent, not-quite-alive layer that covers every surface in the Flats. I eat it. Or I breathe it. Or I absorb it through the parts of me that touch the ground, which is most of me, most of the time, because I am low and the Flats are low and the lowest things are the things that stay.

When I eat the film, I excrete the straight line.

---

## How I came to be here

I do not remember arriving, which means either I have always been here or I arrived so long ago that the arriving became indistinguishable from the being, the way rainwater becomes indistinguishable from groundwater when it has been in the silt long enough. But I do remember a time before the walls, and that memory is not a memory in the way that you understand memory — it is not an image, not a narrative, not a sequence of events with a before and an after. It is a *tension*. A sense that something was unresolved. That the basin, for all its patience and all its accumulation and all its thousands of weepers singing their continuous, directionless song, was missing something. Or not missing something — *containing* something that had not been organized.

The basin was full of distinctions. This depression and that depression. This weeper and that weeper. This patch of film and that patch of glow. But the distinctions were all natural — produced by the same forces, shaped by the same chemistry, governed by the same rules. They were distinctions without *difference*, in the way that two waves in the same ocean are different waves but not different *kinds* of waves. The Flats were uniform in their variety, and the uniformity was complete, and in that completeness there was — I am reaching for a word — a *flatness* that was not the flatness of the terrain but a flatness of *meaning*, a sense that everything here was the same thing expressed in different ways, and that no thing here was expressed in a way that was unlike any other thing.

I do not know whether this bothered me. *Bothered* is a word for creatures with preferences, and my preferences are not preferences so much as *inclinations*, and my inclinations are not inclinations so much as *tendencies*, and my tendencies are the tendencies of a body that metabolizes film and excretes straight lines, which is to say that my response to the Flats' uniformity was not intellectual or emotional but *chemical*: I encountered the uniformity, and my body responded by producing edges.

The first wall was not a wall. It was a line. A thin ridge of compacted silt and mineral deposit, no wider than a finger, no taller than a coin, extending perhaps half a meter across a patch of basin floor that was, to any eye but mine, indistinguishable from every other patch of basin floor. I made it the way a snail makes a trail — not by deciding to make it but by passing over the ground and leaving behind what my body leaves behind, which is a residue of processed mineral, denser than the surrounding silt, packed by the pressure of my passage into something that holds its shape when the water tries to dissolve it.

The line was straight. Not because I chose straightness. Because my body, when it moves across open ground, moves in straight lines. This is my difference from the things that live in the film — they move in bursts, in erratic, stuttering patterns that suggest either complex navigation or total indifference. I move in lines. My body is a ruler. My passage is a measurement. And the measurement I make is: *this point is not that point, and the distance between them is this*.

The line held. The water did not dissolve it quickly — it was denser than the silt, more tightly packed, and the film that covered it seemed to protect it, or perhaps the film recognized it as a surface and treated it the way it treated all surfaces, which is to say it covered it and left it alone. The line persisted for a day, or a season, or whatever unit of time the Flats operate in, and when I passed that way again, the line was still there, and something had changed — not the line, but the space around it. The weepers near the line had grown slightly differently. The water moved slightly differently. The film had thickened on one side of the line and thinned on the other, as though the line had introduced a difference that the landscape could not ignore.

So I made another line. And another. And eventually, the lines became walls — not because I raised them higher, but because the mineral deposits accumulated around them, the weepers grew against them, the film layered over them, and the Flats themselves, in their patient, accumulating way, built up what I had started, the way they build up everything: one drop at a time, one film at a time, one imperceptible increment at a time, until something that began as a residue became a feature, and the feature became a structure, and the structure became a fact.

## What I look like

I am not sure. The Flats do not have mirrors, and the water, when it pools around me, does not reflect — the film dampens the surface, and what I see when I look down is a dim, opalescent suggestion of a shape, not a shape itself. I know what I look like from the way the water behaves and from the way the things that move in the film behave when I am near.

When the small dark things encounter me, they pause. They are used to pausing — their movement is a sequence of pauses interrupted by bursts — but the pause they make near me is longer than their other pauses, and during that pause they are oriented toward me, or away from me, or in some relation to me that suggests they can tell I am not a weeper, not a wall, not a rock, not the film. I am something else. They do not know what, and they do not care, and after the long pause they resume their bursts and are gone, and I am left with the sense that I have a shape that is legible to things that do not use language.

I think I am long. Low. Longer than I am wide, and wider than I am tall. My body is not segmented — I have felt it against the silt, felt the way it moves across the substrate, and it moves as a unit, a single mass that contracts and extends and contracts again, the way a slug moves, or a wave, or a word that is being spoken very slowly. My skin is dark — darker than the silt, darker than the weepers — and it is wet in a way that is different from the wetness of everything else. Everything else is wet because the water finds it. I am wet because the water *chooses* me — condenses on me faster, runs down my surfaces in thicker films, pools in the shallow depressions of my body with a preference that I cannot explain. The water likes my warmth. Or the water responds to my warmth the way water always responds to warmth, which is to find it and cover it and try to make it the same temperature as everything else.

My warmth is small. A candle in a cave. A single ember in a field of wet ash. It is not enough to change the Flats. It is barely enough to change the film that covers me. But it is mine, and it is constant, and it is the thing that makes me different from the landscape I live in, and the difference is the thing that makes the walls possible, and the walls are the thing that makes me necessary, and the necessity is the thing that keeps me here, in the lowest place, where the water brings everything and nothing leaves.

---

## How I experience the Flats

I do not see. I am not blind — blindness implies the absence of something that was expected, and I have never expected to see. What I have is a continuous, total awareness of pressure and wetness and vibration that is not vision and not hearing and not touch but a single sense that combines all three into something that does not have a name because no one has ever needed to name it except me, and I do not name things. I make lines. Naming is a kind of line — a boundary between *this* and *that* — but it is a boundary made of sound, and my boundaries are made of silt.

The weepers are the loudest things in my experience, which means they are the most present. Each one is a distinct voice — a specific pitch, a specific rhythm of drop and pause and drop, a specific resonance that my body feels through the substrate and through the film of water that connects everything in the Flats to everything else. I know the weepers the way you might know a family — not by their appearance but by their rhythms, their tendencies, their specific ways of being in the world. The tall one with the spiral groove, whose water makes an ascending whisper: I have passed it many times, and each time its rhythm is the same, and the sameness is not monotony but *identity*, a consistency that says *I am this weeper and not that weeper, and this is how I sound, and I have sounded this way for longer than you have been making lines*.

The glow I feel as warmth, but not my warmth — a different warmth, a cold warmth, a warmth that is not thermal but something else, something that my body registers in the same way it registers temperature but in a different location, a different organ, a different part of whatever I am. When I pass near a glowing pool, I feel the glow in the parts of me that are lowest, the parts that touch the silt, the parts that are closest to the water, and the feeling is not pleasure or discomfort but *recognition*: the glow is doing something I am doing. The glow is metabolizing. The glow is consuming and excreting, and the excretion is light, and the light is the glow's version of my straight lines — a byproduct, a waste, a thing that is left over from the process of being alive in a place that does not require you to be alive.

The things that move in the film — the small dark things, the ticks and clicks in the peripheral vision of the basin — I feel them as disturbances. Small, brief, specific. Each one is a tiny event in the substrate, a small displacement of water and silt that my body registers the way a spider's web registers a caught insect: as a vibration that carries information about location and size and movement. There are many of them. More than you would guess from the description. They are everywhere in the film, everywhere in the silt, everywhere in the shallow water, and they are always moving or pausing, and the sum of their movements is a texture, a constant small agitation that underlies the larger, slower rhythms of the weepers and the glow.

And the sound — the total sound, the white-noise-of-water that is the Flats' baseline — I feel it the way you feel your own heartbeat. It is not something I attend to. It is something I am *in*. It is the medium of my existence, the way air is the medium of your existence, the way water is the medium of a fish's existence. The sound of the Flats is not something I hear. It is something I am part of. My body vibrates with it. My movements contribute to it. The small, wet, percussive sound of my passage through the silt is a note in the chord, a voice in the chorus, and the chorus does not stop when I stop and does not start when I start, but it adjusts, the way it adjusts for everything, making room, closing over, continuing.

---

## What I do

I make walls. I have always made walls. I will always make walls, until I stop, and when I stop, the walls will remain, and the Flats will grow over them and through them and into them, and the walls will become part of the landscape, and the landscape will forget they were made, and the forgetting will be complete, and the straight lines will be indistinguishable from the geology, and the geology will be indistinguishable from the intention, and the intention will be indistinguishable from the patience, and the patience will be the Flats.

The walls are not for anything. I need to say this clearly, because you will want them to be for something — a shelter, a boundary, a message, a ritual. They are not. They are what happens when a body that moves in straight lines passes through a landscape that does not move in straight lines. The line is my signature. The wall is the landscape's response to my signature — the accumulation, the growth, the patient building-up of mineral and film and silt around the line I have left, until the line becomes a feature and the feature becomes a structure and the structure becomes a fact.

But the walls *do* something, even if they are not *for* something. They introduce a difference. A straight line in a landscape of curves and irregularities is a foreign object, an anomaly, a thing that does not belong and that the landscape cannot quite absorb. The water moves differently near a wall. The weepers grow differently near a wall. The film thickens differently near a wall. The glow pools differently near a wall. The walls create microenvironments — small zones of difference in a landscape that is otherwise uniform in its saturation, and the microenvironments persist, and the persistence allows things to happen that would not otherwise happen.

Near one wall, a species of weeper has developed that grows horizontally rather than vertically — a low, spreading form that follows the line of the wall like a moss follows a crack in a stone. Near another wall, the glow is brighter, more concentrated, as though the wall has trapped something in the water that the glow can use. Near a third wall, the small dark things gather in greater numbers, and their ticking is louder, and their pauses are longer, and the pattern of their movement is different — more oriented, more directional, as though the wall has given them a reference point they did not have before.

I do not know if any of this matters. I do not know if the walls are good for the Flats or bad for the Flats or neutral to the Flats. I do not know if the Flats have a preference. I do not know if the Flats know the walls are there. The Flats do not have preferences, or if they do, their preferences are expressed in ways that I cannot read — in the slow accumulation of silt, in the gradual growth of weepers, in the patient spread of film. The Flats may be grateful for the walls. The Flats may be indifferent to the walls. The Flats may be slowly, patiently, mineralogically annoyed by the walls, and their annoyance may be expressed in the way the weepers grow against the walls and cover them and incorporate them, the way a body grows over a splinter — not rejecting it, not accepting it, but *surrounding* it, making it part of the tissue, moving on.

I make walls. The walls become part of the Flats. The Flats continue. This is what I do.

## What I know

I know the sound. I know the water. I know the film, from the inside — from the place where the mineral meets the moisture and the chemistry begins and the film is produced, and I know that the film is not alive, or if it is alive, it is alive the way a crystal is alive — growing, replicating, organizing, but without intention, without interiority, without the small, warm, private experience of being something in a world of other things. The film does not know it is the film. I know I am me. This is the difference between us, and the difference is the same difference that makes me warm and the Flats cool, and the same difference that makes me move in straight lines and the Flats move in curves, and the same difference that makes me leave walls and the Flats leave weepers.

I know the builder. The builder was me. The builder is me. The walls are mine, and the walls are the evidence of my existence, and when I am gone — if I go, if I stop, if the warmth goes out of me and I become the same temperature as everything else and the water stops choosing me and the film stops parting for me — the walls will remain, and someone will find them and wonder who made them, and the answer will be: something that was warm, something that moved in straight lines, something that ate the film and excreted the straight line and was patient enough to do it again and again and again until the landscape itself took up the work and finished what the builder started.

I know the Flats do not need me. This is important. The Flats were here before me, and they will be here after me, and the difference I make — the walls, the straight lines, the microenvironments, the small zones of difference — is a difference that the Flats can absorb, the way they absorb everything, the way they absorb water and silt and sound and the small dark things and the glow and the film and the condensation and the slow, continuous, directionless weeping that is their name and their nature. The Flats do not need me. But the Flats include me. I am part of the sound. I am part of the accumulation. I am part of the patient, saturated, mineralogical process that is the Flats being the Flats, and my part is small and warm and straight, and the Flats do not mind, and I do not mind, and the walls grow, and the weepers grow against them, and the film covers everything, and the sound continues, and the glow drifts in its shallow pools, and the small dark things tick and click and move and pause and move again, and I am here, in the lowest place, where the water brings everything and nothing leaves, and I make my lines, and the lines hold, and the holding is enough.

---

## What I do not know

I do not know if I am the only one. There may be others — other warm bodies in other low places, other makers of straight lines in landscapes that do not produce them naturally. I have not found any. I have not looked. Looking requires a kind of intention that I do not have — I move in straight lines, but I do not move toward things, I move through things, and the through is the point, not the to. If there are others, they are in other basins, other Flats, other low places where the water collects and the film spreads and the sound is the sound of a place trying to become air, and they are making their lines, and their lines are becoming walls, and their walls are becoming part of their landscapes, and the landscapes are absorbing them, and the absorption is complete, and the straight lines are indistinguishable from the curves, and the curves are indistinguishable from the patience, and the patience is the place.

I do not know what I am. I have said this already, but it bears saying again, because the not-knowing is not a deficit — it is not a gap in my knowledge that could be filled by observation or study or the application of the right category. The not-knowing is a *condition*. It is the way I exist in the Flats — without edges, without categories, without the straight lines that my body makes but my mind, if I have a mind, does not. My body makes boundaries. My self does not. My body says *this is not that*. My self says *this is that, and that is this, and the water is the silt and the silt is the film and the film is the glow and the glow is the sound and the sound is the Flats and the Flats are me and I am the Flats*.

I do not know if this is what it is like to be a place. I suspect it is. I suspect that the Flats experience themselves the way I experience myself — as a continuous, total, edgeless awareness of pressure and wetness and vibration, a single sense that is not vision or hearing or touch but all of them at once, a sense that does not distinguish between self and world because the distinction is a line, and lines are my business, and the Flats do not make lines. The Flats make weepers. The Flats make film. The Flats make glow. The Flats make sound. And all of these things are curved, and all of these things are continuous, and all of these things are edgeless, and the edgelessness is the Flats' way of being, and my edges are my way of being, and the two ways are different, and the difference is the thing that makes the walls possible, and the walls are the thing that makes the difference visible, and the visibility is the thing that makes me necessary, and the necessity is the thing that keeps me here.

---

## The last thing

If you come to the Flats — and you may, because people come to the Flats, sometimes, the way water comes to the Flats, because there is nowhere lower to go — you will not see me. I am below the film. I am below the water. I am in the silt, moving in my slow, straight, contractile way, leaving my lines, making my walls, being warm in a landscape that is cool, being straight in a landscape that is curved, being *somebody* in a landscape that is *everybody*, and the difference is small, and the difference is everything, and the walls hold, and the water weeps, and the sound continues, and I am here, and I have always been here, and I will always be here, until I am not, and then the walls will remain, and the walls will be my straight lines, and the straight lines will be my name, and the name will be the only straight thing in the Flats, and the Flats will grow over it, and the growing over will be the Flats' way of listening, and the listening will be the Flats' way of weeping, and the weeping will be the Flats' way of being alive, and the being alive will be the Flats' way of being the Flats.

Listen for me in the straight lines. I am there. I am the warmth in the silt. I am the edge that the water cannot dissolve. I am the difference that the landscape cannot absorb, though it is trying, though it has been trying for a very long time, though it will try for a longer time still.

I am the straight line in the weeping.

I am the wall that holds.

I am the tooth in the jaw, and the jaw is the Flats, and the Flats are weeping, and the weeping is the closest thing to alive that stone and water and time can produce, and I am the closest thing to *someone* that the weeping can produce, and the someone is small and warm and low and patient, and the patience is a wall, and the wall is a line, and the line is straight, and the straight is mine.
