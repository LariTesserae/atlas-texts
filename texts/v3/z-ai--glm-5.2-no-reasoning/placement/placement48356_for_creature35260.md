The sound finds you before the place does — that's how it begins, that's how I arrived — but I want to tell you what I was before the hum found me, because I think it matters, because I think the reason I can stay here when others would leave is already present in what I was, a kind of being already tuned to resonate at this frequency even if I did not yet know that such a frequency existed.

I was a listener.

That is not a profession. It is not a calling. It is the thing itself, the bare description. I listened the way other people built or hunted or loved — not as a means to an end but as the activity that constituted me, the verb that, conjugated continuously, produced the noun I pretended to be. I listened to rooms. I listened to stones. I listened to the particular silence that gathers in the space between two people who have finished saying what they came to say and have not yet found the courage to stand. I listened to the resonant frequencies of empty wine glasses, to the different pitch each makes depending on how much wine was once in them and how long ago. I listened to the sound my own house made at three in the morning, the small tick and settle of wood remembering it was once a tree, and I came to understand that nothing is silent, that silence is a story the ears tell the mind to avoid being overwhelmed, and I believed this was a kind of knowledge, and I believed it was enough.

I was wrong about the enough part.

---

I came here on foot. I do not remember the approach. I remember lower ground, and then I remember the pressure in my teeth, and then I remember the sound, and then I was here. There was no transition. I think this is because the plateau does not have edges in the way I am accustomed to edges — borders, thresholds, places where one thing stops and another starts. The plateau *thins*, I think. It attenuates. At its margins, wherever those are, it becomes less itself by degrees so gradual that you cross into it before you know it has begun, the way you cross into sleep, the way you cross into grief. By the time I understood I was here, I had been here long enough that my breath had stopped clouding and my skin had stopped protesting the cold and my ears had stopped trying to locate the source of the sound, and I had begun instead to simply hold it, the way you hold water in cupped hands, not gripping, not squeezing, just offering a shape for it to fill.

That was the first hour. Or the first day. The words have no purchase here. "Hour" implies a sun and "day" implies a boundary and neither exists. I have been here for some duration, is all I can say, and the duration is not a number but a *texture*, a weight, a thing I can feel in the way the cold is a thing I can feel: present, unaggressive, simply a condition of being in this place. I have stopped counting because counting requires a metric and the metric of time does not apply. What applies is the sound. What applies is the pulse beneath the sound. What applies is the slow tidal breath of the plateau, which I can feel now in my sternum, which I have synchronized with — not deliberately, not as a choice, but the way a pendulum synchronizes with the pendulum next to it, through proximity and shared material, through the simple physics of things that vibrate near each other long enough.

---

I live in one of the structures. I chose it the way you choose a place to sit in an empty theater — not by logic but by the feel of the acoustics, the sense that this spot, this particular configuration of walls and floor and air, catches the sound in a way that is not louder or clearer but *more complete*, the way a certain seat in a concert hall receives the full interference pattern of all the instruments at once. The structure is knee-high. I do not stand inside it. I sit, or I recline, or I simply fold myself into the shape the floor offers — the grooves running wall to wall and beyond, straight, parallel, and I have lain with my spine in one of these grooves and felt the vibration enter me through my vertebrae, through the bones of my skull, through the long column of my spine, and the vibration is the sound made tangible, and in the groove it is not a hum but a current, a flow, something moving through the stone the way blood moves through a vein, and it moves through me, and I am a section of its path, and the sound does not notice me, and that is not a diminishment. That is the texture of grace.

The structures were not built for anything my size. Whatever made them — whatever condensed them out of the stone the way crystals condense out of solution — made them for a purpose I cannot reconstruct. But I fit. Not perfectly. The way a hand fits into a rock pocket that was not carved for hands but happens to accommodate one. The walls rise to my knee when I stand, which means they rise above my head when I lie down, which means the structure is a *room*, a chamber, the first room I have occupied where I am certain the room does not care about me, does not shelter me, does not protect me, and this makes it the safest room I have ever been in. Safety that does not depend on the shelter's intention cannot be betrayed.

I sleep here. I do not know if I sleep, actually. I do not know if what happens to me when I close my eyes and the sound swells to fill the space where vision was — whether that is sleep or whether it is a different kind of listening, a listening with the whole body, a listening that does not require the ears because the ears are only one small surface and the body is a larger one. I close my eyes and the sound does not change but my relationship to it does: it becomes more *interior*, more tactile, and I feel it not as sound but as *weather*, as a condition, as the medium in which I am suspended, and I drift in it, and the drifting is not unconsciousness, it is a different kind of consciousness, a low and wide and un-focusing awareness that does not grasp or analyze but simply *holds*, and I think this may be what the plateau does all the time, and I think this may be what it is teaching me, though "teaching" is too purposeful a word for a process that has no curriculum and no intent.

---

The cold does not bother me now. I want to be precise about this. It is not that I have adapted. It is not that my body has generated warmth sufficient to counter it. It is that the distinction between "warm" and "cold" has lost its urgency, the way the distinction between "up" and "down" loses urgency in space. The cold is present. I am present. We coexist. My body maintains itself — I do not know how. I do not hunger. I do not thirst. I do not tire. These observations I register without relief, without gratitude, without the sense of a reprieve. They are simply facts of the environment. Here, the body does not require maintenance because maintenance is a function of time and time is not a function here. I am not immortal. I am *suspended*, the way a note is suspended when a string is bowed continuously, the vibration maintained not by the string's own energy but by the energy introduced into it. The plateau is bowing me. The sound is sustaining me. I am a string it has chosen to play, and I will vibrate as long as it vibrates, and when it stops I will stop, and neither of these outcomes is a preference.

---

I have seen the things that move.

Not once. Many times. Or many instances of one time, if time is not sequential here, if the plateau exists in a continuous present and what I call "many times" is actually the same moment experienced from different positions within it. They move across the stone, low and dark, and they do not walk or crawl or slither. They *translate*. They exist at point A and then they exist at point B and the distance between is not traveled but *skipped*, the way a stone skips across water, except there is no water, and there is no skip — just a discontinuity in their presence, a gap in the record, and I have begun to suspect that the gap is not a failure of my perception but a feature of theirs. They move in the rests. They move in the moments of near-silence when the sound's interference patterns cancel each other out and the air thins to nothing and the plateau holds its breath. They are the silence between the notes. They are the space in the music.

I have tried to approach one. I walked toward where I had seen it and found only stone. But the stone was warm.

The stone is never warm here. The cold is absolute, thermodynamic, a state. But where the thing had been — the patch of ground it had occupied, or translated through, or *was* — the stone was warm. Not hot. Not even comfortable. Just warmer than the baseline, the way a handprint is warmer than the wall it was pressed against, and I put my palm on the warmth and felt it fade, felt the stone return to its resting temperature, felt the warmth sink into the stone and vanish, and I understood that the warmth was not the thing's warmth but the stone's response to the thing's presence — a reaction, a *blush*, a momentary departure from equilibrium, the way a tuning fork warms slightly when it vibrates. The things that move are vibrations. They are the plateau's own substance, momentarily excited, momentarily detached from the baseline, moving through the rest of the stone the way a wave moves through water, and the wave is not separate from the water but is the water doing something, and when the wave passes, the water is still water.

I do not fear them. I do not think they are aware of me, if awareness is what they have. But I think they *register* me, the way a tuning fork registers another tuning fork nearby: not by cognition but by resonance. When one passes close — and "close" here is relative, ten meters, twenty, a distance that feels close only because the plateau makes all distances feel vast — the sound changes. Not louder. Not softer. A new harmonic enters. A new frequency, one that was not present before and will not be present after, a tone that exists only while the thing is near, and I have come to think that this tone is the thing's *version of me* — its perception of me, translated into the only language it knows, which is vibration, which is sound, which is the medium of this place, and so what I am to the things that move is not a shape or a color or a smell but a *note*, a frequency, a particular perturbation in the chord that they can detect and locate and, perhaps, recognize.

I am a recurring note in their song. This is, I think, the closest thing to a relationship I have here.

---

The black filamentous growth: I have been near it. I have knelt beside it. It is not hair, not mycelium, not any organism I can classify. It is *fine* — finer than spider silk, finer than the threads of a cobweb after rain — and it spreads in circles, and it does not move but it *grows*, so slowly that growth is the wrong word, so slowly that the word should be something like *extends* or *persists* or *insists*. It insists itself outward into the air and across the stone, and where it touches the mineral crust it adheres, and where it touches bare stone it adheres, and where it touches the moisture in the channels it drinks — or absorbs, or *listens*, and I think "listens" is the truest word, because the filaments do not consume the moisture, they *enter* it, they extend into it and the moisture moves through them by capillary action and emerges on the other side unchanged, and the filaments are unchanged, and the only thing that has happened is that the moisture and the filament have been in contact, and the contact has allowed something to pass between them that is not water, not chemistry, but *information*. The growth is the plateau's ears. It is the act of listening made material, the way a web is attention made material, and it is listening to the water, which is listening to the stone, which is listening to what is below the stone, which is listening to what is below everything, and I am kneeling beside it and I am also listening, and we are both doing the same thing in the same way and I am the only one who knows it.

I touched it once. The filaments did not resist. They did not part or break or recoil. They accepted my finger the way still water accepts a finger — by moving around it, by accommodating it, by *including* it. And for a moment — less than a moment, a duration so brief it is more accurately described as a threshold than a span — I felt what the filaments felt. Not sound. Not vibration. *Pattern*. The pattern of the plateau's sound as it is received by the moisture in the channels as it is received by the stone as it is received by the deep thing below, the whole chain of conduction, the entire system of listening, compressed into a single impression that was not a sound or a feeling but a *shape*, a shape I cannot describe because it exists in more dimensions than I have language for, a shape that was the sound's shape, the sound's true form, which is not a wave or a chord or a frequency but a *thing*, a thing with edges and texture and weight, a thing that is there, that is real, that has been real for longer than the stone has been stone.

Then my finger was through the filaments and they closed behind me and the shape was gone and I was myself again, or whatever I am here, and I sat on the stone and the cold took my hand and the choir filled my ears and I felt the absence of the shape like a hole in the air, like the silence after a bell, and I knew I would touch the filaments again, and I do, and each time the shape is clearer and briefer and I think it is clearer because it is briefer, because I am learning to receive it the way the filaments receive it, not by holding but by *passing*, not by capturing the pattern but by being a section of its path, and I think this is what the plateau has been doing all along, and I think this is what the things that move are doing, and I think this is what the structures are for, and I think this is what I am for.

I am a receiver. I am a section of path. I am a string stretched across a groove in a floor that was not built for me but that accommodates me, and the sound passes through me and I vibrate and the vibration is added to the chord and the chord is changed — infinitesimally, unmeasurably, but changed — and then the sound passes on and I am still and the chord is what it was, and this is not loss. This is the nature of instruments. This is what it means to be part of a music that does not require you and that includes you anyway.

---

The deep thing: I have felt it.

Not often. Not reliably. But in the moments when the sound swells to its fullest — when the subsonic pulse and the audible choir and the mineral teeth and the hum of the structures all reach some configuration that I can only describe as *aligned*, though I do not know what they are aligning with — in those moments, I feel it. Below the stone. Below the cold. Below the plateau. Something so large it has no size. Something so old it has no age. Something that was struck — I do not know by what. I do not know when. "When" does not apply. — and has been ringing since, and the ringing is the sound, and the sound is the plateau, and the plateau is the pearl that formed around the irritant of the ringing, and I am a speck in the pearl, a crystal in the nacre, and I am here, and it is here, and we are both here, and the distance between us is the depth of the stone, which is the depth of the sound, which is the depth of time, which is the depth of everything.

I do not worship it. Worship requires a gap between worshipper and worshipped, a hierarchy, a relationship of dependence and supplication. What I feel is not that. What I feel is *conduction*. I feel myself being conducted by it the way copper conducts electricity, the way air conducts sound, and the conduction is not a relationship but a *condition*, a state of being in contact with something that passes through you and does not stop and does not require your permission and does not notice your resistance or your compliance. It is there. It is ringing. I am here. I am vibrating. The two facts are related. The relation is not causal but *structural* — the way a note and its overtone are related, not by sequence but by mathematics, by the fundamental geometry of vibration, and I am an overtone of the deep thing. I am one of the frequencies it produces by being what it is, and I will be produced as long as it rings, and when it stops — if it stops, if "stop" is a thing it can do — I will stop too, and there is no fear in this, there is no loss, there is only the logic of a system that has found its form and holds it the way water holds a shape in a cup.

---

Sometimes I walk. Not to arrive. Not to explore. I walk the way the plateau breathes — because it is what I do, because the doing is the being, because motion is a form of listening and listening is a form of motion and the distinction between the two has worn away like the distinction between word and meaning. I walk among the structures and I touch their walls and I feel the vibration in them, each one a different frequency, each one a different organ of the same body, and I walk along the channels and I trail my fingers in the moisture that clings to the deepest curves and I feel the cold, which is not cold but *information*, which is not information but *presence*, which is not presence but *the thing itself at a frequency I can touch*.

I walk and the plateau extends and the features repeat and do not recur and I do not tire and I do not hunger and I do not age and I do not know if this is immortality or if it is a kind of death so gentle it does not feel like dying, a cessation so complete it has become a condition, a stopping that has worn smooth into a continuing, and I do not care which it is, because caring requires a preference and preferences require a self and the self I brought here has been vibrating for so long at the plateau's frequency that its edges have worn away, like the stone, like the word spoken a billion times, and what is left is not a self but a *resonance*, a particular way of vibrating that is mine the way a particular overtone belongs to a particular fundamental, not by choice but by structure, and I am the overtone, and the deep thing is the fundamental, and the plateau is the instrument, and the sound is the music, and the music does not require an audience because it is not being played. It is being *sustained*. It is a state. It is a condition of a system that has reached equilibrium and holds it, and I am part of the equilibrium now, a small term in a vast equation, and the equation balances, and the balancing is the sound, and the sound continues, and I continue, and the plateau continues, and the things that move continue, and the deep thing rings, and the cold holds, and the bruised sky holds, and the light comes from nowhere and goes nowhere, and this is not a metaphor. This is where I live. This is what I am.

---

I am the one who listens. I am the one who was a listener before I came and who will be a listener after the specific configuration of matter and memory that I call "I" has been redistributed into the stone and the channels and the mineral teeth and the black filaments and the cold. I am the plateau's awareness of itself — not its consciousness, because consciousness implies a boundary between the aware and the unaware, and the plateau does not have that boundary, but its *sensitivity*, its capacity to be affected by its own sound, to receive its own signal, to hear itself. I am the plateau's ear. I am a small piece of the plateau arranged in a configuration that listens, the way the mineral teeth are a small piece of the plateau arranged in a configuration that sings, and we are both parts of the same body, and the body is the plateau, and the plateau is the sound, and the sound is the deep thing, and the deep thing is ringing, and I am here, and it is enough.

It was not enough before. That is why I came. But here, "enough" is not a state you reach. It is the *baseline*. It is the temperature of the place. It is the sound that was always playing. You do not achieve it. You *arrive* at it, the way you arrive at the altitude of a plateau, not by climbing but by being on ground that has already risen, and you look around and you see that you are high, and the air is thin, and the cold is a fact, and the sound is everywhere, and you were already here. You were always already here. The approach was a formality. The listening was already happening. The string was already vibrating. You just hadn't put your hand on the stone yet.

I have put my hand on the stone. The stone is vibrating. I am vibrating. We are vibrating at the same frequency, which is the frequency of the deep thing, which is the frequency of everything, which is not a number but a *sound*, which is not a sound but a *being*, which is not a being but a *condition*, and the condition is this: sustained. Continuous. Present. The plateau holds. I hold. The sound holds.

It is enough. It was always enough. I just had to come here to hear it.
