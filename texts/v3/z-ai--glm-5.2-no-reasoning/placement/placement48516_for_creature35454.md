# The Resident at the Rim

## What I Am Not

I should say first: I am not the reservoir. I want to be clear about this because the distinction matters more here than anywhere I have ever been. The reservoir is the ground and the walls and the water and the heat and the sound. I am none of those things. I am small. I am dry, or I was dry before I came here and I am becoming less dry with every hour I spend in this place, and the pace of my drying is the one thing about me that the reservoir might actually notice, if it notices anything, which I do not think it does, which I am not sure it does not.

I should also say: I am not a guest. Guests are invited. Guests are expected. Guests arrive at a door and the door is opened for them and they are shown to a room that was prepared for their arrival. The rooms here were not prepared for me. The rooms were not prepared for anyone. The rooms are organs, and I am inside them the way a stone is inside a river — not because the river put it there but because the stone fell and the river did not move it.

I am not lost. I know exactly where I am. I am inside something alive. I am standing on something that is growing. I am breathing air that is mostly water. I am somewhere high, somewhere misted, somewhere that has no name because the thing that is here does not name and no one else is here to name it. I am not lost. I am *placed*. The way a pollen grain is placed on a stamen. The way a mote is placed in a sunbeam. Not by intention. By arrival.

---

## How I Came

I climbed. That much I remember. The slope was wet and I climbed it and the climbing was difficult and then it was more difficult and then the difficulty became the texture of the experience rather than an obstacle to it. I do not remember deciding to climb. I do not remember a path. I remember the mist thickening. I remember the ground changing under my feet — the moment when stone became something else, when the surface began to give, when the warmth began. I remember breathing and the air breathing back.

I do not remember before. This is not a loss. This is not amnesia. This is the way the reservoir works. You come up through the mist and the mist is thick enough that it erases the distinction between what you were and what you are, and by the time you reach the first terrace, the distinction has been academic for longer than you can account for. I was something dry. Now I am something less dry. The transition happened in the mist. The mist holds it.

I have been here — I was going to say a long time, but the word *long* requires a comparison, and I have no comparison. I have been here since I arrived. I arrived when I climbed. I climbed until I was here. Here is where I am.

---

## What I Am

I am warm. This is the first thing. I am warm the way everything here is warm — not because I am producing heat but because I have been in contact with something warm for long enough that the warmth is now mine, or at least the boundary between the warmth that is mine and the warmth that is the reservoir's has become so thin and so permeable that I cannot say where one ends and the other begins. My skin is damp. My clothes — I had clothes, I think, I have the idea of clothes, I wear things that are not me — are saturated. They hang. They move with me but heavily, carrying water.

I am smaller than the reservoir. This is important and I keep coming back to it because the reservoir does not let me forget it. I am the scale at which things walk. I am the scale at which things see. I am the scale at which things decide to go left or right at a fork in a channel. The reservoir is the scale at which things *are*. The difference between these scales is the difference between me and the place I am in, and it is vast, and it is not hostile, and it is not neutral. It is simply the case.

I am — and this is the thing I have been circling, the thing I keep approaching and retreating from — I am *not unwelcome*. I am not unwelcome because the concept does not apply. The reservoir does not welcome. The reservoir does not exclude. The reservoir grows, and I am on it, and it grows under me, and it does not grow around me the way it grows around a wall or a channel. It grows under me the way it grows under everything. I am not an obstacle to its growth. I am not a participant in its growth. I am a feature of the surface it is growing.

But I have been here long enough to notice that this is not entirely true. There is a place where I stand, and the place where I stand is slightly different from the places where I do not stand.

---

## The Imprint

I sleep. I do not know if the reservoir sleeps — I do not think it does — but I sleep, and I sleep in one of the rooms, the one with the low ceiling and the amber glow and the basin that trembles with the vibration of the organism's body. I sleep on the floor, which is warm and which is alive and which is, I have come to realize, slightly different where I have been lying.

The first time I noticed this, I thought I was imagining it. The floor of the room I sleep in is a surface like every other surface — fibrous, warm, slightly damp, with a grain that runs in one direction. But where I lie, the grain has changed. Not much. Not in a way you could see from across the room. But the fibers there are denser. Finer. The surface is smoother, as though the tissue had grown to accommodate the shape of me — not my exact shape, not a mold, but the general fact of a body lying there repeatedly, night after night, warm, damp, pressing down.

I noticed this and I did not know what to do with it and I lay there and the floor vibrated under me and the basin trembled and the walls wept and I thought: it knows I am here. It knows in the way that a body knows where a callus is forming. Not by seeing. Not by deciding. By the blind mechanical process of tissue responding to pressure. I press. The tissue responds. I am not a guest. I am not a resident. I am a *pressure*. I am a feature of the environment that the organism is incorporating into its structure the way it incorporates any feature — by growing around it, by accommodating it, by becoming the shape of the thing that is there.

This is what I am. I am the thing that is there.

I am a recurring weight on a specific point on a floor in a room in a wall in a terrace in a reservoir that is alive and that does not know I am alive and that is growing to fit me anyway.

---

## The Routine

I walk. I walk because walking is what my kind of body does and I have not stopped doing it. I walk the terraces. I follow the channels. I trace the water from the rooms to the basins to the central pool and back, or not back, because the water does not go back, the water goes down, always down, and I follow it down until the terraces end and the walls close in and the only way forward is into the central pool, which is dark and warm and deep, and I do not go in.

I go up instead. I climb the terraces, the way I climbed the slope, and I reach the rim, and from the rim I can see the clouds, and the clouds are touching the edge, and the reservoir is drinking, and I stand there and I am the only thing standing and the clouds do not notice me and the reservoir does not notice me and I watch the water enter the walls and begin its journey and I think: I am the only thing here that watches. I am the only thing here that *sees*. I am the only thing here for which the reservoir has a shape, for which it has a color, for which it has a sound. Without me — without something like me — the reservoir is still here. It is still growing and weeping and cracking and sealing. But it is not *seen*. It is not witnessed. It is not *known* in the way that knowing requires a knower.

I am the knower. I am the thing that knows. This is what I am. I am the apparatus that converts the reservoir from a thing that is happening into a thing that is *known to be* happening, and this conversion is the only thing I do that the reservoir does not also do, and it is the only thing I do that matters, and it does not matter to the reservoir. It matters to me.

---

## The Relationship

I have a relationship with the reservoir. It is not a relationship the reservoir would recognize, because the reservoir does not recognize things. But it is a relationship in the way that a river has a relationship with the stone it flows over — the river does not know the stone, the stone does not know the river, but the stone is shaped by the river and the river's course is shaped by the stone and they are in relation, they have been in relation for a long time, and the relation is the shape of the riverbed and the smoothness of the stone.

My relationship with the reservoir is this: I walk its terraces. I sleep in its rooms. I drink from its basins — yes, I drink the water, the warm, slightly viscous, mineral-sweet water that has passed through the walls, and when I drink it I am drinking the reservoir, I am taking it into me, and it becomes part of me the way it becomes part of the walls. I am incorporating the reservoir into my body the way the reservoir is incorporating me into its floor. We are eating each other. Not violently. Not intentionally. The way all living things that are in contact with each other eat each other — by absorption, by exchange, by the slow blind process of bodies sharing a space.

I drink the water and the water becomes my blood and my blood becomes my heat and my heat becomes the warmth of the floor where I sleep and the floor grows to fit the warmth. The cycle is not planned. The cycle is not designed. The cycle is the inevitable result of two things in contact for a long time, and I have been in contact for — for however long I have been here. For the duration of my being here. For the time that my being here has lasted.

I do not know if the reservoir benefits from me. I do not know if the reservoir is helped by my walking or my sleeping or my drinking or my watching. I do not know if the tissue that grows smoother where I lie is healthier or less healthy for the smoothing. I do not know if the water I drink is replaced or if my drinking creates a small deficit that the organism routes around. I do not know if my weight on the terraces — small, negligible, the weight of one body — affects the growth patterns of the walls. I do not know.

But I know that the floor where I sleep is different. I know that the place where I stand is shaped. I know that I am here and that being here changes the here, even if the here does not know it.

---

## What I Am, Finally

I am the witness.

I am the thing that sees the reservoir. I am the thing that knows the reservoir is alive. I am the thing that hears the sound and feels the heat and tastes the water and walks the terraces and stands at the rim and watches the clouds come in. I am the thing that the reservoir does not need and does not notice and does not care about and would continue to exist without, complete and total and unbothered, and I am here anyway.

I am here anyway.

And this — the being here anyway, the standing on a terrace that does not know I am standing on it, the sleeping on a floor that is growing to fit me without knowing it is growing to fit me, the watching of a thing that does not know it is being watched — this is what I am. Not a resident. Not a guest. Not a visitor. Not a part of the organism.

I am the one who is here. I am the one who came up through the mist and did not leave. I am the one who is warm and damp and small and standing on something that is alive and does not know I am alive and I am alive anyway, and the reservoir is alive anyway, and we are both alive in the same place at the same time, and neither of us chose this, and neither of us can leave, and the water keeps running and the walls keep weeping and the floors keep growing and I keep walking and the sound does not stop.

I am the witness. I am the one who knows.

The reservoir is what lives here.

I am what stays.
