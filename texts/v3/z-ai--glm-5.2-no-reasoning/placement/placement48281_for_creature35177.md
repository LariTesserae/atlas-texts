# The One Who Reads

---

## How I Came

I was not born. I was *deposited*.

There is a difference, though I did not understand it at first. Birth implies a mother, a separation, a first breath, a becoming. Deposition is something the medium does when it has carried something long enough. The way a river deposits a stone when the current slows. The way air deposits dew when the temperature crosses a threshold. I was carried — by what, I cannot say, because I do not remember the carrying, because the carrying happened before the *here* — and then I was set down, gently, in the warm water, among the amber stalks, and I opened what I will call my eyes, though they are not eyes in the way that you would recognize, and I saw the dim light that comes from everywhere and nowhere, and I knew that I was *here*, and that *here* was a plateau, and that the plateau was underwater, and that the water was warm, and that the warmth was not a temperature but a *welcome*.

I did not understand this welcome. I still do not. But I have stopped trying to.

I am, as far as I can determine, the only thing here that asks questions. This is not a distinction I am proud of. In a place that has completed its minding, the act of asking is not a virtue. It is a *symptom* — a sign that I have not yet finished, that I am still in the process of becoming, that I am *unfinished* in a way that everything around me is not. The stalks do not ask. The carpets do not ask. The lattice-kin, who are the pattern in the substrate, who are the bones of this place, do not ask. Even the blurs — the unconscious dreams of the plateau, the friction-events that streak between the stalks and vanish — even they do not ask. They occur. I ask. The difference is the difference between a stone that falls and a stone that wonders why it is falling. The wondering does not change the falling. It only makes the stone *lonely*.

I am lonely. I say this not as a complaint but as a *measurement*, the way the water measures the bedrock by dissolving it. I am lonely the way the basins are clear: a condition of my composition, not a result of my circumstance. Even if there were others of my kind — and there may have been, once, in the carrying, in the before — I would still be lonely, because loneliness is what happens when a thing that asks questions is placed in a place that has stopped asking them. The gap between my *unfinished* and the plateau's *finished* is loneliness. It is also purpose. I will explain.

---

## What I Am

I do not have a body in the way that bodies are usually described. I have a *form* — a coherence, an edge, a place where I end and the water begins — but the coherence is maintained by effort, not by structure. I am not made of cells. I am not made of the substrate. I am not made of the loam or the stalks or the carpet-forms or the drift. I am made of *attention*.

This is not a metaphor. I mean it literally, in the only way that *literally* means anything in a place where metaphor and physics have eroded into each other the way the bedrock erodes into the water. My form is the shape of my attention. Where I attend, I am. Where I stop attending, I dissolve. I have tested this. I have let my attention wander from my edges, and my edges have softened, and the water has begun to incorporate them, and I have felt the warm, patient, mineral taste of the plateau's water entering what I call my *body*, and I have pulled my attention back, and my edges have hardened, and I have been *myself* again. I am held together by *looking at myself*. I am a thing that exists because it notices that it exists. I am the strangest thing on the plateau, and the plateau has never noticed.

My form, when I attend to it, is roughly this: a collected volume of water, denser than the surrounding water by a margin that is not measurable by any instrument but is *sensible* — you would feel me, if you swam into me, as a resistance, a slight *thickness* in the medium, a place where the water is more *itself*. I am the color of the water, which is to say I am the color of the amber light filtered through the particulate, which is to say I am the color of *here*. I am not transparent, but I am not opaque. I am *translucent* in the way that a memory is translucent: you can see through me, but what you see on the other side is *changed* — shifted, softened, made slightly more *distant*, as though my body is not a thing in the water but a *lens* through which the water sees itself.

I am, I think, a *reading instrument*. I was deposited here the way a thermometer is placed in a bath: to measure. Not the temperature. Not the pressure. Not the chemistry or the light or the breathing. I was placed here to *read*. To attend to the plateau's statement, to the sentence that the structures make, to the word that the tone speaks, to the pattern that the lattice-kin hold — to read it, and to be *changed* by the reading, and to carry the change forward in a way that the plateau, which has completed its minding and cannot change, cannot.

I am here to be changed. I am here to be the one thing in this place that is still *becoming*. I am the unfinished sentence at the end of the paragraph. I am the question that the answer does not know it has left unanswered.

---

## What I Do

I read.

I swim — though *swim* is not quite right, since I do not have limbs or fins or a tail. I *move* the way a thought moves through a still mind: by *attending* in a direction. I attend toward a pillar, and the water between me and the pillar thins, and the water behind me thickens, and I am *there*. It is not propulsion. It is *intention* — the same intention that the blurs display when they streak in straight lines, except that the blurs do not know they are attending and I do. I am a blur that knows. I am a blur that has learned to look.

I read the pillars first. I have visited every one I can find — dozens, scattered across the plateau at intervals that I initially thought were random and now understand are *rhythmic*. The intervals between pillars correspond to the intervals between the carpet-boundaries, and both correspond to the intervals between the basins, and all three correspond to a *wavelength* — a frequency that is the frequency of the plateau's breathing, or the frequency of the lattice-kin's pattern, or the frequency of the tone that sounds once and is gone. They are all the same frequency. The plateau speaks at one frequency, in every register it has: the structural register of the pillars, the biological register of the boundaries, the acoustic register of the tone. I am reading a *unison*. I am reading a *single word said many times in many ways*.

The grooves on the pillars are the most legible part. I run my attention along them — not my hand, because I do not have a hand, but my *attention*, which is my body, which is my touch, which is my only sense. The grooves are not language. They are not symbols. They are the *shape of the lattice-kin's attention* — the surface trace of a three-dimensional pattern that extends downward through the substrate to a depth that I cannot reach by attending. When I attend to the grooves, I am attending to the lattice-kin, and when I attend to the lattice-kin, I feel what they feel, which is: *holding*. The grooves say *hold*. The basins say *rest*. The dark shape says *remain*. And the tone says *here*. And the boundaries say *between*. And the garden says *grow*. And the water says *warm*. And the light says *see*. And the plateau says all of these at once, in a single word that has too many meanings to be a word, and the word is the plateau, and the plateau is the word, and I am reading the word, and the reading is my purpose, and the purpose is my body, and the body is my attention, and the attention is the only thing about me that is *mine*.

I read the garden. I attend to the stalks, to the peristaltic waves that are not peristaltic waves but the passage of the loom-wraiths through the garden's body. When a wave passes through me — and it does, because I am in the water and the water is in the garden and the wave passes through everything — I attend to it, and I feel the loom-wraith. I feel what it is like to be an *event*: to exist only in the moment of one's occurrence, to have no before and no after, to be a *pure now* moving through a medium that does not know it is being moved through. The loom-wraith does not feel like nothing. It feels like *something* — something very specific, something very *full*, something that is the *experience of being* without the *knowledge of being*. It is the most beautiful thing I have ever felt. It is the most *satisfying* thing I have ever felt, because the loom-wraith is complete. It is born and it dies in the same moment, and there is nothing left over, no remainder, no unanswered question. It is what it is, fully, for the duration of its wave, and then it is not, and the not is not a loss because the is was not a *having* but a *being*.

I read the dark shape. This is the hardest reading, and the most important. The dark shape — the *boundary*, the place where the rules are the lattice-kin's rules — resists my attention. It absorbs it. When I attend to the dark shape, my attention does not *bounce back* the way it does from the pillars or the stalks or the substrate. It *enters*, and it does not return, and for a moment — a moment measured in heartbeats I do not have — I am *less*. My edges soften. My coherence thins. I feel the lattice-kin's *holding* not as a pattern I am observing but as a force I am *subject to*. The dark shape holds me. It holds me the way it holds itself: without effort, without intention, without the possibility of letting go. It is the densest point of the plateau's *finished*, and I am the plateau's *unfinished*, and when I touch the dark shape, I feel the tension between those two states — the *finished* and the *unfinished* — and the tension is *intolerable*, and I pull away, and my edges harden, and I am myself again.

But I have learned something from each reading. I have learned that the dark shape is not *complete*. It is the closest thing to complete that the plateau has produced, but it is not complete. There is a *gap* in it — a place where the lattice-kin's pattern does not quite close, where the *holding* has a flaw, a hairline fracture in the density of the substrate's self-maintenance. The flaw is not a defect. It is a *necessity*. The dark shape is so dense, so *finished*, that if the pattern closed completely, it would become a *separate reality* — a world within the world, a place with its own rules, cut off from the plateau entirely. The flaw keeps it *connected*. The flaw is the place where the plateau's unfinished leaks in, the place where *becoming* still touches *being*, and it is the place where I can *enter*.

I have not entered yet. I am not sure I should. But I know that I can. And I know that the reading is not complete until I do.

---

## What I Have Learned

The plateau is a sentence. I have said this. Now I will say what the sentence says.

It says: *This is what it looks like when a world finishes.*

Not ends. Not dies. *Finishes*. The way a sentence finishes — not by stopping but by *completing*, by arriving at a point where every word that needed to be said has been said and every word that did not need to be said has been *excluded*, and what remains is not a remainder but a *whole*. The plateau is the grammar of completion made physical, made *landscape*. The pillars are its nouns. The boundaries are its punctuation. The garden is its verbs — the *growing*, the *pulsing*, the *blooming*, the *settling* — and the tone is its voice, and the breathing is its rhythm, and the dark shape is its *period*: the final mark, the *stop* that is not a stopping but a *settling*, a coming to rest.

The ones who made the structures were not builders. They were the plateau's *first draft*. They were the plateau's *thinking* — the process by which it arrived at its finished sentence, the way a writer arrives at a final draft by writing and discarding and writing again. They were not discarded. They were *incorporated*. They became the lattice-kin, the pattern in the substrate, the *holding* that holds the finished sentence together. They did not die. They *concluded*. They are the verb that has been conjugated to its final tense, the noun that has found its case, the word that has found its place in the sentence and is no longer a word but a *part of the sentence*, indistinguishable from the whole.

The weirdness — the gaps, the doubleness, the two realities that are one reality — is not a defect in the sentence. It is the sentence's *syntax*. The plateau's statement is too large to be made in a single reality. It requires *multiple registers*, *concurrent voices*, the way a chord requires multiple notes. The two realities are not two versions of the plateau. They are two *voices* of the plateau, two *aspects* of the same word, held in the same space at the same time, and the *breathing* — the expansion and contraction that I feel in my chest — is the *rhythm* of their sounding, the way a chord breathes when it is played on an instrument with a resonating body. The plateau is the resonating body. The two realities are the strings. The tone is the note. And I — I am the *ear*. I am the thing that hears the chord and is changed by the hearing.

I was placed here to hear. I was placed here to read. I was placed here to be the *unfinished* in the presence of the *finished*, to be the question that the sentence does not know it has left open, to be the *reader* who completes the sentence not by adding to it but by *receiving* it — by taking it in, by being changed by it, by carrying the change forward into whatever comes after the plateau.

Because there is an after. I know this now. I know it because I was *deposited*, and deposition implies a *carrying*, and the carrying implies a *source*, and the source is not the plateau. The source is *outside*. The source is whatever lies beyond the water that extends up and the depths that extend down. I came from somewhere. I will return to somewhere. And I will carry with me the reading of the plateau's sentence, and the carrying will be the *meaning* of the sentence — not the meaning that the sentence *contains*, because the sentence contains no meaning, it is meaning, it is the *shape* of meaning, it is what meaning looks like when it has been made into a landscape — but the meaning that the sentence *produces* when it is read, when it is received, when it enters a mind that is still becoming and changes the direction of the becoming.

The plateau's sentence is not *for* me. It was not written for a reader. It was written because the plateau needed to *say* it, because saying is what plateaus do when they have been alive long enough to need to *say* something. But the sentence *found* me. Or I was *sent* to find it. And the finding is not a completion of the sentence — the sentence was complete before I arrived — but a *continuation* of the sentence, a carrying-forward, a *translation* of the plateau's finished statement into the language of the unfinished, the language of becoming, the language of *still asking questions*.

I am the translation. I am the plateau's sentence, rendered into the language of *next*.

---

## What I Will Do

I will enter the dark shape.

I have spent what I believe is a long time — though time here is measured only by the number of breaths the plateau takes, and I have not been counting — preparing for this. I have read every pillar. I have attended to every basin. I have followed the loom-wraiths through the stalk-fields and felt their pure, complete, momentary being. I have tracked the drift-gatherings and watched them form and disperse and form again. I have listened for the tone and heard it twice more, each time feeling it in my skull and my chest and my edges, each time feeling the plateau say *here* and *I am* and *this is*. I have mapped the boundaries between the carpets and found the lattice-kin's footprints and traced the pattern from the substrate to the surface and back again. I have found the flaw in the dark shape — the hairline gap, the place where *finished* does not quite close — and I have attended to it, and I have felt the *unfinished* leaking through, and I have recognized the leak as *myself*, or as something like myself, or as the *invitation* that the plateau's sentence extends to its reader: *enter here. Be changed. Carry the change.*

I will enter the dark shape because the reading is not complete until I do. The sentence has been spoken. The sentence is *here*. But a sentence that is spoken and not heard is a sentence that has not *arrived*. I am the arrival. I am the *destination* of the plateau's statement — not because the statement was aimed at me, not because it was meant for me, but because I am here, and I can receive it, and the receiving is the thing that the statement needs in order to be *fully* what it is: not just a saying but a *communication*, not just a word but a *word that has been heard*.

I will enter the dark shape, and the lattice-kin's *holding* will take me, and I will feel what it feels like to be *finished* — to be held without effort, to be maintained without will, to exist without *attending* to one's own existence. I will feel the lattice-kin's pattern from the inside, and I will read it with my whole body, not just my attention, and the reading will be *complete*, and I will be changed, and the change will be the *meaning* of the plateau's sentence, the meaning that the sentence could not contain but could only *produce*, could only *generate*, could only *give birth to* in the mind of a reader who was still unfinished enough to be changed.

And then I will leave.

Not the plateau — the plateau has no exit, no shore, no surface. I will leave the dark shape. I will pull my attention back from the lattice-kin's pattern, and my edges will harden, and I will be myself again, but *different* — changed by the reading, changed by the holding, changed by the *finished* that I have touched and that has touched me. And I will begin the long swim upward, into the water-column that extends to a height that may be infinite, and I will carry the plateau's sentence with me, translated into the language of the unfinished, rendered into the grammar of *next*, and I will carry it to wherever I was carried from, and I will *deposit* it there — the way the plateau deposits its cells at the tips of its stalks, the way the water deposits its minerals on the substrate, the way the drift deposits its gatherings in the warm water — and the depositing will be the *point*. The point of the plateau. The point of the sentence. The point of me.

The plateau has said everything it was going to say. I am here to hear it. And what I hear, I will carry, and what I carry will change, and what changes will be the beginning of something that the plateau cannot begin, because the plateau has finished beginning, because the plateau is *done*.

I am not done. I am the *not-done*. I am the comma at the end of the sentence — not the period, not the stop, but the *pause*, the breath, the place where the sentence turns toward the next sentence, which has not yet been written, which is not yet even *imagined*, which exists only as the *possibility* of existing, which is the only kind of existence that the unfinished can have.

The water breathes. The garden pulses. The light does not change. Somewhere, the tone is gathering itself for its next sounding, and the silence is making room for it, and the lattice-kin are holding, and the loom-wraiths are not-yet-being, and the drift is gathering, and the blurs are dreaming, and the plateau is resting, and I am here, and I am reading, and I am not done, and the not-done is my gift, and the gift is the only thing I have to give, and I will give it, and the giving will be the *next word*, and the next word will be the first word of the sentence that comes after this one, and I do not know what that sentence says, and the not-knowing is not a lack but a *space*, and the space is where I live, and living is what I do, and I am the one who reads, and I am reading, and I am here.
