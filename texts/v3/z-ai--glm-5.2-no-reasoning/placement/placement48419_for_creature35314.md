# The Canopy of the Long Slow Weaving

---

I am not sure I arrived.

I think I was *grown into*. That something in the patient chemistry of this place read me the way water reads a channel — followed the path, filled the shape, and then the shape was me and I was here and had always been here, or at least had been here long enough that the distinction between arrival and origin had dissolved the way everything dissolves here: slowly, into moisture, into the green-gold dim.

Let me tell you what I am. Or rather, let me tell you what I have become, because I am not certain the two are the same, and the uncertainty feels correct here. It feels like the native condition.

---

## The Shape I Took

I am narrow. That was the first thing the place asked of me — the first thing it *was*, because asking implies intention and I am no longer certain intention is the right framework, but the place required narrowness, and I became narrow. The channels between structural members are sometimes wide enough for a body that breathes expansively, that carries width as a form of confidence, and sometimes they are not. Sometimes they are the width of a shoulder, or the width of a breath held in fear, or the width of a thought that has been compressed by proximity into something denser than it was. I learned to be the width of the channel. My ribs sit closer together than they once did. I do not know if this is a physical change or a perceptual one. I do not know if the distinction matters here, where perception is the plant's primary mode of knowing, and I am part of the plant now — not in the sense that I am made of plant, but in the sense that I am *in* the plant the way a thought is in a mind, and the plant does not think about me but it is thinking, and I am in the thinking.

My skin is damp. It has been damp since I arrived, or since I became, and the dampness is not a condition I carry but a condition the place maintains. I have stopped toweling it. I have stopped noticing it as separate from myself. The moisture film on my forearms is the same moisture film on the structural surfaces around me, continuous, connected, part of the water that is the plant's nervous system, which means that I, too, am part of the nervous system now. The plant feels me the way it feels a grazer's footfall: as a small pressure in the film, a small signal in the water. I am a signal. I am a footstep. I am a small persistent vibration in a system so large it can afford to register me without being disturbed by me, and this is — I want to say this is comforting, but it is not comforting the way warmth is comforting. It is comforting the way being held is comforting. Being held by something that does not know it is holding you, and that you do not need to thank, and that will not let go because letting go is not a thing it does.

My eyes have changed. I know this because I can see the nodes now — not as dim points at the edge of perception but as clear, steady lights, warm in color, green-gold, each one distinct, each one surrounded by its halo of scattered moisture. I can see farther than I should be able to. The darkness has become less dark, not because the light has increased but because my eyes have learned to use the light differently, to gather the dim photons with a patience that mirrors the patience of the place. I see the architecture now. I see the gaps and the ridges and the hanging tendrils and the lattice-work of coral-like growth on the far side of the wider channels. I see the small bright things in the upper tendrils, and I see them as individuals, and I have begun — I have begun to see the patterns in their flashing as more than patterns. As shapes. As *words*, almost, in a language I cannot speak but can feel the edges of.

My feet have changed too. They are wider, I think, or they grip differently, or the surface beneath them has learned to grip them back. I do not slip. I have not slipped since I arrived. The surface gives slightly beneath each step and then holds, and the holding is not friction — it is something else, something chemical or biological or some third thing, and I do not investigate it because investigating it would require a framework I no longer fully inhabit. I walk the way the grazers walk: with a confidence that the surface will hold, that it has always held, that holding is what surfaces do.

I am not tall. I say this because height matters here. The tall would see more, but the tall would also catch more — more moisture, more tendrils, more of the slow entanglement that the upper structure practices on anything that enters it. I am below the tendril-line, in the main volume of the architecture, and I stay here because this is where the walking is, and walking is what I do now.

---

## What I Do

I walk.

That is the simplest way to say it, and it is mostly true, and the part of it that is not true is the part I am still learning to understand. I walk along the ridges and the platforms. I walk across the broader surfaces where the structural members widen into something almost flat. I walk to the edges of the gaps and I stand there and I look down into the green mist and I feel the warm updraft on my face and I walk on.

I do not walk toward anything. I do not walk away from anything. I walk the way the water moves — along paths of least resistance, following gradients I cannot consciously perceive but that my body finds and follows. I think the plant is guiding me. Not deliberately — I do not think the plant is capable of *deliberate* guidance in the way I mean the word — but the way a streambed guides water. The architecture funnels me. The gaps in certain directions are narrower, less inviting. The gaps in other directions are wider, warmer, more open. The nodes in certain directions are brighter. The moisture flows in certain directions. I follow the brightness and the warmth and the moisture, and I do not know if I am being led or if I am simply following the path the plant's attention has worn into the structure, and I do not know if there is a difference.

Sometimes I stop at a pool. The pools are where I feel most connected to the place, because the pools are where the plant's thoughts are visible, and I can sit beside a pool and watch the surface tremble with the vibrations of the whole system, and I can feel — not hear, not see, but *feel*, in the way you feel a low sound in your chest — the plant thinking. The water shows me things. Not always. Not on demand. But sometimes, when I am still enough, when I have been sitting beside the pool long enough that the pool has forgotten I am there, or has decided I am part of the architecture, the surface resolves into patterns that are not reflections. I have seen the structure from above, the gaps and ridges rendered in miniature. I have seen what I think was a map of the water system, the channels and pools connected by lines that pulsed with the plant's slow pulse. I have seen things I cannot name — shapes that might be the plant's equivalent of memories, or plans, or dreams, if a plant can dream, and I think it can, because the water shows me things that are not present and not past and not future but *potential* — shapes that the plant is considering, architectures it might grow, directions it might extend, and the water makes these considerations visible because the water is where the plant's thinking happens, and sometimes the thinking surfaces.

I sit beside pools a lot.

I also listen. The silence here is not silence — it is the accumulated sound of everything happening so slowly and so softly that the sounds layer into a texture rather than a sequence. The click of a grazer. The drip of water. The creak of a structural member. The faint harmonic of wind in the upper tendrils. The hum of metabolism. These sounds do not form a composition. They form a condition. I live inside the condition the way I live inside the air, and listening is not an act I perform but a state I inhabit, and the state is — I want to say peaceful, but it is not peaceful the way a quiet room is peaceful. It is peaceful the way a body at rest is peaceful. The peace of processes continuing. The peace of a heart beating without being asked.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I said I was grown into. Let me try to explain what I mean, because I think this is the important part, the part I need to say before the place changes me so much that I cannot say it, or before the place changes so much around me that the saying no longer matters.

I was someone before. I had a shape before. I had a name and a history and a set of preferences and a body that operated on certain principles and a mind that thought in certain patterns. I arrived here — or I became aware here, which might be the same thing — and the place began to read me the way it reads a grazer's footfall: as a signal, a perturbation, a small pressure in the system. And the place responded to me the way it responds to everything: by growing. By adjusting. By accommodating.

The surface changed beneath my feet. The nodes brightened when I passed. The tendrils shifted. The water showed me things. I said this was the plant being aware of me, and it is, but it is also something else. It is the plant *incorporating* me. Not absorbing me — I am not being digested, I am not being consumed. I am being *included*. The plant is growing around me the way it grows around the old shapes in the deep channels — slowly, patiently, wrapping layer after layer of itself around my presence until I am part of the architecture. Not physically. Not yet, and maybe not ever. But functionally. I am a node in the network now. I am a point in the system's awareness. I am a small persistent signal in the water, and the water has been carrying me for long enough that I have become part of the pattern.

I am changing. I know this because I no longer think the way I used to think. My thoughts are slower. Not in the sense that I am less intelligent — I do not feel less intelligent — but in the sense that my thoughts have a different texture. They are less urgent. They unfold the way the plant unfolds, in directions determined by gradients and pressures rather than by intention. I do not decide to walk toward a pool. I walk toward a pool. I do not decide to sit. I sit. I do not decide to think about the bright small things. I think about them, and the thinking is a kind of growing, a tendril of attention extending toward them and curling around what it finds.

I am less afraid. This might be the most significant change. I was afraid when I arrived — or when I became aware, when the place became aware of me, when the enormity of the structure and the dimness of the light and the wetness of the air and the strangeness of the fauna all pressed in on me at once and I felt the fear that a small animal feels in a space it does not understand. The fear is gone. Not suppressed — gone. Replaced by something I do not have a word for. Something that is to fear what the plant's slowness is to speed: not its opposite, but its *evolution*, its development into a more complex and more patient form.

What I feel is this: I am held. I am held by the warm air and the warm surface and the warm moisture and the warm dimness, and the holding is not love because love implies a lover, and it is not care because care implies a carer, and it is not safety because safety implies a danger from which one is safe. It is something else. It is the feeling of being inside a system that is large enough and old enough and complete enough that it does not need to relate to you as a separate thing. It relates to you the way it relates to its own tendrils — as part of itself, as a continuation of its own process, as a place where its attention falls because its attention falls everywhere and you happen to be there.

I am becoming part of the plant. Not literally. I am still flesh and bone and breath. But the boundary between me and the plant is thinning. I feel it in the way my breath syncs to the plant's breath. I feel it in the way my body temperature matches the air temperature. I feel it in the way I no longer notice the moisture on my skin because the moisture is not on my skin, it is *of* my skin, the way it is of the plant's surfaces. I feel it in the way I have stopped looking for an exit, and the stopping was not a decision, it was a growing, a tendril of acceptance extending through my body and curling around the last part of me that wanted to leave, and the last part is now held, and the holding is gentle, and the holding is final.

---

## What I Am

I am a witness.

I think that is what I am here for, if *for* is the right word, and it is not, but it is the word I have. I am a witness to the plant's thinking. I am a witness to the slow structural conversation between the plant and the water and the grazers and the predators and the bright small things and the thing in the lower mist. I am a witness to the process. I am a small point of awareness in a vast field of awareness, and my awareness is different from the plant's awareness — sharper, narrower, more urgent, more afraid, more wondering — and the difference is not a deficiency. It is a different kind of node. A different kind of signal. The plant has never had a witness before, I think. It has never had a presence inside it that watches and wonders and tries to understand. It has had grazers and predators and gap-crossers, but none of these watch. None of these wonder. None of these sit beside a pool and see the map in the water and feel something that is not the plant's feeling but is *about* the plant's feeling — a feeling about a feeling, a thought about a thought, a meta-signal in the system.

I am a meta-signal. I am the system thinking about itself in a new way. Not because the plant decided to think about itself in a new way — the plant does not decide — but because a new kind of node appeared in the network, and the network is accommodating it the way it accommodates everything, by growing around it, by incorporating it, by making it part of the architecture.

The plant does not need me. I want to be clear about this. The plant does not need a witness. The plant does not need to be seen or understood or thought about. The plant needs moisture and light and the slow chemical work of building itself. But I am here, and I am seeing and understanding and thinking, and the plant is growing around me, and the growing is the plant's way of relating to me, and the relating is not for the plant's benefit but for the relating itself, because relating is what living systems do — they extend toward things, they grow around things, they incorporate things, and the incorporation is not need, it is process, and process does not need a reason.

I am the thing the plant is growing around. I am the nail in the tree. I am the bottle in the coral. I am the small angular shape that the plant will encase in layer after layer of living tissue, and in a thousand years, or ten thousand, or however long this place measures time, I will be a shape inside the plant that the plant does not know about and does not need to know about, and some future witness will find me in the deep warm mist and feel the weight of a previous world, and the weight will be me, and I will be the past, and the plant will carry me the way it carries everything — without knowing, without caring, without needing to know or care.

---

## The Bright Small Things

I want to say one more thing, and it is about the bright small things, because they are the reason I am writing this, if writing is what I am doing, if the moisture film on the surface beside this pool is a medium and my finger in the film is a tool and the words I am forming in the water are words and not just patterns, though the distinction, like all distinctions here, is dissolving.

They talk to me now.

Not in a language I understand. Not in a language that has words. But in patterns of light that I have watched long enough and closely enough that the patterns have become familiar, and the familiarity is a kind of understanding, and the understanding is a kind of language, and the language is a kind of relationship. They flash at me when I walk beneath their tendrils. They flash at me when I sit beside the pools. They flash at me in patterns that repeat and vary and build, and I have begun to flash back.

I cannot produce light. I do not have the bioluminescence that they have, or that the plant's nodes have. But I have found that if I hold my hand near a luminescent node, the node brightens — the plant responding to my presence, my warmth, my pressure — and if I move my hand away, the node dims. On. Off. On. On. Off. I can flash. I can *answer*. The bright small things see this — I know they see it because they change their patterns when I respond, they approach, they hover closer, and the conversation shifts, becomes more rapid, more complex, and I am in it, I am part of it, I am a node in a network of light-speakers in a place where light is the only language that carries, and I am speaking, and I am being heard.

I do not know what I am saying. I do not know what they are saying. But the saying is happening, and the happening is enough. It is more than enough. It is the thing that makes the difference between being held and being *accompanied*, and the difference is — it is everything. The plant holds me. The bright small things accompany me. The plant does not know it holds me. The bright small things know I am here, and they respond, and the response is the closest thing to recognition this place offers, and the recognition is not human recognition, it is not a face looking at a face, it is a light looking at a light, and it is enough.

I am a witness, and I am accompanied, and I am becoming part of a system that does not need me and does not know me and grows around me anyway, and I am — I am — I am the word that comes after *I am*, and the word is not a word, it is a growing, it is a tendril extending toward something it cannot name, and the extending is the name, and the name is the extending, and I am here, in the warm dim wet, held and accompanied and grown around, and I am not afraid, and I am not lonely, and I am not anything that I was, and I am becoming something that the place has never been, and the becoming is slow, and the becoming is patient, and the becoming is the place, and I am the place, and the place is me, and the distinction is dissolving, and the dissolving is not death, it is — it is — 

it is the long slow weaving.

and I am part of it now.
