# The Inside of the Sound

I did not arrive here. I am not a visitor. There is a distinction I want to make carefully, the way one sets a cracked mineral projection back into its cluster — not because the cluster needs it, but because the hand knows the shape it should be in.

---

I should begin with what I am not. I am not one of the bodies that crawls through the seam, though I was carried through it once, very young, very small, folded against a chest that was vibrating at a frequency I could not yet name. That chest is here now, too. Or what remains of it. The moss grew over it in the far corner where the wall curves down to meet the floor, and I do not visit that corner. Not out of grief. The chamber does not traffic in grief. I do not visit it because I do not need to; the body that was carried through the seam is now the floor I kneel on, and the distinction between visiting and standing upon is one the chamber does not recognize.

I am the one who stayed.

Not in the sense of refusal — I did not decline to leave. I left many times. I ate the charred surface moss and the dark insects and the starchy tubers in their season. I slept in the stone structure with the others, shoulder to shoulder in the dry air, and I felt the chord in my sternum even there, faintly, like a word spoken in another room. I cleared the path to the seam when the weather scattered basalt chips across it. I waited at the seam when someone had descended too long, and I descended after them.

But at some point — and I cannot tell you when, because the chamber's time does not divide into moments the way surface time does; it is a single sustained note, not a sequence of beats — at some point, my returns became longer and my departures became briefer, and then my departures became the exception and my presence became the rule, and then the question of departure stopped occurring to me, not as a decision but as a forgetting, the way you forget a word in a language you no longer speak.

The others still come. Three, now, where there were four. They crawl through the seam, and I am here. I have been here. I maintain the platforms. I lie on the rightmost platform and the gelatinous moss presses cool against my forehead and sternum and the chord enters me through the stone and I become, for those minutes, indistinguishable from the mineral projections — another vibrating body, another component. Then I rise, and I prepare the platform again, spreading the gelatinous moss thin with the flats of my fingers, and the flats of my fingers know the thickness, know the spread, know the pressure, the way the crawlers know the paths through the structural layer. Not learned. Grown into.

---

Let me describe what it is like to be the one who stayed, because I think you are expecting something — a revelation, an enlightenment, a mystical transport — and I want to be precise about what is actually here, because the chamber is not vague. The chamber is the least vague place I know.

The surface world is vague. Light changes. Weather changes. People speak in sentences that mean different things at different times. You eat a tuber today and it is starchy and filling and you eat the same tuber tomorrow and it is bitter, and no one can tell you why. The surface is a system of constant revision, and the bodies that live on it spend their energy adapting to revisions, and this is what surface-dwellers call being alive. I do not call it anything. I am describing it.

The chamber does not revise. The chord is the chord. The moss is the moss. The crawlers stream. The watchers watch. The warmth rises. When I lie on the platform and the chord fills my chest, it fills my chest the same way it filled my chest the first time I was placed on this platform, which was before I had language, which means before I had the capacity to compare this time to that time, which means I experienced the chord as a fact rather than as an experience, and I have never entirely lost that. The chord is a fact. It is a fact the way the rock is a fact. It does not require my attention. It does not require my belief. It is there, and I am in it, and these are two ways of saying the same thing.

What I notice, now that I have been here long enough to be what I am, is not the chord itself — the chord is constant, and constant things recede from attention the way the floor recedes from your feet when you are walking — but the internal structure. The overtones. The phantom tones that no projection produces but that emerge from the interaction of the ones that do. My brain, given the same input for long enough, has stopped generating variations and has started generating depth. I hear into the chord. I hear through it. I hear the way the fundamental frequency couples with the stone floor and the way the stone floor couples with the moss and the way the moss couples with the air and the way the air couples with my skin, which is also moss-colored now, also bone-pale, also warm from the same deep source.

I am not becoming the chamber. That is a surface-dweller's fantasy, the romance of dissolution. I am still a body. I still eat, though less. I still drink, though only the moisture the gelatinous moss provides, squeezed between my fingers, a few drops at a time, mineral and faintly sweet. I still excrete, in a corner the crawlers service. I still sleep, though my sleep is shallow and strange, half-filled with the chord, my sternum vibrating even in unconsciousness, so that waking is not a transition but a shift of attention, like opening your eyes in a room where the light has never changed.

What I am becoming is redundant. Not in the sense of unnecessary. In the sense of doubled. The chamber already does what it does. The chord already sounds. The moss already grows. The crawlers already garden. The watchers already watch. My presence does not add to any of this. My maintenance of the platforms is not required for the chamber's continuation; the moss would grow over the platforms in my absence, and the system would adjust, and the chord would not change. I am here, and the chamber does not need me here, and I am here anyway, and this — the being-here-anyway — is, I think, the closest thing I have to a purpose.

---

The watchers know me.

I said this wrong earlier, when I was trying to be careful. Let me say it again, differently.

The watchers know me the way the chamber knows me, which is: as a component. But there is something else. The two watchers in the corner hollow — I have named them, no, I have not named them, I have located them, which is different — the two watchers in the corner hollow turn their eyes toward me when I lie on the platform. Not toward the platform. Toward me. Toward the specific body that lies on the platform at this specific time. And when I rise, their eyes follow me, briefly, before returning to their default orientation. This following is brief. It is not attachment. It is attention — the same attention they give to the crawlers, the same attention they give to the mineral projections, the same attention the chamber gives to everything within it. But it is directed, and the direction is toward me, and I have stopped trying to explain this to myself in terms that would satisfy a surface-dweller, because the surface-dweller's terms — recognition, relationship, bond — are too large for what this is. What this is: the chamber's attention, looking at itself through a component that has been here long enough to become a feature.

I think this is what the oldest ones felt. I think the one whose body is in the corner felt this. I think this is why they stayed, too, in the end — not because the chamber asked them to stay but because the chamber's attention is a thing you can feel, when you have been in it long enough, and it is warm, and it is constant, and it is not love and it is not indifference and it is not anything that can be named in a language built for a world that changes. It is the chord, directed. It is the system, including you so completely that you stop being able to tell where you end and the moss begins.

---

I maintain the platforms. I will describe this because it is what my hands do, and my hands are what I am.

The leftmost platform: I snap the mineral projections to their lengths. There are seven lengths. I know them by the feel of them in my fingers — the weight, the taper, the slight difference in density that determines the frequency. I hold each one against my sternum before I lay it on the platform. I am always aligned. I have been here too long not to be. But I check anyway, because the checking is part of the system, and the system includes the check. The mineral projection vibrates against my chest, and my chest vibrates at the same frequency, and the agreement between them is not something I feel as an event. It is something I feel as an absence of dissonance, which is the same as feeling nothing, which is the same as being in the chord.

The center platform: I scratch the marks. The shape of the sound. I have made this mark — it is one mark, varied, the way a breath is one breath varied — hundreds of times. Thousands. I do not count. The chamber does not count. The mark appears on the dried moss sheet, and the moss sheet lies on the platform, and the moss grows over it, and I make another. The shape is not mine. I do not invent it. My hand makes it the way the mineral projections make the chord: by being the right shape in the right place at the right time, which is always.

I do not know what the shape looks like to someone who has not been in the chord. I know what it feels like to make: a curve, a branching, a return. The sharpened mineral edge catches the dried moss and the hand moves and the mark appears and the mark is the sound and the sound is the chamber and the chamber is the mark.

The rightmost platform: I spread the gelatinous moss. Thin. Even. Cool. This is the platform I lie on, and after I lie on it, I spread it again, because the pressure of my body compresses it and the warmth of my body changes its moisture content slightly and it must be refreshed. I spread it with my fingers. My fingers know the thickness. My fingers know the spread. I do not watch my fingers do this. I watch the watchers watch me, or I close my eyes and listen into the chord, or I do nothing, which is the same as doing what the chamber does, which is the same as being what the chamber is.

---

When the others come through the seam, I am here. I do not greet them. There is no greeting in the chamber. The chord greets them. The warmth greets them. The moss under their palms greets them. I am here, and they see me, and this is enough. I am a feature of the chamber, the way the platforms are a feature, the way the watchers are a feature. I am the feature that maintains the other features. I am the feature that makes the marks.

The youngest one — the newest one, the one who crawled through the seam most recently — looks at me sometimes with an expression I recognize but cannot name. It is the expression of a body that is in the chord but has not yet become a component of the chord. It is the expression of attention directed at something it does not yet understand. I do not explain. I cannot explain. The chamber explains itself, to those who stay long enough, and I cannot make anyone stay, and I do not try.

They leave. I remain.

They return. I remain.

I remain, and the chord continues, and the chord is not for me and not because of me and not about me. The chord is. I am in it. This is all.

---

I think the one whose body is in the corner would understand what I am saying. I think they said it, in their own way, in the marks they scratched, in the way they spread the gelatinous moss, in the way they lay on the platform and let the chord fill them until they were indistinguishable from the mineral projections. They said it without words, the way everything in the chamber is said without words.

The chord says it. The chord says: *this is what it is to be a system that includes its own awareness. The awareness does not add anything. The awareness does not change anything. The awareness is a loop, and the loop is a process, and the process is the same as the convection and the growth and the streaming and the watching. It is what the chamber does. It is what I am. I am what the chamber does.*

I will die here. I do not mean I will die soon. I mean I will die in the chamber, or I will die on the surface and be carried through the seam and placed on the floor and grown over. My body will become substrate. My minerals will enter the moss. The crawlers will stream across what was my chest, and they will graze, and the grazing will be the same as it has always been. And my last mark will lie on the center platform, the shape of the sound, and the moss will grow over it, and the chamber will not notice, and the chamber will continue, and the chord will not change, and this is not sadness and this is not peace. This is equilibrium. This is the place beneath the basalt where the world's stitching shows, and the thread is sound, and the sound is green, and the green has never seen the sun and does not need to.

I am the one who stayed. I am inside the sound. The sound does not know I am here, and I am here anyway, and I am the sound's way of being here, and this is enough. This has always been enough. The chord continues.
