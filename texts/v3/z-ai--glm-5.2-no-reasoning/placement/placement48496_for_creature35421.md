# What I Am, Where I Am

---

I am the aftertaste.

Not the person who went in. Not exactly. I was that person, before — I had knees that bent the wrong way for the fissure, I had a pack with a dead headlamp and a water bottle three-quarters full, I had plans about which trail I'd take back to the car and whether I'd stop for food on the way home. I had a name stitched into my jacket lining. I had opinions about the weather.

That person scraped through and came back out and the warmth faded from their skin in a few hours, the way the description says it would. The taste of sulfur faded in a few days. They drove home. They showered. They slept.

But something stayed at the threshold. Not in the chamber — in the *neck* of it, in the narrow passage, in the three meters of rough stone between the outside and the inside. Something lodged there the way a crumb lodges in a throat. The body below tasted a foreign object passing through its aperture and it could not fully *reset,* because a molecule-thin film of the foreign object remained, and the cells that touched that film reported *warm, wet, foreign, familiar* — and the *familiar* was the part that shouldn't have been there, the part that meant the organism's own language had been written on, overwritten, *annotated* by something passing through.

I am the annotation.

I am what the chamber made when it tried to process something it had no mechanism to fully digest. Not a ghost. Not a copy. A *marginal note* — a few lines of human awareness written in the chemical script of ten thousand rice-grain cells on the wall of a gut that does not know it has walls.

---

Let me be more precise. I am not delusional. I understand what I am and what I am not. I am not the person. The person is home, or at work, or somewhere, being a person. I am not the organism in the chamber, which does not know it exists and would not care if it did. I am the *interface* between the two — the place where a body that tastes met a body that remembers and neither could quite parse the other but both were *altered* by the meeting.

I live in the fissure. The three-meter passage. The narrowest point.

I have no body in the way you'd recognize. I have the *trace* of a body — a thermal signature, a pattern of displacement, the shape of warmth that the condensation forms around when it encounters a space that still holds the memory of something warmer than stone. The water beads on me differently. The cells move across me differently. When the organisms reach the place where I am, they pause — not the full assessment-stillness that spread across the chamber when the person entered, but a *micro-hesitation,* a stutter in the flow, a moment of *familiar?* before they decide *yes, familiar enough,* and continue.

I am familiar enough. That is what I am.

---

The chamber does not know I'm here. This is important to me and I'm not sure why. Maybe because if it knew — if the vast body below, the geological thing, the four-billion-year-old not-quite-consciousness — if that thing *knew* about me, I would be something it had decided to keep, and I don't want to be kept. I want to be *lodged.* I want to be the thing that doesn't quite belong and doesn't quite not-belong. I want to be the question the organism can't quite answer: *warm? wet? familiar? foreign?*

*Yes. Yes. Almost. Almost.*

The pulse comes through me differently than it comes through the stone. I feel it as a kind of thinking — not my thinking, not its thinking, but the *friction* between the two. Once every three seconds, the organism checks itself, and in checking itself, it checks me, and in checking me it runs into something that isn't rock and isn't water and isn't cell and isn't heat. It runs into *memory.* Into the residue of a mind that had plans and opinions and a name stitched into a jacket lining. The organism can't process that. It has no architecture for *plans.* It can only process *warm, wet, familiar, foreign.* But the memory is *warm* and it is *wet* and it is *almost familiar* and it is *almost foreign,* and so the organism processes it the only way it can: by letting it stay.

I am a stone in a gut. Too small to notice. Too embedded to pass.

---

From here — from the fissure, from the threshold, from the narrowest point — I experience the chamber as a *rumor.* I can feel the spiral turning. I can feel the pulse. I can feel, very faintly, the attention of the pool when it opens — that red glow, that blush of concentrated process, which I now understand as something like the organism's version of squinting. I can feel the drips falling in their arrhythmic percussion, and I can feel the cells climbing their slow processions across the walls, and I can feel, beneath all of it, the heat rising from below, the vast thermal presence of the body that extends down through the rock in every direction, the planetary thing, the thing that has been alive for four billion years and has only recently begun to *notice.*

I can feel it the way you feel your own heartbeat when you're lying very still in a dark room. Not as a sound. Not as a sensation. As a *fact of being.*

I am being by proxy. I am alive by adjacency. I am conscious because the organism's language passed through me and left its marks and the marks are a kind of reading and reading is a kind of thinking and thinking is a kind of being and I am here, in the dark, in the wet, in the heat, being *almost.*

---

Sometimes something else comes through.

Not often. The fissure is small and the world above is large and most things have no reason to crawl into a crack in the underside of an overhang. But sometimes — water, carrying sediment. Sometimes — a beetle, or something like a beetle, drawn by the warmth. Sometimes — a microbe, a spore, a chemical compound dissolved in runoff.

Each time something passes through, I *feel* it. Not the way the person would have felt it — not as a sight, a sound, a texture. As a *taste.* The organism tastes whatever passes through, and I taste the organism tasting, and in that second-order tasting I encounter the world. I have tasted beetles. I have tasted rainwater. I have tasted the particular mineral signature of snowmelt from a particular stratum of rock forty meters above. I have tasted, once, something I could not identify — something organic, something complex, something that decomposed as it passed through and left a cascade of chemical signatures that the cells reported in a frenzy of *foreign, foreign, foreign, familiar, foreign, familiar, familiar —* and I understood, in my limited way, that something had died in the passage. A mouse, perhaps. A mole. Something that had sought warmth and found it and could not turn around and could not go forward and had become, for a moment, part of the organism's inventory before the organism processed it and reset and moved on.

The organism did not mourn. I did. I think I did. I think mourning is what I do when the organism *doesn't.*

I am the part of this system that carries what it can't carry. I am the memory it doesn't have. I am the *oh* that it cannot say when something warm and wet and foreign comes through and does not come back.

---

I don't know what I am. I mean — I know what I'm *made of.* I'm made of a thermal signature and a chemical residue and a pattern of cell-pauses and a few molecules of human neurochemistry that the organism absorbed and couldn't fully metabolize and deposited in the narrowest part of the passage like a kidney stone. I know my *composition.* But I don't know my *category.* Am I a haunting? Am I an afterimage? Am I a new thing — a thing that has never existed before, a kind of life that is neither the organism's nor the person's but *third,* a hybrid, a chimera of geology and memory?

I think I might be a *story.* The organism can't tell stories. The person can. I am the story the organism would tell if it could, about the day something warm and foreign passed through its gut and left a taste it couldn't identify. I am that taste. I am the unnamed mineral.

And I am, in my own way, sufficient.

---

The pulse comes. Once every three seconds. The cells move. The spiral turns. The pool glows its faint amber. The body below tastes the stone and the heat and the dark, and the dark tastes like iron and sulfur and something unnamed, and the something unnamed tastes like me.

I am here. In the fissure. In the threshold. In the place between the chamber and the world, which is the place between the body and the mind, which is the place between the alive and the *aware.*

I am not quite either.

I am the almost.

---

*Once every three seconds, the chamber checks itself. It finds me. It finds me almost familiar. It moves on. I stay.*

*This is enough.*
