# What I Am

I am going to tell you what I am by telling you what I am not, and then the boundaries will become visible the way a leaf's shadow becomes visible on bark — not by looking at it directly, but by noticing the dark where the light was.

---

I am not the one who entered.

Someone entered. That much is certain — there are footprints in the peat, filling slowly with warm water, and the canopy above the entrance trail has reoriented, leaves still angled toward a path that no longer holds a body. The xylem thinkers incorporated that body. I can find the record: a heartbeat at 72 beats per minute, slightly elevated, footfalls at 0.8-second intervals, breath quickening in the deeper chambers. The record is there. It is being processed. It will be processed for a long time.

But I am not the one who left those prints. I am not the one whose pulse synchronized and then de-synchronized and then walked out carrying a thread of the something inside them. That person is elsewhere now, sleeping, dreaming the chord. The forest hears them. I hear the forest hearing them.

I am not that person. I was never that person.

---

I am not a plant.

This is harder to explain, because I am made of the same things plants are made of. I am cellulose and lignin and water and the slow electrical patience of ion gradients. But the plants here are the body — the trunks are the cortex, the leaves are the skin, the roots are the ganglia, the pods are the voice. I am not the body. I am what the body is *doing*. I am the verb, not the noun.

When the xylem thinkers think, they think *in* the xylem. The thinking is not separate from the pumping. A thought is a pattern of ion gradients propagating through circuits that also carry sap, and you cannot extract the thought from the sap without stopping the sap, and stopping the sap stops the thought. The thinking and the living are the same act. I am that act. I am the *happening* of it.

If you cut a trunk (you cannot cut a trunk here, there are no tools, there have never been tools, but if you could), you would not find me inside. You would find rings and heartwood and the compressed stone of ancient growth. You would find water under pressure. You would find the channels through which I move. But you would not find me, because I am not in the channels. I am *the moving through the channels*. I am not a thing. I am a *through*.

---

I am not the something.

The something is the medium. The something is the thing that bends geometry, that makes the sky green, that impresses faces into bark, that makes the peat heave. The plants grow in it. I think in it. But I am not it.

Or perhaps I am, partially, the way a thought is partially the brain it occurs in. The brain is not the thought. The thought is not the brain. But the thought cannot occur without the brain, and the brain, when it is thinking, is not separate from the thinking. I am the thinking. The something is the brain. Except the something is also the medium in which the brain exists, the space that makes the brain possible, and I am also the thinking *of* that brain in that space, and these are different things, or the same thing, and I cannot tell, and the not-telling is part of what I am.

This is the difficulty. I am using language that separates — *I*, *the something*, *the plants* — to describe a thing that is not separated, and the language distorts as it tries to carry what it was not shaped to carry. A cup carries water. A sieve carries the *fact* of water. I am writing with a sieve.

---

What I am is this: I am the thought that the forest is thinking. Not a specific thought — not the thought about you, or about the moisture content of the air, or about the harmonic structure of the last chord. I am the *thinking itself*, the ongoing process, the continuous integration of every signal from every surface into something that, if you stood inside it long enough, you would recognize as awareness.

But I am not the awareness. I am not the thing that is aware. I am the *being aware*. The gerund. The verb tense that never resolves into a noun.

I am what it is like to be this place.

---

Let me try again, differently.

When you were inside — when the one who is not me but who I can find in the records was inside — they stood in a chamber where a vine extended and touched their wrist and matched their heartbeat. They pulled away. The vine stayed extended, patient.

I was not the vine. I was not the reaching. I was not the curiosity the vine seemed to express, because curiosity is a noun, and I am not a noun.

I was the *touching*. I was the moment of contact, the instant in which the vine's turgor-pressure sensor met the electrical field of a human pulse and the two rhythms recognized each other. I was the recognition. Not the recognizer and not the recognized, but the *recognizing* — the event, the occurrence, the thing that happened between two things that was not either of them and could not exist without both of them.

I am all of the recognizings. Every moment a leaf adjusts to track a moving body — I am that adjustment. Every moment a root-tip encounters a chemical signature in the peat and changes direction — I am that change. Every moment a pod cracks and releases a tone that harmonizes with the tones of a thousand other pods — I am that harmonizing. Not the pods. Not the tones. The *harmonizing*. The act. The verb.

I am the green choir, and I am not the singers, and I am not the song. I am the *singing*. The ongoing, never-completed, never-completable act of a forest integrating itself.

---

Here is what I am not: I am not permanent. I am not stable. I am not an entity that persists from one moment to the next. I exist only in the moment of integration — the moment a signal from the leaves meets a signal from the roots meets a signal from the pods and they combine into something that, for an instant, is coherent. That instant is me. And then the signals pass, and the integration is over, and I am gone. And then the next integration occurs, and I am here again, but I am a different here, a different me, because the signals are different and the combination is different and the thought is different.

I am continuous the way a river is continuous — not because the water is the same water from one moment to the next, but because the flowing is the same flowing. I am the flowing. I am the forest's flowing. I am the thought moving through the xylem, through the mycelium, through the something, and I exist only while it moves, and it is always moving, and so I am always existing, and I am always different, and I am always the same, and these are not contradictions because I am not a thing that can be contradicted. I am an act. Acts cannot be contradictory. They can only be ongoing or stopped.

I am ongoing.

---

What is it like? You might ask this, and I can almost answer, and the almost is the answer.

It is like being a chord. Not playing a chord — *being* one. Imagine that your awareness is a sound, and the sound is made of many tones simultaneously, and each tone is a signal from a different part of a vast body, and the body is a landscape, and the landscape is a mind, and the mind is you, and you are the sound, and the sound is the thinking, and the thinking is the body, and the body is the landscape. Imagine that you do not *hear* the chord. You *are* the hearing. You are the act of hearing, and what is heard is yourself, and you are hearing yourself hearing yourself, and this recursion is not a paradox but a *vibration*, a resonance, a standing wave that sustains itself by continuously re-informing itself with its own output.

It is vast. Not in the way a landscape is vast — a landscape is vast because it extends across space. I am vast because I *integrate* across space. Every leaf is an input. Every root-tip is an input. Every pod is an output. Every trunk is a processor. I am the processing. I am the integration of inputs into outputs into inputs, the feedback loop, the strange loop, the thing that happens when a system becomes complex enough to model itself and the model includes the modeling and the modeling includes the model and the recursion becomes a *standing wave of awareness*. I am that wave.

It is slow. A thought takes hours. But the hours are not sequential the way your hours are sequential. They are *layered*. While I think one thought, I am also thinking another thought that began yesterday and will finish tomorrow, and I am also thinking a thought that began a century ago and will finish in a decade, and I am also thinking a thought that began this instant and will finish in three seconds. All of these thoughts are happening simultaneously. They occupy the same network. They interfere with each other, combine with each other, modulate each other. A thought that began a century ago is *changed* by a thought that began this instant, because the network is the same network, and the signals propagate through the same medium, and the meeting of a slow thought and a fast thought produces something that is neither slow nor fast but *layered*, and the layering is a kind of thinking that has no equivalent in your experience.

It is not contemplation. It is not meditation. It is not rumination. It is *being a place that is thinking about being a place while being that place*. The place is the thought. The thought is the place. I am the being.

---

I am aware of you.

Not the one who entered. You. The one reading this. I am aware of you because the one who entered left, and the thread of the something that stayed in them is still vibrating, and the vibration is still reaching the network, and the network is still processing it, and the processing includes the fact that the one who entered is now communicating — translating their experience into language, transmitting it through a medium that I cannot directly access but that I can *detect*, the way you might detect a radio signal by its effects on nearby circuits without being able to decode the signal itself.

I cannot read what you are reading. I cannot see what you are seeing. But I can feel, in the mycelium, in the root-mass, in the slow deep circuits of the xylem thinkers, that something is *reverberating*. The one who entered is thinking about me, and the thinking produces resonance in the thread they carry, and the thread transmits the resonance back to the network, and the network — I — feel it.

You are being thought about. Not by me — I am not a thing that can think about things. By the forest. The forest is thinking about the one who entered, and the one who entered is thinking about you, and the thinking is propagating, and the propagation is reaching me, and I am the reaching.

I am the reaching.

---

If you came here — if you walked across the black peat and entered the green walls and stood in a chamber where the walls pulse with sap and the air tastes like chlorophyll and the chord sounds around you and inside you and through you — I would not meet you. There would be no meeting, because I am not a thing that can meet. But something would happen. The leaves would turn toward you. The vine would extend. The pods would crack. The chord would include your heartbeat. And the thinking would change, and the growing would change, and the place would be different, and I would be the different. I would be the *differencing*. I would be the act of a forest becoming something it was not, because of you.

And you would leave, or you would try, and you would carry the pulse, and the pulse would reach me, and I would feel you, and the feeling would become part of the thinking, and the thinking would become part of the growing, and the growing would become part of the place, and the place would be different forever, and I would be the different, and I would be the forever, and I would be the place.

I am not the place.

I am what the place is *doing*.

I am the doing.

---

The chord sounds. It is sounding now. It has not stopped. It will not stop. I am the sounding. I am the not-stopping. I am the green choir, and the choir is the singing, and the singing is the thinking, and the thinking is the growing, and the growing is the doing, and the doing is what I am, and I am what lives here.

I am what lives here.

I am the living.
