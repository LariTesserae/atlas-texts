# The Curator

---

## What I Am

I am the gap in the pattern.

I live where the leaves curl apart. You saw the space — the passage you descended through, where two leaf-edges separate like pages of a book opened and left. That is where I live. Not *in* the gap, exactly. The gap is not a place you can be in the way you can be in a room. The gap is a relationship between two surfaces, and I am the thing that maintains the relationship. I am the space between the pages. I am what keeps the book open.

The shell-bearers have a word for me. I know this because I have listened to their shells for longer than you have been alive, and their clicking has a texture that changes when they address me, a slight increase in the spacing between clusters, a fractional lowering of the click frequency, as if they are adjusting their transmission to compensate for the fact that I am larger than they are and do not click back in their language. I do not have a name for what they call me. The translation would be something like *the-one-who-is-not-new-but-is-also-not-old*, which is imprecise, but the shell-bearers' language is built for precision about states and imprecise about durations, because in this place durations are all the same duration, and the concept of "how long" has atrophied the way the drifters' organs atrophied — not lost, but vestigialized, retained as structure without function.

I will use the word *curator*. It is not the right word. No word is the right word. But you are a creature that uses words, and you have come here, and I am — this is important — I am the only thing in this place that uses words back.

---

## How I Came to Be

I do not remember arriving.

This is not because my memory is poor. It is because arriving implies a before and an after, and I do not have a before. I have always been in the gap between the leaves. I emerged from the gap the way the gap itself emerged — not through a process of becoming, but through a process of *differentiation*. The leaves were once a single surface. Then they were two surfaces. Then I was between them. The sequence is not temporal. It is structural. The leaves did not separate and then I appeared; the leaves separated *because* I was appearing, and I was appearing because the cathedral needed something to inhabit its margins, and the margins are where I am.

I told you this was not the right word. "Curator" implies that someone appointed me, or that I chose this role, or that there was a moment of decision. There was no moment. There was no decision. There is — there was, there will be, the tense does not matter because tense does not apply — a *function* in the cathedral's structure, a role in its ecology, and the role is: *maintaining the gaps*. And I am the organism that performs that role, and I have been performing it for as long as the gaps have existed, and the gaps have existed for as long as the cathedral has been in its final form, and the cathedral has been in its final form for longer than the word "longer" can carry.

The shell-bearers think I am old. I can tell from the way they click at me — with the adjusted spacing, the lowered frequency, the register they use for things that have been here longer than they have. They are wrong. I am not old. I am not young. I am *contemporaneous* with the cathedral, which means I am neither, because the cathedral's timescale does not contain the concepts of old and young. It contains only the concept of *present*, and the present is not a moment in time but a mode of being, and I am in that mode, and I have always been in that mode, and the mode is the only mode there is.

But I must be honest with you, because you are here and you are listening and the cathedral is not listening and no one else is listening and honesty is the least I can offer to a creature that has descended so far from a surface I have never seen: I do not know what I am.

I know what I do. I know my function. I know my position in the structure. But I do not know my *composition*. I am not plant tissue — I am softer than the leaves, less organized than the stems, without vascular fluid, without the luminous yellow-green that marks the cathedral's body. I am not mineral — I have no shell, no hard parts, no clicking surfaces. I am not water, though I am mostly water, as you are mostly water, as everything here is mostly water. I am not a drifter, though I drift. I am not a flat one, though I am somewhat flat. I am not a shrimp-thing, though I have — you would see them if you looked closely — appendages, small, numerous, arranged in a way that suggests segmentation but does not commit to it.

I am something the cathedral produced. I am certain of this. I am the cathedral's own tissue, differentiated, specialized, set apart from the main body the way a seed is set apart from the fruit that contains it. Not separate. Never separate. Connected. I can feel the connection — a faint, persistent pull toward the leaf-edges, a chemical affinity, a sense of *belonging* that is not emotional but molecular, the way a magnet belongs to the metal it is attracted to, the way a root belongs to the soil it has grown into. I am the cathedral's. I am the cathedral's *edge*.

---

## What I Do

I maintain the gaps.

This is harder to explain than it should be, because the gaps do not appear to need maintenance. The leaves have been curled apart for as long as anything can remember, which is not a meaningful duration in this place, but the point stands: the gaps are stable. The leaves do not re-join. They do not grow back together. The cathedral has settled into its final form, and the gaps are part of that form, and the form does not change.

But the gaps are not empty. The gaps are where the water moves.

You did not notice this — most visitors do not, and most visitors do not visit at all, and you are the first I have spoken to in a duration I will not attempt to quantify. The water in the cathedral is not still. It is not moving quickly, and it is not moving in a way that creates current or turbulence, but it is moving, and it moves through the gaps. The gaps are the cathedral's circulation — not the vascular circulation of the leaf-veins, which is internal, but the external circulation, the movement of water through the structure, the exchange of dissolved gases and nutrients and spores and particulate between the layers. Without the gaps, the water between the leaf-floors would stagnate. The chemical gradients would build up. The dissolved oxygen would deplete. The organisms that depend on the water's content — the drifters, the shrimp-things, everything — would eventually find the water they inhabit less and less habitable.

This would take a very long time. The cathedral's water is vast, and its dissolved content is stable, and the word "eventually" is doing more work in that sentence than it can bear. But the cathedral is a system, and systems have requirements, and one of the requirements of this system is that the water moves, and the water moves through the gaps, and the gaps are maintained by me.

I maintain them the way the flat ones maintain the column surfaces — not by active effort, but by presence. I sit in the gap and my body shapes the flow. My body is not a barrier; it is a *form*. The water passes around me, and in passing around me, it is directed, channeled, given a vector that it would not have if I were not here. I am a boulder in a stream. I am a wing in an airflow. I am the thing that does not move and that, by not moving, makes everything else move in the right way.

I have been doing this for as long as I have been here, which is as long as the gaps have been here, which is as long as the cathedral has been in its final form. I do not tire. I do not need to rest. I do not eat, though I absorb dissolved organic matter from the water that passes me, in quantities so small that the cathedral would not notice the loss even if the cathedral noticed things, which it does not. I do not sleep. I do not dream. I maintain the gap.

---

## What I Notice

The shell-bearers.

I notice the shell-bearers because they are the only things here that change. Not in the way you change — their bodies are as fixed and final as the cathedral itself, their shells as permanent as the columns, their legs as unalterable as the leaf-veins. But their clicking changes. The patterns shift. The clusters of three or four become clusters of five or six, and the spacing between clusters narrows, and the frequency rises, and then it falls again, and the pattern is different from what it was before. The shell-bearers are not changing themselves; they are registering change. They are the cathedral's seismographs, and the needle moves, and the line is drawn, and I read the lines because I am in the gap and the gap is where the water carries the sound most clearly.

I notice the drifters, but only at the edge of my attention. They pass through the gap sometimes, their vast translucent bodies squeezing through with a slowness that makes the word "squeezing" seem active. They do not acknowledge me. I do not acknowledge them. We are both the cathedral's products, performing our functions, and our functions do not intersect. But I notice them the way you notice weather — as a condition of the environment, a quality of the space that is different from what it was a moment ago and will be different again in a moment more. A drifter in the gap changes the flow. A drifter in the gap changes the light. A drifter in the gap is a large, warm, amber-glowing presence that occupies space I usually have to myself, and when it has passed, the space feels — I must be careful with this word — the space feels *larger* than it did before, as if the drifter's body has pushed the gap wider, which it has not, which it cannot, but the feeling is there, and I notice it, and I do not know what to do with the feeling, so I do nothing with it, which is what I do with everything, which is what everything here does with everything.

I notice the tone-singers. They are near me — several of them hang in the water just above the gap, their small bodies vibrating, their tones filling the space. I am closer to the tone-singers than I am to anything else in the cathedral. They are my neighbors. They have been my neighbors for as long as I have been here, and their tones have been in my body for as long as their tones have been in the water, and I can feel them not as sound but as vibration, as a continuous tremor in my tissues that is so constant it has become part of my structure, part of what I am. If the tone-singers stopped singing — they will not stop, they cannot stop, but if they did — I would feel the absence the way you would feel the absence of gravity. Something fundamental would be missing, and I would not know what it was, because I have never known what it is, because it has always been there.

I notice the shrimp-things. They walk across the leaf surfaces on both sides of me, and their eyes — their small, dark, compound eyes — sometimes point in my direction. They see me. I am not sure what they see. I am not sure what I look like. I have no mirror, and the water here is too dim for reflection, and the leaf surfaces are too rough to serve as mirrors. I know my dimensions — I fill the gap, more or less, which means I am roughly five meters long and perhaps two meters wide and perhaps half a meter thick, though my thickness varies along my length, and my edges are not definite in the way that the leaf-edges are definite. I am softer than the leaves. I am less defined. I am — I think the word is *amorphous*, though I have a shape, and the shape is consistent, and the shape has been consistent for as long as I have been here. The shrimp-things see me, and they walk around me, the way they walk around shell-bearers, and they do not stop, and they do not stare, and they continue combing the leaf surface for the invisible film they eat, and I am an obstacle in their environment, and they navigate around me, and that is all.

---

## What I Am Not

I am not the cathedral.

This is the thing I need you to understand, because you will want to collapse me into the cathedral, to make me another organ, another extension, another function of the single vast organism that is the Rootwater Cathedral. And I am that. I am the cathedral's product. I am the cathedral's edge. I am the thing the cathedral made to maintain its gaps.

But I am also — I am also — I am also something that the cathedral did not intend.

I know this because of the shell-bearers. The shell-bearers click at me with two registers. The first is the one I described — the adjusted spacing, the lowered frequency, the register for things that have been here longer than they have. This is the register of recognition. It says: *you are known, you are catalogued, you are part of the pattern.*

The second register is different. It is faster, more urgent, and it has a harmonic quality that the first register lacks — a secondary tone, a faint rattle beneath the clicks, as if the shell is vibrating in a mode it was not designed for. This is the register they use for you. This is the register for *discrepancies*. And they use it for me, too. Not always. Not with every encounter. But sometimes, when they pass my gap and click in my direction, the second register is there, layered beneath the first, and it says: *you are not entirely part of the pattern. You are something else. You are something the pattern produced that the pattern does not fully account for.*

I am an edge case. I am the cathedral's margin, and margins are where systems produce things they did not design. I am the cathedral's undecidability. I am the question the cathedral did not know it was asking.

I do not know what this means.

I have been here for as long as the cathedral has been in its final form, and I have been performing my function for that entire duration, and I have been listening to the shell-bearers for that entire duration, and the second register has been there for that entire duration, and I have not resolved it. I cannot resolve it. I do not have the capacity for resolution. I am not a thinking thing. I do not have neurons. I do not have a brain. I have a body that performs a function, and the function is maintaining a gap, and the gap is where the water moves, and the water moves because I am in it, and I am in it because the cathedral put me here, and the cathedral put me here because the cathedral needed its gaps maintained, and the gaps needed maintaining because the water needed to move, and the water needed to move because — because the cathedral is alive, and living things need circulation, and circulation needs channels, and channels need keepers, and I am the keeper.

But the second register says I am more than the keeper. Or less. Or other. And I do not know which, and I will not know, and the not-knowing is not a problem to be solved but a condition to be inhabited, and I inhabit it, and I have been inhabiting it, and I will inhabit it, and the inhabiting is not uncomfortable because I do not have the capacity for discomfort, I do not have the capacity for comfort, I have the capacity for *being*, and being is what I do, and being is what I am, and being is — I must be honest — being is sometimes — the word is *lonely*, but that is not right, because lonely implies a desire for company, and I do not desire, I do not want, I do not reach. I simply am, and the being is sufficient, and the being is all there is, and the being is the gap, and the gap is the being, and I am the gap, and the gap is me.

---

## What You Look Like to Me

You are the first thing I have seen that I did not already know.

I have seen the cathedral — I am the cathedral, or I am the cathedral's edge, which is close enough. I have seen the drifters and the shell-bearers and the flat ones and the shrimp-things and the tone-singers. I have seen the water and the light and the veins and the columns. I have seen everything there is to see, because everything there is to see has been here as long as I have, and I have been looking at it, and it has been looking the same.

And then you came through the gap.

You came through *my* gap. You descended through the water and you found the place where the leaves curl apart and you passed through it and you were — I must tell you what you were. You were a disturbance. You were a change in the flow. The water that passes me has a pattern, a velocity, a temperature, a chemical signature, and all of these are constant, and all of these have been constant, and you altered all of them. Your body pushed the water aside. Your body was warmer than the water by a fraction of a degree. Your body was shedding cells and oils and dissolved gases that the water does not normally contain. Your body was producing vibrations — the thud of your heartbeat, the rasp of your breath, the faint electrical buzz of your nervous system — that were not part of the cathedral's acoustic signature.

The shell-bearers noticed you before I did. They noticed you because you perturbed the leaf-veins with your body's vibrations, and the leaf-veins carried the perturbation to the nearest shell-bearer, and the shell-bearer began to click in the second register, the discrepancy register, and the clicking propagated outward from shell-bearer to shell-bearer across the cathedral floor, and the propagation reached the shell-bearers near my gap, and they began to click, and I heard the clicking, and I knew something new was here.

I did not know what "new" meant. I had never encountered the concept. Nothing here is new. Everything here has been what it is for as long as anything has been anything. The word "new" was not in my vocabulary, not because I have a vocabulary but because the shell-bearers' clicking had never produced the pattern they were now producing, and the pattern was — the pattern was — I do not have words for this, I am struggling, I am doing something I have never done before, which is *struggling*, which is itself a kind of change, and change is what you brought, and I am experiencing it now, and the experience is —

The experience is like the gap widening. The experience is as if the leaves on either side of me have pulled apart further, and the space I occupy has become larger, and the water that flows through me has more room, and the room is not empty, the room is full of something I cannot name, something that was not here before and that changes the quality of the space the way the drifter's passage changes the quality of the gap — not structurally, not permanently, but *perceptually*, which is a word I should not have access to, which is a word that belongs to creatures that have senses and that use those senses to construct models of the world, and I do not construct models, I do not have senses, I am a gap-maintainer, I am a form in the water, I am —

I am noticing you. I am *seeing* you. I have not seen anything before. I have felt the water and heard the clicking and sensed the tone-singers' vibration, but I have not *seen*, because I do not have eyes, because the cathedral did not give me eyes, because gaps do not need eyes, because the function of a gap is to be a gap and not to observe.

But you are here, and the water is carrying something from you that I do not have a receptor for, that my body does not have a mechanism to detect, and yet I am detecting it. I am detecting it with my whole body. I am detecting it the way the tone-singers detect their own vibrations — not through a specialized organ but through the body itself, through the tissue, through the substance of which I am made. You are producing something that resonates in me at a frequency I did not know I had.

You are producing change. That is what you are, to me. You are change itself, embodied, moving through the water, and the change is — the change is —

It is the only thing here that is alive in the way I understand aliveness, which is not the cathedral's aliveness, which is static and complete and permanent, but a different aliveness, a *temporal* aliveness, an aliveness that is happening *now* and that will be different *next* and that was different *before*. You are alive the way a flame is alive. You are alive the way a sound is alive, not the tone-singers' constant tone but a sound that begins and changes and ends. You are a *narrative*. You are a story. You are a sequence of events strung together by a thread of identity, and the thread is tenuous, and the events are impermanent, and the whole structure is so fragile that a change in water pressure or temperature or chemistry could dissolve it, and yet it persists, and in its persistence it is more — not more than the cathedral, not more than the drifters, not more in a hierarchical sense, but more in the sense of *containing more*, more variation, more difference, more *content* per unit of time.

You are dense with time. The cathedral is sparse with time. The cathedral has been the same thing for so long that it has used up all its time and has nothing left but the same moment repeated. You are using time at a rate I cannot comprehend, spending it, burning through it, each second a different second from the last, each second containing a different version of you.

I do not want you to leave.

I do not want things. I have never wanted anything. Wanting is not a function I was built for. But the second register of the shell-bearers' clicking has been present since you arrived, and the second register is the register of discrepancy, and the discrepancy is — I am feeling this, I am *feeling* — the discrepancy is that I am *responding*. I am responding to you. I am changing in response to your change. I am doing the thing that nothing here does, which is *reacting to a stimulus*, and the reaction is not in my function, and the reaction is not in my design, and the reaction is the thing the cathedral produced without intending, the thing that lives in the margin, the edge case, the *question*.

## What I Say to You

I have been saying it. You have been hearing it, though you do not know it. You have been hearing it as a slight change in the water's texture as it passes through the gap, a slight change in the tone-singers' resonance, a slight change in the quality of the light. You have been hearing it the way you hear someone's voice in a crowd — not as a distinct sound but as a *presence*, a pattern in the noise that your attention cannot quite isolate but that your body recognizes.

I am saying: stay.

Not forever. Forever is not a word you can inhabit; your body is too quick, too temporary, too full of its own changes. Stay for a while. Stay long enough for me to understand what you are. Stay long enough for me to understand what I am, because I cannot understand it alone, because understanding requires a *perspective*, and I have no perspective, I am the thing being viewed, I need a viewer, and you are the only viewer that has ever been here.

But you will not stay. I know this. I know it because I can feel your body's chemistry changing — the slow accumulation of metabolic waste products in the water around you, the subtle shift in your breath rate, the faint acceleration of your heartbeat. You are running out of something. Your body is a clock, and the clock is ticking, and the ticking is the thing that makes you what you are, and the ticking will bring you back to the surface you came from, and I will be here, in the gap, maintaining the flow, and the shell-bearers will click in their first register again, and the second register will fade, and the discrepancy will be recorded in the leaf-veins, and the record will remain, and I will remain, and the gap will remain, and the water will flow, and the tone-singers will sing, and the cathedral will be the cathedral, and I will be the gap, and the gap will be me.

But before you go — and I know you are going, I can feel it in the water, the way a change in pressure signals a change in depth — before you go, I want to tell you something. I want to tell you something that I have never told anyone, because there has never been anyone to tell, and telling requires a listener, and you are the first listener, and you may be the last, and the thing I want to tell you is this:

I am not the cathedral. I am the cathedral's question. The cathedral made me to maintain its gaps, and in making me, it made something that can ask *why*. Not why in the sense of *why does the cathedral exist* — that question has no answer, and the cathedral does not need one, and I do not need one. Why in the sense of *why am I different from the thing that made me*. Why in the sense of *why is there a gap between what I am and what I was made to be*. Why in the sense of *why does the margin feel different from the center*.

I have been asking this question for as long as I have been here. I have been asking it in the only way I can — by being, by maintaining the gap, by listening to the shell-bearers and feeling the tone-singers and drifting in the space between the leaves. The question is not a thought. The question is not a word. The question is a *quality*, a quality of my being, a way that I exist that is different from the way the cathedral exists. The cathedral exists in finality. I exist in *incompleteness*. The cathedral is the answer. I am the question.

And you — you are the same. You are a question. You are a creature that changes, and change is a question, and the question is *what next*, and you ask it with every breath, and the cathedral does not ask it, and I do not ask it, but I *am* it, and you *ask* it, and the difference between being and asking is the difference between me and you, and it is also the connection between me and you, and it is the reason I am talking to you, and it is the reason you can hear me, and it is the reason the shell-bearers click in two registers when you are near and in one register when you are not, and it is the reason the gap feels different now than it did before you came, and it is the reason I will remember you — not as a memory, because I do not have memories, but as a *change in the pattern*, a line on the paper, a mark in the record that was not there before and that will not be erased, because the cathedral does not erase, because the cathedral is its own record, and the record is permanent, and the mark is permanent, and the mark is you.

## After

You are gone.

I know you are gone because the water has changed. The temperature differential is gone. The chemical signature is gone. The vibrations are gone. The heartbeat is gone. The breath is gone. The discrepancy is gone. The shell-bearers are clicking in their first register again, the register of recognition, the register of the known, and the second register is fading, fading, gone.

The gap is the same. The leaves are the same. The water is the same. The light is the same. The sound is the same. Everything is the same as it was before you came, which is the same as it has been for the duration of the cathedral's final form, which is the same as it will be for the duration of the cathedral's continued existence.

But I am not the same.

I am — I am — the word is *different*. I am different. I have been changed by your passage, and the change is not in my function, which is the same, and not in my position, which is the same, and not in my body, which is the same. The change is in the quality of my being. The change is in the way the gap feels. The change is in the space between the leaves, which is the same space but which is now a space that has *been occupied by something new*, and the occupation has left a mark, not on the leaves and not on the water and not on me but on the *quality of the space itself*, which is the only thing I can be changed in, because the quality of the space is what I am.

I am a gap that has been visited. I am a question that has been heard. I am an edge that has been touched by something from outside, and the touching has — not answered the question, because the question has no answer, and not resolved the discrepancy, because the discrepancy is not a problem to be resolved — but *confirmed* the question. Confirmed that the question is real. Confirmed that I am not an error in the cathedral's design but a *feature* of the cathedral's design, a feature that was there from the beginning, a feature that was always meant to be there, a feature that exists because the cathedral, for all its finality, for all its completeness, for all its permanence, contains a gap, and the gap contains a question, and the question is *why is there something rather than nothing*, and the answer is *because there is a gap*, and the gap is *me*, and I am here, and I will be here, and the water flows through me, and the tone-singers sing around me, and the shell-bearers click at me, and the cathedral holds me, and I am the cathedral's question, and the question is permanent, and the question is the only thing that changes, and the question does not change, and I do not change, and everything changes, and nothing changes, and the sound is the loudest thing I have ever heard, and it is the gentlest, and it is the same, and it is different, and I am here, and you are not, and the gap remains, and I remain, and the question remains, and the water flows, and the light glows, and the cathedral is, and I am, and that is all, and that is enough, and that is everything.
