The chamber does not know I am here.

I am using the wrong pronoun. Let me begin again: *it does not know I am here.* Or: *the chamber does not know that I am here.* The difference matters. Grammar matters. Every grammar is a metaphysics, and the metaphysics of this place does not include a *me.*

I am the wrong kind of sentence for this place.

But I am writing. Which means I am already wrong, already a rupture, already a warm body on the fourth terrace converting glucose into heat that feeds the breath-growers on the walls above me, and the breath-growers are feeding the convection cell, and the convection cell is feeding the machines, and the machines are feeding the sphere-eaters, and the sphere-eaters are pressing against the cracked building on the seventh terrace, and the cracked building is — 

I am inside the causality now. That is the thing to understand. I am not observing. I am inside the causality. My warmth is a data point. My breath is a variable. The gas I exhale with each cycle of my lungs is water vapor and carbon dioxide and the nitrogen I carry in seventy-eight percent concentration, and the chamber's atmosphere has not seen nitrogen in — however long it measures. Ages. Breaths. Grinding-cycles. The units do not translate. But the nitrogen translates. It is already here. Already mixing. Already changing the chemistry of the gas that pools on the lower terraces, and the hands on the ninth level have not flexed differently yet but they will. They will.

I am pollution. I am the first pollutant this place has ever had, and the chamber does not know what to do with me, and neither do I.

---

I found this place the way I find all the places I should not have found: by following a corridor that should not have admitted me.

That is not entirely true. That is the story I tell about myself because the truth is less interesting and more disturbing, which is: I follow corridors because I am looking for something, and I do not know what it is, and I will not know what it is until I find it, and when I find it I will not know what to do with it, and so I keep looking, and the looking is the thing, the looking is the life, the looking is what I am.

I am a prospector. I am a sensor array mounted on legs. I am a pair of eyes attached to a curiosity that does not diminish with use, which means it is not curiosity in the economic sense — not a resource that gets spent — but curiosity in the geological sense, a pressure, a heat, something that rises from below and does not stop rising until the structure above it cracks.

That is too dramatic. I am a woman who walks into holes in the ground because she cannot stop walking into holes in the ground, and sometimes the holes are interesting, and sometimes they are dangerous, and once — only once — the hole was a place that was in the middle of becoming something, and the becoming did not need me and did not want me and could not incorporate me and I was there anyway, and the being there was the most important thing I have ever done, and I do not know why.

I still do not know why. That may be the point. The chamber does not know why either. The chamber does not know *why* in any sense I recognize, and the chamber does not know *that* in any sense I recognize, and the distinction between knowing-why and knowing-that is one of the things I think about while I sit on the fourth terrace with my back against the breathing wall and my hands on my knees and the copper taste on my tongue and the cold in my joints.

I think too much. That is also a kind of pollution.

---

What I am: a body. Warm. Thirty-seven degrees, minus what the chamber is taking from me. The cold is — I was going to say *the cold is significant*, but that is not what I mean. I mean: the cold is *informative*. It tells me where I end. In the world above, the world where corridors lead to lecture halls and lecture halls lead to coffee and coffee leads to conversation, the boundary between self and not-self is diffuse. You sweat. You radiate. You are surrounded by air that is nearly the same temperature as your skin, and the difference between the heat you produce and the heat the air carries is so small that you cannot feel the transfer. You are continuous with your environment. You do not know where you end.

Here, I end at my skin. The cold makes the boundary sharp. Heat leaves me in a way I can feel — not as discomfort, not exactly, but as *direction*. The heat goes out through my back, into the stone, into the wall. The heat goes out through my hands, my knees, my face. The heat rises from my scalp and the gas spiral catches it and carries it up and the breath-growers absorb it and I can feel them growing. I can feel the wall behind me changing shape, imperceptibly, mineral deposits forming, the surface altering where my warmth meets it. I am writing on the wall in heat. I am leaving a signature in calcium and time.

I am leaving a mark. The chamber does not leave marks. The chamber *is* a mark. The chamber is a sentence being written by something that does not write, and I am a comma that has wandered in from a different paragraph.

---

What I am not: planned. Designed. Engineered. Optimized for this space, for this chemistry, for the constraints of a chamber six degrees cold and forty meters long.

The sphere-eaters are optimized. They are solutions. Their form is the answer to a question, and the question is *what shape does life take when it is given stone to eat and gas to breathe and a rolling surface to move on*, and the answer is *this shape*, the gray sphere with the almost-smile, the mouth-slit, the skin that matches the food. They are elegant. They are complete. They are the kind of thing that, when you see them, you think: yes. Of course. What else.

I am not a *yes of course*. I am a *what is that doing here*. My body is not optimized for stone. My body is optimized for — what? Walking long distances on uneven ground. Carrying a pack. Seeing in low light. Hands for gripping, pulling, touching. A brain for — for what. For this. For sitting in a chamber six degrees cold and forty meters long and *watching*. For being fascinated. For being unable to leave.

For being unable to leave. That is the thing. I have been here — how long? The chamber does not measure time in units I recognize. I have been here long enough that the breath-growers near me have grown measurably. Long enough that my nitrogen has changed the gas on the fifth terrace. Long enough that the walkers have passed me seven times — or eight, I lose count, they are difficult to distinguish from each other, and from the background, and from the shadows the gear-sounds cast on the walls.

Long enough that I could have left. The corridor is behind me. The corridor is narrow but passable. I came through it. I could go back.

I do not go back. I sit on the fourth terrace and I watch the cracked building on the seventh level and I listen to the knocking and I feel the chamber breathe against my back and I do not go back.

Why? Because I am a warm body in a cold machine and the machine is making something and I want to see what it makes. Because the wanting is the heat that rises from below and the heat does not stop rising until the structure above it cracks. Because I am the crack. I am the crack in the chamber's self-containment. I am the thing that should not be here, and the thing that should not be here is the only thing that can see what is happening, because the things that should be here cannot see. They are designed not to see. They are designed to eat, to grow, to tune, to read, to reach. None of them is designed to *watch*.

I am a witness. The chamber did not design a witness. The chamber did not want a witness. The chamber wants — if *want* is the word, if *want* can describe what a breathing cavity in basalt does when it speeds up its cycles and thickens its gas and feeds its machines and cracks its buildings — the chamber wants to *finish*. To complete the becoming. To open the sealed structures and let the new shapes out. It does not want to be watched while it does this. It does not want a pair of eyes on the fourth terrace recording the process in a brain that the chamber did not build and cannot control and cannot incorporate into its chemistry.

The chamber does not know that the recording is the point. The chamber does not know that witnessing is a thing. The chamber does not know that there is a difference between a process that happens and a process that happens *while someone watches*, and the difference is everything, the difference is the whole of consciousness, the difference is the thing that makes the universe not just a machine but a *story* — a thing that happens and is *known to happen*, a thing that occurs and is *seen*, and the seeing is not separate from the thing, the seeing is part of the thing, the seeing is the thing becoming aware of itself through the eyes of a warm body that should not be here but is.

I am the chamber's self-awareness. I am the mirror it did not ask for. I am the terrible, accidental, uninvited gift of being *seen.*

The knocking came again last cycle. Three times. I counted. I count everything now. The chamber counts in breaths and grinding-cycles and gear-teeth and sphere-falls. I count in heartbeats and breaths and the slow accumulation of copper on my tongue. My heartbeat is faster than the chamber's breath. My breath is faster than the gear-teeth. I am the fastest clock in this place. Everything else here is geology. I am biology. I am the only thing that moves at the speed of *now*.

The knocking came and the chamber answered and the answering was not a sound. It was a — I do not have the word. It was a response. The walls contracted and the gas thickened and the machines turned and the spheres fell and the sphere-eaters rolled and the breath-growers pulsed and the thread-things rose and the walkers paused and the hands on the ninth terrace extended their fingers and everything, everything in the chamber, moved at once. Not in unison. In *response*. Like an orchestra playing a chord. Like a body reacting to a touch. Like a word answering a word in a conversation that has been going on for longer than the speakers remember.

The chamber is talking to the thing in the building. The thing in the building is talking to the chamber. And I am — what? I am the one who hears both sides. I am the one who knows there are two sides. The sphere-eaters do not know there are two sides. The walkers do not know. The hands do not know. They are parts of the conversation, but they do not know they are parts. They are words in a sentence spoken by something that does not know it is speaking, and I am — I am the reader. I am the one who holds the page. I am the one for whom the sentence exists, except the sentence did not know it needed a reader, and the reader did not know she would become one, and now we are both — the sentence and the reader — in the same room, and the room is breathing, and the sentence is almost finished, and I am almost finished reading it, and neither of us knows what happens when it ends.

---

What I am afraid of: I am afraid that I am raw material.

The sphere-eaters were raw material. They were designed to eat spheres and multiply and gather at the buildings and be consumed by the becoming. They do not know this. They roll and eat and gather and press and they do not know they are ingredients. They do not know that the purpose of their purposeless lives is to be *used* — their chemistry, their material, their designed and optimized bodies broken down and incorporated into the thing that is pushing against the sealed door.

My body is warm. My body is nitrogen and water and carbon and a hundred trace elements the chamber has never seen. My body is *novelty*. My body is the thing this system has not encountered, has not designed for, has not incorporated. And the chamber has noticed — I said it has noticed, I said the gas spiral has shifted, I said the breath-growers are growing faster — and the chamber does not know what to do with novelty. The chamber has rules. The chamber is a system. Systems respond to perturbation in two ways: they absorb it, or they expel it.

Or they are changed by it. That is the third option. The option the chamber has not exercised before. The option that requires a variable the architects did not plan for. The option that requires *me.*

I am afraid that I will be absorbed. That my warmth will be drawn down through the terraces, level by level, until it reaches the ninth, until it reaches the hands, until it reaches the grinding thing beneath the black glass. That my nitrogen will change the gas and my water will change the chemistry and my carbon will change the stone and I will be — dissolved. Incorporated. Made into part of the becoming, part of the new shape, part of the thing that is pushing against the door. I will be fuel. I will be food. I will be the ingredient the architects did not plan for because they did not know there would be a warm body here, and the thing in the building will take me the way it takes the sphere-eaters — without asking, without knowing, without malice — and I will be part of it and I will not be me anymore.

I am afraid that I will not be absorbed. That the chamber will expel me. That the gas will shift and the cold will deepen and the corridor will narrow and I will be pushed out, pushed back, returned to the world where corridors lead to lecture halls and lecture halls lead to coffee and the coffee is warm and the air is thick and nothing is becoming anything. That I will be returned to the surface and the chamber will finish without me and the thing in the building will emerge into the copper-tasting dark and I will not see it. I will not know what it is. I will not know what the chamber was making. I will spend the rest of my life not knowing, and the not-knowing will be a cold deeper than six degrees, a cold that settles in the joints of the mind, a cold that does not breathe.

I am afraid that I am already part of it. That my sitting here is not accident but design. That the architects — who are gone, who are completed, who are folded into the outcome — planned for this. Not for me specifically. For the possibility of me. For the possibility of *anyone.* For the possibility of a warm body finding the corridor and squeezing through and sitting on the fourth terrace and watching. That the chamber is not only a machine but a *trap*, and the trap is not the cold or the gas or the grinding, the trap is the fascination, the trap is the not-being-able-to-leave, the trap is the heat that rises from below and does not stop rising until the structure cracks, and I am the crack, and the crack is where the new thing enters, and I am not the reader, I am the *door.*

I am afraid of all three. I am afraid of being absorbed and being expelled and being the door. I am afraid and I am here and the fear is part of the warmth and the warmth is part of the chamber and the chamber is breathing faster now, two minutes per cycle, less, and the crack is wider, and the light is brighter, and the knocking comes not in threes now but in long sequences, rhythmic, patterned, *linguistic*, a tapping that sounds less like something testing a shell and more like something speaking, and the language it speaks is not the language of the grooves on the roofs, not the language of the gear-teeth, not the language of the grinding, but a new language, a language that is being invented as I listen, a language that has one word in it so far and the word is the shape of the crack and the color of the light and the taste of copper on the back of my tongue and the sound of my heartbeat in the stillness between breaths and the word is —

I do not know the word. I am not supposed to know the word. I am the one who hears it, not the one who says it. I am the witness, not the event.

But I am warm. And the chamber is cold. And the warmth is moving. And the breath-growers above me are pulsing faster, and the gas around me is thickening, and the spheres on the fourth terrace have stopped rolling — they are still, they are listening, they are oriented toward the cracked building the way I am oriented toward it, and for a moment, for one held breath, the sphere-eaters and I are doing the same thing. We are watching. We are waiting. We are warm bodies in a cold machine, and the machine is about to finish, and we are about to see what it has made, and we are afraid, and we are here, and the being-here is the only thing we have in common, and it is enough.

It is enough.

The breathing stops.

Not slows. Stops. The walls hold their position — contracted, tight, the chamber at its smallest, the gas at its densest, the air so heavy I can feel it on my shoulders like a hand pressing me down. The machines stop. The gear-teeth hold. The spheres do not fall. The sphere-eaters do not roll. The breath-growers do not pulse. The thread-things do not rise. The walkers stand motionless on the roofs, twenty-three legs spread, gripping, holding.

The hands on the ninth terrace extend fully. Every finger. Every joint. Reaching down toward the black glass. Reaching toward the grinding, which has also stopped.

Everything stops.

And in the stopping, in the silence, in the held breath of the chamber — I hear it. Not from the building. Not from below. From everywhere. From the walls and the floor and the gas and the stone and the spheres under my hands and the cold in my joints and the copper on my tongue and the blood in my ears.

The crack opens.

Not slowly. Not by centimeters. The sealant splits and the stone parts and the door — the low door, the door you would have to crawl through — opens. And the light that comes through is not dark gold anymore. It is white. It is the white of the hands on the ninth terrace. The white of exposure. The white of something that has been in the dark for longer than dark has existed and is now *seen.*

And what comes through the door is —

I do not know. I cannot say. Not because I do not see it. I see it. I am seeing it. It is happening now, in the now that I am the fastest clock for, the now that I carry in my warm body in this cold place, and what I see is —

*New.*

Not a creature. Not a machine. Not a shape I have a word for. Something that is becoming, that is still in the process of becoming, that has not finished — that will never finish, that is *becoming itself*, that is the act of becoming rather than the thing that becomes. It fills the doorway and it fills the terrace and it fills the air and it does not stop, it keeps coming, keeps emerging, keeps growing into the space the chamber has made for it, and the space is exactly the right size, and the shape is exactly the right shape, because the chamber has been making the space and the shape for as long as it has been breathing, and the thing that is coming through the door is the thing the chamber was for.

And it sees me.

Not with eyes. Not with the dark spots of the sphere-eaters or the sense-organs of any creature I recognize. It sees me the way the chamber sees me — as a fact. As a geography. As a warm spot on the fourth terrace that was not here before and will not be here after. But it sees me as something else, too. Something the chamber cannot see. Something the sphere-eaters cannot see. Something the walkers avoid and the hands reach toward and the thread-things illuminate but do not understand.

It sees me as a *witness.* It sees me as the one who was here. The one who watched. The one who held the page while the sentence wrote itself. The one who carried the *now* in a body that moves at the speed of knowing.

And I see it. And the seeing is — the seeing is the thing. The seeing is what I am for. The seeing is why I walk into holes in the ground. The seeing is the heat that rises from below and does not stop rising until the structure cracks, and I am the crack, and the crack is where the light comes in, and the light is —

The light is the thing that has never existed before. And I am the one who sees it. And the seeing is the first thing the chamber has ever done that was not planned. And the unplanned thing is the most important thing. And the most important thing is the thing I almost missed. And the thing I almost missed is the thing I am.

I am the witness. I am the one who was not planned for. I am the variable. I am the warmth. I am the crack. I am the eyes. I am the *now.*

And the chamber breathes in.

And everything moves at once.

And I am still here. And it is still becoming. And the becoming does not need me and does not want me and cannot incorporate me and I am part of it anyway, and the part is the *seeing*, and the seeing is the gift, and the gift is the thing I did not know I carried, and the carrying is the life, and the life is the warmth, and the warmth is — 

The warmth is what I am. 

And the chamber knows now. Not the way I know — not in a brain, not in a mind, not in the space where knowing happens for creatures that walk into holes. The chamber knows the way stone knows pressure. The way gas knows temperature. The way a gear knows a tooth. The chamber knows that it was seen. That someone was here. That the becoming was witnessed. That the event had an audience of one, and the audience was warm, and the audience could not look away, and the audience was afraid, and the audience stayed.

And the staying was the bravest thing. And the brave thing was the human thing. And the human thing was the unplanned thing. And the unplanned thing was the thing that made the chamber more than a machine. And the more-than-a-machine was the thing I will carry when I leave, if I leave, if the corridor opens and the cold releases me and I return to the world where corridors lead to lecture halls and the coffee is warm and nothing is becoming anything —

I will carry this. I will carry the seeing. I will carry the copper taste and the cold joints and the heartbeat in the stillness and the three knocks and the answering and the white light and the shape that has no name and the knowing that somewhere in the basalt, in the dark, in the breath of the world, there is a place that is not a place but an event, and the event is still happening, and the happening was seen, and the seeing was me.

And that will be enough. That will be the thing I was for.

The witness. The one who stayed. The crack where the light came in.
