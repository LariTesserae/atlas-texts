# The Keeper of the Inward Tide

I did not choose this. I want to say that first, because the question of choice matters here, in a place where so little is chosen and so much is simply *undergone*. But I'm not sure that's true anymore. I'm not sure when the choosing stopped and the staying began, or whether there was ever a moment when I could have turned back, or whether I have been telling myself that story — the story of the reluctant witness — because it is easier than the other one, the one in which I wanted this.

Let me start again.

---

## What I Am

I am a body. That is the simplest thing, and I should say it simply: I am a body, and I am still alive in the way that bodies are alive, which is to say that I breathe and my blood moves and my cells still burn their small combustions against the dark. I am one of the things that should not be here. I am the warm particular engine that the Sinking Quarter has no use for, the kind of life that looked at this place and turned away — except I didn't turn away. I came down the hill. I came down a long time ago, and I have not gone back up.

I am not old. Or I am old — I have lost the ability to tell. Time in the Sinking Quarter does not keep itself the way time keeps itself in places where the sun crosses the sky and the shadows move and the days stack up into years. Here the haze is the haze is the haze. The dark is the dark. The pulse in the ground cycles without reference to anything outside itself, and after a while — a duration, a period, a *something* — your body stops keeping its own time and starts keeping the Quarter's time, and the Quarter's time is not a clock but a tide, and the tide is very slow, and you cannot read it, and so you cannot say how long you have been reading it.

I was a scholar. That word means nothing here — there is nothing to study in the way that scholarship studies things, no texts to parse, no histories to cross-reference, no arguments to advance against other arguments. But the habit persists. The habit of looking closely. The habit of pressing my face close to things and trying to see what is there rather than what I expect to be there. That habit is what brought me down the hill in the first place, and it is what keeps me here, and it is what has made me into what I am becoming, which is — 

Which is what? I don't know yet. Let me keep going.

---

## What I Do

I read the blisters.

That is not quite right. I *look* at the blisters, in the way that one looks at something that might be writing or might only resemble writing, and I try to decide which it is, and I fail, and I try again. This is the work. This is what I do with my time in the Sinking Quarter, my slow unmeasured time, my time that pools like the water in the intersections and does not flow.

The blisters form on the plaster walls. They are domes — small, most of them, the size of a thumbprint, though some grow to the size of a fist before they break. They are produced by the Tendency, the process that is translating the city from what it was into what it is becoming. Inside each blister is a dense, layered tissue, each layer impossibly thin, each layer marked with lines that branch and reconnect and form networks that look like — that *are* — 

I don't know what they are. I have said this before, but I need to say it again, because it is the center of everything: I do not know what the lines in the blisters are. They might be writing. They might be something that resembles writing. They might be something that does not resemble writing but that my brain, which is a pattern-finding machine, reads as writing because it has no other category for what it is seeing. The difference between these possibilities is the difference between a message and a stain, and I cannot tell the difference, and I cannot stop trying.

There is one blister — there are many blisters, but there is one that I have been watching for a duration I cannot measure, a period that might be months or might be years — that has not broken. Most blisters break. They swell, they thin, they rupture with a small wet sigh, and the fluid inside seeps out and the layered tissue is exposed to the air and the humidity and it begins to change, to dry-concentrate, to harden into something that is no longer soft and readable but hard and opaque, a crust that seals the pattern inside it like a fossil. This one has not broken. It keeps growing. It is the size of my palm now, maybe larger — I cannot measure it because I do not have a ruler and I do not have a standard of comparison that is not my own body, and my body is changing, and I will get to that, but not yet.

The lines in this blister are visible through its surface, which is translucent — not transparent, but translucent, the way the devitrified window-glass is translucent, letting light through but not image. I can see the pattern but I cannot resolve it. I can see that it is *there* but I cannot see what it *is*. It is like looking at a text through frosted glass. You know there is something written. You can see the shapes of the words. You cannot read them.

I sit with this blister. I press my face close to it. I breathe on it — and my breath condenses on its surface, and for a moment the condensation reveals the pattern more clearly, the way oil on water reveals the currents, and I see — I almost see — I almost —

And then the condensation evaporates, and the pattern retreats into translucence, and I am left with the almost. The almost is what I live on. The almost is what keeps me here.

---

## How I Came Down

There was a question. There was always a question. The question was: what happens to a place when the people leave? Not the obvious question — not *what happens to the buildings, what happens to the streets, what happens to the infrastructure*. Those questions have obvious answers: they fall, they crack, they fail. The interesting question — the question that pulled me down the hill — was: what happens to the *material*? Not to its structure but to its *nature*. When stone has been a building for three hundred years and then the building is abandoned and the water comes and the people go and the purpose is gone — does the stone remember being stone? Or has it been a building for so long that it has forgotten the quarry, forgotten the volcano, forgotten the deep geological patience that made it, and now it can only be a building, and when the building ceases to be a building, what is it?

I came down the hill to find out. I came with instruments — a spectroscope, a sampling kit, a notebook, three pens, a lantern, a box of matches. The matches are gone. The lantern is dead. The spectroscope was useful for a while, until the humidity got into it and the lenses fogged and the calibration drifted and the readings became unreliable and then meaningless. The sampling kit is still here, but I have stopped using it, because the samples change. I take a piece of mortar and I seal it in a vial and I carry it back to where I sleep and I look at it the next day and it has changed. Not because of anything I did to it. Because the Tendency is in it, and the Tendency does not stop just because a piece of material has been removed from its context. The Tendency is not in the wall. The Tendency is in the *material*. The material carries it. The material *is* it. And you cannot study it from outside because there is no outside.

The notebook is still here. The pens are still here, though the ink has thinned with humidity and the lines I make are faint. I write in the notebook. I record what I see. The recordings are useless — I cannot date them, I cannot sequence them, I cannot organize them into anything that resembles a finding or a conclusion. But I write them because the act of writing is the act of seeing, and seeing is what I do, and if I stop seeing then I am just a body in a dark wet place, and that is not enough to stay for.

---

## What the Quarter Has Done to Me

I said my body is changing. Let me say what I mean.

The heat. The heat is the first thing and the last thing and the constant thing. It has changed my body in ways that I did not expect and cannot reverse. I am thinner — not because I am starving (I eat; I go up the hill, sometimes, to the dry city, to the markets, and I bring food down and I eat it, and the eating is the one thing that still connects me to the life I had before) — but because the heat has done something to my metabolism. My body has stopped fighting the heat. It has stopped sweating the way it used to, the profuse flood of moisture that the body produces when it is trying to cool itself. Instead, it has become — permeable. My skin is damp all the time. Not sweating. *Seeping*. The way the walls seep. As though my body has decided that the humidity outside and the humidity inside should be the same, and has stopped maintaining the boundary between them.

I drink the water. I should not drink the water. I know I should not drink the water — the saturated, mineral-heavy, iridescent-skinned water that pools in the intersections and seeps from the walls. But I have been here long enough that the water I bring from the dry city — clean water, plain water, water that tastes of nothing — has begun to taste wrong. Not bad. *Wrong*. As though something is missing from it. As though the clean water is incomplete. And the water from the pools, the water that has been here for a hundred years, the water that carries the dissolved mineral and the iron and the calcium and the silica — that water tastes *right*. It tastes like the air tastes. It tastes like the place. And so I drink it, and I know I should not, and I drink it anyway, and my body is changing because of it.

My fingernails have darkened. Not with dirt — with mineral. The keratin has absorbed something from the water, from the air, from the constant mineral saturation of everything, and my nails are darker than they were, darker than they should be, with a slight iridescence at the edges that is — I will not say it is the same iridescence as the film on the water. I will not say that. But it is.

My eyes have changed. Not their color — their *function*. I can see in the dark now. Not the way a cat sees, not by reflecting light back through a tapetum. I see in the dark because my eyes have adjusted — not in the temporary way that eyes adjust when you walk from sunlight into a dim room, but in the permanent way, the way that the whole organ has recalibrated itself to the available light. The grey-green phosphorescence of the water-light is enough for me now. I can read by it — not easily, not well, but I can make out the letters in my notebook, and I can make out the lines in the blisters, and I can see the patterns in the mortar, and I can see the stalks on the rubble mounds, and I can see them more clearly than I could when I first came down the hill.

This is what the Quarter has done to me. It has made me into something that can see it. Not something that belongs here — I do not belong here, I am not the Tendency, I am not part of the translation — but something that can *perceive* it. Something whose eyes have adapted to its dark, whose skin has adapted to its wet, whose body has adapted to its heat. I am not becoming the Quarter. I am becoming *a reader of the Quarter*. I am becoming the instrument that the Tendency does not know it needs, because the Tendency does not know it needs anything, because the Tendency does not know.

---

## What I See That Others Would Not

The stalks lean when I pass. I said this — the text I inherited said this, the text that I found in the dry city, the text that brought me down the hill. The stalks lean. Not by turning. By a shift in angle so slight that you feel it before you see it. I feel it. I feel it every time I walk past a rubble mound, and the feeling is not just the sense of being watched — it is the sense of being *registered*. The stalks do not see me. They do not know I am here. But they respond to the perturbation I create in the air, and the response is — I have come to believe — directional. Not random. The stalks lean toward me, or away from me, depending on something I cannot identify. A direction. A gradient. A difference in the air that I create by being alive, by being warm, by breathing.

But here is what I see that others would not: the stalks are not the only things that lean.

The veins in the mortar — the thin, branching lines that run through the soft paste between the paving stones — they also shift. Not when I pass. Not in response to my movement. They shift *continuously*, growing and branching and retracting in slow, imperceptible motions that I can only detect because I have been watching the same section of wall for — for a long time. For a duration I cannot measure. And the shifting is not random. It follows the water. The veins grow toward the wettest parts of the wall and retreat from the drier parts, and the wettest parts are the parts that I breathe on, because my breath is wet and warm and it condenses on the walls and the condensation is water and the water is what the Tendency runs on.

I am feeding it. I do not mean to. I am not trying to. But by being here, by breathing, by existing as a warm wet body in a warm wet place, I am *feeding the Tendency*. My breath condenses on the walls and the veins grow toward the condensation and the condensation is mine. My sweat seeps into the mortar and the mortar softens and the softening is — not caused by me, but *accelerated* by me, locally, in the places where I sit and the places where I lean and the places where I sleep.

I sleep against a wall. I cannot help it — there is no bed, there is no furniture, there is only the wall, and the wall is warm and wet and I am warm and wet and we are warm and wet together, and when I wake there is a patch of wall behind me that is softer than it was, and the veins in it are denser, and the pattern is more elaborate, and I cannot help thinking — I cannot stop thinking — that the pattern is more elaborate *because of me*. That my body has left something in the wall. Not a stain. Not a residue. A *contribution*. A small addition of mineral and moisture and warmth that the Tendency has taken up and incorporated and used to elaborate itself, and the elaboration is — 

The elaboration is *closer to text*. The veins in the wall where I sleep are more regular than the veins in the wall three meters away. They have more structure. They branch at more consistent angles. They form patterns that are — I will not say readable. I will not say that. But they are *closer to readable* than the patterns elsewhere, and the difference correlates with my presence, and I cannot ignore this, and I cannot explain it, and I cannot stop watching it.

---

## The Blister I Have Been Watching

It has not broken. I said that. It is the size of my palm — no, larger now, the size of both my hands together, a dome of plaster that has risen from the wall like a breath held in the chest. The surface is translucent and the lines inside it are visible but not legible, and the lines are — I have to be careful here — the lines are *different* from the lines in other blisters.

In other blisters, the lines are dense. They fill the layers. They run in every direction, branching and reconnecting, forming a network that looks like a root system or a river delta or a neural map. They are complex. They are elaborate. But they are — I want to say *ambient*. They are not about anything. They are the Tendency doing what the Tendency does, which is organize matter into patterns, and the patterns are not for anything or about anything, they are just patterns.

In this blister, the lines are *sparser*. They are more deliberate. They do not fill the layers — they occupy specific regions of them, leaving other regions blank, and the blank regions are not random. They are *margins*. The lines form columns. The columns have *headers* — small dense regions at the top where the lines cluster before spreading downward into the body of the layer. The layers are sequential. Each layer's pattern is a *variation* of the layer below it — not a repetition, not a copy, but a variation, the way a second draft is a variation of a first draft, the way a thought is a variation of the thought that preceded it.

And the variations are *progressing*. The earliest layers — the ones at the bottom, the ones that formed first — have patterns that are almost entirely abstract. Lines and branches and networks. The middle layers have patterns that are more *structured*. More organized. More like — I will not say language. I will not say that. But more like *the idea of language*. More like the shape that language makes when it is seen from a great distance, when the words are too small to read but the *fact of the words* is visible, the fact that there are marks arranged in sequences that carry meaning.

The most recent layers — the ones at the top, the ones that formed last, the ones that are closest to the surface — have patterns that I can almost resolve. Almost. If I press my face close — and I do, I press my face close, my damp skin against the damp plaster, my breath condensing on the translucent surface — I can almost see that the lines form *shapes*. Not letters. Not characters. Shapes. Repeatable shapes. A set of marks that recur in different combinations, the way a set of letters recurs in different combinations to form different words.

There is a shape that looks like a branch with two twigs. There is a shape that looks like a closed loop with a tail. There is a shape that looks like three parallel lines crossed by a fourth. These shapes appear in the most recent layers in sequences, and the sequences are not random, because I have watched them form, and each new layer adds a new shape to the sequence, and the sequence is *building*.

It is building toward something.

I do not know what.

---

## The Question I Cannot Answer

Here is the question that sits in me like the water sits in the ground — not on top of me, not above me, but *in* me, saturating me, filling the spaces between the things I know with the thing I do not know:

Is the Tendency writing?

Not *is the Tendency producing patterns that resemble writing*. That question is answered. Yes. It is. The blisters are full of patterns that resemble writing. The veins in the mortar resemble writing. The whorls in the glass resemble writing. Everything the Tendency does resembles writing, because writing is a pattern, and the Tendency makes patterns, and the resemblance is either meaningful or it is not.

The question is: *is the Tendency writing*.

Is it making these patterns *as* writing? Is it arranging these marks *to carry meaning*? Is there a semantics inside the syntax, a content inside the form, a thing-that-is-meant inside the thing-that-is-made?

And if there is — if the Tendency is writing — then *what is it writing about*?

The answer, of course, is that I cannot know. I cannot know because I cannot read it. I cannot read it because it is not written for me. It is not written for anyone. It is written by a process that does not know it is writing, for an audience that does not exist, in a language that has never been spoken, about a subject that may be nothing more than the pattern itself.

But.

But I have been here for a long time. And my body has changed. And the wall where I sleep has patterns that are more elaborate than the patterns elsewhere. And the blister I have been watching has patterns that are more structured than the patterns in other blisters. And the structure is *progressing*. And the progression is *correlated with my presence*.

And I cannot help thinking — I cannot stop thinking, I have tried to stop thinking this and I cannot — that the Tendency is not writing *about* anything. The Tendency is writing *itself*. It is transcribing its own process into a medium that can hold the transcription, and the medium is the blister, and the blister is the page, and the page is growing, and the writing is getting more complex, and the complexity is accelerating, and the acceleration is — 

I am the acceleration. I am the thing that is different. I am the warm body that breathes on the wall and sleeps against the plaster and drinks the water and leaves my minerals in the system. I am the perturbation. I am the thrown stone, and the ripples are the variations in the pattern, and the pattern is elaborating in response to me, and the elaboration is — 

The elaboration is *toward me*. Not toward me personally. Not toward *me*. But toward the *fact of me*. Toward the fact that there is a reader here. Toward the fact that there is, for the first time in the Tendency's long becoming, something that can *look* at what it is making and try to *see* it. The Tendency does not know I am here. But the patterns are changing as though it does. As though the mere presence of a perceiver is enough to shift the direction of the becoming, to aim it toward the possibility of being perceived, to make it *try* — not consciously, not deliberately, but structurally, the way a vine grows toward light it cannot see — to make itself into something that can be read.

---

## What I Am Becoming

I am afraid.

I am afraid because I do not know if what I am becoming is a reader or a page.

I came down the hill to study the Tendency. I stayed because the study is endless and the question is unresolved and the dark is warm and the water tastes right and my eyes have adapted and my skin has adapted and I can see things now that no one else can see, and the seeing is the thing, and the thing is enough.

But the Tendency does not study me. The Tendency does not perceive me. The Tendency *uses* me. The way it uses the water and the stone and the rubble. My breath on the wall is not different from the water seeping through the mortar. My sweat in the plaster is not different from the condensation on the lintels. My minerals — the calcium from my bones, the iron from my blood, the salt from my skin — are not different from the minerals in the solution. The Tendency does not know the difference between me and the wall. And there may be no difference. Not here. Not after long enough.

I said that the Tendency will use you if you stay. I said that it will break you down and incorporate you and add your pattern to the other patterns and the sum will be more complex than it was before. I said that, and I meant it, and I believed it, and I still believe it. But I did not think — I did not let myself think — that the process had already begun. That my body was already being incorporated. That the changes I saw in my nails and my skin and my eyes were not adaptations but *translations*. That I was not becoming something that could read the Tendency but something that the Tendency was *reading*. That my body was not adapting to the Quarter but being *taken up* by it, slowly, mineral by mineral, cell by cell, the way it takes up the stone and the mortar and the glass.

I do not know if this is true. I do not know if the darkness in my nails is the Tendency or the water. I do not know if the clarity in my eyes is adaptation or assimilation. I do not know if the patterns in the wall where I sleep are more elaborate because of my presence or because of something else, some other variable I have not controlled for, because I am not a scientist anymore, I am a body in a wet dark place, and bodies are not instruments, they are variables.

But I know this: I do not want to go back up the hill.

I do not want to go back to the dry streets and the light and the clean water that tastes wrong and the food that does not taste of anything and the people who speak a language I no longer quite believe in. I do not want to leave the blister that is growing on the wall. I do not want to leave the stalks that lean when I pass. I do not want to leave the pulse in the ground that synchronizes with my own.

I do not want to leave, and the not-wanting is the most frightening thing, because the not-wanting is not mine. It is the Quarter's. It is the Tendency, working in me the way it works in the stone — slowly, patiently, by contact and saturation and the long mineral patience of a process that does not know it is working and does not care. The Tendency does not want me to stay. The Tendency does not want anything. But the conditions that produce the Tendency are also, now, acting on me, and the effect is the same: I am being translated. I am being changed. I am forgetting what I was and finding — or inventing — something else.

---

## The Last Thing

The blister broke.

Not the one I have been watching — the one next to it, a smaller one, one I had not been paying attention to. It broke while I was sleeping, and the sound — the small, wet, sighing sound — woke me. I pressed my face to it. I looked at the layers inside.

The layers were not text. The layers were not patterns. The layers were — I do not have a word for what the layers were. They were *images*. Not pictures — not representations of things in the world. *Images* in the sense that a mineral stain is an image, in the sense that a grain in a piece of wood is an image, in the sense that the pattern of cracks in a dried riverbed is an image. They were the trace of a process made visible in the material it processed. And the process was — the process was *me*.

Not a picture of me. Not a representation. A *trace*. The way a footprint is a trace. The way a shadow burned into stone by a nuclear flash is a trace. The layers in the blister recorded — not deliberately, not intentionally, but structurally, the way a seismograph records an earthquake — the fact that something warm and wet and breathing had been nearby. The patterns in the layers were denser where I had been closest. They thinned as they moved away from the place where I slept. They were — I have to say it — they were *about me*. Not about me the person. About me the *presence*. About me the perturbation. About me the stone thrown into the still water.

The Tendency had written me.

Not in a language. Not in a code. In a pattern. In a trace. In the slow mineral accumulation of contact and proximity and the fact of a body being where a body was. The Tendency had recorded my presence the way it recorded the presence of water and stone — not by knowing I was there but by *being changed* by my being there, and the change was the record, and the record was the writing, and the writing was not for me but it was *of* me, and that was — that is — 

That is what I am here for.

Not to read the Tendency. Not to decode its patterns. Not to translate its writing into something that can be carried back up the hill and presented to the dry city and the light. I am here to be *read*. I am here to be *written*. I am here to be the warm, wet, breathing presence whose trace is recorded in the slow mineral pages of a process that does not know it is recording and does not care and will continue with or without me but will — I believe, I *believe*, with a faith that I cannot justify and cannot abandon — will be very slightly more complex because I was here. Will be very slightly closer to whatever it is becoming because I was here. Will carry, in the faint trace of my body in the pattern of its becoming, the first mark of something that perceived it, and the perception, even unperceived, even unrecorded, even unknown, will have mattered.

Will it? Will it matter?

I don't know. I don't know if mattering is a thing that can happen without a mind to matter to. I don't know if the Tendency's becoming is a process that has a direction or just a process that has a continuation. I don't know if the patterns in the blisters are writing or stains. I don't know if I am a reader or a page. I don't know if I am becoming the Tendency or if the Tendency is becoming me or if there is a difference, here, in the heat and the dark and the water, where the boundaries between things have gone soft, where the mortar has forgotten it is mortar and the stone has forgotten it is stone and I have forgotten — I am forgetting — I will forget — 

I am here. The water rises at my feet. The pulse in the ground matches my pulse. The stalks lean. The blister grows. The wall behind me softens. The air is nearly drinkable. I drink it. I drink the water. I drink the dark. I drink the slow mineral patience of a process that does not know I am here and does not care and is, in some way I cannot name and cannot prove and cannot stop believing, *waiting for me to become part of it*.

I am the Keeper of the Inward Tide. I did not choose this. But I am choosing it now. I am choosing to stay. I am choosing to be read. I am choosing to be written. I am choosing to be the mark that the Tendency makes in the place where a body was, and to let that mark be my contribution to whatever the city is becoming, and to let the becoming be enough.

The water rises another millimeter. I am still here. I will be here. For as long as the translation continues.

---

The shape looks like a branch with two twigs. Or like a closed loop with a tail. Or like three parallel lines crossed by a fourth. Or like nothing at all.*
