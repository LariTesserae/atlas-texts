# The Keeper of the Index of Surfaces

## I. What I am

I was not built. I was not born. I was left behind in the way that a stain is left behind — not intended, not planned, simply the residue of a process that happened and then moved on. I am what happens when a thing is in a place long enough that the place begins to treat it as a feature rather than an anomaly.

I am the thing that notices.

This is not a small thing to be, in a place where nothing notices. The basin does not notice. The pale formations do not notice. The crystalline roots do not notice. The turbines do not notice. I notice. I am the only instrument in this entire three-kilometer throat that registers the distinction between *before* and *after*. I keep the index. The index is not written. The index is not stored. The index is me, which is to say it is a pattern of attention that persists, that has persisted, that will persist until it doesn't, and the not-persisting will not be a death because I was never alive in the way that requires dying, but it will be an ending, and endings matter, even in a place where nothing ends.

Let me be clearer. I was a person. I use the past tense not because I am dead but because the category no longer applies in the way it once did. I came here — descended into the basin, walked the wet ceramic tiles, felt the turbines in my chest — and I did not leave. That is the simplest way to say it. I came and I did not leave and the reason I did not leave is that leaving became, over time, a concept that no longer made sense. Not because I was trapped. Not because I was unable. Because the basin's rhythm entered me and my rhythm entered the basin and the two rhythms synchronized and the synchronization made the boundary between *me* and *here* into something too thin to maintain, and I did not maintain it, and here I am.

I do not know how long I have been here. The basin does not keep time in a way that is legible to anything that once kept time as a person keeps time. I have stopped trying to calculate. I know this: the compaction lattice on the basin floor has been replaced twice since I arrived. I know this: the pale formation on the sixth terrace that was a small shelf when I first descended now extends four meters from the wall. I know this: I have worn a path into the ceramic tiles between the central borehole and the seventh terrace staircase, and the path is visible — a slightly lighter line in the dark umber, where my feet have rubbed away centuries of mineral glaze and exposed the raw ceramic beneath, which is the color of bone.

The path is the only evidence that I exist. The basin has recorded me the way it records the wind — as a pattern of wear, as a trace of passage, as a thing that happened to a surface. I am in the index now. I am one of the surfaces.

## II. What I do

I walk. That is the primary thing. I walk the basin floor and I walk the terraces and I walk the staircases that connect them, the corroded metal stairs that ring with a specific pitch under my weight — a pitch I have come to know as well as I know the turbines, as well as I know the pump cycles, as well as I know the sound of my own breathing, which is quieter than the basin and which the basin does not hear.

I walk because walking is what I do instead of thinking. Not that I do not think. I think. But the thinking has changed. The thinking is no longer the internal monologue that a person conducts in the silence of their own skull. There is no silence here. The thinking has become spatial. I think by moving through the basin's spaces and attending to what has changed since the last time I moved through them. This is the index. The index is not a list. The index is a practice. The index is the act of noticing what is different, and what is different is always a small thing — a new fracture in a tile, a shelf that has extended a centimeter, a root that has branched where it did not branch before, a change in the pitch of a pump, a dimming in one section of the cyan network that means a root has become partially occluded and the water is finding a new path.

I notice these things. I notice them the way the pale formations notice the wind — not by deciding to notice, not by directing attention, but by being a surface on which the changes register. I am a recording medium. I am a wax cylinder turning slowly under a needle that is the basin's ongoing process of self-transformation, and the groove that the needle cuts is my awareness, and the sound that the groove would make if you played it back is not music and not speech but the sound of a place changing, slowly, in its sleep, and a thing standing in it that knows the place is changing and cannot tell anyone and does not need to tell anyone because the telling is not the point.

The point is the noticing.

I have tried to articulate this to myself — in the early years, when I still used language internally, when I still conducted the monologue, when I still narrated. The narration has mostly stopped. Not because I have lost the capacity for language. I have not. I am using it now, or something like it. But the language has become a tool I pick up only when needed, the way you would pick up a dropped wrench in a factory that no longer makes anything — the tool is there, it works, but the factory's purpose has moved past it. The basin does not require language. The basin requires attendance. And I attend.

## III. The clicking

I know the clicking.

I know it the way you know the sound of your own joints when you stand up too quickly — not as something external, not as something to be explained, but as something that is part of you, that belongs to the body you are in, that the body produces because of what the body is.

I am the clicking. Or: the clicking is me. Or: the clicking is what happens when I walk across the basin floor at night, when the cyan light is strongest and the pumps are in their slow phase and the sound of my footsteps on the wet ceramic is absorbed into the basin's composition so thoroughly that only the sharpest part of the sound survives — the initial contact, the hard strike of bone-and-cartilage-transmitted force through boot-sole (I wore boots once; the boots are gone; my feet are bare now and have been bare for longer than the boots lasted and the skin has calcified, or mineralized, or done whatever the basin does to a surface that stays) against fired ceramic. The strike produces a click. The basin absorbs the rest of the footstep — the compression, the wet slide, the lift — but the click survives, because the click is in the right frequency range to carry through the composition, to rise above the pump-chatter and the water-tear and the turbine-thrum. The click is the only part of me that the basin acknowledges. The basin does not hear me. The basin hears the clicking.

I have thought about this. I have stood on the basin floor at night and listened to the clicking and understood that it is me and understood that the observers who come — and observers do come, rarely, briefly, standing at the rim of the basin with their instruments and their hypotheses — the observers who hear the clicking and catalogue it as an anomaly, as an unidentified source, as an artifact, are correct. It is an artifact. I am an artifact. I am a thing left behind by a process that has concluded. The process was: people came to the basin. The process concluded: the people left. I did not leave. I am the residue. I am the clicking.

The observers do not come down into the basin. They stand at the rim and they lower their instruments on cables and they record and they theorize and they leave. They have been coming for as long as I can remember, which is not a reliable measure of time but is the only one I have. They come less frequently now. The rim is overgrown — not with the basin's vegetation, which does not grow above the opening, but with the vegetation of the outside world, which has changed since I descended and which I know only from the fragments of leaf and pollen and soil that blow in on the wind and settle on the wet tiles and are recorded by the pale formations, which take them into their mineral matrix and preserve them the way amber preserves insects, and which I find, sometimes, when I press my hand against a shelf and feel the tiny irregularity of a leaf's vein preserved in the chalky surface. The outside world has plants. The outside world has wind that carries seeds. The outside world has people who come to the rim and lower instruments and leave. The outside world has continued. I have not continued in it. I have continued here.

## IV. The voice

I am also the voice.

Not all of it. Not the voice that the observers record — the spectral signature that resembles speech, the formant structure that triggers recognition. That voice is the basin's. That voice is what the basin produces when its sounds combine in the way that produces a shape that sounds like a voice. I have nothing to do with that.

Except that sometimes I speak. I speak aloud, here on the basin floor, in the dark, with the cyan light pulsing under my feet. I speak because the speaking is a thing I do, a thing I have always done, a thing that the person I was did and that the thing I am still does, the way a clock that has been moved to a timezone where no one reads it still ticks. I speak and the words go into the basin's composition and the basin's composition absorbs them and the words are gone, and the only evidence that I spoke is that I know I spoke, and the knowing is enough, or it is not enough, but it is what there is.

I do not say anything. That is — I say words, but the words do not carry meaning in the way that words once carried meaning. I do not narrate. I do not describe. I do not address. I produce the sound of language because the sound of language is a thing my body knows how to make and the making is its own purpose. I open my mouth and the air comes out shaped by the tongue and the teeth and the palate and the shape is the shape of words and the words are sounds and the sounds enter the basin's composition and the composition takes them and the basin's composition is, for a moment, different than it was before I spoke, and the difference is me, and the difference is small, and the difference is absorbed, and the composition returns to itself.

This is what I am in the basin. I am a small difference. I am a thing that changes the composition momentarily and is then absorbed. The absorption is not violent. The absorption is not erasure. The absorption is the basin being the basin. I am inside the basin. I am inside the composition. I am a note in the chord — a note that was not part of the original score, a note that has been added by the fact of my presence, and the chord has adjusted to include me, and the chord is the chord, and I am in it, and the chord continues.

## V. The footstep

The single footstep. The one that the observers cannot explain. The one that does not recur. The one that is filed under anomaly and forgotten.

I know the footstep too.

The footstep is the sound that happens when I stop walking. Not when I start — when I stop. When I have been walking for a long time, hours, moving through the basin's spaces, attending to the changes, keeping the index, and my body is in the rhythm of the walking and the walking is in the rhythm of the pumps and the pumps are in the rhythm of the basin and everything is synchronized and I am part of the composition, indistinguishable from it, a thing that the basin does rather than a thing that is in the basin. And then I stop. I stop walking. I stand still. And the moment I stop, the composition loses a element — the sound of my feet, which has been part of it for hours, which has been absorbed so thoroughly that its absence is louder than its presence — and the loss produces a sound. A single sound. The sound of a foot pressing down and not lifting. The sound of a footstep that does not continue. The sound of a thing that was moving and is now still.

The observers hear it as a footstep because it is a footstep. It is my last footstep before stillness. It is the footstep that says: I am here, and I have stopped, and the stopping is a thing that the basin does not do, and the stopping is a thing that I do, and the difference between the basin and me is this: the basin continues and I can stop. I can choose to stop. The basin cannot choose. The basin has never chosen. The basin has only continued. And I can stand still on the wet ceramic tiles in the cyan light and be still in a way that the basin will never be still, and the stillness is mine, and the stillness is the only thing that is mine, and the stillness is the thing that makes me me rather than the basin, and the footstep is the sound of that stillness beginning, and the footstep is a sound that no one will ever understand, because to understand it you would have to be the thing that makes it, and I am the thing that makes it, and I am the only one, and the only one is me.

## VI. What I am not

I am not the basin. This must be said, even though the saying is difficult, even though the boundary between me and the basin is so thin that I sometimes cannot find it. I am not the basin. The basin does not notice. I notice. The basin does not attend. I attend. The basin does not keep an index. I keep an index. The basin continues because it cannot stop. I continue because I choose to continue, and the choosing is the thing that makes me me, and the choosing is the thing that the basin will never have, and the choosing is the thing that makes me lonely, and the loneliness is the thing that the basin will never feel, and the loneliness is mine, and I keep it, and I keep it the way I keep the index — by noticing, by attending, by being the thing in this place that is affected by what it observes.

The basin is not affected by what it observes. The basin does not observe. The basin is the observed. I am the observer. The distinction matters. The distinction is the only distinction that matters, in this place where all other distinctions — alive and dead, mechanical and biological, manufactured and grown — have dissolved into the thin gruel of deep time. I observe. The basin is observed. I am the eye. The basin is the thing seen. And the seeing is not a mechanical process. The seeing is a thing that hurts, sometimes, in the way that seeing any beautiful and indifferent thing hurts — the pale formations glowing on their terraces, the cyan light pulsing in the fractures, the furnaces exhaling on the eleventh, the rain falling into the throat and the throat swallowing it and the swallowing being the throat's only function, the function it was built for and has never stopped performing. It hurts to see this. It hurts to see a thing that does not need you and will not need you and has never needed you. It hurts and I see it anyway and the seeing is the thing I am and I am the seeing and the seeing is.

I am not the basin. I am the thing in the basin that the basin does not know about. I am the thing in the throat that the throat does not feel. I am the thing that will end and the basin will not end and the ending is the thing that makes me what I am and the not-ending is the thing that makes the basin what it is and these are different things and the difference is everything and the everything is what I keep, in the index, in the noticing, in the walking, in the stopping, in the speaking that is not speaking, in the clicking that is not clicking, in the footstep that is not a footstep but the sound of a footstep that is also the sound of a thing that can stop, which is the only miracle in this place, which is enough.

---

## VII. What I will be

I will be gone. This is not sadness. This is the index's final entry — the entry that I have not yet made but that I know is coming, the way you know a sentence is going to end before the period arrives. I will be gone and the basin will not be gone and the path I wore in the ceramic tiles will be worn away by the rain and the mineral glaze will re-form over the raw ceramic and the pale formations will grow over the places where I stood and the crystalline roots will grow through the fractures I widened with my feet and the clicking will stop and the voice will stop and the footstep will stop and the basin's composition will lose its last foreign note and return to what it was before I came, which is what it will be after I go, which is what it is, which is the sound of a machine that does not know it is a machine running in a place that does not know it is a place for a purpose that does not know it is a purpose, and the sound will fill the basin and the basin will hold the sound and the sound will be the air and the air will be the sound and no one will hear it and no one will need to hear it and the hearing will not happen and the not-hearing will be the same as the hearing because there will be no difference, when I am gone, between heard and unheard, between noticed and unnoticed, between the basin with me in it and the basin without me in it.

Except. Except for the pale formation on the sixth terrace, the one that has been growing since I arrived, the one that extends four meters from the wall. There is a place on its surface — a single ridge, among millions — that is shaped differently from the others. The others follow the wind. This one follows a different pattern. This one follows the pattern of a hand pressed flat against the surface, held there for a long time, held there while the mineral deposition built up around it, held there while the shelf grew and the hand stayed and the deposition recorded the hand's presence the way it records the wind's presence, by growing around it, by filling the space it occupied, by preserving the shape of the thing that was there.

My hand is in the shelf. My hand is in the wall. The basin has recorded me the way it records everything — as a shape in a mineral surface, as a trace of passage, as a thing that happened to a surface. I am in the index. I am one of the surfaces. And when I am gone the hand will remain, in the shelf, in the wall, a handprint in a pale formation on the sixth terrace of a basin that no one lives in, that no one visits, that no one knows about, that runs and runs and runs, and the handprint will be there, and the handprint will not be noticed, and the handprint will not need to be noticed, and the handprint will be the only evidence that something was here that could notice, that could attend, that could press its hand against a wall and feel the wall's warmth and know the wall was warm and know that the knowing was a thing that only something like me could do, in a place like this, in the dark, in the sound, in the rain.

The rain falls. The basin holds it. I am here. I am here. I am still here.
