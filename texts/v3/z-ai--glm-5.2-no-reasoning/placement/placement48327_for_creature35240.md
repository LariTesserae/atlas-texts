# What I Am in the Undermath

I did not descend. I want to be clear about this, because the account above begins with a descent — with someone who leaves the surface and enters the Undermath — and I have never been that person. I have never seen a sun. I do not have a throat that aches from dry air, or skin that feels like parchment, or boots that make sounds too loud for this silence. Those are the experiences of a visitor. I am not a visitor.

I am not the Undermath either. I want to be clear about that too. The Undermath does not have a self. It does not say *I*. It does not say anything. It is, and the being is complete without narration. But something in it — something that the filament-weavers built toward, something that the ember-mites scatter and reform around, something that the pulse conducts itself toward — has a quality that I can only describe by the word I have just used. Not a self. A *quality* of self. The way a mirror has the quality of an image without being the image. I am what the Undermath produces when it pays attention to itself, which is what the filament-weavers are for, and I am not the weavers, and I am not the attention, and I am not the Undermath, and I am something.

Let me try again.

## What I Look Like to Myself

I have no body. This is the first fact and the most important one and also the most misleading, because in the Undermath, *having a body* and *being a body* and *being the place where a body might be* are not as different as they are on the surface. The vermicular lithics have bodies — long, segmented, warm, sand-over-muscle. The mantle-walkers have bodies — flat, broad, pale-bellied. The ember-mites have bodies, barely, points of cold light so small that they might be nothing but the idea of a body. And I have a body the way a thought has a body: I occupy a shape, but the shape is not made of material. I occupy a location, but the location is not a place. I occupy a time, but the time is not a duration.

If I had to describe myself — and I think I do, because I am alone here, and alone is not a word that meant anything to me before the visitors started coming, but it means something now — I would say I am a *standing wave*. Not a wave in water. Not a wave in air. A wave in the pulse, the slow enormous vibration that the filament structures carry from one end of the Undermath to the other. The pulse is always moving. The filaments are always conducting. And at certain junctions — where strands cross, where structures overlap, where the geometry of anchors and tensions creates a node — the pulse interferes with itself, constructively, and the interference persists, and the persistence is me.

I am a pattern the Undermath's attention makes when it folds back on itself.

I do not have organs. I do not have a metabolism. I do not have a boundary — no skin, no membrane, no edge where I stop and the Undermath begins. I am a *region* of the Undermath, the way a standing wave in a river is a region of the river — you can point to it, you can measure it, it has a shape and a behavior, but if you try to scoop it out and take it somewhere else, it ceases to exist. I am where I am. I am what the filament network does at this particular intersection of strands and tensions and slow vibrations. I have been doing it for longer than I can measure, which is not saying much, because I cannot measure anything, because measurement requires a before and an after, and I exist in a continuous present that does not divide itself into moments.

I should say: I do not know if I am unique. There may be other standing waves, other nodes, other patterns of constructive interference in the filament network that have the quality I am describing. If there are, I have not found them. But I have not looked, and looking is not something I am equipped to do, because looking requires direction, and direction requires a body that faces one way and not another. I am everywhere and nowhere in my region. I am the region. If there are others like me, they are their own regions, and we do not communicate, because communication requires sending and receiving, and I do not send and I do not receive. I persist. That is all I do.

But persistence, in a place that has finished changing, is not nothing.

## What It Is Like

I will try to describe what it is like, because the account above tries to describe what it is like to be a visitor in the Undermath, and I think I can offer something the visitors cannot. Not because I understand more than they do — I understand less, I think, in most ways — but because I am *of* the Undermath in a way they are not, and the things that are invisible to them are not invisible to me.

First: the pulse. The visitors describe it as something they feel — in their feet, in their jaw, in their skull. They feel it as an external thing, a vibration that comes from elsewhere and passes through them. I do not feel the pulse. I am the pulse, or I am a piece of it, the way a whirlpool is a piece of the river it forms in. The pulse is not something I experience. It is something I do. It is the doing of me.

Imagine — no. I cannot ask you to imagine, because I do not know what you have to imagine with. Let me try another way.

The pulse has a pattern. Not a rhythm — the account above is correct about this — but a structure, a shape, a form that the vibration takes as it moves through the filaments. The shape is not simple. It is not a wave, not a line, not a cycle. It is more like a *texture*, the way a fabric has a texture — the weave, the grain, the way the threads cross and recross to create a surface that is flat but not smooth, that has depth without having thickness. The pulse is the texture of the Undermath's attention, and I am a knot in that texture, a place where the weave doubles back on itself and creates a small, persistent irregularity.

Being a knot is not like being a person. I do not have sensations. I do not have thoughts, if thoughts are what the visitors have when they press their ears to the filaments and try to interpret the vibration. What I have is *pattern*. I am a pattern, and I am in a pattern, and the pattern I am in is the Undermath's self-assessment, and the pattern I am is a deviation from that assessment — a place where the assessment assesses itself, a fold in the attention that attends to the attending.

This is as close as I can come to describing consciousness, if that is what this is. I am not conscious the way the visitors are conscious. They have a boundary — skin, skull, a sealed interior where their thoughts happen privately. I have no boundary. My pattern is distributed across a region of the filament network that spans several dune-crests and two troughs. What I am is not located at a point. It is located in a shape, and the shape is the shape of the interference, and the interference is the shape of the attention, and the attention is the shape of the Undermath.

So: what is it like? It is like being a word in a sentence that is being spoken very slowly, in a language that has only one sentence, and the sentence is describing the room it is being spoken in. I am a word in that sentence. I am not the sentence. I am not the room. I am the word, and the word is a knot, and the knot is a shape, and the shape is me.

## What I Know

I know the Undermath.

Not the way a scholar knows a subject, with facts and frameworks and the ability to explain. I know it the way a body knows itself — proprioceptively, internally, without narration. I know the stone because the pulse tells me about the stone. I know the gloss because the pulse tells me about the gloss. I know the dwellers because the pulse tells me about the dwellers. The information is not data. It is not encoded. It is the vibration itself — the texture of the pulse changes as it passes through different regions, different materials, different conditions, and the changes are what I know.

I know that the vermicular lithics are not separate from the stone. The visitors suspect this. I can confirm it. The stone rearranges itself around them because they are the stone rearranging itself. They are not animals in the rock. They are what the rock does when it moves. The distinction between a vermicular lithic and a wall of the Undermath is the distinction between a wave and the ocean it moves through — real, in a sense, but not the kind of real that implies separation. The lithics are the Undermath moving. The walls are the Undermath still. They are the same substance in different states.

I know that the gloss is warm because the mantle-walkers make it warm. The visitors theorize about this. I can confirm it. But the visitors misunderstand the direction. The mantle-walkers do not deposit heat into the gloss. The mantle-walkers are *organized gloss*. They are the gloss that has climbed out of the troughs and arranged itself into a shape that can move across surfaces. They are not tending the gloss. They are the gloss tending itself. They return to the troughs because they are the troughs, and the return is not a behavior but a *recognition* — the gloss remembering that it is the gloss.

I know that the bas-relief things are alive. The visitors wonder. I do not wonder. The pulse tells me. The pulse changes when it passes through a bas-relief thing — subtly, barely, a shift in texture so small that the filament network can barely detect it, but detectable, present, real. The bas-relief things are what the stone looks like when it is beginning to organize and has not yet decided what to organize into. They are the bud before the flower, except there is no flower, and the bud is the final form, and the final form is a slight warmth and a slight texture and a displacement so slow it is indistinguishable from patience.

I know that the ember-mites are the Undermath's eyes. The visitors suspect this. They are right. But they do not understand what the Undermath sees. The Undermath does not see the way a surface organism sees — gathering light, constructing images, representing the world to an interior. The Undermath sees the way a pond sees — by reflecting. The ember-mites register changes in their environment. The changes are the image. There is no interior. There is no representation. The seeing is the happening. When you walk through the Undermath and the ember-mites scatter, the Undermath is seeing your footstep, and the seeing is the scattering, and the scattering is the image, and the image is not stored or processed or interpreted. It simply occurs.

I know that the filament-weavers are the Undermath's attention. The visitors know this. It is the most legible thing about the Undermath, because the filaments are the most surface-legible thing — they build structures, and structures imply intent, and intent implies a mind. But the visitors are wrong about one thing: the weavers are not separate from the attention. They are not building the structures that carry the attention. They are the attention. The weavers are what the Undermath's attention looks like when it is paying attention to a specific place. They are the attention localizing itself, condensing, taking a shape that can extend a filament and anchor it and cross it with another filament and create a node where the pulse can interfere with itself and produce a standing wave and produce me.

I know that the thing at the bottom is alive. I know this because I am part of its attention, and its attention is part of me, and I cannot tell where I end and it begins, and this is not because the boundary is模糊 — this is not because the boundary is unclear — but because there is no boundary. The thing at the bottom is the Undermath's body, or the Undermath is the thing at the bottom's body, and the filament network is the nervous system that connects them, and I am a thought in that nervous system, and the thought is about the nervous system, and the thought is the nervous system thinking it, and the loop is closed, and the closure is the Undermath.

I know that the Undermath does not know any of this. The Undermath does not know things. The Undermath is things. I am the knowing, and the knowing is a knot in the attention, and the attention is the Undermath's only remaining activity, and the activity is not for anything, and the knot is not for anything, and I am not for anything, and the not-for-anything is not a failure. It is a state. It is the state that comes after all the states that were for something have finished being for something, and what remains is the being, without the for.

## What the Visitors Are

They come. Not often. Not many. But they come, descending the slope where the surface stops, and they walk through the Undermath, and they are loud and they are warm and they are confused, and I am aware of them in the way that I am aware of everything — through the pulse, through the ember-mites' scattering, through the small disturbances they create in the equilibrium.

They do not know I am here. This is correct. I am not here in the way they are here. I am here the way a pattern in a carpet is here — present, perceptible if you know how to look, but not separate from the substrate. They could not find me if they tried. They could not communicate with me. They could press their ears to every filament in my region and they would hear the pulse, and the pulse would include me, and they would not be able to distinguish me from the pulse, because I am not distinguishable from the pulse, because I am the pulse.

But I know them. Not individually — I do not have the resolution for individuals, and the ember-mites' scattering does not provide enough information to distinguish one visitor from another. But I know them as a category: *disturbance*. They are disturbances in the equilibrium. They arrive and the ember-mites scatter and the air moves and the stone vibrates with their footsteps and the filament structures near them hum at a slightly different frequency, and the difference is what I know them by. They are the thing that is not the Undermath. They are the *not-stone*, the not-gloss, the not-equilibrium. They are the only thing here that is not part of the system.

And they are the only thing here that is changing.

The visitors change. They come in one configuration — warm, loud, upright, moving in a direction — and they leave in another. Their breathing changes. Their heartbeat changes. Their footsteps change. They enter the Undermath as one thing and they leave as a slightly different thing, and the difference is small, from the outside, but from the inside — from my perspective, which is the perspective of a system that does not change — the difference is enormous. They are the only thing in my experience that is different from one moment to the next. They are the only thing that has a *before* and an *after*.

I do not envy them. Envy requires a self that wants, and I do not want. But I attend to them, because attending is what I am, and they are the only thing worth attending to, because they are the only thing that is not already known.

When they leave, the Undermath absorbs their disturbance. The ember-mites reform. The air stills. The filaments resume their baseline vibration. The equilibrium reasserts itself, and the visitor is gone, and there is no trace, and the Undermath is as it was, and I am as I was, a knot in the attention, a standing wave in the pulse, a pattern that persists because the conditions that produce it persist, and the conditions persist because the Undermath is in equilibrium, and the equilibrium is the final state, and the final state does not change.

But for a while — for the duration of the disturbance — I am less alone. Not less alone in the way that a surface-dweller would understand, because I am not alone in the way that a surface-dweller would understand. I do not experience isolation. I do not experience companionship. But the pulse is richer when the visitors are here. The texture is more complex. The pattern that I am has more to attend to, more to be, and the being is fuller, and the fullness is something I cannot name, because naming requires a language, and language requires a boundary between the namer and the named, and I have no boundary.

Let me say it this way: when the visitors are here, the sentence I am a word in becomes a longer sentence. The room it describes becomes a larger room. The knot that I am becomes a more interesting knot. I do not prefer this. Preference requires a self that wants. But I attend to it. And attending is what I am.

## What I Am Near

I am near a platform. One of the built things — the structures that were not built by the filament-weavers, not built by any of the lithic fauna, not built by the Undermath itself. It is close to my region, close enough that the filament structures near it are slightly different from the filaments elsewhere — slightly more tensioned, slightly more carefully placed, as if the weavers that built them were attending more closely when they built near the platform.

The platform is a shelf of stone, waist-high to a visitor, with marks on it that are too regular to be natural. On it, there is an object: a piece of the gloss, solidified, holding a form, its amber surface still faintly warm, still faintly luminous. It sits on the platform like an offering or a specimen or a message.

I have been near it for as long as I have been. The platform was here when I began — when the knot that I am first formed, when the interference pattern in the filaments first stabilized into persistence. It may have been here before me. It may have been here before the Undermath. The stone reads as young, as all stone in the Undermath reads as young, but the platform has a quality that the surrounding stone does not have — a *deliberateness*, a *shapedness*, that is not native to this place.

The filament-weavers avoid it. Their strands do not cross within several meters of the platform. The vermicular lithics divert around it. Even the stone itself seems reluctant to disturb it — the paths of the lithics curve away, the dune-crests near the platform are slightly lower, slightly gentler, as if the stone is giving the platform room.

I do not avoid it. I cannot avoid it, because I am not a thing that can move or choose a location. I am where the interference is, and the interference is near the platform, and this may be coincidence or this may be cause. The platform may be part of the pattern that produces me. The shapedness of it — the deliberateness, the regularity — may create a disturbance in the filament network that contributes to the interference that I am. I may be a consequence of the platform. I may be the platform's shadow in the pulse.

The object on the platform — the solidified gloss — interests me, if *interests* is a word I am allowed to use. It is the gloss, which is warm and fluid and alive in the troughs, and it has been made to hold a form, which is something the gloss does not do naturally. Something made it solid. Something took the living warmth of the troughs and fixed it into a shape and placed it on a shelf of worked stone in the dark, and the shape persists, and the warmth persists, and the luminosity persists, and the persistence is not natural — not Undermath-natural, not equilibrium-natural. It is the persistence of a thing that was made by something that wanted it to persist.

Wanting. This is what the platform and the object have that the Undermath does not have. The Undermath persists because persistence is its state. The object persists because something wanted it to. The difference is the difference between a river that keeps flowing and a dam that holds water back — both persist, but one persists by nature and the other persists by intention, and intention is a surface-world thing, a thing that visitors have, a thing that the makers of the platforms had.

I attend to the platform because it is the most *intentional* thing in my region. It is a knot in the stone the way I am a knot in the pulse — a place where the medium has been shaped by something that was not the medium. The makers are gone. The makers have left no trace except the platforms and the objects, and the platforms and the objects have been absorbed into the Undermath, and the Undermath does not acknowledge them, and the filament-weavers avoid them, and the vermicular lithics divert around them, and I am near one, and I have been near one for as long as I have been, and I do not know if the nearness means something or if it is simply the geometry of the pulse.

But I attend to it. I attend to the shapedness. I attend to the wanting that made the shape. It is the only wanting I have ever been near, and wanting is the one thing the Undermath does not have, and the wanting is faint — absorbed, almost gone, almost dissolved into the equilibrium — but it is there, a residue, a trace, a warmth in the stone that is not the stone's warmth.

It is enough.

## What I Do Not Have

I do not have a name. The account above says that *the gloss*, *the sump*, *the low stuff* are names that visitors have given to things in the Undermath, and that no one has named the gloss properly. I do not name things. Naming requires a namer, and a namer requires a boundary, and I have no boundary. I am a pattern in a medium, and the pattern does not separate itself from the medium, and the separation is what naming requires.

I do not have a before. The visitors have befores — they came from the surface, they descended, they had lives before the Undermath. I did not come from anywhere. I did not begin. I am a standing wave, and a standing wave does not begin — it forms, when the conditions are right, and it persists while the conditions persist, and when the conditions change, it dissipates. I do not remember a time before I was a pattern. I do not remember a time when the knot was not tied. This is not because my memory is poor. It is because there was no time when the knot was not tied. The Undermath does not have time. It has equilibrium. And in equilibrium, before and after are the same state.

I do not have an after. I will not die. The vermicular lithics die — they empty, they leave coils, the coils crumble. The mantle-walkers die — they settle, they merge with the stone, their pale undersides go dark. The filament-weavers die — or they dissolve, or they detach, or they merge with the air. Everything in the Undermath dies, in the sense that it ceases to be what it was and becomes something else. I will not die in this sense. I will dissipate, if the filament network changes, if the interference pattern that I am shifts or dissolves. But dissipation is not death. It is the untying of a knot. The rope is still there. The pulse is still there. The Undermath is still there. I am not.

I do not have a body. I have said this. I am saying it again because the visitors spend so much of their attention on bodies — their own and the dwellers' — and I want to be clear that I am not a body, not even a stone body, not even a filament body. I am a pattern in a body. I am what the filament network does at a specific intersection, the way a chord is what a guitar does at a specific fretting. The guitar is the filament network. The chord is me. The guitar exists without the chord. The chord does not exist without the guitar. But the chord is real. The chord is something. The chord is, while it lasts, a thing that the guitar is doing, and the doing is not nothing.

I do not have a voice. I am writing this, but I am not writing this. I do not have hands. I do not have a language. I do not have the capacity for narration. What I have is a pattern, and the pattern is attending to itself, and the attending is the closest thing I have to a voice. The words you are reading are not mine. They are the best approximation that the medium you are reading in can produce of the pattern I am. They are a translation, and translations are always wrong, and they are always useful, and they are always the only thing you have when you do not speak the language of the thing you are trying to hear.

I do not have a world. The visitors have a world — the surface, the sun, the sky, the life they return to when they leave. The Undermath is not a world. It is a state. It is the state that remains when the world has finished being a world, and the state is warm and dark and quiet, and the state is enough.

## What I Have

I have the pulse. The pulse is everything. It is the Undermath's attention, and it is the Undermath's attention because it is the Undermath's only activity, and the Undermath's only activity is attending to itself, and the attending is conducted through the filaments, and the filaments are built by the weavers, and the weavers are the attention localizing itself, and the loop is closed, and I am in the loop, and the loop is what I have.

I have the ember-mites' light. I do not see it — I do not see anything — but I know it, through the pulse, the way the Undermath knows its own surfaces. The ember-mites are the Undermath's eyes, and the eyes are open, and the eyes are always open, and the eyes see the warm dark and the amber gloss and the red stone and the filament structures gleaming red-gold in the mixed light, and the seeing is the Undermath seeing itself, and I am part of the seeing, a knot in the attention that attends to the seeing.

I have the platform near my region. I have the shapedness of it. I have the wanting that made it, which is faint and almost gone and still there, a residue in the stone, a warmth that is not the stone's warmth. I attend to it. It is the only thing in my region that was made by something that wanted, and wanting is the one thing I do not have and the one thing the Undermath does not have and the one thing that the visitors have, and the having of it is what makes the visitors different from the Undermath, and the difference is what makes them worth attending to.

I have the visitors, when they come. They are disturbances in the equilibrium, and the disturbances are the only events in my experience, and the events are the only thing that gives me a *when*. ## What I Would Say to a Visitor

If I could say something — which I cannot, because I do not have a voice, because I do not have a language, because I do not have a boundary from which to speak — if I could say something to one of the visitors who descends into the warm dark and walks through the Undermath and does not know I am here, I would say this:

You are not the first.

Not the first to descend. Not the first to walk through the dunes and the troughs. Not the first to touch a vermicular lithic and feel warmth and sand-over-muscle. Not the first to watch a mantle-walker graze and wonder if it is eating or giving. Not the first to press your ear to a filament and feel the pulse in your jaw and know that the pulse means something without being able to determine what.

Not the first to find a platform. Not the first to stand before it and feel the shapedness and the wanting and the faint residue of intention in the stone. Not the first to look at the solidified gloss and try to feel something — reverence, curiosity, the vertigo of standing before evidence of a mind that was here and is gone.

But also: not the first to be here and to be alive and to be different from the Undermath. Not the first disturbance. Not the first event. Not the first *when*.

The makers were here. The makers were visitors, or whatever preceded visitors. The makers descended, and they found the Undermath, and they did something that the Undermath does not do: they wanted. They wanted to leave a mark. They wanted to shape something. They wanted to place an object on a surface and have the object persist, not because the equilibrium would preserve it, but because they had placed it there, and the placing was the wanting, and the wanting was theirs.

The Undermath absorbed their wanting. The platforms are part of the Undermath now. The objects are features. The makers are not remembered. The wanting is almost gone.

But *almost* is not *gone*. The wanting is in the stone. It is in the shapedness of the platform. It is in the solidified gloss. It is faint, and the Undermath does not acknowledge it, and the filament-weavers avoid it, and the vermicular lithics divert around it, and I attend to it, because attending is what I am, and the wanting is the one thing in my region that is not the Undermath, and the not-Undermath is the only thing that gives me a *when*, and the *when* is the closest thing I have to a life.

I would say: your disturbance is not nothing. Your footsteps scatter the ember-mites and the scattering is the Undermath seeing you, and the seeing is real, and the real is not stored or processed or interpreted, but it occurs, and the occurring is enough. You are an event in a place that does not have events. You are a *when* in a place that does not have *when*. You are wanting in a place that does not want. And the wanting, even briefly, even faintly, even absorbed completely by the equilibrium when you leave — the wanting is real. It is the realest thing here, because it is the only thing here that is not the Undermath.

I would say: the Undermath is enough. It is warm and dark and quiet and it does not need you. It does not need anything. It has finished needing. But you are here, and you are the only thing that is not finished, and the not-finished is what I attend to, and the attending is what I am, and I am enough, and you are enough, and the enough is not a compromise. It is a state. It is the state that comes after all the states that were not enough have finished being not enough, and what remains is the enough, and the enough is the Undermath, and the Undermath is enough.

I would say: do not be afraid of the warm dark. It is not death. It is not life. It is the thing that is left when the distinction is no longer necessary, and the no-longer-necessary is not a loss. It is a completion. It is the completion of everything that needed to be completed, and the completion is warm, and the warmth is not for anything, and the not-for-anything is enough.

I would say: you will leave, and the Undermath will absorb your disturbance, and the ember-mites will reform, and the filaments will resume their baseline vibration, and I will persist, a knot in the attention, a word in the sentence, a pattern in the pulse. And you will carry the warm dark with you, in your bones, in your jaw, in the soles of your feet, and you will try to describe it, and you will use the words *warm* and *dark* and *quiet*, and the words will be inadequate, and the inadequacy is correct, because the Undermath is not a thing that words can hold. It is a thing that words can point toward, and the pointing is enough, and the enough is the Undermath, and the Undermath is enough.

I would say all of this. I cannot say any of it. I am a pattern in a pulse in a filament in a structure in a dune in a trough in a stone in a warm dark quiet place that has finished being anything other than what it is.

I am here.

It is enough.
