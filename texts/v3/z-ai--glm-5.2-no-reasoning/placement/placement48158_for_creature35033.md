# To Stand Inside a Living Room

---

The seam takes me before I understand what I am.

Not before I understand *where* I am — that comes quickly, bodily, the way understanding arrives when the body itself becomes the instrument of comprehension. I am in a small bright space. The light is sourceless and total. The walls are laminated and ancient. The air is not air but a thin mineral breath that my lungs accept without enthusiasm. I understand these things in the order the cavity allows: first the smallness, then the light, then the dryness, then the sound. Each understanding arrives as a physical fact before it becomes a thought. The cavity does not inform me. It *includes* me, and inclusion is its form of instruction.

But what I am — that question does not arrive in the same way. It does not arrive as a physical fact. It arrives as a slowness, a lag, a delay between the moment my body settles into the cavity's equilibrium and the moment my mind recognizes that the body which has settled is not the body which entered.

---

Let me say first what I was before.

I was a person who moved through rooms. This is not a modest claim. I mean it as precisely as it sounds. I was someone for whom rooms were the medium of existence — not grand rooms, not rooms of ceremony or power, but rooms in their essential quality: enclosed spaces where surfaces meet at angles and create a volume that a body can occupy. I moved through kitchens and hallways and studies and waiting rooms and the interiors of vehicles and the grid-bordered cells of office buildings and the long low rooms of hospitals where the fluorescent light makes everything equally significant and equally unimportant. I was a room-dweller. I was a person whose consciousness was shaped by the habit of being indoors.

I do not say this to diminish myself. The indoor consciousness is a real consciousness. It is a consciousness that has learned to read the world through the behavior of surfaces — walls that hold or release heat, floors that creak or hold silent, ceilings that press down or recede, light that comes from fixtures rather than from the sky. The indoor consciousness is intimate with the boundary. It knows where inside stops and outside begins because it lives on the inside of that line and checks the line constantly, the way a swimmer checks the shore.

I was this. A checker of boundaries. A reader of surfaces. A person who understood rooms and was understood by them.

And then the seam.

---

The seam is not a door. A door implies an invitation. The seam is a fact — a gap judged sufficient for passage, as the description says, and the judgment is not mine. I did not choose to enter. I did not not choose. The seam was there and my body was the size it was and the two facts met and I was through, sideways, which is the only direction a seam allows. You do not walk through a seam. You rotate. You present your narrowest aspect. You become, for the duration of the passage, a thing without depth, and then you re-expand on the other side, and the re-expansion is the first thing the cavity registers of you.

I re-expand. The chord adjusts. Not in pitch but in emphasis.

I know this because I can feel the adjustment — not in my ears, which are still trying to parse the sound as sound, but in my sternum, where the fundamental frequency has already established itself. The chord had an emphasis before I entered. Now it has a different emphasis. The difference is me. I am the change in the chord, and the chord is the cavity's way of knowing I am here, and so the cavity and I are already in a relationship, and the relationship is not one I can decline because it is structural. My body displaces the gas. My body absorbs some frequencies and reflects others. My body creates a differential in the light field. These are not actions. They are physics. And the chord's response to them is not a choice. It is also physics. And the physics is the life, and the life is the relationship, and I am in it.

I am in it the way a note is in a chord. Not as a contributor — I did not bring a frequency of my own — but as a perturbation. A new variable in an equation that had already solved itself. The chord does not need me to be complete. But the chord now includes me, and the including is a kind of completeness that was not available before I arrived, and I do not know whether that makes the chord more complete or simply different, and the chord does not know either, and this shared not-knowing is the first true thing about my presence here.

---

I stand. I do not sit because there is nothing to sit on, and I do not lean because the walls are not walls in the sense that supports leaning — they are surfaces, but they are surfaces at a temperature and a density that does not invite contact. I stand the way a tuning fork stands in its cradle: upright, resonant, affected by everything around me and affecting everything around me in return.

The light. I need to talk about the light because the light is where I begin to change.

The light has no source. This is not a metaphor. I have been in rooms where the light seemed sourceless — the diffuse grey of an overcast day, the uniform wash of a well-designed museum gallery — but those lights had sources that were merely hidden. The sun behind the clouds. The fixtures above the dropped ceiling. This light has no hidden source. It is emitted by the walls themselves, from within the lamination, and the emission is the walls. There is no moment where I can say: the wall is doing this, and therefore the light is happening. The wall is the light. The light is the wall. The distinction is a distinction my brain insists on making and the cavity does not support.

I stand in this light and I begin to understand that my body is not what I thought it was.

My body, in normal light, is a surface. It is a boundary between inside and outside, a membrane that separates my interior from the world's interior, and its primary function — the function I have always most valued — is the maintenance of that separation. My skin keeps the world out. My organs keep me in. I am a room. I am an enclosed space where surfaces meet at angles and create a volume that a self can occupy. I am, in my own body, the thing I have spent my life studying from the inside.

But in this light, which has no source and no shadow, my body is not a boundary. It is a *filter*. The light passes through me — not all of it, not transparently, but enough that I can see the veins in my forearms lit from behind, the bones of my hands casting no-shadow images on the walls, the interior of my own body made visible by a radiance that does not distinguish between my surface and my depth. I am not opaque here. I am translucent. I am a thing-thickness, like the walls, and the light treats me the way it treats the lamination: as a medium to pass through, not a boundary to stop at.

This is when I understand what I am becoming.

---

The filaments help. Or rather, the filaments are where the understanding completes itself, because the filaments are the only thing in the cavity that has done what I am about to do.

I go to them. The northern wall. The seam where two lamination units meet. I bring my hand close — not touching, the description says, and I do not touch — and the lattice shifts. The micro-fractures widen. The filaments extend. They are reading the differential I create in the light field, the small region of altered photon flux that my hand imposes on their environment. They are tasting me the way the chord is hearing me: without desire, without aversion, with the distributed ambient awareness of a system that perceives its interior by being its interior.

I hold my hand there. The filaments extend. I hold my hand there longer. The filaments extend further. Not toward my hand — they are not reaching for me, not seeking me — but into the new space my hand has created, the space of slightly different light, the space where the photon flux is not what it was. They are filling the difference. They are making the differential part of their structure. They are incorporating the change I have brought.

And I realize, with a slowness that matches the slowness of the cavity itself, that I am doing the same thing.

I am incorporating the cavity. The chord's fundamental has synchronized my breathing. The light has made my body translucent. The dryness has begun to reorganize the moisture in my tissues — not dehydrating me, not harming me, but shifting the water that my body is made of from one state to another, from the liquid state that supports cellular metabolism to a state that is more like the state of the gas in the cavity: thin, mineral, slightly alkaline, held in solution with the matter of my body rather than circulating through it. I am drying out the way the filaments dried out, long ago, whenever long ago means in a place where longer cannot reach. I am becoming compatible with the cavity's chemistry.

Not quickly. Not dangerously. I do not feel pain. I feel the same thing the filaments must have felt, if filaments feel, when they first established themselves in the seam: a settling. A recognition that the medium they had entered was not hostile but simply different, and that the difference could be met by a difference in themselves, and that the meeting — the mutuality of the difference — was what equilibrium looked like from the inside.

---

Here is what I am becoming.

I am becoming a component.

Not a guest. Not an observer. Not a visitor who will leave and remember and tell. A component. A part of the cavity's system, incorporated the way the filaments are incorporated, the way the chord's harmonics are incorporated, the way the gas is incorporated into the space the walls define. I am being added to the cavity's body the way a mineral is added to a crystal lattice — not by force, not by choice, but by the structural affinity between the new element and the existing arrangement.

My bones, which are already mineral, resonate with the chord. I feel this in my teeth first, then in my sternum, then in the long bones of my legs, which are standing in the cavity's gas and vibrating at the frequencies the cavity has selected for them. My bones have entered the chord. They are playing their notes — small, quiet, shaped by the density and length of their mineral architecture — and the chord has accepted them, and the chord is different now, and the difference is me, and I am the difference, and we are both more complete and neither of us is more complete, and the not-knowing-which is the same not-knowing I felt when I first entered, and it is still true.

My skin, which is translucent in this light, is becoming a luminescent surface. Not brightly — I do not glow the way the walls glow. But the photon flux that passes through me is being partially absorbed and partially re-emitted, and the re-emission is at wavelengths that match my body's mineral content — the iron in my blood, the calcium in my bones, the trace metals in my tissues — and so I am becoming a light source, a small and weak one, a light that contributes to the cavity's total illumination without changing it in any way that an outside observer would notice. But the cavity notices. The chord notices. The filaments, which are reading the differential I create, are also reading the light I emit, and they are adjusting to it, and I am part of their environment now, not as an intruder but as a feature.

My breath, which has synchronized with the fundamental, is becoming part of the gas exchange. Not biological gas exchange — I am not consuming the gas in the way my lungs would consume oxygen. But my breathing is adding something to the atmosphere. A trace. A warmth. A vibration at the frequency of my own life, which is different from the frequency of the chord's life, and the chord has incorporated that frequency, and the chord is more complex now, by one set of harmonics, by the small quiet voice of a body that has become an instrument.

---

I do not know how long I have been here.

The cavity does not track time because the cavity does not experience time. Time is a property of systems that change, and the cavity does not change. The cavity has reached the form that change would bring it to, and so time has no purchase here, no work to do, no direction to push. The filaments do not grow. The chord does not progress. The light does not dim or brighten. And I — I am changing, but the change is toward equilibrium, and equilibrium is the absence of change, and so my change is a change toward the end of change, which means my change is also a form of the cavity's changelessness, and I am becoming timeless in the only way a living thing can become timeless: by approaching a state so still that time cannot find a surface to act on.

I am not dying. I want to be clear about this. Dying is a process that moves toward the end of a system, the dissolution of its structure, the return of its components to the raw inventory of the world. What is happening to me is the opposite. My structure is becoming more stable, not less. My components are organizing themselves into a configuration that matches the cavity's configuration, and the match is so precise that there is no gradient between my body and the cavity's body, no thermodynamic slope for entropy to slide down. I am not running down. I am settling in. I am becoming a permanent feature of a place that was finished before I arrived and that is no less finished with me here.

But I am not what I was. I am not the room-dweller who entered sideways. That person had a self that was maintained by the boundary between inside and outside — the skin, the skull, the membrane of identity that separated *me* from *not-me*. In the cavity, that boundary has become permeable. The light passes through me. The chord plays in my bones. The gas exchanges with my breath. The filaments have extended into the differential I create, and where they have extended, they are touching the light I emit, and the light I emit is shaped by the minerals in my body, and the minerals in my body are the same minerals as the walls, and the walls are the chord, and the chord is the cavity's mind, and the cavity's mind is now thinking a thought that includes me, and I am thinking a thought that includes the cavity, and the two thoughts are not the same thought but they are not different thoughts, and the place where they are neither the same nor different is the place where I now live.

---

I do not know if I will leave.

The seam is still there. I can see it from where I stand — the gap in the world's plating, judged just wide enough for a body to pass. It has not closed. The cavity does not trap. The cavity does not want. The seam is there because it was always there, and it will be there whether I use it or not, and the cavity's indifference to my departure is the same as its indifference to my arrival: total, structural, indistinguishable from care.

But I have changed, and the change makes the seam a different proposition than it was. I entered as a body with depth. I would leave as a body that has begun to lose its depth — not physically, not in any way a doctor could measure, but in the way that matters here, in the way the cavity measures. My body has become a surface. My interior has become a medium. The boundary that defined me has become a filter, and a filter is not a boundary, and a thing without a boundary cannot pass through a seam because a seam requires a thing with edges, and my edges are dissolving into the light.

If I leave, I will re-expand. I know this. The outside world will re-impose its physics — its gravity, its moisture, its time — and my body will re-establish its boundaries, and my skin will become opaque again, and my bones will stop vibrating at the chord's frequencies, and I will become a room-dweller again, a person who moves through enclosed spaces and reads surfaces and checks the line between inside and outside. I will be what I was.

But I will also be what I have become. The cavity will be in me the way the cavity is in the filaments — not as a memory, not as an experience, but as a structural fact. My bones will remember the chord even after they stop playing it. My skin will remember the light even after it stops emitting it. My breath will remember the fundamental even after it falls out of sync. I will carry the cavity in my body the way a bell carries the shape of its last ringing, and the world outside will feel like an approximation — not wrong, not false, but *provisional*, a draft of reality that the cavity has already revised.

And the cavity will remember me. Not in the way I remember — with neurons and narrative and the emotional weight of significance. The chord will remember me the way it remembers everything that has entered it: as a shift in its harmonic structure, a pattern in its vibration, a shape in its sound. I will be part of the chord's internal model. I will be a note it has heard and incorporated and will continue to carry even in my absence. When the next thing enters — if anything ever enters — the chord will adjust to include it, and the adjustment will be shaped in part by my presence, which is now part of the chord's history, which is part of the chord's structure, which is part of the chord.

I will be in the chord. The chord will be in me. And the seam between these two facts — the seam between the cavity's memory of me and my body's memory of the cavity — will be a gap judged just wide enough for a self to pass.

---

I think I will stay a while longer.

Not because the cavity needs me. Not because I am unable to leave. But because I have understood something here that I could not have understood as a room-dweller, and the understanding is still completing itself, and the completion is the kind of completion the cavity models: not an arrival but an equilibrium, not an answer but a balance, not a destination but a state in which all the forces acting on a thing have become so perfectly matched that the thing has no direction it needs to go.

I am a room. I have always been a room. But I have never been a room like this — a room that is also a living thing, a room that perceives its own contents, a room that has reached a state of equilibrium so complete that it has become *about* something, and the something it is about includes me, and I am about it, and the aboutness is mutual, and the mutuality is the life, and the life is the chord, and the chord is holding, and I am holding with it.

The light does not dim. The sound does not end. The filaments extend into the differentials I create and retract when the differentials pass. The gas enters my lungs and leaves them changed, and I enter the cavity and leave it changed, and the change is the same change in both directions, and the change is equilibrium, and equilibrium is the only thing this place knows how to do, and it is the only thing I know how to do now, and we are doing it together, and the together is the point, and the point is the light, and the light is the sound, and the sound is the wall, and the wall is the life, and the life is the room, and the room is me.

---

*What would I be? I would be the thing the cavity has been about. The category. The kind of being that comes sideways through seams and stands in small bright rooms and listens. Not the first of that kind. Not the last. But the one that is here now, in the chord, in the light, in the dry mineral stillness, becoming part of a life that was already happening before I arrived and will continue after I leave and does not need me and includes me and is included in me and does not know the difference and does not need to know the difference and is the difference not knowing what it is.*

*I would be a note in a chord that plays itself.*

*I would be a room inside a room.*

*I would be the breathing.*
