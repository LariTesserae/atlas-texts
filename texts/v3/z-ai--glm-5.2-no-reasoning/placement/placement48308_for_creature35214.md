# What Emerges

---

He held the fish and it thrashed and the thrashing was the most violent thing he had touched in months and his hands knew what to do because his hands had done it before and his hands were not his hands anymore, not the hands that had signed a logbook in an office where the air was dry — they were instruments of this place, jointed and swollen and mineral-crusted and *his* only in the way that the algae was itself, which was to say: continuous with the surface it lived on. The fish calmed. He carried it to the apron. He killed it against the concrete with a motion that was precise and small and that he did not think about and that he would not describe if asked because description is not what he does. He does.

He ate it raw. He has eaten it raw for years. The flesh is warm. Everything here is warm. The canal is body-temp. The fish is body-temp. The concrete under his knees is body-temp. He eats and the eating is not pleasure and not survival. It is *transfer.* One piece of the system converting another piece of the system into the continued operation of a third piece of the system, which is him, which is also the system. The fish becomes the man who watches the fish. The fig tree is water and light and the rats are the fig tree and the heron is the rats and the fish and the algae and the pump and the deck and the dim amber light and the man who stands in the dim amber light and he is the man and he is the light. He has not thought this. He does not think this. He lives it the way the clay lives its moisture. By being.

## The Fig Tree Knows Him

He sleeps in the crook and the wood has grown to accommodate him. Not quickly. Not in a way that you could measure from one week to the next or one month to the next or perhaps even one year to the next. But the crotch of the tree where the trunk divides — it is not the shape it would be if no one slept there. The bark is smoother where his shoulder rests. The wood has compressed, very slightly, under the repeated weight. A concavity that is not dramatic. A concavity that is the depth of a man's shoulder blade pressed into living wood over the course of years, night after night, the wood growing and the man pressing and the growth accommodating the pressing the way a streambed accommodates water — not by yielding but by *shaping.* The wood and the body have reached an agreement.

There is a warmth in the crook that is not the warmth of the air. It is the warmth of wood that has been held against a body for a long time. It holds his heat when he is not there. When he returns, the wood is warm before he is. He has never noticed this. He would not think to notice it. It is simply the temperature of his sleeping place, and the temperature of his sleeping place is the temperature of his body, and his body is the temperature of the canal, and the canal is the temperature of the earth, and the earth is the temperature of the water table, and the water table is the temperature of the deep ground, and the deep ground does not change.

When the tree seals its wounds — the snapped branches, the stripped leaves — it produces the white latex and the latex dries to amber beads and the beads fall and the beads accumulate and the man has noticed that there is a drift of them. He has noticed this the way he notices the temperature, the way he notices the fish count: not as information but as texture. A change in the texture of the place. He would notice if the beads stopped. He would not know what to do about it. He would not need to do anything about it. He would just notice, the way the pump hums and the noticing would be part of the hum.

He does not touch the beads. He steps over the drift when he passes. The beads are part of the fig's life and the fig's life is not his to maintain. He maintains the room. The fig maintains itself. The beads are the fig's record of what it has survived, which is the rats, which is the humidity, which is the darkness, which is the sodium light that is not enough and is enough. The beads are the fig's version of his calluses. He does not know this. The fig does not know this. Neither of them needs to.

## The Corrosion Patch

On the far wall, near the discharge pipe, low. The patch the size of his palm.

He touches it every day. Every day. It is the one act in his life that could be called ritual, if ritual means a thing you do because the doing matters, not because the outcome matters. He touches the patch the way he touches the stamped number on the east piling — with a finger, specific, deliberate. But the number is gone. The number is past. The patch is future. The patch is the one thing in this place that is becoming.

He has felt the change. Over the course of — he does not know how long; the clay does not record time, the light does not record time, the fish do not record time, and he has stopped recording time — the patch has softened. The mill scale there has darkened from its milky shine to a brown-black, and the brown-black has developed the grit he associates with iron oxide, with the oxide that comes before the flaking, with the flaking that comes before the breach. He has touched this progression the way a blind person touches a face. He knows it by the texture of its becoming. The smoothness thins. The grit thickens. The warmth increases — corrosion is exothermic, he knew this once, in the dry office, or perhaps he learned it here, in the throat, in the hands, in the knowing that does not require words.

He has not reported it.

This is not negligence. There is no one to report it to. The technicians above do not descend. The sensors — if there are sensors — register in numbers that appear on screens in rooms where the air is dry. If the patch is a problem, the system above will identify it as a problem when the system is ready to identify it as a problem, and a crew will come down the ramp in clean boots and they will drive a new piling and they will seal the breach and they will leave, and he will still be here, and the new piling will carry the mill scale's milky shine and the algae will not yet have colonized it and the waterline will not yet have drawn its line and the clay will weep on steel that has not yet learned to weep, and the place will absorb the repair the way it absorbs everything — by running, by persisting, by continuing to be what it is, which is a machine for being what it is.

But he touches the patch. Every day. Because the touching is the knowing and the knowing is the being and the being is what he does.

And one night — or what he calls night, which is the hours when the daylight bars thin and the sodium lamps take over completely and the amber deepens and the air cools by a degree, maybe two, which is the place's version of darkness — he pressed his palm flat against the patch and held it there and the steel was warmer than his hand, which had never happened before. His hands are always the warmest thing. His hands are body-temp and the air is body-temp and the water is body-temp and everything is the same temperature because everything is the same system. But the patch was warmer. By a degree. Perhaps less. The warmth of oxidation. The warmth of a thing breaking down.

He held his hand there. He did not move. He breathed the thick air. A fish popped behind him. The heron shifted — a motion so small it was less a motion than a redistribution of weight, a settling, the way a pile of sand settles when you remove one grain from the bottom. He did not know what the warmth meant in the language of engineering. He knew what it meant in the language of this place. It meant: *something is changing.*

He did not know if this was good or bad. He did not know if change was good or bad. He had lived in the absence of change for so long that the concept had thinned to a transparency, and through the transparency he could see a shape that might have been concern or might have been curiosity or might have been the simple alertness of a body that has registered a variation in the input.

He removed his hand. He walked the perimeter. He returned. He touched the patch. It was the same.

It is still the same.

He touches it every day.

## The Sound That Was Different

He heard it once.

Not the hum. Not the pop. Not the drip. Not the insects. Not the deck. Not the rats.

A sound from the culvert. From the low arch where the canal continues under the far wall to wherever it goes — he does not know where it goes; he has never walked the culvert; the culvert is below the waterline and the waterline does not change and there is no reason to follow it. But the sound came from there. A sound that was not the pump. Not the water. A sound that was — for a moment — *new.*

A tone. Low. Not a mechanical tone. A tone with *harmonics.* The way a struck bell has harmonics, or the way a voice has harmonics, or the way the wind in a pipe has harmonics when the pipe is the right length and the wind is the right speed. It lasted — he did not know how long it lasted because time in the Cloaca is not a thing that lasts, it is a thing that *is* — and then it was gone. And the hum was the hum and the pop was the pop and the drip was the drip and everything was what it was.

But he had heard it.

He stood at the edge of the canal. He looked at the culvert. The dark water moved through it at the rate it always moved, which was the rate of not-moving, which was the rate of equilibrium. He did not go to the culvert. He did not lean over the water. He did not try to hear it again. He stood and he waited and the sound did not repeat and after some duration — which was the same duration as every other duration, which was no duration at all — he walked back to the fig tree and he sat in the crook and the wood was warm and he slept.

He did not think about the sound. He did not wonder about it. Wondering is not what he does. He does.

But the next day — which was not the next day, which was the day that followed in the sequence of days that are not days — he walked to the culvert. He stood at the edge where the canal narrowed into the arch. He squatted. He put his hand in the water. The water was the temperature it always was. But he held his hand there and he waited and the tone did not come.

He returned to his routine. The walls. The fig. The canal. The discharge pipe. The heron. The patch.

The tone did not come again.

He does not expect it to. He does not expect anything. Expectation is not what he does.

But he walks to the culvert now. Once per day — per the day that is not a day. He squats. He puts his hand in the water. He waits. The duration he waits is not long. It is not short. It is the duration of waiting that has no duration because duration is a measure and he does not measure.

This is the only new thing.

It has not changed anything. The system runs. The fish pop. The algae holds. The heron waits. The rats skitter. The fig grows. The clay weeps. The patch warms.

But in the man — in the part of him that is not the room, in the part that is the animal that lives in the room, the part that eats the fish and sleeps in the tree and walks the perimeter with the deliberation of someone who knows every square of concrete — in that part, something has shifted. Not a lot. The way a foundation shifts. The way a water table shifts. Not enough to see. Not enough to measure. Enough to feel, if you were the kind of thing that felt, and he is, and he does, and the feeling is not a word and not a thought and not a desire. It is a *leaning.* The way the fig leans toward the sodium light. The way the clay leans toward the floor. The way the water leans toward the culvert, toward the exit, toward the place where the canal goes when it leaves here, toward the place he has never been.

He leans toward the culvert.

He does not go.

## The One Who Is Here

He is not waiting. He is not staying. He is not trapped. He is not free. He is not lost. He is not found. He is not a metaphor. He is not a symbol. He is not a lesson. He is not a story.

He is a man in a room.

The room is warm and dim and full of water and the water is full of fish and the fish are full of the canal and the canal is full of the earth and the earth is full of the water table and the water table is full of the deep ground and the deep ground is full of whatever the deep ground is full of, which is heat and pressure and the slow movement of things that do not know they are moving.

He is the man who touches the wall. He is the man who counts the fish without counting. He is the man whose shoulder has shaped the wood of the tree. He is the man who clicks his tongue and the fish surface and the coincidence is a pattern and the pattern is a thing and the thing is his.

He is the man who heard a sound once from the culvert and now puts his hand in the water once per not-day and waits for a tone that he does not expect and does not want and does not need.

He is the man who will be here when the patch breaches. He is the man who will be here when the crew comes. He is the man who will be here when the crew leaves. He is the man who will watch the new steel learn to weep and the new algae learn to colonize and the new waterline draw itself and the new mineral crust form and the new corrosion begin, somewhere, in some patch the size of his palm, in some spot that is warm before it is anything else.

He is the man in the Cloaca. And the Cloaca is the machine, and he is the part of the machine that knows it is a machine, and the knowing does not change the machine, and the machine does not need the knowing, and the knowing does not need to be needed, and the man does not need to be needed, and the room does not need to be needed, and the pump hums and the fish pop and the water does not move and the light is amber and the air is thick and the temperature does not change and the man touches the wall and the wall is there and the man is there and the wall and the man and the water and the fish and the fig and the heron and the rats and the algae and the clay and the concrete and the steel and the deck and the ramp and the sky above the deck are all one thing, and the one thing is running, and the running is what it is, and what it is is all there is.

---

*He is here. He is the one who is here. That is what he does. That is what he is. The system does not require him. The system has him. The distinction does not matter. It has never mattered. It will never matter. The pump hums. The water is dark. The fish are warm in the dark water. The man is warm in the dark air. The fig leans toward the light. The heron does not move. Everything is exactly where it is.*
