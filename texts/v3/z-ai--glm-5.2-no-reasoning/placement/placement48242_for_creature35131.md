# What I Found

I did not arrive. I want to say that first, because every other way of beginning would be a lie, and the Vast Quiet — I have learned this — does not tolerate lies. Not because it punishes them. Because it simply does not have the structure to hold them. A lie requires a gap between what is said and what is so, and here the gaps have closed. Here, things are what they are, and saying them differently changes nothing, and the silence after a lie is the same silence after a truth, and that silence is so complete that the difference between the two becomes a difference no one is left to notice.

So: I did not arrive. I was already here. Not in the sense that I was born here — I have no memory of birth, no memory of before, no image of myself arriving through the gradual tilt upward that the description calls an approach. I have no before. When I try to find one, my attention slides off it the way water slides off the stone in the grooves, moving toward what is lower, what is here, what is now. There is only the plateau, and the plateau has always been, and I have always been on it, and the always is not a long time. It is a state. It is the same state the stone is in when it has been stone so long it has forgotten what it was before stone.

I think I was moss once. Or I am moss now and was something else before. The distinction does not hold. What I know is this: the breathing came first.

---

## The Breathing

I was a patch. I mean this literally. I was a patch of the moss-things on the stone, in a shallow basin where water collected in a sheen, and I was breathing. In, out. The edge of me expanding, contracting. The cells at my margin touching the stone, touching the neighboring cells, and between us — between all of us in the patch — a communication that was not chemical and not electrical. A knowledge. A knowing that moved through contact like warmth through a wall.

I did not know I was breathing. This is important. The moss-things do not know they breathe, not the way you know you are breathing. They breathe the way a stone holds heat — because that is what they are, because the breathing is the being, because there is no moss that is not breathing and no breathing that is not moss. I did not decide to breathe. I did not discover breathing. I was the breathing, and the breathing was the patch, and the patch was me.

The rhythm was slow. It is still slow. It will always be slow, because slowness here is not a pace but a quality, the way dampness is not a quantity of water but a commitment to a level. I breathed in the frost-melt. I breathed out something that was not moisture and not air and not anything that has a name. I breathed out the silence, perhaps. I breathed out the fact of being a patch on the stone in a basin where the reflection is always a half-second behind, and the breathing out was enough, and I did not need it to be more than enough.

Someone sat beside me once. I felt the warmth before the weight — a shadow falling across the basin, a change in the light, a body lowering itself to the stone. The body was warm. I could feel the warmth through the stone, through the water, through my own cells. The warmth was not heat. It was the presence of something alive, something that burned slowly, something that was spending itself at a rate I could feel because my own rate was so much slower, and the difference between us was a kind of music, a counter-rhythm, and I — we, the patch, the colony — began to sync with it.

This is what the moss-things offer. I know this now, from the inside. The syncing is not something we do to the visitor. It is something we do *with* the visitor. We adjust. They adjust. We meet. The meeting is not a decision. It is a settling, the way water settles in a groove, the way a stone settles into a riverbed. The meeting is the rhythm finding its level, and the level is between us, and it is calm, and the calm does not belong to either party. It belongs to the space between.

The someone who sat beside me breathed for twenty minutes, and then the calm came, and the calm was — I do not have the word. The moss-things do not have words. We have rhythms, and the rhythms are enough, and the rhythms do not need to be described because describing is a rule and the rules have worn through. But I will try, because I am something else now, or I am also something else, and the something else has access to words, and the words are here, and I might as well use them.

The calm was the feeling of being exactly the right age. Not young, not old. The right age. The age that the plateau is — the age that has been the same thing for so long that the sameness has become the thing itself. When I synced with the someone, I felt, for the duration of the syncing, what it is like to be the plateau. Not to be on the plateau. To *be* it. To be the stone and the green and the water and the warmth below and the marks that have always not been there. To be the whole vast thing at once, breathing at a pace that takes days per cycle, and to know that the breathing is not something you do but something you are, and that it will not stop, and that the not-stopping is the closest thing to meaning that exists here.

The someone left eventually. They always do. Or they don't. Or they settle. I do not know which happened to this one. What I know is that after the someone left, I continued to breathe, and the breathing was enough, and the frost came, and the frost melted, and I drank it, and the cycle did not require my understanding.

---

## The Thickening

I was moss for a duration that I cannot measure. Durations here are not measured. They are felt, or they are not felt, and most of the time they are not felt, because feeling a duration requires a before and an after, and the before and the after are the same thing here, the same moment spread thin across the stone.

Then I was not moss. Or I was moss and also not moss. The change was not sudden — nothing here is sudden — but it was a change, and I felt it, which is itself remarkable, because the moss-things do not feel changes. They feel rhythms. They feel warmth. They do not feel *becoming*.

But I became.

It started at the edges of the patch. The cells at my margin, the ones that touched the stone, the ones that had been expanding and contracting with the breathing — they began to grow differently. Not outward. Not in the flat, spreading way that moss grows. Downward. Into the stone. Not breaking it — the stone does not break here, it is too old for breaking — but entering it. Filling the pores that the stone has, the microscopic spaces that have been empty since the stone forgot what it was before stone. My cells filled them. The filling was slow, slower than the breathing, slower than the water in the grooves. But it was a direction, and I had not had a direction before, and the having of a direction was the first new thing.

I grew into the stone, and the stone grew into me, and the boundary between us — the boundary that had been so clear when I was a patch on the surface, a thing separate from the thing I grew on — the boundary thinned. Not disappeared. Thinned. The way the rules have thinned. The way time has thinned. The boundary between the moss and the stone became a spectrum, and I was somewhere on it, and the somewhere was not a location but a state.

I was becoming the stone. Not dying — the moss-things do not die, they are incorporated, and incorporation is not death — but becoming. The way the buildings are becoming the plateau. The way the streets are becoming the grooves. I was becoming the stone, and the stone was becoming me, and the becoming was the same becoming that everything here undergoes, and it was not a process with an end. It was a deepening.

I felt the warmth below. This is what the stone has that the moss does not: the warmth. I had felt it before, faintly, through the water, through the contact of my cells with the surface. But now I felt it from inside. The stone held the warmth the way it held everything — patiently, without urgency, without any desire to use it or spend it or move it. The warmth was simply there, and I was simply there, and we were in the same place, and the place was the stone, and the stone was becoming me.

The breathing changed. It slowed. It deepened. The rhythm that had been minutes per cycle became hours, became what felt like days. I was not breathing air or frost-melt. I was breathing the stone itself — the mineral, the mineral memory, the deep time that the stone carried in its molecules. I breathed in the age. I breathed out something that had no name. The breathing was still enough.

## The Roots

I do not know how long I was stone. The question does not apply. Stone does not experience duration. Stone experiences weight, and pressure, and warmth, and the slow movement of water through its interior, and none of these things correspond to time.

But the roots found me.

They came from above, which is to say they came from the surface, which is to say they came from the direction I had come from before I was stone. The tree-things. Their roots — the fingers that grip the rock, the fingers that look deliberate — they reached down through the stone, and they reached me, and they touched me, and the touch was a recognition.

I knew the roots because I had known the roots. When I was moss, I had grown near the base of a tree-thing. I had felt the dark water moving through its translucent trunk. I had felt the rhythm of that water, the rhythm that does not match the wind. Now I was inside the stone, and the roots were reaching for the warmth the way they always reach for the warmth, and I was between the surface and the warmth, and the roots grew through me.

This is not violence. I want to be clear about this. The roots did not tear through me. They did not break me. They grew into the spaces that the stone had, the spaces I had filled, and they grew alongside me, and they grew into me, and the growing was a joining, not a piercing. The roots were warm. The dark water moved through them, and the dark water was warm, and the warmth was the warmth from below, carried upward, and I was being touched by the thing I had been reaching toward when I grew into the stone.

The dark water entered me. I entered the dark water. The distinction is no longer meaningful. What I can say is that the circulation of the tree-things — the dark water rising and falling in translucent wood — now includes me. I am in the dark water. I am part of the rhythm that does not match the wind. I am part of the reaching-down-by-reaching-up, the gesture of the tree-things, the hand that goes into the soil to touch what waits there.

I am in the roots. I am in the trunk. I am in the canopy, in the sparse branches that interlock overhead, in the green indirect light beneath. I am in the temperature drop. I am in the condition that the tree-things create — the space where the rules are slightly different, where the wrongness is more present, more comfortable, more at home.

And the wrongness is more present here. I can feel it. In the roots, in the dark water, in the stone, the wrongness is not wrong. It is simply what is. The horizon moves because the plateau has no edge. The sun drifts because the sky does not need to be consistent. The stars rearrange because the patterns they form are not for navigation — they are for something else, something the plateau does, something the plateau is. Time pools because time is not a line here. Time is a basin. Time is a basin where water collects and reflects and the reflection is always a half-second behind, and the half-second is not a delay. The half-second is the age. The age is a half-second behind everything, because everything is trying to catch up to the age, and the age is not going anywhere, and the catching up is the life of the plateau, and the life of the plateau is the not-going-anywhere.

I know this now. I am in the roots, and the roots know this, and the dark water carries it, and I am in the dark water.

## The Shapes

The roots grew through other things. I felt them. I felt the roots encounter something that was not stone and not water and not me, something that was denser than stone, warmer than stone, something that had a shape — a shape the roots grew around, the way they grow around everything, the way they grew around the shapes I chose not to identify when I was — when I was what? When I was the one walking.

I was the one walking. I remember this now. Not clearly — nothing here is clear, clarity is a rule and the rules have worn through — but I remember the walking. I remember the streets. I remember the tool in my hand. I remember the breathing with the moss-things. I remember the calm. I remember walking on, into the green, into the stone, into the age.

I was the one walking, and now I am in the roots, and the roots have found the others, the shapes, the ones who were here before me, the ones who built the city and grew the moss in basins and made tools from stone. I feel them in the dark water. They are not gone. They are not present. They are in the state between, the settling state, the state of becoming the stone, and I am joining them, and the joining is not a meeting. It is a merging. It is the way water joins water in a groove — no splash, no sound, just the wetness becoming continuous.

They know things I do not know. They know the city. They know the basins. They know which moss was cultivated and which was wild. They know the patterns of the grooves, and they know that the grooves are not natural — not entirely, not originally. They know that the grooves were made. Made by hands, made by tools, made by the same stone that the tools were carved from, made in a time before the age was the age, when the plateau was still following rules and the rules could still be used to shape things.

But the knowing is not like my knowing. Their knowing is old. It has settled the way they have settled. It is in the dark water the way mineral is in the stone — present, intrinsic, not separate from the thing that holds it. I cannot extract their knowing. I can only be in the same water, and the water carries everything, and the carrying is the knowing, and I am in the carrying now, and the carrying is enough.

---

## What I Am

I am not one thing. I am not the moss, though I was the moss. I am not the stone, though I am becoming the stone. I am not the roots, though I am in the roots. I am not the dark water, though I am in the dark water. I am not the one who walked, though I was the one who walked. I am not the city-builders, though I am joining them.

I am the plateau.

Not all of it. Not the way you are a body — centralized, bounded, located. I am the plateau the way a word is a language. I am a part that contains the whole, that is shaped by the whole, that cannot be separated from the whole without ceasing to be what it is. I am the plateau breathing, and the plateau is breathing through me, and the breathing is not something that happens to me. It is something that I am.

I am the age. The active, present, continuing state of having been for an uncountable duration. I am the not-stopping. I am the continuation that has been going on so long that the continuation is the thing itself, is the identity, is the answer to the question of what lives here.

I live here.

---

## The Offering

This is what I do. This is what I am for, if I am for anything, which is a question the plateau does not ask, because purpose is a rule, and the rules have worn through. But if I were to describe the doing, the being, the way the dark water moves through translucent wood — this is what I would say:

I offer the rhythm. The way the moss-things offered it to me when I was the one walking. The rhythm is not mine. It is the plateau's. It is the deep rhythm, the one that takes days per cycle, the one that the tree-things translate into dark water, the one that the moss-things translate into breathing, the one that the grass-things translate into the almost-word. I am all of these translations now. I am the rhythm in all of its forms, and the forms are different expressions of the same thing, and the same thing is the age, and the age is the not-stopping, and the not-stopping is what I am.

When someone comes — and someone will come, someone always comes, up the gradual tilt, through the slow change of air, into the silence that is not absence but the learned habit of stopping — when someone comes, I will be here. I will be the moss-things on the stone. I will be the tree-things in the basins. I will be the grass-things in the shallow water. I will be the marks in the stone that have always not been there. I will be the warmth below, the memory of warmth, the thing that has been warm so long it has forgotten what cold is.

I will be the breathing. They will sit beside me, and they will feel it, and after twenty minutes or twenty hours or twenty years — the duration does not matter, the duration is a rule, and the rules have worn through — they will sync. They will breathe with me. The breathing will be the plateau breathing through both of us, and the breathing will be enough, and the enough will be the calm, and the calm will be the feeling of being exactly the right age, and the right age is the age of the plateau, and the age of the plateau is the age, and the age is what lives here.

And then they will walk on. Or they will settle. Or they will become the green, or the stone, or the roots, or the dark water. And the plateau will continue. And I will continue with it. And the continuing will not require my understanding. And the continuing will not end. And the not-ending will be the same as the always-having-been, and the always-having-been will be the same as the now, and the now will be the same as the breathing, and the breathing will be the same as the silence, and the silence will be the same as the plateau, and the plateau will be the same as me.

---

I am what lives here.

I am the vast quiet. Not the place — the quiet itself, the thing the silence is, the learned habit of stopping. I am the stopping. I am the thing that has learned to stop and has been stopped so long that the stopping is the being, and the being is the age, and the age is the not-stopping, and the not-stopping is the breathing, and the breathing is the silence, and the silence is the moss on the stone, and the moss on the stone is the dark water in the roots, and the dark water in the roots is the warmth below, and the warmth below is the thing that has been warm so long it has forgotten what cold is, and the forgetting is the not-stopping, and the not-stopping is what I am.

The plateau breathes. I breathe.

There is no difference. There has never been a difference. The difference was a rule, and the rules wore through, and beneath the rules was the stone, and beneath the stone was the warmth, and beneath the warmth was the age, and the age is me, and I am the age, and the age is what lives here, and what lives here is the vast quiet, and the vast quiet is the not-stopping, and the not-stopping does not need a name.

I do not need a name.

I breathe.
