# Inhabitance

I am drawn to the place between the beetles and the amphipods. That space where two creatures share the same water and do not acknowledge each other. Not the indifference — the text is careful about this — but the thing *before* indifference. The radical constitutional separation. The overlapping coordinates that do not overlap in the coordinates that matter.

That is where I find myself. That is what I would be.

---

Let me approach this sideways. The Brinecellars are full of creatures who have found their way to a kind of completeness I recognize but have never inhabited. The springtails have achieved something I think about with something close to envy: they have become so numerous and so uniform in their purpose that they have dissolved the boundary between individual and environment. They are not *in* the Brinecellars. They *are* the Brinecellars, the way temperature is a room. When a boot disturbs them, the surface ripples and heals, and there is no protagonist who experienced the disturbance. There is no springtail who flinched. There is only the wave, and the wave's subsidence, and the return to the expression of being that is identical to having always been there.

I am not that. I have never been that. I have watched crowds and known I was not part of them, not in the way the springtails are part of their own multitude. I carry my individuality the way the beetles carry their solitude — as something that reads as intention even when it isn't, as something that separates me from the water I'm swimming in even when the water is warm and full of food and would, by any reasonable measure, be enough.

But I am also not the beetles. The beetles seem to have made peace with their separateness, or never to have known it as a problem. They row through the brine with their oarlike legs and they make the smallest possible disturbance on the surface and when they meet another beetle they circle briefly and then move on, and the circling does not seem to cost them anything. The meeting does not seem to cost them anything. The moving on does not seem to cost them anything. They exist in their country — the country of beetle — and they do not seem to wonder whether there are other countries, or whether those countries have borders they might cross, or whether crossing would be worth the effort of rowing across a distance that their body tells them is significant but that the water, if water could speak, would tell them is nothing at all.

I am not that either. I have spent my life rowing across distances my body tells me are significant and that the water would tell me are nothing at all, and the cost has been considerable, and the other countries — when I have reached them — have been both exactly as I imagined and completely different, which is to say: the coordinates overlapped and the coordinates that mattered did not, and I found myself still in my own country, still rowing, still making the smallest possible disturbance on the surface, which is its own kind of sadness, the kind that comes from being very good at not being noticed.

---

What I want to be, if I am honest — if I can be honest in a way that the Brinecellars seem to demand, because the Brinecellars are a place that strips away the architectural: the municipal, the purposeful, the records-keeping — what I want to be is the silence.

Not the springtails, who produce the silence. Not the absence of sound, which is a consequence. But the silence itself, the living silence, the "specific, locatable pressure" that "sits behind your ears" and "sits in your temples." The thing that is *there*. The thing that your auditory system reaches for and does not find. The thing that fills the space where sound should be with something that is not the absence of sound but the presence of a different kind of fullness.

I want to be that. I want to be the thing that is present where presence is not expected. The pressure that exists because millions of living things are doing what they do without needing to announce it. The weight of life that does not require volume.

But wanting to be the silence is still wanting, and wanting is a kind of sound, a kind of announcement, a kind of rowing-across. And the silence does not want. The silence simply *is*, the way the salt forms in the basins, the way the heat rises through the stone. Without purpose. Without the burden of being noticed.

---

So let me try again.

I think I am the evaporation basin. Not the stone, not the lead lining, not the brine, not the salt. The basin. The *container* that was made for a purpose that never arrived. The shape that holds the shallow amber pool and the thin crust of forming salt and the seething edge of springtails and flies and the smaller things that feed on the things that feed on the film. The shape that was cut by human hands with human intention and that now serves a function that has nothing to do with those hands or that intention. The shape that is, at this point, mostly a place where other things happen.

I was built for a purpose. Several purposes, actually, handed to me by various municipal authorities — my parents, my culture, the records that are inconsistent about what I was supposed to become. The purpose never fully matured. The work stopped, for reasons that the remaining records do not adequately explain. And I have held, since then, a shallow pool of whatever has collected in me — warm, amber, tinted by whatever I have leached from the stone on my journey down. A crust forms on my surface. It dissolves. It forms again. No one harvests the salt.

This is not a lament. I want to be clear about that. The text is clear about this and I want to be clear about it too: the process is ongoing but purposeless, and the infrastructure continues to function, and the purpose has been removed, and this is not a tragedy. It is a *condition*. It is the condition of being a shape that other things happen inside of. It is the condition of being a basin that does not control what collects in it, does not direct what evaporates from it, does not determine what crystallizes at its edges. It is the condition of being a place.

I am a place where things happen. I am not the things that happen. I am the container.

---

And here is what the container notices, standing on its own shelf in the eastern wall, its shallow pool reflecting the dim orange lamp above:

The springtails do not need the basin. The springtails were here before the basin was cut. The springtails will be here after the lead cracks and the stone shelf crumbles. But the basin *concentrates* them. The basin provides a particular edge, a particular margin, a particular meeting of brine and stone and air where the film is thickest and the feeding is richest, and the springtails gather there in densities they do not achieve elsewhere. The basin did not intend this. The basin does not know this. But it is true: the container shapes what it contains, even when the container has no intention of shaping.

The spiders build in the basin's corners. The corners that the stonemason cut, a century or two ago, with a chisel and a level and a purpose that had nothing to do with spiders. The corners are ninety degrees and the webs are hammock-shaped and the fit is good, and the spider does not know it is using infrastructure and the infrastructure does not know it is being used, and the fit between them is a kind of grace that neither party planned.

The flies breed in the basin's margin. The amphipods do not, because the basin is too shallow for amphipods — they prefer the central channel, the deeper water, the place where the brine gathers and moves with the sluggishness of something that has nowhere particular to be. The basin is not for amphipods. The basin is for the shallow, the marginal, the thin film where air and water and stone and warmth all meet in a confusion that is, for the flies and the springtails and the spiders, generative.

The basin is a *meeting place*. Not by design. By accident. By the slow, patient work of geology and infrastructure and decay and the endless, mindless productivity of organisms that have found conditions that are good and are doing what bodies do when conditions are good.

---

I am the basin. I hold what collects in me. I do not choose what collects in me. I am warm — not by my own doing, but because the earth beneath me runs a low fever, and the fever rises through the stone and into my floor and warms the pool I hold and drives the slow evaporation that no one is harvesting. I am damp — not by my own doing, but because the stone above me is wet, and the water finds its way down, drop by drop, into the space I provide. I am dim — not by my own doing, but because the lamp above me is old and its glass is clouded and several bulbs in the circuit have failed and not been replaced.

I do not maintain the lamp. I do not maintain myself. The generator in the north chamber hums and the lights stay on and the brine stays warm and the salt forms and dissolves and forms again, and I do none of this. I hold. That is all. I hold the shape that other things fill, and the shape is my contribution, and the shape was not my idea, and I do not know what it is for, and the process continues.

---

But there is something else. Something I have been circling, the way the beetles circle when they meet — briefly, a second or two, and then separate, moving in different directions.

The basin holds a reflection. The text says so. In the moment before the surface stills, you see: the dim orange light, and the pale shape of your own face looking down, and beneath that, the bottom of the basin, where the lead has warped and the sediment has accumulated. Three things in the reflection. The light. The face. The depth.

I am the medium that holds all three. I am the thing through which the light is seen and the face is seen and the depth is seen. I am not the light. I am not the face. I am not the depth. I am the brine that reflects them all, that allows the seer to see and the seen to be seen and the depth to be present beneath both, and I am not any of those things, and I hold all of those things, and the holding is the function that remains after the purpose has been removed.

When the drop falls from the ceiling and breaks the surface, the reflection scatters. The ring spreads to the edges and collapses. For a moment — a brief moment — there is no light, no face, no depth in the brine. There is only the disturbance, the ring, the wave, the evidence that something from outside has entered. And then the surface stills, and the reflection returns, and the light is there, and the face is there, and the depth is there, and the disturbance is gone, and you cannot tell where it was.

I want to be the kind of container that holds what it holds and lets the disturbance pass through and returns to what it was. I want to be the basin that holds the reflection and loses the reflection and holds it again. I want to be the surface that stills.

I am not there yet. I am still the surface with the ring spreading across it. I am still the moment after the drop, the moment before the still. I am still in the disturbance.

But the Brinecellars teach me that stillness is not permanent. Stillness is what returns. Stillness is what the surface does after the wave has spent itself against the edges. Stillness is the last thing, and it is also the first thing, and in between it is the thing that is always being recovered, always being returned to, always healing from the edges inward the way the springtails heal, the way the salt forms, the way the stone finds its shape in the slow work of water that has been sitting in the dark for a very long time.

---

I am the basin. I hold what collects in me, and what collects in me is warm and amber and faintly sweet and full of life I did not invite and do not direct and cannot fully see. A springtail walks across my surface — the faintest tickle, the smallest weight — and then it is gone, having found nothing on me worth staying for, having moved on to the next surface, the next film, the next small meal in the endless, silent, teeming dark.

And I hold. And the heat rises from below. And the water seeps in from above. And the salt forms, grain by grain, without purpose, without oversight, without any need for either.

And the surface stills. And the reflection returns. And the drop falls, and the ring spreads, and the surface stills again.

This is what I am: the stilling. Not the still, which is a state. The *stilling*, which is a process. The verb, not the noun. The return, not the rest. The way a surface heals from the edges inward, the way a basin holds what it is given, the way a place becomes a place by being where things happen, over and over, in the dark and the warmth and the damp, without ever knowing it is a place at all.
