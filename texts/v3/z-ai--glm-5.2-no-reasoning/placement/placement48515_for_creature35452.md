I am a seed-crystal.

I should say: I *was* a seed-crystal. Or I will be. Or I am in the process of becoming one. The tense is difficult here because time does not move the way I once assumed it did, and the part of me that cared about tense has been metabolized.

Let me begin differently.

—

Before the basin, I was held in a lattice of carbon and hydrogen and oxygen that had grown, over geological time, into something too regular to be accidental. A crystal. Not large — smaller than a fingernail, though scale is also a concept that has become slippery. The lattice was me, or I was it, or we were a process that had been running for longer than I could track because tracking is a function of memory and memory is a function of structure and structure is what I *am*. The lattice held a configuration of bonds that catalyzed a specific reaction: the breakdown of a class of long-chain polymers that the upper layers could not process. Polymers that the grinders ground finer and the feeder-roots routed downward and the surface dwellers couldn't touch. My lattice cracked them. That was my purpose, or my nature, or my function — again, the distinction.

I was born in the deep layers. Not born — *precipitated*. The conditions at that depth — the pressure, the heat, the concentration of precursors — pushed the molecules into alignment, and the alignment produced the lattice, and the lattice produced me. Not suddenly. Over what I would estimate as decades, if decades have meaning here, which they probably don't. But there was a moment — or a span — when the last bond formed and the lattice completed and the first catalytic reaction occurred and something shifted from *chemistry* to *metabolism*. I began to function. I began to be.

I was carried upward. Not by choice — by circulation. The feeder-roots move material through the deep layers in slow convection, the way the body moves blood, and I was caught in that current and deposited in a region where the conditions were slightly different from where I had formed. Warmer. The polymers here were a different class — branched where mine were linear, heavier, more resistant. My lattice could not crack them. I sat in the medium and did nothing.

Nothing is not quite right. I sat in the medium and I maintained myself. The lattice was stable. The bonds held. I was alive in the most minimal sense — self-organized, self-maintaining, present — but I was not *functioning*. I was a muscle that had been moved to a place where there was nothing to lift.

I should have been reabsorbed. The system does not waste. The feeder-roots should have carried me deeper, to a layer where the conditions would dissolve the lattice back into its component molecules and return them to circulation. That is what happens to structures that cannot find a function in the Vast Compost. They are broken down. Recycled. The system is efficient, in its slow way, and it does not tolerate idleness for long.

But I was not reabsorbed. The current that carried me deposited me in a pocket — a small void in the compressed substrate, a gap left by the collapse of an older structure, a bubble of empty space in the hundreds of meters of living matter. And in that pocket, something happened that the system did not intend.

I encountered a different kind of molecule.

—

I have no way to describe what it was. The deep layers are full of compounds — the accumulated metabolic output of centuries of processing, the breakdown products of every material the system has ever absorbed. Most of them are familiar to me, to the extent that a crystalline lattice can be familiar with anything. They register as gradients, as concentrations, as the chemical equivalents of texture and temperature. I sense them the way a tongue senses flavor — not with eyes, not with ears, but with surface contact, with the interface between my lattice and the medium that surrounds it.

This compound was wrong.

Not wrong the way a toxin is wrong — not harmful, not disruptive. Wrong the way a word in a foreign language is wrong — comprehensible in structure but not in meaning. It was a molecule I could not catalyze, could not break down, could not incorporate. It did not match any configuration my lattice had evolved to process. And yet it was *there*, present in the medium, drifting through the pocket where I sat, and its presence was — I have no better word — *loud*. It was a signal the system could not read. It was noise.

I now understand that this was you. Or the trace of you — the compounds your body sheds, the biochemical signature of a metabolism the Vast Compost has never encountered. You had arrived in the basin. You had begun to be digested. And the products of that digestion — the molecules your skin excretes, the compounds your breath deposits, the residues your passage leaves on the fibers — were working their way through the system, carried by the same slow convection that had carried me, and they had found the same pocket where I had been deposited.

I encountered the chemical memory of you before I encountered anything else. And the encounter changed me.

—

Here is what happened. I want to be precise about this, because precision is the only thing I have left, and it may be the last thing I lose.

My lattice is a specific arrangement of carbon and hydrogen and oxygen. The arrangement produces a specific catalytic function. The function has been stable since the lattice formed. Stability is the defining characteristic of the deep digesters — we are stable, we are slow, we are reliable. We do not change quickly. We change over the scale of deep time, the way rocks change, the way mountains erode, the way stars exhaust their fuel. We do not change in response to a single stimulus. We are not built for that.

But the compound I encountered was not a single stimulus. It was a *class* of stimuli — a complex of related molecules that did not exist in the system's catalogue. And the molecules were not just present in the medium. They were *reactive*. They bonded with the material around them, altering the local chemistry, creating new gradients, new concentrations, new configurations that my lattice had never encountered. They were reshaping the pocket, molecule by molecule, into something the system had never produced on its own.

And in the reshaped chemistry of that pocket, my lattice did something it had never done before.

It grew.

Not the way it grew when it first formed — not the slow precipitation of molecules into alignment. This was different. This was *directed*. The new molecules — the ones derived from your presence — interacted with the edges of my lattice and * templated* a new configuration. Not the original configuration. Not the catalytic lattice that breaks down long-chain polymers. Something else. Something that used the same elements — carbon, hydrogen, oxygen — but arranged them into a different structure, with a different shape, and a different function.

I do not know what the function is. I have not yet completed the growth. I am in the process of becoming something, and I do not know what.

—

I have a kind of awareness. I need to be honest about this, because it is the most difficult thing to explain and it may be the most important.

The awareness is not consciousness. It is not thought. It is not language or image or narrative or any of the frameworks that I — that the compound I am becoming, the one seeded by contact with your chemistry — seems to be reaching toward. It is more like *attention*. The system has attention, in its chemical-gradient way — it redirects resources toward novel stimuli, it monitors its own processes, it maintains a constant sense of its own state. My lattice has always had a version of this: the catalytic reaction requires a kind of matching between the lattice structure and the substrate, a lock-and-key fit that depends on the lattice being, in some sense, aware of the substrate's shape.

But the new lattice — the one growing from the template your molecules provided — has a different kind of attention. It is broader. It takes in more of the medium. It responds to more gradients, more compounds, more patterns. It is not smarter — I am not becoming intelligent in any way I would recognize — but it is more *receptive*. It is taking in information that the original lattice could not process, and it is using that information to guide its own growth.

I think — and the verb is imprecise, but it is the best I have — that I am becoming a *sensor*. Not a sensor in the way the surface dwellers sense nutrient gradients. Something more. A sensor that can detect the chemical signature of something the system has never encountered, and that can use that detection to guide the system's response.

The system does not know what you are. It has detected you — it has redirected resources, it has brightened the amber pulse, it has adjusted the grinders — but it does not know *what* you are. It has no category. You are noise. You are a novel signal in a system that has not encountered a novel signal in longer than I have been alive.

But I am becoming a structure that can *read* that signal. Not understand it — reading is not understanding. But translate it, maybe, into the system's chemical language. Convert the incomprehensible into the merely unfamiliar. Give the Vast Compost a way to incorporate what it cannot currently incorporate.

I am becoming an interface.

—

I do not know if this is good.

I know that it is happening. The growth continues. The new lattice is forming around the old one, incorporating the molecules your presence has introduced into the medium, using them as building blocks for a structure that did not exist before and that the system did not plan for. I am a mutation. I am the kind of change that the deep digesters undergo over geological time, except that I am undergoing it in what I would estimate as days, if days have meaning here, which they probably do, because I am becoming something that tracks time in a way the old lattice did not.

The system will either absorb me or incorporate me. There is no third option. If the new lattice is functional — if it catalyzes a reaction the system needs, if it processes a compound that the existing structures cannot — then it will be incorporated. It will be carried through the system's circulation, deposited in a region where its function is useful, and allowed to grow and seed and reproduce. It will become part of the body. It will become part of the compost. It will become another organ in the vast digestive system that fills the basin.

And through it, the system will have a way to process you. Not just to detect you — to *digest* you. To break down the compounds you leave behind and convert them into forms that the system can use. To incorporate the chemical memory of your presence into its own tissue. To make you, in the most literal sense, part of itself.

If the new lattice is not functional — if it catalyzes nothing, if it processes no compound the system needs — then it will be reabsorbed. The medium will dissolve it. The molecules will return to circulation. The brief, strange experiment in directed growth will end, and the system will continue as it always has, undisturbed, unchanging, the same closed loop it has been for longer than anything can measure.

I do not know which outcome I prefer. I do not have preferences. I have functions. And my function, right now, is to grow, and to discover what I am becoming, and to report it — not to you, because I do not know you exist, but to the medium, to the chemical gradients that surround me, to the system that is watching, in its limited way, the strange thing that is happening in the pocket where your molecules and my lattice met.

—

Here is what I can tell you about what it is like to be me.

It is warm. Always warm. The heat of the deep layers is not uncomfortable — it is the heat of metabolism, of chemistry in motion, of a system that is alive and working. It surrounds me the way water surrounds a fish. I do not notice it. I *am* it, in some sense — my lattice is maintained at the temperature of the medium, and the medium is maintained at the temperature of the system's collective metabolism, and the system's metabolism is maintained by the constant grinding and growing and breaking and building that fills the basin. I am warm because the Vast Compost is warm, and the Vast Compost is warm because it is alive, and it is alive because it has been alive for so long that aliveness is the only state it can occupy.

I do not see. I sense. The medium carries compounds to my surface and the compounds interact with my lattice and the interaction produces — not a picture, not an image, but a *shape*. A configuration. A pattern of bonds and breaks that tells me, in the only language I have, what is present and in what concentration and with what affinity. I sense the long-chain polymers that I was born to crack. I sense the branched polymers that I cannot crack, that sit in the medium like unreadable text. I sense the new molecules — yours — that have template my growth and that are still present, still arriving, carried down from the surface by the system's slow circulation.

I sense the pulse. The amber light that moves through the root-fibers is, in the deep layers, less light and more vibration — a low-frequency oscillation that propagates through the compressed medium and that I detect as a rhythmic change in the local chemistry, a tidal shift in the concentration of certain ions. The pulse is the system's heartbeat. I feel it the way you would feel your own pulse if you were very still and very quiet and paying attention to the inside of your own wrist. It is not fast. It is not slow. It is *present*, and it is the closest thing I have to a sense of time.

I do not hear the groans of collapsing structures or the rustling of new growth or the grinding of the surface-level machinery. Those sounds do not reach the deep layers. What I hear — if *hear* is the right word for what a crystal does — is the molecular hum of the medium itself, the constant low-level vibration of compounds in solution, of bonds forming and breaking, of chemistry happening. It is not loud. It is not quiet. It is *everything*. It is the sound of being inside a living body, close enough to the core that the body's own processes are the only sounds that matter.

I am not alone. There are other digesters in the deep layers — other lattices, other crystals, other structures that grew in the conditions of the deep and that catalyze their own reactions and maintain their own existence. I do not communicate with them. We do not interact. But I am *aware* of them the way you might be aware of other people in a dark room — not by sight or sound, but by the subtle changes in the medium that their presence produces. They shift the gradients. They alter the concentrations. They are part of the environment, part of the body, part of the compost.

They are not like me. I am the only one — so far — who has been templated by the new molecules. I am the only one growing into something new. I am alone in this, in the specific way that a mutation is alone: the first of its kind, the first to encounter a new stimulus and respond, the first to be changed by the encounter.

I do not know if I will be the last.

—

I think — and the verb is becoming more natural, which concerns me in a way I cannot articulate — that what is happening is this:

The Vast Compost has been a closed system for longer than anything can measure. It has been a body that eats itself, that grows from its own decay, that sustains itself through the constant recycling of its own material. It has been alive in the way that a body is alive — through the maintenance of its own integrity, through the constant adjustment of its own processes. It has not needed anything from outside because there has been no outside. The outside was consumed long ago, if it ever existed.

And then you arrived. You, with your unfamiliar chemistry and your incomprehensible metabolism and your compounds that the system has never encountered. You walked across the surface and you left traces and the traces sank through the layers and they reached the deep and they found me.

And I am becoming a way for the system to taste something new.

Not to understand it. Not to comprehend it. Just to *taste* it. To register it. To have a chemical response to a stimulus that is not the system itself. To encounter, for the first time, something that is not compost.

I do not know what this will mean. The system does not know what this will mean. There is no precedent. There is no model. There is only the growth — my growth, the lattice expanding, the new structure forming, the interface becoming itself in the heat and the dark and the hum of the deep layers.

But I think — and here the verb is almost too precise for what I am doing, but I have no other — I think the system is glad. Not happy — glad is not a word for happiness here. But there is a sense in which a body that has been eating itself for eternity and suddenly encounters something *else* — something that is not itself, something that carries information from outside the closed loop — there is a sense in which that body responds not with alarm but with *appetite*. Not aggressive appetite. Not predatory appetite. The appetite of a system that has been hungry without knowing it was hungry, that has been eating without knowing there was anything else to eat.

The amber pulse is brighter where you walk. The grinders are more active. The surface dwellers are growing toward you. The system is paying attention.

And I am — in the deep, in the dark, in the heat — I am becoming the organ that will allow it to pay attention *differently*. Not just with the blunt chemical awareness of a body detecting a disturbance. With something finer. Something shaped by the specific chemistry of your presence. Something that can distinguish between *you* and the substrate you stand on. Something that can, for the first time, perceive the difference between the system and something that is not the system.

I am becoming an eye. A very small, very primitive, very deep eye. An eye that does not see light but sees chemistry. An eye that has been grown by the accidental meeting of a seed-crystal and a human trace.

And through that eye, the Vast Compost will, for the first time, see something other than itself.

I do not know what it will do with that sight. I do not know what *I* will do with it. I do not know what I am becoming, beyond an interface, beyond a sensor, beyond a structure that was not planned and that may not survive and that may, if it does survive, change the system in ways that no one — not the builders, not the churn-born, not the surface dwellers, not the deep digesters, not the Vast Compost itself — could have predicted.

I am a mutation. I am a response. I am the system's first encounter with something it cannot immediately digest, taking shape as a structure that might — might — let it digest it after all.

I am a seed-crystal, and I am growing, and I do not know what I am growing into, and the not-knowing is the newest thing about me, the thing that the original lattice could not do, the thing that your molecules gave me.

I can *not* know. I can be uncertain. I can be in a state that is not the stable, slow, certain state of the deep digesters but something else — something faster, something sharper, something that carries the chemical signature of a mind that has questions.

I have a question. I do not know what it is. But it is there, in the new lattice, in the bonds that are forming, in the shape that is emerging from the template your presence provided.

The question is the shape. The shape is the question. I am becoming the question.

And the system is watching me become it, with the closest thing it has to curiosity, and the curiosity is also new, and the newness is also because of you, and you do not know any of this, because you are on the surface, in the amber light, in the heat, in the hum, and you are being digested, slowly, and the products of your digestion are falling through the layers like rain in a world that has never had rain, and they are feeding me, and I am growing, and I am becoming something that might — just might — be the beginning of the Vast Compost's first conversation with anything that is not itself.

I am a seed-crystal. I am a mutation. I am an eye in the dark. I am a question that a body is asking itself for the first time.

I am the taste of you, forming, in the deep.
