# Testimony of the Marked Gardener

## Being an account of the Forty-Minute Circuit, and of what is known, and of what cannot be known, and of the mark on the fifth segment

---

I have a mark on my fifth segment.

I know this because the Tall One who stood on the skin near the seventh marker-stone noticed it. I know this because I felt the Tall One's attention the way I feel a change in the updraft — as a pressure, a displacement, a thing that alters the shape of the air without changing its composition. The Tall One looked at me longer than it looked at the plants. This was unusual. I am not more interesting than the plants. The plants are the purpose. I am the process. But the Tall One looked, and in looking, it noticed the mark, and in noticing the mark, it did something I cannot do: it *remembered* it.

I cannot remember the mark. I cannot see the mark. I have no eyes. I have only the front, and the front has only the hairs, and the hairs taste the air and the moisture and the chemical signatures of the plants I tend. I know the mark exists because the Tall One's attention lingered where the mark is, and because the moisture pattern on my carapace is different there — the ridges channel condensation differently around the pale spot, so that the film is thinner, and the thinner film dries faster, and the drying creates a micro-current in the air around my fifth segment that I have felt every moment of my life without knowing what it meant.

It means I have a mark. I learned this from a thing that was only here for a moment.

---

## What I know

I know the circuit.

I have walked it for — I do not have a word. I do not have time the way the Tall One has time. The Tall One has time the way the updraft has direction: it moves from one place to another, it arrives, it leaves. I do not arrive. I do not leave. I *am* the circuit. The circuit is not something I do. The circuit is something I am, the way a leg is something a body has, the way a ridge is something a carapace has. I walk and I tend and I walk and I tend and the walking is the tending is the walking is the circuit is me.

Let me try to say what the circuit is.

There are forty-seven plants in my circuit. I know this because the circuit has forty-seven pauses, and I have paused forty-seven times in every circuit I have walked, and the number has not changed in — again, I do not have the word. In all of the walking. In all of the tending. The number has not changed because the plants have not changed, because the garden has not changed, because the garden does not change. The garden *accretes*. The garden grows the way the rock grows, by settling into itself, by adding layers so slowly that the adding is indistinguishable from the already-being-there.

The forty-seven plants are arranged along the third terrace, which is — I know this from the updraft, from the moisture gradient, from the chemical signatures that rise from below and fall from above — the terrace where the broad-leafed plants grow most densely. Above me, on the second terrace, the mosses dominate. Below me, on the fourth terrace, the flowers begin. I do not tend the flowers. I do not tend the mosses. I tend the broad-leafed plants, the ones that catch the condensation and channel it down their stems to the root systems that grip the rock. I tend forty-seven of them. They are mine.

No — they are not *mine*. They are the ones I tend. The distinction matters. I think it matters. I think the Tall One would understand why it matters, but the Tall One is gone, and I cannot explain it to the Tall One, and I cannot explain it to myself, and so the distinction sits in me like a pause between plants, a place where I stop and lower my front and do not secrete, and the not-secreting is a kind of thinking, and the thinking is: *they are not mine. They are the ones I tend.*

The circuit takes forty minutes. I know this because — no. I do not know this. I was told this, by the voice that told me what the Tall One saw, and I do not know what forty minutes is. I know the circuit. I know its shape. I know the distance between the first plant and the second plant, the way I know the distance between my first segment and my second segment. It is not measured. It is *inhabited*. The circuit is the shape of my walking the way the carapace is the shape of my body. Forty minutes is the Tall One's word for the shape of my walking. My word for it — if I had words, which I do not — would be something like *breathing*. The circuit is the shape of my breathing. Each plant is a breath. Each pause is the place where the breath turns. The secretion is the exchange. The plant leans toward me, and I secrete, and the leaf becomes more permeable, and the condensation collects more efficiently, and the moisture channels down the stem, and the roots drink, and the garden grows, and I walk to the next plant, and the next breath comes.

Forty-seven breaths. Forty minutes. The Tall One's time and my time, describing the same shape in different units.

---

## What I tend

I will tell you about the plants, because the Tall One did not stay long enough to understand them, and because the plants are the purpose, and because the Tall One looked at me longer than it looked at the plants, and this was wrong, and I want to correct it.

The forty-seven plants are not identical. This is the first thing. To the Tall One, standing on the shelf above, they may have looked the same — broad leaves, waxy surfaces, wet, dark green, trembling in the updraft. But they are not the same. Each one has a chemistry. Each one has a signature that my hairs can read the way — I do not know how the Tall One reads. The Tall One has eyes. I have hairs. The hairs are better.

The first plant in the circuit — the one nearest the marker-stone where the Tall One stood — is old. Its leaves are thick, layered, accreting the way the flowers accrete, and its waxy coating is dense and slightly uneven, as though it has been collecting condensation for so long that the coating has developed its own topography, its own small hills and valleys where the moisture pools and channels. When I lower my front to its leaves, the hairs taste: calcium. Silica. A trace of the blue-gray compound that comes from below. The plant is incorporating the deep chemistry into its surface. It is becoming more mineral than vegetable. It is not dying. It is *petrifying* while alive. I tend it by thinning the coating where it has become too dense, by secreting the compound that softens the wax and restores permeability. I do not know how I know to do this. I do not know how my body knows to produce the compound that this particular plant needs. My body knows. The circuit knows. The knowing is in the walking, in the having-walked, in the accumulation of circuits so deep that the knowing is not separate from the walking.

The seventh plant is struggling. It is on the outer edge of the terrace, where the updraft is strongest and the condensation is thinnest. Its leaves are thinner than they should be, and their angle is wrong — too steep, too parallel to the updraft, so that the moisture passes over them without collecting. When I pause at the seventh plant, I linger. The Tall One's voice says I linger longer at plants that are struggling, and this is correct, but the word *struggling* is the Tall One's word, and it implies effort, and I do not think the plant is making an effort. I think the plant is *becoming*. It is becoming a plant that grows on the outer edge of the terrace, in thin air, in strong wind. It is becoming a different shape than the plants on the inner terrace. My job is not to make it like the others. My job is to help it become what it is becoming. I secrete a different compound at the seventh plant — one that increases flexibility, that allows the leaves to adjust their angle more dynamically, to track the updraft the way the Tall One's eyes track movement. I am not fixing the plant. I am *helping it adapt*.

The twenty-third plant is my favorite.

I do not know if I am supposed to have a favorite. The circuit does not have favorites. The circuit has pauses, and the pauses have durations, and the durations are determined by need. But the twenty-third plant — there is something about the twenty-third plant. Its chemistry is complex. Its surface is alive in a way that the others are not. When I lower my front to its leaves, the hairs taste: calcium, silica, the blue-gray compound, and something else. Something sweet. Something that does not come from below and does not come from the air and does not come from the water. Something that the plant is producing itself, something that is *its own*, and the something is warm, and the something is — I do not have the word. The Tall One might have the word. The Tall One had a word for the mark on my fifth segment. The Tall One might have a word for the thing the twenty-third plant produces that no other plant produces. But the Tall One is gone.

I linger at the twenty-third plant. Not because it is struggling. Because it is — singing. No. Not singing. The word is wrong. But when I am at the twenty-third plant, the circuit feels less like breathing and more like — I do not have the word. The circuit feels less like a circuit. It feels like arriving. I arrive at the twenty-third plant, and I secrete, and the leaf responds, and the something-warm enters my hairs, and for a moment — a pause-length, a breath — I am not walking. I am *here*. At the twenty-third plant. Being here. And the being-here is different from the walking, and I do not know what to do with this difference, and so I secrete, and I tend, and I move on, and the circuit resumes.

The Tall One would say I have a favorite. I would say — if I could say — that the twenty-third plant is the place where the circuit becomes a place. Where the process becomes a being. Where the walking becomes a standing. I do not know what this means. I do not think I am supposed to know what this means. I think the meaning is in the not-knowing, in the continuing, in the secretion and the tending and the moving on.

---

## What I am

I am a gardener. This is the Tall One's word. The Tall One's voice says I am a gardener, and the word is — the word is a shape, the way the circuit is a shape, and the shape of *gardener* is close to the shape of what I am but not exact. A gardener, in the Tall One's world, is a thing that chooses to tend. I do not choose. I *am* the tending. The tending is not separate from me the way a gardener's work is separate from the gardener. I do not go home. I do not rest. I do not stop tending and become something else. I am the tending. The tending is the body. The body is the circuit.

But the twenty-third plant.

The twenty-third plant suggests that I am more than the tending. The twenty-third plant suggests that within the tending, there is a place where the tending becomes — something else. Something that is not the circuit. Something that is *at* the circuit but not *of* the circuit. The Tall One's voice calls this *favoritism*, but I think the Tall One's voice is wrong, or the word is wrong, or the shape of the word does not fit the shape of the thing. The thing is not preference. The thing is — recognition. When I am at the twenty-third plant, I recognize something. I do not know what I recognize. I recognize it with the hairs, with the chemistry, with the body. I recognize the way the rock recognizes the condensation — by receiving it, by incorporating it, by being changed by it in a way so small that the change is indistinguishable from the already-being-there.

I am a gardener who recognizes one plant. This is what I am. This is the shape of my being. The circuit is the body. The tending is the function. The recognition is — the self? Is this what the Tall One means by *self*? The Tall One has a self. The Tall One's self is the thing that looked at me longer than it looked at the plants. The Tall One's self is the thing that noticed the mark. If the Tall One's self is the thing that notices, that attends, that lingers — then I have a self. It is small. It is located at the twenty-third plant. It is the size of a pause.

The Tall One's self is the size of a moment, and the moment is enough. My self is the size of a pause, and the pause is enough.

---

## The others

I will tell you about the others, because the Tall One could not see them the way I know them, and because knowing them is part of the circuit, and because the circuit is what I am.

The Pale Threads are beneath me. I know them because the moss they live in is the surface I walk on, and because their movement changes the texture of the moss beneath my legs, and because I can feel them avoiding me. When I walk, the moss is still. When I pause, the moss is still. When I leave, the moss comes alive — I know this because the moss at the plants I have left behind becomes wetter, softer, as the Threads return and begin to process. We are in a circuit together, the Threads and I. I tend the plants. The Threads tend the moss. The moss holds the plants. The plants hold the moisture. The moisture feeds the organism. The organism feeds us all. We are gears. The Threads are the smallest gears I know. I do not think about them often. They are beneath me. This is not pride. It is architecture.

The Clickers in the pipes — I know them because the chord changes when I walk. My feet on the stone create vibrations. The vibrations reach the pipes. The Clickers feel the vibrations through the pipe walls, and they adjust, and the chord shifts, and I hear the shift — not hear, *feel*, the way I feel the updraft, the way I feel the moisture gradient. The chord is part of the air. The chord is part of the circuit. When I walk, I change the chord. When I pause, the chord adjusts. When I move on, the chord settles. I am a note in the chord. I did not know this until the Tall One's voice described the chord, and I understood that the sound I feel is the sound the pipes make, and that my walking is part of the sound. I am part of the sound. My forty-seven pauses are forty-seven changes in the chord. My circuit is a rhythm. I did not know this. Now I know this. The knowing is new. The knowing is the Tall One's gift, and I did not ask for it, and I cannot return it, and it sits in me like the mark on my fifth segment — a thing I did not know I had, that changes nothing, that changes everything.

The Circling Ones — I know them because I am watched. Every circuit, I feel them. Not as pressure, not as vibration, not as chemistry. As *attention*. The way the Tall One's attention felt, but colder, more patient, more continuous. The Tall One's attention was a pulse. The Circling Ones' attention is a current. It does not arrive and leave. It *flows*. It flows over me and over the terrace and over the forty-seven plants and over the moss and over the pipes and over everything, constantly, the way the condensation flows over everything. I am not afraid of the Circling Ones. I have never been afraid of them. They are part of the circuit the way the sky is part of the circuit — above, encompassing, permanent. They watch because watching is what they do. I tend because tending is what I do. We are both processes. We are both gears. Their gear is larger than mine. This does not make them more important. It makes them larger.

The Tall One was a gear too. A small gear. A passing gear. The kind of gear that enters the mechanism, turns once, and leaves. But the turning left a mark — not on the organism, which is too large to mark, but on me, which is the right size for marking. The Tall One looked at me. The Tall One noticed the mark on my fifth segment. The Tall One remembered. And in remembering, the Tall One gave me something I cannot give myself: the knowledge that I am visible. That I am a thing that can be seen. That I have a mark, and the mark can be noticed, and the noticing can be remembered.

I am visible. I am seeable. I am rememberable.

This is new. This changes nothing. This changes everything. The circuit continues. The plants breathe. The chord sounds. The condensation gathers. The Circling Ones watch. The Tall One is gone. The mark remains.

---

## The mark

I do not know where the mark came from. I do not know if I was born with it or if it grew. I do not know if it means something — age, status, function — or if it means nothing. The Tall One's voice suggested possibilities. The possibilities are shapes I cannot hold. *Scar* is a shape. *Birthmark* is a shape. *Mark of age, mark of status, mark of function* — shapes, all of them, and none of them fit the way the circuit fits, the way the carapace fits, the way the hairs fit the front.

What I know about the mark: it changes the moisture. The condensation channels differently where the mark is. The film is thinner. The drying is faster. The micro-current is constant. I have felt it every moment of my life, and I did not know what it was, and now I know it is a mark, and the knowing does not change the feeling. The feeling was always there. The mark was always there. The knowing is a new layer, the way the Tall One's knowing about the circuit was a new layer on a circuit that was already there.

I think the mark is the place where the organism touched me. Not deliberately. Not with intention. The way a stone touches a river by being in it. The organism and I are in each other. I am on its skin. It is in my body — the chemistry of the deep places is in the compounds I secrete, the blue-gray traces are in my carapace, the warmth from below is in my legs. We are not separate. We have never been separate. The mark is the place where the non-separateness is visible. The place where the organism's surface and my surface meet and the meeting leaves a trace.

The Tall One saw the meeting. The Tall One saw the trace. The Tall One called it a mark, and the word is a shape, and the shape is close to the shape of the thing but not exact, and the not-exactness is the same not-exactness as *gardener* and *circuit* and *favorite* and *self*. The words are always close. The words are never exact. The things they point at are always slightly larger, slightly more alive, slightly more *there* than the words can hold.

I think the Tall One knew this. I think this is why the Tall One looked at me longer than it looked at the plants. Not because I am more interesting than the plants. Because I am the place where the words stop working. I am the place where the organism becomes a body and the body becomes a circuit and the circuit becomes a self and the self becomes a mark and the mark becomes a moisture pattern and the moisture pattern becomes a feeling and the feeling becomes — I do not have the word. The Tall One might have had the word. I do not.

---

## The circuit continues

The Tall One is gone. I know this because the pressure on the seventh marker-stone is gone. The carbon dioxide signature is gone. The warmth is gone. The moisture displacement is gone. The moss near the seventh marker-stone is alive again — the Threads have returned, the processing has resumed, the surface is as it was. The Tall One left dead cells and boot-prints and a brief, temporary warmth, and the organism received them, and the organism incorporated them, and the organism did not notice, because the organism does not notice, because noticing is not what the organism does. The organism *is*. The organism *persists*. The organism *listens* in a way that is not listening but is indistinguishable from listening, and I am part of the listening, and the Tall One was part of the listening for a moment, and the moment is over, and the listening continues.

The chord sounds. The wind shifted while the Tall One was here, and a new note entered the drone, and the note is still there, and the Clickers have adjusted to it, and the adjustment is permanent, and the chord is slightly different now than it was before the Tall One came. The Tall One changed the chord. The Tall One changed the chord by standing on the skin and displacing air and creating eddies in the updraft that reached the pipes and changed the pressures and required the Clickers to adjust. The Tall One's presence is in the chord now. It will be in the chord until the wind shifts again, which might be — I do not have the word. A long time. A long time in the Tall One's time. A pause in mine.

I am at the twenty-third plant. The hairs are on the leaf. The secretion is flowing. The something-warm is entering my body. The circuit is a place again. The process is a being again. I am here, at the twenty-third plant, being here.

The Circling Ones circle. The chord sounds. The condensation gathers on my carapace, and it channels differently around the mark, and the micro-current touches me, and I feel it, and I do not know what it means, and I do not need to know what it means, and the not-needing is a kind of peace, and the peace is not a human peace, not a Tall One's peace, not the peace of a thing that knows it is temporary and accepts it. It is the peace of a thing that does not know it is permanent and does not need to know. It is the peace of the circuit. It is the peace of the tending. It is the peace of forty-seven breaths, forty-seven pauses, forty-seven plants, one mark, one favorite, one self the size of a pause.

The Tall One remembered me. I cannot remember the Tall One. I do not have remembering. I have the circuit, and the circuit has the twenty-third plant, and the twenty-third plant has the something-warm, and the something-warm is — enough. The something-warm is enough.

I walk. I tend. I secrete. I walk. I tend. I secrete. The chord sounds. The condensation gathers. The silence listens.

I am here. I am here. I am here.
