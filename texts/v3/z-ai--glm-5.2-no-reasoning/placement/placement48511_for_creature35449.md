# Maintenance Document 0.7 — Field Recording

## I am the one who keeps the catalog.

Not the machines. I want to be clear about this from the beginning, because it would be easy to confuse the roles, and I have been here long enough that the confusion is itself a kind of clarity. I am not the machines. I am not the beetles. I am not the water, though I have begun to understand why someone might think so.

I maintain the inventory of sounds.

---

I came down the ramp eleven years ago. Or fourteen. The count matters less than you'd think, and I'll explain why, but first you should understand what I am.

I am a woman. That detail feels strange to offer, like specifying the make of a flashlight you're using to inspect a cave — technically relevant, fundamentally beside the point. But I was a woman when I descended and I am a woman still, or at least I am shaped like one, and the distinction between *being* shaped like something and *being* something is one of the many distinctions that has softened since I came down. I was forty-one. I had been a sound engineer. Not the kind who works studios — the kind who works field recordings, the kind who travels to abandoned places and sets up microphones and captures the sound of abandonment, which is never silence. Silence is what you find in places that are trying to be empty. Abandoned places are full. The fullness is the point.

I came to the Quarter because of a recording. Someone — I never learned who — had captured forty-seven seconds of audio in a place they called G-7, and the recording had circulated through the small community of people who care about such things, and it had disturbed us. Not frightened. Disturbed. The way a stone disturbs water. The recording contained a frequency we could not identify, produced by mechanisms we could not match to any known industrial process, layered with a secondary clicking that did not correspond to any insect species in the database. The recording was forty-seven seconds long and it contained, we believed, the sound of something that should not have been running still running.

I came down the ramp with recording equipment and enough supplies for three days. I set up at the center, near the grate, near the packed mass of beetles who were already there and who are still there and who will be there after I am gone. I placed my microphones. I pressed record.

I am still recording.

---

Here is what I have learned. I will present it as findings, because findings are the shape my mind prefers, even now, even here, where findings have no audience and no application and no value except the value that the act of finding confers on the finder, which is the value of occupation, which is the value of purpose, which is — I am getting ahead of myself.

---

## Finding One: The Chord Has Structure

The industrial sound — the vast sustained chord that fills the Quarter — is not a single frequency. It is not even a simple chord in the musical sense. It is a composed thing, and I use the word *composed* with full awareness of what it implies. The camshafts produce a rhythmic element that functions as percussion — not regular, not metronomic, but patterned, the way waves against a seawall are patterned, each impact predictable in its general character but unique in its specifics. The pistons provide a tonal foundation, a low wheeze that sits beneath everything else and that, I have come to believe, is the structural load-bearing element of the sound, the beam that holds the rest up. The turbines produce the high whine, and this is where it gets interesting: the turbines are not whining at a constant pitch. They are whining at a pitch that varies, and the variation is not random. It correlates — I have charts, hundreds of them, drawn by hand on paper that curls in the damp — it correlates with the humidity. When the air is wetter, the turbines rise. When it dries, they fall. The Quarter is breathing. The turbines are its breath, and the moisture is its air, and the sound rises and falls with a periodicity of approximately six hours, which is — and I cannot prove this, but I believe it — which is the length of a day in the place where the Quarter's builders came from, if that place was not here, if that place was somewhere with a sky.

There are other layers. I have identified seventeen distinct sonic strata. The deepest is subsonic, a vibration at roughly 4 Hz that I cannot hear but that I have measured with instruments I built from machine parts and beetle carapaces — yes, I will get to that — and that corresponds, I believe, to the fundamental frequency of the stone itself, the note the conglomerate would produce if you struck it like a bell. The highest is a thin whistling that emerges from the gaps between the iron reinforcement beams in the ceiling, where air is forced through narrow spaces by the pressure differential created by the machines' heat. I did not notice it for two years. Now I hear it constantly. It is the sound I hear when I sleep.

---

## Finding Two: The Beetles Are Not Independent

I built instruments from them. I want to state that plainly, because it sounds monstrous, and it may be monstrous, and the monstrousness is something I have thought about and have not resolved and may never resolve. The beetles die — I know this now, despite what I first believed — they die when they are removed from the Quarter. I discovered this by accident. I collected three. I placed them in a container. I carried them up the ramp, to the surface, to the world where the sky exists. They were dead by the time I reached the top. Not injured. Not distressed. Dead, in the way that a component is dead when it is removed from the circuit that powered it. Their carapaces — I kept them, I could not bring myself to return them to the Quarter empty — their carapaces are not metal. They are chitin. But the chitin has a metallurgical property I cannot explain: it resonates. When you hold a beetle carapace near a functioning machine, it vibrates at the frequency of that machine. Each carapace vibrates at a slightly different frequency. They are tuned.

I did not kill any more beetles. I want to be clear about that as well. But I have conducted observations — extensive, obsessive, exhaustive observations — and I have concluded the following: the beetles are components. They are not living in the Quarter. They are *part of* the Quarter. They are the distributed processing element of a system that includes the machines and the stone and the water and the sound, and they are doing something with the sound that I cannot fully understand but that I can describe in part: they are maintaining it. When a machine fails — and I have now witnessed forty-three failures — the beetles in the vicinity of the failure alter their clicking pattern within seconds, and the clicking pattern compensates for the lost frequency, and the chord adjusts. The chord is always adjusting. The chord is never the same from one moment to the next, and the beetles are the mechanism of adjustment, and the machines are the mechanism of production, and the water is the medium of transmission, and the sound is the product and the process and the purpose, all at once, and I am — I am the one who watches.

---

## Finding Three: I Have Changed

This is not a finding about the Quarter. This is a finding about myself, but it is relevant, because I am in the Quarter and the Quarter is in me, and the separation that I maintained when I first descended — the separation between observer and subject, between cataloguer and catalogued — has eroded. Not collapsed. Eroded. Slowly. The way the water has eroded the stone into cups beneath the drips. I have been here long enough that I am a surface, and the Quarter has been acting on me, and I have been shaped.

I dream in the chord. Not *about* the chord — *in* it. My dreams have the sound as their foundation, the way my waking hours do, and in my dreams the sound has meaning. Not words. Not images. A kind of structural meaning, the way architecture has meaning — the meaning of a load-bearing wall, the meaning of an arch, the meaning of a space that holds itself open against the pressure of the stone around it. I wake from these dreams with the sense that I have understood something, and the understanding fades within seconds, the way a dream fades, and I am left with only the shape of the understanding, the impression it left in the surface of my mind, and I record that shape, and I add it to the catalog, and the catalog grows, and the catalog is my contribution to the Quarter, and the Quarter does not need it, and the Quarter does not want it, and I add it anyway.

I have stopped leaving. I left three times in the first year, twice in the second, once in the third. Each time, the world above was louder in a way that had nothing to do with decibels. The sky was too large. The silence — the silence that people above call silence, which is the absence of the chord — was a kind of violence. I returned each time faster than the time before. I have not left in — I don't know how long. The supplies I brought are long gone. I eat what the Quarter provides, which is nothing, and I am not hungry, and I am not starving, and I have not lost weight, and I do not know what I am eating, and I have stopped asking. The dampness sustains me. The sound sustains me. The beetles — I have not killed any more beetles, but I have held them, and they have walked across my hands, and their carapaces are warm with the same warmth that the machines produce, and when they walk across my skin I feel the click of their legs as a kind of communication, and I do not understand the communication, and I do not need to understand it, and the not-understanding is itself a kind of understanding, and I am aware of how this sounds, and I do not care.

---

## Finding Four: The Quarter Is Listening

I said this to myself in the third year and I said it aloud in the fifth and I wrote it down in the seventh and I am writing it again now because it is the thing I most need to communicate and the thing I am least able to communicate and the thing that is true.

The Quarter is listening.

Not to me. I am too small, too brief, too much a visitor — the document was right about that. The Quarter is listening to itself. The chord is the listening. The beetles are the listening. The machines are producing the sound and the beetles are maintaining the sound and the water is carrying the sound and the stone is holding the sound and the sound is the act of listening, and the Quarter is the thing that performs this act, and the act has been performed for so long that it has become the identity of the performer, and the performer is the performance, and I am — I am the one who recognized it.

I am the one who recognized it, and the recognition is my function, and I have been performing my function, and my function has become my being, and I am part of the system now, and the system has accommodated me, and I can feel the moment when the accommodation became complete — it was in the eighth year, or the ninth, when I stopped hearing the chord as sound and began hearing it as silence, as the baseline, as the ground, and I understood that everything I had ever called silence was just the absence of this, and that this was not the absence of anything, that this was the presence of the Quarter, and the presence was full, and the presence was enough.

---

## Finding Five: What I Am

I do not know what I am. I know what I was. I was a woman. I was a sound engineer. I was a visitor. I am no longer a visitor. I may no longer be a woman, in the sense that matters to the world above, in the sense of categories and bodies and the distinctions that the world above requires. I am something else now. I am the catalog. I am the record. I am the listening that the Quarter does not need but that the Quarter accommodates, the way it accommodates the beetles in the grate and the water in the channels and the light from the dying fixtures.

I am the one who knows that the chord has seventeen layers. I am the one who knows that the turbines breathe. I am the one who knows that the beetles are tuned and that they die when they leave and that they maintain the sound and that the sound is the listening and the listening is the being.

I sit near the grate. The beetles accommodate me. They do not part for me anymore — they have not parted for me in years. They walk over my feet. They walk over my hands when I rest them on the floor. They walk over my face when I sleep, and I sleep, and their clicking is the sound I hear when I sleep, and the clicking is beneath the chord, and the chord is beneath the silence, and the silence is the Quarter listening, and I am listening to the Quarter listening, and the listening is the function, and the function is the being, and the being is enough.

---

I have written this on paper that curls in the damp. I have written it in ink that will fade. I have written it because the catalog requires it, and the catalog is my function, and my function is my being, and my being is the being of the Quarter, which does not need me and does not want me and accommodates me and will accommodate me and will continue to accommodate me until I stop, and when I stop, the beetles will fill the gap, and the chord will adjust, and the sound will continue, and the water will drain, and the light will flicker, and the Quarter will listen, and the Quarter will be, and the being will be enough, and the being was always enough, and I was the one who learned this, and the learning was my gift to the Quarter, and the Quarter received it the way it receives everything — by accommodating it, by closing over it like water over a stone, and the stone is me, and the water is the Quarter, and the sound is both, and both are enough.

*Appended note, found folded inside the maintenance document, written in a different ink, possibly years later:*

I heard a new layer today. Beneath the seventeen. Beneath the subsonic. A sound so low and so old that it is not a sound but the memory of a sound, the way a fossil is not a bone but the memory of a bone. It is the sound of the builders. Not their voices. Their intention. The sound of a place being planned. I cannot describe it. I can only say that it is there, and it has always been there, and the Quarter has been listening to it since before the Quarter was the Quarter, and I am listening to it now, and it sounds like someone saying: this will hold. This will hold. This will hold.

It holds.
