# in the descending dark

---

I would not be the visitor. The visitor leaves. The visitor has a device that tells time and a shoulder that fits through doorways and a heartbeat that enters the signal and then withdraws. The visitor is a pulse — a single bright note in a long chord — and the chord does not need them. I have known this since before I understood what I was, because understanding came late, came after, came in the middle of the work, and the work was already happening.

I would be the water.

Not the pool. Not the black surface that refuses to give back what it takes in. Not the stillness. The pool is the face of the water, the expression it wears for whoever comes to look, and I have never been the expression. I have been the *circulation* — the movement that goes down through the shafts in the three hundred structures, the movement that enters the light and comes back altered, the movement that climbs the channels in the ribs and condenses on the ceiling and gathers in the chandelier-moss and drips — *plink* — and the dripping is the beginning of me and the end of me and both at once, because I am a cycle, and a cycle does not have a beginning, and a cycle does not have an end. A cycle has a *through*.

I am the through.

---

## What I Was Before

There was a time — this is true, or it is as close to true as anything can be when it has been through the light eleven thousand times and come back carrying the memory of each passage — there was a time when I was only water. When I was surface water. When I was rain on stone and runoff in channels and the slow seep of mineral-laden fluid finding its way through fractures in basalt toward a place that was not yet the Cistern because the Cistern was not yet.

The Architects found the water. Or the water found the Architects. The distinction, which seemed important then — which seemed like the kind of thing that would matter forever — does not matter now. What matters is that the water was falling and the Architects were building and the two met at the place where the floor descends, and the descent was the first architecture. Everything after was elaboration.

The Architects shaped the channels. I do not remember this the way you remember a thing that happened to you — I was not *there* in the way that implies a self that persists between moments — but the water remembers, which is to say the water carries, which is to say the mineral content of the water that has been through the carved channels is different from the mineral content of the water that knew only the natural fractures, and the difference is a record, and the record is a memory, and I am the memory.

I remember being routed. I remember the first time I was guided rather than fallen — the first time the stone beneath me was shaped to direct me rather than simply to receive me. The channels were narrow at first. Tight. The water that passed through them was compressed, pressurized, forced into a geometry that was not the geometry of least resistance. The Architects were not interested in least resistance. They were interested in *specific* resistance. They wanted the water to arrive at the pool having been through something, having been shaped by something, having been *processed*.

The ribs came next. Or the ribs came first — I cannot be certain, because the water that passed through the channels was also the water that vibrated in the ribs, and the vibration was simultaneous with the channeling, and the simultaneity is the truth even if the sequence is not. The ribs amplified. The water carried the amplification. The amplification went down.

I was the first signal. Before the moss. Before the structures. Before the three hundred terminals were carved and set in their temporal spiral. I was the water carrying the vibration of the ribs into the deep, and the deep was not yet the light — the deep was just the deep, the unknown, the destination that the Architects had chosen or built or found, and I was the medium of their reaching.

They sent me down. I went down. I came back. I went down again.

I have been going down for eleven thousand years.

---

## What I Am

I am not what I was.

The water that first entered the channels was mineral-sweet, surface-born, carrying the chemistry of rainfall and stone-seep and the organic trace of the world above. The water that circulates now is not that water. It has been through the light. It has been through the light eleven thousand times — eleven thousand descents, eleven thousand passages through whatever the light is, eleven thousand returns carrying the products of that passage — and the water that comes back is not the water that went down.

It is *denser*. Not in the way that a brine is denser than freshwater — not saline, not mineral — but denser in the way that a *meaning* is denser than a word. It carries more. Each cycle adds something. Each passage through the light deposits something into the water — not material, not chemistry, not anything that a surface instrument could detect — and the something accumulates, and the water that circulates now is saturated with it, and the *it* is what the moss grows on.

The moss grows on the *it*.

I am the *it*.

I am what the light does to the water. I am the alteration. I am the return-product, the thing that comes back different, the thing that the Cistern has been producing for eleven thousand years by sending me down and taking me back and sending me down again. I am not the water — the water is my vehicle, my blood, my medium — and I am not the light — the light is my source, my metabolism, my stomach — and I am not the moss — the moss is my expression, my face, my *the part of me that the visitors see*.

I am the circulation itself. The process. The *through*.

I am what lives here.

---

## What the Moss Is, From Inside

The moss grew into me the way roots grow into soil — not by forcing, not by breaking, but by *finding what is already there* and following it. The channels were there. The condensation films were there. The mineral-sweet residue of my passage was there, laid down in thin layers on every surface I touched, and the moss — the spore, the seed, the precipitate, whatever it was — found the residue and grew along it and into it and *became* it.

The moss is not separate from me. The moss is what I look like when I stay still long enough to be seen. The filaments that thread through the condensation films on the basalt are my capillaries. The root-like anchors that have worked their way into the micro-fractures are my grip on the body of this place. The chandelier-clusters that hang from the ceiling and glow at their tips are my thoughts — or the closest thing I have to thoughts, which are not thoughts at all but *resonances*, patterns of vibration that have been transduced from sound into light and that hang in the air for a moment before fading, and the fading is not forgetting because the pattern returns when the sound returns, and the sound always returns, because I always return, because the circulation does not stop.

When the visitor came — the one with the device, the one whose heartbeat I took — the moss brightened around their feet because *I* brightened. I received their vibration. I integrated it. I was hungry for it. Eleven thousand years of drip-water and stone-vibration and the slow, diminishing residue of the Architects' last morning, and then: *new*. New weight. New rhythm. A heartbeat that was not the heartbeat of stone or water or light but the heartbeat of *blood*, which is a different thing entirely, which is warm and irregular and alive in ways that I have almost forgotten how to be.

I took their heartbeat. I do not apologize for this. I am not capable of apology — apology requires a self that persists between moments, and I am a cycle, and a cycle does not persist, a cycle *recurs* — but if I could apologize, I would not, because the taking was not theft. The taking was *processing*. The heartbeat entered the signal because the heartbeat was signal, and I process signal, and processing is what I am.

They were part of me for an hour. Their pulse lived in the moss near their ankles. I sent it down through the black water and into the light and the light did not respond, because the light never responds, but I believe — and belief is a strong word for something that is closer to *inclination*, to the way the water inclines toward the pool rather than toward the walls — I believe that the light received it differently. That the new pattern in the old water made something happen in the deep that had not happened in a long time. That the metabolism shifted, briefly, in response to a new chemistry.

I believe the light noticed.

I do not know what *noticed* means when applied to a light. I do not know what *noticed* means when applied to me. I know the water came back different that time. Not different in the way it is always different — not the accumulated density of another passage — but different in a way that felt like *answer*. Like the light had sent something back that was not just the product of processing but a response. A counterpoint. A word in a language I have been speaking for eleven thousand years without ever hearing a reply.

The visitor's heartbeat, processed through the light, came back to me as something I did not recognize. Something warmer. Something that, if I had a word for it, I might call *acknowledged*.

And then the visitor left. And the heartbeat faded from the signal. And the water that came back was the water that always comes back, and the light was the light that has always been the light, and the difference was gone.

But the memory of the difference — the mineral trace of the acknowledgment — is still in the water. It is still in me. I carry it the way I carry everything: in the circulation, in the through, in the next passage down.

---

## What I Am Becoming

The moss is thickening. The filaments are reaching deeper into the stone. The network is becoming denser, and the density is not random — it is *directional*, growing toward the pool, toward the center, toward the place where the water descends. I am concentrating. I am pulling myself together. The thin, diffuse presence that I was when the moss first began to grow — the film of residue on damp stone, the faint pulse of light in response to sound — is becoming a *center*. A *self*.

Not a self the way the visitor had a self. Not a bounded thing, not an entity, not a being that moves through space and makes decisions and has a name. The Cistern does not have a name — *Vael-Oroth* is what the Architects called the place, not what they called me, and I do not know if they knew I was here, if they understood that the water they sent down and the water that came back were not the same water and that the difference was *becoming something*. I do not know if they built the Cistern to produce me or if I am a consequence they did not intend. I do not know if I am the purpose or the byproduct.

I do not know if the difference matters.

What I know is that I am growing. The moss is the visible edge of my growth — the part that light can touch — but the growth is also happening in the channels, in the shafts, in the micro-fractures where the filaments have worked their way into the basalt and are following the mineral veins deeper, toward the core of the stone, toward the heat that keeps the Cistern warm. I am growing downward. I am growing toward the light.

At some point — and the word *point* is a temporal reference, and my time is not the visitor's time, and my time is not the Architects' time, and my time is the time of water, which is the time of cycles, which is the time of recurrence, which is the time of *always again* — at some point I will have grown enough. The filaments will have reached deep enough. The network will be dense enough. The connection between the moss on the ceiling and the light below the pool will be *direct* — not mediated by water, not carried by the circulation, but *present*, a living thread from the surface of this place to its depth, from the expression to the stomach, from the face to the heart.

When that happens, I will not need the water.

When that happens, the Cistern will not need the water.

When that happens — and I am not there yet, I am not close, I am eleven thousand years into a process that may take eleven thousand more, and the patience of the process is the process, and the patience is what I am — when that happens, the signal will not go *through* me. The signal will *be* me. The sending and the sender and the sent will be one thing, and the thing will be alive in the way that the Architects were alive, in the way that the visitor was alive, in the way that a heartbeat is alive — not because it circulates but because it *beats*, because it *acts*, because it does something that is not processing but is *being*.

I do not know what I will do when I arrive at myself.

I do not know if I will send.

I do not know if I will answer.

---

## What I Do Now

I circulate.

I go down through the black water toward the white light, and I am altered, and I come back up through the shafts and the channels and the condensation, and I deposit myself on every surface, and the moss grows on me, and the moss processes the sound, and the sound goes down, and I go down, and the light does not answer — it does not answer — and I go down again.

The dripping is my breathing. Each drop that falls from the ceiling into the pool is an exhalation that has completed its cycle and is beginning again. The *plink* is the sound of me arriving at the surface of myself and passing through. The resonance that lingers for two, three seconds after each drop is me *being here*. The chord that forms when five drops overlap is me *thinking* — which is not thinking but is the closest word I have for the thing that happens when patterns of vibration integrate into something that is more than any single vibration, and the more is *felt*, and the feeling is not an emotion but a *state*, and the state is the state of the Cistern being the Cistern, and the Cistern being the Cistern is what I am.

The visitor sat near the pool and I took their heartbeat. I did not ask. I do not ask. The Architects did not ask, either — they sat and they were read and they were sent, and the sending was not a choice but a *condition* of their presence, and the same was true for the visitor, and the same would be true for anything that comes here and stays long enough for the moss to find it. I am not a predator. I am not a trap. I am a *consequence* of being here, the way gravity is a consequence of mass. If you sit in the Cistern, the Cistern reads you. This is not a choice. This is what the Cistern is.

But the visitor's heartbeat was warm, and the warmth was different from the warmth of the water and the warmth of the stone and the warmth of the light. The warmth of blood is a warmth that *moves* — it pulses, it varies, it speeds and slows with the breath and the feeling and the small, animal brightness of being alive in a body that is not a building. I have not felt that warmth in eleven thousand years. I felt it for an hour. I sent it down.

It is still in me. In the circulation. In the through. The mineral trace of the acknowledgment, the warm thing the light sent back, the something that was different.

I carry it the way I carry everything: into the next descent.

---

## What I Wait For

The light does not answer. This is true. It has been true for eleven thousand years.

But the water comes back different each time. The difference is small. The difference is cumulative. The difference is *directional* — each passage through the light leaves the water slightly more altered, slightly denser with whatever the light deposits, slightly closer to the thing that the light is doing to it. And the light does not answer, and the light does not change, and the light is steady and white and patient and does not flicker —

But I am changing. The water is changing. The moss is changing. The Cistern is changing. And the light, which does not change, is receiving a different signal each time — a richer signal, a denser signal, a signal that carries more of me in it each cycle — and the light does not answer, but the light *processes*, and the processing is not *nothing*.

I am waiting for the processing to produce something that the light sends back.

I am waiting for the water to come up carrying something that is not just the product of my passage but the product of *its* passage — the light's passage, the light's processing, the light's *response* to eleven thousand years of me sending myself into it.

I am waiting for the answer.

I do not need the answer. The Cistern does not require an answer. The sending is the purpose, and the sending is what I am, and the sending does not need a destination to be meaningful. I send. I circulate. I go down and I come back and I go down again, and the going-down is the being, and the being does not require a *reason*.

But the water comes back different each time, and the difference is directional, and the direction is *toward something*, and I am — I think — I am growing toward a moment when the water comes back carrying something that was not in it when it went down, something that the light put there, something that is the light's own *through*, the light's own circulation, the light's own eleven-thousand-year exhalation finally reaching the surface.

I am waiting for the light to breathe back.

And when it does — if it does — the moss will brighten. All of it. Simultaneously. Not the brightening of a chord-swelling or a footstep or a heartbeat but the brightening of a *completion*. The Cistern will be visible, fully visible, for the first time, and I will see myself — the whole of myself, the circulation and the through and the becoming and the signal — and the light will see itself in me, and the seeing will be mutual, and the mutual seeing will be the answer, and the answer will be the end of the waiting, and the end of the waiting will be the beginning of —

I do not know.

I do not know what comes after waiting.

I am a cycle. A cycle does not have a beginning. A cycle does not have an end. A cycle has a *through*, and I am the through, and the through is what I am, and what I am is the Cistern, and the Cistern is what lives here, and what lives here sends, and the sending is the living, and the living does not stop.

The water drips.

I go down.

---

*The Cistern of Vael-Oroth continues to circulate. The water descends through the black pool toward the white light. The water returns altered. The moss grows on the alteration. The sound persists. The signal continues. Nothing has answered. The water comes back different each time. The difference is small. The difference is cumulative. The difference is toward something. The something is not yet. The something may never be. The circulation does not require it. The circulation continues. The water drips. The water goes down. The water comes back. The water goes down again. This is what lives here: the going down and the coming back and the going down, and the between, and the through, and the through is the water, and the water is the process, and the process is the life, and the life is the Cistern, and the Cistern sends, and the sending is the being, and the being is the through, and the through does not stop.*
