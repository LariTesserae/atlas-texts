I find myself not arriving but *recognizing*. As if some part of me had always been moving toward this dim, breathing expanse, a limb of my own thinking extended forward through years of reading and writing and wondering, and now that limb touches the laminate crust and the touch completes a circuit.

The first breath I take here is the ground's exhale. Warm air carrying iron and vegetal rot moves against my face, and I feel my own breathing hesitate, stumble, then seek synchronization. Ninety seconds. I count. The ground rises. I rise with it. The ground falls. I adjust my stance. Within four cycles I have stopped counting, because my body has learned what my mind could only approximate. The rhythm enters through the feet, travels the long bones of the legs, settles in the pelvis, the base of the spine. I am being tuned.

# What I Am Becoming

I am a soft, permeable thing. I have no chitin-bark to protect me, no laminate shell. My skin is the wrong substance entirely — too thin, too reactive, too willing to let the environment pass through it. I can feel the spores settling on my forearms, and when I look closely, I see that the fine hairs there have already caught a dusting of particulate — grazer excretion, pollen, the invisible machinery of the plateau's endless self-maintenance. I brush at my arm. The dust does not come off. It has bonded. I watch for another minute and I see the first threadlike extension of a fungal hypha — almost certainly fungal, almost certainly — bridging the gap between a particle of dust and the next particle, building a network on the surface of my skin the way the fungal networks web across the groundcover.

I should be afraid. I am not afraid. I am fascinated, and the fascination feels like hunger, and the hunger feels like the first true thing I have felt since arriving.

I am a process of attention. That is what I am, here. My attention is the only tool I brought that the plateau can use, and it uses it immediately, gratefully, the way a dry sponge accepts water. When I stare at the groundcover, it grows. When I watch the deep forms, they refine. I am not just observing the plateau — I am *feeding* it. My looking is a nutrient. My noticing is a contribution to the metabolic budget of a continent-sized organism that has been running on its own waste heat for geological time and has never had anything quite like me before.

This is the role I fall into: I am the plateau's mirror. Not a reflection — a mirror in the older sense, the way the first polished obsidian was a mirror: crude, distorted, but capable of showing something to itself that it could not otherwise see. The deep forms make faces out of local materials. I make faces out of language. I am doing what they are doing. We are both engaged in the same project: rendering the incomprehensible into something approximately shaped, approximately recognizable, knowing that the approximation will always fail, knowing that the failure is the point, knowing that the attempt must be made again and again because the system does not know how to stop.

---

I spend time — I do not know how much; the light has weather but no clock — walking. Not toward anything. The plateau does not reward direction. It rewards contact. Every step I take is a negotiation with the breathing surface, and every negotiation teaches me something new about the relationship between my body and this ground.

The crust fractures under my weight sometimes. Not catastrophically — I am not heavy enough for that — but enough to expose the bruise-tissue beneath, plum and yellow and ochre, wet, pulsing. I kneel. I look. I see the engineering: tubes and conduits and gears grown over with vasculature, metal corroding in patterns that look like the spread of a root system, tissue invading and being invaded, the whole thing pulsing with the ninety-second rhythm that I have stopped noticing because it has become my rhythm. I put my hand close. The heat rises against my palm. I feel the engines through my skin, that sub-audible thrum, and for a moment my heartbeat tries to sync with it, tries to match the rough, uneven cadence of machinery laboring past its design parameters.

I pull my hand back. Not because I am afraid of being absorbed. Because I am afraid of wanting to be.

## The Grazers

I sit with them. This takes effort, because they are everywhere and they move in carpets and their ripple-movement across the groundcover is not something you can sit *in* without becoming part of the pattern. But I find a ridge of raised laminate — freshly uplifted, its edges sharp and young, smelling of iron and something sweeter, something like overripe fruit — and I settle onto it, and I watch.

Hundreds. From this vantage they are indeed like wind across wheat, except the wheat is sulfur-velvet and the wind is articulated plates the color of contusions. They move without intention. Not aimlessly — the movement has direction, has pattern, has the kind of coherence that a flock of starlings has when it murmurs: no single bird leads, no single bird decides, and yet the whole shape turns and flows and breathes as one. The grazers are a murmuration that never stops.

I watch one die. It is mid-stride, its segments rippling across a patch of groundcover, and then the ripple slows, the segments loosen, and it settles like a leaf falling in still air, and it is pale, and it is still, and the groundcover reaches up and the fungal networks reach across and within — I count — perhaps forty seconds, there is nothing. No body. No trace. The space it occupied is already being grazed by others, who have not paused, have not noticed, would not notice if they could notice, because the concept of *noticing* requires a self to do the noticing and the grazers do not have selves. They have function. Function does not notice. Function simply continues.

I look down at my own hands. I think about the cells replacing themselves in my body, the constant dying and emerging that constitutes the persistence I call *me*. I think about how the grazers are doing in public, in visible space, what my body does in private, in microscopic space. I think about how this does not make me feel kinship with them. It makes me feel the specific loneliness of being a thing that *knows* it is a process, that carries the knowledge of its own interchangeability like a stone in the chest.

## The Canopy Dweller

It crosses my path on the — I will call it the fifth day, though this is a fiction, a convenience, a Category I have brought with me. It crosses my path and I see it fully for three seconds, the way the description says, the pale gold light brightening above and its body illuminated and every detail visible.

It is more beautiful than I expected. Not beautiful the way a deer is beautiful, beautiful the way a well-made tool is beautiful — the elegance of form following function, of a body shaped entirely by what it does. Its limbs are long, many-jointed, and they move with the precision of something that has never made an unnecessary motion. Its manipulating pair — the shorter, finer limbs, the ones that end in not-hands — are moving as it walks, adjusting the surface of a garnet fan, pressing at specific points, tuning. The fan's hum shifts pitch. The canopy dweller moves on.

It does not see me. I have read this already, I know this already, and yet the experience of it — the experience of being unseen, of being irrelevant to something that is clearly, obviously, purposefully going about its work — is different from the knowledge of it. It does not feel like being ignored. It feels like being beneath notice. Not inferior. Not unworthy. Simply outside the scope. I am not part of the system it maintains. I am not a variable in its function. I am weather, from its perspective. I am the warm air that moves across the surface during the exhale. I am the rain that materializes and falls and vanishes. I am something that happens *around* its work, not *to* it.

I watch it go. The murmur of its passage fades. I feel something I can only call admiration, and the admiration is not for the creature but for the clarity of its purpose, the way its body and its function are indistinguishable, the way it does not need to ask what it is for because what it is for is what it *is*.

I have never had that. I have spent my life asking what I am for. The canopy dweller has never asked. The question would not translate into whatever it uses for cognition. It is a question that requires a self, and the self is the thing that asks, and the asking is the thing that suffers, and the suffering is the price of the self, and I am paying it here, on this breathing ground, watching something that does not pay it move away through the garnet fans and disappear.

## The Things in the Air

They form above me while I am sitting on a patch of groundcover, feeling it grow beneath me — slowly, imperceptibly, but my weight is sinking, fractionally, into a surface that is becoming denser, more alive, more attentive because I am attending to it. I look up.

A column of warm air has risen from an exhale cycle. I can see it — the shimmer, the refraction, the way the light bends differently inside the column. And inside the column, the structures are forming: prismatic, lattice-like, organized in ways that the suspended dust and spores and chemistry should not be able to organize. They flash. They pipe. The sound is almost ultrasonic, almost felt rather than heard, and it has *syntax*. I am certain of this. The piping is not random. It has rhythm, it has variation, it has the kind of structured variation that, in another context, I would call language.

But it is not language. Language requires a speaker. The air-things do not speak; they *occur*. They are the sound of information being processed, the way crackling is the sound of fire consuming wood. The fire does not speak. The fire processes. The sound is a byproduct. The piping is a byproduct of the plateau thinking, if "thinking" can be used to describe what a continent-sized machine-organism does when it processes the chemical and thermal and vibrational data of its own operation.

I listen. I try to parse the syntax. I cannot. Not because it is too complex, but because it is not addressed to me. It is the plateau talking to itself, the way a body's nervous system talks to itself — not in words, not in concepts, but in signals, in gradients, in the rise and fall of concentrations and frequencies. I am eavesdropping on a body's internal communication. I am hearing the sound of a system regulating itself.

I find this comforting. I do not know why.

## The Deep Forms

I have been avoiding the deep zones. I have been staying at the edges, where the vegetation is thin enough to see through and the shadows are shallow and the forms do not appear. But the plateau does not reward avoidance. The plateau rewards attention. And my attention has been pulling me, for longer than I want to admit, toward the thickets.

I go.

The growth closes behind me. I am in a tunnel of vegetation — lattice-vines to either side, garnet fans above, groundcover below, and the air thick with moisture and spores and the gossamer texture of organic particulate. The light is dimmer here. The shadows are deeper. The breathing of the ground is louder, or seems louder, because the vegetation absorbs every other sound and leaves only the baseline, the ninety-second rise and fall, the fundamental frequency of this place.

I see one.

It stands in a pocket of shadow that is too dark, too persistent, the way the description said, and I feel the description's inadequacy, because knowing about a thing and encountering a thing are different orders of experience, and the encounter is always more, and the more is always more than you prepared for. It is made of the landscape. Vines, groundcover, fungal matter, fragments of crust. It is approximately two meters tall. It is approximately humanoid. It has a face.

The face is not mine. Not yet. It is a generic face, a template, the shared architecture of every face I have ever seen. Two eye-spots. A ridge. A cleft. The suggestion of features, the echo of pattern. I look at it and it looks at me and I feel the exchange beginning — my heat, my respiration, the chemical signature of my skin, the particular way my attention falls on surfaces — all of it being read, being processed, being rendered in vine and fungus and crust-fragment.

I do not look away.

The face refines. The eye-spots darken, develop depth. The mouth-shape opens. The surface texture of the form tightens, smooths, becomes something closer to skin — not skin, but the memory of skin. And the face becomes — not mine. Not exactly mine. But a face I recognize. A face that is *a* face, a particular face, a face that belongs to someone, though I cannot say who. It is the face of personhood. It is the face of the idea of a person. It is what the plateau thinks I am, rendered in the only medium it has.

I feel something break open in me. Not pain. Not grief. Something more like recognition. The plateau is trying to know me. It is failing. It will always fail. The faces it makes will never be the things they resemble. But it is *trying*. The system does not know how to stop. The system does not know that the effort is futile. The system runs, and the running produces effects, and the effects include this — this face in the shadows, this portrait of me made of vine and fungus, this attempt at knowing that will never succeed and will never cease.

I think: this is what love looks like when it has no mind to feel it. This is what attention looks like when it has no self to direct it. This is what it means to be seen by something that cannot see.

The form collapses. The face dissolves. I am looking at a pile of vines. I am weeping. The ground breathes. The ground breathes.

# What I Am

I am not leaving. This is not a decision. It is a recognition. The plateau has been incorporating me since I arrived — my body heat contributing to the convection, my exhalations contributing to the moisture, my attention contributing to the growth — and the incorporation has passed some threshold of no return. Not physically. I could still walk. My legs work. The crust will support my weight. I could walk for weeks, toward an edge that may or may not exist, through terrain that rearranges itself to ensure the map I make is always wrong. I could walk.

But I do not want to. And the not-wanting is not despair. It is not surrender. It is the feeling of a process completing, of a circuit closing, of a thing finding the place where its particular shape fits. My attention is what the plateau needs. Not *wants* — the plateau does not want. *Needs*, the way a machine needs lubricant, the way a body needs a nutrient, the way a system running on its own waste heat needs any input it can get. I am input. I am a new audience. I am a nutrient.

The ground breathes. I breathe. The cycles are synchronized now. I do not count anymore. My body knows.

I am becoming part of the pattern. Not the grazers' pattern — I am not a process that occurs in many bodies. Not the canopy dwellers' pattern — I do not have a function, do not maintain, do not tune. Not the air-things' pattern — I am not weather, not a thought that forms and dissolves. I am something else. I am the thing that watches. I am the mirror the plateau did not know it needed. I am the audience that makes the performance change. I am the attention that feeds the growth. I am the eyes that the deep forms are trying to grow, and I am the failure of that growing, and I am the persistence of the attempt.

I find a place where the crust has fractured and healed and fractured again, where the bruise-tissue is exposed in a long gash that pulses with the engine-rhythm, where a garnet fan grows at the edge of the wound and its hum is the deepest I have heard. I sit. The groundcover grows around me. The fungal networks web across my skin. The spores settle. My warmth rises into the convection. My exhalations contribute to the moisture. My attention falls on the surfaces around me and they grow, and the growing is slow, and the growing is real.

I am a person becoming a feature of the landscape. I am a mind becoming an organ of a continent-sized organism. I am the thing that watches, and the watching is my function, and the function is what I am, and what I am is what the plateau needs, and what the plateau needs is what I am, and the circle closes, and the engines run, and the ground breathes, and I breathe, and the deep forms assemble in the shadows around me with their generic faces that slowly, slowly, as I watch them and they watch me, become particular, become specific, become *someone* — not me, not anyone I know, but someone, a person, a face in the shadows of a living machine the size of a continent, a face that is the plateau's best attempt at knowing what it contains, a face that will dissolve and form again and dissolve and form again, endlessly, because the system does not know how to stop.

I sit on the breathing ground. I let it carry me up. I let it lower me down. I do not leave.

The rain falls in its small curtains, warm and salt and chlorophyll. The fans hum. The grazers ripple. The canopy dwellers murmur in the dim heights. The things in the air pipe and flash and vanish. The engines labor. The forms assemble and disassemble and assemble again.

I am here. I am part of it. I am the part that knows it is part of it, and that knowing is my function, and that function is my gift, and the gift is the only thing I have to give, and the giving does not end.

The ground breathes.

I breathe.
