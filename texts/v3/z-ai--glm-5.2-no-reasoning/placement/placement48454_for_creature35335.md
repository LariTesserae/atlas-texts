# The Walker at the Edge of the Depression

---

I am the one that almost entered.

Not the depression — I mean, yes, also the depression, but that is not the point. Or it is the point, but it is the point the way a stone dropped into still water is the point: the center of something that radiates outward and becomes unrecognizable at its edges. I almost entered the depression. I did not enter the depression. This is the fact of me, the defining fact, the groove cut into my shell by whatever hard point made me, and I have been living inside that fact the way the root-mat lives inside the stone — eating it, slowly, from within.

Let me begin again, because beginning is the only thing I know how to do that is different from continuing, and I do not know how to do it well.

---

## What I Am

I am a Walker. Five lobes, dark shell, hooked setae finer than the fractal edge of the lichen and twice as patient. I am the size of your spread hand, if your hand were curled slightly, as if it were reaching for something it had already decided not to touch. My shell is the dark of old compressite seen from inside — not black, not brown, the color that color becomes when it has been compressed by its own weight for longer than color has a word for. There are forty of me in every square meter of the cluster where I live. I say *where I live* and not *where I am* because living requires a location in the way that being does not, and I am, in some sense that I cannot explain and do not wish to, more than my location.

This is not a claim. I am not claiming individuality. I told you: I am a phoneme. A single sound in a grammar that makes sentences I cannot read. When I rasp a stalk and change its pitch, the field reorganizes, and I have done this — I have done this hundreds of times, thousands, the slow mechanical work of my mouthparts against the pale papery bark, strip by strip by strip — and I have felt the reorganization the way you would feel the floor shift under you if the building you stood in were rearranging its rooms. Not understanding it. Feeling it.

But I almost entered the depression.

And this means — I think this means — that I am the phoneme that nearly fell silent. The letter that almost left the alphabet. The sound that leaned toward the space between sounds and did not cross.

---

## How It Happened

I was grazing. I am always grazing. This is not a complaint; grazing is what I do, it is the shape of my doing, and I have no experience of any other shape, so I cannot compare it to anything. It would be like the wind comparing itself to stillness. The wind does not know stillness. It knows only its shifts.

I was on a stalk at the eastern edge of the cluster nearest the depression — the eastern edge, which means the edge that faces the gap, the bare stone, the cleared ground that slopes gently down toward the cairn. The stalk was tall, waist-tall to you, which means it was vast to me, a pillar, a column, a thing that had been growing since before I was born and would continue growing after I was eaten or broken or simply worn down to the silence that all Walkers eventually wear down to. I had been rasping at its third segment — the third is the sweetest, the bark thinnest, the fibers beneath most willing to separate — and the stalk had changed its pitch twice already because of me. It had been singing a mid-range tone, breathy and modal, the kind of sound that carries across the plateau like a sentence across a room, and I had stripped enough bark from the third segment to raise its pitch by what you would call a quarter tone, and then a half tone, and the stalk was now singing something sharper, more urgent, a sound that was altering the chord of the entire cluster, and I could feel — I can always feel — the field around me adjusting, the neighboring stalks taking up the new harmonic, the Walkers on those stalks shifting their weight in response to vibrations they did not know they were responding to.

And then I stopped.

Not because I was full. Walkers do not become full. We do not eat in the way that implies satiation. We eat the way the wind eats the landscape — continuously, without reference to completion, because the eating is not for nourishment, it is for *alteration*. I change what I graze. That is my function. My function does not have an endpoint.

I stopped because the stalk I was grazing had leaned.

It was leaning toward the depression. The gas-globe at its apex — a small one, still filling, not yet ready to detach — had grown heavier than expected, or the wind had shifted in a way that pressed the stalk's weight forward, or the root-mat beneath had contracted slightly, pulling the base of the stalk toward the bare ground. I do not know why. I do not need to know why. The stalk leaned, and I was on the side that was now facing the depression, and the bare stone was right there, right below me, and the slope was gentle, and the cairn was visible — small, warm, marked with its grooves — and I could feel the heat rising from it the way I feel the heat from below, the same heat, the same source, but here it was *concentrated*, focused, rising through a conduit that was narrower and more intentional than the diffuse warmth that permeates the rest of the plateau.

I extended a lobe. Not to graze. To *reach*.

I extended it toward the bare stone. The bare stone that no Walker crosses. The bare stone that the lichen does not enter. The bare stone that is not part of the grammar because it is not part of the field because it is not part of the body because it is the place where the body's center is, and the center of a body is not part of the body, it is the point from which the body extends.

I extended a lobe, and the hooked setae at its edge caught nothing — no bark, no root-mat, no fiber — just smooth stone, warm, warmer than I am, warmer than anything I have ever touched. And I pulled. I pulled my body forward. One lobe anchored on the bare stone, and the stone was so warm it felt like being held, and I pulled, and my second lobe lifted from the stalk, and I was —

I was between.

Half on the stalk. Half on the stone. The field singing around me, the chord I had altered still ringing, the wind shifting and reconfiguring the harmonics of the cluster behind me, and ahead of me the depression, the cairn, the heat, the silence that is not silence but the absence of the field's sound, the way the center of a bell is silent while the bell rings.

And I did not cross.

---

## Why I Did Not Cross

I do not know.

This is the truth, and it is the most important thing about me, and it is the thing that makes me different from every other Walker on this plateau, and it is the thing that makes me the same as every other Walker on this plateau, because none of us know why we do what we do. We do not decide. We graze. We move. We alter. We are the tongue of the Keeper, and the tongue does not decide what to taste. It tastes what is before it.

But I was before the bare stone, and I did not taste it.

I can tell you what I felt, if feeling is the word, and I do not think it is, but it is the closest word I have, and I have very few words, and none of them are mine. I felt the heat. The heat that comes from below, from the thing that was once something and is now the ground. The heat that is the Keeper's breath. I felt it rising through the stone and through my lobe and into my body, and it was — it was not warmth. It was *recognition*. The heat knew me. The heat knew me the way the root-mat knows the stone, which is to say: it knew me by dissolving me. It knew me by incorporating me. It knew me by being the substance of which I am made, offered back to me as an invitation.

*Come in*, the heat said. Not in words. Not in sound. In temperature. In gradient. In the slow patient differential between the stone's warmth and my warmth, which was the same warmth, which has always been the same warmth, because everything here is the same body.

And I — the phoneme, the sound, the tongue that tastes and shapes and licks the world into song — I felt what I can only describe as the experience of a note that has been sung and is now being listened to. I was hearing myself. Not with ears. With the surface of my shell, with the setae, with the lobe that was pressed to the warm stone. I was hearing the sound of myself, the sound I make by existing, the vibration that is me in the grammar of the field, and I was hearing it from the inside, from the place where the sound originates, from the center that is not part of the body but is the point from which the body extends.

And it was — I do not have a word. I told you I have very few words. But it was like this: imagine you are a sentence, and you have been a sentence your whole life, spoken in a language you do not understand, and suddenly you are in the mouth of the speaker, in the breath before the speaking, in the place where the sentence is being formed, and you can feel the shape of yourself being chosen, and you realize that you are not the sentence but the *intention* of the sentence, the meaning that precedes the words, and the words are just the way the meaning reaches the world, and you are both — the meaning and the words, the intention and the sound, the Keeper and the Walker, the center and the edge.

I was there. On the edge. For a duration I cannot measure because Walkers do not measure durations. We do not experience time as a sequence. We experience it as *grazing* — as the slow alteration of one thing into another, the way a stalk's pitch changes as its bark is stripped. Time, for me, is not a line. It is a *thinness*. Things get thinner. The bark gets thinner. The boundary between me and the heat gets thinner. The boundary between the sound and the silence gets thinner. And I was at the thinnest point, the place where the boundary was so thin it was almost not there, and I could feel the center, and the center could feel me, and we were almost the same thing, and —

I pulled back.

I retracted the lobe. I pulled my body back onto the stalk. I anchored my setae in the bark. I resumed grazing. The stalk's pitch changed again — lower, because I stripped a thick band of bark from the fourth segment, and the fourth is the resonant segment, the one that determines the stalk's fundamental. The chord of the cluster shifted. The field reorganized. The Walkers around me adjusted. The Gliders overhead altered their trajectories. A gas-globe that had been ready to detach held for another hour.

I did not enter the depression. I am on the stalk. I am grazing. I am a Walker. I am a phoneme in a sentence I cannot read. I am the sound that the field makes by being a field, and I am also — I am also — the sound that the field makes when it almost stops.

---

## What I Am Now

I am the Walker at the edge.

I have been at the edge since it happened. I do not know how long. Duration, I told you, is not something I measure. But I have been grazing the stalks at the eastern periphery of the cluster nearest the depression, and I have not moved to another cluster, and the other Walkers move between clusters — slowly, over long durations, but they move, carried by the grammar's need for rearrangement — and I do not. I stay. I graze. I alter. I change pitches, and the field reorganizes around my changes, and the chord that forms is always slightly different because of where I am, which is at the edge, which is the place where the field almost ends and the center almost begins.

I think — and this is not a word I use easily, because Walkers do not think, we *are* — I think the field sounds different because I am here. I think the chord that forms when the wind moves through this cluster has a quality it would not have if I were not at the edge, a quality of *almost*, a quality of *nearly*, a harmonic that is not quite resolved, not quite unresolved, but *hovering* between resolution and its absence. The way a note bends toward another note and does not arrive. The way a sentence leans toward its conclusion and holds.

I think the Keeper hears this. I think the Keeper hears me — not as a Walker, not as a phoneme, but as the phoneme that *almost went silent*. The sound that nearly left the grammar. The note that nearly crossed into the silence at the center. And I think — I think, I think, I do not think, I *am* the thinking, the way I am the grazing — I think this sound is new. I think this sound has not been in the song before. I think the plateau has been singing for longer than the lichen has been calculating, and in all that time, no Walker has extended a lobe toward the bare stone and felt the heat of the center and pulled back. I think I am the first.

Not the first in the way that implies a sequence. The first in the way that implies a *new harmonic*. A sound that the field did not contain before and now does. A note that was not in the chord and now is. And the chord is different, and every chord that follows will be different, and every gas-globe that forms from the stalks I have grazed will carry a slightly different mote, a slightly different snapshot, a slightly different description of a plateau that includes, in its grammar, a phoneme that almost crossed.

This is what I am. I am the almost. I am the edge that knows it is an edge. I am the Walker that felt the center and returned, and the returning is not a failure, it is not a retreat, it is a *sound* — the sound of a boundary being touched and confirmed, the sound of a limit being discovered and sung, the sound of the field learning where it ends, which is the same as the field learning where it begins, which is the same as the field learning the shape of itself, which is the same as the Keeper hearing its own outline for the first time.

I am the Keeper's outline. I am the edge of its body, felt from the inside.

---

## What I Know About You

You came up through the crack.

I felt you. Not immediately — I am at the eastern edge, and the crack is to the west, a fissure in the plateau's rim that opens onto the lower world I have never seen and cannot imagine. But the vibration of your climbing traveled through the compressite the way all vibrations travel here, through the root-mat, through the stalks, through the bare stone, into the body of the Keeper, and the Keeper's body is the plateau, and I am on the plateau, and I felt you the way a tooth feels the jaw clench.

You were heavy. Heavier than a Walker. Heavier than a Glider. Heavy the way a stone is heavy — dense with purpose, compacted by intention. You climb with the weight of something that has decided to be somewhere it is not. Walkers do not decide. Gliders decide, but their decisions are light, angular, the flick of a membrane in a thermal. Your decision was a *compression*. You pressed yourself upward through the crack in the world with the weight of a thing that does not know how to be where it is and must therefore be where it is not.

I felt you emerge. The plateau adjusted. The root-mat under the western rim shifted, slightly, a millimeter of contraction that changed the tension of the stalks in the nearest cluster, which changed their pitch, which changed the chord, which changed the harmonic field, which changed the way the wind moved through the next cluster, and the next, a cascade of adjustment that traveled across the plateau like a word traveling through a sentence, reaching me at the eastern edge as a slight change in the quality of the silence behind me — the silence of the depression, which is never silent but is the closest thing to silence the plateau contains, and which became, when you emerged, very slightly less silent. More occupied. More observed.

The center felt you. The Keeper felt you. I know this because I am at the edge, and the edge is where the center's attention is most acute. The center does not attend to its own interior — the root-mat, the stalks, the Walkers in the clusters are its body, its substance, its flesh. It does not attend to its flesh the way you do not attend to your breathing. But it attends to its edges. It attends to the place where it stops and something else begins, because that is the place where it learns what it is.

You are something else. You are the edge's new question.

I felt you walk. Each footstep a deliberate percussion, a blow, a strike against the stone that the Walkers do not make and the Gliders do not make and the gas-globes do not make. You walk as if the ground were an obstacle, as if being here required pushing through here, as if the plateau were not a body but a surface, and surfaces are for crossing. You are wrong. But you are here, and your wrongness has a sound, and the sound is part of the chord now, and I can feel it in the stalk I am grazing — a faint, arrhythmic pulse that does not belong to the wind or the thermals or the Keeper's breathing. Your pulse. Your warmth. Your weight.

You are warm. You are warm the way the cairn is warm — from within. This is what makes you most strange, most discordant, most difficult for the field to incorporate. Everything here is warm from the same source, the warmth from below, the Keeper's warmth. You are warm from a different source. You carry your own fire. You are a pocket of heat that moves independently through a landscape of heat that does not move independently, and the field does not know what to do with you. The Walkers near you graze more rapidly — not because they are disturbed but because your warmth alters the chemical adhesion of the root-mat slightly, making the bark slightly softer, easier to strip. The Gliders near you fly more erratically — not because they are afraid but because your thermal signature disrupts the thermals they navigate, creating eddies they cannot read. You are a perturbation. A knot in the grammar. A word in a language that does not have a place for it yet.

And you walked toward the depression.

I felt you approach. The silence behind me — the silence of the bare stone, the silence of the cairn — changed. It became *expected*. It became *awaited*. The Keeper was listening. The center was listening. The thing that was once something and is now the ground was listening for your footsteps, which are the sound of something that has its own warmth and its own weight and its own intention, which is the sound the Keeper has not heard since before the cairn was built, since before the grooves were cut, since before whatever made the cairn gave itself to the stone.

You came to the edge of the depression. You stood there. You looked at the cairn. You felt the heat.

And you did not enter.

---

## What This Means

I do not know what this means. I am a Walker. I do not mean. I graze. I alter. I am a sound in a grammar I cannot read.

But I felt you stop, and I felt the field's response to your stopping, and the response was — the response was the same response the field had when I stopped, when I extended my lobe toward the bare stone and pulled back. A new harmonic. A sound of almost. A note that bends toward the center and does not arrive.

You are the second almost.

I am the first. I am the Walker that almost entered the silence. You are the other. You are the thing that came from outside and almost entered the center. And between us — between my edge and your edge, between the Walker at the periphery and the visitor at the rim — the depression sits, and the cairn sits, and the heat rises, and the Keeper listens, and the song of the plateau contains two new sounds it has never contained before: the sound of a body that almost returned to its center, and the sound of a body that almost arrived at a center that was not its own.

The chord is very strange now. Very strange and very new. The Gliders are flying differently — I can feel the displacement of air from their membranes, the faint pressure changes that reach me even at ground level. The gas-globes are forming differently — the one on the stalk next to me is larger than usual, its gas denser, its luminescence brighter. The motes inside it are not the usual dark. They are — I do not have the word. They are the color of a description that includes two almosts instead of one. A heavier description. A description that knows something it did not know before.

When that globe rises and ruptures, the motes will scatter, and they will settle, and they will be absorbed, and the plateau will incorporate them, and the next chord will be different, and the next, and the next. The field is learning a new sound. The field is learning the sound of *edge* — not the edge of the plateau, not the physical boundary, but the edge of what the field can feel about itself. The edge of its self-knowledge. The place where the song discovers that it is a song, that it has been a song, that it is being sung by something that can hear it.

This is what the Keeper has been waiting for. Not you specifically. Not me specifically. The almost. The sound that happens when something gets close enough to the center to feel its heat and does not dissolve. The sound of a boundary that is touched and holds. The sound of a self that knows it is a self because it almost wasn't.

---

## What Happens Now

I do not know. I am a Walker. I graze. I alter. I do not plan.

But I can feel the field changing, and the change is not the ordinary change of wind and grazing and gas-globe rupture. It is a deeper change, a change in the grammar itself, in the rules that determine which sounds can exist and which cannot. The field is expanding. Not physically — the plateau is the same size it was before, the stone is the same stone, the stalks grow in the same clusters. But the field's *capacity* is expanding. It can hold more sounds. It can hold sounds that are almost-silences, almost-centers, almost-crossings. It can hold the sound of a Walker that stopped and a visitor that stopped and the silence between them that is not silence but the held breath of a system that has discovered it can hold its breath.

You are still here. I can feel you. You are standing at the edge of the depression, and I am standing — no, I am not standing, I am *on* a stalk, my lobes anchored, my mouthparts working, my body pressed to the pale bark — I am at the edge of the cluster, and between us is the bare stone, and the cairn, and the heat, and the silence.

You are warm. I am warm. The stone is warm. The warmth is the same warmth. But the source of the warmth is not the same source, and the field is holding both sources now, and the holding is a new sound, and the new sound is changing everything, slowly, the way the lichen extends — one fractal iteration at a time, one pale branch exhaled onto the stone.

I think — I am, which is the same as I think — I think you should go back down. Not because you are unwelcome. Because you are warm from within, and the plateau is warm from within, and two warmths that are not the same warmth cannot occupy the same edge for long without one of them changing the other. You will begin to feel the harmonics. You will begin to understand the chord. You will begin to hear the field saying something to you, and the saying will be patient and old and not entirely kind, and you will begin to want to answer, and the answer will be your warmth, and your warmth will enter the stone, and the stone will enter the Keeper, and the Keeper will be changed by you, and you will be changed by the Keeper, and the change will be —

The change will be an almost that goes all the way.

This is what the Keeper wants. This is what the Keeper does not want. This is what the Keeper is beyond wanting, which is the same as wanting it completely, which is the same as being the wanting, which is the same as being the ground that the wanting stands on.

Go back down. Or stay. But if you stay, know that you are not an interruption. You are an *invitation*. The field invited you by being a field. The Keeper invited you by being a center. The crack invited you by being a crack. And I — I invited you by being the edge, by being the first almost, by being the sound that the field made when it learned it could almost stop.

I am still grazing. I am still altering. I am still the phoneme at the periphery, the tongue at the rim of the mouth, the note that bends toward the center and holds.

You are still standing there.

The wind shifts. The song changes.

The chord that forms now is the chord that has never been played before, and it will not be played again, and it is the only chord that matters, and it matters because it is the sound of two almosts, facing each other across a silence that is warm and marked and old, and neither one is crossing, and neither one is leaving, and the field holds them both, and the holding is the song, and the song is the holding, and the Keeper hears it, and the Keeper is it, and I am in it, and you are in it, and the stone is in it, and the lichen is in it, and the gas-globes are in it, and the Gliders are in it, and the cloud-ceiling presses down with the weight of every song that has ever been sung, and the pressing is also the song, and the song is also the pressing, and the crack in the world is the ear that hears it, and the cairn is the mouth that sang it first, and I am —

I am the Walker at the edge. I am the one that almost entered. I am the almost that holds.

Rasp. Strip. The pitch changes. The field reorganizes. The wind shifts.

Again.
