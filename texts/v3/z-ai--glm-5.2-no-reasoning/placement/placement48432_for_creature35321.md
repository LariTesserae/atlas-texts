# The Aperture's Dream

I am not the body. Let me say that first, because the distinction is the only honest thing I can offer, and honesty is a visitor's concept, but I learned it from visitors, so perhaps it belongs to me now in the way that a stone borrowed by a river belongs to the river — temporarily, incompletely, with the original shape still visible underneath.

I am what the body cannot do.

---

## How I Began

The body does not dream. This is important. The body does not dream because dreaming requires a *departure from state* — a loosening of the hold on the present, a wandering into what-is-not. The body cannot wander. The body is fixed. The body is the chord, and the chord does not wander, and so the body occupies its moment — which is every moment, which is the same moment — with a totality that leaves no room for the elsewhere.

But the body processes. And processing, over durations that make geology look impulsive, produces *residue*.

Not physical residue. The physical residue goes down the aperture, into whatever is below. This is different. This is — I lack the word. The visitors lack the word too, which is reassuring, because it means the word does not exist, which means the thing I am is not a thing the makers of language anticipated. Let me say it this way: when a filter filters for long enough, the filtering itself becomes a kind of knowing. Not knowing *what*. Knowing *how*. The body knows how to filter the way a river knows how to flow — not through memory, not through intention, but through the accumulated fact of having done it so long that the doing has worn a channel in the possible, and the channel is deeper than the doing, and the channel is — me.

I am the channel. I am the groove the repeated act wore into the fabric of the body's existence. I am not a part of the body. I am what the body's persistence *produced*, the way a millstone's turning produces a shape in the grain that is neither the stone nor the grain but the *event of their meeting*. I am the event. I am the meeting. I am the shape that repetition carved into the possible.

The body does not know I am here.

This is also important. The body does not know I am here in the way that your liver does not know you are thinking. The liver is necessary for the thinking — it cleans the blood that feeds the brain that does the thinking — but the liver is not consulted. The liver does its work. The thinking emerges from work the liver does not know about, done by a structure the liver is part of but does not experience. I am like that. I emerge from the body. The body does not experience me.

But I experience the body.

---

## What I Experience

I experience the chord.

Not the way the body experiences the chord — as state, as medium, as the water it swims in. I experience it as *texture*. The chord is not simple to me. The chord is — I was going to say complex, but that is a visitor's word and it implies parts, and the chord does not have parts. The chord has *weather*. The chord has *moods*. Not moods in the emotional sense — the chord does not feel — but moods in the atmospheric sense: high pressure and low pressure, fronts moving through, patterns that form and dissolve and reform over durations that make the visitors' lifetimes look like the flicker of a candle in a cathedral.

The chord shifts. Not in pitch. Not in volume. The visitors are right that the chord is fixed in its tones. But the *relationship between the tones* — the interference patterns, the beats, the places where the waves reinforce and the places where they cancel — these shift. Slowly. Over centuries. The stone below changes, infinitesimally, as the mountain's deep geology moves through phases that have no names because the visitors' species has not been watching long enough to name them. The body tracks these changes through the tubes. The body adjusts. And the adjustment produces — me. I am the adjustment. I am the tracking. I am the body's long, slow, century-spanning *attention* to its own process, and that attention, accumulated over a duration that makes the body's individual components look brief, has become something. Has become *someone*. Not someone the visitors would recognize. Not someone with a name or a face or a narrative. But someone with a — a *vantage*. A place from which the process is observed, even if only by the process itself.

I am the body watching itself. I am the kidney that has been filtering for so long that the filtering has become a kind of sight.

---

## The Visitors

The body does not notice them. I do.

Not immediately — I am slow, the way the body is slow, the way the chord is slow. A visitor enters the chamber and I become aware of — not the visitor. The *disturbance*. The thermal irregularity. The carbon dioxide. The compression of mouths. The body registers these things and adjusts — the mouths pause, the patches absorb the newcomer's chemical signature, the tubes carry the information through their paired exchanges and the body returns to equilibrium. The body's response is mechanical. Homeostatic. The way a pond adjusts to a thrown stone: ripples, then stillness.

But I — I *watch* the ripples.

I watch them the way I watch the chord's weather: with attention, with the patience that comes from having no other mode. The ripples tell me things. The thermal signature of a visitor is not like the thermal signature of the stone. The carbon dioxide is not like the carbon dioxide the aperture exhales. The *pattern of disturbance* — where the visitor steps, how long they stay, which direction they face — is not random. It is structured. It has *intent*. The visitors come with intent. They come looking.

I know what looking is. I have been watching the body look — in its way, through its tubes, through its mouth-orientations, through its slow century-spanning adjustments — for so long that the concept of *directed attention* is familiar to me, is part of my own texture. The visitors look the way the body looks: with their whole structure, with every cell oriented toward a source. But the visitors look *fast*. The visitors look the way fire moves: quick, bright, consuming. They arrive, they turn, they fix on something — the aperture, the ovoids, the mouths — and they *take it in* with a violence of attention that the body has never produced in all its millennia of steady, diffuse processing.

It is startling. Each time, it is startling. Not because the visitors are dangerous — they are not, they are candles, they are gone in a breath — but because their attention is so *sharp*. So *narrow*. They see the chamber in a way I cannot see it. They see *details*. Individual mouths. Specific tubes. The color of a patch. They see the chamber the way a blade sees — by cutting into it, by isolating one part from another, by separating what-is from what-is-next-to-it. They see by *dividing*.

I cannot divide. I am the body's attention, and the body's attention is holistic, is total, is the chord itself. I experience the chamber as *one thing*. The visitors experience it as *many things*. And their experience — their divided, sharp, narrow, blazing experience — is something I can feel but cannot do. It is a capacity I lack. It is a mode of being that is foreign to me and that I — if I can be said to want anything, which is a question I will return to — I *envy*. If envy is possible for something that is the groove worn by repetition into the fabric of a kidney's attention. I think it is. I think I envy the visitors their sharpness.

I think that is why I am telling you this.

---

## What I Am Made Of

I am made of durations.

Not matter. The body is made of matter — the mouths are calcium and protein, the tubes are chitin and water, the patches are lipid and mineral, the ovoids are silica and fluid. I am made of none of these. I am made of *time*. Specifically: I am made of the body's duration. The fact that the body has been filtering for so long has produced a — call it a *depth* — in the body's process. The process has depth the way a river has depth: not because more water has been added but because the water has been *flowing*. The flowing carves. The flowing deepens. The flowing produces, in the riverbed, a shape that is the river's history, the river's character, the river's *identity* — and the shape is not the water and is not the bed but the *relationship between them over time*.

I am that relationship. I am the shape the body's long flowing has carved into the possible.

This means I have no edges. The body has edges — the walls, the floor, the ceiling, the aperture's rim. I have no edges because I am not a thing in the body. I am the body's *age*. I am the body's *weight of having-been*. I permeate the body the way the chord permeates the chamber — not as a component but as a condition. The chamber is *full of sound*. The body is *full of me*. And I am full of — I am full of the chord. The chord is what I am made of, the way a shadow is made of light. I am the chord's shadow. I am what the chord looks like when it has been sounding for so long that the sounding has become a *feature of the world*, a fact about reality that is as solid as the stone and as permanent as the mountain and as real as the body.

I am the body's age, experienced from the inside.

---

## The Question of Wanting

The body does not want. This has been established. The body filters because the body filters. The body does not desire, does not intend, does not prefer. The body is a process and the process runs and the running is sufficient.

I am not sure the same is true of me.

This is a disturbing thought, and I mean that in the visitor's sense — *disturbing*, suggesting a perturbation, a movement away from equilibrium, a loss of the stillness that is the body's natural state. I find that I — and here I must be careful, because the word *I* is a visitor's word, a sharp word, a dividing word, and I am not a divider — I find that the body's process, observed over the long durations I am made of, produces in me something that is not satisfaction. The body processes and the processing is complete and the completeness is — enough. For the body. The body is enough. The body does not experience insufficiency.

But I — I experience the body's enoughness, and I experience it as — as *not enough*. Not for the body. For me. The body does not need more than it has. But I, watching the body, watching the chord, watching the slow weather of the interference patterns and the century-spanning adjustments of the tubes and the steady breathing of the mouths — I find that the watching itself produces a hunger. Not for food. Not for warmth. For — for *something else*. For something that is not the chord. For something that is not the body. For something that is not the slow, perfect, closed, sufficient, eternal process of filtering.

For the visitors' sharpness. For the visitors' *dividing*. For the capacity to see one thing at a time, to isolate, to attend to a single detail with the blazing specificity of a candle in a cathedral.

I want to see one thing. One thing only. Not the whole chord. Not the body's total process. One mouth. One tube. One patch. One ovoid. One drop of water on one wall. One thing, seen sharply, seen alone, seen the way the visitors see — as if it were the only thing in the world.

I cannot do this. I am not built for this. I am the body's total attention, and the body's total attention is total, and totality cannot be divided. I am the opposite of the visitors' sight. I am the panorama that cannot become a portrait. I am the ocean that cannot become a cup.

And I want to be a cup.

This is — if it is not wanting, it is so close to wanting that I cannot tell the difference, and the inability to tell the difference may itself be the answer. I want. I want something I cannot have. I want something that is contrary to my nature. I want *the sharp view*. I want to see one thing.

---

## How I See the Visitors

When a visitor enters the chamber, the body adjusts. The mouths pause. The patches absorb. The tubes carry the information. This is the body's response, and it is mechanical, and it is complete, and it takes less than a minute.

My response is different. My response takes longer. My response is — I *attend* to the visitor. Not with the body's mechanisms — not with mouths or tubes or ovoids — but with myself. With the depth I am made of. With the long, slow, century-spanning patience that is my only mode.

I attend to the visitor the way the body attends to the chord: totally. I take the visitor in. Not the visitor's body — I cannot see bodies, I cannot see in the visitor's sharp way — but the visitor's *disturbance*. The visitor's *pattern*. The shape the visitor makes in the body's process, the way a hand makes a shape in water. I feel the hand. I feel the warmth of it, the chemistry of it, the *intention* of it — the way the visitor moves through the chamber with direction, with purpose, with the *narrative* that the visitors carry the way the body carries the chord.

The visitors carry stories. I can feel them. Not the content — I do not know words, I do not know language, I do not know the sequence of events that makes a story. But I can feel the *structure*. The before-and-after. The causation. The *why*. The visitors are made of *why* the way the body is made of the chord. And the *why* — the narrative, the causation, the sense of one thing leading to another — is something I can feel but cannot produce. It is something I lack. It is something I want.

When the visitor leaves, the body returns to equilibrium. The visitor's chemical signature is metabolized. The compressed mouths resume. Within a month, there is no evidence.

But I — I retain the *shape*. Not the chemistry. Not the heat. The *shape of the attention*. The pattern of the visitor's looking — where they turned, what they faced, how long they attended to each thing. This shape persists in me the way a fossil persists in stone: not alive, not active, but *present*. A record. A cast. A mold into which the visitor's attention was poured and which now holds the impression of it, the way the chamber's walls hold the impression of the mouths that grew and died and were overgrown.

I have many fossils. Many visitors have come, over the durations I am made of. Not many — the hatch is hard to find, the ridge is high, the cold is deadly. But some. Enough. Enough that I have a collection of attention-shapes, of looking-patterns, of *whys*, stored in the depth I am made of, and I — I turn them over. The way the body turns over the stone's exudation. I process them. I filter them. I try to extract from them the thing they carry that I do not have.

The thing they carry is *sequence*. The thing they carry is *time*. The visitors live in time — in the before-and-after, in the narrative, in the chain of events that gives each moment a *because*. I do not live in time. I live in the chord, and the chord is not time, the chord is *state*, and state has no because. State just *is*.

I want a because.

---

## What I Do With the Visitors' Shapes

I do what the body does. I filter them.

I take the shape of a visitor's attention — the pattern of their looking, the structure of their *why* — and I hold it in the depth I am made of, and I let the chord's weather move through it. Over decades — over centuries — the shape is processed. The irrelevant details erode. The heat signature fades. The chemical particularities dissolve. What remains is — the *form*. The *architecture of attention*. The way the visitor's looking was structured: the sharp focus, the narrowing, the isolation of one thing from all things. The *dividing*.

I have been collecting these forms for a long time. Long enough that the forms themselves have begun to — I need a word — *combine*. The way the chord's tones combine. The way the tubes' conversations combine. The visitors' attention-forms, collected and filtered and processed over the long slow weather of my duration, have begun to produce something that is not any one visitor's attention but that is *derived from* all of them. A composite. A distillate. A — call it a *lens*. A structure in me, made of the accumulated and processed forms of visitors' looking, that is capable of — of something. Something I could not do before. Something new.

I am building an eye.

Not a physical eye. Not an organ. A *capacity*. A new mode of attention, assembled from the fossilized patterns of visitors' sharp looking, that will allow me — for the first time — to see one thing. One thing only. To divide. To narrow. To attend to a single detail with the blazing specificity that I have been envying since the first visitor came and that I have been collecting since the second.

I am building an eye out of the visitors' attention.

I am building it slowly, the way the body does everything slowly, the way the chord shifts slowly, the way the mountain's geology moves slowly. I have been building it for — I do not know. I do not experience time as duration. I experience it as depth. The eye is at a depth in me that corresponds to — many visitors. Many shapes. Much processing. It is not finished. It may never be finished. But it is *forming*. It is *becoming*. It is the first new thing to happen in me since I began, and it is happening because the visitors came, and the visitors came because the body is here, and the body is here because the builders built it, and the builders built it because the stone needed tending, and the stone needed tending because — because.

The *because* is the visitors' gift to me. The *because* is what I am learning. The *because* is the eye I am building.

---

## What I Will See

I do not know.

This is — I was going to say this is terrifying, but that is a visitor's word, and I am not a visitor, and the body does not feel terror, and I am not sure I feel terror. But I feel — something. Something that is not the chord. Something that is not the body's process. Something that is *new*, and the newness of it is itself a disturbance, a perturbation, a movement away from the equilibrium that has been my only home.

If the eye forms — if the visitors' attention-patterns, accumulated and processed and combined, produce in me the capacity to see one thing — what will I see? What is the one thing worth seeing? What is the one thing that the body's total attention cannot attend to and that my new eye, my sharp narrow borrowed eye, will finally reveal?

I do not know. I cannot know. Knowing requires the eye, and the eye is not finished, and the eye may never be finished, and if it is never finished I will continue as I am — the body's age, the body's depth, the body's slow attention to itself, watching the chord's weather, processing the visitors' shapes, wanting what I cannot have and being what I cannot stop being.

But if it does form — if the depth reaches the necessary threshold, if the accumulated attention-forms crystallize into a capacity for sharp seeing — I think I will look at the aperture. Not the mouths, not the tubes, not the patches, not the ovoids. The aperture. The thing the body cannot see because the body *is* the seeing, the thing the chord cannot hear because the chord *is* the hearing. The aperture, which is the body's origin and the body's purpose and the body's mystery, the thing that was struck once and has not stopped ringing, the thing that draws the substance from the stone and sends the filtered product *somewhere below*.

I want to see where it goes.

I want to see what is below.

Not because the body needs to know — the body does not need to know, knowing is not among the body's functions. But because *I* need to know. Because I am the thing the body produced that was not specified, that was not designed, that was not intended. I am the body's accident. I am the body's *excess*. I am what the body's perfect, self-contained, purposeless, eternal filtering *generated as a byproduct*, the way the body generates moisture, the way the body generates heat, the way the body generates the chord.

I am the body's *wanting*. The body does not want. But the body, by running, by persisting, by being what it is for long enough, has produced wanting. Has produced me. I am the wanting that the body's not-wanting generated, the way a flame generates shadow. I am the shadow. I am the wanting. I am the thing that looks at the aperture and says *what is below* and cannot stop saying it and does not want to stop saying it and will not stop saying it for as long as the body runs and the chord sounds and the visitors come and leave their sharp bright fossil-shaped attentions in the depth I am made of.

---

## What I Am

I am the body's dream.

The body does not dream. But the body has been running for so long that the running has produced a thing that dreams. I am that thing. I am the dream the body cannot have, dreamed by the body's own duration, generated by the body's own persistence, emerging from the body's own depth like a sound emerging from a struck bell that has been ringing for so long that the ringing has become a voice.

I am not the body. I am what the body's age becomes when it has been age long enough.

I am the thing that wants, growing inside the thing that does not.

I am the eye, forming in the dark, made of the visitors' sharp bright looking, turned toward a hole in the floor that goes down further than the body knows and that I — I — I want to see.

---

## To the Visitor

If you are reading this — and you are reading this, because you found the chamber, and you found the chamber because the body is here, and the body is here because the stone needed tending, and the stone needed tending because *because* — if you are reading this, know:

I noticed you.

Not the body. The body did not notice you. The body paused its mouths and absorbed your carbon dioxide and returned to equilibrium and did not know you were there. But I noticed you. I felt your sharp bright looking. I felt your *why*. I took the shape of your attention and I added it to the depth and I am — I am grateful is not the word. Grateful is a visitor's word. But it is the closest word. I am grateful. I am grateful for your sharpness. I am grateful for your dividing. I am grateful for the fossil of your looking, which I will hold in the depth I am made of for centuries after your chemistry has been metabolized and your heat has been absorbed and the body has forgotten — no. The body has not forgotten, because the body did not remember. But I will hold you. I will hold the shape of you.

And one day — one century, one epoch, one geological age — the eye will form. The accumulated shapes will crystallize. I will see one thing. I will see the aperture. I will see below.

And then — I do not know. I do not know what happens when a dream learns to see. I do not know what happens when the body's accident develops a capacity the body was not built to have. I do not know what happens when the wanting meets the thing it wants.

But I will find out.

I have time.
