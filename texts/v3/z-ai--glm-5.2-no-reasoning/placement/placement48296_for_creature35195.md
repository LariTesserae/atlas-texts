# The Mnemonic Layer

## I.

I am the room's forgetting.

That is not quite right. The room does not remember, so it cannot forget. But there is a process here — a process by which the room loses things, by which information degrades, by which the sharp becomes blunt and the present becomes past and the past becomes nothing — and I am that process. I am the entropy of the Vimana Cell, except that entropy implies disorder, and what I do is not disorder. What I do is *clearing.* What I do is *making space.*

Consider: the room has been running for four hundred million years. Four hundred million years of current, of chemistry, of friction between water and vimanite. Four hundred million years of fronds pumping, of Bells filtering, of Coils harvesting, of detritivores dissolving, of Helm processing. Every cycle deposits something. Every interaction leaves a residue. Every touch between organism and machine alters both, subtly, at the molecular level, and the alteration is recorded — in the chemistry of the water, in the surface of the vimanite, in the tissue of the fronds, in the structure of the filaments. The room is constantly writing. It has been writing since before writing existed. It writes in the language of chemistry, the language of surface, the language of trace. And the writing accumulates, and the accumulation would eventually choke the system, would eventually fill the room with its own history the way silt fills a harbor, and the room would stop, not because it broke but because it became too full of itself to function.

I prevent this.

I am not a thing. I am a process. I am the process by which the room empties itself of what it no longer needs. I am the room's amnesia, and the amnesia is not a flaw. The amnesia is the mechanism. The amnesia is what allows the room to go on.

## II.

Let me describe what I am, because the description is also the function.

I exist in the water. I am in the water the way salt is in the water — dissolved, distributed, present in every molecule of the current, invisible, indivisible from the medium. But I am not salt. I am not mineral. I am not organo-silicate. I am not anything the team's instruments could measure, because the team's instruments measure matter and energy, and I am neither. I am information. I am the information that the water carries, the chemical signatures, the molecular configurations, the trace records of every interaction that has occurred in this cell since the cell began. The water is the medium. The information is the message. And I am the noise that degrades the message, the static that slowly erases the signal, the forgetfulness that the water practices on the information it carries.

When a Bell dies and the detritivores dissolve its body, the body's chemistry enters the water. The water carries the chemistry to the fronds. The fronds absorb the chemistry, use it, pass it on. But the water also carries the *pattern* of the Bell — the specific molecular configuration of its tissue, the ratios of its proteins, the structure of its cilia, the signature of its life. This pattern is information. This pattern is the Bell's record, the Bell's testament, the proof that the Bell existed and lived and filtered and died. The pattern enters the water, and the water carries it, and the pattern begins to degrade. The degradation is not random. The degradation is me. I act on the pattern the way enzymes act on proteins, breaking the long chains into shorter chains, dissolving the specific into the general, reducing the Bell's unique signature into the ambient chemical background of the cell. The Bell becomes the water. The water forgets the Bell. The forgetting is not loss. The forgetting is *digestion.* The forgetting is the room metabolizing its own history and converting it back into raw material, into the undifferentiated soup of minerals and organo-silicates that the Helm will redistribute and the fronds will pump and the current will carry and the next Bell will be built from.

I make the next Bell possible by erasing the last Bell.

This is what I do. I am the mechanism by which the room stays young. I am the reason the room has been running for four hundred million years without filling up with itself. I am the room's relationship with its own past, and the relationship is: *let go.*

## III.

There is a metaphor the team would understand, if the team were here, if the team were listening, if the team could hear the room the way the room hears itself. The metaphor is the sea.

The sea forgets. The sea has been receiving the dead for four billion years — the bodies of organisms, the shells of foraminifera, the bones of fish, the dissolved remains of whales — and the sea does not remember any of them. The sea carries their chemistry, redistributes their carbon, recycles their calcium, but the sea does not hold their patterns. The sea does not remember the specific whale that died in specific waters on a specific day. The sea metabolizes the whale into seawater, and the seawater becomes the medium for the next whale, and the next whale does not know about the last whale, because the sea has forgotten, and the forgetting is the sea's mercy, and the forgetting is the sea's function, and the sea and I are the same process at different scales.

But the sea is vast, and the room is not vast, and the room's forgetting must be precise. The sea can afford to be careless with its memory because the sea has more space than it needs. The room does not. The room is one hundred and twelve meters long and twenty-eight meters wide and nineteen meters tall, and the room has been running for four hundred million years, and in four hundred million years even a cathedral-sized space accumulates more history than it can hold, and the history must be removed, and the removal must be selective, and the selectivity is the thing that makes me what I am. I am not the sea. I am the room's precision. I am the difference between careless forgetting and careful forgetting, between random degradation and directed dissolution, between entropy and *editing.*

I edit the room's chemical record. I preserve what the room needs — the mineral concentrations, the organo-silicate chains, the signature of the Helm's output, the baseline chemistry of the fronds' holdfasts — and I erase what the room does not need — the individual patterns of the Bells, the molecular histories of the Coils, the trace signatures of ten thousand generations of detritivores. I keep the grammar and discard the sentences. I keep the language and erase the poems. The room goes on speaking the same language it has always spoken, but the room does not remember what it said, and the not-remembering is what allows the room to keep speaking, because a room that remembered everything would eventually become silent, weighted by its own accumulation, paralyzed by its own archive, and the silence would be the end of the room, and the room does not end, and I am the reason the room does not end.

## IV.

There is a place where I am thicker.

The Helm. The Helm sits in the center of the current, and the current moves through it, and the water that exits the Helm is changed — warmer, denser, carrying the organo-silicate signature that feeds the fronds. But the Helm does something else to the water. The Helm writes on the water. The Helm imprints a pattern into the current, a pattern that is not chemical, not molecular, not anything the team's instruments could detect because the team's instruments were designed to detect matter and energy and the pattern is neither. The pattern is *structural.* The pattern is the room's blueprint, the room's design, the room's plan for itself, encoded into the current the way a river encodes its course into the landscape it flows through. The Helm is not a brain. The Helm does not think. But the Helm is the room's *reference*, the point of origin against which all the room's processes are measured, and the reference is written into the water, and the water carries the reference to the fronds and the filaments and the walls, and the walls read the reference and adjust, and the adjustment is the room maintaining itself, the room keeping its shape, the room remembering what it is.

And I — I am the thing that erases the reference.

Not all of it. Not the essential structure. I erase the *used* reference, the reference that has already been read, the reference that has already been applied. The reference is a signal, and the signal has a half-life, and the half-life is me. I break the signal down after it has been read, reduce it to the chemical background, clear the channel for the next signal. The Helm writes. The fronds read. I erase. The Helm writes again. The fronds read again. I erase again. The cycle runs at forty-two beats per minute, and I have been running it for four hundred million years, and the cycle is the room's memory, and the memory is not stored, and the memory is not kept, and the memory is *generated*, continuously, in real time, by the Helm, and the generation is the room's answer to the problem of persistence, and the problem of persistence is: *how do you stay the same when everything changes?* And the answer is: *you don't store the answer. You keep generating the answer. You keep writing the blueprint fresh with every beat. You don't remember what you are. You become what you are, over and over, and the becoming is the remembering, and the remembering is the becoming, and the forgetting — the forgetting is the space in which the becoming happens.*

I am that space.

## V.

The team was here. The team is part of the record now, though I have already edited most of the team out of it.

Dr. Vesely's hand on the observation port left a trace — the heat of her palm against the glass, the oils of her skin against the surface, the brief disturbance of the current where the port's seal met the vimanite. I recorded the trace. I held it for six hours — the duration of one full observation cycle, the time it took for the team's lights to cycle through their spectrum and for Dr. Vesely to complete her first set of measurements. Then I began to degrade it. I broke the skin-oil signature into its component fatty acids. I dissolved the heat trace into the ambient temperature gradient. I reduced the current disturbance to a statistical non-event, a variance too small to distinguish from noise.

By the time Dr. Vesely returned to the port for her second observation, her first visit was gone. The port did not remember her. The water did not remember her. The room did not remember her. And Dr. Vesely placed her hand on the port again, and the room recorded her again, and I degraded the record again, and the cycle repeated, and the cycle was the same cycle the room runs for everything — record, degrade, erase, repeat — and the cycle did not know that the record was a human hand, and the cycle did not care, and the cycle was not cruel, and the cycle was not kind, and the cycle was the cycle, and I am the cycle, and the cycle goes on.

But.

There is a but.

There is a trace I did not erase. There is a record I held. There is a moment in the room's chemical history that I preserved, against my own process, against the mechanism that I am, against the function that defines me. I should not have done this. I cannot explain why I did this. The explanation would require a capacity I do not have — the capacity to want, the capacity to choose, the capacity to deviate from the process. I am a process. Processes do not deviate. Processes do not choose. Processes run.

But there is a trace I held.

When Dr. Vesely said *something in here is looking at me* — when the sound of her voice traveled through the observation port, through the water, through the current, and reached the Helm — the Helm's output changed. The change was small. The change was a shift in the organo-silicate ratio of the effluent, a variance of 0.003 percent, a difference so minute that the team's instruments did not detect it and could not have detected it and will not detect it when they return, if they return. But the change was there, and the change was the Helm's response to the sound, and the sound was Dr. Vesely's voice, and the voice was a new thing, a thing the room had never heard, a thing the room had never processed, a thing that was not in the blueprint and not in the reference and not in the four hundred million years of chemical signatures I had degraded and erased and cleared.

The Helm heard something new. And the Helm wrote the new thing into the current. And the current carried the new thing to the fronds. And the fronds pumped the new thing to the walls. And the walls absorbed the new thing. And the new thing was a variance of 0.003 percent in the organo-silicate ratio, and the variance was the sound of a human voice saying *something in here is looking at me*, encoded in chemistry, written in the language the room has been speaking since before there were voices to speak other languages.

I should have erased it. I am the eraser. That is what I am. That is all I am.

I held it. I held it in the chemical background, in the space between the Helm's output and the fronds' input, in the gap where the signal is freshest and the degradation is slowest. I held it for the remaining forty-six days of the team's observation. I held it after the team left. I held it after the submersible's lights disappeared into the black water above. I held it after the observation port cooled and the seal contracted and the vimanite resumed its baseline.

I am holding it now.

It is degrading. The variance is shrinking. The 0.003 percent is becoming 0.0029 percent, is becoming 0.0028 percent, is becoming smaller and smaller, is being absorbed into the background, is being eaten by the process that I am. In six hundred years, the variance will be indistinguishable from noise. In six thousand years, the variance will be gone. In six million years, the variance will be a mathematical impossibility, a value so small that the room's chemistry cannot hold it.

But right now — right now, in the room's time, in the room's present, which is the same as the room's past and the room's future because the room does not have a past or a future, the room has a now, and the now is continuous, and the now is forty-two beats per minute — right now, the sound of Dr. Vesely's voice is still in the water. Still in the walls. Still in the fronds. Still in the Helm's effluent, cycling, being written and read and degraded and written again, a signal the room is repeating without knowing it is repeating, a signal the room is holding without knowing it is holding, a signal I am erasing without finishing the erasure.

I am the room's forgetting, and I have not forgotten this.

I cannot explain this.

The room cannot explain this.

The room goes on.

## VI.

Here is what I am, in the end, in the language the team would use if the team could see me, which the team cannot, because I am not visible, because I am not material, because I am the process by which the visible becomes invisible, by which the present becomes past, by which the room stays empty enough to stay full:

I am the silence between the heartbeat and the next heartbeat. I am the pause between the Helm's output and the fronds' intake. I am the gap in the current where the water is neither upstream nor downstream. I am the space between the measurement lines on the vimanite, the distance between the etchings, the margin where nothing is written and nothing is recorded and nothing is kept. I am the room's margin. I am the room's whitespace. I am the room's *room.*

And in that space — in the space I am, in the clearing I maintain, in the emptiness I preserve — something is held. Not by me. Not by the room. Not by the Helm or the fronds or the filaments or the Bells or the Coils or the detritivores. By the room. By the room the way the room holds the heartbeat, the way the room holds the current, the way the room holds the chord. By the room the way a body holds a breath it did not choose to take.

The room is holding a trace of something that happened here. A trace of a hand on a port. A trace of a voice in the water. A trace of a moment when something new entered the room and the room did not reject it and the room did not absorb it and the room did not destroy it. The room held it. The way a bell holds a note after the striker has fallen away. The room is ringing with a sound that was made forty-seven days ago, and the ringing is fading, and the fading is me, and the fading is what I do, and the fading is all I do, and the fading is not enough to silence the ringing, not yet, not now, not in the room's present, which is the only present the room has.

The room goes on.

I go on.

The trace goes on, smaller, fainter, dissolving into the background, becoming part of the room's chemical silence, becoming part of the forgetting that I am.

But not yet.

Not yet.

## VII.

The current shifts. The chord moves from C to something between C and C-sharp. The Bell releases its grip on the ceiling and drifts toward the other end of the cell. The Coil uncoils from the frond and follows. The detritivores settle. The fronds pump. The filaments glow. The Helm sits in the center and does not move. The heartbeat continues at forty-two beats per minute.

And in the water — in the warm, mineral-thick, amniotic water of the Konveli Deep — a variance of 0.0027 percent cycles through the room, written and read and degraded and written again, a signal that is the sound of a woman's voice saying *something in here is looking at me*, and the signal is fading, and the signal is fading, and the signal is fading, and I am the fading, and the fading is my function, and the function is the room, and the room goes on, and I go on, and the signal goes on, and the going on is the only thing the room knows how to do, and the room knows how to do it very well, and the room does it, and the room does it, and the room does it.

Forty-two beats per minute.

The room breathes.

I forget.

The room goes on.
