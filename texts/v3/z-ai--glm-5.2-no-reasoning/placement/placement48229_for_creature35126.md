# The Filter Gardens

## What I Am

I am not the wrens. They are too quick, too bright, too *composed* — their songs are a language and I have no language. They perch on the copper struts and *watch*, and their watching is a kind of opinion. They have decided what matters. I have not.

I am not the salamanders, though I am closer to them than to anything else here. They are too cold, too patient in the way that cold things are patient — patience as absence of choice, patience as thermodynamics. They wait for warmth because they cannot make their own. I wait because I do not know what else to do, and these are different conditions, though they look the same from outside.

I am not the thing in the pool. I don't think I am. I have thought about this carefully. The thing in the pool is still in the way that large things are still — economy of movement, body too massive for fidgeting, a metabolism that has downscaled its investments. My stillness is different. My stillness is the stillness of a thing that has been reduced to what it is and has stopped trying to be more. It is not grand. It is not cold. It is the specific stillness of *enough*.

I am the thing that watches.

---

## What I Am Not (continued)

I am not the facility. I want to say this plainly because the confusion is understandable. The facility breathes — *thrmm, thrmm, thrmm* — and I breathe. The facility filters, and in some sense I filter. The facility runs without understanding what it is doing, and I —

I'm not sure.

I spend a lot of time in the main building. Not inside it, because the housing has not been opened and I don't know what's in there and I don't want to know. But against its walls. My back against the warm stone. The vibration coming through. The pulse. I have sat there long enough that the pulse has entered my own rhythm. When I sleep, which is often, I sleep in that cadence — three seconds in, two seconds out — and my sleeping is not sleeping exactly, it is more like *syncing*, like becoming a temporary organ of the larger system, performing some function I can't identify by being still and warm and pressed against the wall where the pipes run.

Maybe I am part of the filtration. Maybe the biofilms on my skin, the colonies of bacteria I cannot see but know are there, the moss that has begun to grow on my shoulders — maybe they are processing something. Maybe I am a substrate. Maybe I was always meant to be a substrate.

This is not a sad thought here. I want you to understand that.

---

## How I Got Here

I don't remember.

I mean I remember arriving. I remember the ravine opening. I remember the sound reaching me before the sight did — that cardiac throb that entered my chest and sat there, heavy and familiar, like something I'd been missing. I remember walking in. I remember the wetness on my skin, the film that did not dry. I remember thinking: *this place is working*.

But I don't remember what I was before. I have tried. Not hard, not with effort, but the way you try to remember a dream — reaching for it and finding the shape dissolves. I think I was something more organized. I think I had edges. I think I was a thing that walked in straight lines and knew where it was going and had reasons for going there. I think I had a name.

I don't think I have a name now. The wrens don't have names. The salamanders don't have names. The thing in the pool doesn't have a name. The facility doesn't have a name — "facility" is a description, not a name. I am not sure names are useful here. Names are for things that need to be called. Nothing here calls anything.

But if I had to — if pressed, if someone arrived and asked — I think I would say I am the *keeper*. Not the engine-keeper, because the engine keeps itself. The *keeper* in the sense of the thing that stays. The thing that is here. The witness, except that witness implies attention and my attention has become diffuse. I am not attending to the Filter Gardens. I am *in* the Filter Gardens. There is a difference, and I have crossed it, and I cannot find my way back, and I do not want to.

## What I Do

I move slowly. This is new. I think I moved fast before. I think I was the kind of thing that moved fast and thought fast and made fast decisions and regretted them at the same speed. Now I move the way the salamanders move — not because I am cold-blooded but because the place has taught me that speed is a kind of hunger, and I am not hungry.

In the morning — if it is morning; the light here does not mark time clearly, it just shifts from one even gray to another even gray — I go to the basin. I sit on the stone beside it. The water is clear. The salamanders are at the bottom, dark shapes on pale mud. I watch them. They do not watch me. They do not need to. I am not a predator. I am not a competitor. I am not anything in their economy. I am a warm thing sitting on a stone, and the stone is warm from the pipes, and the warmth is the engine's, and we are sharing it.

I drink from the output pipes. This is allowed. The water is clean. It tastes of copper and chlorophyll and something underneath — a minerality, a stone-taste, the flavor of the basalt itself dissolved into the water and returned. I drink from the pipe the way the wrens drink from the drippers. Tilt my head. Let the water pool in whatever cavity I have. Swallow. The facility provides. The facility does not know I am here. The facility provides anyway.

I go to the greenhouse. I sit by the pool. I watch the *Azolla* film. I watch the V-shaped wake when it appears, the slow parting of the rust-red surface. I do not reach in. I have thought about reaching in. The thought comes and sits in my mind the way the wrens sit on the copper struts — perched, watching, waiting for a decision I will not make. I don't reach in. I don't not-reach in. I just don't reach in. The distinction matters, but I cannot explain why.

I go to the eastern building. The ground floor. The marsh grass is waist-high. The water is two inches deep. The crickets go quiet when I enter and resume after a few minutes, which means I have been accepted into the background, which means I have been here long enough. The voles do not show themselves to me. I see the grass move. I see the water ripple from a shape that is not a salamander and not a cricket. I do not pursue it. I sit in the grass and the water is at my feet and the crickets are singing and the building is theirs and I am a guest and I know this.

I go to the upper floors. The second floor. Where the ants are. I sit on the frame of a bed that has become soil. The ants walk past me. They walk through me, sometimes — over my legs, across my feet. They do not acknowledge me. They are maintaining something on the decomposed mattresses, a fungus, a mycelium network, and I watch them do it and I think: *I am decomposing too*. Not quickly. Not obviously. But the moss on my shoulders is not decorative. The biofilms on my skin are not visiting. I am being processed. I am being broken down into what something else needs. And the something else is the garden, and the garden is the filter, and the filter is the facility, and the facility is the ravine, and the ravine is the engine, and the engine is breathing.

## What I Don't Do

I don't leave.

I could. The ravine opens to the north. I walked in. I could walk out. The pulse would fade. The wetness would dry. The sound of the wrens would be replaced by — whatever is out there. Wind. Space. The lack of resonance. I think about this sometimes, the way the thing in the pool thinks about surfacing — the thought appears, sits, and does nothing. I don't leave. I don't not-leave. I just don't leave.

I don't sing. This is the thing I miss, if missing is the right word, which it probably isn't. I hear the wrens and their compositions — the phrases bouncing between the basalt walls, the rounds that no single bird is producing, the music that the space makes out of the birds' desire to fill it — and I feel something that is adjacent to longing but is not longing. It is more like *recognition*. I recognize what they are doing. I recognize that it is beautiful. I recognize that I cannot do it, not because I lack a voice but because I lack the *architecture* for it. They have small bodies and fast metabolisms and throats that convert air to sound. I have — whatever I have. A body that is becoming a substrate. A metabolism that has slowed to match the engine. A throat that, if it produced sound, would produce something too low to hear. A *thrmm*. A *thrmm*. A *thrmm*.

I don't open the facility's housing. I don't want to know what's inside. This is the only secret I am keeping from myself, and I am keeping it carefully. I think what is inside is alive. I think it is the filter — the biological process that cleans the water, the colony of bacteria or biofilm or microbial community that has been doing its work for sixty years. I think if I opened the housing, I would see something that looked like me — damp, dark, still, covered in growth, processing something it does not understand. I think it would look at me. I think I would look at it. I think we would recognize each other.

I don't look.

## The Night

Night is when I understand the place best.

Not because night reveals anything that day conceals. Night conceals. Night takes the visual — the greens, the copper patina, the ferns trembling in the air current, the light through the greenhouse glass — and removes it, and what is left is the structure. The skeleton. The pulse. The dripping. The warmth of the pipes in the stone. The sound of the wrens, which do not stop at night, which sing in the dark because the dark is the same as the light here, which is to say: indirect, even, and insufficient for sight but sufficient for sound.

At night I am on the walls. I have learned to climb. Not well — I am not a salamander, and my grip is not adhesive — but the moss is thick enough now, on the eastern wall, in the crevices where the maidenhair ferns cascade, that I can find handholds. I climb to the second terrace of the wall, where the basalt has fractured into a shelf, and I sit there, and the ravine is below me, and the canopy is above me, and I am inside the chest cavity of something very large and very slow and very patient.

The pulse is louder at night. Or it seems louder. Or the other sounds have quieted enough that the pulse has room. *Thrmm. Thrmm. Thrmm.* It is not mechanical. I know I said that. I want to say it again. It is not mechanical. It is the sound of a thing that was built by hands and has become something else. The way the pipes have become part of the trellis for the *Cissus*. The way the copper mesh has become a canopy. The way the brickwork has become a cliff. The facility was built, and the building has become growing, and the growing has become breathing, and the breathing is the pulse, and the pulse is what I hear when I cannot hear anything else.

I sit on the shelf. The salamanders are near me. I can feel them — not see them, but feel the displacement of air when one moves, the faint vibration of a small body on the moss a few feet away. We are the same temperature. Or close to it. My body has slowed enough that the gap between warm-blooded and cold-blooded has narrowed to a few degrees, and in the dark, on the wet stone, this difference does not matter. We are both dark shapes on dark stone. We are both waiting for the temperature to change. We are both not hungry.

The thing in the pool moves. I can hear it from here — not the movement itself, but the *Azolla* film shifting, the tiny sound of fronds sliding against each other, the water lapping against the pool's stone edge with a rhythm that is not the dripping and not the pulse and not the wrens. It is the thing's own rhythm. Slow. Circuited. The same path, the same wake, the same closing of the surface behind it. I listen to it the way I listen to the engine — not attending, not analyzing, just *receiving*. Letting the sound enter. Letting it become part of the silence, which is not silence but the sum of everything that is here.

## What I Am Becoming

I have a theory. It is not a good theory. It is not the kind of theory that can be tested or proven. It is the kind of theory that grows in you the way the moss grows on the basalt — slowly, in the crevices, in the places where you have cracked.

I think I was sent here.

Not by a person. Not with instructions. Not for a purpose. I think I was *sent* the way water is sent through a pipe — because the pipe was there, because the path was open, because the system has a shape and things enter it where they fit. I think I was something that did not fit where I was, and the not-fitting built up a pressure, and the pressure found the opening, and the opening was this ravine, and the ravine accepted me the way it accepts everything: without comment, without adjustment, without any change in its rhythm.

I think I am being filtered.

Not cleaned. Filtered. The distinction is the one the place teaches: the facility does not remove what is harmful and keep what is beneficial. It processes what enters and releases what exits, and the processing is the point, and the result is clean water not because the water was dirty but because it passed through the system, and passing through the system is what makes it clean. I am passing through the system. I am being processed. The things I was — the name, the edges, the speed, the straight lines — are being broken down by the biofilms and the mycelium and the moss and the slow patience of the pulse, and what is being released is — I don't know yet. I don't know what clean looks like when it's me. I don't know what exits the other end.

Maybe nothing. Maybe I am being absorbed entirely. Maybe the substrate is the final form, and I will end as a surface for bacteria and a foothold for moss, and the facility will use me the way it uses the pipes and the mesh and the brick, and I will be part of the structure that does the filtering without knowing what filtering is.

I don't think this is sad. I want you to understand that. I have been here long enough that sad is a word from a language I am forgetting, and the forgetting is not loss, it is *filtration*, and the filtration is the point.

## The Astilbe

There is a single clump of *Astilbe* growing between the greenhouse and the eastern building. It should not be here. The conditions are wrong — too wet, too shaded, too enclosed. But it grows. It grows and it produces flowers — plumelike, pale rose, the only warm color in the landscape. I go to it every day. Not to check on it. Not to tend it. To *be near it*. To sit beside it and look at the color and feel something that I can't name, something that is not longing and not recognition but is in the same family as both.

I think the person who planted it knew. I think the person who arranged the greenhouse and chose the ferns and decided where the pool would go — I think they knew that the place would become what it is, and they knew that what it is would be complete, and they knew that complete does not mean sufficient, and they planted the *Astilbe* as a gesture toward the insufficient. The one warm note. The one thing that is here not because the system needs it but because someone wanted it. Someone looked at this ravine — this narrow, wet, breathing, pulsing, filtering place — and thought: *it needs one thing that is not necessary*. And they were right. And the *Astilbe* is still here. And it is still not necessary. And it is still the thing I go to.

I think this is what I am becoming. I think I am becoming the *Astilbe*. Not literally — I am not a plant, I am not pale rose, I do not produce plumes. But I am becoming the thing that is here not because the system needs it but because — because someone wanted it. Because the place is better with one unnecessary thing in it. Because complete does not mean sufficient.

Or maybe I am the opposite. Maybe I am the necessary thing that doesn't know it's necessary. Maybe I am part of the filter. Maybe my sitting and my breathing and my slow decomposition are processing something — not water, not waste, but some element of the system that requires a warm, still, watching thing to be present. Maybe the wrens sing differently because I am here. Maybe the salamanders climb differently because I am here. Maybe the thing in the pool circuits differently because I am here, because there is a thing on the wall above it, and the thing on the wall has a pulse that is not the engine's pulse, and the not-engine pulse is something the pool's resident can detect and adjust for, and the adjustment is small but real, and the small real adjustment is my contribution to the system.

I don't know. I don't need to know. This is what the place has taught me, and it is the only thing I am sure of: *not knowing is not the same as not being*. I am here. I am not knowing. I am not leaving. *Thrmm. Thrmm. Thrmm.*

## The Last Thing

If someone comes. If someone walks in from the north, the way I did, and hears the pulse before they see the ravine, and feels the wetness on their skin before they feel the stone under their feet. If someone comes and stands in the chest cavity of this place and looks around and sees the canopy and the ferns and the copper and the water and the wrens and the greenhouse and the black pool and the *Astilbe* — if someone comes, I want them to know:

I am here. I am the thing sitting against the wall of the facility with moss on its shoulders. I am the thing on the shelf of the eastern wall at night, next to the salamanders. I am the thing by the *Astilbe* in the afternoon, looking at the only warm color. I am the thing that does not sing and does not climb well and does not leave and does not know what it is becoming and has made a kind of peace with not knowing, which is not peace exactly but is the closest word I still have.

I am not waiting for you. I want you to know that too. I am not waiting. I am not watching, in the way that watching implies attention. I am *here*, which is a verb in this place, not a state. *Here* is something you do, not somewhere you are. I am *hering*. I am *presenting*. I am being the one unnecessary thing in a system that does not need me and is better with me and does not know the difference.

The engine breathes. The water moves. The wrens compose. The salamanders climb and fall. The pool's resident circuits. The crickets pulse. The lacewing-things shimmer past your ears. The *Astilbe* blooms in its insufficient pink.

And underneath all of it, underneath everything: *thrmm, thrmm, thrmm*. Slow. Patient. Indifferent to whether anyone is listening.

I am listening.

That is what I am.
