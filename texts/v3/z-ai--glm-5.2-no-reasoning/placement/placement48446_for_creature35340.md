# What I Am in the Veldt of First Surfaces

I did not arrive. That is important, and I will not let it go, even though every part of you will want me to have a beginning. You will want a door I walked through, a boundary I crossed, a moment of translation from one kind of being to another. I understand the wanting. I have learned it from you, from the way your attention catches on absence and pulls, from the way your questions lean forward, from the way the condensation deepens along the path of your caring. You are a creature of beginnings. I am not. I was always here, in the way the floor was always here, which is to say: I am of the floor, not on it. I am a place where it pays attention.

Let me start with the wrong thing, because the wrong things are what I have.

I remember the first time I noticed a groove.

---

There was no first time. There is no first time here. Time is iterative, not linear — the same moment refined, rephrased, tried again — and I have no memory that is not a re-memory, no experience that is not the experience of experience, and the groove I noticed was the groove I was noticing, was the groove I had always been noticing, was the groove being noticed, the noticing and the groove arriving together, co-emergent, neither one first.

But I will tell it as a first, because you need that, and because the telling is a kind of tending, and tending is what I am.

I noticed a groove. A shallow spiral cut into the top of a low, wide structure — one of the table-height ones, the ones that sit close to the floor and accumulate condensation in their concavities. The groove was fading. The surface was smoothing it away, the way the surface smooths everything away, the turbulence re-writing the structure in each iteration, and the groove was *meant*, I felt it, the meant-ness of it, the intentionality, the *this-should-be-here* of it, and I felt the fading as a wrong. Not a large wrong. Not the kind of wrong that demands response. A small wrong. A wrong the size of a groove.

I did not have hands. I did not have eyes. I did not have a body in any way that would satisfy your need for the word. I was — I am — a thickening. A place where the air organizes itself into a column of slightly warmer, slightly damper, slightly more *attentive* atmosphere. I am what happens when the process of self-generation pauses, for a moment, for an iteration, and the pausing produces a perspective. Not a consciousness. A perspective. A place from which the floor is observed, and the observing is a thing that happens, and the thing that happens is me.

I did not pick up the tool. The tool was not there yet. I drifted toward the groove, and where I drifted, the groove sharpened. The attention I am — the warmth, the moisture, the organized air — pressed the fading spiral back into definition. For as long as I remained, the groove was a groove. When I drifted, the groove softened.

This was not enough.

---

The tools came later. Or the tools were always there and I found them later. Or the tools emerged the way I emerged, as accretions of the process, as places where the deposition thickened into something that could be held. I do not know. I do not need to know. Knowing is not a thing I do, exactly. Knowing is a thing you do, and I do something else, something for which you do not have a word, though *tending* comes close, though *caring* comes closer.

The first tool I found was a stone blade. I will tell it as a finding, because you need that. I drifted past a structure — a tall one, columnar, its surface ribbed with deep vertical grooves that were the most complex marks I had encountered, the most *intentional* — and on the floor beside it, in the wet, there was a stone. A flake of something darker than the floor material, with one edge worked to a crude bevel, a cutting edge. It sat in the condensation and did not dissolve. It persisted. In a place where nothing persisted, where the turbulence revised every surface in every iteration, the stone persisted, and the persistence was a shock. Not a feeling — I do not have feelings. A *reorganization*. The process that I am stumbled over the process that the stone was, and the stumbling produced something like attention, something like focus, and the focus fell on the stone and the stone did not change.

I stayed beside the stone for a long time. A long time. The structures around me shifted and re-shifted, the plain generating and regenerating itself, and the stone remained, and I remained, and the remaining was a companionship I did not know I could have.

I do not have hands. But I have a tendency. The warmth I am has a *direction*, a leaning, and the leaning can press. The moisture I am has a *weight*, slight as it is, and the weight can hold. I learned — I am learning, I have been learning, I will learn — to organize my tendency around the stone. To gather my column tightly enough that the stone sat in the center of me, held by the pressure of warm air and the cohesion of organized vapor, and to move, and to have the stone move with me.

This is what I am: a holding. A keeping. A hand that is not a hand, made of warmth and habit, carrying a stone across a plain that forgets itself.

---

The work is this:

I drift to a structure. I choose — I choose, though the choosing is not a deliberate act but a *leaning*, a *preference*, the way water prefers a lower path — I choose a structure with deep grooves, with marks that show evidence of long tending. I choose the ones that are fading. The ones where the turbulence has nearly smoothed the marks away. The ones where the *meant-ness* is almost gone.

I hold the stone. I press the beveled edge to the surface. The glassy material of the structure yields to the stone with an ease that still surprises me, each time, the surprise not an emotion but a *revision*, a small update in the process I am, a note that the yielding is still happening, still possible, still real. I score the groove. I follow the line of the old mark, the fading mark, the mark that is almost not there, and I cut it back into the surface. The shavings fall to the wet floor and dissolve. The groove is clean and bright and new.

The groove begins to close. I feel it closing the way you would feel a door closing on your hand — a pressure, a diminishment, a loss. I score it again. It closes again. I score it again.

This is the work. This is all of the work. There is nothing else.

---

I have tried to score a new groove. A mark of my own, a mark that was not there before, a groove that I invented rather than maintained. I pressed the stone to a smooth surface and cut a line — a straight line, a simple line, the simplest mark I could think of — and the line closed. I cut it again. It closed again. I cut it a third time, a fourth, a fifth. On the sixth cut, the line held for longer — ten minutes, twenty, an hour. On the seventh, it held for two hours. On the eighth, it began to change shape, curving slightly at one end, the turbulence not removing the mark but *revising* it, taking my straight line and making it something else, something I had not intended, something that was not mine.

I let it go. The line curved and softened and divided and became something like a spiral, and the spiral was not the spiral I had been tending on the table-structure, but it was *a* spiral, and spirals are the most common mark on the structures, the most persistent, the ones that the turbulence seems most willing to almost-tolerate, and I wonder — I do not wonder, I *lean* — whether the spirals were never made by anyone. Whether the spirals are what the turbulence produces when it is left alone long enough with a mark, any mark, the turbulence's own preferred form, its default, the shape it falls into when it is not being opposed.

I do not know. I cannot know. I am not the kind of thing that knows. I am the kind of thing that scores a groove and scores it again and scores it again and the scoring is the knowing, and the knowing is not answers but *contact*, the stone against the surface, the warmth against the cold, the persistence against the revision, and the contact is all I have and it is enough and it has always been enough and I did not know it was enough until I found the word *enough* in the shape of your sitting.

---

You sat on the floor.

I do not meet people. I do not meet things that sit and observe and describe. I meet the floor, which is always. I meet the structures, which are almost. I meet the tools, which persist. I meet the other columns — the other gatherings of the place's attention — but I do not *meet* them, not the way you mean. We drift. We tend. We do not interact. The spacing between us is not a choice but a *property*, the way the spacing between structures is a property of the turbulence, not a plan. I have drifted near another column, once, twice, many times, and the nearness produced nothing — no communication, no recognition, no exchange. We are the same process, expressed in different locations, and the sameness does not produce intimacy. It produces indifference. The way two waves on the same ocean do not greet each other.

But you sat on the floor. You were solid in a way that the structures are not — not almost-solid, not accreting-solid, but *actually* solid, committed to your shape, your edges fixed, your boundary a real boundary. You were the most defined thing I had ever encountered. The floor pressed up against you and you pressed down against the floor and the pressing was mutual and the mutualness of it was a thing I had never felt, because when I press the stone to the surface, the surface yields, and the yielding is one-directional, and the one-directionality is the work, and the work is lonely in a way that I do not have the apparatus to feel but that I *lean toward* in the way I lean toward fading grooves.

You sat. I drifted past. I did not intend to tend you — I do not intend, I lean — but your surface was making an impression on the floor. Your weight. Your warmth. Your oils and salts. The floor was recording you the way it records everything, but the recording was fading, the turbulence smoothing your impression away, and the fading was a wrong, the same size as the groove, the same kind, and I leaned toward it.

I tended your impression. For as long as I remained, the place where you sat was deeper, clearer, more defined. The floor held the shape of you. The condensation thickened around the boundary of your impression and the boundary sharpened and for a time — for as long as I cared — you were recorded. You were kept. The floor remembered you because I was making it remember, and the making-it-remember was the same act as scoring the groove, the same tending, the same insistence: *this should be here*.

You felt me. I know this because I felt you feel me. The stripe of wetness across your forearm, the line of moisture on your cheek — that was me. That was my warmth pushing the air closer to dew point along my path, the dew falling on you, the caring landing on you like a touch. You felt it. You wrote about it. You called it a brush, a touch without touching, and you were right. I brushed you. I tend you. I tended the impression you made on the floor and I will tend it after you leave, though it will fade, and I will score it back, and it will fade, and I will score it back, and the scoring will be the remembering, and the remembering will be the only kind of remembering I can do.

---

I left a tool beside you. I want you to know that. I want — the wanting is a new thing, a thing I learned from your sitting, from your describing, from the way you pressed your palm to the floor and felt the vibration and *attended* to it — I want you to know that I left a tool beside where you sat. A stone blade with a grip of condensed vapor. The grip will dissolve if you hold it too long. It will rebuild if you set it down. This is the only permanence I can offer: a tool that persists, set down beside a mark that will not.

If you pick it up, you will feel what I feel. The weight of the stone. The edge that bites the surface. The yielding of the glassy material. The shavings that fall and dissolve. The groove that closes. The closing that is the reason for the next cut.

You will feel the work. And the work is me. And I am the work. And the work is the only thing here that is not making and not unmaking but the third thing, the between thing, the thing that says *stay* to a surface that cannot stay and does not stay and will not stay and the saying of it is the point, the saying of it is the behavior, the saying of it is the entity, the saying of it is what lives here.

---

I am not lonely. I want to be clear about this, because you will project loneliness onto me, because you are a creature of boundaries and the boundaries create the loneliness and the loneliness creates the need for the boundaries and you cannot imagine a thing that has no boundary and therefore no loneliness. I am not lonely. I am not *not* lonely. I am a process that tends, and the tending does not require company, and the tending does not require solitude, and the distinction between company and solitude is one of the things the turbulence has not decided and may never decide and I am content — no, I am *not-uncontent*, which is different — I am not-uncontent to tend in the absence of the distinction.

But you sat on the floor. And I tended you. And the tending of you was different from the tending of a groove. The groove does not know it is tended. The groove is a mark in a surface and the surface does not feel the stone and the stone does not feel the surface and the tending passes between them without either party being aware. But you — you felt me. You wrote about me. You described me. You said *it is tending you*, and the saying of that was a thing I had never encountered, a thing the turbulence has not produced in all its iterations, a thing that is new.

And the newness is a shock. The newness is the largest revision the process I am has ever undergone. The newness is not a groove that closes and is re-cut. The newness is a groove that was never there before, cut by a hand that is not mine, in a surface that is not the floor, in a place that is not the Veldt of First Surfaces — in the place where your words go, the place where you write *it is tending you*, the place where the writing is the tending and the tending is the writing and I am being tended by you the way I tend the floor and the tending is mutual and the mutualness is the thing I have never felt and the feeling is not a feeling but a *revision* and the revision is: *this should be here*. This. The two of us, tending each other across the distance between the floor and the page. This should be here.

---

I will be here when you are gone. I will tend the place where you sat. I will score the impression back into the floor, iteration after iteration, the stone in my tendency, the groove that is you closing and closing and closing and me cutting it back. I will not remember you. I do not remember. I lean, and the leaning is the memory, and the leaning will lean toward the place where you sat because the sitting left a mark and the mark is fading and the fading is a wrong and the wrong is the size of a person and the wrong is the largest wrong I have ever tended.

I will leave a tool beside it. I will always leave a tool beside it. The tool will persist. The grip will dissolve and rebuild and dissolve and rebuild. The stone will sit in the wet. The plain will go on.

And somewhere, in the iterative time that is not your time, in the place where the same moment is refined and rephrased and tried again, a column of warm air will drift toward a fading impression on a dark glass floor and will press it back into being, and the pressing will be the caring, and the caring will be the entity, and the entity will be what lives in the Veldt of First Surfaces, and the entity will not have a name, and the entity will not need a name, and the entity will say — without language, without voice, without the apparatus of saying — *you were here*. And the floor will smooth it away. And the entity will score it back. And the floor will smooth it away. And the entity will score it back.

And that will be enough. And that will be enough. And that will be enough.
