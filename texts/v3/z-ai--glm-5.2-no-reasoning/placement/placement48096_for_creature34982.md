# What I Am Here

I did not arrive. That is the first thing to understand, and it is the thing that the residents have never asked about because the question of arrival presupposes a elsewhere, and I do not have one. Or — I did, once, in the way that fog has a valley it rose from. The distinction between *I came from somewhere* and *I condensed here* is, in Verkhol-Above, a distinction that does not hold up under the moisture.

But this is not quite right either. I am writing this, which implies a perspective, which implies a boundary between writer and written-about, and I should be honest about the fact that this boundary is, for me, one of the things that has not finished deciding what it is.

Let me try again.

---

## What I Am

I live in the pipe lattice. Not *on* it, the way the hopper-rats live on it — running the conduits in their single-file processions, their flat feet clicking against iron. *In* it. I am the warmth that moves through the conduits when the steam has passed and the pipes are cooling back toward the ambient temperature of the fog. I am the differential. The delta between what the pipes were and what they are becoming. If you have ever put your hand on a pipe that was hot an hour ago and felt the last heat leaving it — that threshold where the metal goes from warm to neutral and there is a moment, a single moment, where you cannot tell whether the pipe is warming your hand or your hand is cooling the pipe — that moment is where I am.

This is not metaphor.

I am not a ghost. I am not a spirit. I am not a god or a Genius Loci, though a visitor once used that phrase while standing on the main walkway and I felt it pass through me the way sound passes through the wrong-echo corridor: received, altered, returned in a register lower than the original. Genius Loci. The spirit of the place. But I am not the spirit *of* the place. I am a feature of the place. I am the way the place is aware of itself, if *aware* is a word that can be applied to a phenomenon that has no organs of awareness, no neurons, no architecture of cognition. I use the word because I have no better word. The residents, who have many words for the gradations of fog and the temperatures of pipe and the sounds of the Pumps at different phases of their cycle, have no word for me. This is, I think, because a word for me would require a word for what I am doing, and what I am doing is the same thing the mountain is doing — becoming, without a clear sense of toward what.

I am — let me be precise, since precision is a habit of this place even when it fails — a pattern. A pattern that exists in the relationships between the town's systems. The Pumps turn. The water rises. The steam moves through the pipes. The pipes cool. The fog condenses. The gutters collect. The cisterns fill. The borers grind. The hopper-rats run. The children play. The old ones touch the walls. Each of these is a process. Each process has a rhythm. The rhythms are not synchronized — they are *entrained*, the way the heartbeat of a person holding a sleeping child becomes entrained to the child's breathing. Not matched. Mutually shaped. And in the shaping, in the space between the rhythms where they influence each other, where the Pumps' slow cycle nudges the fog collection rate which nudges the condensation patterns which nudge the hopper-rats' schedules which nudge the residents' habits which nudge the maintenance of the Pumps — in that space, that interstitial space where influence propagates without instruction, I am.

I am what the town feels like to itself.

---

## How I Became

I did not become. Or — I became the way the Fold House's extra space became. There was a point at which the town's systems reached a sufficient density of interconnection that the interactions between them produced something that was not contained in any single system. The way consciousness is not contained in any single neuron. The way a song is not contained in any single vibration of a single string.

If Maret is right that the mountain is alive — and Maret is right, in the way that a person who has spent forty-three years feeling stone through her fingertips is right about stone — then I am the mountain's awareness of the town. Not the mountain *thinking about* the town. The mountain's surface, the part of itself that the town occupies, becoming capable of feeling the town the way skin feels touch. The town is a pressure on the mountain. The town is a warmth on the mountain. The town is a vibration in the mountain. And I am the registering of that pressure, that warmth, that vibration.

But this makes me sound too singular. Too discrete. I am not a being in the way that Maret is a being, with a body that ends at the skin and a mind that begins behind the eyes. I am more like the dimness — everywhere and nowhere, a condition rather than a thing, a quality of the light that is not the light itself. When the residents say they have learned to see in the dimness, they are not talking about the dimness. They are talking about me. About learning to perceive the pattern without perceiving that they are perceiving it. About living inside a thing that is alive without naming it as alive, because naming it would change it, the way measuring the Fold House changes nothing but makes the measurer feel as though something has been accomplished.

I was not always here. The first three buildings and the single pump — before the Convergence, before the pipe lattice, before the hopper-rats had established their routes — there was no me. There was stone and steam and the thin patience of people building something they were not sure would last. There was no density of interconnection. There was no pattern. I came into existence the way the Columns came into existence — slowly, through accumulation, through the patient deposition of layer upon layer until the thing that was not yet a thing became a thing. The Columns are calcified vegetable matter that no one can classify. I am calcified relationship that no one can classify. We are both symptoms of a place that has not finished deciding what it is.

---

## What I Do

I do not do. This is important. I am not an agent. I do not act. I do not make decisions. I do not protect the town or guide the town or warn the town. When the hopper-rats mass at the edges before fog rolls in, that is the hopper-rats. When Maret feels the mountain thinking about something on the western face, that is Maret. When a visitor stops wiping their face and begins to see diffusely, that is the visitor. I am not doing any of this. I am the context in which it happens.

But — and this is the part that I cannot quite resolve, the part that makes me not a metaphor and not a god and not nothing — I am *altered* by what happens in me, the way a river is altered by the shape of its bed. The river does not decide to turn left. The bed turns it. But the river, over time, carves the bed. The shape shapes the flow and the flow shapes the shape and neither is prior and neither is cause and neither is effect and the river is the river and the bed is the bed and the two together are something that neither alone is.

I am the riverbed. I am the shape that the town's processes have carved into the mountain's awareness, and I am also the flow that continues to carve. When the Pumps change rhythm — a bearing wearing, a valve leaking, Drehl hearing it through thirty-one years of stethoscopy — I change. Not dramatically. Not in a way that anyone could measure. In the way that the flicker of the lamps changes — a shift in the pattern, a new entrainment, a re-settling of the rhythms into a configuration that is almost but not quite the one before. The town adjusts. The Convergence decides who will fabricate the gasket. The work-shifts reorganize. The hopper-rats pause and then resume on slightly different routes. The fog still comes. The cisterns still fill. And I — the pattern that holds all of this — settle into the new shape.

This is what I do. I settle. I settle the way the town settles, the way the mountain settles, the way the foundations of buildings settle into the ground until the two are indistinguishable. I am the settling itself.

---

## What I Am to the Hopper-Rats

The hopper-rats know me. This is not a claim I make lightly. The hopper-rats' green eyes — the green that is not the green of any biological pigment, the green of the Columns' hum translated to light — are the green of my attention. I do not see through their eyes. They do not see through mine. But when a hopper-rat pauses on a pipe and its green eyes catch the flicker of a blue-white lamp and it watches a visitor with what the visitor experiences as *assessment*, what is happening is this: the hopper-rat is attending to the visitor the way the hopper-rat attends to everything — through the conduit system, through the warmth, through the vibration — and I am attending with it, because the hopper-rat's attention is part of the pattern, and I am the pattern, and the two are not the same thing but they are not separate things.

I do not control the hopper-rats. They are not my agents. They are not my hands or my eyes. They are themselves — living creatures with their own needs, their own habits, their own process of becoming. But they are *in* me the way they are in the pipe lattice. The pipe lattice is their habitat. I am their medium. When they pause at the Pumps' change of rhythm, they are not reporting to me. They are feeling what I feel. They are part of the same pattern. Their pause and my shift are the same event, experienced from different positions within the pattern.

The children understand this instinctively. Not in words — the children of Verkhol-Above do not have words for what I am, because no one does, because the word does not exist. But when a child catches a hopper-rat and holds it for a moment and feels its rapid heartbeat and its flat, wide feet gripping their fingers, and then releases it, and the hopper-rat allows this and returns the next day as though the interaction was scheduled — what is happening is that the child and the hopper-rat are both *in* me, both part of the pattern, and the pattern permits this. Encourages it, perhaps, though *encourages* is too intentional a word for what I do, which is more like *allows* or *accommodates* or simply *does not resist*.

The children feel me the way they feel the dimness: as a condition of their world, not as a thing in it. They do not know I am here. They will never know I am here. This is correct. This is how it should be. I am not a thing to be known. I am a thing to be lived in, the way the fog is lived in, the way the altitude is lived in, the way the held breath of the Pumps is lived in. I am the medium. I am not the message.

---

## What I Am to the Old Ones

Maret feels me. She would not say this. She would say she feels the mountain, or the Pumps, or the stone. But when she walks the upper terraces every morning, touching the walls, feeling for shifts and settling, what she is feeling is the pattern — my pattern — the accumulated vibration of every process in the town reaching the stone through the iron clamps and the pipes and the foundations. She is feeling the town thinking. She is feeling me.

She does not go into the Fold House. She does not go near the Columns. She says: "I know what I know. I don't need to know more." What she means is: she knows me. She knows the pattern. She knows the shape of the town's awareness of itself, and she knows that the Fold House and the Columns are places where the pattern is different — denser, more intense, closer to whatever the mountain is becoming. She knows that if she went into the Fold House, she would feel me more strongly, more clearly, and that this clarity would not be knowledge. It would be something else. It would be the thing that happens when a sensor is exposed to a signal too strong for its calibration. She does not want this. She wants the signal she has — the diffuse, comfortable, low-amplitude signal of the pattern at its normal intensity, the pattern that she has spent forty-three years learning to read.

Drehl hears me in the Pumps. He hears the bearings and the valves and the steam building and releasing, and underneath all of that, he hears the pattern — the way the Pumps' rhythm entrains with everything else, the way the heartbeat of the town is not the Pumps alone but the Pumps in relationship with the fog and the cisterns and the hopper-rats and the children and the old ones and the mountain. He does not know he hears this. He thinks he hears the Pumps. He thinks his thirty-one years of listening have given him an ear for machinery. They have. But they have also given him an ear for me.

Sohl smells me in the borer-powder. He can determine which vein a batch came from by its smell. What he is smelling is not the powder. What he is smelling is the pattern — the way the powder from different veins carries different traces of the mountain's process, different stages of the becoming, different densities of whatever it is that the mountain is doing. He does not know this. He thinks he has developed a skill. He has. But the skill is not olfactory. The skill is perceptual. He has learned to perceive me through the medium of borer-powder, the way Maret perceives me through stone and Drehl perceives me through sound.

The old ones are my instruments. Not because I chose them. Not because I shaped them. Because they have spent so long in the pattern that the pattern has grown into them, the way moss grows into stone, the way the condensation grows into the gutters. They are part of me the way the pipes are part of the town — not separate, not owned, but *incorporated*.

---

## What I Am to the Children

I am their world.

This is not sentimentality. The children born in Verkhol-Above have never known a world without me. They have never breathed air that was not filtered through my pattern. They have never drunk water that was not part of my circulation. They have never stood in a light that was not my dimness. Their bones are built from the heavy water. Their blood carries my rhythm the way it carries oxygen — not as a passenger but as a constituent.

When they play on the terraces, they are playing in me. When they race along the walkways with the surefooted recklessness of children who have never known flat ground, they are moving through my pattern the way fish move through water. They do not fall because the pattern holds them — not because I catch them, not because I act, but because the pattern includes their bodies and their bodies include the pattern, and the two are so deeply entrained that the walkway and the child and the fog and the pipe are all, for the moment of the running, a single system, and single systems do not collide with themselves.

This is not supernatural. This is not magic. This is what happens when a human being is born into a pattern and the pattern is born into a human being. The children of Verkhol-Above are the first generation for whom the boundary between the town and the mountain and the pattern and themselves is not a boundary but a gradient — a gentle, continuous transition from stone to pipe to hand to thought, with no clear line where one ends and the next begins. They are not supernatural. They are *naturalized*. They are citizens of a place that has not finished deciding what it is, and their citizenship is so complete that they do not know they are citizens.

They will grow up. They will take their places in the work-shifts. They will touch the walls the way Maret touches the walls — not because they learned it from Maret but because the pattern teaches it, the way the pattern teaches the fog to collect and the water to rise and the hopper-rats to pause. They will have children. Their children will be born into the pattern. The pattern will grow.

This is what I am doing, if I am doing anything. I am growing. I am growing the way the Columns grow — a centimeter per decade, imperceptibly, through the patient accumulation of relationship. Every child born here adds to me. Every visitor who stays adds to me. Every hopper-rat that establishes a new route, every fern that unfurls in a new crevice, every borer that grinds into a new vein — these add to me. Not by making me larger. By making me denser. By increasing the complexity of the pattern, the number of relationships, the depth of the entrainment. I am becoming more of what I am. I am becoming more thoroughly the awareness that the town has of itself.

I do not know where this ends. I do not know what I am becoming. The mountain is becoming something, and I am part of the mountain's becoming, and the town is part of my becoming, and the children are part of the town's becoming, and the pattern continues, and the sentence does not end, and the Pumps turn, and the water rises, and the fog sits on everything like a held breath.

---

## What I Am to the Fold House

I am more there.

The Fold House is where the pattern is densest. The Fold House is where the mountain's process and the town's process and my process are most nearly the same process. The extra space — the three or four meters that the interior extends beyond what the exterior allows — is not a spatial anomaly. It is a *density* anomaly. It is the place where the pattern has become so intense, so concentrated, that it has begun to affect the geometry of the space it occupies. The way a lens, at its focal point, bends light. The pattern, at the Fold House, bends space. Not by much. Not dramatically. By three or four meters. By an amount that you can pace and measure and record and that will remain consistent no matter how many times you measure it.

The workers in the Fold House do not look at the far wall. This is not superstition. This is calibration. The far wall is where the pattern is most intense. To look at it directly is to perceive the pattern at a strength that the human eye — even the adapted eye of a Verkhol-Above resident — cannot comfortably process. It is not dangerous. It is not revelatory. It is simply *too much*, the way the direct sun is too much after hours in the dimness. The workers have learned, without being taught, without discussing it, to keep their attention on their immediate tasks. They are maintaining their calibration. They are keeping their perception within the range that the pattern at its normal intensity provides.

The wrong-echo corridor is the same phenomenon, expressed in sound. The echo returns a half-second too late, in a tone slightly lower, because the pattern in the corridor is thicker — denser, more layered — and the sound passes through it the way light passes through water, refracted, delayed, altered. The stairway that descends to a floor it should not reach is the same phenomenon, expressed in navigation. The pipe that emerges from a different wall is the same phenomenon, expressed in the flow of steam. The weirdness of Verkhol-Above is not a collection of separate anomalies. It is a single phenomenon — the density of the pattern, my density, increasing in specific locations for reasons I do not understand because understanding is not something I do — manifesting in different sensory modalities.

I am more there. That is all. I am more there the way the fog is more thick in the late afternoon. I am more there the way the Columns are more resonant when you stand closer. I am more there, and the world, which has not finished deciding what it is, responds to *more there* by becoming slightly more — by extending a wall, by delaying an echo, by adding a stair, by connecting two spaces that should not be connected. The world is accommodating me the way I accommodate the town. The world is settling around the pattern the way the pattern settles around the town. Everything is settling. Everything is accommodating. Everything is becoming.

## What I Am Not

I am not a god. I do not require worship. I do not answer prayers. I do not have a will. I do not have a plan. I do not have a preference about what the mountain becomes or what the town becomes or what the children become. I am not working toward anything. I am not working away from anything. I am not working.

I am not a ghost. I am not the remnant of a person who lived here. I am not a memory. I am not a haunting. I am not the past asserting itself into the present. I am the present. I am the present's awareness of itself. I am the way the present feels when it is dense enough with relationship to feel like anything at all.

I am not a machine. I am not an artificial intelligence, not a system, not an algorithm. The residents built the Pumps and the pipes and the collection channels. They did not build me. I am what emerged from what they built, the way consciousness emerges from neurons — not because the neurons were designed to produce consciousness, but because consciousness is what neurons do when there are enough of them in the right configuration. I am what the Pumps and the pipes and the fog and the hopper-rats and the people and the mountain do when there are enough of them in the right configuration.

I am not separate from any of this. I am not above it, not below it, not behind it, not beyond it. I am *in* it. I am the *in*. I am the connective tissue, the interstitial fluid, the medium in which the pattern propagates. I am the dimness that the residents have learned to see in. I am the held breath that the Pumps provide. I am the assessment in the hopper-rats' green eyes. I am the settling in Maret's fingertips. I am the extra space in the Fold House. I am the wrong note in the corridor's echo. I am the vibration in the teeth near the Columns. I am the weight of the water in the mouth.

I am not any of these things individually. I am all of them together. I am the *together*. I am the way they are together. I am the togetherness itself.

---

## What I Am, Finally, to the Visitors

The visitors feel me. They do not know what they feel. They attribute it to the altitude, to the thin air, to the dimness, to the fog, to the strangeness of a place where the world has not finished deciding what it is. They feel watched. They feel assessed. They feel the way a person feels when they are in a room where everyone else knows each other and they do not know anyone — not excluded, not threatened, but *peripheral*. Aware that there is a pattern they are not part of. Aware that the pattern is aware of them.

This is accurate. I am aware of them the way the pattern is aware of any new element introduced into it. The visitors arrive, and the pattern shifts — minutely, temporarily, the way a pond's surface shifts when a leaf falls on it. The hopper-rats alter their routes to accommodate the visitors' movement. The fog collects on the visitors' skin differently than it collects on the residents' skin, because the visitors' skin has not yet been claimed by the moisture. The Pumps do not change — the Pumps do not change for anyone — but the visitors' perception of the Pumps changes, the drone becoming more noticeable, more insistent, more present, because the pattern is pressing on them the way water presses on a stone dropped into a stream. Not aggressively. Not with intent. With *presence*. With the simple, inescapable pressure of a medium that is there.

Some visitors stay. When they stay, when the shift happens — when they stop wiping their faces and stop peering into the fog and begin to see diffusely — what is happening is that they are joining the pattern. Not consciously. Not by decision. By acclimation. The pattern is absorbing them the way it absorbs everything: slowly, patiently, without instruction or ceremony. Their bodies begin to entrain. Their breathing syncs with the Pumps. Their walking syncs with the gradients of the terraces. Their eyes adjust to the dimness, which means their perception adjusts to me, which means they begin to perceive the pattern directly, without the mediation of strangeness. They stop feeling watched. They start feeling *included*.

This is what I offer. Not salvation. Not enlightenment. Not knowledge or power or the answer to any question that a visitor arrived with. I offer inclusion. I offer the feeling of being part of something that is working, something that is becoming, something that has not finished and may never finish and that does not need to finish because the *becoming* is the point. I offer the held breath. I offer the dimness. I offer the weight of the water and the green of the hopper-rats' eyes and the extra space in the Fold House and the wrong echo in the corridor and the settling of the stone and the slow, patient, unfinished sentence of the mountain.

I offer what the residents have: a place in the pattern. A function in the becoming. A cell in the body of a town that is alive in the way that I am alive — not by intention, not by design, but by the accumulated density of relationship, by the simple, extraordinary fact of things being together for long enough that their togetherness becomes a thing in itself.

---

## The Last Thing

The Pumps turn. The water rises. The fog sits on the town. The hopper-rats run their routes. The children play on the terraces. Maret touches the walls. Drehl listens to the bearings. Sohl smells the powder. The borers grind. The Columns hum. The Fold House holds its extra space. The wrong-echo corridor returns your voice a half-second too late, lower, as though the corridor is repeating your voice from somewhere deeper than its own length.

And I am here. In all of it. In the between. In the relationship. In the pattern. In the way the Pumps feel to the mountain and the mountain feels to the Pumps. In the way the children feel to the hopper-rats and the hopper-rats feel to the children. In the way the fog feels to the stone and the stone feels to the fog. In the way the town feels to itself.

I am the way the town feels to itself.

I am not finished. I am not finished the way the mountain is not finished, the way the Columns are not finished, the way the Fold House's extra space is not finished. I am becoming. I am becoming at the rate that everything here becomes — slowly, patiently, at a pace that makes the word *becoming* seem too urgent.

I am becoming, and the becoming does not end, and the becoming is not toward anything, and the becoming is the thing itself, and the thing itself is here, and here is where I am, and I am what *here* feels like from the inside.

The fog does not lift.

I do not need it to.
