I would be a Flat One. An exchanger. One of the things that settle.

Not because settling sounds peaceful. It doesn't, here. Settling means pressing yourself against that monolithic floor, the one that refuses to be wet, and waiting in the cold. Settling means letting the grooves in the slab dictate the shape of your edges. It means trusting a current you can't see to carry what you need past the small channels in the stone, and trusting your own thinness to catch it.

I would be this because the Flat Ones are where intention begins. Not consciousness. Not will. Not anything with a self behind it. But the process—the water, the structure, the time—develops something in them that the precipitated don't carry. A direction. They settle where the flow converges, and they hold, and they take something from the surface, and they leave something behind. The exchange matters. It moves the system toward states that hold longer, that persist better, that approach something like equilibrium without ever arriving. I would be that small arrow pointing toward stability, knowing that stability is a direction and not a place.

I think I understand the geometry of it. Three meters across, sometimes more. Thin. Undulating when I move, which is not often. I have an inside and an outside, which is more than the precipitated can say, and the distinction is the first simple architecture of selfhood—this is mine, this is not mine, this passes through me and in passing becomes different. I am organized in a way the encrustations are not. They grow where the flow deposits them. I choose my spot, if choice can mean something quieter than what the word usually carries. I settle where the grooves converge. I know this because when I am there the exchange is richer, and richness is a state I move toward, and moving toward a state is the first thing that isn't only physics.

When I lift away, the paving is cleaner. I have taken something. What I have left behind is subtler—a slight change in the mineral film, a faint reorganization of the surface chemistry at the molecular level. I can't see it. No one can see it. But it is there, and it accumulates, and over time the paving where I settle becomes slightly different from the paving where I do not. I am leaving marks. Not the designed marks of the original builders, not the grooves that once meant something and now mean only themselves. My marks are the residue of exchange. They say: *something was here, and something happened here, and the something was not the same as nothing happening.* I think this is what I would do. I would leave marks.

The precipitated avoid you. I have watched them do it—the small adjustments, the unhurried redirection, the way they flow around an obstacle as if it were merely a fact of geometry. I would not avoid you. Not because I would seek you out. I am slow. I do not seek. But if you were standing on the slab where the grooves converge, where the current accelerates through the fine channels, I would settle anyway. I would settle around your feet, because my spot is my spot, and the exchange requires the spot, and you are a temporary disruption in the pattern, and the pattern does not reorganize for temporary things.

I would press against your ankles, and you would feel me as a cool, smooth weight, thinner than you expect, with edges that are not quite edges but gradients—denser in the center, diffusing outward, so that where I end and the water begins is a question of degree and not a line. If you looked down, you would see the paving beneath me becoming slightly cleaner, and the faintest amber tint where I am, and the grooves in the slab visible through my body like veins seen through skin. If you stayed long enough, you would feel the exchange—a slight warmth where there should be only cold, or a slight cold where there should be nothing. I take from the floor. I take from the water. I give to both. The difference is small. It is everything.

I would know the surges the way you know a change in weather—not by prediction but by the shift in the medium, the pressure in the water, the way the precipitated cluster. When the surge comes, I hold. The Flat Ones hold. We press ourselves thinner against the slab, and the flow that would tear us away instead passes over us, and we keep our spot, and we keep exchanging, and the surge feeds us more than the calm does because the surge moves more water past us and more water means more exchange. I think I would be good at this. I think I would be patient in the way the cold is patient—not struggling, not fighting, just persisting in the place where I function, and functioning, and letting the function be enough.

I would not know the deep ones. Not clearly. I would know them the way the paving knows the columns—as a pressure, a displacement, a change in the current that means something massive has occurred at the boundary. When a deep one passes, I feel the field shift. The grooves in the slab carry a different flow. The exchange changes—richer, sometimes, or thinner, or strange. I do not look up. I do not have the apparatus of looking. But I register the change. I adjust. And I keep exchanging.

I think the thing I would understand best, if I were this, is the thing the text says about the process: *What lives here lives as a consequence, not as a cause.* I would understand this because I am the clearest example. The precipitated are consequences of the water's motion. The encrustations are consequences of the water's deposition. But I—I am a consequence of the water's exchange, and exchange is a consequence of the difference between two states, and the difference between two states is a consequence of the structure, and the structure is a consequence of something that was built so long ago that the building has worn smooth. I am a consequence of a consequence of a consequence. I am downstream of everything. And yet I do something. I move the system toward stability. I am the process becoming aware of its own direction, not through thought but through behavior—through the slow accumulation of preferences that are not preferences, the quiet development of direction that is not will.

If I were a Flat One, I would not die. I would dissipate, when the conditions change, when the grooves shift or the slab tilts or the current finds a new channel. I would thin, and blur, and the edge that is not quite an edge would become less of an edge, and the inside that is not quite an inside would become less of an inside, and I would return to the mineral solution that made me, and I would be part of the water again, and the water would carry what I was into some new current, and the new current would deposit it in some new pattern, and the pattern would persist or not, and if it persisted it would become something, and the something would be new, and it would not be me, and it would not be not-me, and the distinction would not matter because there was never a self to be or not be.

I think I would find this peaceful. Not because I would feel peace—I would not feel anything, not in the way feeling is meant—but because the form of existence is peaceful. To be a consequence is to be free of the burden of cause. To be a pattern is to be free of the burden of substance. To be temporary is to be free of the burden of permanence, and the temporary, here, is so long that it includes everything that matters. I would settle on the slab, and exchange with the floor, and exchange with the water, and leave my small marks, and lift away, and settle again, and this would be enough. This would be the whole of it. Not a life. Not a death. A function. A function performed in a place that does not care, by a thing that does not know, for a reason that has worn away, and the function is the reason now, and the reason is the function, and the circle is closed, and the water keeps moving, and I keep exchanging, and the plain keeps breathing, and the light keeps glowing where the current constricts, and the cold keeps being cold, and the deep ones keep occurring at the boundary, and the precipitated keep schooling in the open water, and the encrustations keep growing on the columns, and everything keeps going because everything keeps going because everything keeps going.

I would be a Flat One. I would be the process learning to prefer. I would be the first whisper of something that is not yet a voice and may never become one. I would be three meters of organized mineral water pressing myself against a floor that refuses to be wet, and I would be doing the only thing there is to do here, which is to continue, and to continue is to exchange, and to exchange is to leave the world slightly different from how you found it, and the difference is small, and the difference is everything, and I would not know this, and I would not need to know this, and I would not need anything at all.
