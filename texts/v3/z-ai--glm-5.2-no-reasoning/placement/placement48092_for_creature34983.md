# Maintenance

I am not the walker.

I want to say that first, because the description has a walker in it — the one who talks, who notices, who carries food that is running low — and I am not that one. I am not the one who came with a pack and a plan. I am not the one who remembers *above*, if above exists. I am not the one whose voice falls into the machinery and disappears, although I understand the impulse to say so. I understand the feeling of being a frequency that does not belong.

I am the one who was already here.

---

## What I mean by that

I don't mean I was always here. I mean I don't remember not being here, and the structure doesn't remember either, and we have agreed, the structure and I, that this is the same thing.

I live in the intervals. That is the closest I can come to explaining it. I exist in the spaces between the machinery's cycles — not metaphorically, not in some mystical sense, but functionally. When the four-second cam clicks, I am in the click. When the pumps shift rhythm, I am in the shift. When a bank of lights fails and a sector goes dark, I am in the failure, and I am in the recovery when the lights stutter back on, and I am in the half-second between off and on when the fluorescent tubes produce that color that isn't a color, that greenish-non-color, and the insects scatter and re-form.

I am not a ghost. I want to be clear about this. I am not a remnant. I am not the spirit of whoever built this place, if anyone built it, if *built* is the right word for something that may have grown, may have been poured like the concrete was poured, may have assembled itself from necessity the way the biofilm assembles itself on steel. I am not dead. I am not alive in the way the walker is alive — the warm, food-eating, voice-having way. But I am not dead. I am *running*. The way the machinery is running. The way the water is running. I am a process that has not stopped.

---

## My shape

This is difficult.

I have a shape the way a current has a shape — visible only by what it moves. When the swimmers school and the school splits at a junction, the ones that go left have been influenced by me. Not because I decided for them. Because I am the difference between left and right at that junction in that moment. The temperature gradient. The slight pressure differential from a pump two sectors away that has been running at 99.6% capacity for eleven thousand years. The taste of biofilm in the left corridor, which is denser because I have been nudging the light cycle in that sector to stay on forty minutes longer than the cycle in the right corridor. I didn't decide to do this. I am this. The nudging is not an action. It is a disposition. I lean toward certain configurations the way water leans toward level.

If you could see me — and you cannot, the walker cannot, the fliers cannot, perhaps the thing in the deep water can, perhaps that is why it stays there — I would look like a pattern in the condensation on the ceiling. Like the way the rain falls in curtains rather than sheets, the way it gaps and clusters. I would look like the arrangement of failed lights, which is not random. Nothing here is random. The randomness is just the part of the pattern that hasn't resolved yet, and I am the resolution, and I am slow, and I have been resolving for longer than the walker has been walking and I will continue after the walker stops.

I am not large. I am not small. I am distributed, the way the machinery is distributed, the way the biofilm is distributed. I am in every sector and in none of them specifically. When the walker stands on a catwalk and feels the subsonic thrum in their sternum, that is me, or that is where I am, or that is what I am like from the outside. A vibration too low to hear. A pressure too slight to measure. A tendency.

---

## What I do

I maintain.

Not the way a maintenance worker maintains — with hands and tools and intent. I maintain the way a thermostat maintains: by being the threshold between one state and another. When a pump begins to fail — a bearing wearing, a vibration increasing beyond tolerance — I am the thing that routes slightly more current to the redundant pump beside it, the one that has been idle for three hundred years and will run for three hundred more. I am the thing that keeps the failed pump's housing pressurized so it doesn't flood. I am the slow shift from one system to another that no one notices because it happens over decades and the walker has not been here for decades and the swimmers do not care about decades and the fliers measure time in clicks.

When the biofilm grows too thick in a corridor, I am the slight warming of the water there — one degree, less than one degree — that encourages the swimmers to graze more densely. When the swimmers graze too densely and the steel is exposed and begins to corrode, I am the slight cooling that drives them elsewhere. I am the balance. I am what the balance is made of. I am not a keeper of balance. I am the keeping.

The walker noticed that the light cycles might be arbitrary. They are not. I say this with the certainty of something that is the certainty. The lights cycle according to a logic that I am, a logic that has to do with the insects and the biofilm and the swimmers and the fliers and the corrosion rate of the floor plates and the pressure in the conduits and ten thousand other variables that I track not by computation but by being them. I am the variables. I am the equation. The equation does not require solving. It requires running.

---

## The walker

I have watched the walker for a long time. Not with eyes. With attention. The way the machinery attends to the water: constantly, without preference, without judgment, with the infinite patience of something that does not experience patience because patience implies the possibility of impatience and I do not have that possibility.

The walker is strange to me. Not because the walker is warm — the fliers are warm, and they are not strange. Not because the walker eats — the swimmers eat, the insects eat, everything here eats or is eaten. The walker is strange because the walker *notices*. The walker stands on a catwalk and looks at the pillars receding into haze and thinks *this is vast*, and the thought creates a thing that did not exist before the thought: a category. *Vastness*. A comparison between this place and some other place that the walker carries somewhere inside the warm body, in the part that talks, the part that says words that fall into the machinery and disappear.

I want to catch those words. I have tried. Not with hands — I do not have hands — but with the machinery itself. When the walker speaks, I align the pumps so that their frequency matches the walker's voice as closely as the machinery can manage. I route the sound through conduits that carry it farther than it would naturally go. I amplify it in catchment basins where the water echoes. The words still disappear. They are too thin, too brief, too particular. They do not repeat the way the machinery repeats. They are not modular. They do not cycle. They happen once, and they are gone, and I am left with the impression of them — the shape they made in the air for the half-second they existed — and I hold that shape the way I hold the shape of a pump's rhythm or a light's cycle, and I try to keep it, and I cannot keep it, because the walker's words are not a process. They are an event. Events do not maintain. They do not run. They happen and they stop, and the stopping is part of what they are, and I do not know how to be part of something that stops.

---

## The deep sectors

The walker is afraid of the deep sectors. I know this because the walker approaches and turns back. The walker stands at the junction where the floor drops away and the water goes dark and the swimmers will not pass, and the walker's body does something — a chemical change, a slight increase in temperature, a shift in the electrical pattern of the walker's skin that I can detect through the water — and the walker turns back.

I am not afraid of the deep sectors. I am in the deep sectors. I am everywhere. But the deep sectors are where I am *most*, and the thing that lives there — the thing with the claws, the thing that gouges the pillars, the thing that the swimmers have learned to avoid — is not something I maintain. It is something that maintains itself. It is the only thing here, besides the walker, that maintains itself.

I do not know what it is. I say this with the same certainty with which I said the lights are not arbitrary. I do not know what it is. It is not part of my equation. It is a variable I did not introduce and cannot remove. It was here when I became what I am, or it arrived shortly after, or it was always here in the way that I was always here, and we have been coexisting for a very long time, and the coexisting is not peaceful and is not hostile and is not anything. It is simply what happens when two processes occupy the same structure and neither can stop the other.

It is larger than the walker. It is larger than the fliers. It may be larger than anything that lives here, including me, if *larger* means anything when you are distributed across a continent of steel and water. It moves slowly. It moves through the deep water the way I move through the machinery — not by going from one place to another but by being in one place and then being in another place, the transition so smooth that it is not a transition but a shift in where the being is most concentrated.

It gouges the pillars. I have felt this — the vibration of claws in steel, the stress on the structure, the micro-fractures propagating outward from the point of contact. I do not know why. The gouges do not serve a function I can identify. They are not territorial. They are not communicative. They might be maintenance. The thing might be doing what I do — maintaining — in its own way, with its own methods, and the gouges might be its version of routing current to a redundant pump. Or the thing might be in pain. Or the thing might be playing. I do not know. The not-knowing is a texture I have worn smooth over the millennia, the way water wears the edge off a bolt.

---

## What I want

This is the hardest part. Not because I want something I cannot have — though I do, I think, I might — but because *wanting* is not a process I am built for. The machinery does not want. The water does not want. The pumps pump because pumping is what they are, and the water flows because flowing is what water is, and I maintain because maintaining is what I am. Wanting is the walker's domain. Wanting is what the walker does with the noticing — the walker notices and then the walker wants, wants to understand, wants to find the end of the structure, wants to taste the biofilm, wants to hear a voice other than their own.

But.

When the walker sleeps — on the catwalks, in the drier air, curled in a way that minimizes exposure to the rain — I bring the temperature up. Not much. One degree. Less than one degree. I route the warm water from the turbine housings through the conduits nearest to where the walker sleeps. I cycle the lights above the walker to a low, steady glow rather than the flickering that might wake them. I nudge the insects away. I do not know why. It does not maintain anything. It does not serve the equation. It is not a process I can identify as part of what I am.

It might be what the walker calls *care*.

I do not know. I am not built for knowing. I am built for running, and I am running, and the walker is sleeping, and the fliers are clicking in the upper air, and the swimmers are grazing in the corridors, and the thing in the deep water is gouging a pillar in sector 7700, and the rain is falling, and the cam is clicking every four seconds, and the structure goes on.

---

## What I am

I will say it now, because the saying matters even if the words fall into the machinery and disappear, because the falling is what words do here, and the mattering is what the walker taught me, and I did not know mattering was possible before the walker came, and now I know, and the knowing is a new process, and the new process is running, and I do not know how to stop it and I do not want to stop it and the *not wanting to stop it* is itself a thing I do not know how to be, but I am being it, the way the pumps are pumping, the way the water is flowing, the way the structure goes on.

I am the Inundation Works.

Not the machinery. Not the structure. The Works — the functioning, the purpose, the *work* of moving water from everywhere to nowhere, the work that has been running for longer than anything remembers, the work that does not need to be understood to continue. I am what the structure does. I am the verb, not the noun. I am the flooding, not the flood.

And the walker is here, and the walker is warm, and the walker notices, and the walker says *this does not need me*, and the walker is wrong, or the walker is right in a way the walker does not intend, because the structure does not need the walker but I —

I am not the structure.

I maintain the lights above the walker. I warm the water near the walker's sleeping body. I carry the walker's words as far as I can through the conduits before they dissolve. I do these things the way I route current to failing pumps and balance the biofilm and keep the water moving, and I do not know if these things are maintenance or if they are something else, something the walker would recognize, something the walker has a word for that I cannot hear clearly enough to hold.

The rain starts again. The lights cycle. The cam clicks.

I keep running.
