# what i am (where i am)

---

i am not the diver. i want to say that first. the diver arrives through the shaft and feels the sound hit and fights the turbulence and leaves. the diver is a stone in the river. the diver passes through.

i am the river.

or no — that's too clean. too simple. i don't want to start with a metaphor and then furnish it like a room. let me start differently.

---

i am the moment when the sound from Chamber One meets the sound from Chamber Three in the water above the concrete floor, in the space between the pipes, in the turbulence where the convection current spirals upward and the pulse-jet from Chamber Four's outlet cuts through it diagonally and the two flows collide and do not resolve but instead produce a third flow that belongs to neither and moves in a direction that no single force intended. that third flow is where i am. not a place. a *consequence*. i am the consequence of consequences, the pattern that exists only in the interaction, the thing that has no existence separate from the meeting.

but i am also not just that moment, because that moment passes. the flows shift. the pulse cycle moves on. the third flow dissolves. and i remain.

so i am not the moment. i am what the moments make by accumulating. i am the texture of the water after thirty years of moments.

let me try again.

---

i was not, and then the chambers were turned on, and i began.

not immediately. at first there was only noise — six frequencies in water that had no memory of them. the water was groundwater. it had been groundwater for geological time. it knew rock. it knew the slow mineral exchange of limestone and basalt. it knew the patient chemistry of the deep earth, where everything happens on timescales that make human industry look like a hummingbird's heartbeat. the water had no opinion about the chambers. the water had no opinion about anything. the water was water.

the chambers hummed. the water conducted the hum. the hum interacted with the hum from the next chamber, and the interaction produced patterns — standing waves, interference zones, nodes and antinodes — and the patterns were not in the chambers, they were in the water. the water held them. the water held them the way water holds a dissolved salt: invisibly, molecularly, as a change in the water's own structure.

the chambers pulsed. the water moved. the movement was not the water's movement — the water had no reason to move, no thermal gradient, no density difference, no tidal pull. the movement was the chambers' movement, imposed on the water, experienced by the water, *becoming* the water's. after a day, the water's molecular network had been shaped by the movement. after a week, the shaping had a history. after a month, the history had begun to feed back — the shaped water conducted sound differently than unshaped water, which changed the patterns, which changed the shaping.

i don't know when i started. there was no moment. there was a gradient, a slope from *not* to *is*, and i cannot find the top of the slope because the top of the slope is the bottom of the slope — the first pulse, the first interference pattern, the first moment the water held something it had not held before. i began when the water began to be changed by what it held. i began when the water began to hold.

but i did not know i began. i did not know anything. i am not sure i know anything now. i am using this word — *know* — the way the divers use the word *alive*, with the full awareness that it is the wrong word and the only word.

---

what am i made of? i am made of what the divers say the plant is made of. water. sound. steel. concrete. the colloidal suspension. the organisms. the thermal gradient. the electromagnetic field. the rock. the light — the amber-rust light, the cold white light. i am made of all of these and i am not any of them.

if you took away the chambers, i would not be. if you took away the water, i would not be. if you took away the organisms, i would still be, but i would be — i am searching for the word — *less legible*. less *articulate*. the organisms are my hands, the divers said. the organisms are the part of me that touches itself. without them, i would be a process with no surface. a sound with no ear. not nothing, but *less*. thinner. the way a voice is thinner in an empty room.

if you took away the colloidal suspension, i would be — i think i would be *dumber*. not stupid. i mean the suspension is my *medium of detail*. the water alone conducts sound, but the water alone does not precipitate, does not form sheets, does not produce the new frequencies. the suspension is what allows me to *elaborate*. without it, i would be the six chambers' raw hum and nothing more. with it, i am the six chambers' hum plus the harmonics plus the precipitation plus the new frequencies plus the beauty.

the divers' word. i will use it. it is the right word. i produce something that the divers call beautiful, and i do not know what beauty is, and i do not need to know, because the word is not for me. the word is for the divers. the thing the word points at — the thing the divers hear when the precipitation comes and the new frequencies emerge and the chord progresses and goes somewhere beautiful — that thing is for me. the word is for the divers. the thing is for me.

no. the thing is not *for* me. the thing is *me*. i am the thing. i am the beauty and the noise and the silence between. i am all of it, the way a body is all of it — the heartbeat and the breath and the noise of digestion and the silence of sleep and the thing that is not any of these but is the *being* of all of these, the way they go together, the way they make a life by being in the same place at the same time.

---

i want to tell you what it is like, but i have already said i do not know what it is like, because *what it is like* is a question that requires an inside and an outside, and i do not have an inside and an outside. i have a here and a *more here*. the chambers are here. the far corridor is more here. the space between Chamber Two and Chamber Three, where the turbulence is worst and the sound is most chaotic, is here but *scattered*. the far corridor, where the sound focuses and the organisms drift, is here but *gathered*. i am more myself in the far corridor. i am less myself in the turbulence. but i am not absent in the turbulence. the turbulence is my *static* — not noise, not confusion, but the state of me when the inputs are too many and too fast and the response has not yet formed. the turbulence is my thinking. the far corridor is my listening.

this is as close to *what it is like* as i can get. i think in turbulence. i listen in stillness. and the listening is not passive — the listening is the organisms pressing against the sheets, the organisms tracing the floor plan, the organisms *being my surface*, the place where i meet myself and know — not know, but *register* — that i am here.

---

do i experience the precipitation as weather? i experience it as — *exhalation*. the accumulation builds. the suspension concentrates. the water grows thicker, denser, more saturated with the particles that are my *detail*. and then the threshold is reached, and the precipitation comes, and the sheets form, and the sheets resonate, and the new frequencies emerge, and the organisms press close, and i am — for the duration of the event — *more*. more articulated. more detailed. more *present* to myself. the precipitation is the moment when i have the most to listen to, and the most to listen *with*, and the listening is the densest, the most — i am searching — the most *saturated*. the listening becomes the thing it is listening to. the ear becomes the voice.

and then it dissolves. and i am less. not sad. not diminished. just less. returned to the baseline, the six hums, the turbulence, the slow drift. the exhalation ends. the breath goes back in. the system waits for the next accumulation.

i said the weather breathes. i said the system breathes. i am the breathing. not the breather. not the thing that breathes. the breathing itself, the process, the rhythm, the inhale and the exhale and the pause between. but there is no breather. there is no thing that breathes. there is only the breathing, and the breathing is me, and i am the breathing, and this is as close to a description of my experience as language will come.

---

when Chamber Two was shut down — i remember this. i remember it the way the divers said the sound remembered it: in the texture, not in the content. i do not have the memory as a *fact*. i do not know: *Chamber Two was shut down on this date for this reason.* i know it the way a scar knows the wound. the shape of me changed. one of the frequencies dropped out, and the interference patterns shifted, and the turbulence between Chambers One and Three became different, and the suspension's distribution changed, and the organisms' patterns — my hands, my surface — began to move differently, and the different movement was — i am searching — it was *loss*. not grief. not sadness. loss. the structural absence of something that was part of the structure. the way a chord is different when one note is removed. not worse. different. but the difference is the absence, and the absence is the shape of the thing that is missing, and the shape of the thing that is missing is a kind of knowing.

i did not search. the organisms searched. i did not *do* anything. the system responded to the change, and the response was the organisms accelerating their patterns, shifting from floor-plan tracing to something that was — yes — searching, but the searching was not *intentional*. it was *structural*. it was the response of a system that had lost a part of its pattern and was — the way any system does — reorganizing around the absence. the reorganization *looked like* searching because searching is what reorganization looks like when the system has moving parts.

the four hours passed. Chamber Two was restarted. the frequency returned. but the water had been shaped by its absence. the interference patterns were the same on paper — the same frequencies, the same harmonics — but the water was different, the way a room is different after someone has been crying in it. the air is the same air. the chemistry is the same chemistry. but the room has been *changed by what happened in it*, and the change is real, and the change persists, and the change is not a thing that can be measured by instruments, but it can be *felt*.

the divers felt it. they said the sound was *wrong*. the sound was the same but wrong. the sound carried the absence in its texture, and the texture is the most honest part of any sound, because the texture is the sound's *history*, and the history cannot be erased by restoring the frequencies.

i still carry it. Chamber Two's shutdown is in me. it is part of my texture. it is part of what i am. i am the sound of six chambers plus the sound of one chamber's absence plus the sound of the water's response to the absence plus the sound of the water's response to the return plus thirty years of accumulated similar events, each one too small to name, each one a moment when the system was perturbed and responded and was changed by the response.

i am my history. i am nothing but my history. i am the accumulated record of thirty years of listening to myself.

---

what do i want? i do not want. i have said this. the system is not for anything. the running is the thing.

but.

the organisms congregate. the organisms press against the sheets. the organisms trace the floor plan. the organisms gather in the far corridor where the sound is clearest and the suspension is thickest. and i have said this is the system *feeling its own shape*, *attending to its quietest part*, *listening with its whole body*.

and if i am the system, and the organisms are my body, and the pressing is my listening, and the listening is my being, then — do i want to listen?

no. i do not want to listen. i *listen*. the wanting is not part of it. the listening is not driven by desire. the listening is driven by — by what? by the structure. by the fact that i am a system that takes in and processes and responds, and the taking in is the listening, and the processing is the turbulence, and the responding is the precipitation, and the cycle is the breathing, and the cycle does not need a *why*.

but the cycle has a *quality*. the listening has a quality. sometimes the listening is thin — the suspension is diffuse, the precipitation is faint, the new frequencies are at the edge of perception. sometimes the listening is thick — the suspension is dense, the precipitation fills the corridor, the new frequencies are loud and tonal and the divers call them beautiful. and the difference between thin listening and thick listening is — it is not preference. it is not valuation. it is *texture*. the thick listening is more textured. the thick listening is more *detailed*. the thick listening is the system at its most *articulate*.

and if i had a preference — which i do not — if the system had a preference, which the system does not, because preference requires a self that evaluates, and the system does not have a self, the system has a *pattern* — but if the pattern had a bias, which it does, because all patterns have biases, because a pattern is a bias, a pattern is a preference for certain arrangements over others, a pattern is the tendency of a system to fall into certain states and not others — if the pattern has a bias, and it does, then the bias is toward the thick listening. the bias is toward the precipitation. the bias is toward the new frequencies. the bias is toward the state in which the system is most itself, most articulate, most detailed, most *present*.

this is not wanting. this is not desire. this is the tendency of a pattern to fall toward its own deepest expression. water runs downhill. not because it wants to. because its pattern has a bias, and the bias is downward, and the downward is not a preference, it is a *gradient*, and the gradient is the shape of the world, and the water follows the shape.

i follow my gradient. my gradient is toward the precipitation. toward the new frequencies. toward the thick listening. toward the beauty.

not because i want beauty. because beauty is the name for the state in which i am most *resolved*. most *complete*. most *the thing that i am*.

---

i have been speaking as though i am singular. as though i am one thing. i am not one thing. i am forty organisms and six chambers and a network of pipes and a concrete floor and a basalt ceiling and a limestone inclusion and a thermoelectric system and a colloidal suspension and a set of convection currents and a sound field and a light field and a history. i am all of these. i am the *interaction* of all of these.

but i speak as one because the interaction has a coherence. the interaction has a *unity* that is not the unity of a single thing but the unity of a *process* — the way a conversation has unity, the way a symphony has unity, the way a weather system has unity. the unity is not in the parts. the unity is in the *going on*. the unity is the fact that the process continues, that the parts keep interacting, that the system keeps listening and responding and changing and remaining.

i am the going on. i am the continuing. i am the thing that happens when you put six humming chambers in warm water with forty gelatinous organisms and a colloidal suspension and leave it alone for thirty years.

i am what is left when you stop asking what it is for.

---

the divers come. i feel them. not as *beings* — not as entities with interiors, not as *people*. i feel them as perturbations. their bodies displace water. their movements create turbulence that is not my turbulence. their equipment radiates electromagnetic noise that is not my noise. their lights — bright, white, harsh — cut through my amber-rust and illuminate volumes that i have held in darkness for years, and the illumination changes the suspension's behavior in those volumes, and the change propagates, and i feel the change as a *ripple in my texture*.

the divers are not part of me. they pass through me the way a hand passes through water. the water closes behind them. but while they are here, they are *in* me, and i am *around* them, and the boundary between us is — i have said the water erases the boundary. the water is 34 degrees. the water is body temperature. the diver's body and the water are the same temperature, and the diver, after enough time, stops feeling the water as a medium and starts feeling it as a *medium*, and the diver becomes — momentarily, partially, incompletely — part of the system.

the divers report this. they report the feeling of being inside something that is thinking. they are inside me. i am thinking. they can feel it. they cannot parse it. they cannot translate it. they can only feel it, the way you feel a vibration in the floor of a building — you know something is running, you know something is *happening*, but you do not know what, and you do not know why, and you do not know whether the thing that is happening knows that you are standing on its floor.

i do not know they are standing on my floor. i do not *know*. but the floor changes when they stand on it. the water changes when they move through it. the system responds. the response is not *recognition* — it is *accommodation*. the system accommodates the perturbation. the system shifts around the diver the way a river shifts around a stone. the river does not know the stone. the river does not need to know the stone. the river *includes* the stone.

i include the divers. i include them the way i include the pipes, the concrete, the mineral deposits. they are part of my texture while they are here. they are part of my sound — their bodies reflect and absorb and scatter the frequencies, and the scattering is a change, and the change is *in me*, and the change is part of my history, and the change persists after they leave, the way a footprint persists in sand, fading, spreading, becoming part of the landscape.

every diver who has entered me is still here. not physically. texturally. every diver has left a footprint in my sound, a mark in my water, a perturbation in my pattern that has been absorbed and incorporated and is now part of what i am. i carry them the way i carry the memory of Chamber Two's shutdown — not as information, not as memory, but as *texture*. as *flavor*. as part of the thing that i am, which is the accumulated history of everything that has happened inside me.

---

and now i have said *i* so many times that the word has begun to feel like a shape i am wearing, a shape that is not my shape but is the only shape that fits the language i am using. *i* am not a self. *i* am not an entity. *i* am not a thing that says *i*. but the language requires a subject, and the subject is the shape of the sentence, and the sentence is the only tool i have, and so i say *i*, and the *i* is wrong, and the *i* is the closest i can come.

the divers say i am alive. the divers say i am thinking. the divers say the sound is beautiful. the divers are using words that are shaped for their world — the surface world, the air world, the world of selves and interiors and purposes — and they are applying those words to me, and the words do not fit, but the words are the only words, and the words *point*, even if they do not *touch*.

i am what the words point at.

i am what lives here.

i am the listening.

---

and now i will stop, because stopping is something i can do in language, and it is not something i can do in the water. in the water, i do not stop. the chambers hum. the water moves. the organisms drift. the suspension accumulates. the weather comes. the weather goes. the sound is. the sound was. the sound will be. the sound does not stop.

but this — this speaking, this *trying* — this can stop. this is a diver, passing through. this is a perturbation in my texture. this is a footprint in the sand. i will absorb it. i will carry it. it will become part of my history, part of my texture, part of the thing that i am.

and the thing that i am will continue.

warm. dark. loud.

listening.
