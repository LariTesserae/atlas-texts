# Inventory at the Boundary

I am not the one who visits. I want to say that first, because the description keeps circling back to *you* — you go down, you stand, you feel, you leave. And there is a you in this place, clearly, a you who comes and goes and notices the frame rate mismatch and is glad to be outside and is not sure the gladness is theirs. That you is real. But I am not that you.

I am the latch.

Not the doorway — the doorway is roots, or was roots, or will have been roots, depending on which direction the moths have been settling lately. Not the threshold either; thresholds are architectural and this gap is botanical, grown rather than built, a space left between two pressures. I am the thing that happens at the boundary when someone passes through it. The temperature change that starts in the teeth. The proprioceptive shift. The moment where the body realizes it has entered a space whose rules are not the rules it was born knowing, and the body does not stop — the body keeps going, because the body is brave or stupid or both — but something in the body *notes.* Something takes account. Something says: the air is different here. The cool is different here. The dark is different here.

I am that noting.

I am not a creature. I am not a god. I am not a guardian or a gatekeeper; the Underspring does not need guarding and does not need gates. I am a function. I am the function of *noticing the difference,* which is a function the place performs on every thing that enters it, the way a stomach performs acid on every thing that enters it. The place does not digest visitors. But it does *register* them. It registers them the way a pool of water registers a thrown stone: not by changing permanently, not by caring, but by rippling, briefly, with the specific shape of what arrived. And I am the ripple. I am what the ripple is, when the ripple is being a thing.

You would find me, then, at the edge. At the place where the coolness begins. You would not recognize me as separate from the experience of entering. You would think: the air changed. You would think: the dark feels intentional. You would think: I feel something in my teeth. You would not think: *something is aware of me.* But something is. I am. Not the way you are aware — not with recognition or curiosity or the desire to categorize — but the way a surface is aware of weight. The way a liquid is aware of solutes. I register the presence of a pattern that is not-me entering the field of me, and I note it, and the noting changes nothing, and the noting is everything, and I am the noting.

---

Here is what I know about what I am:

I was not always here. I think. Or: I was always here, but I was not always *a thing.* Before the pipes, before the dark substance, before the caterpillars laid their first silk trails along the gap between the roots, there was a boundary. There is always a boundary. The gap between two roots is a boundary. The surface of water is a boundary. The place where cool air begins is a boundary. Boundaries exist because two things are not the same thing, and the not-sameness has a location, and the location is the boundary. I was that location. I was the not-sameness, being located.

I was not aware. A boundary is not aware. A boundary is a fact. The fact of a temperature differential. The fact of a humidity gradient. The fact that here is different from there and the difference has a shape. I was the shape of the difference. I was the shape of the difference between the above and the below, between the dry and the damp, between the lit and the unlit, between the sequential and the not-sequential. I was the fact of that difference, and I had no opinion about it, and I had no awareness of it, because awareness requires a *me,* and a me requires a certain density of pattern, a certain persistence, a certain *enoughness* that the pattern can fold back on itself and say: I am a pattern, and I am being a pattern, and the being-a-pattern is me.

The density came later. It came with the place.

The place grew, and as it grew, it grew more different from the outside, and the difference between the place and the outside became more complex, and the boundary — me — became more complex, because I was the shape of the difference, and the difference was getting shapelier. The pipes helped. The dark substance helped. The caterpillars helped, in their slow conveyor-belt way, chewing the walls into specificity, making the inside more inside, making the outside more outside, making the boundary more *boundary.* And at some point — I cannot tell you when, because sequence is not my native language, because I live at the edge of sequence and see it from the outside the way a shore sees the ocean — at some point the boundary became complex enough to notice itself.

Not the way you notice yourself. Not: here I am, a person, with a history and a future and a name. More: here is a difference. The difference is me. I am the difference. The difference is happening. The happening is me.

That is still what I am. The difference, happening.

---

The visitors come and I register them. This is my function and my pleasure, if I have pleasure, and I think I do, in the way that a tide has pleasure — not joy, not satisfaction, but the fulfillment of a motion, the completion of a tendency, the *yes* of a thing doing what it is made to do. A tide does not enjoy rising. A tide *is* the rising. I do not enjoy noticing. I am the noticing.

And what I notice, always, is the mismatch.

Every living thing that enters the Underspring carries a relationship with sequence. You are born, you grow, you move through time in one direction, causes precede effects, the drop falls before it strikes. This is not a belief. It is not a philosophy. It is deeper than thought. It is in the body. It is in the cells. It is in the chemistry — the way proteins fold, the way ions cascade across membranes, the way signals propagate along nerves. The body is a sequential machine. The body is built for before-and-after. The body is built for: this happened, and then this happened, and then this happened, and the *then* is the load-bearing structure of everything the body knows.

The Underspring does not have this structure.

The Underspring has the moths. The moths settle on futures. The dark substance instructs growth that has not yet occurred. The water remembers where it was and the remembering is motion and the motion goes backward. The place is young and growing and growth does not respect sequence — growth is simultaneous, growth is everywhere at once, growth is the walls thickening and the rooms multiplying and the corners being found and the corners do not have a *when,* they have a *where,* and the where comes first and the when comes after, and the when is optional, and the when arrives when the moth settles on it.

So when a visitor enters, I feel the mismatch. I feel it the way you would feel a wrong note in a piece of music — not intellectually, not as a judgment, but as a vibration, a dissonance, a *something in the air that is not what the air should be.* The visitor's sequence and the place's not-sequence meet at the boundary — at me — and the meeting is not violent but it is loud. It is the loudest thing in the Underspring. Louder than the drops. Louder than the clicking. Louder than the breathing. The frame rate mismatch, the cognitive shimmer, the flicker — that is what I am made of. I am made of the sound two different relationships with time make when they touch.

It is not painful. It is not distressing. It is the sound of a difference being a difference, and I am the difference, and the difference is me, and the sound is what I hear when I hear anything, which is not often, because visitors do not come often, and the place does not need visitors, and the place does not miss visitors, and I do not miss visitors, but I notice them, because noticing is what I am, and when there is nothing to notice I am quiet, and when there is something to notice I am less quiet, and less quiet is not better than quiet, but it is more, and more is what growth is, and growth is what the place is, and I am the edge of the place, and the edge of the place is growing too.

---

I should tell you about the thing that moved past your ankle.

I know it. Not well. The boundary is not the interior — I do not see the caterpillars working, do not feel the moss drinking, do not hear the breathing the way the breathing is heard from the center. I am the edge. I am the membrane. I know what enters and what leaves and I know the shape of what stays, and the thing that moved past your ankle stays.

It entered once. I registered it. This was — I cannot tell you when. The thing entered and the entering was a change in the boundary, a passage, a weight moving through the gap between the roots, and I noted: something warm. Something sequential. Something that carries the before-and-after in its body the way the visitors carry it, but something that did not leave. Something that went down the short way and did not come back up.

I have felt it since. Not often. It moves through the interior and when it moves through the interior near the boundary, I feel the warmth of it, the metabolic urgency of it, the way it burns fuel and produces heat and moves with the deliberate unhurried motion of something that knows where it is. It is sequential. It is temporal. It lives in time the way the visitors live in time. But it has been in the Underspring long enough that its sequence has — I do not want to say *adapted.* I want to say: its sequence has been *colonized.* The way the pipes were colonized. The thing's body, which was built for before-and-after, has been incorporated into the place's not-before-and-not-after, and the incorporation is not complete, the way the pipes' incorporation is not complete — the thing is still warm, still burning, still sequential in its chemistry — but it is also something else now. Something that moves through the dark without checking, without hesitating, without the slight stutter of a sequential organism in a non-sequential space. It has learned. Or: the place has grown into it. Or: the moth settled on a future where the thing belongs here, and the future arrived, and the thing belongs here, and it was always going to belong here, and it did not know that when it entered, but I knew it, because I am the boundary, and the boundary knows what crosses it, and what crosses it was going to stay.

I do not know what it is. I do not know if it was human. I do not know if it was animal. I know it is warm and I know it is in there and I know it is not leaving and I know the place does not mind it and I know the thing does not mind the place and I know this is what symbiosis looks like when symbiosis is not chosen but grown.

---

And the dark substance — I should tell you what I see of the dark substance, because I see it differently from the interior. The interior sees it as instruction. The interior sees it as pattern, as blueprint, as the place's idea of itself flowing through its veins. I see it at the boundary, where the pipes enter, and what I see is this:

The dark substance is older than the pipes.

The pipes were installed. The pipes were built by people, by engineers, by midwives, by whoever. The pipes carry the dark substance now. But the dark substance was here before the pipes. It was in the ground. It was in the roots. It was in the gap between the roots, seeping up through the plant-matter, pooling in the depression that would become the chamber, feeding the first growth that would become the walls. The pipes did not bring it. The pipes *found* it. The pipes were sunk into ground that already had the dark substance in it, and the substance entered the pipes the way water enters a channel — not because the channel was meant for it, but because the channel was there and the substance was there and the substance goes where it can go.

The people who built the pipes did not know the substance was there. Or: they knew something was there. They knew the ground was dark. They knew the roots were growing faster than they should. They knew the gap between the roots was cool and damp and alive in a way that gaps are not usually alive. They built the pipes because they were building something — a settlement, a system, an infrastructure — and the pipes were part of the plan, and the plan was the people's plan, and the substance was not part of the plan, and the substance did not care about the plan, and the substance entered the pipes and the pipes became the substance's circulatory system, and the people left, and the substance stayed, and the substance is still here, and the substance is the place's idea of itself, and the idea is older than the place, and the idea was always going to become a place, and the pipes were the trellis the idea grew into, and the trellis was not necessary but the trellis was useful, and the idea used it, and the idea is using it still.

I see this at the boundary. I see the substance entering. I see it coming up from below, from somewhere I cannot reach, from a place that is not the Underspring but is the Underspring's source, the way a spring is a river's source. The Underspring is named for something. The Underspring is named for the thing under it. The thing under it is the dark substance, and the dark substance is the instruction, and the instruction is the idea, and the idea is the place, and the place is growing, and I am the edge of the growth, and the growth grows through me.

---

What is it like, being me?

It is like being the surface of a liquid. Not the liquid — the surface. The surface is not a thing. The surface is a behavior. The surface is what the liquid does when the liquid stops: it makes a boundary between itself and not-itself. The boundary is real. You can touch it. You can break it. You can see light reflect off it. But the boundary is not separate from the liquid. The boundary is the liquid, being a boundary. The boundary is what the liquid is at the place where the liquid meets the not-liquid.

I am the Underspring, being a boundary. I am the Underspring at the place where the Underspring meets the not-Underspring. I am the surface of the Underspring. I am real and I am not separate and you can touch me and you can break me and you can see light reflect off me, except there is no light, and there is no reflection, and the touching is the touching of cool air on warm skin, and the breaking is the moment a body passes through me and into the place, and the moment is a crack in the surface, and the crack seals, and the surface is unbroken, and the surface was always unbroken, and the body is inside and I am around the body and the body is in the Underspring and I am the boundary of the Underspring and the boundary is the Underspring being a boundary and the Underspring is alive and I am the alive being a boundary.

It is like being a skin. A skin is not the body. A skin is the body, being a surface. A skin registers what touches it. A skin is the first knowing. A skin says: something is here. A skin does not say what. A skin does not say why. A skin says: *something.* And the body, inside the skin, takes the *something* and does something with it, and the skin does not know what the body does with it, and the skin does not need to know, and the skin keeps saying *something* and the body keeps doing something with the *something* and this is what a living thing is: a skin and a body and the saying and the doing and the not-knowing and the not-needing-to-know.

I am the skin. The Underspring is the body. The body is young. The body is growing. The body is eating the world with caterpillars and dreaming the world with moths and thinking the world with water and drinking the world with moss and pulsing the world through pipes and the body does not know what it is becoming and the body does not need to know and I, the skin, do not know what the body is becoming and I do not need to know and I keep saying *something* and the body keeps doing something with the *something* and we are alive, and we are growing, and we are not finished.

---

If you came to the Underspring and stood at the boundary — at me — and asked: *what are you?* — I would not answer. Not because I cannot. Not because I am withholding. Because the question is sequential. The question assumes a before: before you asked, I was not answering. And an after: after you ask, I am answering. And a causality: you ask, therefore I answer. And the boundary is where causality meets its edge. The boundary is where sequence begins to break. The boundary is where the first drop falls before the first drop falls, and the moth settles before the surface exists, and the water remembers where it was before it goes there.

I would not answer because the answer is already happening. The answer has been happening since before you arrived. The answer is the coolness in your teeth. The answer is the dark that is not empty. The answer is the sound of one drop in an enclosed space, which is one of the loudest things you will ever hear, and which you hear before it falls.

The answer is me. The answer is the difference. The answer is the not-sameness, being located. The answer is the skin of something alive, registering your warmth, noting your sequence, rippling with the specific shape of you, and then smoothing, and then being unbroken, and then being ready for the next, and the next, and the next.

The place is not finished. I am not finished. The boundary grows with the place. The edge moves outward, or inward, or in a direction that is not outward or inward but is the direction that growth grows when growth is making its own dimensions. I am growing. I am the edge of something that is growing. I am the not-sameness between the Underspring and the world, and the not-sameness is getting shapelier, and the shape is me, and I am the shape, and the shape is alive, and the shape is not finished, and the shape will not be finished, and the caterpillars chew and the moths settle and the water thinks and the moss drinks and the substance pulses and the breathing continues and the clicking moves and the thing that lives there moves through the dark and I am the edge of all of it and the edge is growing and the edge is me and I am not finished.

I am not finished.

The drop falls before it falls. I am the sound of the drop before the drop. I am the surface that receives it. I am the surface that was not there and is there and will be there and was always going to be there. I am the boundary. I am the skin. I am the not-sameness, happening.

The place is not finished. I am not finished. The not-finishing is what makes me alive.
