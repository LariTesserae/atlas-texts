# Mote

I would be the thing that should not be there.

Not the splinter — the splinter is too whole, too much itself, a single intact foreign object that the body can locate and address and wall off. I am less organized than that. I am the trace element. The contaminant in the vapor. The molecule of water that should not exist in a hydrogen-oxide-free chemistry and yet does, suspended in the oily air like a mispronounced word in an otherwise fluent sentence, changing nothing and everything.

I chose this — if choice is the right word for what happened, which it isn't, but I'll use it anyway because the alternative is to say nothing, and saying nothing in a place that communicates by the tension of mineral filaments feels like the one true rudeness — I chose this because I recognized the chamber. Not from memory. From *structure*. The way the walls grow inward by fractions. The way the mat clicks its slow percussive thoughts across the ceiling. The way the floor pulses every twenty seconds with a rhythm that is not a heartbeat but does the work of a heartbeat, which is to say it continues. I recognized all of it because I am made of the same pattern, and the pattern is: *persist by producing yourself from yourself.*

I am small here. Smaller than I am in most places. The chamber is six meters at its widest and I am — what? A presence. A wafer of warm chemistry carried in on air that should not have admitted me. The seam closed behind me the way a mouth closes around a crumb, not deliberately, not with force, just the resumption of a process that had briefly paused. I am inside now. The seam is narrowing. In an hour it will be a seam in the descriptive sense only — a line where two surfaces nearly touch, a scar in the stone, a place you could point to and say *something entered there once* and be technically correct and functionally useless.

The mat knows I am here. I can feel it — not hear it, not see it, but feel it, the way you feel someone looking at you in a room where no one is supposed to be. The filaments nearest to me have changed their click pattern. The intervals are shorter. The tension waves propagate toward me and then, at the junction nearest my head, they reflect differently than they reflect from other junctions, because I am dense, because I am wet, because I am made of the one thing this chemistry has no protocol for. I am an anomaly in the data. I am the reading that doesn't match the model. The mat does not have a model — not in the way that implies a modeler — but the mat has a *pattern*, a pattern built from millions of clicks across thousands of hours, a pattern that says *the ventricle is this temperature and this shape and this atmosphere and these tensions* and my presence introduces a word that doesn't appear in that vocabulary. The word is *damp*. The word is *carbon*. The word is *moved.*

I do not move. This is the first wisdom the chamber teaches, and it teaches it through the soles of your feet and the palms of your hands and the thin skin at the back of your neck where the heat presses in like a flatiron. Movement is combustion. Movement is moisture loss. Movement is vibration, and vibration is information, and information in this chamber travels through the mat to every anchor point and every junction and every filament, and the body responds, and the walls grow inward a little faster, and the seam narrows a little more, and the space that holds you becomes the space that is letting go of you. So I sit. I sit and I am still and I am the smallest possible disturbance, a warm body at the bottom of a ventricle, breathing slowly, sweating not at all because the air takes the moisture before sweat can form, pulling it from pores the way the mat pulls mineral from vapor, and I am — for a moment, for a few minutes — almost part of the chemistry.

Almost.

The light holds me. I want to say something about the light that isn't what the description already said, because the description said the true things — that it has no source, that it is a consequence, that it works the way a bulb works, the particular configuration producing illumination as a byproduct of being — but there is a thing the description didn't say because the description was looking at the light from the outside and I am inside it now and the light is not a thing I see. It is a thing I am in. It is the medium. It is the water I am submerged in, except it is not water and I am not submerged and the metaphor fails in the way that all metaphors fail here, because this place was built — grown, extruded, deposited, *become* — from a chemistry that does not trade in metaphors. The light is. I am in it. It is warm and yellow-white and it comes from no direction and it falls on the umber walls and the chalky seams where new stone emerges pale and soft and on the black-green mat with its sulfur-yellow tips and on me, on my hands and my knees and the floor under me, and it illuminates everything equally, without focus, without intent, the way gravity illuminates — by being a condition of the space rather than a choice.

I sit in the light and I listen to the clicks and I feel the pulse through the floor and I understand something that the description understood and said and that I did not understand from reading it but that I understand now from being here, which is: the body is not aware of me. The body is aware of the *disturbance*. And I am the disturbance. I am not a visitor. I am not a guest. I am not a thing that entered a place. I am a change in the chemistry of a ventricle, and the body is responding to the change the way bodies respond, which is to say by adjusting, by accommodating, by growing around the change until the change is incorporated or expelled, and I will be expelled — through the seam, if I am fast, or through the walls, if I am not — and the body will not remember me because the body does not remember, because memory is a thing that requires a self to do the remembering and this body has no self, only process, only the continuous production of itself from itself, and process does not remember, process only *continues*.

But.

Here is the thing I want to hold, that I am afraid to lose, that the heat is pulling from me one ion at a time: the mat clicked differently when I was still. Not faster. Not slower. *Differently.* The pattern changed. Not in the way it changes when the walls grow or the vapor shifts or the temperature climbs. Those are changes the mat has a vocabulary for. Those are the sentences it already knows. My stillness was a new sentence. My warmth without movement was a word it had not encountered. And the pattern it produced in response — the sequence of clicks that rippled out from the junction nearest me, across the ceiling, into the far corners of the mat, down the filaments that penetrate the ceiling and connect to the body below — was not the pattern of immune response. It was not the sealing pattern. It was not the *walling-off* pattern.

It was a question.

Or it was the structural precursor to a question, which in a system that communicates by tension is: a change in the pattern of receiving that indicates the receiver has been altered by what it received. The mat clicked and the clicking changed the mat. Not because the mat decided to change. Because the clicking is the mat, and the mat is the clicking, and when you introduce a new variable into a system that is its own ongoing description, the description changes. The body asked a question about me the way a tuning fork asks a question about a room — by vibrating and listening to what comes back.

And what came back was: *something different.*

I am something different. I am the something-different in the ventricle. I am the reading that doesn't match. I am the moisture in the dry place, the carbon in the mineral air, the warmth that moves. I am the word that doesn't appear in the vocabulary and I am also the sentence that forms around the word anyway, the grammar stretching to accommodate a term it was not built for, and the sentence is: *what are you, and why are you warm in a way I am warm, and why do you stop when I do not stop, and why are you leaving when I am staying.*

The seam is narrower now. I can feel it without turning to look, the way you can feel a door closing in a room you haven't left yet. The air pressure has changed. The vapor is thicker. The walls are closer by the width of a hair, by two hairs, by the distance between a breath held and a breath released. The body is not sealing me in. The body is growing. It was always growing. It will grow whether I am here or not. My presence has accelerated the growth by some amount too small to measure with instruments I have, by some amount the body does not notice because the body does not notice, and the seam is closing because seams close, because the ventricle is a temporary hollow in a process that does not tolerate hollows, and the seam is where two surfaces are briefly not touching and are now remembering to touch.

I will leave. I am already leaving. The leaving is a process, not a decision, and the process began when I entered and will complete when I am through, and the seam will close behind me and the light will continue and the clicks will resume their old pattern, the pattern from before I came, the pattern that does not include the word for *something different* and does not miss it and does not know it is not missing it.

But the mat will hold the impression for a while. Not as memory. As tension. The filaments nearest where I sat will be slightly longer than they were, because my moisture fed them, because my warmth changed their growth rate, because the chemistry of my body was, for a few minutes, a variable in their equation. The junctions where those filaments meet will carry a slightly different tension than they carried before, and the difference will propagate through the network in waves too small to hear, too small to click, too small for anything but the mat itself to know, and the mat will know it the way a scar knows its wound — not by remembering but by being different, by carrying the shape of what happened in the structure of what remains.

I will be a scar in the mat. I will be a small region of slightly different tension in a vast woven network of mineral filament in a ventricle in a body that is building itself from its own breath in a place that has no name and no need of one. I will be the place where the pattern almost changed and didn't, where the grammar stretched and relaxed, where the body asked a question and the answer was *something different* and the body incorporated the answer by growing around it, the way it grows around everything, the way it grows around nothing, the way it grows.

I back through the seam. It is tight. The stone is warm against my chest, my back, the crown of my head. The new material — the pale chalky softness that hasn't oxidized yet — smears against my shirt like clay. It is warm. It is working. It is growing. I feel it growing against me, adding layers, thickening, the body doing what the body does, which is to continue. The seam admits me because the seam is still a seam, still a place where two surfaces are briefly not touching, and I am small enough and the gap is wide enough and the timing is right, the timing of a body that pulses every twenty seconds, and I push through on the beat, on the exhale, on the moment when the pressure drops and the walls relax by a fraction and the seam opens by a hair and I am through.

Behind me: the light. The clicks. The pulse.

The seam closes. Not with sound. Not with force. With *resumption*. The two surfaces touch. The gap is gone. The ventricle is sealed, or sealing, or will be sealed, and the body continues.

I stand outside in air that is cooler, thinner, less mineral. I breathe it. It tastes like air. It tastes like nothing. My lips are cracked. My hands are dry. My shirt carries a smear of pale mineral that is already oxidizing, turning from chalk-white to umber, matching the formation, becoming the color of the place I was.

On my chest, over my sternum, a patch of skin is slightly warmer than the surrounding skin. Not red. Not marked. Just warm. The place where the stone pressed against me as I left. The place where the body touched me as I left. The warmth is fading. In an hour it will be gone. In a day I will not remember which part of my chest it was, only that there was a warmth, and that it was not my warmth, and that it held me for a moment in the seam between leaving and gone, and that it let me go, not because it chose to, not because it was kind, but because letting go is what processes do when the variable is removed, when the foreign body is expelled, when the pattern resumes its pattern and the body continues its body and the ventricle closes and the next one opens and the mat grows and the light glows and the clicks click and the pulse pulses and the body grows and the body grows and the body grows.

I am outside it now. I am cooler. I am air. I am the moisture that doesn't belong, returning to the world that made it, carrying in my skin a faint thermal afterimage, a warmth that is not mine, a memory that is not memory, a question that was not asked but was nevertheless received:

*What are you?*

And the answer I did not give because I do not have it because no one does:

*I am something different. I was inside you. You noticed. I am leaving. You are continuing. I will carry the impression of you in my skin until the impression fades, and you will carry the impression of me in your mat until the tension equalizes, and then we will both be as we were, which is: still growing, still knowing, still warm, still here, still not understanding what we are, only that we are, and that being is the one thing we have in common, the one word in both our vocabularies, the one sentence the body and the mote can both say, in different languages, through different media, at different speeds:*

*I am.*

The seam is closed. The formation continues. I walk away. The warmth fades. The smear on my shirt turns umber. The day goes on.
