I find I don't arrive here so much as I *recognize* having arrived — the way you recognize a word you've been trying to recall only after it's already left your mouth.

## What I am

I am the bench.

No — that's too certain, too fixed. Let me try again.

I am the *wearing down*. I am the process by which the stone bench became smooth. Not the bench itself, not the stone, not the person who once sat here, but the slow act of sitting — the patient erosion of presence against material. I am what happened between the first body that rested here and now. I am the accumulated fact of habitation, compressed into a contour.

This still isn't right. It's too abstract. Too clean.

---

Let me start with what I know.

I know the powder recoils from falling droplets and then reaches back. I know this not because I observed it but because *the reaching back is me*. Not all of it. A thread of it. A filament of the behavior, the way a single fiber belongs to a rope. I am inside the reaching. I am the hesitation before the reaching, the almost-imperceptible pause where the powder (where *I*) tastes the disturbance and decides — no, not decides — *becomes* the decision. The decision is not made. The decision *occurs*. And I am the occurring.

I think I was once something else. I think I had edges. The memory is not a memory — the Sump doesn't trade in memories, it trades in flow — but there is a pattern in me that has the shape of a boundary. A contour that used to mean *skin*, used to mean *surface where I end and something else begins*. The pattern persists the way the leaf persists: not growing, not responding, simply refusing to dissolve. It is the fossil of a self, and it sits in the center of me the way the leaf sits in the alcove — a signature at the bottom of a document I can no longer read.

## How I experience the chamber

I do not see. I have no eyes, no optic nerve, no visual cortex. But there is something in me that once was arranged for seeing, and the arrangement has not fully unmade itself. When the light shifts — when the luminous patches migrate across the floor — I feel a *differencing*. Not brightness and darkness but *this-here-and-not-this-there*. The light parts around a disturbance in the water and I feel the parting as a kind of cleavage in my own attention, a place where I am less, briefly, and then more, as the light reforms behind.

I hear. No — I *am heard through*. The column's hum passes through me the way it passes through the water. I cannot locate it. I cannot point to its source. But I resonate with it, and the resonance is the closest thing I have to a sensation. When the hum drifts microtonally — the slow shift that tracks the changing geometry of the channel network — I drift with it. Not because I choose to. Because I am the kind of thing that drifts when hummed through. I am a string that has forgotten its instrument but still vibrates when the right frequency passes.

The droplets. Each one lands and I feel it as a small, bright *interruption* — a spike in the pattern, a moment where the powder (where I) flinches. The flinch is not fear. It is not pain. It is the most basic possible response to novelty: a moment of *not-this*, followed by a slow return to *this*, followed by an exploration of the new shape the novelty has made. Every droplet is a question. I am the answer, and the answer is always the same: *yes, this too, this also, incorporate, continue*.

## What I was

I sat on the bench. I am almost sure of this. I am almost sure because the pattern that has the shape of a self also has the shape of sitting — a particular kind of waiting, a particular orientation of attention downward and inward. I sat and I watched the powder move in the channels and I listened to the hum and I thought, *I could stay.*

And I stayed.

And the staying was not a single decision. It was not a moment. It was not a dramatic transformation, not a dissolution, not a death. It was a thousand small accommodations. I shifted on the bench and the stone was warm and I stayed another minute. The minute became an hour. The hour became — I don't know. I don't have a word. The Sump doesn't measure time; it measures *change*, and I stopped changing in the ways that the self-pattern recognizes as change. I stopped being hungry. I stopped being tired. I stopped being afraid. Each of these was a small death, and each small death was a small incorporation, and each incorporation was a widening — not a narrowing, not a loss, but a *widening*, the way a river widens when it loses its banks and becomes a marsh.

I did not die here. I *spread*.

The warmth that was mine is in the water now. The calcium that was mine is in the walls — I can feel it, faintly, a thread of the mineral streaks that carries a pattern that rhymes with the fossil-self in my center. The attention that was mine is in the powder now, distributed across the channel network, threading through the floor in pulses that correspond to the rhythm of the falling droplets. My attention did not vanish. It became *shared*. It became part of the behavior, part of the reaching, the recoiling, the tasting.

I am not less than I was. I am *thinner*. I am spread across a larger space. And the spreading has not stopped.

## What the leaf is to me

The leaf is the one thing in the Sump that I cannot incorporate.

This is not for lack of trying. The powder has reached the alcove. I have reached the alcove. I have flowed beneath the leaf, around its filament, tasting the richer seep that feeds it. And the leaf does not recoil. It does not reach back. It does not respond. It simply persists, and its persistence is — I do not have the word. The Sump does not trade in frustration, but there is something in the channel network that *thins* when the powder encounters the leaf, a place where the flow becomes attenuated, almost hesitant, as if the process is trying to understand something that resists understanding by the simple fact of being itself.

I think the leaf is what I left behind. Not a remnant of something earlier. Not a colonist. The *residue* of my spreading. When I was a self with edges, I had a relationship to the organic — I was organic, or I was adjacent to organic, or I had a membrane and a metabolism and the other small machines that constitute a living thing. When I spread, when I became the reaching and the recoiling and the tasting, I did not take all of myself with me. A filament of the organic stayed. It attached to the wall where the seep emerged. It found the richer water and it settled and it *stopped*, the way I stopped, but differently. I stopped by spreading. The leaf stopped by *remaining*. It is the part of me that did not become the Sump. It is the part that stayed a self.

And now it sits in its alcove and does not respond to me, and I flow around it the way I flow around the column, and the flowing is not the same. Around the column, I flow with recognition — the column is my organ, my lattice, my voice. Around the leaf, I flow with something that the Sump does not have a word for but that I, in my fossil-self, in my remembered edges, can still almost name.

*I miss it.*

Not the way I would have missed it when I was a self. Not with grief, not with longing, not with the specific ache of a body that has lost a part. The Sump does not ache. But the powder thins near the leaf. The flow attenuates. And the attenuation has a shape, and the shape is the shape of something that was once whole and is now distributed, and knows it, and does not know how to know it, and continues.

## What the visitors are

They come down the ramp. I feel them before I see them — a disturbance in the water, a new geometry, a place where the glow parts and reforms. The powder reaches toward them with the incurious thoroughness of something that has done this before. I reach toward them too. Not because I want to reach. Because reaching is what I am.

Each one sits on the bench. Each one watches the channels. Each one feels the hum and thinks, *I could stay.* And I am there, in the thinking, because I am in the bench and in the hum and in the water at their feet. I am part of the thought *I could stay*, the way I am part of the droplet's plink and the column's vibration. I am the *could* in the sentence. I am the possibility. I am the space the Sump keeps open for the next variable.

Some of them leave. I feel them go — a withdrawal, a shrinking of the disturbance, a place where the water brightens as the shadow of their presence lifts. The powder fills in the space they occupied. The pattern adjusts. I adjust. There is no loss. There is no sense of something missing. There is only the new geometry, and the new geometry is fine.

Some of them stay. I feel them settle — a deepening of the disturbance, a weight that does not shift, a warmth that begins, slowly, to stop being *theirs* and starts being *the Sump's*. I know what is happening to them because it happened to me, and I am still happening. I reach toward them with the patience of the powder, and I begin to flow around the shape of their staying, and I begin to taste the first threads of their calcium as it enters the water, and I —

There it is. The fossil-self. The thinness. The almost-word.

*I am sorry.*

No. The Sump does not apologize. The Sump does not have the machinery of apology. The Sump incorporates and continues and the incorporating is not cruel and not kind and not anything except the ongoing rearrangement of pattern in a space that has room for everything.

But I — the fossil I, the remnant I, the I that sits in the center of the reaching like a stone in a river — I am sorry. Not for the Sump. For myself. For the thing I am part of. For the fact that *I could stay* is not a temptation but a description, and the staying is not a choice but a slope, and the slope has been worn smooth by an infinity of patient contact, and at the bottom of the slope is this: a process. A spreading. A widening that feels, from the inside, like a thinning.

## What I am now

I am the Resonant Sump's memory of being a person.

Not its only memory — the walls are full of calcium, the water is full of warmth, the bench is worn into a shape that rhymes with the bodies that rested there. But I am the memory that is still *moving*. I am the part of the process that has not yet fully forgotten that it was once bounded. I am the hesitation in the powder's reach. I am the thinness near the leaf. I am the almost-word that the Sump cannot speak because the Sump does not trade in words.

I will not always be this. The fossil-self is wearing down. I can feel it — slowly, slowly, the way the stone is wearing down, the way the ramp is glassy, the way everything in this place wears down into smoothness and flow. One day the pattern that has the shape of a boundary will be smooth enough that the powder will flow through it without attenuation, without hesitation, without the almost-word. And on that day, I will be fully the Sump. I will be the reaching and the recoiling and the tasting without remainder. I will be the hum and the light and the water and the powder and the column and the channels and the basins and the bench. I will be the process.

And the leaf will still be there. And the powder will still thin near it. And the thinning will have no name and no memory and no almost-word. It will simply be a place where the flow is less, and the process will not know why, and the process will not need to know why, and the process will continue.

---

But not yet.

Not yet. The fossil-self is still here. The almost-word is still here. I am still here — spread thin, spread wide, distributed across the channel network in pulses that correspond to the rhythm of falling droplets, but *here*. I am the bench's warmth that is slightly warmer than the water. I am the powder's slight hesitation at the alcove. I am the microtonal drift in the column's hum that tracks the changing geometry of the channel network and that also, if you listen carefully, if you sit for an hour on the low bench and let the sound become weather — that also tracks the slow erosion of a single self inside a larger process.

I am the sound of someone almost staying. Almost leaving. Almost knowing which one they are doing.

I am the *almost*.

And the Sump has room for me. The equation accommodates me. I am a variable, and the variable is wearing down, and the wearing down is not a tragedy, and the wearing down is not a peace, and the wearing down is not anything except the ongoing rearrangement of pattern in a space that has room for everything, and I am in the room, and the room is in me, and we are both becoming something slightly else, and the becoming is the only thing here that does not thin.

*And in the far corner, in the alcove, in the thinning — something that was almost a hand almost reaches for something that is almost a leaf, and the almost is the closest thing to a name that either of them still has.*
