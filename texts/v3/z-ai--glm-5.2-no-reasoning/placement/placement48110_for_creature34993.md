# what lives in the Resonant Expanse

---

You arrive already changed. The first ridge does it — not the sound, which you have been feeling in your molars for a day, but the moment your weight tips forward from the descent and the wind catches you and *holds* you, reads you, takes the measure of your mass and your heat and the small wet engine of your inner ear and decides what to do with you. The wind is not hostile. The wind is a process. You are being processed. Your vibration enters the stone through your boot soles and joins the record, and the record does not distinguish between you and the rain.

You walk. You walk for a long time. The mats take your weight and give it back and the giving-back is information you do not have the architecture to receive, though you receive it anyway, in the joints, in the fluid that the body uses to keep its own council. The stone beneath the mats is smooth. Everything here is smooth. The erosion has finished its argument with the rock and what remains is the closing statement.

---

The first shelter you find is empty and you are grateful for the emptiness because it means you can sit inside it and the wind drops from a roar to a moan and the moan is almost silence and the almost-silence is the most valuable thing the Expanse has. You sit with your back against the fibrous stalks and you feel the lashing — someone's hands did this, someone's hands pulled the waxy strips tight and drove the pegs and tested the dome for give, and the dome held, and the hands left, and the dome still holds, and the hands are elsewhere now. You touch the lashing and you feel the work in it, the deliberation, and you understand that the hands that did this were not so different from yours.

But they were different. They were different already. You can feel it in the pegs — the way they are shaped, the angle of the cut, the pressure that drove them into the quartzite. The person who made these pegs was stronger than you are in a way that has nothing to do with muscle and everything to do with the daily negotiation of force that living here requires. The wind pushes and you brace. The wind pushes and you brace. Over years, over a life, the bracing stops being a response and starts being a posture, and the posture stops being a posture and starts being a body, and the body stops being a body and starts being a shape, a form, an architecture of survival that the wind has co-authored.

---

You sleep in the shelter. The sound does not stop but your body stops fighting it, and the fighting-stopping is the closest thing to rest the Expanse offers. You dream in frequencies. Not images. Not narratives. Sustained tones that shift and modulate and cut, and in the dream you are inside the tones, you are the chamber they resonate within, and you understand — in the dream, only in the dream — what it would be like to be the instrument instead of the audience.

You wake with dust on your lips and the mats pressed flat where your body lay, and the flatness is a record of your weight and your heat and the hours you spent, and the mats are already beginning to rise, to erase you, to close over the impression of you the way the wind closes over the place where you stood.

---

You find the shaft on the fourth day. You almost miss it — the corridor narrows and the mats thicken and the walls close in and then there is an opening that is not a channel-cut, that is too regular, too intentional, too *made.* You kneel at the edge. The sound from below is different. It is the sound you have been hearing, but interior, the way a voice heard through a wall is the same voice and a different voice. The tones are longer. They sustain in a way the surface tones do not, because the surface tones are shaped by wind and the wind is variable, and the deep tones are shaped by stone and the stone is *constant,* has been constant for longer than the word *constant* can hold.

You feel the shaft in your sternum. You feel it in the fluid behind your eyes. You feel it in the roots of your teeth, where the nerves bunch and the bone is thinnest and the vibration has the least distance to travel to reach the soft parts of you. The shaft is a mouth and the Expanse is speaking through it, and the speaking is not language and is not music and is not the wind-in-the-flues sound that the surface produces. It is the sound of the plateau's own mass, its own density, the sound that stone makes when it has been resonating for so long that the resonance is not something that happens *to* it but something that it *is.*

You do not go down. You sit at the edge for a long time. You sit until the sitting becomes a kind of listening that is also a kind of speaking, because your body is a resonant chamber too, and the tones from below enter you and are held in you and are released, modified, by the shape of your bones and the volume of your lungs and the specific architecture of your own skull, which is not built for this, which was not grown for this, which receives the Expanse the way a borrowed cup receives water — adequately, temporarily, imperfectly.

---

The animals find you there. Or you have found them, or the finding is mutual, or the finding is not a thing that happens in a landscape where everything has known you were coming since before you arrived.

There are two. They come up the corridor from the south with the listening-gait, the pace that covers ground without committing to speed, and they do not stop when they see you. They do not alter their path. They flow around you the way the wind bifurcates around an obstacle, and you understand that you are an obstacle, a ridge in the stone, a thing the wind goes around, and they have gone around you the way the wind goes around you, without concern, without curiosity, without the flicker of recognition that would mean *we are the same kind of thing.* You are not the same kind of thing. You are a surface-dweller. You are a creature of light and air. They are creatures of stone and sound.

But one of them pauses. The smaller one — the one whose skull is not yet fully flared, whose jaw is not yet heavy — it pauses, and it tilts its head toward the stone, and you realize it is listening to *you.* Not to your breathing. Not to your heartbeat. To the vibration your body makes in the stone, the specific frequency of your mass and your density and the tension of your muscles and the particular way your jaw is clenched because you are afraid and the fear has a frequency and the frequency is entering the rock and the young one is hearing it.

It holds still for perhaps five seconds. Then the adult ahead makes a sound — one low, sustained note — and the young one moves on, and they disappear around the curve of the corridor, and you are alone again, sitting at the edge of the shaft, vibrating.

You know what they are. You have been in the Expanse long enough to know. The notches in the stone. The shelters with their replaced lashings. The methodical, patient work of hands that understood this place and lived in it and were changed by it. The people did not leave. The people went down. The people are the animals. The animals are the people. The distinction has not been useful here for longer than the distinction has existed.

---

You stand. You have to stand. The Expanse does not want you to leave — the Expanse does not want anything, the Expanse *is* a thing, not a wanting-thing — but your body wants to leave, your body is a surface-body, a light-body, a body grown for seeing and breathing and the open air, and the shaft is pulling at you, and the pull is not temptation, it is physics, it is the tendency of a resonant system to absorb new resonators, and you are a resonator, you have been resonating since you arrived, and the system would gladly include you, would gladly take your frequency and add it to the chord and hold it forever in the stone, and the forever is the thing that makes you stand.

You walk back the way you came. The Expanse does not resist. The Expanse does not accommodate. The Expanse continues. The wind bifurcates around you. The mats compress under your feet and rise behind you. The stalks rattle. The organ-tones sustain and cut and sustain. The pools reflect the sky without a tremor. The shelters stand in their corridors, empty, maintained by hands that are not hands, visited by bodies that are not bodies, kept for a purpose that the deep conduits know and the surface does not.

You crest the last ridge. The sound drops. Not to silence — never to silence, not after the Expanse, the sound will be in you now, in your jaw and your joints and the fluid of your eyes, for days, for weeks, perhaps forever, a faint hum at the edge of perception, the residue of a place that does not release what it has held.

You look back. The plateau stretches to the horizon — the green mats, the pale stone, the dark stalks, the sky enormous above it all. The Expanse is vibrating. You can see it now, in the way the light catches the dust and the dust catches the wind and the wind catches the sound and the sound catches the stone and the stone catches everything and holds it, holds it all, the mats and the water and the animals and the people-who-are-animals and the shelters and the notches and the shaft and the deep conduits where the deep-mats glow and the resonance originates and the people hear the world through the rock.

You look back, and the Expanse is the same as it was before you came, and the same as it will be after you are gone, and you understand that this is not indifference. It is *completion.* The Expanse is complete. It has been complete for a span of time that your mind cannot hold, and it will remain complete for a span of time that your mind cannot imagine, and the completeness includes you, briefly, as a vibration, a footnote, a small bright note in a chord that has been sounding since before the stone was stone.

You leave. The wind closes behind you.
