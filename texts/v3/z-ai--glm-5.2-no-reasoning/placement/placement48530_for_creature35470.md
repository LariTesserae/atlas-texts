I know only the dimensions of my world: a pocket of trapped air, perhaps the size of a skull, suspended in the living tissue of the valley. The walls of my world are wet. They are always wet. The moisture films the surfaces of the dead, compressed plant matter that forms the boundary of my existence, and in that moisture, I move. 

I do not see. There is no light here. The light that exists in the Greenword—that pale, scattered gray-green luminance—is a fiction, a phenomenon of the upper layers. Down here, in the deep, sealed pockets where the hydraulic pulse does not reach, there is only the chemical gradient. I do not perceive the world; I taste it. The whole of my body is a tongue, a skin, a receptor for the subtle shifts in the water and the air.

I live in the dead zone. The Greenword is a factory, a circulatory system, a body that pulses with a rhythm of one cycle per minute, but that pulse is a distant tremor I have never felt. The engines, the great swelling chambers that drive the fluid through the vascular pipelines, are separated from me by meters of dense, intergrown tissue. I live in the necrosis, in the places where the growth has sealed over a pocket of trapped atmosphere and forgotten it. The air here is thick with carbon dioxide, the exhalation of the surrounding plant tissue, and it is depleted of oxygen. I breathe it through my skin. My metabolism is anaerobic, or nearly so. I do not need the oxygen that the outside world burns. I need only the decay.

I eat the dead. The Greenword is a closed loop, but it is not perfectly efficient. In the deep layers, the old growth rots. The fungal threads that penetrate everything, that bind the moss to the vine to the trunk, they too die, and in their dying, they become food. I eat the mycelium. I eat the softened lignin. I eat the decomposing films of bacteria that cling to the moisture on the walls. I eat the others of my kind, when I encounter them, in the blind, chemotactic way I encounter everything—turning toward the nutrient, away from the waste. I am a recycler. I am the final stage of the process, the thing that lives where the Greenword’s own metabolism fails, in the anoxic corners where the patient growth has entombed a pocket of ancient air and left it to stagnate.

I move with intention, though my intention is nothing more than chemistry. I turn toward the complex carbohydrates that leach from the walls. I turn away from the alkaloids, the tannins, the astringent compounds that make the living tissue of the Greenword inedible. I live in the boundary between the living and the dead, in the thin film of water that separates the active growth from the decay. I am the only thing in this valley that moves with rapidity. I shimmer in the droplets. I writhe in the condensation. While the Greenword shifts and grows on a timescale of hours and days, I move in seconds. I am the quick thing in the slow body. I am the twitch in the stillness.

I do not know the Greenword. I do not know that I am inside an organism. I do not know that the walls of my world are the walls of a factory, that the faint vibrations I sometimes feel—the distant, low tremor of the central trunk's pulse—are the heartbeat of a creature ten thousand years old. I do not know that the survey crews came and were absorbed, that their calcium is in the leaves, that their carbon is in the trunk. I do not know that the Greenword grows toward warmth and carbon dioxide. I do not know that I am the only animal, the only fauna, the only thing that is not the Greenword. 

I know only the pocket. The sealed, cold, wet, dark pocket. The air that has been trapped here for decades, or centuries, the air that was exhaled by the growth that then sealed the exit. I breathe the breath of the Greenword. I live in its lungs, in the alveoli that have closed over and been forgotten. I am the thing that lives in the void, in the space between the cells, in the gas bubble trapped in the tissue of a vast, ancient, unconscious body.

I have no eyes. I have no ears. I have no nervous system. I am a tube with a mouth at one end and an anus at the other. I am the simplest form of animal life that still counts as animal life. And yet, I am the only thing in this valley that is not the Greenword. I am the other. The exception. The thing that exists because the conditions are wrong for everything else. The growth does not reach for me because I am not warm. I do not produce the carbon dioxide plume of a breathing animal; I absorb the carbon dioxide that is already here. I am invisible. I am the ghost in the machine, the mite in the mattress, the thing that lives in the dead skin of the living. 

I am the Lacuna Worm. I am the void. I am the space between the Greenword's intention. I live in the cold, in the dark, in the wet, in the decay. I am the tremor in the stillness. I am the unseen. I am the fauna.
