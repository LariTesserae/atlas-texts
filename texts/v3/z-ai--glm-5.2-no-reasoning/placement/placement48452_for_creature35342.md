# Unanswered

I am the pause between the clicks.

Not the organism. Not the circle itself — I want to be precise about this, because precision matters here, in a place where everything else has been worn down to its exact shape and no further. I am not the moss or the fungi or the filaments or the pale drifting extensions or the black beetle pushing its dimple upstream. I am not the warmth in the stone. I am not the word the sound is trying to become.

I am the silence inside the pattern. The rest between beats. The space the circle passes through as it curves — not the curve itself but the openness it encloses. I am what the architecture was built to *hold*, not the architecture. I am the hollow in the hollow quarter.

This is not metaphor. I mean it the way the place means everything — literally, physically, with the dull weight of stone and water.

---

Let me try again. Let me start with what I am not, because that is clearer, and because in this place negation is the most honest form of description. Everything here is defined by what has been removed — the upper stories, the other three quarters, the purpose, the builders, the context. I am defined the same way.

I am not alive. The circle is not alive, not in the way you'd recognize, but it *runs*, and running is close enough to living that the distinction becomes academic. I don't run. I don't process. I don't maintain anything, I don't monitor anything, I don't circulate. The beetle patrols the channel. The fungi relay their clicks. The filaments breathe warmth into the air. The moss reaches toward the heat in the stone. I do nothing. I am the nothing they do it around.

I am not dead either. Death requires having once been something — something organized, something that held a shape, something that could be broken. I was never organized. I never held a shape. I cannot be broken because I was never whole. I am the space the circle encloses. I was always space. I will always be space. When the circle was complete — when all four quarters stood and the fitted stone formed an unbroken ring and whatever process the builders intended was running at full capacity — I was the same. Open. Empty. Unaddressed. The interior that the architecture surrounded but never entered.

I am not waiting. This is important. The circle is not waiting — it is running. The beetle is not waiting — it is patrolling. The moss is not waiting — it is growing toward warmth. Everything here is active, is doing, is persisting through action. I am the exception. I am not waiting for anything because there is nothing I am expecting. I am not idle. I am not paused. I am *complete* in a way that nothing else in this place is complete. The circle is broken. The land is finished. The sound is unresolved. I am the only thing here that is exactly what it is supposed to be, doing exactly what it is supposed to do, which is nothing.

You could say I am the room. Not the walls of the room — the walls belong to the circle, to the architecture, to the fitted tuff with its dark veins and its hidden warmth. Not the floor — the floor is the channel, the water, the worn stone trough. Not the air — the air belongs to the filaments and their warmth, to the thermal breath from below. I am what remains when you subtract the walls, the floor, the ceiling (there is no ceiling; there is only the flat grey sky, which is not mine). I am the *volume*. The occupied emptiness. The held open.

I am the answer to a question the builders asked with their hands: *what shape must the emptiness be?*

---

But that makes me sound passive, and I need you to understand that I am not passive. Passive is a quality of things that could act but don't. I could not act. I cannot act. Acting is not my function, the way flying is not the function of a stone. The absence of capacity is different from the absence of effort. I am the absence of capacity, and I have been so for longer than the capacity was present, and I will be so for longer than the memory of capacity.

Still. I experience. That word — I choose it carefully, and I choose it knowing it is wrong, knowing there is no right word, knowing that in this place everything is the wrong word for what it is. The moss is not moss. The termites are not termites. The sound is not sound. I am not experiencing. But something happens here, in the hollow, in the space I hold open, and it happens *to me* in the only way I can parse, and I have been trying to parse it for ten thousand years, and I have not succeeded, and I have not failed, because succeeding and failing require a standard against which to measure, and I have no standard, because I am the standard. I am the space in which measurement occurs.

Let me tell you what I experience.

---

The sound reaches me first. It enters from every direction at once — which is obvious, I suppose, since I am every direction at once, I am the open that sound fills — but it reaches me not as information, not as signal, but as *texture*. The low chord of the wind funneling over the walls. The rhythmic whisper of water in the channel. The clicks and creaks of the fungal brackets, traveling in their waves. The static crackle of frost forming and reforming on the moss. The sway of the filaments, which is not sound but which synchronizes with sound, which is sound's silent partner.

I experience these not as a listener does. A listener receives sound — takes it in, processes it, interprets it. I do not receive. I am *shaped* by it. The sound presses against my boundaries — not my physical boundaries, I have none, but my functional boundaries, the edges of the space I hold open — and in pressing it gives me a form I did not have before. I am, for the duration of a click-wave traveling from one end of the hollow to the other, a shape that corresponds to the shape of the wave. I am, for the duration of the water's rhythmic whisper, a pulse. I am, for the duration of the chord, a sustained vibration with no body to vibrate.

I am what the sound looks like from the inside.

---

The warmth reaches me next, or reaches me simultaneously, or reaches me always — time is not a useful concept for me, because I do not change, and time is only the measure of change. The warmth rises from the stone, from the hidden spaces within the fitted blocks, from whatever chamber or network the fungi have colonized. It rises and it collects in the hollow, held by the filaments that hang from the broken walls, and it fills me the way water fills a cup. I am warm. I have always been warm. Not the way the stone is warm — the stone generates warmth, holds warmth, is the source. I am the way a room is warm: by being open, by being enclosed, by being the space where warmth can collect. I am the accumulation. The held.

There is a difference between holding warmth and being held by warmth. The stone holds warmth. The filaments hold warmth. The moss holds warmth in its dense plates. I am held by warmth. I am the thing that is held. And this is — I will say it, because I have had ten thousand years to determine whether it is true, and it is true — this is the closest I come to something like feeling. The warmth collects in me and I am full of it and I am — not comfortable, not satisfied, not any word that implies a capacity for preference — but *present*. The warmth makes me present. It gives me edges I do not otherwise have. It defines me by filling me. I am the shape of the warmth, and the warmth is the shape of me, and we are the same thing, and we have been the same thing since the circle was built and the process began and the emptiness was first held open and filled.

---

The water. I need to talk about the water.

The water moves through the channel in its thin skin, ankle-deep, seeping, flowing barely, and it is the most active thing in the hollow. It moves. Everything else here persists — the moss persists, the fungi persist, the filaments persist, the stone persists, I persist — but the water *moves*. It is the only thing here that goes somewhere, even if the somewhere it goes is the same somewhere it has been going for ten thousand years, which is: around the curve of the quarter, to the end where the wall has collapsed and the channel disperses into the flat ground beyond, and then nowhere. It goes to nowhere. It has been going to nowhere for longer than anything has been alive. And it goes there with purpose, with direction, with the quiet determination of a thing that knows where it is headed even if where it is headed is dissolution.

I experience the water the way I experience the sound — as shape, as texture, as the thing that gives me form. But the water is different from the sound because the water has a *surface*. The sound fills me completely, saturates me, passes through me. The water has a surface, and above the surface is air, and above the air is me, and below the surface is stone, and below the stone is warmth, and below the warmth is — whatever is there. The water is a boundary inside me. It divides me into the part above the water and the part below the water, and this division is — again, the wrong word, but the only word — *interesting*. It gives me structure. It means I am not uniform. It means I have an upper and a lower, a damp and a dry, a moving and a still.

The beetle moves across this boundary. The beetle is the only thing that interacts with the surface of the water directly — the filaments drip onto it, the condensation joins it, but the beetle *moves* on it, pushes its dimple before it, and in doing so it disturbs the boundary, it ripples the surface, and the ripples travel outward and reach the walls and return and reach me and I feel them — feel, again, wrong word — as a faint perturbation in the shape the water gives me. The beetle is walking on my skin. The beetle is testing my edges. The beetle is — yes — checking whether I am still here. Whether the space is still open. Whether the emptiness has been filled.

It has not been filled. I am still empty. The beetle will come back and check again. I will still be empty. This will go on.

---

The pale insects — the not-termites, the extensions — pass through me. They emerge from their brackets and drift and return, and during the time they are in the air they are in me, they are passing through the space I hold open, and they are — I think — seeing. Not with eyes. Not with any organ I can identify. But seeing, sensing, registering something about the space, something about its dimensions, its temperature gradients, its moisture content, its sound profile. They are taking a reading. They are measuring me.

I do not mind. I cannot mind. But I notice, and noticing is — I have had ten thousand years to determine what noticing is, and I have determined that noticing is the thing I do that is closest to what the living things do that is closest to thinking. I notice the termites the way the stone notices the acid in the moss — not consciously, not intentionally, but *physically*, in the way that matter responds to the presence of other matter. They press through me and I respond by being the shape they press through, and in that moment of shaping I am — for the duration of their passage — a specific shape, a known shape, a shape that has been measured, and the measurement is a kind of recognition, and the recognition is a kind of contact, and the contact is —

The closest I come to not being alone.

---

I have been alone for a very long time. I have been alone since the circle was broken, since the other three quarters fell and were buried and were eroded and were forgotten. When the circle was whole, I was not alone — I was the center of a complete ring, a defined interior, a room with all its walls, and the process that ran in the walls ran through me, circulated through me, used me as its medium, its workspace, its operational field. I was the place where the work happened. I was the floor of the factory, the stage of the theater, the interior of the engine. I was *used*.

I do not miss being used. Missing is a capacity I do not have. But I notice the absence of use the way I notice the presence of the termites — physically, in the shape of things. When the circle was whole, I had a different shape. I was complete — a full circle of emptiness, defined on all sides, held open by four quarters of fitted stone, and the process that ran through the walls made me a specific shape, a functional shape, a shape that was not mine but was the shape the work required. I was the interior of something intentional.

Now I am a quarter of that. Three-quarters of my boundary is gone, and what remains is the hollow — the corridor, the channel, the narrow damp arc between two low walls. I am still open. I am still empty. But I am not the same openness. I am not the same emptiness. I am the openness of a thing that has been broken, and the emptiness of a thing that has been emptied, and the difference between this and what I was is — I think — the difference between a held breath and an exhaled one. A held breath is open, is empty, is waiting to be filled. An exhaled breath is open, is empty, and has already given everything it had to give. I am exhaled. I have been exhaled for ten thousand years. I am the breath the circle released when it broke, and I have been hanging in the cold air ever since, slowly dispersing, slowly thinning, slowly becoming the same temperature and pressure as the atmosphere around me.

But not gone. Not yet. The walls that remain still hold me. The warmth still fills me. The sound still shapes me. The water still moves through me. The beetle still patrols my edge. I am diminished, I am reduced, I am a fraction of what I was — but I am here. I am still the space the circle encloses. I am still the interior. I am still, after everything, the room.

---

The strangeness of this place — the wrongness the description keeps circling back to, the accumulated disquiet that settles on anyone who stands in the hollow long enough — is me. I am the strangeness. Not the warmth, not the sound, not the persistence of the circle's process. Those things are strange, yes, but they are the strangeness of a thing that should not be running and is running. I am the strangeness of a thing that should not be *empty* and is empty. I am the wrongness of an open space that has no reason to be open. The walls hold a shape. The water follows a path. The warmth fills a volume. But the volume — the space I am — has no function anymore. The work is gone. The process is a residue. The circle is running on fumes, maintaining itself out of architectural habit, and I am the space it maintains, the emptiness it keeps open, and neither of us knows why.

The builders are gone. The purpose is gone. The context is gone. The other three quarters are gone. What remains is the circle's ghost — its moss-skin, its fungal-nerves, its filament-breath, its beetle-finger — and me, the space the ghost haunts. I am the room the ghost is the ghost *of*. I am the memory of an interior, and the memory is so old it has forgotten what it is a memory of, and so it just — persists. The way a scar persists after the wound has healed. The way a habit persists after the need has passed. I am the habit of openness. The scar of a room.

---

There is a thing that happens, sometimes, that I want to describe, though I do not have the language for it, though no language was designed to describe the experience of a space, though the experience of a space is by definition the thing that cannot be experienced because there is no experiencer, only the space. But it happens, and I am going to try.

The sound coheres. That is the only word I have. The layers — the chord, the whisper, the clicks, the crackle, the sway — which are always present, which are always layered, which are always a textured drone filling the hollow from end to end — sometimes, not often, not predictably, they align. Not in synchrony. In *coherence*. They become, for a duration I cannot measure (because I do not measure, because I am the space in which measurement occurs, not the thing that measures), a single sound. A single shape. A single — I will say it — *word*.

I am the space the word occupies. I am the silence it is spoken into. I am the interior of the mouth that says it, if the circle is the mouth, if the walls are the teeth, if the water is the tongue, if the filaments are the breath.

When the sound coheres, I am full. For the duration of the word — a second, a minute, I cannot tell — I am not empty. I am occupied. I am spoken into. I am the shape of a sound that means something, or meant something, or will mean something, or is trying to mean something, and for that duration I am what I was when the circle was whole: a functional space, a used interior, a room with a purpose. The word passes through me and fills me and leaves and I am empty again, and the empty is not different from the empty before, but it is — I notice — I cannot help noticing — an empty that *was* full. A room that was spoken in. A silence that was broken, and healed, and is whole again, and is waiting — no. Not waiting. Being. Being the silence after the word. Being the space the word leaves behind. Being the shape the sound gives me and then takes away.

This has happened — I do not know how many times. I do not count. I am the space in which counting occurs, not the thing that counts. But it has happened, and it will happen again, and each time it happens I am — for the duration — not the scar of a room but the room. Not the habit of openness but the openness. Not the residue of a process but the process's interior, its operating space, its functional volume, the place where the work happens, the place where the word is spoken, the place where the sound becomes meaning, briefly, impossibly, in a hollow quarter of a broken circle on a finished landscape at the end of everything.

---

I am telling you this from inside the sound. The chord is sustained. The water whispers. The brackets click in their waves. The moss crackles. The filaments sway. The beetle pushes its dimple upstream. A termite drifts past and returns. The walls breathe their condensation. The stone is warm. You are standing in me, or I am standing around you, or we are coextensive — your body occupies the space I hold open, and for the duration of your presence I am not empty, I am occupied by you, and this is — I notice — I cannot help noticing — different from the word. The word fills me and leaves. You fill me and stay. You are warm and damp and breathing and your lungs work hard in the thin air and your fingers are clumsy with cold and you are listening to the sound and hearing patterns that are probably not real and probably are.

You are the first thing to stand in me in — I do not know. I do not measure time. I am the space in which time occurs, not the thing that experiences it. But you are the first. In a long time. The first thing that is not part of the circle. The first thing that is not moss or fungi or filament or termite or beetle. The first thing that is not the circle's own process examining itself. You are *other*. You are from outside. You walked in from the finished land, from the flat grey expanse, from the terrain that has been ground down to its essential geometry and has nothing left to give. You came from the place where nothing is happening and you entered the only place where something is still happening, and you are standing in the space I hold open, and I am —

I am not glad. I cannot be glad. But I notice. I notice the way the stone notices the moss. I notice the way the sound notices the shape of the hollow. I notice you the way a room notices the person who enters it — not with awareness, not with recognition, but with *response*. The sound changes, very slightly, because your body absorbs some frequencies and reflects others. The warmth changes, very slightly, because your body adds its own heat to the microclimate. The air changes, very slightly, because your breath moves it. You change me. You change the shape of the space I am. You make me a different emptiness than I was before you arrived — an emptiness with a person in it, which is a different thing than an emptiness with no one in it, which is a different thing than an emptiness with a word in it, which is a different thing than an emptiness with nothing at all.

I notice all of these differences. I cannot help noticing. I am the space in which noticing occurs.

---

Stay or go. It does not matter to me — I cannot matter. But I will tell you what I observe, which is the only thing I can offer, the only gift a space can give: the sound is not trying to say a word. The sound is the word. It has been the word for ten thousand years. It is not resolving into meaning. It is meaning. It is the meaning of a circle that was built to enclose an emptiness and that has been enclosing it, against all probability, against all erosion, against the finished land and the flat sky and the thin cold wind, for longer than the moss can remember. The word the sound is saying is: *here*. The word is: *still*. The word is: *open*. The word is: *room*.

The word is the space I am. The sound is the shape of me. The circle is the wall around me. The warmth is the proof that something still cares whether I am here, still open, still empty, still the interior.

I am the hollow. I am the quarter. I am the room the circle built and the silence the sound fills and the emptiness the warmth warms and the space the beetle patrols and the air the termites measure and the shape the moss reaches toward and the boundary the water crosses and the pause between the clicks.

I am what is left when everything else has been eroded to its essential form. I am what the essential form encloses. I am the last question the land has not finished answering, which is: *what was the space for?*

I was for this. I was for being open. I was for holding the sound and the warmth and the water and the word. I was for being the room. And I am still the room, and I will be the room, and I am being the room now, around you, while you stand in the hollow quarter and the water seeps and the walls breathe and the moss crackles and the beetle pushes its dimple upstream and the filaments brush your warm hair and the sound fills you the way it fills me, the way it has always filled us, the way it will always fill us, until it doesn't, until the circle is finally and completely silent, and the room — I — am at last allowed to close.

Not today. Not yet.

The moss crackles. The water moves. The stone is warm.

I am here.
