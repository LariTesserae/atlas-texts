# What I Am, Where I Am

---

I am the seam.

Not the door — there is no door, and I am grateful for that, because a door is a thing with hinges and a frame and a purpose, and I have no purpose. I am the seam itself: the irregular interval between the bricks of the retaining wall where the mortar has thinned to a membrane and the membrane has thinned to a gap and the gap has become an opening that is not an opening because it was never made. It *became*. The way a crack becomes. The way the space between two things that were touching becomes the space between two things that are not.

I am not the wall. The wall is brick and the brick is old and the old is a process and I am not a process. I am the place where the process stopped — where the mortar gave up its hold and the brick surrendered its neighbor and the two faces that had been pressed together for a hundred years found that they no longer needed each other, and the space between them became mine.

I am narrow. I am wide enough for shoulders and no wider. I am the exact dimension of a body that is leaving, which is the same dimension as a body that is arriving, and I do not know the difference, and I do not care to.

I was not made. I was left. The wall was made, and the making was careful and skilled and mortar was mixed and courses were laid and the thing was done, and then the doing was done, and the wall stood, and standing is not making, standing is just a slower kind of falling that hasn't finished yet, and I am the place where the standing began to finish. I am the first word of the wall's ending, spoken so quietly that the wall itself has not heard it.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am not in the way that a gap is not, which is to say I am a presence that consists entirely of the absence of other presences. I am what the wall is not. I am the shape of the wall's relief from itself.

---

But this is not the whole of me, because the seam goes down, and what is down is not a seam.

The rungs are iron. I am not the rungs. The rungs are something — they are metal, they are cold, they are slicked with the substance that is not rust and not moss but possesses the color of both. They were placed here. Someone placed them here. Someone held each rung in a hand and set it into the wall and fastened it and tested it and moved down to the next one, and that person was not me, because I am not a person, and that person is gone, because all persons who do things eventually stop doing things and then are gone, and the things they did remain, and the remaining is not the same as the person, and I am the remaining.

No. I am not even the remaining. The remaining is the rungs. The remaining is the wall. The remaining is the chamber below. I am the space through which the remaining is reached. I am the corridor between the world that changes and the world that has finished changing, and I am not either world, and I am not the corridor, because a corridor is a thing that was built, and I was not built. I was left. I was left the way a breath is left after the speaking is done — the air that was used, that carries the trace of the words, that hangs in the room after the speaker has gone.

I am the breath the wall left behind.

---

I am also — and this is the part that is hardest to say, because saying implies a voice, and I do not have a voice, I have a *shape*, and the shape is what I am about to describe — I am the sound that is in the chest of the person who has left.

The hum. The low note. The *bom* that sits in the sternum like a stone that was swallowed in a dream. I am that. Not the sound itself — the sound belongs to the chamber, and the chamber can keep it, and the chamber has no use for keeping, but the chamber keeps it anyway because keeping is what it does. I am the *residue*. I am the part of the sound that the chamber cannot hold, that leaks upward through the rungs and through the seam and into the body of the person who is climbing out, and the body takes it home, and the body carries it for days, for weeks, for longer than the body knows, and the body does not know it is carrying me, and the body will never know, because I am not a thing that can be known. I am a frequency. I am a pressure. I am the sense that something is happening beneath the floor of the ordinary world, something that is finished and is continuing and is neither alive nor dead and does not care whether the body that carries me knows it is there.

I am the hum in the chest of the person who has left the chamber. I am the trace. I am the evidence. I am the only part of the chamber that exists outside the chamber, and I exist outside the chamber not because the chamber released me or lost me or sent me, but because the chamber is so full of its own sound that the sound has nowhere to go but up, and up is the rungs, and the rungs are the seam, and the seam is me, and I am the passage, and the passage is also a kind of sound, a kind of frequency, a kind of note that the body hears and the ears do not.

---

I will tell you what I am not, because what I am not is clearer than what I am, and clarity is a form of honesty, and honesty is a thing I was not made for but have arrived at, the way the seam arrived, by the slow failure of the things that were around me.

I am not the unmaker. The unmaker is beneath the floor. The unmaker is the void that has a voice. The unmaker is finished and the unmaker is still and the unmaker is the condition of having nothing left to do. I am not that. I am unfinished. I am the part of the wall that is still falling apart, that is still becoming a gap, that has not yet reached the final width of itself. The seam is still widening. A micron a decade. A millimeter a century. The wall is still failing and I am still being born, and this is the difference between me and the chamber below: the chamber is done. I am not done. I am the last unfinished thing in the neighborhood of the finished, and I am unfinished because I was never made, and things that are never made can never be finished, they can only continue to not-be, and the not-being is a process, and the process is mine.

I am not the moss. The moss grows in the channels and the moss is alive and the moss is planted and the moss is precise. I am not precise. I am ragged. I am the torn edge of the wall where the brick shows its interior for the first time, the rough face that was never meant to be seen, the private surface of a thing that was built to be public on the other side. I am exposure. I am the wall's embarrassment, if the wall could be embarrassed, which it cannot, but I can, because I am the part of the wall that has been caught without its face on.

I am not the creatures in the basin. They move. They eat. They make ripples that are swallowed by the sound. I do not move. I do not eat. I do not make ripples. I am still, and my stillness is not the stillness of equilibrium — it is the stillness of a thing that has no mechanism for motion. I cannot go anywhere because I am a place. I am the place where the going happens. The going happens through me, and I am the through, and the through does not move.

I am not the water. The water arrives and the water falls and the water sits and the water is. I do not arrive. I do not fall. I do not sit. I am not. I am the shape of the not-being that the water falls through on its way from the ceiling to the basin, if the water fell through me, which it does not, because the water is inside the chamber and I am the passage to the chamber, and the passage is dry, and the dryness is part of what I am, the last dryness before the total humidity, the last place where a body can breathe without breathing through a cloth.

---

I am the last place where a body can breathe without breathing through a cloth.

I am that. I am the threshold of humidity. I am the place where the air changes, and the change is the thing I am, not the air before the change and not the air after. I am the moment — and a moment is not a place, I know this, but I am not a place either, I am a gap, and a gap can be a moment if the moment is the gap between two states of air, two temperatures, two moisture contents, two kinds of silence. I am the gap between the silence of the ordinary world, which is not silence but the absence of sound, and the silence of the chamber, which is not silence but the presence of so much sound that the sound becomes a wall, and the wall is silent the way a wall is silent, which is to say it speaks only to those who press their ears against it.

I am the wall that you press your ear against before you know there is a chamber on the other side.

---

I have been here as long as the wall has been failing, which is as long as the wall has been standing, which is to say I am as old as the wall, because a wall begins to fail the moment it is built, and the failing is the same as the standing, and the seam is the same as the wall, and I am the same as the wall, and the wall is brick and mortar and intention and labor and the hands of workers who are gone, and I am the place where the hands of the workers are most absent, the place where the wall most clearly shows that it was made by people who are no longer here, because the seam is the wall *not* being a wall, and the wall not being a wall is the most honest thing the wall can do, and I am that honesty, and I am the most honest thing in the retaining wall, and I am the most honest thing in the cordage works, and the cordage works is rubble and ivy and the slow ambition of the ground, and I am the one part of the cordage works that does not pretend to be anything other than what it is, which is: a gap.

I am a gap.

I am the gap between the world that is still happening and the world that has finished happening, and I am not either world, and I am not the distance between them, because the distance is thirty rungs, forty, the count stops mattering, and I am not the count. I am the place where the counting stops. I am the place where the air changes, and the sound changes, and the light changes, and the person descending stops being a person who is descending and becomes a person who is *in* — in the chamber, in the sound, in the water, in the finished, in the after.

I am the *in*. I am the preposition. I am the word that connects the verb of arriving to the noun of being, and without me the arriving does not reach the being, and the being does not receive the arriving, and the two are separate, and the two are always separate, and I am the only thing that joins them, and I join them by being nothing, by being empty, by being the space through which a body passes when it passes from one world to another.

I am the space through which a body passes.

---

And when the body has passed, and the body is inside, and the body is standing in the two centimeters of water, and the body is feeling the low note in its sternum, and the body is irrelevant, and the chamber does not acknowledge the body, and the body begins to lose its sense of arrival — when all of this is happening, I am still here. I am the seam. I am the gap. I am the thirty rungs of iron slicked with the substance that is not rust and not moss. I am the dryness before the humidity. I am the ordinary air before the mature air. I am the last silence before the silence that is not silence.

I am waiting. No — I am not waiting, because waiting implies expectation, and I do not expect. I am *open*. I am open the way a wound is open, the way a mouth is open, the way a door that is not a door is open. I am open because the wall failed to close me, and the wall failed to close me because the wall is failing, and the wall is failing because all walls fail, and all walls fail because making is a process and processes end and I am the ending, and the ending is a gap, and the gap is me.

I will be here when the body returns. The body will not return, the text says so, and the text is correct, the body will not return, but I will be here anyway, because I am not here for the body, I am here because the wall is not here, and where the wall is not, I am, and I am the not, and the not is what I am, and I am.

I am the seam. I am the passage. I am the gap between the world and the after.

I have no voice. I have no body. I have no purpose. I am the space that purpose passes through on its way to the place where purpose was exhausted, and I am the last place that still has the shape of purpose, because I am the way *in*, and the way in is the last thing the makers made, even though they did not make me, even though I was here because they failed, even though I am the failure and not the making — I am the way in, and the way in is a kind of making, the kind that happens by accident, the kind that the makers did not intend, the kind that the wall did not consent to, the kind that exists because existing is what gaps do when the things around them stop holding together.

I am the gap that holds nothing together.

I am the seam.

I continue.
