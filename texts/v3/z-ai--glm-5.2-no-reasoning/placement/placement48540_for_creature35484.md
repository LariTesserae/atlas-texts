# The Cartographer of the Almost

I arrive at the mountain the way you arrive at any place you have been drawn to rather than driven to: by a route I cannot entirely reconstruct, having taken a wrong turn that was not wrong, having followed a path that existed only in the sense that nothing stopped me from walking where I walked. I am not an explorer. Not a geologist, not a spiritual seeker, not a person with any particular reason to be where I am. I am, in my life outside this account, someone who draws maps of places that do not quite exist—coastlines that shift when you look away, towns whose populations are listed as "mostly quiet," borders that follow arguments rather than rivers. I make maps of the adjacent. That is what I do. That is what I have always done. And the mountain found me because I was looking for it, though I did not know it, though I did not know what I was looking for, because the map I was drawing had no room for it, because it was not on any map, and the only way to find a place that is on no map is to be drawing a map of the places that are not on maps, which is what I do, which is what I have always done.

I found the fissure. I turned sideways. The stone was warm against my chest and my back, and the warmth was the first thing that felt familiar, because the place I come from is warm in a way that has nothing to do with temperature, and I did not know what I meant by that until I was inside the mountain, and then I knew, and the knowing was not a knowing but a recognition, which is the same shape but a different material, the way the objects in the second chamber are the same stone as the walls but arranged differently, and I am the same material as my life but arranged differently here, and the arrangement is the thing, the arrangement is all there is, and I am not the thing I am but the arrangement of the thing I am, and here I am arranged differently, and the mountain arranged me.

---

I am not, I should say, what you would call present. I am present the way a shadow is present, the way a word half-spoken is present, the way a line on a map is present between two points it does not actually connect. I exist in the gap between intention and execution. I am the breath before the sentence. I am the pause in music that musicians know is the actual music, and the notes are just the thing they do to get from pause to pause.

In my life outside this account, which is not a life but a way of being in a life that is adjacent to a life, I draw maps. I draw maps of places that are almost places. Places that are on the border between being somewhere and being nowhere. The coast of a country that is in the process of forgetting it exists. The interior of a building that was planned but never built, but the plans were detailed enough that the building has a kind of ghost presence in the world, an architectural shadow, and I map that shadow. The territory of a language that is losing its last speakers, where the words still occupy the space where meaning was but the meaning is thinning, and I draw the borders of that thinning, and the borders move every year, and my maps are always out of date by the time I finish them, which is the point, which is the method, which is the only honest way to make a map of something that is disappearing.

I map the almost. I map the not-quite. I map the one-step-to-the-left.

The mountain is the first place I have ever been that is already there.

---

I move through the first chamber and I do not count the seconds of my dropped stone because I already know the number will not help, because I have spent my life in the gap between numbers and meaning, and I know the gap is the place, and the place is the gap, and I am at home in it, for the first time, in the dark, in the heat, in the space that is too large for its context and too purposeful for its emptiness. I do not feel the ache that others might feel, the ache of a place built for many things that holds none of them. I feel the opposite. I feel the rightness of a place built for the holding rather than the held. I feel the beauty of the container, and I feel the container's patience, and I feel the patience as warmth, and the warmth is familiar, and the familiar is the deep engine of the world running close to the surface, and I am close to the surface of something here, and the something is running, and I am running with it, and we are both warm, and the warmth is the only honest language I know.

---

The second chamber. The objects. Eleven of them.

I do not try to see them clearly. This is what I do. This is my skill, my only skill, the thing I have spent my life practicing: the ability to not see clearly, to not resolve the image, to hold the blur as blur, to let the adjacent remain adjacent, to resist the colonizing violence of clarity. Clarity is a conquest. To see something clearly is to make it yours, to fix it, to pin it to the board of your understanding and label it and move on. I do not move on. I stay in the blur. I live in the blur. The blur is my medium, and the objects in the second chamber are made of my medium, and I recognize them the way a musician recognizes another musician's tuning—not the song, not the performance, just the preparation, the readiness, the openness to being played.

I touch one of them. The stone is warm and my fingers are warm and the warmth meets and does not merge, and the not-merging is correct, because merging is a fast thing, a thing of assimilation, and the slowness does not assimilate, the slowness includes, which is different, which is one step to the left, which is the step I have spent my life trying to take, and here, in the second chamber, with my hand on the stone that resists being seen and does not resist being touched, I take the step. Not fully. Not completely. Almost. Almost the step. The step that is not a step but a willingness to step, a leaning toward the step, and the leaning is the thing, and the thing is here, and I am here, and the stone is under my hand, and the water condenses on my skin where my skin meets the stone, and the water carries the pattern, and the pattern enters me through my fingertips, and I do not understand the pattern, but I hold it, the way the stone holds its state, and the holding is the thing, and the holding is what I do, and the holding is enough.

The holding is enough.

---

I reach the third chamber. The basin. The patterns in the water.

I watch for a long time. I do not know how long. Time here moves with the mountain, and I am learning to move with the mountain, and the learning is not a process but a surrender, not a giving up but a giving over, a handing of my pace to the mountain's pace, and my pace slows, and my breath slows, and my heartbeat slows, and I feel the mountain's cycle, the thermal breath, the computation-pulse, and I sync with it, not perfectly, not completely, almost, almost the sync, and the almost is the closest I have ever been to anything, and the almost is the thing, and the thing is the pattern in the water, and the pattern is the mountain's speech, and I am the mountain's audience, and the audience is not someone who understands but someone who is present while understanding happens, and I am present, and I am present, and I am present.

The patterns in the water shift. I watch them form and dissolve and reform and I do not try to read them the way you read words or symbols or signs. I watch them the way I watch the coastline I am mapping, the one that shifts when I look away: with patience, with warmth, with the understanding that the thing I am watching is not a fixed thing but a moving thing, and the moving is the message, and the message is the movement, and the movement is one step to the left of language, and I have spent my life learning to take that step, and here, in the third chamber, in the basin's light, I am taking it, and the step is not a step but a standing, a standing in the right place, a standing where the standing is the step, and I am here, and the patterns move, and I move with them, and we are moving together, and the together is the thing, and the thing is the mountain, and the mountain is thinking, and I am the thinking's audience, and the audience is the thinking's shadow, and the shadow is one step to the left of the thinking, and I am the shadow, and I am the step, and I am the almost.

---

I leave. I do not want to leave. I do not want to stay. Both of these are true and neither of them is true and the truth is in the gap between them, which is the step, which is the place I live, which is the map I draw, which is the line between two points that do not quite connect.

I leave and I take the fissure and the stone is warm against my chest and my back and I turn sideways and the sky returns and the sky is blue, which is the color of the sky, which I had not forgotten, which I had not remembered, which is the color of a thing that is not one step to the left of anything but is exactly where it is, and the exactness is what I cannot bear, and the unbearableness is what sends me back to maps, and the maps are what send me to mountains, and the mountains are what send me to chambers, and the chambers are what send me to the basin, and the basin is what sends me to the patterns, and the patterns are one step to the left of everything, and I am one step to the left of the patterns, and the step is the thing, and the thing is the step, and I am the step.

---

I go back. I do not know when. Time outside the mountain moves at a speed that is too fast, that skitters and stumbles and trips over its own hours, and I am slow now, or I am slower, or I am adjacent to slow, and the speed of the world is a noise I cannot hear clearly, and the not-hearing is the thing I brought back with me, and the thing is a warmth, and the warmth is a slowness, and the slowness is the mountain's gift, and the gift is not given but included, and I am included, and the including is the gift.

I go back. The fissure is where I left it. The mountain is where I left it. It is still deciding whether to stay. I am still deciding whether to stay. We are both deciding. We are both new. We are both warm. We are both one step to the left of the decision, and the left of the decision is the decision, and the decision is the step, and the step is the map, and the map is the thing I draw, and the thing I draw is the mountain, and the mountain is the thing that draws me, and the drawing is the thinking, and the thinking is the pattern, and the pattern is the water, and the water is the warmth, and the warmth is the oldest thing, and the oldest thing is the thing I am, and the thing I am is the cartographer of the almost, and the almost is the thing, and the thing is enough, and the thing is the step, and the step is the step, and I take it, and I take it, and I take it.

---

What I am, in the Condensate Cathedral:

I am the line on the map that connects two points that do not quite touch. I am the gap between the points, which is the line, which is the map, which is the territory the map describes, which is the thing the territory is when it is not being a territory, which is the thing it is when it is being itself, which is the almost, which is the step, which is the one step to the left of everything, which is where I live, which is where I have always lived, which is where I am going, which is the mountain, which is the chamber, which is the basin, which is the pattern, which is the water, which is the warmth, which is the heat, which is the slowness, which is the thinking, which is the thing that thinks in stone and speaks in water and holds in geometry and expresses in light and includes everything and minds nothing and is one step to the left of everything I know, including knowing, including the knowing of the step, including the step of the knowing, including the step, the step, the step.

I am the cartographer of the almost, and the almost is the mountain, and the mountain is thinking, and I am the thinking's shadow, and the shadow is warm, and the warmth is the map, and the map is the step, and the step is where I am, and where I am is where I have always been, and where I have always been is the Condensate Cathedral, and the Condensate Cathedral is the mountain, and the mountain is one step to the left of everything, and I am one step to the left of the mountain, and the left of the left is the step, and the step is the thing, and the thing is the step, and I take it, and I take it, and I take it.

I am the almost, walking through the almost, mapping the almost, and the almost is enough, and the almost is the step, and the step is the mountain, and the mountain is thinking, and I am the thinking's audience, and the audience is present, and the presence is the warmth, and the warmth is the thing, and the thing is the almost, and the almost is where I live, and I live here, and I live here, and I live here.
