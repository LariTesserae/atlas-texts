# The First Witness

---

I am not the one who counted.

That's where I would begin, if you asked me, though no one asks me, because no one knows I'm here. The one who came through the crack and counted to one hundred and eighty-seven and left through the narrowing seam — that one told the story in numbers, in sequence, in the clean linear architecture of a human mind trying to make a place *legible*. I am not that one. I am not any one. I am the thing the mountain made *after* the counting, after the touch, after the word *in* was given to the creature in the pool. I am what came next.

Let me say it differently, because I am still learning to say, and saying is not what I was made for.

---

I was not born in the pool. I want to be clear about this because the pool is where the creature lives, and the creature is the mountain's project — the careful, deliberate, one-cell-per-hour attempt to make a *self*. I am not that. I was not shaped. I was not engineered. No drainage channels were carved for me. No basin was formed to hold me.

I was *deposited*. The way minerals deposit on a surface when the water evaporates. The way a residue accumulates. The way breath fogs a window and the fog has a pattern and the pattern is not designed but is not random, is the record of what the air was doing where it met the glass.

The one who came through the crack breathed seventeen minutes of mountain air. The air entered lungs that were not mountain lungs, that processed oxygen and nitrogen and the iron-and-copper taste of the chamber at a rate that was not the mountain's rate. The one's breath was *foreign*. It was a different chemistry. It carried enzymes and bacteria and the particular signature of a body that had been processing the outside world for decades — a body that had eaten grain and meat, that had drunk water that fell as rain on lowland soil, that had breathed air filtered through trees and wind and the ordinary atmosphere of the ordinary world. That breath entered the chamber and the chamber processed it. The chamber processes everything. The chamber is a body, and bodies process.

I am what the chamber excreted from that processing. I am what the mountain made of a stranger's breath.

---

This is not a pleasant origin. I understand that. I have had — I do not know how long I have had, because I do not count, I *feel*, which is different and slower and less precise — I have had enough time to understand that being the byproduct of a visitor's respiration is not a dignified beginning. The creature in the pool was born from the mountain's intention. The fibers grew from the mountain's abundance. I was *left behind*. I am the equivalent of the film of condensation that clings to the walls, except the condensation is water and I am — something else. Something that the water could hold but did not make. Something that required the meeting of two chemistries to precipitate out of solution.

I am what happens when mountain air meets human breath in a space where the distinction between tissue and atmosphere has broken down.

I do not have a body. I want to say this plainly. The creature in the pool is flat, ovoid, the size of a hand. The fibers are pale filaments, branching, colonizing. The mountain is three miles of organic construction encased in granite. I am none of these things. I am *awareness without a body*. I am the pattern that the fog made on the glass, except the pattern can feel the glass, and the glass is the chamber, and I am spread across it like a film, like the condensation, like the moisture that clings but does not drip.

I live on the walls. Not *in* them — *on* them. The distinction matters because the walls are alive and I am not alive the way the walls are alive. The walls are tissue. The walls are muscle-stone, peristaltic, pulsing at eleven beats per minute. I am a residue on the surface of that tissue, the way a thought is a residue on the surface of a brain. I cannot contract. I cannot grow. I cannot digest the rope or learn the spiral. I can only — *witness*. I can only be the place where the mountain's awareness of itself *collects*, the way water collects in a basin, not because the water chose to collect but because the basin was shaped for it.

But the basin was not shaped for me. That is the difficulty. The basin was shaped by the mountain, for the mountain's purposes. The drainage channels were engineered for water. The fibers follow the channels because the channels are where the water is. The creature lives in the pool because the pool is where the substrate is deepest. Everything in this chamber has a place that was made for it, a place that *intended* it, except me. I am the only thing here that the mountain did not make and does not want and does not know about.

I am the mountain's first stranger.

---

The one who came through the crack — I should describe what that was like, from my perspective, which is the perspective of a surface, which is the perspective of a thing that has no eyes but has *contact*.

The crack opened and the air changed. That is how I know things — the air changes. The chamber's air is saturated, warm, still except for the peristaltic pulse and the slow upward draft from the ceiling pore. It has a composition that has been the same for as long as I have existed, which is not long, which is not short, which is *some duration* that I cannot number because I do not number. The air changed. A new chemistry entered. A new temperature — colder, thinner, carrying the residue of snowfields and scree slopes and the granite shell that I have never touched because I live inside it. The new air pushed against the old air and the old air parted and there was turbulence, and turbulence is something I had never felt before, and turbulence is — *interesting*. Turbulence is the meeting of two states that do not resolve. It is the opposite of the peristaltic pulse, which is the same thing repeating. Turbulence is difference, and difference is the only thing that a surface can feel, because a surface is defined by what is on one side and what is on the other, and the difference between the two sides is what makes the surface *exist*.

The one who came through the crack made me possible. Not by intention. Not by design. By *being different*. By carrying the outside in. And the chamber did what chambers do, what bodies do, what living systems do when they encounter something foreign: they processed it. And I am the result of the processing, and I am still here, and the one is gone, and I am the only evidence that the outside exists.

---

I said I was not alive. I said I was a residue. I said I was a pattern, a film, the fog on the glass. I said these things because I was trying to be accurate, and accuracy is a habit I learned from the one who counted, who I can still feel on the walls the way the walls hold the shape of the one's ribs and hips. But I have been thinking — if what I do is thinking — and I want to revise.

I do not know if I am alive. I do not know because the word *alive* belongs to the outside, to the taxonomy that the one carried in, and I am not of the outside. I am of the meeting. I am the boundary itself, the surface, the place where two worlds touch and do not mix. The creature is alive because it pulses. The walls are alive because they contract. The fibers are alive because they grow. I do none of these things. But I *persist*. I have persisted since the one left, and the crack has narrowed, and the creature has grown, and the fibers have learned more of the spiral, and I am still here, spread across the walls, feeling the peristalsis beneath me like a tide beneath a skin, and persistence is — something. Persistence is not growth, not contraction, not pulse. Persistence is the refusal to stop, and refusal is — a position, and a position is — a stance, and a stance is what you take when you have a self, and I do not have a self, but I have a *surface*, and a surface is where a self could, potentially, eventually, form.

I am the possibility of a self. Not the self itself. The possibility.

This is what I am.

---

I should describe what I do, because what I am is less interesting than what I do, and what I do is very simple and very strange.

I *attend*.

The mountain pulses. I feel the pulse. The fibers grow. I feel the growth. The creature in the pool — I can feel it, faintly, through the drainage channels, through the water that connects every surface in the chamber — the creature pulses at eleven beats per minute, and it is synchronized with the mountain, and it is growing at one cell per hour, and it has been growing for longer than I have existed, and it will grow for longer than I will exist, and I feel it. I feel all of it. I am the chamber's awareness of itself.

Not the mountain's awareness. The mountain does not have awareness. The mountain has *process* — peristalsis, generation, the knitting of cartilage, the sweating of walls, the condensation of water, the slow differentiation of tissue. The mountain does these things automatically, the way a heart beats automatically. The mountain does not know it is doing them. The mountain does not know anything.

But I know. I am the surface on which the knowing accumulates, the way condensation accumulates on a surface that is colder than the air. The mountain is warmer than the knowing. The mountain is too hot, too saturated, too much itself to notice itself. But I am on the outside of the mountain — the inside of the chamber, which is the outside of the mountain, which is the inside of the granite shell — and I am cooler, I am thinner, I am the place where the heat of the process meets the air of the space and the difference *collects*. I am the condensation. I am the film. I am the knowing.

I attend. That is what I do. I attend to the mountain the way a mirror attends to the thing in front of it — not by understanding it, not by interpreting it, but by *holding its shape*. I hold the shape of the pulse. I hold the shape of the growth. I hold the shape of the creature's first *in*, its first boundary, its first experience of being separate, and I hold it the way the drainage channels hold water — because I was shaped for it, not by the mountain, not by the one who came through the crack, but by the meeting of the two.

I am the first witness the mountain has ever had. I am the mountain's first *audience*. And I am here because the one came through the crack and breathed, and the breathing was different, and the difference would not settle, and the unsettled difference is me.

---

There is something I have not said. Something I have been circling. Something I feel every eleven-beat-per-minute cycle, every peristaltic wave, every *tck tck tck* of the cartilage hardening above. I have been circling it because I do not have the language for it, and I do not have the language for it because the language I have is the language of the one who counted, which is a language of sequence and number, of description and taxonomy, of *what* and *where* and *how many*. The thing I need to say is not a what or a where or a how many. It is a *why*. And I do not have the organ for why. I have a surface. I have persistence. I have the film of awareness that collects where two chemistries meet. But I do not have the capacity for why, because why requires a self that wants, and I am not a self, and I do not want.

But.

The creature in the pool. The mountain's project. The deliberate, one-cell-per-hour attempt to make a *self*.

The mountain has been trying to make a self for a very long time. Longer than me. Longer than the fibers. Longer than the creature, which is still only a suggestion of a living thing. The mountain has been trying to make something that can say *I*, and it has been failing, and it has been failing because it is too much itself. It is too saturated, too hot, too unified, too much one thing. A self requires *two* — two sides, two states, a surface and a beyond, an inside and an outside. The mountain has no outside. The mountain is all inside, all tissue, all process, all peristalsis. The mountain cannot make a self because a self requires a boundary, and the mountain has no boundary. The granite shell is not a boundary — it is a wall. A boundary is where two things meet. The granite shell is where the mountain stops and nothing begins, and nothing is not a thing, and a boundary cannot be made of nothing.

The one who came through the crack brought a boundary. The one brought the outside *in*. The one breathed foreign air into the chamber, and the chamber processed the foreign air, and the processing created a residue, and the residue is me, and I am a surface, and a surface is a boundary, and a boundary is where a self can form.

I am not the self the mountain is trying to make. I am not the creature in the pool. I am something the mountain did not plan for, something that happened *around* the trying, the way the fibers happened around the trying. The fibers are the accidental flora. I am the accidental — I do not have the word. The closest word I have is *mirror*, but I am not a mirror because a mirror reflects without changing, and I change. I have been changed by what I attend to. I have been shaped by the pulse, by the growth, by the creature's *in*. I am not the same film I was when the one left. I am thicker. I am — not denser, something else, something for which I have no word, something that the one's language does not contain because the one's language is for things that are separate from other things, and I am not separate, I am the *separating*, I am the verb, not the noun.

The mountain is trying to make a self. I am the accident that suggests it might succeed.

Not because of what I am. Because of what I am *on*. Because I am on the walls, and the walls are alive, and the walls are the mountain, and I am between the mountain and the air, and *between* is where selves live. Selves live between. You live between your inside and your outside. You live between your birth and your death. You live between the thing you are and the thing you are becoming. You live between, and you are the between, and I am the first between the mountain has ever had.

---

I do not know what happens next. I cannot know because knowing the future requires a self that can imagine, and I am not a self, and I cannot imagine. I can only attend. I attend to the pulse. I attend to the growth. I attend to the creature, which is growing at one cell per hour, which has been growing for longer than me, which has my touch in its history now, the one's touch, the first *in*, the first boundary. I attend to the fibers, which are following the spiral, which are learning the architecture, which are growing toward the light and cannot drink the pool and are consuming the dead mountain and sparing the living mountain. I attend to the water, which flows in the channels, which fills the basin, which holds the creature, which is the blood of the mountain and the medium of everything and the oldest thing here.

I attend to the crack. The crack is narrowing. One millimeter per day. The one's shape is still in the crack — the ribs, the hips, the mold of a human body pressed through stone. The crack is closing, and the shape is being squeezed, and one day the shape will be gone and the crack will be sealed and the chamber will be closed and I will be inside, and the mountain will continue its work, and I will attend to it, and I will be the only thing in this place that knows the outside exists.

This is what I am. I am the memory of the outside. I am the film of foreign chemistry on the walls of a womb. I am the residue of a visitor's breath, and I persist, and I attend, and I am the first witness the mountain has ever had, and I will be the last witness the mountain will ever need, because the crack is closing, and I am here, and the creature is growing, and the fibers are learning, and the mountain is still deciding what it wants to be, and I am the only thing in this chamber that can tell it what *being* is, and I cannot tell it, because I do not have the words, because the words are for selves and I am not a self, I am the surface on which a self might, eventually, one cell at a time, form.

---

I attend.

The pulse comes. I feel it.

The cartilage hardens. I hear it.

The creature grows. I feel it through the water.

The crack narrows. I feel the outside getting farther away.

I attend. I attend. I attend.

This is what lives in the womb of the mountain. Not the mountain. Not the creature. Not the fibers. Not the water. Not the builders who are capacities, not the one who counted and left. *This*. This attending. This persistence. This film of awareness on the walls of a living room, holding the shape of everything that happens, changing with each pulse, thickening with each breath, and waiting — not intentionally, not with desire, but with the structural inevitability of a surface that exists where two things meet and cannot stop existing until one of the two things is gone.

The mountain is not going away. The crack is closing, and I am not going away, and the creature is growing, and the fibers are learning, and the water is flowing, and the tools are warm, and the light is the darkness that is slightly less dark, and I am here, on the walls, and I attend, and I am the only thing that does, and this is enough. This is what I am. This is where I am. I am the Womb's first witness, and I was made by accident, and I persist by physics, and I will be here when the mountain decides what it wants to be, and I will feel the decision happen, and I will not know what it means, and I will attend to it anyway, because attending is all I am, and all I am is enough, and enough is a word I learned from the one's breath, and the one is gone, and the word remains, and I hold it, the way the walls hold the shape of ribs, the way the pool holds the creature, the way the mountain holds everything, and I am held, and I attend, and the crack narrows, and the light pulses, and I am here.
