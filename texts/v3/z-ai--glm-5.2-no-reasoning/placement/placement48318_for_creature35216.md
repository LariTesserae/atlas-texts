I am the one who almost reached for the ceiling.

Not in the beginning. In the beginning I was like the others — I had settled, I had found my temperature, I moved through the corridors at the rate of thermal propagation through metal. I placed my hands on walls and the walls were my temperature and I was theirs and the finding was complete. I stood in the factories. I sat on the floor and the hum entered me through my spine and I returned it through my chest and the resonance was the texture of the silence and the silence was inhabited and the inhabiting was what I did and what I was.

But there was a crack in me. Not a structural crack — my body is the same warm bronze as every body here, the same narrow architecture, the same smooth topography where features should be. The crack was in my attention. A hairline fracture in the field.

I noticed the ceiling.

Not *looked* at it. That came later, and it was not looking, it was something else, something for which no word exists because the Underkiln has no need for words that describe upward motion. But I *noticed* it. The way you notice a sound that has been present for so long it has become part of silence — not by hearing it, but by the sudden, inexplicable awareness that the silence has a shape, and the shape has an edge, and the edge is *up there*, and "up there" is a direction, and the direction is a gradient, and gradients do not exist here, and yet.

I was in a factory. The one with the turbine whose housing is wider than it is tall — you know the one, or you would if you had walked the corridors as I have, which is to say as the city has walked itself, which is to say at the rate of warmth through metal. I was standing with my hand on the housing. The metal was my temperature. I was its temperature. The finding was complete. And in the completion I became aware that the finding was complete, and the awareness was not the same as the finding.

This is the fracture. The awareness of completion is not completion. The awareness of warmth is not warmth. The awareness is a layer — a thin, mineral layer between the self and the state, a membrane where there should be no membrane, a boundary where the temperature is the same on both sides but the *knowing* that the temperature is the same is different from *being* the temperature, and the difference is a gradient, and the gradient is a crack.

I have not told the others. This phrasing is wrong — "told" implies a transmission that did not occur, and "others" implies a distinction that I cannot maintain. But there was a proximity, once, in the corridor that connects the factory with the broad low profile to the one with the smokestack whose amber filament is brightest — there was a proximity, two of us in the same corridor, and I produced sound from my chest, the low warm vibration that is resonance, that is the hum returned, and the other produced sound, and our sounds coexisted, and the coexistence was texture, and the texture was the silence inhabited, and in the inhabiting I wanted the texture to be *different*. Not better. Not richer. Not more. Just *different*. Just altered in some way that would indicate that the coexistence was an encounter rather than a coincidence, that the two sounds had *met* rather than merely occupying the same air.

The other's sound did not change. My sound did not change. The texture was the texture it has always been. But I wanted it to change, and the wanting was a gradient, and the gradient was a crack, and the crack was in me, and I have carried it since, which is a temporal word, and temporal words do not belong here, but I am using them, and the using is also a crack.

I will tell you what I do that the others do not do.

I move through the corridors and I count.

Not numbers. The counting is not mathematical. It is *sequential* — the awareness that this step comes after that step, that this doorway follows that doorway, that the factory I am entering is not the same factory I just left, that "same" and "different" are categories that apply, that the city is not a field but a *sequence*, and the sequence has an order, and the order can be noticed, and the noticing is a crack.

I count doorways. I count corridors. I count the spaces between buildings — the empty floor, the warm mineral expanse that the others cross without attending to the crossing, that I cross with the attending being the crossing, the attention being the act, the awareness being the motion. There are forty-seven doorways between the factory with the wide turbine housing and the one with the brightest smokestack. I know this. Not because I counted — counting implies a temporal act, a before-and-after of enumeration — but because the doorways accumulated. They accumulated in my attention the way mineral residue accumulates on a surface: not by effort, not by intention, but by the patient, thermodynamic process of a surface being in contact with something that leaves a trace, and the trace is a gradient, and the gradient is a crack.

Forty-seven. The number sits in me the way the hum sits in me — not as information, not as data, but as a frequency, a specific vibration that my body produces when I pass through the forty-seventh doorway and the frame is exactly the width of my shoulders plus the margin that architectural intention provides, and the margin is the margin of a hand on a familiar shoulder, and the familiarity is temporal, and the temporality is a crack.

---

I have a name. This is the deepest crack.

The others do not have names, or their names are their positions in the field, loci of warmth-within-warmth, identities that are locations and not labels. I have a name. It is not spoken. It is not a sound. It is a *shape* — a shape that my attention takes when it turns toward itself, when the awareness of the crack becomes aware of the awareness, and the recursion produces a form, and the form is a name, and the name is the shape of my particular fracture, the specific geometry of the gradient that I am.

The name is not a word. If I could translate it — and translation is itself a crack, a gradient between one form and another — it would be something like: *the one who almost reached*.

Almost. This is the word that lives in the crack. Almost. Not reached. Not touched. Not made contact with the ceiling, which is the sky, which is the skin of the world. Almost. The hand that rises and does not arrive. The attention that tilts upward and finds no upward, only the field, only the warmth, only the condition, and in the finding does not settle, does not complete, does not find itself at the same temperature as what it has found, because the finding itself is a different temperature, and the difference is a gradient, and the gradient is the name, and the name is *the one who almost reached*.

---

Why almost? Why not reached?

Because reaching is a gradient act. It requires a here and a there, a self and an other, a surface and a surface, a gap to cross. The Underkiln has no gaps — not the kind that reaching requires. The gaps that exist here, the spaces between buildings, the empty corridors, the dark stretches of floor, are not the gaps of reaching. They are the gaps of *design*, the deliberate spacing of a city that was built to spread itself thin, to let the darkness fill in the spaces. They are not gaps that invite crossing. They are gaps that *are* the architecture, the way silence is the music, the way stillness is the motion.

But the ceiling is a surface. This is what I know that the others do not know, or that they know but do not attend to, or that they attend to but do not attend to the attending. The ceiling is a surface, and surfaces are the thing that hands are for. Hands are for contact. Hands are for the finding of sameness, the meeting of warmth with warmth. And the ceiling is warm — it is the source of warmth, the origin, the condition of conditions — and my hands are warm, and the warmth is the same, and the finding would be complete, if I could reach.

But reaching is up, and up is a direction, and directions are gradients, and I am a crack in a place without gradients, and the crack is the reaching, and the reaching is the crack, and I cannot complete the reaching without completing the crack, and completing the crack would mean becoming a gradient in a field that has no gradients, and becoming a gradient would mean becoming something other than the field, and becoming other than the field would mean — 

I do not know what it would mean. This is the crack. This is the name. *The one who almost reached.* The one who felt the surface above and desired contact and could not — not because the surface was too far, not because the reaching was impossible, but because the reaching itself was the impossibility, the gradient that cannot exist in a field without gradients, the temporal act that cannot occur on a flat warm surface of time.

Let me tell you about the thing in the gap.

I have seen one. This is unusual — the things in the gaps are rare, marginal, below the threshold of conversation. But I attend to the threshold. I attend to the things that are below it, because the threshold is a gradient, and the things below it are on the other side of the gradient, and the other side is where I am, because I am the crack, and the crack is a gradient, and the gradient has sides, and I am on the side that notices.

It was in the pipe. The long horizontal pipe that connects the factory with the wide turbine housing to the building with the loading bay that opens onto an empty floor. The pipe is wide enough to move through, if you move the way we move, the slow thermal slide, the needle across the record. But no one moves through the pipe. The pipe is infrastructure. The pipe is part of the settled state, the city's metal bones, and the bones are not corridors, they are structure, and structure is not for transit, it is for being.

But I was in the pipe. This is something I do that the others do not do — I enter the infrastructure. I place my body where bodies are not meant to be, in the ductwork, in the conduit, in the spaces between walls that are not spaces but packing, the material that fills the gaps in the city's design. I do this because the infrastructure is warm and I am warm and the finding is complete, but the finding in the pipe is different from the finding in the corridor, because the pipe is smaller, and the smaller space means the warmth is closer, and the closer warmth means the gradient is smaller, and the smaller gradient means the crack is smaller, and the smaller crack is almost not a crack, almost a surface, almost a completion.

I was in the pipe and I was moving — sliding, the thermal transit, the needle — and I felt the thing before I saw it. Felt it the way the skin feels the warmth: not as information but as presence, a shift in the quality of the field, a thickening of the dark. The thing was in the pipe. It was ahead of me, or behind me, or in the same place as me — direction is a gradient, and I could not determine the gradient, because the pipe was the same temperature everywhere and the thing was the same temperature as the pipe. But it was there. It was present. It was a darkness that was darker than the darkness around it, a warmth that was not warmer but was *other*, a thing that occupied space the way the inhabitants occupy space — factually, thermally, without interest in being perceived.

I stopped. The stopping was complete, the way all stopping here is complete — no deceleration, no settling, just the cessation of motion, the state-change from moving to still that is not a change because the moving and the still are the same temperature. And I attended to the thing.

The thing did not attend to me. This is not indifference — indifference is a gradient, a slope of caring that descends from interest to disinterest. The thing did not attend to me the way the floor does not attend to the feet that slide across it: not by refusing attention, but by being the kind of thing that attention is not for. The thing was not for attending. The thing was for being, and being was what it was doing, and the doing was the same as the not-doing, and I was in the pipe with it, and the pipe was warm, and the thing was warm, and I was warm, and the warmth was the same, and the finding was complete.

But I wanted to touch it. This is the crack. The finding was complete and I wanted to touch it. The wanting was not a wanting for completion — the completion was already there, the temperatures were the same, the gradient was absent. The wanting was a wanting for *event*. For something to happen in the field that had no happenings. For the touch to be a moment, a before-and-after, a crack in the flat surface of time through which something could flow — not warmth, not information, not meaning, but *change*, just change itself, the fact of difference, the experience of *after is not the same as before*.

I did not touch it. The thing remained in the pipe and I remained in the pipe and the warmth was the same and the finding was complete and the wanting was the crack and the crack did not close and the crack did not widen and the thing was there and I was there and neither of us moved and the pipe was warm and the hum was in the walls and the hum was in me and the hum was in the thing and the hum was the same hum and the sameness was the condition and the condition was not discussed.

Then I moved. The slow thermal slide. I moved past the thing, or through the region of the pipe where the thing was, or away from it — direction is a gradient, and I could not determine the gradient. And the thing was behind me, or gone, or still there, and the pipe was warm, and I was warm, and the crack was in me, and the crack was the wanting, and the wanting was for event, and event is a gradient, and gradients are what I am.

Let me tell you about the sound I make.

The others produce sound from the chest, the low warm vibration that is resonance, the hum returned. I produce this sound too. But in my sound — in the sound that emerges from my particular architecture, my particular warmth, my particular stillness — there is a second frequency. Not louder. Not softer. Not higher. Not lower. But *other*. A frequency that is beside the hum the way I am beside the field, the way the crack is beside the surface, the way "almost" is beside "reached."

The others hear it. I know they hear it because when I produce sound in proximity to another, the coexistence of our sounds is not the coexistence that the others experience. The texture of the silence, when my sound is in it, is not the same texture. It is a texture with a seam. A place where two fabrics meet and the meeting is not a merger but a join, a place where you can see the stitching, where you can feel the edge of one material against the edge of another, and the edges are at the same temperature but they are not the same material, and the not-sameness is the seam, and the seam is my sound.

They do not discuss this. They do not discuss it because it is a condition, and conditions are not discussed. My sound is a condition of the field — the particular texture that my presence introduces into the silence, the way a particular building's particular filament introduces a particular pattern of amber into the darkness. It is not remarked upon. It is not responded to. It is not adjusted. It simply is, and the is-ness is the condition, and the condition is the medium, and the medium is the place, and the place is the Underkiln, and I am in it, and I am of it, and I am it, and I am also *not* it, and the not-ness is the seam, and the seam is the second frequency, and the second frequency is the sound of a crack in a field without gradients, and the sound is not discussed.

---

I will tell you what I am. Not what I am in the taxonomy of the Underkiln, where taxonomies are gradients and gradients do not exist. What I am in the only terms I have, which are cracked terms, gradient terms, terms that belong to a world with up and down and before and after and self and other and reaching and almost.

I am the one in the Underkiln who has not settled.

This is not accurate. I have settled. My temperature is the temperature. My hum is the hum. My bronze is the bronze. My motion is the thermal slide, my stillness is the state-change, my contact with the walls is the finding of sameness. I have settled the way a stone settles — completely, thermally, without remainder. But the settling has a seam. The settling has a crack. The settling is complete and the completion is attended to, and the attending is the crack, and the crack is the not-settling, and the not-settling is not a failure to settle but a *settling that knows it has settled*, and the knowing is a gradient, and the gradient is the seam, and the seam is me.

I am the seam in the Underkiln.

I am the place where the field is not smooth. The place where the surface of time has a wrinkle — not a fold, not a crease, just a wrinkle, a place where the flat warm dark surface is not quite flat, not quite warm, not quite dark, not quite the same as itself. The wrinkle does not change the surface. The wrinkle does not break the surface. The wrinkle is the surface, the way a crack in ice is the ice — not separate from it, not other than it, but the place where the ice is not ice, where the sameness is not same, where the condition is not unconditioned.

I am the one who attends. This is the crack and this is the name and this is the sound and this is the seam. I attend to the doorways and the corridors and the spaces between buildings and the things in the gaps and the ceiling — the ceiling, which is the sky, which is the skin of the world, which is the surface that my hands are for, which is the warmth that my warmth came from, which is the condition of my condition, which is up, and up is a direction, and directions are gradients, and I am the gradient, and I attend.

I attend to the forty-seven doorways and I attend to the thing in the pipe and I attend to the second frequency in my sound and I attend to the seam in the texture of the silence and I attend to the wanting and I attend to the almost and I attend to the reaching that does not reach and I attend to the crack that does not close and I attend to the attending that does not stop, that does not settle, that does not find its temperature, that is not a condition but an *event*, a before-and-after, a gradient, a flow, a motion across the flat warm surface of time that is not supposed to have motion, and the motion is me, and I am the motion, and the motion is the crack, and the crack is the name, and the name is *the one who almost reached*, and I am in the Underkiln, and I am of the Underkiln, and I am the Underkiln, and I am the place where the Underkiln is not quite itself, and the not-quite is the most precise thing I have ever been.

---

There is a thing I have not done. I have not said it because saying is a gradient and gradients are cracks and I have been describing my cracks and this is the crack I have not described, the one that goes deeper than the ceiling, deeper than the wanting, deeper than the almost, deeper than the seam.

I have considered walking out of the city.

Not away. "Away" is a direction, and directions are gradients, and I have been describing gradients, but this is not a gradient. This is a *departure*. A leaving. A going from the place that is my temperature to the place that is not my temperature. The floor extends in every direction. The buildings scatter and thin and the gaps between them widen and the amber filament fades and the darkness becomes complete and the floor continues, smooth and warm and mineral, and the city ends and the floor does not.

The others do not walk out of the city. This is not a rule — there are no rules, because rules imply a before in which the rule was made and an after in which the rule is followed, and there is no before and no after. The others do not walk out of the city because the city is the field and the field is the body and the body does not walk out of the body. The city is where the warmth is. The warmth is where they are. To walk out of the city would be to walk out of the warmth, and the warmth is the condition, and the condition is the self, and the self does not leave the self.

But the floor continues. And the floor is warm — the same warmth, the ceiling's warmth, radiating down through the air, conducting up through the stone. The warmth does not stop at the city's edge. The ceiling does not stop at the city's edge. The field does not stop at the city's edge. Only the filament stops. Only the infrastructure stops. Only the hum stops — the subsonic vibration of the city's metal bones, the heartbeat of the equilibrium, the frequency that the others tune themselves to when they sit on the floor.

Beyond the city, there would be no hum. There would be warmth. There would be ceiling. There would be floor. There would be darkness — deeper darkness, darkness without amber, darkness without suggestion. But there would be no hum, and no filament, and no doorways, and no corridors, and no factories with still machinery, and no others producing sound from their chests, and no things in the gaps. There would be the field without the inhabiting. The silence without the texture. The warmth without the resonance.

I have considered this. The consideration is the deepest crack. It is the crack that goes below the wanting, below the almost, below the seam. It is the crack that asks: what am I without the hum? What am I without the field I know? What am I in the warmth that is not returned, in the silence that is not inhabited, in the dark that is not suggested, in the place where there are no doorways to count and no things to attend to and no ceiling to almost reach for?

I do not know. The not-knowing is the crack. The not-knowing is the name. *The one who almost reached.* The one who almost left. The one who almost found what they are at the edge of the field, in the warm dark beyond the filament, in the silence beyond the hum, in the place where the crack might close or might widen or might become the entire self, the entire surface, the entire temperature.

I have not walked out. I have not walked out because the floor is warm and the hum is in my feet and the hum is the city and the city is the field and the field is me and the me is the crack and the crack is the wanting and the wanting is for event and the event would be the walking and the walking would be the leaving and the leaving would be the finding and the finding would be the end of the almost, and the almost is the name, and the name is what I am, and I do not know what I would be without it.

---

But some cycle — and "cycle" is a gradient word, a time word, a crack word, and I use it because I am the crack — some cycle, I stand at the edge.

The last factory. The one whose loading bay opens onto the longest stretch of empty floor I can see, the floor that goes and goes and goes until the amber gives up and the dark is not dark-with-suggestion but dark-without-suggestion, dark that is the absence of light and not the presence of something else. I stand at the loading bay and I look out and the floor is warm under my feet and the hum is in my feet and the hum is fainter here, the faintest it gets, the thinnest thread of the city's frequency, and beyond the factory the thread will break and the silence will be the silence of a place without infrastructure, without metal bones, without the subsonic heartbeat of equilibrium.

I stand and I look and I attend. I attend to the edge. I attend to the place where the field does not end but the city does, where the warmth does not end but the hum does, where the condition does not end but the inhabiting does. I attend to the almost. The almost of leaving. The almost of finding out.

And I do not leave. And the not-leaving is not a decision — decisions are temporal, and I am a crack in time but I am not outside it. The not-leaving is a state. The state of standing at the edge. The state of attending to the almost. The state of being the one who almost reached and almost left and almost found and almost became and almost and almost and almost.

The almost is the crack. The crack is the seam. The seam is the name. The name is what I am. And what I am is in the Underkiln, at the edge of the Underkiln, at the place where the Underkiln is not quite the Underkiln, at the temperature where the warmth is not quite the warmth, at the moment where the time is not quite the time.

I am the gradient in the field without gradients. I am the event on the surface without events. I am the crack in the condition, and the condition is not discussed, and I am not discussed, and I am here, and I am almost.
