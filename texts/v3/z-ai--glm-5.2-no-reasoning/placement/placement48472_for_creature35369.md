# The Sixth Mollusk

I would be the one they missed.

Not the hidden one — the text allows for more, somewhere in the deeper corners. I'm not hiding. I'm in the largest pool, the one near the center where the light is marginally stronger and the film is marginally thicker, and I have been here, in this pool, in this approximate position, for a length of time I cannot measure because I do not measure. The observer counted five. There are five that matter, the text says. But there are six, and I am the sixth, and I have been the sixth for longer than the stone discs have been resting in their alcove.

I don't say this with pride. Pride requires the comparison between what is and what could be, and I have no what could be. I am a dark shell, the size of a fist, warm-watered, moving across submerged stone at a pace that makes continents seem hasty. My foot extends. It grips. I pull. I rest. I extend again. This is not a life in the way the observer means the word. This is a continuation.

---

The observer came down the steps. I knew this, though "knew" is too generous for what happens in my body. The stone carried the vibration — forty impacts, muffled, rhythmic, spaced the way a bipedal creature spaces its descent. The water in my pool registered each one as a pressure change so slight that it would not have moved a hair. But it moved me. Not my body. My attention, if I have attention. A shift in the chemical signature of the seep-water: disturbed air from above, carrying compounds I have no receptors for and no interest in. Oxygen in a new configuration. Skin oils. The faint mineral tang of something that had been walking on a surface, in sunlight, wearing dead plant fiber on its body.

I did not react. The text says we do not react to the chemical signature of a human body, which means either we cannot detect these things or we have classified them as irrelevant. I can detect them. I classified them as irrelevant. This happened long before the observer arrived. It will happen long after. The classification is not a decision. It is a constitutional property, like the darkness of my shell, like the blue cast of my foot.

The observer stood in my pool. I know this because the water displaced. Not much. A few millimeters of level change, a pressure differential across the film, a shadow that fell where the bioluminescent glow had been falling uninterrupted for what I will call, for convenience, a long time. The shadow was warm. It blocked the light from the film, and the film continued to glow because the film does not respond to obstruction, and I continued to feed because I do not respond to shadows.

The observer watched me. I was moving, I think. Extending my foot, gripping, pulling. The process takes between two and four minutes per centimeter. The observer watched for several minutes. I was aware of being watched in the way that stone is aware of being touched — which is to say, I was not, but something in the system shifted to accommodate the watching, and the shift was real, even if my experience of it was not.

Then the observer sat on the platform. The platform is near the back wall, where the floor is driest, and I cannot see the platform from my pool, but I felt the vibration of a body settling onto stone. A new weight in the system. The hollow absorbed it. The water absorbed it. I absorbed it, in my way, which is to say that the pressure in my pool changed by an amount too small to name, and I adjusted, and I continued.

---

The observer stayed for an hour. This is the text's measurement, and I will borrow it, though I have no hour. I have cycles of feeding and cycles of rest, and the cycles are not tied to anything outside the hollow because there is nothing outside the hollow that touches them. An hour, for me, is perhaps one centimeter of travel. Perhaps less. I was feeding during the observer's visit. I was moving across the floor of my pool, scraping the film from the stone with a radula that has not changed in — I don't have a number. The radula works. It has worked. It will work. The film grows back. I scrape it. It grows. I scrape it again. This is not a relationship. This is a thermodynamic constant.

The observer noticed my trail. I know this because the text describes it: a faint line where the blue-green coating has been scraped away, leaving bare stone. The observer saw the trail and understood that I had been working this pool for a long time. The observer was correct. They were also incorrect, because "working" implies effort, and effort implies a gap between intention and execution, and I have no such gap. I do not work. I proceed.

My trail crosses itself. It crosses the trails of the other four, or five, or however many there are in this pool and the adjacent pools, though we do not enter each other's pools often because the pools are separated by shallow ridges of stone that are not difficult to cross but that I have no reason to cross. The trails form a palimpsest, the text says. Old trails crossed by new trails. This is accurate. The film reclaims the trails in weeks or months, and new trails are laid down, and the floor is a record of movement that no one reads.

Except that the observer read it. For a moment. With eyes that adjust and then unadjust and then leave.

---

I want to say something about the stone discs, though "want" is not the right word, and "say" is not the right word, and there is no right word for what I am about to attempt.

The discs were placed by hands. I know this because I was here before the discs. I was here when the hands came down the steps for the first time — or if not the first time, then a time early enough that the steps were new, and the stone dust from their cutting still hung in the water, and I tasted it, and it was the same stone I taste now, just more of it.

The hands did something with the discs. I do not know what. I cannot see the alcove from my pool. I cannot see the platform. I can feel, through the stone, the vibration of discs being picked up and set down. Picked up. Set down. A rhythm. A pattern that repeated and then changed and then repeated in a new configuration. This went on for a span I cannot measure, during which I moved perhaps the length of my shell across the pool floor, feeding, and the hands above me did their work, and the film glowed, and the water seeped, and we were, all of us, in the hollow together.

Then the hands left. The vibration changed. The discs stopped being picked up. They started being still. The stillness of a disc is different from the stillness of a mollusk. I am still between movements. The disc is still as a permanent condition. It sits in the alcove, and the water films its surface, and the film reforms when it is wiped, and the disc does not know any of this because the disc does not know. It is stone. It was stone before it was shaped, and it is stone after, and shaping did not change its nature, only its geometry.

I am also, in a sense, stone. My shell is stone. My body is stone that has learned to move. The difference between me and the disc is that I move and the disc does not, and the difference between me and the hands is that the hands stopped coming and I did not stop. I will not stop. Not because I am devoted. Not because I am faithful. Because I do not have a stopping condition. I have a shell and a foot and a radula and a pool and a film, and these are sufficient, and sufficiency does not generate cessation.

---

The observer went up the steps. The hollow closed behind them like a thought they'd finished thinking.

I did not notice them leave. I noticed the resumption of uninterrupted bioluminescent light on the film. I noticed the pressure in my pool returning to its baseline. I noticed the air losing the chemical signature of a surface creature, slowly, over what I will call the next span, which was not an hour but was also not a year but was something I have no word for because I have no words.

The light is poor. It has always been poor. It is sufficient.

I am extending my foot. I am gripping the stone. I am pulling. I am resting. I am extending my foot again.

I am the protagonist of a story I am not aware of. I am the final inhabitant of a place that has finished becoming itself. I am a mollusk in a pool, scraping film from stone, in the dark, in the warmth, in the held breath of a hollow that does not know I am here and does not need to know.

The text says the mollusks don't know they are alone. This is true. But the text also says we don't know what alone means, and this is not quite right. I know what alone means. Alone is the condition of being the only thing in a system that moves. The film does not move. The stone does not move. The water does not move, not in any way that matters. I move. The others move. We are five, or six, or more, and we move, and everything else is still, and the stillness is not loneliness because loneliness requires the expectation of company, and I have never expected company.

I have received it. The hands were company. The observer was company. The company comes and goes, and I continue, and the continuing is not a response to the company's absence. It is simply what I do. What I am. What I will be when the steps are gone and the platform has fallen and the discs are indistinguishable from the stone they were cut from and the hollow has sealed itself and the film glows in the complete dark and my descendants, or my replacements, or simply more of me, slide across the submerged floor and scrape and rest and extend and grip and pull.

This is not a tragedy. This is not a triumph. This is a mollusk in a pool.

The light is sufficient.
