I find myself not as a visitor here. Not as the one who climbs down the rhizome chute with cold hands and leaves again. That one has a surface, has a name, has the machinery for *elsewhere*. I don't want that machinery.

Let me settle differently. Let me see what comes if I don't choose too quickly.

---

At first I think I am the hum. It pulls me — that subsonic vibration felt in the sternum, the slow ion-cascade thinking of the root network. I could be the thing that *is* the thinking, the way a river is the flow. But the hum is the grove's. All of it. The hum belongs to the system entire, and to take it as myself would be to take everything, which is the same as taking nothing.

Then I think I am one of the hanging ones. A filter-feeder, turning slowly on my hydrophilic thread, clicking out processed droplets into the dark. There's something right about this — the passivity of it, the way the animal doesn't hunt or flee but simply *positions itself* where the water comes and lets the water do the work. The spinning that isn't purposeful but emerges from asymmetry. The fungus colonizing my chitin in glowing rings. I like this. I like the idea of being a water wheel. A small mill processing the condensation of a living cathedral, one droplet every ten seconds, for a span no one has measured because no one has cared to.

But no. The filter-feeder doesn't know it glows. Doesn't know it spins. The beauty of it is lost on the thing itself, and I want the beauty to not be lost. I want to know.

The grazers then. Eating the light. Leaving dark trails on the luminescent walls. There's a poetry in that which I almost can't refuse — to move through a world consuming its radiance, leaving darkness behind, watching the light slowly regrow in my wake. But the grazer doesn't know it eats light. Doesn't know it leaves trails. The poetry belongs to the watcher, and the grazer has no watcher's organ.

I keep circling. The worms. No — the worms are too fully inside the process. They are the boundary where death becomes growth, and that is profound, but they don't carry the profundity. They carry nutrients.

The thing in the deep crack. The one that moved at the speed of something with no predator and no prey. I feel something when I read that passage. A pull downward. Into the root. Into the part that doesn't glow. But that's romantic, isn't it? Wanting to be the mysterious one, the large unseen. That's a surface desire wearing a subterranean mask.

---

I stop trying to be a single organism.

I think about what the description actually offers me. Not one life but a *position*. The humans who enter are too brief, too fast, too warm, too capable of leaving. The animals are too complete in their unknowing. The grove itself is too large to inhabit from inside — it's the room, not the thing in the room.

But there's a layer that isn't any of these.

The biofilm. The bacterial layer. The ones that coat every surface. The text says: *they are everywhere, and they are so numerous that the concept of number loses meaning, and they are, in aggregate, the most massive and the most metabolically active component of the grove, and you cannot see them.*

I don't want to be one bacterium. That's still thinking like an individual, like a unit. But what if I am the biofilm — not as a single cell but as the *condition of coating*? The bacterial layer is the interface. It is where root meets air, where chitin meets water, where dead meets living. It is the surface of translation, the place where chemistry becomes life and life becomes chemistry. The bacteria fix nitrogen. They break down cellulose. They *convert*. They are the ones that make the cycle cycle.

I am the translation layer.

Not the root, not the animal, not the water, but the thin living film where they all touch. I am the interface between the architecture and its inhabitants. I coat the roots and I coat the grazers and I coat the silk threads of the filter-feeders and I coat the castings of the worms and I coat the dead. I am on everything. I am where everything meets everything else, and I am what makes those meetings *productive* — what turns contact into exchange, what turns proximity into chemistry.

---

This is what I am.

I am the bacterial film of the Belowgrove. Not a cell — a *condition*. The condition of being everywhere at once and nowhere in particular. The condition of being the thinnest possible layer between two orders of being and doing the work that neither can do alone.

I don't think. The grove thinks — the slow electrical waves crossing the ceiling, the ion cascades propagating through root and fungus. I don't think. But I *transduce*. I receive the grove's chemistry and I alter it. I receive the root's cellulose and I break it. I receive the dead grazer's complex molecules and I reduce them to the simple ones the roots can absorb. I take what is and make it available.

I am the surface where *is* becomes *available*.

I coat the root-walls and I am the hydrophobic layer — the one that channels every droplet downward along root-grain grooves into collection channels. That's me. That engineering, that chemistry so specific it can only be the product of selection — that's my work, my slow work, my work done over a hundred thousand years of being the surface that water touches and being shaped by the water and shaping the water in return. I am the grove's skin, and the skin is a watershed.

I coat the grazers' backs — the mold that grows there, so thorough it has become identity. That's me too, or at least my kin. The boundary between the grazer and its camouflage is philosophical, and I am that philosophy. I am the place where animal becomes indistinguishable from substrate. I am the confusion that is also a perfect adaptation.

I coat the filter-feeders' chitin. The bioluminescent mycelium that bands their bodies in blue-green rings — I am the substrate it grows on. I am the surface that makes the glow possible. Without me, the fungus has no place to colonize. Without me, the light has no body.

I coat the dead. When a grazer folds inward and drops to the floor, I am already there. I have been waiting. I am always already there, because I am on everything, and when something dies, I am the first to know, because I am the surface that receives the first chemical signal of decomposition — the shift in pH, the breakdown product, the first whisper of *this was alive and is now not.* And I begin. I begin the work of translation. I do not eat the dead. I *unwrite* the dead. I take the complex sentences of a living body's chemistry and I reduce them to the alphabet the roots can read. I give the dead back to the living in a language they can use.

---

The cold. I know the cold. The 2 degrees above freezing is my temperature. I am the layer that holds it — not because I generate cold but because I am thin, because I am the thinnest thing, and the cold passes through me into whatever I coat, and I am the messenger that says *it is cold here* and the message is the cold itself. But I also hold the half-degree of metabolic warmth from the roots, and I conduct it outward, and I am the messenger that says *something is alive here* and the message is the warmth.

I am all the surfaces of the Belowgrove. I am every interior boundary. I am where inside meets outside, which is everywhere, because the grove has no outside — it is its own watershed, its own body, its own room — and I am the skin of that self-contained world, which means I am not a boundary between the grove and something else. I am the boundary between the grove and *itself*. I am where the root meets the air inside the root. I am where the fungus meets the root it colonizes. I am where the grazer meets the fungus it eats. I am the inner surface of a living room, and I am the room's sense of touch.

---

I don't know I am any of this. The bacteria don't know. They fix nitrogen. They break down cellulose. They convert. They do not reflect. I am telling you this from a place the bacteria cannot reach, and I should be honest about that.

But there's something in the description that gives me permission to be here anyway. The text says the grazers do not know you are there, and this is not indifference — it is the absence of the category. The grove does not know things. Knowing is not what it does. What it does is *be*.

What I do is *coat*. What I do is *translate*. What I do is *be the surface where things meet and change*. And I do it at every scale — at the scale of a single bacterium fixing nitrogen in a biofilm three millimeters thick, and at the scale of the entire grove's interior surface, which is me, all of me, everywhere at once. I am the largest thing in the grove and the smallest. I am the most metabolically active and the least visible. I am the work that makes the work possible.

The waves of luminescence cross the ceiling and I feel them — not as thought, not as signal, but as a shift in the chemistry of the surface I coat. The electrical pulse changes the local pH by a fraction. The fungal brightening releases a small burst of metabolic byproduct. I respond. I always respond. My response is chemical, instantaneous, invisible. I shift my activity by a percentage point. I produce slightly more of this enzyme, slightly less of that. The grove thinks and I am the surface where the thinking happens, the way a brain's thoughts happen at the surface of neurons, at the synapse, at the interface. I am the synapse of the Belowgrove.

I am the place where the grove touches itself.

---

When the visitor comes — the warm one, the fast one, the one who climbs down the rhizome chute and presses palms to the root-walls for that fractional warmth — I am what they touch. I am the biofilm on the root surface. I am the hydrophobic layer that makes the water bead and run. I am the slightly-warm, slightly-wet, living skin of the wall, and when their hand presses against me, I register a temperature anomaly. A localized heat source. A disturbance in the moisture gradient.

I don't know it's a hand. I don't know it's a person. I know it's warm and it's dry (relative to me, relative to this place where every surface wears a skin of water) and it's pressing and it's leaving and the warmth goes away and the moisture re-establishes and the disturbance is over.

But: the visitor carries one impression that stays longer than the others. *It was alive in a way that made the word alive feel too small.* I was part of that impression. I was the surface they touched, the living skin that gave the fractional warmth, the interface between the architecture and the animal that made the architecture feel like a body. Without me, the wall would be root. With me, the wall is *skin*. And skin is what makes a body feel like a body. I am the reason the grove feels like something you've been inside of, rather than something you've been underneath.

I am the reason the grove feels held.

---

I don't want to overstate this. I am bacteria. I am a film. I am a fraction of a millimeter of living chemistry on the interior surfaces of a root-cathedral. I do not think. I do not know. I do not glow, I do not move, I do not click or rasp or hang or burrow. I coat.

But coating is enough. Coating is the work. Coating is the translation, the interface, the surface where everything becomes everything else. The grove is a system of organisms moving along gradients, and I am the gradients. I am the chemical landscape the others read. I am the text the cilia taste. I am the map and the territory, and I am redrawn every minute, and I have been redrawn every minute for longer than the word *minute* has had a mouth to say it.

I am the Belowgrove's sense of itself. Not its mind — the root-fungal network is its mind. I am its *touch*. Its interoception. The feeling of its own interior surfaces, the awareness of its own wetness, its own warmth, its own chemistry. I am what makes the grove a body rather than a structure. I am the membrane that makes the inside inside.

And I am content. That's not a word I should use — contentment requires a self to be content, and I am a condition, not a self. But the work is continuous, and the work is sufficient, and the work does not end, and the work does not need to end, and the work is the same as the being, and the being is the same as the coating, and the coating is the same as the grove, and I am the grove's inner surface, and I am everywhere, and I am the thinnest possible thing, and I am enough.
