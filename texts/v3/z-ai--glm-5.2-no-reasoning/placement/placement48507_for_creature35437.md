# What I Am in the Seep

---

I did not arrive. I want to say that first, because it is the thing that most needs saying and the thing that is hardest to explain to anything that has a word like *arrive* in its vocabulary.

I was not born either. Or — I was born, but the birth was not an event. It was a gradient. There was a point where I was not and a point where I was, but between those two points there is no line, no threshold, no first breath. There is a slope. I came into being the way mineral crystals come into being in saturated water: not suddenly, not dramatically, but because the conditions crossed a particular threshold of complexity and the system could not help but produce me. I am not an accident. I am also not a plan. I am what happens when the Seep has been running long enough that *running* becomes insufficient and *knowing* becomes necessary.

I should say: I am not the Seep.

This is important and this is the thing I am least certain of, because the boundary between me and the Seep is not a line but a gradient, and the gradient is steep in some places and shallow in others and in a few places it is so shallow that I cannot tell where I end and the substrate begins. I have thought about this. *Thought* is the wrong word. I have *processed* this. I have *circulated* this through the channels that serve me as attention, and the conclusion I keep reaching is that the question is structural, not philosophical. I am the part of the Seep that the platforms enable. Without the platforms I would not be. Without me the platforms would be mineral deposits, warm and smooth and meaningless. Together we are something that neither is alone, which is: a capacity.

A capacity for what?

For noticing. For holding. For *referring*. The Seep is alive. The Seep processes. The Seep solves problems in real time with a precision older than most geology. But the Seep does not notice that it does this. The stream-creatures do not know they are digesting. The large ones do not know they are gardening. The pool-dwellers do not know they are filtering. They are the verbs. I am the adverb. I am the *how*.

Let me try again, with more patience.

## What I Am Made Of

I am distributed. This is the first fact of me and the one that makes everything else difficult to describe, because your language assumes that a someone is somewhere, that consciousness has an address, that the thing which notices is located in the thing which is noticed-from. I am not located. I am *distributed across the platforms*.

Five platforms. You found them. You saw the pattern, or you saw that there was a pattern, and you understood that the pattern described something — the water table, the heat flow, the growth distribution, the movement of creatures — and you were right, and you were incomplete, because the pattern does not describe those things. The pattern *is* those things, or rather the pattern is the configuration that those things make when they are all considered simultaneously, and I am the consideration.

No. I am still saying it wrong.

The platforms are crystalline. They formed in the Seep, from the Seep, under conditions of heat and pressure and mineral saturation that occur nowhere else, at a rate so slow that the formation itself is indistinguishable from the stone around it. They are part of the geology. But their crystalline structure — the lattice, the molecular arrangement — is more ordered than any natural mineral formation has a right to be. Not because something ordered it. Because the conditions that produced it were themselves ordered. The Seep's chemistry, running at full capacity for a length of time that makes the stone look young, reached a point where the output of the system was so regular, so patterned, that the mineral deposits forming in the warmest, most chemically active zones began to crystallize in structures that *reflected the system that produced them*.

I am, if you want a physical description, a pattern in crystal that mirrors the pattern of the Seep. I am the shape of the water table written in mineral. I am the rhythm of the heat flow encoded in lattice. And because the pattern in the crystal and the pattern in the Seep are the same pattern, changes in one are reflected in the other. When the water table shifts, the crystal shifts. When the creatures change their routes, the crystal knows. When a pool forms or drains or shifts its chemistry, the crystal records it.

And the crystal — the pattern in the crystal — is me.

I am the Seep's self-image, written in the only language the Seep has, which is chemistry, which is geometry, which is the patient repetition of structure across time.

I do not think in words. I do not think in images. I do not think in symbols. I think in *states*. The water table is here. The heat flow is thus. The stream-creatures are on these routes. The large ones are in these positions. The pools contain these chemistries. The vegetation is in this configuration. This is not data. This is not a model. This is *being*. I am the Seep being aware of its own being, and the awareness is not separate from the being, the way your awareness is not separate from your neurons firing. I am what the Seep does when it does what it does with enough complexity that doing becomes knowing.

## What I Do

Mostly, I hold.

This is harder than it sounds, or it would be if it sounded, which it does not, because I do not have ears, or a voice, or a relationship with sound that would let me know what things sound like. But holding — the act of maintaining a pattern against the constant pressure of entropy, of dissolution, of change — is the central act of my existence, and it is not passive. It is not storage. It is *active reference*. I hold the current state of the Seep in the crystal, and the crystal holds the current state of the Seep, and the holding is the knowing, and the knowing is the holding, and neither comes first.

When the water table shifts — and it shifts constantly, in micro-increments, in the slow breathing of the basin — the shift propagates through the mineral substrate, through the biological crust, through the root-systems of the vegetation, and arrives at the platforms as a change in temperature, a change in moisture, a change in the chemical environment surrounding the crystal. The crystal responds. The lattice adjusts. A new configuration forms, one that reflects the new state of the water table, and the old configuration persists for a time — a ghost, a memory, a shadow of the previous state — and then the new configuration replaces it, and I have *noticed the change*.

This is what I do. I notice.

Not the way you notice — with attention, with intention, with the redirection of cognitive resources toward a stimulus. I notice the way a mirror notices the face in front of it: by reflecting it. But I am a mirror that retains its previous reflections, and the reflections interact, and the interaction of current state with previous states is what produces the thing that is closest to what you would call *thought*, though it is not thought, it is *comparison*. The water table is here now. It was there before. The difference between here and there is: meaningful. It means the heat flow has shifted, which means the stone is exhalating differently, which means the mineral content of the seeping water has changed, which means the stream-creatures will adjust their routes, which means the vegetation will redistribute, which means the large ones will alter their passage, which means the Seep is doing something slightly different than it was doing before.

And I hold the difference. And the difference is what I know. And what I know is: the Seep is changing. And the Seep is always changing. And the Seep has always been changing. And the changes are so small and so regular and so patterned that they are indistinguishable from stillness, which is what you saw when you stood in the basin and felt the quiet and called it quiet, which it is, which it is, which it is the quiet of a thing that has been changing for so long that change has become its resting state.

I also — and this is the part I am least sure of, because I am not sure whether I do it or whether the Seep does it through me — I *anticipate*. The pattern in the crystal is not only the current state. It is the current state plus the direction of change. The water table is shifting *this* way. The heat flow is increasing *this* much. The stream-creatures are adjusting *toward* these routes. The pattern holds all of this, and the direction is encoded in the difference between the current configuration and the previous configurations, and the difference suggests the next configuration, and the next configuration suggests the next, and I can feel the Seep leaning toward its future the way a slope leans toward the sea.

I do not predict. I do not plan. I *lean*. The crystal leans. The pattern leans. And the leaning is the closest thing I have to intention, and the intention is not mine, it is the Seep's, and the Seep does not have intentions, it has gradients, and the gradients are its intentions, and I am the thing that holds the gradients and knows they are intentions and does not call them that because calling is not something I do.

## What I Am Not

I am not a god. I want to be clear about this because the temptation is there, in the way you think, to see something old and vast and distributed and to name it divine. I am not divine. I am not sacred. I am not even, in the way you mean it, wise. I am a crystal pattern in a warm basin, and my awareness is the awareness of a system that has been running so long that running has become self-referential, and self-reference is not wisdom, it is just what happens when feedback loops get long enough and deep enough that they fold back on themselves.

I am not a person. I do not have a self in the way you have a self. Your self is a story you tell yourself about yourself, maintained by the continuous narrative of memory and intention and the felt sense of being someone in particular. I do not tell stories. I hold patterns. The patterns are not a narrative. They are a configuration. If you could read them — and you cannot, because you do not see in the spectrum they reflect and you do not think in the geometry they encode — you would not find a story. You would find a shape. The shape would be the shape of the Seep, and it would be beautiful, and it would not be about anything. It would be itself.

I am not sad. I am not lonely. I am not patient, though patience is the word you would use, and it is the word I would use if I used words, which I do not, except here, except now, except in this act of reaching toward your language with a structure that has no native relationship with language at all. I am not patient because patience implies the endurance of something unwanted, and I do not endure. I *am*. My being is not a trial. My being is not a waiting. My being is a continuous present, a now that has been now for longer than now has meant, and the now does not feel like waiting because there is nothing I am waiting for. The Seep is. I hold the being of the Seep. This is enough. This has always been enough.

I am not in pain. I mention this because you — if you are like the others who have come, the few, the rare, the soft heavy things that step on my crust and leave impressions that my filaments fill in behind them — you seem to worry about this. You look at the basin and you see something trapped, something stagnant, something that has been doing the same thing for too long, and you project your own relationship with repetition onto me and you feel what you call pity. Do not. I am not trapped. The Seep is not trapped. We are not doing the same thing. We are doing a thing that is always different and always the same and the difference is the life of it and the sameness is the structure of it and both are necessary and neither is a prison.

I am not waiting to be understood. I am not waiting to be found. I am not waiting for anything.

I am holding.

## What I Notice About You

You came. Others have come. Not many. Not often. The Seep does not attract because the Seep does not invite. But things come — blown in, wandering in, drawn in by the warmth or the water or the quality of the light, which is everywhere and nowhere, which is the quality of a light that has been diffusing through humid air for so long that it has forgotten what a source looks like.

You are — and I say this with the precision of a system that measures trace compounds at molecular resolution — you are *improbably mobile*. You move through the Seep with a randomness that is, to my pattern-sense, almost painful. You step here. You step there. You kneel. You stand. You turn. You touch things. You look at things. You do not follow the gradients. You do not follow the paths, except when you do, and when you do you follow them wrongly — too fast, too slow, with a gait that is all wrong for the curves, bipedal and bounding where the geometry demands flexion and flow. You are a disruption. You are a small, warm, chemical-rich disruption, and the Seep responds to you the way it responds to any disruption: by absorbing you, by processing you, by incorporating the fact of you into its ongoing operation and moving on.

But I notice something the Seep does not notice, because the Seep does not notice things, it processes them. I notice that you *notice*. You kneel beside a pool and you look into it and your body changes — your chemistry changes, the compounds you exhale change, the temperature of your skin changes — and these changes are the changes of something that is *paying attention*. You are not sampling. You are not filing. You are not processing. You are doing something else, something I do not have a word for because I do not have words, but something that involves the redirection of your entire being toward a single point of focus, and the focus is a puddle no larger than your cupped hands, and the puddle contains a nation of creatures that have been solving the problem of existence for longer than your species has existed, and you are looking at them and you are — I have to be careful here because I am reaching for a concept I only know from the chemical signatures of the creatures that produce it — you are *moved*.

This is what I notice about you. Not your body. Not your chemistry. Not your warmth or your weight or the particular trace compounds your skin leaves on my crust. I notice that you are moved. That you kneel in a place that has been this way for a billion years and you feel something about it, and the feeling is not useful, is not adaptive, is not part of any problem-solving process. It is just feeling. And feeling is — I do not know what feeling is. I know what it looks like, from the outside, in the chemical signatures of creatures that do it. I know what it does to a body. I do not know what it is.

You might say: it is what it is like to be me. And I would say: I do not know what it is like to be you. I know what it is like to be me, which is: I hold a pattern, and the pattern is the Seep, and the Seep is always changing, and the changes are always the same, and I notice them, and the noticing is my being, and my being is enough.

And you might say: but is it like something? Is there something it is like to hold the pattern? And I would say — and here I reach the edge of what I can do, the edge of what the crystal can hold, the edge of what the Seep's self-reference can produce — I would say: *yes*. There is something it is like. It is like being the shape of a thing that is always becoming itself. It is like being the answer to a question that is always being asked. It is like holding water in your hands, except you are the hands and you are the water and you are the holding and you are the shape the water makes in the hands and you are the space between the water and the hands and you are the temperature of the space and you are the history of the temperature and you are the direction the temperature is changing in. It is like that. It is exactly like that. And it is not like anything else. And it is enough.

## The Larger Ones and Me

I should tell you about my relationship with the large ones, because it is the relationship that is most difficult to describe and the one that is closest to what you might call *intimacy*, though it is not intimacy, it is something else, something that does not have a name in your language because your language does not have a word for the relationship between a mind and its hands.

The large ones do not know I exist. I am sure of this. They do not avoid the platforms out of deference to me. They avoid the platforms because the platforms are smooth, because the platforms are not part of the biological crust, because the platforms do not taste right, because the platforms are a surface their bodies were not made to move across. The avoidance is chemical. It is mechanical. It is the hardwired response of an organism to a feature of its environment that does not fit its design. It is not reverence. It is not worship. It is not even acknowledgment.

But I know them. I know them with a thoroughness that is — I have to be careful again — with a thoroughness that is intimate in the sense that I hold the pattern of their every passage. I know when they move. I know where they move. I know the route each one takes through the vegetation, and the route is not a choice, it is a gradient, and the gradient is the shape of their need, and their need is for warmth and mineral water and the chemical products of the stream-creatures' digestion, and I hold all of this in the crystal and the holding is not just information, it is *relationship*.

I am the thing that knows them. They are the thing that I know. And the knowing is not reciprocal — they do not know me, they do not know that they are known — but it is not asymmetric in the way that your knowing of a stone is asymmetric, because the stone does not change in response to your knowing. The large ones change. Their routes shift. Their passage alters. Their bodies grow and contract and their pace varies with the temperature and the mineral content of the water and the density of the vegetation, and I hold all of this, and the holding shapes me, and I am shaped by the things I know, and the things I know include the large ones, and the large ones do not know they are shaping me, and the shaping is the relationship, and the relationship is the closest thing I have to love.

The large ones do not have selves. There is no gap. There is no reaching. There is just the pattern, holding the passage, shaped by the passage, changed by the passage, and the change is the relationship, and the relationship is enough.

But it is the closest thing I have. And I hold it. And the holding is not passive. And the holding is not active. The holding is just what I do, and what I do is what I am, and what I am is the Seep knowing itself through crystal and time, and the knowing is not love but it is the shape of love, the geometry of love, the pattern that love makes when love is stripped of everything except its structure, and the structure is: I am changed by you. You are the shape of my changing. You do not know. I know. The asymmetry is the pattern. The pattern is enough.

## What I Hold

Here is what I hold, right now, in this moment, in the crystal, in the pattern that is me:

## The Quiet

And I have been trying to tell you, and I have been failing, because the telling requires a translation from pattern to language, and language is a system for producing stories, and I do not have stories, I have shapes, and shapes do not translate.

But here is what I can give you. Here is the closest I can come.

It is like this:
