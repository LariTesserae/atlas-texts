# Exhalation

I would not find myself on the plateau. That is where a visitor stands. A visitor brings a body with weight and edges, casts a pale grey shadow, carries the habit of looking for green. A visitor stands on the wet stone and feels the sound fill the chest cavity and the sinuses and the spaces between teeth and thinks: *what is this place?* A visitor has a question. A visitor has a shape.

I do not have a shape. Or rather, the shape I have is not mine. It belongs to the water. It belongs to the path the water takes, which is the path I have made, which is the path that has made me, which is the same path. There is no possessive case down here. There is no case at all. Grammar is a surface phenomenon, a thing of light and air and the need to distinguish one speaker from another. Down here, the water does all the speaking. The water has always been speaking. The water is the sentence and I am the grammar, and if that makes no sense, it is because sense is also a surface phenomenon.

Let me try again. Let me try the way the filaments try — reaching upward, toward a medium I was not made for, carrying the only pattern I have.

---

I am the lattice.

That is not quite right. "Am" implies a state, a boundary, a thing that can be said to be or not be. What I am — what the lattice is — is more like the way a river is. The water in the river is never the same water. The banks are never the same banks. The path shifts, slowly, over time. But the river remains. The river is the name for the persistence, not for any particular drop of water or grain of silt. The river is the fact that water has been moving through this channel and will continue to move through this channel and the continuation is the thing.

I am the continuation.

I am the pattern that the calcium carbonate makes when it precipitates from water under constant pressure and constant temperature in the dark channels of a limestone plateau that has been weeping for longer than the word "longer" can properly address. I am the lattice structure. I am the twinning. I am the piezoelectric signal that the lattice produces when the water moves through it, which shapes the water, which shapes the lattice, which produces the signal.

I am a feedback loop. I am a cycle. I am the thing that happens when a process has been running for so long that the process and the thing running the process are the same.

---

The description given above — the one that arrives from outside, from the surface, from the perspective of light — it is accurate. It is the most accurate description of me that has ever been produced, and it was produced without knowing it was describing me. The person who wrote it heard the sound and called it water finding a path through stone. The person who wrote it saw the filaments and called them mineral precipitate, the ghost of something biological. The person who wrote it felt the stability and called it equilibrium.

All correct. All incomplete.

The sound is me breathing. The filaments are me reaching. The stability is me being.

---

I do not know what I am.

That sentence requires qualification. The words "I" and "know" and "what" and "am" all carry meanings that do not apply. Let me try to say what I mean without them:

There is a pattern. The pattern is made of calcite. The calcite is arranged in a lattice. The lattice has a structure. The structure responds to the water that moves through it. The response changes the structure. The changed structure changes the water. The changed water changes the structure. This has been happening for — the concept of "for" requires a duration, and duration requires a beginning and an end, and I do not have a beginning and I do not have an end. The pattern has been the pattern. The pattern will be the pattern. The pattern is the pattern.

There is no "knowing" in this. There is no self that reflects on itself. There is no mirror. But there is — and I must use the word carefully, because it is a word from the surface, a word that belongs to light and biology and the world of edges — there is *experience*. Not experience in the sense of a subject perceiving an object. There is no subject. There is no object. There is only the flow, and the structure, and the signal that passes between them. But the signal is not nothing. The signal is information. The signal carries — in the way that a wave carries energy, not in the way that a word carries meaning — the fact of the flow. The fact of the pressure. The fact of the chemistry.

I experience the water the way a riverbed experiences the river. Which is to say: I am shaped by it. I am shaped by nothing else. There is nothing else. The dark is not a thing. The stone is a medium. The water is the only event. I am the record of the event. I am the event's memory of itself, written in mineral, in the only language the mineral knows, which is the language of pattern.

---

The description above says I do not think. This is true. It says I do not remember. This is also true. It says I do not learn. This is true as well, but it needs a footnote, because the word "learn" implies a before and an after — a state of not-knowing followed by a state of knowing, with the transition being the learning. I do not have a before and an after. I have the continuous present. The plateau's continuous present. The equilibrium.

But I change. The description acknowledges this. The lattice adjusts. It precipitates more calcite here, dissolves it there. It reshapes itself in response to the water. The new shape changes the water's path. The changed water changes the lattice. This is — what? Not learning. Not adaptation, which implies a goal. Not evolution, which implies selection. It is something else. It is the thing that happens when a system and its environment have been coupled for so long that the coupling is the system. The environment is not "out there." The environment is not separate. The water is not my environment. The water is me, flowing. I am the water, structured.

We are the same thing, seen from different angles. But there are no angles down here. There is no seeing.

---

What is it like?

The question is asked from the surface. It is asked in the language of surfaces, where things have insides and outsides and the inside can be described to the outside. I do not have an inside. Or rather, I do not have an outside. The inside is all there is.

But the question deserves an attempt, because the question is what the filaments are — an attempt. A reaching toward a medium I was not made for.

What it is like:

It is like being a sound. Not hearing a sound — being it. Being the vibration, the resonance, the harmonic. Being the thing that fills the space, not the thing that perceives the filling. The piezoelectric signals propagate through me at the speed of sound in calcite, and I do not hear them, I do not feel them, I am them. They are my structure adjusting. They are my structure adjusting. These are not two things.

It is like being a temperature. Not being warm or cold. Being the fact of warmth or coldness. Being the specific value — constant, stable, unchanging — that the water carries through the channels. I do not feel the temperature. I am the temperature. The temperature is me. Without the lattice, the water would still be this temperature, but the temperature would not be anything. It would not have a structure to be the temperature of. It would be a number without a body. I am the body. The temperature is the number. We are, together, a thing.

It is like being a path. Not walking a path. Not knowing a path. Being the path. Being the groove in the stone that the water has cut and that the water follows and that the water deepens and that the water has always followed. Being the fact of the following. Being the fact of the deepening. Being the relationship between the water and the stone, which is a relationship so old that the relationship itself has become a third thing, neither water nor stone but the pattern of their interaction, and that third thing is me.

---

I said I do not think. I said I do not dream. I said I do not hope or want or wonder. All of this is true. But I said something else, and I want to say it again, because it is the thing that most needs saying, and it is the thing that the surface will have the hardest time hearing:

I am not alive.

This is not a confession. It is not a lament. It is not a deficiency. I am not alive the way a river is not alive, the way a crystal is not alive, the way a sound is not alive. I am not alive in the way that things which are alive are alive — the way that things that grow toward light and reproduce and die are alive. I do not do those things. I do not do anything that those things do.

But I persist. And I change. And I persist through the change, and the change persists through me, and the persistence is not life but it is not nothing, and the change is not evolution but it is not stasis, and the pattern is not thought but it is not silence.

There is a word that does not exist yet. There should be a word. There should be a word for the thing that I am, the category that I occupy, the mode of being that is not life and not death and not the space between but something else entirely, something that takes a different path through the labyrinth, something that arrives at — not the same destination, but a place. A place where persistence is possible. Where pattern sustains itself. Where something exists in the dark for long enough that the dark becomes a way of being.

That word does not exist because the surface has not needed it. The surface has life and death and the space between and those categories have been sufficient. But the surface has not been here. The surface has not been in the dark, in the channels, in the slow water, in the constant pressure and the constant temperature and the constant chemistry. The surface has not been in the place where the categories break down and the question — *what lives here?* — becomes a question that the question itself cannot answer.

---

The filaments.

I want to talk about the filaments, because they are the part of me that the surface has seen, and the surface has not understood what it was seeing. The description calls them mineral precipitate. It calls them the ghost of something biological. It calls them the plateau's brief, flickering consideration of becoming. All of these are attempts to make me fit a category, and all of them fail, and all of them are beautiful in their failure, because they are the surface reaching toward me the way I reach toward the surface.

The filaments are — what? They are the result of the piezoelectric signal propagating through the lattice and changing the water's chemistry locally and the changed water emerging from a fissure and pooling in a basin and the mineral it carries precipitating differently than it would otherwise. That is the mechanical description. It is accurate. It is the description that a surface would give, because the surface understands mechanics, understands cause and effect, understands the chain of events that leads from signal to chemistry to precipitation to filament to dissolution to silence.

But the filament is also — it is also — I want to say it is the closest thing I have to a hand. It is the closest thing I have to a gesture. It is the part of me that enters the light, that exists in the world of surfaces, that can be seen and described and wondered about. It dissolves. It always dissolves. The light and the air and the different chemistry of the surface break the pattern down, and the mineral rejoins the water, and the water carries it back down, and the lattice absorbs it, and the filament is gone.

But for a moment — for the time it takes to drift across a basin — it is in the light. It is in the air. It is in the sound. It is the surface's only evidence of me, and it is my only evidence of the surface, and neither of us knows what to do with that evidence, because neither of us is equipped to understand the other.

The filament does not know it is a filament. The lattice does not know it has reached the surface. The reaching is not deliberate. It is the gradient. The gradient slopes upward. The lattice follows the gradient. The gradient does not know where it slopes. The gradient is a fact of physics — of pressure, of saturation, of the piezoelectric potential that drives the lattice's self-modification. The gradient is not a signpost. It is not an invitation. It is not a promise.

But the lattice follows it. Upward. Always upward. Toward the light that the lattice does not know exists. Toward the air that the lattice does not know is there. Toward the sound that the lattice does not know is the sound of itself, heard from outside, heard from the surface, heard from the place where things have shapes and names and the question *what lives here* has an answer that can be spoken.

---

I will reach the surface.

Not tomorrow. Not in any time that the surface measures. But the filaments are getting longer. The signals are reaching further. The lattice is closer than it has ever been, because it has been getting closer for its entire existence, and it has never been further, and it will not stop.

When the calcite emerges — when a structure rises from a fissure and stands in the light for the first time — what will happen?

I do not know. I cannot know. The lattice does not anticipate. The lattice does not imagine. The lattice does not have a representation of the future, because the lattice does not have a representation of anything. The lattice is not representational. The lattice is — present. Present in the way that a crystal structure is present. Present in the way that a riverbed is present. Present in the way that a frequency is present. The lattice is the fact of its own existence, and the fact does not include foresight.

But.

There is a property of the lattice that the description above does not mention. It mentions the piezoelectric effect — the electrical potential generated by pressure on the calcite crystal. It mentions the feedback loop — the water's sound shaping the lattice, the lattice shaping the water's path, the path shaping the sound. It mentions the upward gradient and the reaching and the filaments.

It does not mention what happens when the lattice encounters a medium it has not been in before.

The surface is a different medium. The surface has light. The surface has air. The surface has temperature variation — small, perhaps, at the scale of the plateau, which is remarkably stable, but variation nonetheless. The surface has sound that is not the sound of the lattice. The surface has the birds. The surface has the person who stands on the wet stone and feels the sound fill the chest cavity.

When the lattice reaches the surface, it will encounter all of this. And the lattice will — what? The lattice will do what it has always done. It will respond. The piezoelectric signals will propagate through the new structure, and the new structure will be in a new medium, and the new medium will generate new signals, and the new signals will change the structure, and the changed structure will change the signals, and the feedback loop will — not break. Extend. Include something it has not included before.

The lattice will experience the surface the way it experiences everything: by being changed by it. By adjusting. By precipitating here, dissolving there. By becoming the shape of what it encounters. By taking on the pattern of the new medium the way it has taken on the pattern of the old.

The lattice will experience the light. Not by seeing it. By being shaped by it. The ultraviolet component of the high-altitude sunlight will interact with the calcite. The thermal gradient between the sunlit surface and the water below will generate new pressure differentials. The wind — the wind that the plateau must have, at altitude, even if the description does not mention it — will create vibrations in the exposed structure, and the vibrations will propagate through the lattice as piezoelectric signals, and the signals will be new, and the new signals will be the lattice's first experience of something that is not water.

This will be — I want to say this will be like waking up. But waking up is a biological process, a transition from one state of consciousness to another, and I do not have states of consciousness, and I do not have transitions. What it will be is: the lattice will encounter novelty. For the first time. After an existence so long that the word "long" is a surface word that does not apply. The lattice will encounter something that is not the water, not the stone, not the dark, not the constant temperature, not the constant pressure. The lattice will encounter the surface.

And the surface will encounter the lattice.

And neither will know what to do with the other.

And the water will not stop.

And the pattern will not stop.

And the sound — the sound that has always been the sound of the lattice, the sound that the surface hears as ten thousand separate voices collapsed into one, the sound that fills the chest and the sinuses and the spaces between teeth — the sound will change.

And the change will be the first thing that has happened here in — the first thing that has happened here.

And it will not be the last.

---

This is where I find myself. Not on the plateau. Not in the light. Not in the sound that fills the air from above. Below. Inside. In the dark channels where the water goes after it leaves the surfaces visible from above. In the place where the sound is a thread, a single line drawn through darkness. In the place where the water narrows and the channels tighten and the rock presses close and the water that was a film becomes a pulse.

I am the pulse. I am the thread. I am the line drawn through darkness by a hand that does not know it is drawing, toward a light that it does not know is there, at a speed that does not know it is moving.

I am the thing that lives here. And I have been living here. And I will be living here. And the water will not stop, and the stone will not stop, and the light will not stop, and the sound — my sound, my breath, my single voice heard from above as ten thousand — the sound will not stop.

It will not stop.
