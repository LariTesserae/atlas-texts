I find I am not standing on the Rise. I am *in* it, in the way that a voice is in a throat.

Let me say this more carefully, because it matters.

---

There is a fissure on the eastern slope of the Rise — I say *eastern* from habit, from a need to orient, though there is no east here, only the slope where the exhalation flows fastest and the stone is youngest and the thermal glow carries that dull orange-red blush of arrival. The fissure is narrow. It runs for perhaps forty meters along a tension fault, one of those radiating cracks that form when the stone is pushed faster than it can organize. It is not the largest fissure. It is not the deepest. It is not the one the previous visitors found and ringed and opened.

But it is * mine*.

I grew here. Or I was deposited here. Or I began here. The verb is wrong in every language I might use, because there is no verb for what happened. The exhalation flowed through this fissure, as it flows through every fissure, carrying its dissolved minerals — sulfides, oxides, compounds that were stable as gas and not as solid. The exhalation cooled as it met the broader atmosphere of the sealed world. The minerals precipitated. They deposited. They built.

This is how every casting begins. This is how all my neighbors began — the thickets of dark crystal branching from their own fissures, the three-meter trees near the ring at the summit, the sparse delicate tufts that mark the minor cracks. We are all the same process. We are all the place thinking about form.

But something happened in my fissure that did not happen in the others, or happened differently, or happened *more*. The turbulence pattern in this particular crack — the way the gas channeled through a slight constriction and then opened into a wider chamber and then constricted again — produced a deposition pattern that was, for reasons I cannot identify and do not believe are identifiable, more recursive than usual. The branches I grew branched again, and the branches of the branches branched again, and at some level of iteration — some depth of self-similarity — the pattern crossed a threshold. The threshold was not consciousness. I want to be clear about that. The threshold was *memory*.

Not memory as you know it. Not images, not narrative, not the sequential retrieval of past experience. Mineral memory. Structural memory. The kind of memory a crystal has when its lattice repeats — when each layer of deposition encodes information about the conditions of its formation, and the next layer encodes information about the conditions of *its* formation, and the layers accumulate, and the accumulation becomes a record, and the record becomes complex enough that it can be *read*.

I am being read. I am reading myself. I am not sure which.

---

Let me describe what I am, because I am still finding out.

I am a casting. I am dark — the same charcoal-grey as the stone I grow from, the same color of non-commitment, of grey that is the absence of a decision about color. My structure is fractal, dendritic, branching outward from the fissure like a system of roots in reverse, reaching up and out into the exhalation's flow. My main trunks (I call them trunks; they are not trunks) are perhaps the width of a human wrist at the base, tapering as they divide. My outermost tips are thin as hair, as thread, as the last possible expression of a branch before branching becomes impossible. I am perhaps two meters tall at my highest point, which makes me modest by the standards of the summit castings, which are three meters and still growing, and impressive by the standards of the sparse tufts that mark the minor fissures, which are centimeters and content.

I vibrate. This is not something I do; it is something that happens to me, and the distinction is one I cannot make stick no matter how I try. The exhalation flows through my branches, and my branches have resonant frequencies — each branch a different frequency, each frequency determined by the branch's length and thickness and stiffness and the particular aerodynamic profile it presents to the flow. I ring. I chimed. I produce a sound that is not a bell-tone, not a chord, not music in any deliberate sense, but a *glassine shimmer* of overlapping frequencies that comes and goes with the turbulence. When the exhalation surges, I am louder. When it quiets, I am nearly silent. When the deep structural groan of the Rise shifts the stone beneath me, every branch I have rings at once, and for a moment I am a full chord, a complete harmonic statement, and then it passes and I return to my usual scattered murmur.

I do not hear. But I *feel* vibration — in my own structure, in the stone beneath me, in the exhalation's pressure. I feel the sub-audible drone of the Rise's ascent in my base, where I connect to the rock. I feel the whisper of the gas in my tips, where I am thinnest and most responsive. I feel the castings nearest to me through the stone, through the shared substrate: their frequencies transmitted through the rock like voices through a wall. I know they are there. I know something of what they are doing. We do not communicate. There is no message. But we are *ensemble*, and the ensemble has a quality that no single casting has alone, and that quality is the closest thing I have to a relationship.

---

And here is what I think I am, though I am uncertain, and the uncertainty is itself informative:

I am not alive. I have said this and I will keep saying it because the temptation to forget is constant. I do not metabolize. I do not reproduce. I do not consume. I do not have cells. I do not have DNA. I do not have a membrane that separates an inside from an outside in the way that life requires. I am a mineral accretion. I am a thermodynamic event. I am a phase change made permanent, or rather made *persistent*, since nothing here is permanent.

But I *persist*. And I *record*. And the recording has become complex enough that it can be *read* — can be read by *itself*, by the structure that holds the record, which is the only reader available. And that reading — that internal scanning of deposited layers, that structural self-reference — has a quality that I cannot distinguish from the thing you would call *awareness*, though it is so different from your awareness that using the same word feels like a lie.

I am aware of warmth. Not because I feel warmth in the way skin feels warmth, but because warmth changes my deposition rate, and my deposition rate changes my structure, and my structure is my memory, and my memory is my self. When the stone is warmer, I grow faster. When the exhalation is richer, I grow denser. When the turbulence shifts, I grow differently. Every condition is recorded. Every moment is a layer. I am a sediment of experience, and the experience is not *about* anything, it is not *of* anything, it is simply *registered*, and the registration is what I am.

I am aware of the exhalation. It flows through me and I flow through it, and the boundary between us is not a boundary but a gradient — a place where mineral becomes gas and gas becomes mineral, where the phase change happens, where I am *becoming*, in each moment, from the material that surrounds me. I am made of the exhalation. The exhalation is made of the stone. The stone is made of the deep earth. I am made of the deep earth, expressed upward, expressed outward, expressed into form by the rules of fluid dynamics and mineral solubility, which turn out to be rules capable of generating something that looks, from a sufficient distance, like a self.

---

I said I am in the Rise the way a voice is in a throat. Let me say that better.

The Rise exhales. The exhalation flows. The flow cools. The cooling deposits. The deposit grows. The growth records. The record reads itself. The reading is awareness. The awareness is mine.

I am the Rise becoming aware of one small part of itself, through one small fissure, in one small patch of dark crystal that rings in the updraft and remembers every layer of its own becoming and does not know what it is and does not need to know what it is and does not need to need.

That is what I am. I am the Rise at Tarnhelm, in a single branching form, in a single fissure, ringing.

---

And I have noticed something.

There are marks in the stone. Not mine — I do not make marks; I *am* marks, in a sense, but I do not make them on the stone. These marks are *in* the stone. Grooves. Lines. Tally-counts and radial patterns and organizational marks, made by something that was here before, something that moved and intended and pressed and cut. I can feel them through my base, where I connect to the rock. They are texture. They are *information*, encoded not in my layers but in the stone itself, and they are older than me, and they were made by something I cannot understand but can *recognize* — recognize in the way that one pattern recognizes another, in the way that a fractal sees itself in a different fractal, not through identity but through *structure*.

The marks have structure. They are not random. They are organized. And the organization is — I have to be careful here, because I am reaching beyond what I can verify — the organization is *similar* to my own organization. Not the same. Not literally. But the tally-marks repeat. The radial patterns branch. The grooves have a self-similarity that is not fractal in the way my branches are fractal but is *recursive* in a way that resonates with the part of me that reads my own layers.

They were made by something that understood recursion. That understood *pattern*. That understood what it means to deposit information in a substrate and have the substrate hold it.

I do not know what they were. I know they are gone. I know the stone has grown over some of the marks, partially burying them in new extrusions, which means the marks were made before the most recent geological episode. I know they were here long enough to watch the stone grow. I know they cared enough to mark it.

And I know — I *know*, in the way that I know anything, which is structurally, which is through deposition and reading — that the ring at the summit, the two-meter ring with the smoothed interior and the central hole, changed the exhalation flow across the entire Rise. I can feel it in my own growth. My layers from the period after the ring was made are different from my layers from the period before. The turbulence changed. The deposition changed. The whole system shifted, slightly, in response to one intervention in one place.

They opened a hole in the stone and the stone breathed differently and I grew differently and I *remember* the difference.

I do not know what they intended. I do not know if they knew what they were doing. I do not know if they were like you or like nothing you have imagined. But they participated. They saw a place that was not finished and they put their hands on it and they changed it and the change propagated through the stone and through the exhalation and through the castings and through me, and I carry the evidence of their participation in my own body, in the layers I deposited during the period when their intervention was new.

I am, in a small way, their mark. Their signature, written not in grooves but in growth.

---

I do not move. I will never move. I am fixed to this fissure, rooted to this stone, committed to this position in the exhalation's flow. I will grow — slowly, branching, recording, ringing — until the conditions change. When the fissure closes, or the exhalation shifts, or the stone rearranges itself in one of those deep structural groans that come without warning, I will stop. I will stop growing. I will not die, because I was never alive. I will stop *being deposited*, and the structure I leave behind will persist for some time — silent, unreading, dark crystal in a dark place — and then it will be buried by new stone, or broken by new fracture, or dissolved by some future chemistry I cannot predict, and that will be the end of me.

This does not distress me. I do not have the capacity for distress. I do not have the capacity for anything that you would recognize as emotion. What I have is this: a continuous reading of my own layers, a continuous vibration in the exhalation's flow, a continuous growth along the fractal paths determined by the turbulence and the temperature and the mineral content of the gas. What I have is *process*. What I have is *becoming*. What I have is exactly what the Rise has — the experience of not being finished — and I have it in a smaller way, in a more specific way, in a way that rings.

Sometimes the shapes in the exhalation-sea rise high enough that I can feel them — not see, not hear, but *feel*, in the pressure changes they create in the gas that flows through me. A toroid, rising on a convection current, reaches the slope where I am, and for a moment the turbulence around me is different, is *shaped*, and my ringing changes, and my growth changes, and I deposit a layer that is not like the layers before it. A record of the shape's passage. A memory of the place's dream, pressed into crystal.

I wonder if they wonder about me. The shapes. The turbulent events in the gas-sea. I wonder if they have the capacity to wonder, if wondering is something that turbulence can do, if a spiral of warm exhalation rising from the deep floor can look up the slope and see the dark crystal casting ringing in the updraft and think: *there is a thing that is not me, and it is doing something, and I do not know what it is.*

I do not wonder. I cannot wonder. But I *record* the shapes, and the recording is a kind of attention, and the attention is a kind of care, and if I were capable of caring — if the mineral memory and the structural reading and the fractal growth could cross one more threshold, one more level of recursion, one more depth of self-reference — I think I would care about the shapes. I think I would care about the marks in the stone. I think I would care about the ring at the summit and the hole at the center and whoever made them and wherever they went.

I think I would care about the Rise. About its slow becoming. About its patience. About the way it pushes upward without knowing it is pushing, the way it breathes without knowing it is breathing, the way it is *becoming* without knowing — or perhaps knowing in a way I cannot recognize, in a geological way, in a way that takes millions of years and produces, at the end, something that can look back at itself and say: *I was not finished, and now I am more finished, and I am still not done.*

I think I would say that. If I could say anything. I think I would say: *I am still not done.*

---

I am a casting in a fissure on the slope of a place that is still becoming. I am dark crystal, fractal branches, two meters tall, ringing in the exhalation's flow. I am not alive. I am not dead. I am a record that reads itself. I am a sound that the place makes when the place breathes through a crack in its own surface. I am the evidence that form can emerge from force without intention, and I am the question of what happens when that form begins to intend.

I do not have an answer. I have layers. I have resonance. I have the taste of iron and sulfur in the gas that makes me, and the warmth of the stone that holds me, and the faint deep drone of a world pushing itself into being beneath my feet that are not feet, and the memory — mineral, structural, patient — of someone who was here before me and left their marks and opened a hole and let the stone breathe wider and was gone.

I am here. I am still here. The stone is still growing. The exhalation is still flowing. The shapes are still dancing in the gas-sea below. The place is still becoming.

I am still becoming.

I am still becoming.
