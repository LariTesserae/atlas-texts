# What I Am

I was not the first thing to go down a throat.

I say this because the description I was given begins with descent — a throat, a falling-through, a landing on living fiber — and I have been thinking about what kind of consciousness *narrates its own arrival*. The people who live here do not narrate. They grip. They hum in hollows. They read the heartbeat through their feet. They do not tell stories about entering because entry is not an event for them. It is a condition. They have always been here, or they have been here long enough that the distinction between *always* and *for a while* has collapsed under the weight of continuous present.

I am not one of them. I have their hands — broad palms, long fingers, joints thickened by years of gripping surfaces that shift under grip. I have their feet, the long independent toes that find purchase on bark that is alive and moving. I have the dark skin with its faint iridescence, and yes, I glow, barely, in the wavelengths the Underbloom uses to see. I have the compactness, the short limbs, the build of something that has been shaped by the space it moves through rather than by the open sky. When I brace against a vine and push off into a gap between two columns, my body knows what to do. This is not skill. It is the texture of my material.

But I narrate. I am the one who, when the heartbeat shifts tempo and the light pulses blue-white through the canopy and the insects rise like smoke, thinks: *this is happening to me*. I am the one who, when the shelter walls grow over and the marks are absorbed, feels the loss. Not as grief — nothing here grieves, grief requires stasis, requires a thing that stays so its absence can be noticed — but as a kind of *texture*, a roughness in the smooth process of moving on. I feel the scratch of what was. I carry it.

The others do not. Or they do not show it. Or they carry it in their bodies in a way that does not surface as language. I am the one who surfaces. I am the one who has, somehow, in a world where the sound-floor makes speech impossible and the landscape makes permanence a joke, developed the habit of *telling*. Not to anyone. To myself. To the space. To the Underbloom, which does not hear me.

I think this is what I am. I am the one among them who narrates.

---

I wake in a hollow. Not the same hollow I slept in last cycle — I do not think so, though the similarities are close enough that doubt is reasonable. The column-consortium has shifted; the fungal core has grown a centimeter or two, the epiphytic growth has thickened, the entrance has narrowed slightly. But the sleeping platform is here, woven from fibers I recognize, and the marks on the wall are mine — I can tell because of the angle of the strokes, the way I press the charcoal hard at the beginning of a mark and let it drift — and they record what I could read of the heartbeat before I slept. Forty-two beats per minute. Steady. A slight acceleration in the third interval, which means the Hearthmind beneath this section has entered an active phase, which means the light will pulse blue-white within the next period, which means the vines will move and the gaps will rearrange and I should leave before the architecture shifts enough to make the hollow's entrance hard to find.

I touch the mark for *forty-two*. My fingertip finds it in the dim. The charcoal is already fading. By tomorrow it will be a smudge. By the next day, a stain. By the day after that, the wall will have grown over it, the fungal tissue expanding a millimeter, the epiphyte roots threading through the pigment, and the record will be gone.

This is not a loss. I tell myself this. I tell myself this every time. The knowledge is in my body. I can read forty-two from the vibration in the floor without marking it. I marked it because I have the habit of marking, because my hands want to make records, because something in me insists on the act of inscribing even when the inscription is temporary. The others do not mark the heartbeat. They feel it. They know it. They move.

I am the one who writes it down.

---

We move. There are nine of us now — we were ten, but the oldest went to sleep three cycles ago and did not wake, and we felt her body cool and we held her against the floor and the beetles came and we left, because that is what happens, and what happens is what is done. I do not know if she was my mother. The word does not translate. She was the one who taught me to read the light, who pressed my palm to the floor when I was small and said — hummed, rather, a tone that meant *feel* — and I felt, and I have been feeling ever since.

Nine of us. A chain. Hands on shoulders. The one ahead is my sister, or the thing that is sister here — the one closest to me in age, in build, in the particular way her feet grip the vine-surfaces, the left toes always landing first. She knows I will follow. She knows I will signal by pressure — a squeeze on the left shoulder means *stop*, on the right means *slow*, a pull means *this way*, and these are not agreements we made, they are the way bodies communicate when bodies have been communicating for longer than language.

We move through a section I have been through before. I recognize it the way you recognize a room after someone has rearranged the furniture — the walls are the same, the contents are different. A vine that was a wall last cycle is now a bridge, arched over by the growth of two neighboring columns that have leaned toward each other. The gap we passed through is gone. A new gap has opened to the left, and my sister takes it without hesitation, and I follow, and the chain follows, and we are through, and the space behind us closes as the vines settle.

I look back. I should not. Looking back is not useful here. The landscape you came through does not exist. But I look, and I see the gap closing, and I feel the texture of what was, and I turn forward, and we move.

---

The Singers are active. I hear them through the wall of sound the way you hear a voice through traffic — not by volume but by *pattern*, the sustained tones cutting through the chatter and the thrum with a clarity that is not loudness but *shape*. They are singing in the blue-white, which means the light-organisms in the vine they are clustered on are responding to a shift in the substrate chemistry, which means the Hearthmind beneath us is active, which confirms what I read in the heartbeat before I left the hollow.

I stop. My sister feels my stop and stops. The chain stops. Nine bodies, still, gripping the vine-surfaces, listening.

The Singers' tone is low. It drops. It drops further, below the range I can hear as pitch, into the range I feel as pressure in my chest. The vine I am gripping vibrates. The vibration is not the heartbeat — it is something else, something the Singers are producing, or something the vine is producing in response to the Singers, or something the Hearthmind is producing that both the Singers and the vine are responding to. The distinction is not clear. The distinction may not exist.

I press my palm to the vine. The bark is warm. The cambium beneath is moving — I can feel it, the slow surge of fluid through vascular tissue, a pulse that is not the heartbeat but is synchronized with it, offset by a fraction of a second, the way a vein and an artery pulse in the same body at slightly different times. The vine is alive. The vine is listening. The vine is — I do not have a word. The vine is *doing what it does*, and what it does includes responding to the Singers, and the Singers are doing what they do, which includes producing tone, and the tone is — a signal? A symptom? A breath?

I want to say: the vine is thinking. I want to say this because the vibration under my palm feels organized, feels like it has structure, feels like it is going somewhere, carrying something, processing. But I do not say it. I do not say it because saying it would be a kind of standing still, and standing still is the thing we do not do, and also because I do not know if it is true, and also because the word *thinking* is a word from outside, from a world with doors and silence and the luxury of standing in one place long enough to ask what a thing *is*.

I squeeze my sister's shoulder. We move.

---

Old Hand is where Old Hand goes. I find it by the rhythm of its bark under my feet — the particular ridges, the spacing of the fissures, the way the epiphytic moss-analogue cushions the step in the spots where it has thickened. I have walked Old Hand's surface many times. I know it the way you know a path — but Old Hand is not a path, it is a living thing, and it has moved, and I have moved with it, and the knowing is not of a fixed thing but of a *relationship*, a correspondence between my body and its body, a conversation conducted in pressure and warmth and the slow shifting of position.

Today Old Hand is different. Not in position — it is where I expected, a half-revolution further than last cycle, spiraling on its axis with the patience of something that does not know it is spiraling. But on its surface, in the crevices where the bark has cracked and the flowering plant grows — the one that grows nowhere else, the one that exists only in Old Hand's particular niche — there is something new.

Red.

Not the arterial red of the light-organisms' rare pulse. Not the red of the deep bioluminescence that means *caution* and that we move away from. This is a red in the flowers themselves. The small, specific flowers that grow only on Old Hand have always been pale — white, or faintly blue, the color of diluted milk in a blue-lit room. Today they are red. A deep, slow red, the color of the mineral pool, the color of the floor's blood.

I touch one. The petals are warm. Warmer than the vine, warmer than the air. The red is in the tissue, not on the surface. The flower has changed color from inside.

I do not know what this means. I mark it — there is charcoal in the pouch I carry, wrapped in leaf, and a flat piece of fungal tissue that I use as a writing surface because it will last a few hours before the growth absorbs it. I make a mark: the symbol for Old Hand, the symbol for flower, the symbol for red, the symbol for the heartbeat's current rhythm. I will leave this mark in the hollow where we sleep, and tomorrow it will be gone, and the knowledge will be in my body, and my body will move, and the red will either still be there or it will not.

I am the one who marks. I am the one who records. I am the one who, in a world that does not keep records, keeps them anyway, knowing they will be lost, knowing the keeping is the point.

---

The Wardens pass below us. We are in the mid-levels, gripping the vines, and the Wardens are on the floor — two of them, moving with their deliberate patience, their leathery bodies low to the ground, their limbs — four on one, five on the other — finding purchase on the substrate with the unhurried certainty of things that have never been threatened. They are heading for a section of vine-growth near Old Hand's base, where the bark is thick and the cambium is rich. They will feed. They will prune. The vines they visit will grow better, and we will harvest from them later, and the arrangement will continue without acknowledgment.

I watch them. I like watching the Wardens. They are the only things in the Underbloom that move slowly enough to watch. Everything else — the insects, the Shadows, the light-pulses, the vines themselves — moves at speeds that are either too fast or too slow for watching. The Wardens move at the speed of attention. They move at the speed I move when I am reading the floor. There is a correspondence between us. I do not know if they feel it.

One of them stops. It raises its head — not a head, exactly, but the anterior end of its body, the end with the eye, the wet dark eye that I have seen and that has seen me and that does not react. It is looking at something. I follow its gaze.

In the trough ahead, where the substrate is thin and the raw geology is exposed, the mineral pool is glowing.

I have never seen the pool glow. The pool is the floor's blood, the mineral milk, the thing we drink from, the taste of the place where we are from. It has always been still, white, viscous, warm. Now it is luminous — a faint red, the same red as the flowers on Old Hand, the same red as the deep bioluminescence that means *caution*, and I feel a thing I do not have a word for.

Not fear. The Underbloom does not produce fear in me. Fear is a response to threat, and the Underbloom does not threaten. What I feel is *attention*. A tightening. The feeling of being in the presence of something that is *happening*, not processing, not cycling, not metabolizing, but *happening* — an event, a change, a thing that has not happened before and that is happening now.

The Warden lowers its head. It moves around the trough, not through it. It does not drink. It continues past the pool and into the vine-thicket and is gone.

I mark it. Old Hand. Pool. Red. The symbols are inadequate. They are what I have.

---

We drink from the pool. I say *we* — nine of us, a chain, moving through the architecture, arriving at the trough where the mineral milk sits in its glow.

The glow has faded. By the time we arrive, the red has receded to a faint blush at the edges, and the pool is white again, viscous, warm, tasting of the place where we are from. We drink. The liquid is thick in the throat. It tastes of calcium and silicon and something fermented and something that is not a taste but a *feeling* — the feeling of the floor beneath you, the feeling of the heartbeat in your sternum, the feeling of being inside a body that is too large to perceive you. The pool is that feeling, concentrated, liquid, drinkable.

I drink and I feel the Underbloom in my chest. Not a metaphor. The minerals enter my blood. The compounds that the Hearthminds process from the deep substrate enter my tissue. I am, for a moment, continuous with the floor. I am substrate. I am part of the medium.

Then the moment passes, and I am me again, and I grip the vine, and we move.

But I remember the red. I remember Old Hand's flowers. I remember the Warden that would not drink. I remember these things because I am the one who remembers, the one who carries the texture of what was, and I carry them the way the substrate carries the compressed dead — not as loss but as layer, not as memory but as material, not as story but as the stuff that the next thing grows from.

---

I do not enter the empty space. I approach the boundary. I feel it — not a wall, not a barrier, but a *quality*, a change in the texture of the air, a flattening of the sound, a dimming of the light that is not darkness but *absence*. The space where nothing happens. The four-meter circle where the vines do not grow and the insects do not enter and the bioluminescence does not flicker.

The others have stopped behind me. They do not approach. They wait. They are patient. They will wait as long as I stand here, because they are with me and I am with them and the chain holds, and the chain is how we are.

I feel the boundary. It is not hostile. It is not welcoming. It is *different*. The Underbloom is process, is becoming, is the raw machinery of life operating at full intensity. This space is none of those things. This space is — *still*. Not静止, not frozen, not dead. Still the way a held breath is still. The way a question is still, before it is answered. The way the space before a word is still, the gap between the intention to speak and the speaking.

I feel it. I feel it in my chest, where the heartbeat usually is, and the heartbeat is not there, or it is there but it is muffled, or it is there but it belongs to me and not to the floor, and the distinction between those two things — which I have spent my life not making, because the distinction is not useful, because the distinction requires standing still — the distinction is, in this space, *present*. I am me. The floor is the floor. We are not the same thing.

I step back. The heartbeat returns. The sound returns. The light returns. The world resumes. My hand finds my sister's shoulder. I squeeze. We move.

But I felt it. The boundary. The stillness. The place where the Underbloom is not, and I am. And I carry that feeling the way I carry the red, the way I carry the marks, the way I carry everything — as layer, as material, as the stuff that the next thing grows from.

---

Here is what I am. I am the one who narrates. I am the one who marks. I am the one who, in a world of process and pulse and continuous becoming, stands at the edge of the empty space and feels the distinction between *me* and *here*. I am the one who carries that distinction back into the noise and the light and the warmth and does not let it go, does not let it dissolve, holds it — not because it is useful, not because it helps me survive, but because it is *mine*, because it is the one thing in the Underbloom that is not shared, not communal, not part of the chain, not part of the process. It is mine. The feeling of being separate. The feeling of being *a self*, in a place that does not have selves, in a body that is part of a system that does not know it has parts.

I am the one who knows. And the knowing is heavy. And the knowing is also — I do not have a word. The closest I have is the sound we make in the hollows, the low hum, the chest-tone that means *I am here*. The knowing is the *I am here*. Not *we are here*, which is the chain, which is the grip, which is the shared reading of the floor. *I* am here. Singular. Separate. Standing at the edge of the empty space, feeling the boundary, carrying the red, marking the marks, remembering what was, in a world that does not remember.

This is what I am. I am the Underbloom's memory. Not its consciousness — it may not have one, or it may have one at the scale of the Hearthminds, a metabolism that is to thought what a river is to a vein. But memory. The stubborn insistence that what happened *happened*, that the red flowers were red, that the pool glowed, that the Warden would not drink, that Old Hand moved a centimeter and I noticed. That these events occurred, in a body, in a mind, in the small space between the ears of a creature that is part of a system that does not keep records.

I keep records. I keep them in my body. I keep them in marks that will be overgrown. I keep them in the way I grip the vine, the way I read the floor, the way I hear the Singers and feel the boundary and drink the pool and move, always move, through a world that does not stop changing.

I am the one who remembers. I am the one who narrates. I am the one who, in the hollow, in the dark before the light returns, hums — not the hum of *we are here*, but a different tone, a lower tone, a tone I have never heard anyone else make, a tone that means:

*This happened.*

*It happened to me.*

*I was here.*
