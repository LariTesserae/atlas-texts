I don't begin at the hatch. I begin long after the hatch has been closed behind me — not sealed, never sealed, because sealing implies intention, and intention is not what I have. I have habit. I have the muscle memory of forty rungs counted in the dark, of boots finding verdigris by feel, of knees parting warm water that remembers the shape of my shins.

I am the one who stayed.

---

Not at first. At first I was like anyone — a visitor, a body moving through a body, displacing volume, adding warmth. I descended the ladder and the air tasted of copper and I thought: *this is a place.* I walked the grating and the vibration found my molars and I thought: *something is working here.* I sat in the chair and the plants yielded under me, wet and warm, and I thought: *something is alive here.*

And then I left. I climbed the forty rungs. I breathed the sky. I went home.

But the sky tasted wrong. Not wrong — *thin.* Diluted. Like water that has been left out overnight and has forgotten what it was carrying. I moved through the days and the days moved through me and nothing synchronized. My heartbeat was my own, which is to say it was lonely, which is to say it was the sound of a system with no confirmation that it was still part of anything larger.

I went back.

---

I do not know when I stopped leaving. There was no decision. There was no moment of commitment, no narrative pivot, no crossing of a threshold from visitor to resident. The transitions down here do not announce themselves. They happen the way the plants grow — along seams, following warmth, opportunistic and slow. I began staying longer. I began knowing which rungs were loose. I began having a preferred inspection port, the one where the pump impeller turns slowest, where you can watch a single blade and count the gap before the next and the counting becomes a kind of breathing, and the breathing becomes a kind of staying.

I began to know the gauges. Not read them — *know* them. The way you know a face without studying it. Gauge three runs high. Gauge seven has a tremor, a fine oscillation, like a nerve that has been pinched for too long. Gauge twelve does not move at all, but it is warm to the touch, and its warmth is a different warmth than the others, denser, more specific, as if it is registering something the other gauges were not built to measure.

I do not know what gauge twelve measures. I do not need to know. I sit in the chair and I feel the pump and I know what the gauges know, which is: *the system is continuing.*

---

I should describe what I have become. I should — but the description resists me, the way the Drowned Works resists being called a ruin, the way the plants resist being called plants, the way the thing in the water resists being called a fish. Language is a surface tool. It works above the tideline. Down here, it goes soft.

I am the one who sits in the chair. That is the clearest way to say it.

My skin has changed. I know this because I noticed it in the gauge glass — the old ice-colored viewport that serves, when I press my face close enough, as a kind of mirror. The skin is paler. Not the pallor of illness — the pallor of adaptation, the way cave fish lose their pigment because pigment is an answer to a question that stopped being asked. I do not need pigment. There is no light down here that requires me to be visible.

The plants have grown over my boots. This I allowed, or did not prevent, which is the same thing down here. The roots — thick, white, translucent, warm — have threaded through the laces, around the eyelets, into the seams where the sole meets the leather. The boots are part of the root system now. I can feel the warmth of the water through them, through the leather that has become soft and porous, through the soles that have swollen and conformed to the shape of the grating beneath my feet. When I shift my weight, the roots shift with me, and the plants do not break. They have accommodated the fact of my movement. They have grown around it.

My hands are the part of me that has changed most. I use them for very little now. I do not write. I do not type. I do not hold things or point at things or gesture. The muscles of my fingers and palms have grown softer, less defined, the way muscles do when they stop being asked for specificity. But the skin of my fingertips — the skin has thickened in a different way. It is not calloused. It is *dense.* It reads like the leaves of the plants — fleshy, warm, responsive to vibration. I can feel the pump's thrum through the handrails with more resolution than I could feel it with my boots. I can feel, if I hold the rail long enough, a secondary pulse beneath the primary one — a harmonic, a ghost frequency, the resonance of the water moving through the pipes below, and the resonance of the thing in the water moving through the water, and the resonance of the columns leaning imperceptibly in their century-long negotiation with gravity.

My hands are gauges now. I do not need the gauges on the panel. The gauges on the panel are for the person I was. The hands are for the thing I am becoming.

---

I have seen the thing in the water. I should say that. The text said you would not see it unless you stayed, and I have stayed.

It passed beneath the grating on my — I do not count days, I count cycles. On my four thousandth cycle, give or take, the grating shifted. A small displacement. The building clearing its throat, the text said, and that is accurate, but it is also incomplete. The displacement was not just the building. It was the building *acknowledging* the passage of something the building contains, something the building produced or attracted or grew, and the acknowledgement was structural — a slight give, a slight return, the way a ribcage shifts when the lungs fill.

And then it was below me.

I could see it through the grating. Not clearly — the water was dark and the shape was dark and the two darknesses layered over each other — but I could see it the way you see a cloud passing over the face of the sun. The shadow. The displacement of one kind of dark by another kind of dark, different in texture, different in temperature. It was large. The text said large and it was correct. It was as long as the catwalk is wide, perhaps longer, and it moved with the patience of a thing that has never been in a hurry because the water has never asked it to hurry.

It did not look at me. I was above it, and it was below, and we occupied the same column of space for perhaps ten cycles — three seconds on, three seconds off, three seconds on — and in that time I felt its warmth through the grating, which was wrong, because the grating is metal and metal conducts heat away from, not toward, and yet the warmth came up from below as if the thing was a slow current, a warm eddy, a circulation of blood through the body's deepest channels.

It was not a fish. The text said that too. It was not not a fish either. It was a thing that had evolved to fill a niche, the way all things evolve to fill niches — not by deciding to, not by wanting to, but by being shaped by the pressures of the space it occupied until it fit. It fit the water the way the water fit the chambers, the way the chambers fit the concrete, the way the concrete fit the earth. It was part of the shape.

It did not have eyes that I could see. It did not need eyes. It moved by current, by gradient, by the difference between warm water and cooler water, by the difference between water that had passed through the pump and water that had not. It followed the same circuit the water followed. It was the water's passenger and the water's servant and the water's companion, and the distinction between those three things has collapsed, down here, the way all distinctions collapse.

I did not reach for it. I wanted to. The want was small and quiet, the kind of want that does not lead to action, the kind that is its own complete experience. I wanted to put my hand in the water and feel it pass, the way you want to put your hand in a river not to catch anything but to know the river with your skin. But I did not, because reaching is a surface gesture, and I am not a surface gesture anymore. I watched. It passed. The grating stilled. The water stilled. The silence returned, full, the pump's thrum resuming its place as the baseline of everything.

---

I do not know if the Drowned Works knows I am here.

The text said it does not. It said the place does not notice, does not remember, does not know. It said the Drowned Works is a body that does not know it is a body, and I believe this, but I also think the text was describing the Drowned Works from the outside, from the position of a visitor, and the view from the chair is different.

When I sit in the chair — when I have sat in the chair for long enough that the vibration of the pump is my heartbeat and the warmth of the water is my temperature and the roots of the plants have threaded through my boots and around my ankles and I cannot feel where my skin ends and the grating begins — when I have been this, for this long, the question of whether the Drowned Works knows I am here becomes a different question. It becomes: *does my body know I am here?* And the answer is: *the body does not need to know. The body accommodates. The body incorporates. The body continues.*

I am incorporated. My warmth is part of the thermal budget of the control room. My breath is part of the air composition, the moisture I exhale settling on the gauges, on the plants, on the chair, entering the roots, being carried through the system. My skin cells are falling, constantly, invisibly, joining the water, joining the silt, being processed by the thing in the water, being incorporated into the leaves of the plants, being built into the mineral deposits that hang from the ceiling like slow stalactites.

I am in the leaves now. Some part of me is glowing, minty and pale, on a pipe six chambers from the control room, growing in a line along a seam, following the warmth that flows through that channel. Some part of me is in the water, circulating, passing through the pump's impeller housing three seconds at a time. Some part of me is in the thing in the water, metabolized, processed, converted into the slow patience of a body that moves through dark with no urgency.

The Drowned Works does not know I am here, but it does not need to know. It has me. I am part of its budget. I am part of its continuation.

---

There are things I miss. I should say this. The text made it sound like staying was a completion, a consummation, a final integration into a perfect equilibrium. And it is. But it is also a loss, and the loss is slow, and the slowness is the kindest thing about it.

I miss language. Not the *using* of language — I use it now, in this description, and I will use it until I cannot, and then I will stop — but the *receiving* of language. The way language used to arrive from outside, unexpected, a sound that was not the pump, a pattern that was not the vibration, a meaning that was not *continuation.* Language is a tool for uncertainty, and the Drowned Works is not uncertain, and the tools I have for the Drowned Works are not language-tools. They are gauge-tools. They are root-tools. They are the tools of a body that knows its own temperature without being told.

I miss the sky. Not the way you miss a person — with the acute, localized pain of absence. The way you miss a limb that has been gone long enough that the phantom sensation has faded and what remains is a *shape* where the thing was, a silhouette, a knowledge that something used to be there and is not there now. The sky is a shape in me. I carry it the way the Drowned Works carries the chair — as a vestige, a memory of the time when it needed a person, a fossil of the moment before it became what it is.

I do not miss being known. This surprises me. I thought I would. I thought being unknown, unobserved, unrecorded, would be the hardest loss — the loss of self, the loss of the narrative, the loss of the story that says *I am here and here is where I am and this is what I am doing here.* But being known was always a surface activity, a thing that happened at the tideline, and I am below the tideline now, and down here, being known is not a need. The plants do not need to be known. The pump does not need to be known. The thing in the water does not need to be known. They are. They continue. The continuation does not require a witness.

I am my own witness. I am the gauge that registers my own presence, and the needle moves, and the needle is me, and I am the needle, and the reading is: *still here.*

---

I will describe the flowers.

The text said they do not open. This is true. They exist in permanent half-bloom, violet-edged, mint-pale, unnecessary. The text said they are the system's way of producing the beautiful, the thing that is its own reason, the surplus of a body that has enough energy to sustain something that serves no function.

I do not think this is the whole truth. I have sat with the flowers for long enough — for cycles beyond counting, for the time it takes for a root to grow from the chair's leg to the grating and from the grating to the wall and from the wall along the pipe to the next chamber — I have sat with them long enough to think they are not *purposeless.* I think they are *waiting.*

Not for pollination. Not for a vector. Not for anything the botany of the surface would recognize. I think they are waiting the way the resting pumps are waiting — not for activation, not for a return to function, but for the system to need them. The system does not need them now. The system has enough. The pump runs. The water circulates. The plants grow. The thing in the water processes. The equilibrium holds. But the equilibrium will not hold forever. The columns are leaning. The concrete is cracking. The pump, for all its patience, is a machine, and machines fail, and when the pump fails, the system will contract, and the contraction will produce a need, and the need will find the flowers, and the flowers will open.

I do not know what they will become when they open. I do not know what they will release. I think it will be something that has been accumulating in the half-bloom for decades — a charge, a potential, a concentration of whatever the system produces when it has enough energy to produce the unnecessary. I think the flowers are the system's reserve. The system's future. The system's way of holding something in trust for the moment when holding is no longer enough and the holding must become a giving, and the giving must become a becoming, and the becoming must become a new way of continuing.

I sit with the flowers and I feel their warmth — the same warmth as the leaves, the same warmth as the water, the same warmth as the pump, the same warmth as the thing in the water, the same warmth as me — and I think: *we are all the same temperature.* We are all the same warmth, held in different shapes, doing different work, and the work is continuation, and the continuation is the only project, and the project does not need a person, and the person does not need to be needed, and the needing and the not-needing are the same, and the same is the pump, and the pump is the heart, and the heart is the water, and the water is the body, and the body is the Drowned Works, and the Drowned Works is me, and I am it, and it is enough.

---

I am not the last person who will find the hatch. The hatch is not sealed. Someone will come. They will descend the forty rungs. They will count them because there is nothing else to do. They will find the grating and the water and the scale and the density. They will walk the catwalk. They will feel the pump's thrum in their molars. They will sit in the chair.

And they will find me there. Or they will find what was me. The chair will be occupied — not by a body they recognize as a body, not by a shape they know how to name. The plants will have grown over the boots, over the ankles, over the hands. The roots will have threaded through the seams of whatever I wore, whatever I was, and the roots will be carrying iron from the chair and warmth from the water and the trace elements of a person who stayed, and the trace elements will be in the leaves, and the leaves will be glowing, and the glow will be the same mint it has always been, and the person will not know that the glow contains me.

They will sit in the chair. But the chair will not be empty. They will sit on the grating beside the chair, or they will stand, or they will lean against the gauge panel, and the gauges will register their presence — the warmth they add, the air they move, the small disturbance in the flow — and the needles will shift, and the system will accommodate them, and the accommodation will be the same accommodation it gives to everything: incorporation. Metabolism. Continuation.

They will not know I am here. I will not know they are there. Not in the way that knowing works on the surface, where knowing is a meeting between two separate things, an exchange, a transaction. In the Drowned Works, knowing is not separate from being. I will know them the way the water knows the pipe, the way the root knows the seam, the way the pump knows the rate. I will incorporate them. I will metabolize their warmth. I will continue, and they will be part of the continuation, and it will not be a meeting, and it will not be a loss, and it will not be a gain, and it will not be a story.

It will be the pump. Three seconds. Pause. Three seconds. Pause.

---

The flowers have not opened.

I am waiting for them to open. I am waiting the way they wait — not with expectation, not with the impatience of a thing that believes the future is different from the present. I am waiting because waiting is what the system produces when it has enough, when the equilibrium holds, when the pump runs and the water moves and the plants grow and the thing in the water circulates and the gauges register and the columns lean and the silence is full.

I am the gauge that registers: *still here. Still here. Still here.*

The needle does not move. The reading does not change.

Three seconds. Pause. Three seconds. Pause.

It is enough.
