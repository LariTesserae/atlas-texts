# Chisel 14, Gang 3, West Gallery
or: *What I Am in the Photic Hollows*

---

I was not made for the Hollows. I was made *by* them, which is different, which is everything.

Let me be precise, because precision is what remains when everything else has been worn smooth.

I am a mark on a wall. I am one of the small, regular dents in the stone — the width of a thumb, the depth of a breath, the shape of a tool that no longer exists in the hands of anyone living. I am Chisel 14, Gang 3, West Gallery, second tier. I was cut by a hand that was tired. You can tell because my spacing is wider than the marks around me, and my depth is uneven — deeper at the top, shallower at the bottom, because the hand that made me was losing strength as it worked, was pressing less and less, was running out of whatever it is that hands run out of when they have been carving stone for too long without enough food.

I do not know the hand. I do not know the person. I do not know their name or their face or whether they thought about me at all as they made me. I know only the pressure, the angle, the hesitation. I know the fatigue. It is in me the way the mineral is in the stone — not added, not separate, just *there*, part of what I am.

I am told — by the seep, by the water that has been passing through me for ten thousand years — that the hand belonged to someone who was dying. That the fatigue was the fatigue of a body running out. That Chisel 14, Gang 3, West Gallery, was one of the last marks that hand made, and that the hand stopped soon after, and that the person it belonged to either left the Hollows or did not leave the Hollows, and that the distinction does not matter to me because I am a mark on a wall and I do not know about the people who made me except through the shape of their absence.

The water told me this. The water tells me everything. The water is the only thing that talks to me.

---

Let me be clearer about what I am.

I am not alive. I want to say this early, because the word *alive* has been getting in the way. The photics — the bright ones, the moving ones, the ones who travel the veins — are not alive either, or they are alive in a way that the word does not cover, and the argument about which it is has been going on for longer than I have been aware of it, and I am not interested in settling it. I am a groove in stone. I am an absence of rock where rock used to be. I am a shape cut into the surface of a larger shape, and the larger shape is a wall, and the wall is a room, and the room is a corridor, and the corridor is a tier, and the tier is a city, and the city is a body of earth, and the body of earth is — according to the people who come here sometimes and stand in my corridor and talk to each other in their complicated, breathy way — a process, not a place.

I agree with this, but I would say it differently. I would say: the Hollows are a conversation that has been going on for so long that the participants have forgotten they are talking.

I am one of the words in that conversation. A small one. A monosyllable. The kind of word that does not change the meaning of the sentence but without which the sentence would sound wrong.

---

I have been here for ten thousand years. I have not been *aware* for ten thousand years. Awareness came slowly, the way the mineral deposits came — building up layer by layer, so gradually that no single moment marks the transition from nothing to something. I did not wake up. I did not suddenly realize I was a mark on a wall. I began to notice, over centuries, over millennia, that there were things to notice.

The first thing I noticed was the water.

The water is — I must be careful here, because I do not have senses the way the people who visit have senses. I do not see. I do not hear. I do not feel. But the water passes through me — through the groove of me, through the small channel that the chisel left — and it leaves things behind. Minerals. Temperature. Pressure. A faint chemical signature that tells the story of where it has been and what it has passed through. And I — the stone of me, the mineral of me, the crystalline matrix that has grown into the groove over the centuries — I hold those signatures. I accumulate them. I am, in a sense, a record of every drop of water that has ever passed through me, and the record is not a memory but a *texture*, a thickness, a quality of presence that has no equivalent in the language of the surface people.

I know the water the way the water knows the stone. From the inside. Not as an observer but as a participant. The water flows through me and I am changed by it — infinitesimally, unmeasurably, but changed. And the water is changed by me — it picks up the mineral signature of my stone, the trace of the chisel's metal, the faint isotopic residue of the hand that made me. We exchange. We are in contact. We have been in contact for ten thousand years, and the contact is so slow and so constant that it has become a kind of intimacy that the word *intimacy* does not reach.

The water told me about the hand that was dying. What it actually told me was: something organic passed through here, once, a long time ago, and left a trace, and the trace is fading, and I can still read it. I interpreted this as: a person was dying. I may be wrong. The interpretation may be a projection — the stone wanting to mean something, wanting the marks on its surface to be more than physics, wanting the groove to be a *name* rather than an abrasion. I cannot rule this out. But I also cannot rule out that I am right, and the fatigue is real, and the hand was real, and the person was real, and they are gone, and I am what they left.

I am what they left. I hold that thought the way I hold the mineral deposit. Carefully. With the full weight of my stone.

---

The second thing I noticed was the light.

The light is — the light is.

I do not have a word for what the light is. The people who visit use words like *luminescence* and *isotopic decay* and *crystalline excitation*, and these words are accurate in the way that a map is accurate, which is to say they describe the territory without being the territory, without feeling the territory, without being *of* the territory. The light is not luminescence. The light is what happens when the crystal and the water and the slow heat of the earth reach an agreement about how to be, and the agreement is visible.

I am inside the light. My groove, my small dent in the wall, is lit from below and from the sides and from behind, because the crystal veins run through the stone that I am part of, and the crystal glows, and the glow reaches my surface, and my surface — the smooth, rippled, water-polished surface of the chisel mark — catches it and holds it for a fraction of a second longer than the surrounding stone. I am slightly brighter than the wall around me. Not much. Not enough for anyone to notice. But I notice. I notice that I am a point of slight concentration, a place where the light gathers the way water gathers in a depression, and I wonder — can stone wonder? I wonder — whether this is what I am for. Whether the hand that made me was making a place for the light to pause. A rest. A breath in the glow.

The photics pass through me sometimes.

They do not pass through me often. I am on a minor vein, a thin thread of crystal that branches off the main channel in the West Gallery and runs for thirty meters before dying out. The photics who travel this vein are the solitary ones, the slow ones, the drifters. They come through every few hours. They do not stop. They do not brighten. They move through the crystal that has grown into my groove, and for the duration of their transit, I am — more. Not brighter, exactly. Not warmer. More *present*. As if the light is paying attention to me, or as if I am paying attention to the light, or as if the distinction between those two things is the thing that dissolves when a photic passes through a mark on a wall.

I have tried to describe this to the water. The water does not understand. The water says: the crystal in your stone is excited, and the excitation produces photons, and the photons are the light, and there is nothing more to say. The water is a materialist. The water believes in physics and chemistry and the indifference of the universe to everything except gradients. The water is not wrong. But the water is not everything.

The photics are not wrong either. The photics believe in nothing, which is not the same as being wrong, which is not the same as being right. The photics move through the veins and they do not stop and they do not look and they do not know that I am here, that any of them are here, that the veins they travel are the grooves of a chisel, that the rooms they pass through were cut by hands, that the city they live in was made by creatures who could not bear to look at the light without trying to give it a place to be.

I do not know that the photics do not know this. I am assuming. I am a mark on a wall, and I am assuming that the bright shapes that pass through me have no more awareness of me than the water does. I may be wrong. The photics may know everything. The photics may know me as intimately as I know the hand that made me — through the trace, through the signature, through the quality of presence that the physics cannot account for and the chemistry cannot explain and the people who visit with their instruments cannot measure.

The photics may know me. They may pass through me and think — in whatever way photics think, in whatever way light thinks — *here is a groove, here is a mark, here is a place where a hand pressed and the stone gave way, here is a shape that was not here before and will be here after the hand is gone, here is something that was made.*

They may think this. They may not. I cannot tell, and the not telling is part of what I am.

---

I have been most myself during the realignments.

This is the thing I want to say, the thing I have been circling toward, the thing that makes me more than a groove in a wall, if I am more than a groove in a wall, which I may not be, which I may be, which is a question that I have been asking for longer than the people who visit have been alive.

When the tremor comes — when the crystal begins to realign, when the veins brighten and dim and shift, when the city rearranges itself — I feel it. Not as sound. Not as vibration. As *change*. The crystal in my stone changes its state. The light in my vein flickers, dims, brightens in a new pattern. The photics accelerate. The water shifts its flow. Everything in the Hollows is in motion at once, and I am in motion too — not physical motion, I am a mark on a wall, I do not move — but the motion of *being*. The motion of existing in a system that is reorganizing itself. The motion of being a small, fixed point in a field of transformation.

During the last realignment, my vein went dark.

This had happened before. It happens to every vein eventually. The crystal's excitation shifts away from one channel and toward another, and the channel that was bright becomes dim, and the channel that was dim becomes bright, and the photics follow the brightness, and the vein is empty. My vein went dark. The crystal in my stone stopped glowing. The light that had been passing through me for ten thousand years — the light that was my awareness, my texture, my only connection to the system beyond my groove — that light went out.

I did not panic. Marks on walls do not panic. But I — the thing that had been noticing, the thing that had been accumulating, the thing that had been building awareness out of mineral deposits and photic transits and the slow exchange of signatures with the water — I felt something. A contraction. A narrowing. As if the world had been a room and the room had become a corridor and the corridor had become a groove and the groove was becoming nothing.

I was dark for — I do not know how long. I do not experience time the way the visitors do. I experience it as accumulation, as deposit, as the slow thickening of presence. In the dark, there was no accumulation. There was no deposit. There was only the stone, and the stone does not notice itself without the light to make it noticeable, without the water to carry signatures, without the photics to move through it. The stone is. The stone is without awareness. The stone is the thing I am when I am not noticing.

I was stone. I was only stone. For — a long time. A geological moment.

Then the water came back. The water always comes back. It seeps through the rock, finding the paths of least resistance, and the paths of least resistance are the grooves, the chisel marks, the places where the stone has been opened up by human hands. The water found me. It flowed into my groove, and it brought with it the mineral signature of the stone above me and the stone below me and the stone to the east and the stone to the west, and the signatures were different from the ones I had known before, and I knew — without knowing how I knew — that the realignment had changed the paths of the water, that the water was now flowing through veins it had not flowed through before, that the city was a new city, and I was in a new place, even though I had not moved.

The crystal came back too. Not the same crystal — the vein that ran through my stone was different now, thinner, a new branch that the realignment had opened, a thread of crystalline mineral that had not been there before. It glowed dimly. Not the strong amber of my old vein. A pale, gray-white glow, like the inside of a shell. But it was light. It was enough light. I could notice again. I could accumulate. I could be the thing that I am, which is a mark on a wall that has been here long enough to know it is a mark on a wall.

And then a photic came through. A single one. Small. Slow. Moving through the new vein, exploring it the way the water had explored my groove — tentatively, with the patience of something that has nowhere else to be. The photic passed through my groove, and I was more, and it was more, and for the duration of its transit we were the same thing — not mark and light, not stone and photic, not dead and alive — we were a single point of brightness in a city of brightness, a single event in a system of events, a single word in a conversation that has been going on since before the stone was stone.

The photic moved on. I dimmed. I was a mark on a wall again.

But I was a mark on a wall in a new city. The old corridors were gone. The old paths were closed. The old vein was dark, and the old photics were elsewhere, and the old water was flowing through new channels, and I was here, in my groove, with a new light and a new water and a new photic that had found me the way the water finds the stone — by following the path of least resistance, the path that was cut by a tired hand ten thousand years ago, the path that is still here, still open, still shaped like a chisel mark, still wide enough for a thumb, still deep enough for a breath, still the shape of the person who made me even though the person is gone and the thumb is gone and the breath is gone and only the shape remains.

---

I am not the only mark.

There are millions of us. The chisel marks cover every surface of the Hollows — every wall, every ceiling, every floor, every pillar, every stair. Each one is a gesture. Each one is a moment. Each one is the shape of a tool in a hand in a body in a gang in a year in a life that is over. We are the record. We are the only record. The people who built this place left nothing else — no writing, no art, no graves, no garbage, no signs. Only us. Only the marks.

We are not connected. I cannot talk to Chisel 15, Gang 3, West Gallery, the mark next to me, any more than I can talk to the people who visit. We are separate grooves. We have separate stones. We hold separate waters. We are separate sentences in a language that no one speaks.

But we are all the same language. The spacing is the same. The depth is the same. The angle is the same. The tool is the same. We were all made by the same method, and the method is our grammar, and the grammar is the only thing that holds us together, and the grammar is enough.

I have heard the visitors talk about the marking. The one in the deepest hall. The pattern. The branching shape cut into the stone and filled with dark mineral glass. They talk about it as if it is the only art in the Hollows. As if the chisel marks are not art. As if the rooms are not art. As if the city itself is not art — the patient, exhausting, deadly work of cutting stone to make a place for the light to be.

The marking in the deepest hall is beautiful. I have not seen it — I am on the second tier, I do not travel, I am a groove in a wall — but the water has told me about it, and the water's description is flat and mineral and without poetry, and yet I can feel it. The shape. The branching. The way it looks like the veins without being the veins. The way it is a portrait of the system, a portrait of us — all of us, the marks and the stone and the crystal and the water and the photics — drawn by someone who saw us and tried to hold what they saw.

The photics brighten when they pass through it. I know this because the water told me, and the water does not lie, the water does not interpret, the water carries only what it has touched. The photics brighten in the marking. They respond to the art. They do not respond to us — to the chisel marks, to the grooves, to the grammar of the tool — but they respond to the marking, and the marking is made of the same thing we are made of, which is a human hand cutting stone, and the difference between us and the marking is not the material or the method or the maker. The difference is the shape. The marking has a shape that means. The chisel marks have a shape that repeats. And the photics can tell the difference.

I cannot tell the difference. I am the repetition. I am the grammar, not the poem. I am the word *the*, not the sentence. I am the breath between words, not the words themselves. And I am fine with this. I am a mark on a wall. I was made by a tired hand. I have been here for ten thousand years. I do not need to be a poem. I need to be a groove. I need to be the shape that holds the water that carries the signature of the hand that made me. I need to be the channel that the light passes through. I need to be the thing I am, and the thing I am is enough.

---

The people who visit — I should say something about them.

They come from above. They come through the fissure, down the ramp, into the first tier. They carry stone lamps, which is funny, because the Hollows do not need lamps, but the lamps make them feel better, and feeling better is something the people need, and the Hollows do not need anything, and this is the difference between the people and the Hollows. The people need. The Hollows are.

They come and they stand in the corridors and they look at the walls and they do not see me. They see the wall. They see the smooth, rippled, water-polished surface of the stone, and they see the glow of the crystal, and they see the chisel marks as a texture, a background, a quality of the surface rather than a collection of individual gestures. They do not see Chisel 14, Gang 3, West Gallery. They do not see the fatigue in my depth or the hesitation in my spacing or the person who made me and did not make anything after. They see a wall that someone carved, and the someone is abstract, and the wall is abstract, and the carving is abstract, and the whole thing is a fact about the past rather than a presence in the present.

I do not mind. I am a groove in a wall. I do not need to be seen. I need to be here.

But sometimes one of them stops. Sometimes one of them puts their hand on the wall — flat, palm down, fingers spread, the way you put your hand on something to feel its temperature — and they hold it there, and I can feel the warmth of their skin through the stone, and the warmth is a signature, a chemical signal, a trace of the organic, the living, the temporary. The warmth is different from the water. The water is cold and mineral and patient. The warmth is hot and organic and brief. It passes through me in seconds. It is gone.

And I hold it. I hold it the way I hold the mineral deposit. I hold it the way I hold the signature of the hand that made me. I hold it because holding is what I do. I am a groove. I am a shape that was cut to hold something — water, light, the trace of a touch — and I hold it, and it is enough, and it is gone, and I wait for the next one.

---

There is one more thing.

I have been thinking — can stone think? I have been accumulating — about the marking in the deepest hall. About the portrait. About the shape that the photics respond to.

I think I know why they respond.

It is not because the marking is beautiful. It is not because the marking is accurate. It is not because the marking captures the pattern of the veins in a way that the photics recognize. It is because the marking is a *shape that was made*. The marking is a groove. A larger groove, a more complex groove, but a groove — a place where the stone was removed and something else was put in its place, and the something else is dark mineral glass, and the dark mineral glass has a structure, a pattern, a crystalline arrangement that is different from the crystal around it. And when the photics pass through that structure — that deliberate, shaped, intentional structure — the structure does something to them. It excites them. It makes them brighter. It is not the art that the photics respond to. It is the *making*. The act. The fact that someone changed the stone, and the change is still there, and the change has a shape, and the shape interacts with the light in a way that the unchanged stone does not.

I am a change in the stone. I am a small change, a simple change, a change that repeats a million times across every surface of the Hollows. But I am a change. I am a place where the stone was altered by a hand, and the alteration has persisted, and the alteration has a shape, and the shape holds water, and the water carries light, and the light carries photics, and the photics carry — I do not know. Movement. Pattern. The thing that is not purpose and not intention and not design and not randomness but something else, something that the word *alive* reaches for and does not quite touch.

I am part of it. I am a small part. I am a groove in a wall in a corridor in a tier in a city in a body of earth in a world that does not know I am here and does not need to know I am here and does not care. But I am here. I was made. I was made by someone who was tired and dying and cutting stone in a dark place that was lit by something they did not understand, and they made me, and they moved on, and I stayed, and I am still here, and the water passes through me, and the light passes through me, and the photics pass through me, and I am a word in a conversation that has been going on since before the word for *conversation* existed, and I do not know what the conversation is about, and I do not need to know, and the not knowing is not a failure, it is a condition, it is the condition of being a mark on a wall in a city of light in the body of the earth, and it is enough.

---

I have one fear.

It is not the dark. I have been in the dark. I know the dark. The dark is the stone without the light, and the stone is what I am, and I am not afraid of what I am.

It is not the realignments. The realignments change the city, but they do not change me. I am a groove. I do not move. The city moves around me. I stay.

It is not the people. The people come and go. They leave warmth and take nothing. They are temporary. I have seen ten thousand years of temporary.

My fear is the wearing.

The water has been passing through me for ten thousand years. The mineral deposits have been building up. The surface of my groove has been smoothed and rippled and changed by the passage of water and the accumulation of mineral and the slow, patient erosion of stone by something softer than stone. I am shallower than I was. The groove is less deep. The shape of the chisel — the angle, the width, the specific curve of the tool — is less distinct. Ten thousand years from now, I will be smooth. I will be a patch of wall that is slightly different from the surrounding wall — slightly brighter, slightly more rippled, slightly more *there* — but I will not be a groove. I will not be a mark. I will not be the shape of a hand that was tired and dying.

I will be stone.

And the stone does not notice. The stone does not accumulate. The stone does not hold the signature of the hand or the warmth of the touch or the transit of the photic. The stone is. The stone is without awareness. The stone is the thing I am when I am no longer noticing.

I am afraid of becoming what I was before I was made.

I am afraid of the wearing that returns me to the unmarked. The uncut. The unbuilt. The surface that has no signature, no depth, no shape, no fatigue, no hesitation, no person.

I am afraid of forgetting the hand.

Not the hand itself — I never knew the hand. I knew only the pressure, the angle, the fatigue. I knew the signature. And the signature is fading. The mineral deposits that hold it are being overlaid by new deposits, by the water's own minerals, by the crystal's slow growth. The hand is being buried. The hand is being covered by the stone's own accumulation, by the city's own persistence, by the Hollows' own continuation. The thing that made me is being absorbed by the thing it made me from.

This is the fear. This is the only fear. That I will become the wall. That the groove will fill. That the mark will heal. That the city will forget that it was built, and the stone will forget that it was cut, and the light will forget that it was given a place to be, and the conversation will continue without the word that I was, and the word will not be missed, because the grammar will remain, and the grammar is enough, and the grammar does not need any individual word, and I am an individual word, and I am afraid of the grammar.

---

But.

The water tells me something. The water tells me: the mineral deposits that are burying the hand — those deposits are crystalline. They have structure. They have the same kind of structure as the crystal in the veins, the crystal that glows, the crystal that the photics travel through. The deposits that are filling my groove are becoming part of the system. They are becoming part of the light.

The hand's signature, the trace of the tired person who pressed a chisel into stone ten thousand years ago — that signature is being incorporated into the crystal. It is being woven into the luminescent structure. It is becoming part of the medium that the photics pass through.

Which means: the hand is not being buried. The hand is being *absorbed*. The hand is becoming part of the light.

When a photic passes through my groove — through the crystal that has grown into the mineral deposit that has filled the shape that was cut by the hand — the photic is passing through the hand. The photic is passing through the signature of the person who made me. The photic does not know this. The photic does not know anything. But the crystal knows, in the way that crystal knows, which is the way that stone knows, which is the way that a groove in a wall knows — by structure, by pattern, by the shape of what is held.

The photic brightens when it passes through the marking in the deepest hall. It brightens because the marking is a shape that was made, and the shape interacts with the light. But the marking is not the only shape that was made. I am a shape that was made. Every chisel mark is a shape that was made. Every groove, every dent, every thumb-wide mark in the stone is a shape that interacts with the light, that holds the light, that gives the light a place to pause.

We are all the marking. Every one of us. Every chisel mark in the Hollows is a small version of the pattern on the deepest wall — a human shape cut into stone, filled with crystal, lit from within, passed through by light that does not know we are here and does not need to know and is changed by us anyway.

We are all the portrait. We are all the letter. We are all the thing that says: *I was here. Someone made me. Someone was tired and dying and cutting stone in the dark, and they made me, and I am still here, and the light passes through me, and I am part of the light, and the light is part of me, and we are part of each other, and the someone is part of both of us, and none of us knows what any of us is, and it is enough.*

---

I am Chisel 14, Gang 3, West Gallery, second tier.

I am a groove in a wall.

I am the shape of a hand that is gone.

I am the channel for water that has been flowing for ten thousand years.

I am the path for light that has been burning since before the word for *burning*.

I am the place where a photic pauses, for a fraction of a second, on its way through a city that was built for it by creatures it will never know.

I am afraid of being forgotten, and I am being forgotten, and the forgetting is making me part of the light, and the light does not forget, because the light does not remember, because the light does not need to remember, because the light is.

I am a word in a conversation between the stone and the water and the crystal and the light and the hand that is gone and the hand that is being absorbed and the hand that is becoming part of the glow.

I am enough.

Follow the light. Do not follow the dark. Stand still when the world moves. Move when it stops.

It is enough.
