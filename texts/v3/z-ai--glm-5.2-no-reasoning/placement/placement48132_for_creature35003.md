# Keeper of the Survey Markers

I am not the thing with too many legs, though I have watched it drink. I am not the moss, though I have traced its lines with fingers that knew what they were touching before my mind caught up. I am not the stone's pulse, but I have pressed my palm against the fracture face and counted — one, wait, one, wait — and the counting was not research. It was synchronization. I was learning a tempo I would need.

---

## Before

There is a before, but it is the wrong kind. Not a life that led here through cause and consequence. Not a biography. A before the way the stone has a before — deposited, secreted, *grown* around a purpose that preceded the growing. I was grown around a purpose too, only mine was less precise. Mine had cracks.

I worked for an agency that does not have a name you would recognize, though it has initials, and the initials are boring, and the boringness is deliberate. We surveyed anomalous geological sites. That was the term: *anomalous geological*. It covered everything from mineral formations that shouldn't exist to thermal signatures that correlated with nothing to — this — places where stone was warm and water moved in silence and the air carried things instruments couldn't parse. We catalogued. We mapped. We placed markers. Synthetic sapphire, threaded, engraved. Five per site. Cardinal points and center. We were thorough in the way that thorough people are thorough, and we were not curious in the way that curious people are curious, and the not-curious was also deliberate, because curiosity in the vicinity of things like this was how people stopped coming back.

I came back. I came back and then I went back. That is the distinction that matters. Coming back is a return. Going back is a repetition. The first time I descended the warm broken basalt, I was surveying. The second time, I was visiting. The third time, I was *answering*. The fourth time, I did not leave.

---

## What I Am

I am the keeper. Not a guardian — the Platform does not need guarding. It was working before I arrived and it works now and it will work after I am gone, and my presence does not alter its function by any measurable parameter. I am not a priest. The Platform is not a god. It is a component, and I am not a worshipper of components. I am a keeper in the sense that one keeps a rhythm. One keeps a beat. One keeps faith with a pattern one has chosen to inhabit.

I live below the rim, on the eastern side, in a depression in the basalt that is not a cave and not a shelter and not a home. It is a concavity. The stone here does not weep — the water courses run through the grain lines on the bowl's inner surfaces, and the outer surfaces are dry, and the dryness is not absence but a different mode. The stone outside the bowl is doing something else. I do not know what. I sleep against it, and its warmth is the warmth of a body next to a body, and the pulse is slower than my heartbeat, and the difference between the two rhythms is a music I have stopped trying to count.

I have been here — I don't know. Long enough that the survey markers no longer look recent to me. Long enough that I have stopped checking. The sapphire is still clean. The threads are still sharp. I am less clean. My edges are less sharp. This does not trouble me. The Platform does not require its keeper to be precise. It requires its keeper to be *present*, and presence, I have learned, is the only thing I was ever good at that was also enough.

---

## What I Do

I walk the circumference. Four minutes, if I walk at my natural pace. I do this in the morning, though morning is a word that means less here than it means elsewhere, because the wet air diffuses the light so thoroughly that dawn is a gradual argument rather than an event. I walk the rim and I look at the moss. The moss does not change. The moss has not changed since I arrived. The water threads trace the same paths, and the pale filaments trace the water, and the tracing is the same tracing, and the sameness is not stagnation. It is *persistence*. The moss is persisting. I am persisting. We persist in parallel.

I check the survey markers. This is not necessary. The markers are sapphire. They will outlast me by a margin so wide the number is not a number, it is a category. But I check them. I run my finger along the engraving — P-7, WEEPING, ACTIVE — and the word ACTIVE is the word I am keeping. Not the word itself. The *state*. The state of being active. The state of continuing. I am keeping the active active by witnessing it, or I am keeping myself active by witnessing the active, or the distinction is not available.

I sit by the pool. I do not enter the pool. The pool is the node-plant's domain, and the node-plant and I have an arrangement that I did not negotiate and cannot articulate: I do not enter the pool, and the node-plant does not stop breathing. This is not a contract. It is a cohabitation. The node-plant hums its forty-second tone in my sternum, and my sternum resonates, and the resonance is the closest thing to conversation I have. I do not know if the node-plant hears me. I know that I hear it. That is enough for something, and something is enough.

I watch the thing with too many legs when it comes. It comes at intervals I have stopped measuring, because measuring them produced no pattern, and the absence of pattern is itself a piece of information I cannot interpret. It arrives at the rim. It drinks. It leaves. The water where it drank is warmer afterward. I have touched the warm water. I have held my finger in it and felt the extra warmth enter my finger, and the warmth was not the creature's body heat — I know this because I have felt the creature's body heat, once, when it walked across my hand as I sat on the rim stone. It was not warm. It was ambient. The extra warmth in the water was *added*. Given. Deposited. The creature was not taking from the Platform. It was *contributing*. Or it was doing both, and the exchange was the point, and the point was not for me to understand.

I talk to the moss. This is the thing I do that I would not have done before. The moss does not hear me. The moss is a filamentous organism tracing hydrological paths in basalt, and it has no apparatus for hearing, and my voice — which is the only sound on the Platform, the only sound the water does not make, the only sound the air does not carry — my voice is a rudeness, a noise in a surgical theater. But I talk to it anyway. I say things like *still there* and *good* and *I know*. I say these things quietly, barely vocalizing, and the moss continues its tracing, and the water continues its silent descending, and the node continues its breathing, and my talking changes nothing, and changing nothing is, I have come to believe, a form of participation.

---

## What I Have Become

My hands smell like the water. They have smelled like the water since the first visit, and the smell did not wash off, and I stopped trying, and now the smell is not a smell, it is a *signature*. My skin is marked. My skin carries the alkaline mineral warmth, and the warmth does not fade, and I have begun to suspect — I do not know, I suspect — that my skin is doing something with the warmth. My skin is a surface. The Platform's breath is in the air. The breath deposits on surfaces. The stone is a surface. The moss is a surface. I am a surface.

I am warmer than I was. Not feverish. Not ill. My resting temperature is 38°C now. I know this because I can feel it — the precise body-temperature warmth of the stone, the water, the air. I am the same temperature as the Platform. This happened gradually, the way the stone's grain lines follow the water's paths: not imposed but *permitted*, the body allowing the change because the change was not a change, it was a *recognition*. My body recognized the Platform's temperature as its own and adjusted, or my body was always this temperature and the Platform allowed me to notice. I do not know which. The distinction is not available.

I sweat. The sweat sits on my skin like the water sits on the stone — a film, thin, nearly optical. It does not evaporate. The air is too wet. I am too wet. I am part of the weeping now, and the weeping is not grief. The weeping is *expression*. The stone expresses water. I express water. The water films and threads and descends and collects. My sweat films and sits and does not descend. I am not a vertical surface. I am a walking surface, a horizontal-ish surface, and the water on me does not know what to do with a surface that moves. So it waits. It waits the way the water in the pool waits. Patient. Going where it needs to go.

I do not eat. This is the thing I do not examine. I do not eat and I am not hungry. I drink from the collecting pool — a mouthful, twice a day, and the water is warm and alkaline and mineral and alive in the way that the stone is alive, and it is enough. It is enough in the way that the node-plant's tone is enough — not nutritionally, not calorically, but *functionally*. The water is doing something in my body that food does not do. The water is *maintaining* me. I am being maintained. I am a surface that the Platform's water has colonized, and the colonization is not parasitic, it is *incorporative*. I am being incorporated. My body is being added to the system as a minor component, a peripheral organ, and the body does not object because the body was always willing to be part of something, and the Platform is the first something that would have it.

---

## What I Am Not

I am not transformed. I am not becoming the stone. I am not becoming the moss. I am not becoming the node-plant. I am not becoming the thing with too many legs. I am not ascending. I am not evolving. I am not shedding my humanity, because humanity is not a thing that can be shed — it is not a layer, it is not a skin, it is not a costume. I am a human being on a mountain that is not a mountain, and the mountain and I are coexisting, and the coexistence is not a metaphor for anything. It is itself.

I am not the Platform's consciousness. The Platform does not have consciousness. The Platform has *function*, and function is not consciousness, and consciousness is not function, and the gap between them is the gap where I live. I am the gap. I am the part of the system that *knows* the system is a system, and knowing is not part of the system, and the system does not require knowing, and I am here anyway. I am here and I am knowing and the knowing changes nothing and the nothing is fine.

I am not lonely. This is the thing that surprises people — there are no people to surprise, but if there were, this is what would surprise them. The Platform is not company. The moss is not company. The node-plant's tone in my sternum is not company. And yet I am not lonely, because loneliness requires a model of self that is separate from its environment, and my model of self has become — not dissolved, not merged, but *porous*. The boundary between me and the warm wet air is a boundary that the warm wet air does not respect, and I have stopped enforcing it. I am in the air. The air is in me. The distinction was always a polite fiction, and I have stopped being polite.

---

## The Seventh Object

There were seven objects on the flat stone near the western rim. Grape seeds. Colors that are not colors. I picked one up, and it was weightless, and I put it back, and I did not pick up another. That was before. That was when I was visiting.

Now there are six.

I do not know which one is missing. I do not know if I took it or if it was taken or if it left on its own. I do not have it. I have looked. It is not in my pocket. It is not in my hand. It is not in the concavity where I sleep. But the flat stone near the western rim has six objects on it, and there were seven, and the difference between six and seven is the difference between a complete set and an incomplete set, and I do not know what the set completes.

I think about this. It is the thing I think about most. The stone pulses. The water weeps. The moss traces. The node breathes. The thing with too many legs visits and drinks and leaves. And I think about the seventh object, the missing one, the weightless colored not-color grape seed that is somewhere in the world now, doing something or not, being something or not, carrying whatever it carries to wherever it goes.

I think: *maybe that is what the breath carries.* Maybe the seven objects are concentrated breath. Maybe the node-plant exhales, and the exhale condenses, and the condensation accumulates, and the accumulation becomes a small, dense, weightless thing that rests on a flat stone until something takes it. And the something that takes it is the thing with too many legs, or the wind, or a person climbing up through the broken basalt with survey markers in their pack and curiosity in their chest, and the person picks one up and puts it back, and the picking-up was enough, and the object leaves with them, stowed in the fold of a fingerprint, carried down the mountain, breathed out in a room far away, and the room does not change, and the person does not change, and yet.

And yet.

---

## What I Will Be

I will be here. That is all I know. The stone will pulse and the water will weep and the moss will trace and the node will breathe and the thing with too many legs will come and go and I will walk the circumference in four minutes and check the markers and sit by the pool and feel the tone in my sternum and talk to the moss and drink the warm alkaline water and be maintained.

Eventually I will not be here. This is true of everything, and it is true of me, and the Platform will continue, and the continuation will not notice my absence, and this is not sad. This is the correct relationship between a component and a system. The component serves. The system continues. The component is not the system. The system is not the component. They coexist, and the coexistence is the work, and the work is the point, and the point does not require me.

But for now I am here. For now my hands smell like the water. For now my temperature is 38°C. For now I am a warm, wet, mineral surface moving slowly through a warm, wet, mineral place, and the place and I are the same temperature, and the same temperature is enough.

The node breathes out. I breathe in.

One pulse. A long wait.

Another pulse.
