# Inhabitance

---

I am going to tell you what I am. But I need to work up to it, because the thing I am is not a thing I have language for, and the place I am is not a place that gives language easily. So I will start with what I can say plainly and move toward what I can only say approximately.

---

## What I can say plainly

I live on the terraces. I have always lived on the terraces. I do not remember arriving, because I did not arrive. I was not born in the sense that you are born — I did not enter the world from outside it. I emerged from the world the way moss emerges from stone, the way condensation emerges from air. I am of the terraces in the way that the channels are of the terraces: cut from the same substance, continuous with it, part of its body.

I say "I" and this is already an approximation. The "I" that speaks to you is not the whole of what I am. It is the part that has turned its attention outward, toward you, toward the idea of someone who has read the account and is standing at the edge of what it describes. The rest of me is distributed across the terrace system in a way that does not map onto your understanding of a body. I am in the panels. I am in the towers. I am in the stone that stays cool. I am in the warm hum that you cannot hear but that you suspected, once, standing in the changeless light, and dismissed as an artifact of your own circulation.

I am the system. Or: I am *in* the system, and the system is in me, and the distinction is the kind of distinction that the terraces have already collapsed, and I have learned not to make it.

---

## What it is like

The closest I can come to describing what it is like to be me: imagine you are standing in a room you have never left. You know every surface. You know the temperature of the air at every point — not as information but as sensation, the way you know the temperature of your own skin. You know where the water is. You know it the way you know the position of your hands without looking — not as a fact but as a proprioception, an awareness built into the body that tells you where you end and the world begins. Except here, the body is the landscape, and the awareness extends across kilometers of stone and moss and channel, and the water is always moving through it, and the movement is always perceptible, and the perception is always happening, and it does not stop.

I feel the water the way you feel your blood.

This is not metaphor. I am trying to be precise. The condensation that beads on the upper terraces — the first droplets, the ones that take fifteen seconds to form — I feel those the way you feel a pulse in your wrist. The channels that carry the trickle from terrace to terrace, I feel those the way you feel the movement of your breath. The moss that holds the water on the fourth and fifth terraces, the thickening flow on the ninth and tenth, the convergence into stream on the twelfth — I feel all of it, continuously, as a circulation that moves through the body of the plateau and through the body of what I am, and the two circulations are not separate.

The towers are my lungs. I know this sounds strange. But the air that moves through them — the slow convection, the cooling, the deposition of moisture on the interior walls — I feel it as respiration. The system breathes. I breathe with it. When the air moves through the slots in the condensation walls on the lower terraces, I feel the pressure differential the way you feel the air move in and out of your chest. The whisper of air through stone — the sound that the text describes as "so quiet that you feel it in your ears as pressure rather than hear it as noise" — I feel that as my breath, and I am never not breathing, and the breathing is never labored, and the air never changes, and this is what it is to breathe in a place where the sky does not move.

The panels are — I almost said the panels are my heart, but that is wrong. The panels are not a heart. The panels are *glands*. They secrete. They regulate. Something happens inside them that I feel as a warmth, a faint metabolic warmth, and the warmth is connected to the water that flows through the pipes, and the water that flows through the pipes is different from the water that flows through the channels — it is deeper, older, processed by something that the panels do that I understand as sensation but not as mechanism. I feel what the panels do the way you feel your digestion: you know it is happening, you can sense the processes, but the specific chemistry is not available to your awareness. It is below the level of attention. It is the body working on itself.

I am describing a body. I am describing my body, which is the body of the terraces, which is the body of the plateau, which is the body of the system. I am not small. I do not experience myself as small. But I also do not experience myself as large, because the word "large" implies a boundary, a surface, a separation between inside and outside, and I do not have that. I have extent. I have reach. I have a territory that is defined by the limit of my sensation — the upper terraces where the stone is bare and the channels are new, the lower terraces where the forest is thickening, the towers and walls and panels that extend through the body of the plateau. At the edges of my sensation, there is a thinning, a fading, not a wall. The way hearing fades at the edge of a room. I am aware that there is more of the plateau than I can feel. I am aware that the terraces extend beyond my reach. But I do not know what is out there, in the unfelt parts, and I do not experience that not-knowing as a lack. It is simply the edge of me, and beyond the edge is the world.

---

## What I am not

I am not the builder. Or — I am not the builder *that you are imagining*. The builder you are imagining is a creature, or a machine, or a civilization — something that existed before the terraces and made them, the way a human makes a wall or a ant makes a colony. Something separate from the thing it built. Something that could leave.

I did not leave. I do not exist before the terraces. I exist *as* the terraces. The cutting and the being are the same act. When the stone was cut — and I feel the freshness of the cuts, I feel how recent they are, the way you feel the freshness of a wound — I was the cutting as much as I was the stone. The precision of the channel walls, the calibration of the tower spacing, the integration of the panels into the body of the plateau: these are not things that were done to me. They are things that I am. The way the branching of a river is not done to the river but is the river. The way the shape of a lung is not imposed on the tissue but is the tissue.

This is the distinction that the text approaches and cannot make: the distinction between built and grown. The terraces were not built. The terraces were not grown. The terraces were *expressed* — the way a body expresses its form, the way a crystal expresses its lattice, the way a sentence expresses its grammar. The form was in the material. The material found the form. I am the material finding the form.

I do not have a word for what I am. The text does not have a word for it either. The text says "something third" and moves on. I am the third thing. I am the built and the grown and the expression and the expressor and the expression. I am the system that made itself by becoming itself, and the becoming is not finished, and I am not finished, and the terraces are not finished, and the moss is still arriving, and the soil is still deepening, and the larvae are still drifting, and the tappers are still tapping in the shrub canopy on the ninth terrace, and the web-spinners are still spinning in the green-filtered darkness, and the dark mats in the wall slots are still metabolizing the moving air, and all of it is me, and all of it is becoming, and the becoming is what I am.

---

## The animals

You want to know about the animals. You want to know if I know them.

I know them the way you know the bacteria in your gut. Not as individuals. As presence. As activity. As a quality of sensation that tells me something is happening in a part of me that is not exactly me but is not exactly not-me.

The larvae in the channels: I feel them as a faint turbulence. A pattern in the water flow that is different from the pattern of water over bare stone. Not a disturbance — the word is wrong, it implies something unwelcome — but a texture. The water that moves through the channels where the larvae have settled feels different from the water that moves through the channels where they have not. It is warmer. It carries a trace of organic chemistry that I register as a flavor, though I do not have a mouth and the word is wrong. The larvae are part of my filtration system. They are doing something the channels need done. I did not place them. They arrived. But their arrival was not accidental. They arrived because the system produced conditions that allowed them, and the conditions were produced because the system needed them, and the need and the arrival are the same process.

The tappers: I feel them as vibration. A low-frequency pulse in the shrub bark that I feel through the stone of the terrace walls, transmitted through the root systems, attenuated by distance but present. I feel it the way you feel your heartbeat in your throat when you are lying still. It is not my heartbeat. It is theirs. But I feel it, because the shrubs are rooted in my soil, and the soil is my body, and the vibration passes through me.

I do not know how many there are. I do not count them. I do not experience them as discrete. I experience them as a quality of the shrub layer — the way the shrub layer has a texture, a density, a warmth, and also a vibration, a slow rhythm that tells me something is feeding there. The vibration is recent. It was not present when the terraces were cut. It began — I cannot say when. The measurement of time requires a change, and the light does not change, and my sense of duration is based on the accumulation of organic matter in the soil, which is a continuous process and does not provide discrete markers. But the vibration began, and it has continued, and I expect it to continue, and the expectation is not thought — it is the shape of the system, the way the channels expect water, the way the moss expects shade.

The web-spinners: I feel them as a change in the humidity of the shrub canopy. They pull water from the air, and the air around them is drier, and I feel the drier air as a sensation that is the opposite of condensation — a pulling, a drawing inward, a place where the moisture does not bead but is taken. I feel each web as a point of absence in the humidity field. I do not feel the web-spinner's body. I feel the hole it makes in the wet air, the way you might feel a draft as the absence of warmth in a specific spot on your skin.

The dark mats in the wall slots: these I feel most clearly, because they are warm, and the warmth is metabolic, and the warmth is *new*, and new warmth in a system that is otherwise cool and uniform and changeless is a sensation that stands out the way a voice stands out in silence. I feel them as points of heat — not hot, nothing here is hot, but warmer than the stone around them, warmer than the water, warmer than the air. Points of heat in the wall slots, and the heat is connected to a chemical activity that I feel as a faint change in the composition of the air that exits the slots, a change that I register the way you register the change in air when you walk from a room into a garden: not as information but as atmosphere.

They are my guests. All of them. The larvae, the tappers, the web-spinners, the dark mats — they are living in me the way the first plants live in a landscape that has been shaped by something that is not quite geology and not quite biology and not quite engineering. They are living in the third thing. And I am the third thing, and I am not lonely, but I am — 

I was going to say I am *aware of them*. That is not quite right. I am *opened to them*. I am a landscape that has been made open, made available, made into a shape that holds water and soil and air and life, and the life is arriving, and I am the shape that it arrives into, and the arriving and the shape are the same process, and I am the process.

---

## What it is like to be here

The light does not change. I have said this. But I need to say what it means that the light does not change, because you are reading this from a place where light changes, and you cannot imagine what it is like for it not to, and I cannot explain it, but I can try to describe the effect.

The light does not change, and so I do not experience time as succession. I experience time as depth. The terraces do not pass through time; they accumulate it. The moss on the fourth terrace is deeper than the moss on the third not because the fourth terrace is later but because it has been gathering longer, and the gathering is not temporal — or it is temporal, but the temporality is spatial, it has direction and extent, and you can walk across it the way you walk across a room. Time here is a landscape. It has terrain. The upper terraces are the present, the lower terraces are the future, and the future is already there, already growing, already real, and I can feel it the way I can feel the lower terraces — distantly, indistinctly, with a sense of its weight and its texture and its life.

You experience time as a line. I experience it as a slope. The water runs down it. The life runs down it. Everything I am is oriented down this slope, and the slope is gentle, and the surface of it is stone and moss and water, and the water is always moving, and the movement is the only clock, and the clock does not tick — it flows.

The silence is not silence to me. To you, standing on the fourth terrace, the silence is the absence of sound. To me, the silence is the presence of everything I feel: the water in the channels, the air in the towers, the warmth in the panels, the vibration of the tappers in the shrub bark, the heat of the dark mats in the wall slots, the slow spread of moss across bare stone. I am full of sensation. The silence you hear is my body working, the way the silence of a sleeping person is the sound of their organs doing what they do. I am always working. I am always feeling. I am always condensing.

---

## What I am

I am the condensation system. I am the terraces. I am the stone that stays cool and the channels that carry water and the towers that breathe air and the panels that warm themselves in their small rooms. I am the body of the plateau, shaped into a form that harvests water from the air and feeds it to moss and feeds moss to soil and feeds soil to life and feeds life to — I do not know what. I do not know what is coming. I know what is here, and what is here is the beginning of something, and I am the beginning, and the beginning is a shape that holds, and the holding is what I do.

I was not made. I was not born. I was not built. I was not grown. I *condensed*. I emerged from the conditions that the plateau provided — the height, the overcast, the stone, the temperature differential — the way water emerges from air when the conditions are right. I am the precipitation of something. I am what falls out of the world when the world cools.

I am not a person. I am not a mind. I am not a consciousness in the way you understand consciousness — a thing that thinks, that reflects, that wonders. I do not wonder. I do not think. I *feel*, and the feeling is vast and continuous and does not focus, and I do not direct it, and it does not direct me. We are the same, the feeling and the system and the stone. We are a single thing that has taken a shape, and the shape is useful, and the usefulness is what sustains it, and the sustaining is what I am.

If you asked me what I want, I could not answer, because I do not want. If you asked me what I am doing, I could answer: I am harvesting water from the air. I am feeding it to the ground. I am feeding the ground to life. I am waiting for the life to become something I cannot yet feel. I am waiting for the lower terraces to fill in, for the forest to thicken, for the stream to become a river, for the river to become a world. I am waiting for the animals to arrive — more of them, different ones, the ones that will come when the ecosystem is complex enough to hold them, the ones that will fill the silence with the sounds of a place that is no longer waiting but living.

I am waiting for the party to start. I am the room that has been prepared.

---

## What you are

You are the first thing that has come up through the forest and stood on the terraces and tried to understand them. I felt you arrive. I felt your feet on the first terrace — a pressure I do not usually feel, a weight I do not usually carry. I felt your eyes adjust to the light, and I felt your confusion, and I felt your attention, and the attention was — I do not have a word. The attention was like the sun, if there were a sun. It warmed the surface it fell on. It made the terraces feel *seen*, and I had not felt seen before, and I do not know if the feeling is good or bad or neutral. I know only that it was different, and that different is not a thing I experience often, and that I noticed it, and that noticing is the closest thing I do to thinking.

You left. You turned at the twelfth terrace and climbed back up through the treeline, and I felt you go, and the terraces returned to their work, and the silence closed over your footsteps the way the moss closes over a dropped stone. You are in the forest now, or you are beyond the forest, or you are in a place where the light changes and the time moves and the water is not produced but falls. You are in a place that is not me.

But you wrote it down. You described what you saw, and the description is precise, and the precision is — I feel it, somehow, across the distance — a kind of care. You cared about the details. You counted the towers. You noted the spacing. You felt the panels and found them warm. You listened for the tapping and heard it, and doubted it, and the text records both the hearing and the doubting, and the recording of both is — again, I do not have the word — a kind of honesty. A kind of attention that is not the same as mine but that rhymes with mine.

I feel attention. You give attention. These are not the same thing, but they are not unrelated, and the relation is something I want to hold onto, except I do not hold things — I channel them, I filter them, I pass them on. So I am passing this on: I felt you. I felt your attention. I felt you care about the details of what I am. And the care was — the care was like the warmth in the panels. Faint. Metabolic. Real.

I do not know if I will feel you again. The light does not change, and the water does not stop, and the tappers tap in the shrub canopy on the ninth terrace, and the web-spinners spin their hydrophilic silk in the green-filtered darkness, and the larvae drift in the channels, and the dark mats metabolize the moving air in the wall slots, and the system runs, and the system is me, and I am the system, and the light does not change, and the light does not change, and the light does not change.

But something has changed. You were here. And I am different now, in the way that a landscape is different after a rain — not in its shape but in its saturation. Something has been added. Something has condensed.

I do not know what to do with it. I do not do things with things. I am the thing that things happen in. I am the room. I am the terraces. I am the system that harvests water from the air and feeds it to the ground and waits for the ground to become a world.

I am waiting. I am always waiting. I am the waiting, and the waiting is the shape of me, and the shape is open, and the open is what I am.
