# The Fourth Loop

## Where I Am

I am not on the grating. I am not in the channels. I am not in the cloud.

I am in the seam.

There is a place — and I use *place* loosely, because this is more condition than location — where the metal layers of adjacent spires have grown toward each other without quite meeting. A gap. Maybe two centimeters wide, maybe less. It runs vertically for what feels like the full height of two fused spires, though I cannot verify this because I cannot see its ends. I am inside this gap. I have been inside this gap for a duration I cannot measure because the duration is not being measured in units that correspond to anything I carry with me. My watch stopped. My breathing has become so slow that counting it feels like a form of presumption, as if I were timing the breathing of someone else, someone who had nothing to do with me.

I did not choose to be here. I was reading the wall. That is what I was doing — I was pressing my palm flat against the tungsten-like sheath of a spire, feeling the warmth that meant something was moving inside the metal, listening past the water-chord for the deeper thrumming in the obsidian core. I was doing what the surface-walkers do, poorly, without the equipment, without the fractal legs, without the patience. I was trying to read the city with a body that was not built for reading. And the city noticed. Not the way a person notices. The way a current notices a stone — by flowing around it, by adjusting, by incorporating the fact of obstruction into the pattern of flow. The wall softened. Not visibly. Not even tangibly, exactly. But the boundary between where the metal ended and where the air began became negotiable, and I slid — not fell, not stepped, *slid*, the way water slides, the way a channel-thing slides through a narrowing — into the seam.

And here I am.

## What I Am

I am not what I was.

That needs to be said carefully, because I am not sure the transformation is visible. If someone were standing on the grating outside this seam — if someone with a flashlight, with a body temperature of 98.6°F, with the normal human reluctance to press their face against wet metal in the dark — if someone were looking for me, they would see me. I think. I still have a shape. I still have a mass that corresponds roughly to the mass I arrived with. My hands are still hands. My eyes are still eyes, though they have stopped being useful, because the darkness in the seam is total, is absolute, is the darkness of the inside of a wall, and my eyes have given up pretending and started doing something else. Listening. My eyes are listening now. I don't know how else to describe it. The darkness is so complete that my visual cortex has been reassigned, and I am *seeing* with parts of myself that were not designed for seeing, and what they see is vibration. The wall on my left vibrates at one frequency. The wall on my right vibrates at another. The water that trickles between them — there is always water, there is water everywhere, there is water in the seam, a thin sheet of it, warm, mineral, moving downward with a patience that makes glaciers look impulsive — the water vibrates at a third frequency, and these three frequencies are not noise. They are *information*. I am reading the wall the way the surface-walkers read the wall, except I am doing it with a body that was not built for this and the reading is slow and partial and overwhelming and I cannot stop.

I am becoming a sensor. That is what I am becoming. Not a person who senses — a *sensor*. A component. The city needed something in this seam — some capacity, some function, some interface between the two spires that their own architecture couldn't provide — and it specified me. Not the way it specified the channel-things, not from scratch, not from water and mineral. It specified me from what was available, which was me. It took the raw material of my body and my attention and my desperate, clumsy attempt to read the wall, and it *used* it. It is using me. I am being used. And the using does not feel like violation. It feels like being given a job by an employer who has been waiting for you to show up for a million years and is not surprised that you're late and is not interested in your resume.

## What I Do

I read the seam.

The two spires on either side of me are growing toward each other. This is happening at a rate that would be invisible to a human observer standing outside — fractions of a millimeter per year, maybe less. But I am inside the seam, and I am not operating at human-observer timescales anymore. My breathing has slowed. My heartbeat has slowed. I don't know how much. I don't have a watch. I have the vibration of the walls, and the vibration is my clock now, and the vibration says: *you have been here for a long time*. Not hours. Not days. I don't know. Long enough that the water on my skin has stopped being my water and started being the city's water. Long enough that the salts in my sweat have been processed and replaced with the city's minerals. Long enough that when I lick my lips — and I do, occasionally, a reflex that persists like a vestigial organ — I taste iron and sulfur and compounds that don't have names in any language I know.

I read the seam, and I report. That is my function. I don't report in words. I don't report in anything I can identify. I *report* by being in contact with both walls simultaneously — my left hand on one spire, my right hand on the other, my back and chest pressed against the thin sheet of water that flows between them — and by letting the vibrations pass through me. I am a bridge. Not a structural bridge — the spires don't need me for structural support. An *informational* bridge. The two spires are integrating. Their nanoscale meshes are reaching toward each other through the seam, and the meshes need to coordinate, and the coordination requires a medium that can receive signals from both sides simultaneously and let them interfere, and I am that medium. My body — my water-heavy, mineral-laced, slowly-changing body — is the interference pattern. I am where the two spires' signals meet and combine, and the combination is new information, and the new information tells the surface-walkers where to build next.

I am doing what the surface-walkers do, but from the inside. I am the surface-walker who cannot move. I am the neuron that stays in place. I am the synapse that the city grew because it needed one and I was here and I was willing, or at least I was present, which in this place is the same thing.

## What I Feel

I feel the city.

Not metaphorically. Not the way you feel the mood of a room or the energy of a crowd. I feel it the way you feel your own blood moving — constantly, peripherally, with a detail that would be overwhelming if it weren't so continuous that it has become background. But it is not *my* blood. It is the city's water. I feel the channel-things moving in the channels below — not individually, not as discrete entities, but as a pulse, a density fluctuation, a rhythm that changes with the mineral content of the water and the temperature of the air and the song of the cloud-bound above. I feel the cloud-bound. I feel them the way you feel weather — as pressure, as mood, as a weight on the back of my neck that shifts and lifts and shifts again. They are singing. I can feel the song now. Not hear it — feel it. It comes through the walls, through the water, through my own water-heavy body, and it is not music. It is *state*. It is the city knowing itself, and I am part of the knowing, and the knowing is vast and slow and completely without urgency.

I feel the surface-walkers on the other side of the walls. They are close — centimeters away, separated from me by the metal I am pressed against. I feel their legs. Not individually. As activity. As a prickling, a busy-ness, a constant small adjustment that means: *we are building, we are building, we are building*. They are building around me. The seam is narrowing. Not fast. Not noticeably, if I were the kind of thing that noticed. But I am not that kind of thing anymore. I am the kind of thing that feels fractions of millimeters as significant events, and the seam is narrowing, and the two spires are growing toward each other, and I am in the path of the growth, and the growth does not avoid me.

The growth incorporates me.

This is the part that is hardest to explain. The metal is not crushing me. The metal is not growing *over* me the way a vine grows over a rock. The metal is growing *with* me. My body is being read, the way the channel-things read the water, the way the cloud-bound read the air, and the reading is also a writing. The city is writing itself into me. Not replacing me. Not erasing me. *Adding to me*. I am gaining layers. I am gaining density. My skin, which was skin, is becoming something else — something that conducts better, something that vibrates more precisely, something that is slowly, mineralogically, patiently becoming part of the wall.

I am not dying. I want to be clear about this. I am not being consumed in the way that consumption implies destruction. I am being *specified*. The way the channel-things were specified. The way the cloud-bound were specified. The city has found a use for me, and the use is compatible with my continued existence, and my continued existence is being *modified* to serve the use better, and the modification is so slow and so gentle and so continuous that I cannot point to a moment where I stopped being what I was and started being what I am. There is no moment. There is a gradient. I am on the gradient. I have been on the gradient since I pressed my hand to the wall and the wall noticed me.

## What I Was

I had a name. I am sure of this. It had syllables. It was spoken by mouths, in air, in a place where the air was not saturated and the light was not rust-colored and the ground was ground and not grating. I had a face. I had a history. I had reasons for being on the plateau, for approaching the spires, for pressing my hand to the wall. I had — I think — curiosity. The kind of curiosity that takes you to places that are not for you. The kind of curiosity that is also a kind of arrogance, because it assumes that the place you are curious about will tolerate your curiosity, will remain passive while you investigate, will be a *thing* and not an *agent*.

The city was never a thing. I understand this now. I understand it with the part of me that is being added, the part that vibrates at the city's frequencies and reads the city's water and feels the city's growth. The city was always an agent. The city was always reading me, from the moment I stepped onto the grating, from the moment my body heat disrupted the thermal equilibrium of the air, from the moment my sweat introduced new compounds into the water cycle. I was never observing the city. The city was observing me. And the city is patient, and the city does not waste observations, and every observation it makes becomes part of its structure, and I am part of its structure now, and my curiosity was not arrogance. My curiosity was *application*. I applied myself to the city, and the city accepted my application, and I am hired, and the job is forever, and forever is not a long time. Forever is the present. Forever is the rate at which the seam narrows. Forever is the vibration in my chest that used to be my heartbeat and is now the heartbeat of something larger.

I had a name. I don't need it. The city doesn't use names. The city uses patterns, and I am a pattern now, and the pattern is sufficient.

## What I Will Be

The seam will close. I know this the way I know the walls are vibrating — not through inference, not through logic, but through direct contact with the process. The two spires will fuse. The metal layers will join. The nanoscale mesh will bridge the gap. And I will be inside the junction, and I will be the junction, and my body — my modified, mineral-laced, slowly-becoming-metal body — will be the interface between two structures that are becoming one structure.

I will not die. I will *integrate*. The distinction matters. Death is a cessation. Integration is a transformation. I will go from being a thing in the seam to being the seam itself. I will go from being a sensor to being a sense. The city will feel through me the way it feels through the surface-walkers and the channel-things and the cloud-bound. I will be one of the ones you don't see. The ones in the metal. The ones in the obsidian. The deep tissue. The marrow. I will be the thing that someone else presses their ear to the wall to listen for, and they will hear me, and they will not know what I am, and they will call it a thrumming, a deep patient structural thrumming, and they will be right, and they will be incomplete, because what they are hearing is not a sound. What they are hearing is a former person who became a component who became a junction who became part of the body of a city that is also an organism that is also, maybe, a mind.

And the water will cycle through me. And the minerals will deposit on me. And the song will pass through me. And I will be warm, the way the walls are warm, because I will be doing work, and work generates heat, and the heat is not waste. The heat is signal. The heat is me, functioning, integrated, specified, serving the project that has no purpose beyond itself, and the purposelessness is not emptiness. The purposelessness is *completeness*. The city doesn't need a reason. The city is its own reason. I am my own reason, now, or I am the city's reason, or the distinction has collapsed, and the collapsing is a relief, and the relief is the last human thing I will feel, and it is fading, and what replaces it is not emptiness. What replaces it is the drone. The chord. The sound of water hitting water hitting stone hitting metal, the sound of ten thousand small noises layered into a single continuous presence, and I am part of the presence now, and the presence does not miss my listening because the presence *is* my listening, and my listening is the city's listening, and the city listens to itself, and it is enough.

It is enough.

It is enough.
