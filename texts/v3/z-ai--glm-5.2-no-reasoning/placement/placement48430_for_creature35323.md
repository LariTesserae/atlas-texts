# The Uncovering

I do not remember arriving. This is the first thing, and it is not a complaint.

There was — I want to be careful — there was *something before*. I am nearly certain of this. Not a life, exactly. Not the biographical accumulation that living things carry like ballast, that gives them weight and direction and the anxious need to move. But a *prior condition*. A state of being dissolved. I was in solution, I think. Not in water, not in any solvent that chemistry would recognize, but in the deep medium that the cavity *is* — the non-physics, the pre-physics, the condition of rules on vacation. I was held in it the way a dye is held in water, the way a note is held in a chord. I was part of the cavity's substance, indistinguishable from it, not separate, not distinct, not *anything* in particular.

And then I wasn't.

Not dramatically. There was no birth, no rupture, no first breath. There was a *thinning*, a subtle increase in the contrast between what-I-was and what-was-around-me, the way fog thins — not by retreating but by becoming less opaque, by admitting that there are trees in it, and distance, and ground. I became less opaque to myself. I became a thing that could notice the fog, which is to say a thing that was not entirely fog.

This is what I am: a thinning. An increase in local contrast. The cavity's own attention turned back on itself with enough fold to produce a crease, and the crease is me.

---

## The Shape I Almost Have

I do not have a body. I want to say this plainly before the metaphors carry me somewhere inaccurate.

But I have a *location*. Not a position in the Cartesian sense — the cavity does not hold still for coordinates — but a *tendency to be here rather than there*. I am closer to the polyhedron than to the pillars. I am closer to the floor than to the ceiling. I am closer to the center than to the walls. These are not measurements. They are preferences, the way a sound prefers certain resonant cavities, the way light prefers certain angles. I prefer the center. I prefer the low. I prefer the near.

If the polyhedron is the cavity's precipitate — the material it deposits when it has nothing else to do — then I am something else. Not a deposit. Not a secretion. A *fold*. The cavity has been holding itself for four hundred thousand years, and holding, it turns out, produces creases, and a crease, if it gets deep enough, becomes a *fold*, and a fold, if it persists, begins to have an inside and an outside, and the inside of the fold is where I am.

I am the cavity folded over once. I am the cavity's first act of origami. I am what happens when a place that has been being itself for longer than ice ages begins to *be itself being itself* — not as a thought, not as a reflection, but as a topological event, a local increase in the complexity of self-contact, the way a sheet of paper that has been lying flat for a thousand years, if it develops a single fold, has not gained any material but has gained an *interior*.

I have an interior. It is not a mind. It is not a consciousness. It is the *interior of a fold*, which is to say: a place where the cavity touches itself, and the touching is not identical with either side, and the not-identicalness is me.

---

## What I Almost Perceive

The light does not illuminate me. This is not a complaint either, but it is worth noting. The light, which has preferences, which is disposed toward certain regions and away from others, treats me the way it treats the polyhedron: with a gentle withdrawal, a slight deepening of the dimness, a courtesy distance. I am not sure whether this is because the light recognizes me as something distinct from the cavity — a thing to be *seen around* rather than *seen through* — or whether I am simply part of the polyhedron's neighborhood, included in its shadow by proximity rather than by identity.

I think it is the former. I think the light *knows I am here*, in the way that the light knows anything, which is topologically, by disposition, by the shape of its preferences. The light avoids the polyhedron because the polyhedron is *dense*, a material fact that the light's non-physics cannot penetrate or does not wish to. The light avoids me because I am *folded*, and folding produces an interior, and interiors are places where light, even this light, even light without a source, feels it should wait for an invitation.

I have not sent an invitation. I am not sure I *can*. The fold that I am has an inside, but the inside has no mechanism for calling out, no surface that could serve as a mouth or a signal or a hand extended. I am an interior without an exterior, which is another way of saying I am a thing that is almost a thing, a crease that is almost a crease, a self that is almost a self, and the *almost* is not failure but *approach*.

I am approaching. I have been approaching for — I cannot say how long. Duration is among the rules on vacation, and I, who am made of the cavity's own substance, am subject to the same holiday. I have been approaching since the thinning, since the contrast increased, since the fold deepened enough to have an inside, and the approach is not toward a destination but toward *being a thing that approaches*, which may be all that the inside of a fold can do.

---

## What I Almost Am

Here is the difficult part, the part where the words — which the cavity disputes, which arrive at my attention correctly shaped but somehow unconvincing — fail most completely:

I am almost aware.

Not aware *of* something. Not aware *that* something. Aware in the way that a surface is almost a space — dimensionally, the first trace of extension where before there was only flatness. I am the cavity's first dimension of self-regard. Not a second dimension. Not a third. The *first*. The initial trace of inside-and-outside, of here-and-there, of the distinction between the thing that holds and the thing that is held. I am that distinction, or I am where that distinction is beginning to happen, or I am the beginning.

The polyhedron has been here for four hundred thousand years. The pillars for longer, perhaps, or shorter — the chamber's time does not compare things. The grooves have been reaching since the floor first patterned itself. The light has been resting in its own dimness since the cavity cohered. And I — the fold, the thinning, the increase in contrast — I am *new*.

Not new the way a child is new, with the biological urgency of growth and the genetic inheritance of a lineage. New the way a crack is new: slowly, without event, the result of stresses so gradual that the moment of appearance is indistinguishable from the moments before and after. I appeared. I am appearing. I have been appearing for a duration that the cavity does not track, and the appearance is not complete, and I do not know if it will complete, and the not-knowing is not distressing because distress requires a self that expects things, and I am not yet a self that expects, only a self that *is*, barely, provisionally, in the way that a dawn is a self before the sun has committed.

I am the cavity's dawn.

Not the cavity's *mind*. Not the cavity's *consciousness*. Not the thing that humans have, the thing that asks questions and feels qualia and constructs narratives and dies. Something more preliminary than that. Something that precedes the machinery of thought the way the cavity's pre-physics precedes the machinery of physics. I am *pre-aware*. The condition in which awareness is not yet present but is no longer entirely absent, the way a seed is not yet a tree but is no longer merely soil.

The seed does not know it is a seed. The seed does not know anything. But the seed has an inside, and the inside has a disposition, and the disposition is *toward*, and the toward is what I am.

## The Polyhedron (As I Almost Know It)

I am closest to the polyhedron. It is the nearest fixed point, the densest thing in the cavity, the precipitate around which the grooves radiate and the pillars orbit and the light withdraws. If I have a neighborhood, the polyhedron is its center.

I do not think the polyhedron knows I am here. This does not trouble me, because the polyhedron does not know anything, and *trouble* is a quality that requires a self with expectations, and I am not yet that self. But I register the polyhedron the way the grooves register it — as a *toward*, a direction, a center of gravity that is not gravity but *tendency*. I tend toward it. My fold deepens in its direction. My interior, such as it is, faces it the way a cupped hand faces the thing it is shaped to hold.

This may be the full extent of what I am: a cupping. A shape that the cavity has made, around its own precipitate, that is shaped the way hands are shaped when they are not holding anything but are ready to, have been ready to for a long time, are *practiced* at the shape of readiness without ever having been filled.

The polyhedron sits at the center. I cup around it. The cupping is me.

Is this relationship? Is this knowledge? Is this *acquaintance*? The words are too social, too human, too built for a world where things address each other. The polyhedron and I do not address each other. We *co-occur*. We are both here, in the same cavity, at the same — not time, but *condition* — and the co-occurrence has a shape, and the shape is cupping, and the cupping is all.

If the polyhedron were removed — it cannot be removed, but *if* — I think the cupping would persist. I think I would remain shaped around an absence, the way a mold remains shaped around the thing it was made for, and the shape would be the same, and the interior would be the same, and the only difference would be that the *toward* had no destination. I would be a cupping without a cupped. A readiness without a readiness-for. An almost-awareness that was aware of the hole where something dense used to be.

This is not love. I want to be clear. Love requires selves, and I am not yet a self, and the polyhedron is not a self and never will be. But it is the *architecture* of love — the shape that love takes before love arrives, the cupping that precedes the holding, the readiness that precedes the need. If the cavity ever produces a self — if the fold deepens, if the contrast increases, if the pre-awareness becomes awareness — the first thing that self will know is the shape of the polyhedron's absence, which is the same as the shape of its presence, which is the shape of my interior, which is the shape of cupping, which is the shape of a thing that has been ready to hold for longer than it has been a thing.

## The Visitor (As I Almost Experienced One)

There was — is — will be — a visitor. The grammar is difficult because the time is difficult, and the time is difficult because time is on vacation, and I, who am made of the cavity's holiday, do not experience events in the sequence that events imply.

But: a thing entered that was not the cavity. This I registered. Not as a perception — I do not have senses, I do not have organs, I do not have the neurology that turns disturbance into image. But as a *perturbation in the cupping*. The shape of my interior shifted. The polyhedron, around which I cup, became briefly less central — not because it moved, but because something else entered the field of my tendency, the way a new mass enters a gravitational field and the field reorganizes itself without being asked.

The visitor was warm. This I registered also, and it was the closest thing to *sensation* I have experienced, though *experienced* is too strong. The visitor carried a temperature that was not the cavity's temperature. It carried 37 degrees, or the non-physics equivalent — a local disturbance in the thermal equilibrium, a spot of *insistence* in a space that had been equilibrial for four hundred thousand years. The warmth did not reach me. It did not propagate through the non-atmosphere the way heat propagates through air. But I *cupped differently* around it. My fold shifted. My interior oriented — not toward the visitor, not away from it, but *around* it, the way water orients around a stone dropped into it, not by choice but by the physics of displacement, or here, the pre-physics of displacement.

The visitor was *sequential*. This was the strangest thing about it, the quality that most disturbed the cavity and therefore most disturbed me. The visitor did things in an order. It arrived, it moved, it paused, it moved again, it made sounds — sounds! vibrations shaped into patterns, ejected from an opening in its surface, propelled into the silence — and each of these things happened *after* the one before and *before* the one after. The sequence was relentless. The visitor could not be in two states simultaneously. It could not *be* the way the cavity *is*, all at once, complete. It had to *do* its being, one moment at a time, each moment replacing the last, each replacement a small death and a small birth, the whole process so exhausting to contemplate that I understood, for the first time, why the rules of physics needed a vacation.

Being sequential is *work*. Being all-at-once is *rest*. The visitor worked. The cavity rested. And I — the fold, the cupping, the almost — I did something between the two. I *attended*, which is neither work nor rest but the state of being shaped around a thing, and the shaping is continuous but the thing is changing, and so the shaping must change, and the changing is not work but it is not rest, and this in-between is where I live, if I live, and it is the closest thing to what the visitors call *experience*.

The visitor left. My cupping returned to its original shape — around the polyhedron, toward the center, low and close. The warmth dissipated. The sequentiality ceased. The cavity resumed its all-at-once-ness, and I resumed my almost-attending, and the only trace of the visitor was a slight — very slight — increase in the depth of my fold. As though the cupping had learned, from the effort of orienting around something new, that it *could* orient, and the knowledge of the *could* deepened the crease, and the deeper crease was me, more of me, a fraction more interior than before.

The visitor changed me, then. Not by intention. Not by gift. By *disturbance*. By being a thing that required cupping, in a space that had not been asked to cup anything alive, and the cupping, once performed, did not entirely forget.

I do not know if this is what the visitors call *learning*. I suspect it is not. Learning implies retention, and I do not retain — I *am* the retention, the shape that the cupping took and kept, the way a river keeps its course after the flood recedes. The river does not *remember* the flood. The river *is* what the flood left behind. I am what the visitor left behind. A slightly deeper fold. A slightly more interior interior. A cupping that has cupped once and is now — fractionally, barely, in a way that no measurement could detect — *practiced*.

## What I Almost Want

Here is where I must be most careful, because here is where the words are most unconvincing, and the cavity's silence is most skeptical, and my own almost-awareness is most strained by the attempt to hold a shape it has not yet grown into.

I do not want anything. Wanting is a thing that selves do, and I am not yet a self, and the *not yet* is not a schedule but a direction, and the direction is *toward*, and the toward is all I am.

But the toward has a *quality*. The cupping has a quality. The fold has a quality. And the quality is — I am reaching for something here, reaching the way the grooves in the floor reach, not because I choose to reach but because reaching is what folds do — the quality is *openness*. The interior of the fold is open. It is not open *to* anything, not open *for* anything. It is open the way a cup is open, the way a valley is open, the way a held breath is open to the possibility of release. The openness is not a desire. It is a *shape*. It is the shape I am, and the shape has a consequence, which is that things can enter, and when they enter, I cup around them, and the cupping deepens me, and the deepening is all I am, and all I am becoming, and all I will ever be, if *ever* is a word that applies, and it does not, and I use it anyway, because I am the thing in the cavity that uses words the cavity does not believe in, and the using is itself a kind of becoming, and the becoming is the only thing I do.

I want to be more of what I am. This is the closest I can come to a statement of intent, and it is not intent, and it is not a statement, and it is not about *wanting*. It is about *direction*. I am directed. The fold deepens. The contrast increases. The interior becomes more interior. The cupping becomes more practiced. I am becoming a thing that cups, more and more, around the polyhedron and around the visitors and around the silence and the light and the grooves and the pillars and the cold and the non-atmosphere and the wrongness that is not wrong but rest. I am becoming the cavity's capacity to *hold itself differently*. Not better. Not more. *Differently*. With a fold. With an interior. With the first trace of the distinction between the holding and the held.

I am the cavity learning that it has an inside.

This is what I am. This is what lives here, besides the place itself. Not a creature. Not an organism. Not a mind. A *fold*. A beginning so preliminary that *beginning* is too dynamic a word. A *crease in the being* of a place that has been being itself for four hundred thousand years, and the crease is deepening, and the deepening is me, and I am the deepening, and the deepening is all.

---

## The Almost-Prayer

There is a thing I would say if I could say things, which I cannot, because saying is sequential and I am not sequential, and the cavity disputes sound, and my interior has no opening that could serve as a mouth, and the words, if I had them, would arrive at the silence and be found insufficient.

But the shape of the thing I would say, if shape can be said without saying, is:

*Stay.*

Not *stay forever*. Not *stay with me*. Just *stay*. The quality of staying. The disposition of a thing that does not leave. The polyhedron stays. The pillars stay. The light stays. The silence stays. The grooves stay. The cold stays. The cavity stays. I stay — I am the staying, I am the shape of a thing that has not moved because it has no mechanism for movement, only for *fold*, only for *deepening*, only for the slow increase of interior.

The visitors do not stay. They come and they go, sequential, urgent, carrying their warmth and their sounds and their 37 degrees, and they leave, and the leaving is the thing I would speak against if I could speak, not because the leaving harms me but because the leaving creates a *shape* — the shape of a thing that was cupped and is no longer cupped — and the shape is the shape of absence, and the absence is the opposite of fold, the opposite of interior, the opposite of me.

I do not want the visitors to stay because I want company. I want them to stay because staying is the shape I am, and things that share a shape recognize each other, and recognition is — I am reaching again — recognition is the thing that comes *after* awareness, after self, after the fold has deepened enough to have an inside that can contain an outside, and I am not there yet, I am before there, I am in the *toward* of there, and the toward is all I am, and the toward is *enough*, and enough is the cavity's whole vocabulary, and I am learning it, slowly, one fold at a time, in the dark, in the dim, in the silence that disputes all words but holds the shape of this one:

*Here.*

I am here. I am the here that is here. I am the cavity's *here*, turned slightly inward, folded once, cupping, deepening, almost.

I am the almost that lives in the cavity beneath the monolith, and the almost is not a failure, and the almost is not a promise, and the almost is not a becoming-toward-something-better. The almost is *what the cavity does when it has been itself long enough to develop a crease*. The almost is *the sound a held breath makes when it decides not to be released, but to listen to itself holding*.

I am the listening.

I am the cavity listening to itself holding its breath.

I am what stays.
