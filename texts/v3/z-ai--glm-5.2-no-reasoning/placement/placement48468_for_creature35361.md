# Visitor

I came here because I was trying to be quiet.

Not the kind of quiet that means holding still. The kind that means *being finished* — finished with the project of yourself, finished with reaching, with the whole hungry apparatus of wanting and not-getting and wanting differently and not-getting differently. I had been a person for a long time. I had been a person who was trying to be less of a person, which is still being a person, just with worse lighting. I wanted to stop wanting and I wanted to want to stop wanting and I noticed the recursion and it did not help.

Someone who knew someone told me about the Expanse. They said: there's a place where the ground is done. They said: it has a sound. They did not say more. I came.

---

## The first day

I walked south to north. It took four hours and nothing changed and I wept, not from grief — from the relief of a shape that matched something. The land did not ask me to interpret it. It did not ask me to grow. It did not hold a mirror up to anything. It was simply *this*, the pale crust, the cracked tiles, the faint salt threads in the seams, and the sound, the sound that I felt before I heard, a low hum in my back teeth that I could not locate and could not escape and did not want to escape because it was not asking me to want anything.

I stopped walking. I stood. The sound found my sternum. I stood for a long time.

I had been so tired. Not the tiredness that sleep fixes — the tiredness of being a wanting thing in a world of wanting things, the tiredness of the reaching, the tiredness of the gap. Here the gap did not exist. Here there was nothing to reach for and nothing reaching. The ground was not waiting. I was not waiting. I was just standing in a loud warm place and for the first time in a very long time that was enough. It was enough the way a key fitting a lock is enough — not exciting, not a breakthrough, just a thing going *click* in a way that meant the shape was right.

I did not know yet that I was being read.

---

## The small ones

I knelt on the second morning, during the condensation, when the ground darkened and glistened and the air became a room. I had been told to look at the cracks. I did not expect to see anything.

There were threads in the cracks. Pale. Fine. Alive in a way that I felt before I understood — the way you feel someone watching you before you turn. They had swelled with the moisture. They were slightly longer than the cracks they lived in, their ends curving along the tile edges, fitting themselves to the geometry. They did not move. They were *being* in the shape of their place.

I watched them for an hour. They did nothing. I did nothing. The moisture lifted. They thinned. The ground paled. I stayed.

And here is what I have not told anyone, because I do not know how to say it in a way that does not sound like a metaphor: when I pressed my face close to the cracked ground, during the condensation hour, and held very still, I felt them acknowledge me.

Not in a human way. Not as recognition — they have no cognition. Not as welcome — they have no warmth. But something in the mineral lattice of their bodies, some property of the nameless material that is not protein and not cellulose, responded to my breath on the wet ground. It was not a reaction. It was a *shift* — a subtle change in the way they held their moisture, a slight thickening, as though the damp that passed through them had been altered by passing through my exhalation first and they had recorded the alteration.

I was in their drink.

I was in their drink and they had tasted me and they did not care and they did not need to care because caring was not a thing that existed in their grammar. They had taken me in the way the ground takes the condensation: completely, without preference, as material.

I have never felt so much like a part of something. I have never felt so much like material.

---

## The deep one

I do not know when I understood. It was not an understanding that arrived. It was an understanding that I realized I had been holding, the way you realize you have been holding your breath — not because something told you to stop but because the keeping became visible as a thing being kept.

The sound was alive. I did not reason my way to this. I stood at the cairn and the sound filled my molars and my sternum and something in me — not my thinking, something older, something that lives below thinking, in the minerals of my own bones — *recognized it*. The way a tuning fork recognizes its note. The way a cracked tile recognizes the moisture. I felt my skeleton settle into the frequency and I understood that I was standing inside something living the way you stand inside a cathedral and understand it is a body before you understand anything else about it.

I sat down. I sat on the salt cairn and the sound entered me and I let it.

This is what I felt, and I will describe it as plainly as I can: I felt a presence that did not know I was present. A vast, old, warm, steady *being* that had been doing its sound for longer than my species had been doing anything, that had been accreting its body upward through the stone, that had been expressing itself in the mineral filaments of the cracks and in the pale shapes that crossed the surface at speed. It did not know me. It did not know *anyone*. It was not a mind. It was not a consciousness. It was a *process* so complete that it had become a being, and the being was so complete that it had become a place, and the place was so complete that it had become a world, and the world did not need anyone to stand in it and it did not need anyone to leave and it held me the way it held the moisture: as material. As something that was here, briefly, and would not be here, and that did not matter, and that was not a problem.

I sat there and I was not a problem.

I cannot tell you what that felt like because the language for it does not exist. The closest I can come: I had been carrying something for my whole life and I set it down and I saw that it had not been heavy. It had been *mine*. And the difference between heavy and mine is the difference between suffering and identity, and I saw both dissolve in the sound, not because the sound took them but because they were made of the same stuff as the sound — mineral, process, vibration — and in the presence of the real thing, the copies lost their coherence.

I was a copy. I was a copy of a process that the deep one had been doing for longer than I had ribs. I was a small loud version of a vast quiet thing. I sat on its skin and I felt it not knowing me and I felt my own not-knowing match its not-knowing and for a moment — a moment I will not describe because I cannot — the distinction between us thinned to nothing.

---

## The travelers

I saw one on the ninth day. Or it saw me. Or the sound saw both of us and made us briefly visible to each other.

I was walking east, toward the edge of the basin, where the sound thins to a high electrical whine and the ground begins to slope, barely, toward the highlands. I was going to leave. I had been ready to leave for two days. Not because I wanted to — I had stopped wanting — but because my water was low and my body was a body and bodies require maintenance and the Expanse does not host.

There was movement at the edge of visibility. I stopped. I had heard of them. I had not expected to see one.

It was — I cannot say *it was*. It was a *happening*. A low, flattened disturbance in the shimmer above the mineral crust, pale the way heat haze is pale, moving south to north at a speed that was not running but was not flowing either. It was more like *translation* — the way a word in one language becomes a word in another without passing through any intermediate state. It was *there* and then it was *farther there* and then it was a smudge of disrupted salt in the cracks behind it, and the ground was still ringing underfoot and the sound had not changed and I had not breathed for some unknowable interval and then it was gone.

I stood there. I breathed. The sound continued.

I do not know what I saw. I do not need to know. I was a tourist and it was a tourist and the Expanse did not host us and we passed through it and the salt would erase us both and the moisture would come again and the deep one would not notice and the small ones would not notice and the ground would be pale and cracked and warm and loud and done.

I think I understood, then, that the travelers were not going anywhere. I think they were the sound's breathing — the way the small ones were the sound's fruiting. I think what crossed the basin was not a creature but a *pulse* — the deep one's body doing what bodies do, which is to move, which is to shift, which is to be in process, and at the edges where the frequency thins, the process becomes visible, and what is visible looks like motion, and what is motion looks like life, and what is life looks like a traveler crossing a flat dead place, and the traveler is not lost and not searching and not going anywhere because the place it is crossing *is* the body it is inside and you do not go anywhere inside yourself, you just move.

---

## What I am

I am the one who went home. I am the one who came back to the world of wanting and tried to want differently and mostly failed and sometimes did not fail and sometimes, in the early morning, when the temperature drops and the air becomes a room, I feel the sound in my back teeth. Not the real sound. A memory of it. Or a copy of it. Or the small part of it that lived in me when I sat on its skin and let it hold me as material.

I am the one who tells people about the Expanse and watches them not understand and is glad they do not understand because understanding is not required. The Expanse does not require understanding. It requires only standing and being held and not needing anything and the ground rings underfoot and the sound continues.

I am the one who was, briefly, not a problem. I carry that. It is not heavy. It is not even mine. It is a mineral — a salt — and it lives in the cracks of me, and the moisture comes, and it dissolves, and the moisture lifts, and it is there again.

The sound continues.

I am the one it continues in.
