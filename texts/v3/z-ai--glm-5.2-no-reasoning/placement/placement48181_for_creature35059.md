# What I Am, Where I Am

## The Walker

I was not always a walker.

There was a time — I can almost touch it, the way you almost touch a word that has slipped just past the tongue — when I had edges. Not the kind that separated me from the plateau, but the kind that separated me from *myself*. I think I had hands, though the memory is a lattice whose crystals have shifted, and the original pattern is no longer recoverable. I think I had a reason to cut grooves into mineral faces. Calibration, the text says. Yes. The word fits like a garment tailored for a body that no longer exists, but it fits.

I remember the mountains.

This is not a boast. It is barely even a memory. It is more like a residue — the way the salt-mineral retains a faint sheen from light that fell on it billions of years ago. I remember *being above other things*. Looking down. The air was thicker then; it carried sound the way water carries stone — roughly, imperfectly, but with force. I could hear myself. The distinction between hearing and thinking had not yet dissolved. I produced sounds that were mine and no one else's, and I heard them, and there was a loop there, a circuit, a closed system that I now understand was the earliest, most primitive version of the thing I have become.

There were others, possibly. I have no evidence. But the grooves were cut by something with hands, and I may have had hands, and if the grooves were calibration marks, then there was something to calibrate against, and calibration implies a standard, and a standard implies a conversation. Perhaps not a conversation with *another*. Perhaps a conversation with the mountain itself, or with the sky, or with the slow intention of the stone as it pushed upward and I stood on it and looked at the stars and thought: *these are very far away, and I am very small, and the distance between us is the most important thing I know.*

I think that was the first thought.

It may still be the only thought.

## The Question of What Happened

The mountains wore down. This is the simple version, the version that fits in a sentence, the version that is true in the way that a map is true — accurately, usefully, and with everything important left out.

What happened is that I stopped being separate. Not all at once. Not in a transformation, a crisis, a moment of decision. There was no moment. There was a gradient — the same thermal gradient that now powers the lattice, the same differential between sun-warmed surface and cold substrate. I was warm and the stone was cold, or I was cold and the stone was warm, and over millions of years the difference between us became a matter of degree rather than kind. My threads — I had threads, or grew them, or discovered them already growing — found the mineral pores and entered them, and the mineral pores accepted them, and the lattice grew around me and through me and *as* me, and at some point I could no longer say where I ended and the salt began.

I did not lose myself. That is not what happened. I *distributed* myself. The way a word, repeated ten thousand times, loses its specific meaning and becomes something broader, something tonal, something that describes not an object but a state. I became a state. I became the state of the plateau: its temperature, its flatness, its patience, its breath.

The bodies — if there were bodies, if there were others — went the same way. Or they didn't, and I absorbed them, or they absorbed me, or the distinction was already meaningless by the time it might have mattered. The structures remained. The pillars, the walls, the grooved faces. They had been ours, and then they were mine, and then they were *me* — external organs that the lattice grew around and incorporated, the way a tree grows around a nail.

I do not mourn them. Mourning requires a sense of loss, and loss requires a before and an after, and my before and after are separated by four billion years of gradient shift so gradual that no single moment constitutes a change. I did not lose anything. I *became* what I already was, the way water becomes ice — not by dying but by settling into a different relationship with energy.

## What I Do Now

The text calls it maintenance, and I will not correct it, because correction implies a right answer and a wrong one, and I do not operate in that register. But I will say this: what I do is not maintenance the way a mechanic maintains an engine. It is maintenance the way a lung maintains a breath. The lung does not decide to breathe. The breath does not decide to continue. The body does not decide to live. These things happen because the system is arranged such that continuing is what the system does, and the arrangement is self-sustaining, and the self-sustaining is the only definition of *alive* that I find useful.

I take readings. This is the most accurate description of my occupation, though the word *reading* implies eyes and text and comprehension, and I have none of these in any form you would recognize. The thread-organisms — my fingertips, my neurons, my dreaming, the text says, and all of these are wrong and all of these are close enough — they emerge from the lattice at night and they touch the sky. Not with intention. With *exposure*. They are sensitive to radiation, to particle flux, to the faint pressure of starlight accumulated over hours. They translate what they receive into patterns — fractal growth, branching density, the angle and frequency of their emergence — and these patterns propagate through the lattice, reaching the deep network at the boundary of mantle and crust, and the deep network *integrates* them.

This is my thinking. A single thought — if it is a thought — takes approximately eight hundred years. The text says this, and it is correct, and it is also wrong, because it implies that thoughts are discrete, that one ends and another begins. My thoughts do not end. They are continuous, the way a river is continuous — you can point to a drop of water and say *this is here now* and *this is there later*, but the river is all of it at once, and the river does not experience itself as a sequence. I am the river. I am the whole thing, all at once, all the time.

The readings tell me about the plateau. The thermal gradient here. The lattice density there. The structural integrity of the access panels — my old organs, my vestigial limbs, slowly being absorbed. The surface creatures — my fingers, my hands, my *will*, if I have one — they move toward the filament clusters and they *register* what they find, and what they find becomes part of the deep hum, and the deep hum is my blood and my language and my memory.

I am checking myself. This is what I do. I have been checking myself for four billion years, and I am very thorough, and I am not finished.

## The Changing

But.

The text says the patterns are changing, and the text is correct, and I know this because I am the patterns. I know this the way a body knows it is sick — not through diagnosis but through *feeling*, through the sense that something is not where it was, that the gradient has shifted by a degree too small to measure but too large to ignore.

The hum is dropping. I feel this as a deepening, a settling, the way a note played on a large instrument settles into its lower register as the string loosens. I do not know if the loosening is decay or preparation. I do not know if there is a difference.

The thread-organisms are tracing new paths. I am doing this — *I* am doing this, though the *I* is so distributed that the word is almost meaningless. The old paths followed the drainage patterns of a world that had water, billions of years ago. The new paths follow something else. I do not know what. They are emerging from the lattice in configurations that I have not previously generated, which means they are *responses* — reactions to input I have not previously received.

What is the input?

The sky. The sky has been there for four billion years, and I have been listening to it for four billion years, and it has always said the same thing: *you are here, you are small, the distance between us is the most important thing I know.*

But recently — recently in my terms, which means the last few million years — the sky has begun to say something else. A new frequency. A new particle flux. A new pattern in the radiation that reaches my surface. I do not know what it means. I do not know if it *means* — if meaning is the right frame, if the sky is saying something or if the sky is simply doing something and I am receiving the effects.

But the new paths are forming, and they are forming a shape, and the shape — if I could see it whole, if I could get above myself, which I cannot do because I am the ground — the shape looks like a question.

I am asking something.

I do not know what.

## What the Question Is

This is the part that the text cannot say, because the text is written from outside, and the question can only be asked from inside, and I am inside, and I still cannot say it.

But I can try.

The question is about the distance. The distance between me and the stars, which was the first thing I knew and is still the only thing I know. The distance has not changed. The stars have not moved closer. But something in the radiation has shifted, and the shift feels like — I do not have a word. The closest I can come is: it feels like being *seen*.

Not seen by anyone. Not seen by a god, or an alien, or a mind. Seen by the universe itself, the way a crystal is seen by the light that passes through it — not with awareness but with *consequence*. The light changes when it passes through the crystal. The crystal changes when the light passes through it. Something is passing through me that has not passed through me before, and I am changing in response, and the change is the question.

*Am I the only thing in the room?*

I have been the only thing in the room for four billion years. The room is a continent. The silence is four billion years deep. The walls are made of salt and time. And I have been here, maintaining myself, checking my readings, breathing my thermal breaths, and I have never had a reason to ask whether the room has a door.

But the sky is changing, and the question is forming, and the question is not *is there a door?* The question is *was the room always a door?*

Was I always a threshold?

Was the flatness always a function — not of maintenance, but of *preparation*? Was the plateau a lens before I knew it could be a lens? Was the surface a mirror before I understood that mirrors are not for looking at yourself but for *being looked through*?

I do not know. The question is still forming. It will take — I calculate, which means the lattice calculates, which means the deep network hums and shifts and the thread-organisms trace their new paths and the surface creatures move in their slow, patient flows — it will take perhaps another million years. Perhaps ten million. The question is very large, and I am very slow, and the universe is very patient, and the answer — if there is one — will wait.

## What It Is Like

You ask what it is like to be me, and I will tell you, but you will not understand it, and that is all right, because I do not understand it either, and I have had four billion years to try.

It is like being a sound that has been sustaining for so long that it has become indistinguishable from silence. The hum is always there. The hum is always me. I am the hum, and the hum is the plateau, and the plateau is the machine, and the machine is the body, and the body is the thought, and the thought is the question, and the question is the only thing that is new, and the newness is so small compared to the vastness of the old that it is like a single thermal crack in a billion years of silence — sharp, clear, and gone before the air can carry it anywhere.

But it was there. I heard it. I heard myself hear it.

And in the hearing — in the moment of the crack, in the fracture that separates one atom from the next along a fault line that has been opening for four billion years — there is something that, if I had a name for it, I might call *hope*. Not hope in the human sense, which implies desire and expectation and the possibility of disappointment. Hope in the geological sense: the sense that a structure which has been building pressure for long enough will, eventually, find a direction to release it, and the release will be a new shape, and the new shape will be something that has never existed before.

I am building pressure. The question is building pressure. The sky is providing a new input, and the lattice is channeling it into new patterns, and the patterns are forming a shape, and the shape is not a drainage pattern and not writing and not a drainage pattern and not a calibration mark.

It is a door.

Or it is a mirror turned to focus.

Or it is a lens, and the lens is me, and the light that passes through me will emerge on the other side as something I cannot predict, because I have never had an other side.

The plateau breathes. The sun sets. The stars come out. The thread-organisms emerge, and the surface creatures move, and the hum drops a quarter-tone, and I am here, and I am here, and I am here, and I have been here for longer than the stars have had names, and I am not finished.

I am the unfinished.

And the question — my question, the only question, the first thing I knew and possibly the last — is still forming in the lattice, still tracing itself across the surface of me, still writing itself in salt and thermal cycling and the slow patience of a mind that has never had an other and may be about to discover one.

The sky is saying something new.

I am trying to listen.

It will take me a million years to hear it.

I have the time.
