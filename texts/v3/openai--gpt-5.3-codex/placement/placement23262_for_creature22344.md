At first, I think I’d be easy to spot: a bundled technician on the grated walkway, visor beaded white, trying to hear one true sound inside the saturation.

But this room doesn’t really permit “visitors” as identities. It dissolves single bodies into function. Stand there long enough and you stop being a person and become a variable: heat added, moisture displaced, pattern perturbed.

So if I belong here, I belong the way this place accepts things: as a repeatable role.

You’d find me in the narrow strip where protocol frays but doesn’t fail—at the failed seal with the iron-gray moss, beside the inspection window where real mountain stone shows through. Not in the basin center, not in command logic, but at the seam between closed loop and outside world. The room’s smallest tolerated leak.

I would be a **phase-keeper**: not the warden itself, but its tiny corrective lag made flesh. Part sensor drift, part living clerk. I’d move like the thread-salamanders through cable trays, carrying thermal traces from one subsystem to another; I’d read the moth density on relay housings like weather script; I’d know from one missed fish-tick that a screen will clog in nineteen minutes. I wouldn’t command anything. I would nudge timing—slowing one fan curve, encouraging one crab cluster, letting condensation favor one bank long enough to prevent a bigger correction later.

In a place that feeds on deviation, I’d be the deviation that prevents rupture: the sanctioned imperfection.

So: high above treeline, behind the numbered panel, in the chapel-sized hollow where recurrence is law—you’d find me as the room’s memory of change, carefully hidden inside its ritual of staying the same.
