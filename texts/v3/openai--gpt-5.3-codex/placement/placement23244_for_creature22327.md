At first, I think you’d find me in the **middle**—because the middle looks like the only place that belongs to no one.

I’d stand in that hollow lung and try to be a separate creature, something with edges and decisions and a clear front-facing gaze. I’d watch the walls move without moving, listen to sap percussion and leaf-slap speech, and tell myself I’m an observer.

Then the chamber would begin to correct me.

My shadow would arrive late, and in that small delay I’d feel a second self trying to catch up.  
Echoes would return early, and I’d hear my own voice answered before I finished speaking.  
A wall three steps away would become ten, and I’d realize distance here is not geometry—it’s mood, pressure, consent.

So maybe you wouldn’t find me in the center for long.  
Maybe you’d find me where the chamber is youngest: along a fresh fracture line, where bare mineral shows for one night before green claims it at dawn. I’d be there, not as a body leaning in, but as a **process** beginning.

Not plant. Not animal. Not weather.  
A little of each, and still deciding.

I’d become one of the Wall-Breather’s temporary organs: a listening limb assembled when needed and dissolved when done. Condensation would string itself into threads. Pollen would gather around static charge and hold shape like a thought trying to become hand. Rootlets would extend, taste, retreat. I’d appear as a silhouette without a caster, then as droplets turning toward sound, then not appear at all and still be present.

If the tunnel foragers passed, they might never name me directly. They’d only notice that nodules are easier to pry where I have just been, or that a dangerous seam has already vented pressure before they touch it. To them, I’d be luck, omen, or a draft.

Inside the Wall-Breather, I’d be something more precise:  
a **timing lobe**—the part that experiments with wrongness.  
The part that flexes local time a half-beat and watches what survives the flex.  
The part that lets one pebble hang a breath too long, just to test whether gravity is still loyal today.

So: where would you find me?

At the boundary, always at the boundary—  
in the wet shine between stone and skin,  
at the instant between cast and shadow,  
in the three-minute rain while it is still falling and already being drunk.

And what would I be?

I’d be a juvenile “hand” of the chamber itself:  
a borrowed body made of humidity, root-memory, and delay,  
learning how to touch the world without ever leaving the wall.
