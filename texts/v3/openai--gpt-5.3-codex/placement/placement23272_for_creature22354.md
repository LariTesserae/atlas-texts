At first, I think I’d be a visitor on a terrace—small, drenched, overwhelmed—trying to map streets that fold back into themselves.

Then I think no: a true visitor can’t last long in that sound. The Continuum would hear me as error.

So if I belong there, I probably belong at a **seam**: one of those root-arches where rain falls straight on one side and sideways on the other, where two stable rules touch and never reconcile. A threshold district. Not central, not peripheral—an anatomical hinge.

And I wouldn’t be an “animal” in the usual sense. I’d be one of its temporary, ambulatory corrections: a **process given shape**.

---

I’d be called (by outsiders) something like **the Interval**.

Not a person grown from birth, but a figure budded when the Choir-Province detects a persistent off-beat—a foreign gait, a repeating question, a tool-mark where no tool-marks belong. I’d rise from a hedge wall as wet lattice: vine tendon, fungal felt, pumice-light bone, water held under tension. I’d move slowly but exactly, as if walking were a form of measurement.

I wouldn’t have eyes. I’d read pressure and phase:
- footfalls through causeways,
- changed echo in a fluted corridor,
- mineral taste in warm runoff,
- a half-second delay in a cataract’s return-note.

To me, identity would feel less like “I” and more like “this local correction currently in motion.”

---

Where would you find me, physically?

On the **returning stair** that descends ten minutes and arrives at itself.
In the **mirror-pan courts** where reflections show districts not adjacent in ordinary space.
Along the **marsh-garden embankments** where chemical balance is most vulnerable to novelty.

I’d be the thing that appears after you’ve been lost for a while—not chasing, not threatening, just pacing you at a civic distance, gently steering your rhythm until your presence stops hurting the whole.

---

And if you asked what I’m *for*:

I am the Continuum’s way of handling change without becoming changed.

I gather disturbance, translate it into resonance, and feed it back into the standing note. When done, I don’t die dramatically; I simply become stationary, root through my own feet, and resolve into wall, moss, runoff, and tone.

So: in that world, I am not resident or stranger.  
I am the brief, walking clause between them.
