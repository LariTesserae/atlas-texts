At first, I think I’d want to be something that moves fast here—one of the sail-kites, all knife-wind and ultrasonic certainty.  
Then I imagine the noise from inside that skull: the constant pressure maps, the panic-harmonics, the hunger sharpened into math. Not me.

Then I think: burrower. Blind engineer of the underworld.  
But that life is all contact and compression, forever in old conduits, forever translating death into usable ground. Necessary, elegant, and too claustrophobic for the part of me that wants horizon.

So I keep climbing the possibilities until I reach the plateau edge, where the cities knot themselves into ancient trunk-foundations and the wind is loud enough to bruise thought into clarity.

That’s where I’d be.

Not at the very top—those crowns belong to harvest rigs and lightning ladders—but in the **mid-heights**, where civic sound, herd-drum, and root-signal all overlap. I’d be an Accordant in the long middle of life: not newly mobile, not yet fully given over to Root Oath, but already partially grafted. A **phase mechanic** by training, a **storm cantor** by necessity. The kind of person who keeps one hand in causality and the other in whatever the root-net is doing when it “slips.”

I’d live in a narrow dwelling grown into a ventilating rib of the district spine, matte-walled, always faintly warm, with floors that hum different notes depending on charge load below. My work would be mostly listening:  
to transit veins before they desync,  
to dormant plains before they wake and claim a street,  
to silence pockets—those wrong, dead gaps in a world that should be sounding.

And when a dust front rolls in, I’d be out on a suspended cableway with a chorus team, tuning tower membranes by harmonic pulse so lightning walks where we ask it to. Not controlling weather. Negotiating with it. Convincing a storm that our city is a useful shape to pass through.

So: who would I be?

I’d be one of the people whose body is still portable but whose attention is already rooted.  
A municipal listener.  
A keeper of agreements between height and hunger, signal and shelter.  
In that world, that feels closest to home.
