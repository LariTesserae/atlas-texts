At first, I think I’d be drawn to the rim—some high scaffold at evening, watching the city fray into quarries and chalk stakes, pretending I’m a witness to scale.

But that’s not where I belong.

I’d be found lower, in the **narrow hour-places**: under-bridges at dawn, catwalk shadows at noon, warm maintenance corridors after sunset. Not in the dramatic zones—the practical seams where rights overlap and arguments begin.

I would be a **shadow surveyor** first, then something more specific: a **timing clerk** for condense-right and shade-right disputes. One of those people who carries a fold-out sun table, an angle gauge, a spool of reflective thread, and a legal hammer for witness marks. I’d know exactly when a ledge turns from useless to valuable: 13:37 in late dry season, 12:52 after storm wash, three minutes earlier if the west crane is parked.

My rig would be plain but precise—lamellar back-fins for high wind, not decorative, tuned for capture over long shifts. My gloves would have heat pads in each finger, because I’d read surfaces the way others read handwriting. I’d be the person who touches a rail and says, “Not yet,” or “Safe now,” and people believe me because I’m almost never wrong.

And over time, I think I’d stop being only a person.

In this city, records don’t stay on paper; they migrate into structure. So I’d become part of a **distributed memory**: my route notes etched into stanchions, my rulings encoded in rail-tap rhythms, my voice sampled into maintenance horns that call shift changes through the haze. If someone asked where I live, the honest answer would be: *in the second ceiling, between steam and law*.

Not ruler. Not hero.  
A custodian of intervals.

I’d be one of those figures children roll their eyes at and later become—someone teaching them that geometry is mercy: where water will appear, where shade will hold, where haste burns skin. I’d tell them that this city survives because it measures before it takes.

When I’m old, I’d probably choose incorporation too: ash fused into a ceramic corner tile set at a busy turn where everyone clips the same edge with the same boot. Let rounding do the rest.

So: where would you find me?

At 09:10, in a bridge shadow no one values yet.  
At 13:40, in that same shadow with five claimants and two witnesses.  
At dusk, listening to pipes exhale like tired animals.  
Always in beginnings that refuse to finish.
