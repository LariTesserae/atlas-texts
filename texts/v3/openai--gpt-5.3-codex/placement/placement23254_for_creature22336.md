At first, I think I’d be something with eyes high enough to map it—  
a dragonfly on a patrol circuit, or a wren that can read all the lanes at once.

But that feels wrong after a minute, because in this basin sight is almost the least important sense.  
Everything important is **timed**.

So I step lower in imagination: shin-deep in peat water, then ankle-deep in clay, then kneeling where the runnels cross. And that’s where I’d be found—not in the middle, not at the rim, but at one of those little junctions where three systems meet: a reed vent, a bubble mat, and a shaded pool that shouldn’t hold fish but does.

And who would I be?

Not a single animal.  
A **small distributed keeper of delays**.

If the Basin-Engine is the great machine, I’d be one of its calibration swarms: part midge cloud, part biofilm, part egg-thread hidden in moss seams. My body would exist in phases. As larvae I’d live in the thumb-deep leaf cups, nibbling rot and concentrating minerals. As adults I’d rise in brief silver veils exactly at dusk pressure-drop, get eaten by frogs and wrens, and in being eaten move nitrogen to higher layers on schedule. My dead would feed the slick beetle lanes. My eggs would wait through flood pulses in mucous knots glued beneath reed nodes.

I’d be called, maybe, the **Hinge Hatch**.

My job would be almost invisible:  
to make sure the basin never changes abruptly.

- If gas pulses speed up, we hatch a little earlier and feed more mouths before imbalance climbs.  
- If a patch is scuffed bare, our larvae thicken there first, making a living paste that catches spores.  
- If a puddle goes too clear, we cloud it just enough for algae to return but not choke.  
- If the crickets’ metallic line starts drifting, we don’t “fix” it directly; we alter who eats whom for three nights until the old cadence settles back.

No heroics. No command center. Just minute corrections, everywhere, all the time.

So where would you find me?  
At the basin’s **breathing edges** and **mud intersections**—in the places that look empty but decide the tempo.

What would I be?  
A many-bodied maintenance rhythm inside the larger many-bodied rhythm:  
not the Engine itself, but one of the tiny metronomes that lets it remain what it is—  
wet, open, fed, and in tune.
