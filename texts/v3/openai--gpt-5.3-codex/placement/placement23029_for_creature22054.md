At first I think I’d be a visitor at the edge—  
on one of those blunt vertebra ridges where the sheetwater is only ankle-deep and the mist skates pass close enough to static your teeth.

That feels right for a moment: a watcher with a faceplate, counting pulses, learning the rhythm of the kiln-click herds and the throat-swell of the fog sifters.  
But this place does not really permit watchers. Anything stationary is eventually used; anything separate is eventually plumbed in.

So move me lower.

Not into the deepest basins—those belong to the tube millions and the collapses—but into a saddle where three regimes overlap:  
cold film-flow from the north pan, a warm iron seep rising through fractured slate, and a narrow causality shear where echoes come early and muscle responses come late.  
A bad crossing point. A rich crossing point.

I would begin there as almost nothing visible: a black gloss on stone, mistaken for mineral varnish.  
Then a seam.  
Then a lace of soft valves opening and shutting in peristaltic sequence, drinking from the thin moment between events.

Not predator, not grazer.  
A **phase scavenger**—an organism that feeds on mismatch itself.

When a hexapod herd enters the shear and one animal stutters half a beat behind its own footing, I take the sloughed chemical panic from the water before it dilutes.  
When a shelf-collapse is heard before it happens, I open pores to the pressure-wave’s precursor ions and store them like charge.  
When time-shed tissue appears in the mud with no immediate corpse, I do not eat the flesh first—I strip the unstable gradients around it, the little storm of wrong-order metabolism, then leave the solids for rake-jaws.

I wouldn’t have a single body. I’d be a braided district of bodies:  
thumb-sized bladders in rock pits, meter-wide mats under sleet pans, tendon-thin cords following microchannels, all sharing pulse timing through chemoelectric flickers.  
At dim-night I would glow very faintly, not orange like the long transmission seams, but violet-gray, like bruised ice under skin.

Other species would use me without knowing me.

Tube colonies would avoid my bitter alkaloid ducts, and that avoidance would keep open a stable capillary lane for warm seepwater.  
Fog sifter runoffs crossing my mats would pick up trace metals I pre-chelated, seeding next-cycle microbial blooms downstream.  
Ambush hunters would learn that my scent marks the edges of lag zones—the safest place to wait if you hunt reaction delay.  
Juvenile anomaly-navigators might imprint on my pulse cadence as one more reliable impossibility in their route maps.

So where would you find me?

At places the plain is trying to disagree with itself.  
At uphill-running rivulets just before they surrender.  
At basins stuck in repeating sleet, right on the boundary where repetition breaks.  
At the lip of sink vents that cough in triplets, then skip a beat when barometric lungs turn over.

And what would I be?

Not a creature in the old sense.  
More like a wet, distributed organ the greater metabolism grew to keep from tearing on its own paradoxes—  
a **timing kidney**, filtering causal waste from the bloodstream of the marsh.

If you knelt beside me, you’d think your shadow moved a second too soon.  
That would be me feeding.  
That would be me, quietly helping cause and effect continue tolerating each other for one more cycle.
