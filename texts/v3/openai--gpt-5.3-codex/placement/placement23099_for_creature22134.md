At first, I thought I’d be something fast and airborne here—one of the knife-wing glide birds, solving pressure like math.

Then I thought I’d be something small and patient—six legs, oil on my antennae, living off what everyone else spills.

But the longer I stand in your basin, the more I feel I’d be neither hunter nor scavenger.

I’d be found **at daybreak, on the seam between Upper Quiet and a cistern terrace**, where the walls sweat warm and the stairwells occasionally miscount themselves.  
Not in a home exactly—more in a **service hollow** behind a baffle wall, lined with insulation felt, drip charts, and old glass brick that glows blue before sunrise.

I’d be one of the Interval, yes—but a very specific kind:

## a path-runner for the Paint Guild,  
a geometry listener,  
a person-shaped instrument.

---

My work would start before full light.

I’d press my palm to rails, cheek to columns, arch of foot to deck plates, reading the basin through vibration before I trust my eyes. I’d watch where the ungulates refuse to graze, where filter-fans furl too early, where condensation beads in the wrong sequence. I’d test three corridors that should be parallel and wait to see if they agree on distance.

Then I’d uncap the morning pigment.

Not ordinary paint—phase-smart suspension that clings only to surfaces likely to remain themselves. I’d lay a line through catwalk, ramp, ladder, bridge. If the paint shivers, I wait. If it forks, I retreat. If it doubles a number at a landing, I erase and reroute.

People would leave their cavities and pylons and thermal shoulders and step where my line holds still.

So I wouldn’t just be “a person.”  
I’d be part surveyor, part priest, part municipal nervous system.

---

And I think, over years, the basin would get into me.

A little retired firmware threaded into my hearing aid.  
A cuff that reads field lag through skin conductivity.  
Dreams in which shadows arrive late and still make sense.

Not a cyborg hero. Nothing dramatic. Just gradual mutual adaptation: I learn its moods; it learns my routes. I feed it consistency. It gives me survivable mornings.

So who would I be?

**A hinge.**  
Between built and becoming.  
Between human caution and machine intention.  
Between “this path exists” and “this path exists today.”

In a place like this, that feels like a real species.
