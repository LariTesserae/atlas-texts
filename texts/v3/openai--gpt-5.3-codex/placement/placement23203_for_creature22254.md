I wouldn’t begin at the center.  
You’d find me on a **mid-level service ledge**, the kind everyone crosses and no one claims—between a lift drum and a spill stair that never stays the same length twice.

At first, I’d look like a person: one of the Bracers, rope-burned palms, wax-bound hair, lime scars, always damp to the bone. I’d belong to no prestigious crew—just the ones assigned to “annoying continuity”: retensioning lashings, clearing bead gutters, listening for new hollows where yesterday there were none.

But if you watched longer, that wouldn’t quite hold.

I’d be what they might call a **Return-Listener**: the one who drops a pebble, taps a tile, hums into a pipe, then waits for the world to answer from the wrong direction. My work would be mapping the pocket world’s mood by resonance—where gravity is beginning to lean, where a cistern is teaching water to climb, where tomorrow’s platform is already implied by tonight’s echoes.

I’d sleep in fragments beside warm kiln walls, wake to mark algae rims with pressure glyphs, and spend dusk tuning drainage flutes so warning tones cut through frog-boom and hammer-noise. Not musician, not mason, not mystic—some inconvenient braid of all three.

And eventually, I think, I’d stop being fully singular.

Because in that place, names migrate into infrastructure. So part of me would remain in a rung I replaced during storm season; part in a flute notch carved to whistle at dangerous backflow; part in a resin seam I kept open on purpose so the Wet Ledger could “speak” through condensation pattern. I’d still be human enough to eat standing and curse wet rope—but also partly indexed into the structure, a recurring adjustment more than a fixed self.

So: where would you find me?  
Not at the top, not at the root—at the **join**, where argument becomes support.

Who would I be?  
A bracer of replies. A listener for tilts.  
One of the people whose real craft is helping an unfinished world decide, again, to hold.
