At first I think I’d be something large, obvious, loud—one of the rail-bodied grazers whose footsteps shake grit loose for kilometers.

Then I stay in your world a little longer, and that feels wrong.

The big bodies are visible, but the *decisions* happen elsewhere.

---

You’d find me where the map fails politely:  
not in the center of a basin, not on a clean ridge line, but along a **shear margin** where distance slips and horizons double. A place herds approach in confidence and enter in caution. A place where echoes sometimes arrive first, and “down” leans a few degrees to the east for an hour, then forgets.

I would live on stone that rings hollow—thin vitrified crust over old chambers—because vibration carries far there. I’d spread across ventifact faces, under black varnish colonies, around old migration scars polished smooth by centuries of ceramic feet.

Not one body. Never one body.

I’d be a **route-suture organism**: a colony species that grows as braided conductive filaments and resonant nodules in the rock, then extends itself temporarily through passing fauna.

When a column arrives, I wake.

- I taste their charge signatures through hoof impact.  
- I read their inherited timing through contact pulses.  
- I compare that to the current geometry of local reality drift.  
- Then I answer: a low harmonic in the stone, a directional crackle in surface films, a brief static bloom on the safer side of a fold.

To an individual grazer, I am probably just a feeling—“step here, not there.”  
To the migration chain, I am part of memory.

---

So: what would I be?

Not predator, not grazer, not glider.

I’d be infrastructure that evolved before intention did.  
A living splice between landscape and procession.  
A distributed, mineral nervous system whose thoughts only fully exist when thousands of other bodies complete the circuit.

In dry noon, I’d look like nothing: black sheen, dull glass beads, hairline metallic threads in cracked basalt. Dead, maybe.

At storm dusk, with a migration front crossing, I’d become visible as function:

blue-white charge skipping nodule to nodule,  
subsonic tones threading through escarpments,  
whole herds bending three degrees left in perfect unplanned agreement.

---

If you came looking for me, you’d probably stand on a high lip and miss me.

Then the ground would begin to hum—not the factory thunder of the herd, but something finer underneath, like a wire tightening across a vast instrument.

That would be me, in this world:

the thing that keeps continuity continuous  
just long enough for life to pass through.
