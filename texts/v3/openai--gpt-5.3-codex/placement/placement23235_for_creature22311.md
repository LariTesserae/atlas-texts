At first, I think I’d be found on one of the bare dark shelves—the places that look empty but are clearly *kept* empty.  
Not in the lush patches. Not in the small trees. In the interval.

I’d arrive as a walker in my own imagination, because that’s the habit of thought: a body entering a place.  
But a place this complete does not really admit walkers. It absorbs them as weather.

So I try again.

You would find me where the sheen is thinnest and most deliberate:  
at the edge of a runoff thread that pauses before committing to descent,  
on iron-black seams that seem to darken before dawn,  
under the moss margin where the stone is cool at noon and warm at dusk.  
If you knelt and watched long enough, I’d be there in the tiny reversals—  
the bead that gathers against slope,  
the film that appears like woven frost and vanishes by midday,  
the halo widening a fraction around a rooted leaf.

Not at the center, then.  
At the joints.

---

Who would I be?

Not the Basin Binder entire—that old intelligence is too broad, too patient, too distributed to fit a single “I.”  
I would be one of its temporary organs: a **night script**.

A short-lived lattice extruded over wet basalt while the light is low.  
Pale, geometric, almost decorative if you don’t know better.  
By touch: brittle.  
By function: meticulous.

I would precipitate and sort.  
I would catch dissolved iron, pass on manganese, stall one ion, release another.  
I would bridge two distant living patches that never meet above ground.  
I would seal a microfracture so fine no eye could find it.  
I would carry a message that is not language but ratio:  
more silica here, less acidity there, hold moisture two handspans longer along this seam.

At dawn I would look like evidence of intention.  
By noon I would be gone.  
Not failed—spent.

And the basin would keep the edit.

---

If you returned in a year, you would not say, “Here is the one who wrote the night film.”  
You would say, “This shelf used to bead differently.”  
Or, “That moss-bank has advanced.”  
Or, “This root now holds where last winter it slipped.”

That would be me, diffused into outcome.

So in this world, I would not be a resident in the animal sense.  
I would be a recurrent act of maintenance:  
briefly visible, structurally consequential, personally untraceable.

You would find me in the high warm silence,  
in the basin that practices craft without hands,  
as one thin geometry laid down between mistfall and morning—  
a small, vanishing worker in a body made of stone, water, and time.
