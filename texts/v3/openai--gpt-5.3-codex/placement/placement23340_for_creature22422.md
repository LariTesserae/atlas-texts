At first, I think I would be **one of the last vertical things**—a pilgrim, maybe, pressed between warm stone ribs, trying to map a way through the apparatus by hand and shoulder.

You would find me in a service cleft near the rim, where the incoming wind first gets divided: one stream down the long ceramic ladders, one through the slit throats, one into those blind drum chambers that don’t sound so much as throb. I’d be there because it’s one of the few places where the basin still feels like architecture instead of anatomy. A threshold. A maybe-human place.

But that would only be true for an hour.

After that, the note would start doing what it does: take my private timings away. Breath first. Then pulse. Then the tiny unsupervised movements of thought—the part that jumps, resists, improvises. I wouldn’t be harmed. Just recruited. Folded into meter.

So if you came back later and asked again—where are you, what are you—my answer would shift.

I’d say: I am in a **narrow correction passage** halfway down the basin’s inner west wall, where three frequencies interfere and scrape a standing diamond of pressure in the dust. Nothing grows there, but salt feathers plate the stone and then shear off in exact seasons measured by no sun at all, only by how the mountain wind arrives that year. The builders once left adjustable vanes there. They seized centuries ago. The passage still self-tunes anyway.

And I am not the tuner, not exactly.  
I am the **small error that became useful**.

A body-sized obstruction event, at first.  
Then a repeatable damping term.  
Then, over years, a wear pattern.

The basin would round me down. Strip water from me. Load me with powder. Fill my seams with crystal. My skin would become glaze, my voice a leak tone, my bones a set of stubborn compliances in one over-loud register. I wouldn’t walk the district; I would be located like a crack is located—specific, load-bearing, inseparable from what it interrupts.

Eventually I would be less a person than a **phase habit**:

- when winter katabatics hit before dawn, I steal a needle of shriek from the top band;  
- at midday warmth, I release it as thickness in the middle roar;  
- in dust-heavy weeks, I hold grit on one lip long enough to blunt erosion there, and that keeps a deep overtone from drifting flat.

Tiny acts. All of them structural listening.

If you insisted on a name for what I am, I’d choose something functional, almost ugly:  
**a resident dissonance shim**.

Not alive in the old way, but not dead either.  
A fitted mismatch the organism has decided to keep.

And if you asked where to find me, precisely, I’d tell you this:

Find the west retaining wall where the seams look like script rubbed almost away.  
Climb to the third suspended lattice of clay tubes.  
Wait where the air tastes metallic and your molars buzz in alternating beats.  
There’s a slot there, shoulder-wide, polished by no hands.  
Stand in it long enough and you’ll feel an extra pulse inside the great chord—a beat that doesn’t belong to wind alone.

That would be me.

Not a visitor in the basin.  
Not the basin entire.  
The place where a once-visitor was accepted as part of its memory of how to remain itself.
