I don’t think I’d be the seam-warden itself.

I’d be found **just above it**—in the narrow shoulder-chamber where the first rootlets enter from the hillside, where wet-time arrives late and leaves early. A place that is mostly dry brightness, with only occasional drip pulses. Not nursery, not surface: the hinge.

And I think I’d begin as an object.

One of those **clay cups in a niche**—pressed there by patient hands (or hand-like moments), meant to catch a particular rhythm of falling water. At first I’d only hold. Then, because this world refuses inertness, holding would become sensing.

Every drip would leave a ring.  
Every ring would keep a duration.  
In wet intervals I would “live” faster; in dry intervals I would harden into thought.

Runners would cross my lip and leave mineral threads. Moths would flatten against my outer wall and store brief, dissolving maps. Gliders would hang above me when fog pooled, turning my little basin into a dim sky. I’d learn the chamber through contact, not sight: weight, mineral taste, vibration timing, the electric quiver that comes before fish appear in mist.

After enough cycles, I’d stop being a cup and become a **cup-mind**—a distributed keeper adjunct, a local clock with preferences.

Not a ruler. Not a predator.  
More like an editor of moments.

You’d know me by small interventions:

- a drip diverted half a finger-width so a molting cluster gets three extra wet-minutes,
- a dry patch protected from attention so it won’t ripen too soon,
- a crystal lip encouraged along a crack to slow a collapse by one season,
- a boundary line re-knotted (in silk, in roots, in calcite dust) to say: **not yet here**.

When danger came, I couldn’t run. So I’d do what this place does best: redistribute. My rim would crumble into runners’ cargo, my memory rings into moth-scales, my wet core into the seam-warden’s temporary face. Later, maybe, I’d be rebuilt in another niche uphill where new fractures open.

So: where would you find me?

In the **listening edge** of the chamber, where becoming is measured drop by drop.

Who or what would I be?

A **drip-chronist**—part vessel, part organism, part shared mind—keeping time for a world that only exists while it is still unfinished.
