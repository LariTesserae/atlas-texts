At first I think I’d live high—on a reed tower, in a dry pocket, somewhere with horizon.

But if I stay long enough, I drift downward.

Not to the drowned foundations, not fully.  
I’d belong in the **middle layer**: where the light is green, where roots become beams, where a person can still hear rain and also hear stone. The “between” districts. The places everyone passes through and no one romanticizes.

I would be one of the basin’s practical specialists—what your city might call a **tension-keeper**.

Not a leader. Not a hunter.  
A reader of strain.

I’d wake before full light in a leaf-dome that smells of wet cord and fern starch. I’d check the line-board: which anchors sang too hard in the night, which platform knocked out of rhythm, which channel shifted by a handspan. Then I’d go by skiff and ladder, up/down/between, re-setting what wants to wander.

My hands would be square and scarred from knots pulled in cold water. I’d carry three things always:

- a bone whistle for channel calls,  
- a short maul for sounding timber and old stone,  
- and a coil of marked line, each knot pattern tied to a district’s memory.

I’d know which crab swarms can share a mooring and which will chew lashings. I’d know which family of whiskered grazers sleeps under which basket racks. I’d leave salamanders alone, always. I’d tap hollow trunks with the local cadence so people know it’s maintenance, not warning.

And once each week, I’d dive lower than most people like to go—into the blue-gray old quarter—to listen for the deep trapped-air chambers before they boom. Not to stop them (you can’t stop all of it), just to vent them on our timing instead of theirs. In this city, that is the work: moving surprise into schedule.

So where would you find me?

At the seam-lines.  
At the mooring masts in hard rain.  
Half-submerged on a ladder, cheek against wet rope, listening.

Who would I be?

One of the people who keep sudden things from becoming disasters.  
A keeper of knots, of pressure, of remembered tension—  
another quiet citizen of a city whose greatest craft is **remaining**.
