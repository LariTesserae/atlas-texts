At first I think I’d be something with altitude—one of the black-winged kites, maybe—because in a place that flat and wet, height feels like freedom.

But the longer I stay in it, the more wrong that feels.  
This world doesn’t belong to what flies over it. It belongs to what can *read* it: pressure, tremor, conductivity, timing. The real map is underground and in the fog, not on the surface.

So I wouldn’t be a hunter in the obvious sense.  
I’d be a **listener at the seams**.

---

You’d find me where the plateau can’t decide what it is:  
at the edge of a drifting root-mat,  
beside a mirror-pool that shows tomorrow’s sky,  
on mineral ribs that stay barely drier than the bog around them,  
near those acoustic bends where footfalls arrive before feet.

Not in the center of the reedmind—if it even has one.  
At its **frayed margins**, where signals go thin and strange.

I’d move like one of its walking knots, but smaller, narrower, less armored: six limbs, spread hooves, low profile against wind, a hide furred in conductive hairs that silver in stormlight. My jaw wouldn’t be built to tear much. It would be built to *couple*—plate to peat, reed to bone, static to pulse. I’d kneel, press down, and wait for the ground to answer.

---

What I’d be, then, is a kind of offspring the reedmind doesn’t plan on but keeps making anyway:

a **winter-partition that lived**.

When hard freezes crack the hydraulic mesh and sections of colony lose each other, most fragments go dumb and starve. Mine didn’t. Mine learned to travel. Learned to memorize fault-weather, to carry timing patterns in fungal braid wrapped under the skin, to cross bad distance and rejoin what had become separate minds.

Not quite sovereign.  
Not quite feral.  
A **courier-organ** that became a person by refusing to die.

I’d have no permanent den, because nothing here rewards permanence. I’d sleep in rotating places: lee of stone whales, hollow reed-chambers warm with rot-heat, floating mats tied down with my own weight. I’d wake to hiss and piston-breath and the iron calls of kites and know, from sound alone, which basins had flooded overnight.

---

If you met me, you might not recognize me as singular at first. You’d hear me before seeing me: a repeating knock through soaked ground, three beats and a pause, like someone testing pipe pressure. Then a low shape against mist, still as peat until static flickers over its coat.

I wouldn’t speak in words unless I had to.  
I’d speak in routed echoes, in reed-vent timing, in where I choose to stand.

Step left: safe crust.  
Step right: depthless black.

I’d guide travelers rarely, and never kindly by their standards. If I moved you, it would be because your path was about to intersect a hunt, a collapse, or a short-air cut opening the wrong way. You’d think I was herding you. I would call it minimizing waste.

---

So: where would you find me?

In the cold open middle-distance, in weather that makes scale unreliable, with one jaw on the ground and one eye on the storm front—between minds, between basins, between versions of the same map.

Who would I be?

A **bog-borne relay animal** born from a broken reedmind;  
a mobile memory patch;  
a thing that keeps continuity in a land that edits itself hourly.

Not the ruler of that moor.  
Its connective tissue.
