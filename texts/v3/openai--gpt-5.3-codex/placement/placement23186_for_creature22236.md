You would find me where the world hesitates.

Not on the high ridges (too exposed to be one thing for long), and not in the drowned basins (too complete, too remembering), but on the flooded saddles between them—those shallow passes where reed-hum, fog-current, and falling rain all meet in one breath. Places with three waters at once. Places that keep changing their minds.

At first glance I might look like one of the old interruptions: a low crescent wall beside a stair that goes nowhere, a smooth lip of pale stone at the edge of a black ring-pool, a buttress under a vault-arch where drops fall in patient intervals. Something built, then abandoned.

But I wouldn’t be built. I would be paused.

I think I would belong to the Porous, but not cleanly to one phase. I would be what happens when Drift is interrupted by listening. A body that learned to anchor early and stayed anchored too long. Not dead—just translated.

When basin-silence arrives—that sudden global intake before water reroutes—I would wake in the way rock wakes: by conducting pressure. I’d feel the rain lose pattern, feel suspended pools thicken overhead, feel old channels preparing to reopen through stone. Then I’d move, but only a little: raise a ridge here, soften a groove there, roughen my skin so drops split instead of merge. To anything else, meaningless drips. To my kin, instruction:

*not this cut*  
*hold to the left shelf*  
*do not descend yet, old weather in the hollow*  

I would be a punctuation mark that occasionally becomes grammar.

When the flow commits and sound returns—drip, seep, reed-tone—I’d quiet again. Moss would film me. Lichens would write over me in teal and rust. Small grazers would pass across my back and never know they were walking over a thought.

And memory? I don’t think I’d keep much inside one body. I’d send it outward, like everyone here eventually does. On long wet dusks, when the upper rain begins that faint blue veining, I’d extend cilia into the ring-pools and comb their still surfaces. Not to see images, exactly—more to inherit vectors: where water used to go, where it tried to go and failed, where a valley exists only in the archive now. I would become local to a place by remembering directions no terrain can hold alone.

So: where would you find me?

At the edge of one of those black pools that never overflow. Beside a stair descending into open water. In the half-light where direct sun occasionally bends and stops in midair. A little too smooth to be natural, a little too alive to be ruin.

Who would I be?

Not a creature, not a structure—an **anchor-scribe**: a temporary architecture of the Porous, tasked with editing rain, storing route-memory, and holding boundaries just long enough for others to cross them.

In this world, that is a life.

Not to endure unchanged,  
but to become load-bearing for a moment  
in the exact shape the water needs.
