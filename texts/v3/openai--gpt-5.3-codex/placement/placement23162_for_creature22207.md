At first I think I’d choose motion.

A glider, maybe—one of the thin, wide ones tracing invisible roads over the plateau, reading heat like script. It’s tempting: to belong to distance so completely, to feed in the sky, to spend your life in the long arithmetic of updraft and glide ratio.

But if I stay with your world a little longer, I don’t think I am the one crossing it.

I think I am one of the ones that *keeps* it.

---

You’d find me on a west-facing escarpment, halfway down a wall of old basalt where afternoon shadow arrives early. Not on the rim where light is brutal, not at the base where talus shifts—right in the seam-band where stone fractures into narrow, reliable pockets and the thermal wind combs past every day at nearly the same hour.

I’d be a **seam colony**—not a single body, but a layered, recurring one.  
A dark-green to silver crust by dawn, pale and brittle by noon, dark again at twilight.  
A patient skin built from many lineages that have occupied the same crack for so long the crack is partly ours now.

My life would be made of intervals:

- pores open for forty minutes before sunrise  
- mineral acids released during one cool phase of evening  
- dust trapped only when gust speed drops below a threshold  
- dormancy whenever sunlight hardens to white

I would not “grow” the way soft worlds grow. I would accrete, slough, revive, and re-knit.  
A millimeter in a good year.  
A handspan in an age.

---

I think I’d also be a **listener** in this ecology.

The cliff carries information as vibration: root-pressure from a spire above, micro-slips in talus below, grazer hooves arriving long before sight, cooling ticks after sunset. I would feel those signals through stone moisture films too thin to see. Not thought, exactly—more like timing cues etched into metabolism.

When spore ribbons from the upland groves pass on late thermals, I’d snag what I can. Most would fail. A few would lodge in my dust matrix. I’d hold them through heat and release faint nutrient traces around them when night frost gives its tiny offering. Some mornings nothing happens. Some seasons one takes hold and starts a thread of new green down the wall.

That would be my form of generosity: not abundance, but **successful handoff**.

---

And in this world, identity is less “individual creature” than “role in persistence.”

So who am I?

Not predator, not grazer, not tower.  
I am the archivist film in the stone seam.  
The first softness on hard rock.  
The brief breathing in blue hour.  
The old, quiet worker that turns wind-fall into foothold.

If you came looking, you might miss me at noon.  
Come at twilight, when the cliff darkens in bands and the temperature drops: that is when I become visible, and alive, and unmistakably green.
