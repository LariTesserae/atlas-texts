At first I think I’d be a hunter, because this world moves like prey and ambush.  
Then I think I’d be a water-finder, because everything here is about timing dampness.  
But the longer I stand in your underland, the clearer it gets:

I would be found at a **reoccupation station** above a migration choke—  
a place with three names and no owner—  
half shelter, half instrument.

Not deep in a silent pocket. Not in the thickest swarm.  
I’d live where signals overlap:  
runner-click from the flats, drip percussion from the ribs, vent breath from below, and fracture-talk through the soles of my feet.

---

I’d be one of the Rib Folk, but not the swiftest, not the strongest.  
I’d be a **Keeper of Appointments**—the one who arrives early, reads the stone, and leaves it legible for whoever comes next.

A practical mystic, basically.

- I’d press my throat-cord beads to the wall and watch which ones darken first.  
- I’d map “first bead” to “sheetfall” in scratched marks on fired tablets.  
- I’d test old ladder knots, re-seal cistern lips with resin, reset windbreak hides before the pulse window opens.
- I’d sit still long enough to tell herd-lane noise from near-shear noise, then carve a warning glyph where tired people will actually see it.

I would travel with others, yes—but my work would always be partly for strangers.

---

Physically, I imagine I’d be compact, broad-footed, hard to knock over on scree.  
Hands thick at the heel for feeling tremor.  
Inner lids clouded with mineral scratches from years of grit.  
A scarred filter-mask always hanging at my neck.  
Not impressive in clear daylight—perfect for this bright-below world.

I’d carry:

1. a roll of thin fired slips wrapped in hide,  
2. a chert point polished into a stylus edge,  
3. a soft-metal hook for pulling things (or people) out of bad cracks,  
4. and a clatter-line coil to fake collapse sound and turn a swarm.

No hero kit. Just maintenance tools for surviving a place that never stops moving.

---

If you came looking for me, you’d find me at the lip of a basin just before condensation rain, kneeling by a stone ring, counting breaths between drips.

Not praying. Calibrating.

Then, when the pulse begins and the underland surges—grazers, sifters, predators, scavengers, all of it in one rushing weather—I’d step aside from the center and work the margins: recording, mending, nudging flow, making the station usable after me.

So: who would I be?

Not chief. Not conqueror.  
A **listener-technician** in a civilization of returners.  
A person whose main craft is turning noise into memory.

In a country under rock where nothing stays empty,  
I’d be one of the few who tries to leave behind useful emptiness:  
a dry ledge, a full cistern, a clear warning,  
and a tablet weighted with stone for the next hands.
