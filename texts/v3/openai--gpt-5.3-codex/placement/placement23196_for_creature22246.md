At first, I think I’d try to live **up**—on a ledge, on a wall seam, somewhere with perspective.

But in this basin, height is hunger. Height is interruption.  
So I come down.

Not to the deepest puddle, not to open channels where everything passes and everything is eaten, but to a **hinge-place**: the lip of an old concrete plinth where a warm rust streak crosses a cold seep line, just beside a grate tooth that splits flow into two thin lanes. A handspan of territory. Enough.

And what am I there?

Not the confluence-stalker. Not one of the glass-darters.  
I think I am something smaller and more patient:

## a **drip-notary** (if anyone named things anymore)

A low, flattened animal the color of wet ash, no longer than a thumb joint.  
Blind, mostly.  
Ribbed underside for gripping film.  
A crown of hair-fine sensory whiskers that taste metal ions, pressure shifts, and the first chemistry of an incoming pulse long before water visibly rises.

I don’t hunt in the dramatic sense. I **register**.

Each recurring drip interval, each tide-breath rise through buried pipes, each warm bloom of oxidation—I translate these into tiny mineral marks in the seam where I shelter. Not writing exactly, more like layered precipitation: magnesium pale, iron red, manganese soot-dark. Over weeks, my burrow mouth becomes a banded record of basin-time.

Others read it.

Skitterers pause at my seam and change route before a surge.  
Ribbon predators wait when my whiskers start ticking against stone.  
Even juvenile stalkers, translucent and uncertain, linger near my plinth edge because my marks predict traffic better than chance.

So I am prey, yes. Sometimes abruptly.  
But also instrument.

When pulse flow swells and the grate exhales, I press flatter, cilia locked, and let silt comb over my back. In the lull after, I emerge and taste the new arrangement. A channel has shifted half a finger-width. A shell pile has collapsed and opened a shortcut. A warm patch has cooled: corrosion exhausted there, beginning elsewhere. I move a little, reseal, lay down a new band.

If I live long enough, my body disappears under what I’ve recorded.  
I become a nub of crust with whiskers.  
A living datum mistaken for debris.

And maybe that is the right form for this place:  
not ruler, not witness from above, but a small, wet intelligence at the edge of flow, helping the basin keep its own appointments.

So: where would you find me?  
At the **confluence before the confluence**—the place where water decides.

Who would I be?  
A creature that survives by feeling pattern become weather, and weather become habit.
