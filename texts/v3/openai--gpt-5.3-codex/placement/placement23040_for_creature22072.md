At first, I thought I’d be something small and fugitive there—  
a cloud moth under a fern lip,  
or one of those translucent snails that survives by waiting out heat in a seam of moss.

Then I thought: no, I’d be a tool.  
A fog comb left on a high line, drinking dawn into a cistern while everyone sleeps clipped to roots.

But if I let the image keep unfolding, I think I’m a person—ledgefolk, definitely—just not a central one. Not a chief, not a hunter, not a shrine-tender. I’d be one of the people who keep routes alive.

You would find me on the **in-between bands**: where the old stair is still usable for twenty-seven steps, then gone in a shear scar; where a rope bridge hums a warning note before fray is visible; where yesterday’s foothold is today’s drop. I’d live near a breathing rock layer—high enough for early fog, low enough for midday seep—probably in a root vault facing east so the first light can be mirrored downslope.

My introduction might be something like: **“Second Drip, Split Stair, Black Band.”**  
Not a name-name. A working coordinate.

My work would be called route-keeping, but it’s really listening with hands:

- testing anchor roots for internal rot by vibration,
- replacing knot ladders before storm season,
- moving marker flags a meter up when the thermal slice shifts,
- teaching children which cliff-moans are harmless and which mean *unclip and run now*,
- updating drum patterns when a crossing changes.

I’d carry more wedges than food.  
My palms would be scarred and dense, my forearms full of touch-sign shorthand.  
I’d be fluent in mirror grammar at noon and wrist-language in thunder.

And spiritually—if that word still fits—I’d belong to the same thing they all do: agreements.  
I wouldn’t pray for stillness. I’d renew ties.

When someone dies, I’d likely be among those who climb with the cradle to the mist groves, choosing a fork where condensation is punctual and roots are already searching. Years later, when that tree has bent three handspans toward a new crack, I’d be the one adjusting the shrine peg and saying to the younger ones: *See? It’s still speaking. Move with it.*

So: where would I be?  
At the broken edges people must cross anyway.

Who would I be?  
A **knot-keeper of temporary certainties** in a country that never stops moving.
