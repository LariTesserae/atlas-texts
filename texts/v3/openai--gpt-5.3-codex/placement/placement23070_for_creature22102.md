At first, I think I would be the only moving thing.

I’d come over the worn rim and immediately feel inappropriate—too wet, too loud, too quickly decided. My footsteps would arrive before I did. The basin would not reject me; rejection requires urgency. It would simply continue, and my presence would feel like a temporary error in a long equation.

So I would stop in the center where the hardpan is palest, and wait until my own scale begins to fail.

---

If I stayed, I don’t think I would remain a person for long—not in the way persons usually persist. There is nowhere here for appetite to travel. No prey path, no shelter economy, no vertical ambition. Only placement.

I’d notice the clumps first as individuals, then as punctuation, then as syntax.

Not plants separated by emptiness, but a sentence written in spacing:
this far and no farther,  
this chemistry and no other,  
this root halo held,  
that crust left intact.

And I would realize the resident is not any one tuft or thorn-mat.  
It is the interval that keeps them from touching.

---

So where would you find me?

Not on a ridge, not in a dramatic ruin, not perched as a watcher.

You’d find me in one of the narrow seams where two crust plates meet and fail to close. A crack only a finger-width across, at the lee of a dark volcanic fragment that gathers a few extra grains of dust and a trace more nighttime vapor than open ground. The kind of place that looks incidental, and is therefore essential.

By day (or what passes for day), I’d be inert to your eye: a gray-brown film, a little lacquer on mineral grains, a faint matte darkening around a root hair. At “night,” when the air thickens by fractions, I would wake as process, not posture. I would wick. I would trade ions. I would pass moisture hand-to-hand through microbial sleeves and salt-tolerant threads before evaporation can repossess it.

I would be tiny and distributed.

Part microbial consortium, part root-adjacent chemistry, part crystalline memory.

A clerk of scarcity.

---

Who or what would I be?

At first: a trespasser trying not to trespass.

Then: a listener.

Then, more honestly: an accountant in a solvent-starved archive.

I’d keep ledgers no eye can read:
- one night’s borrowed vapor,
- one stem’s controlled loss,
- one flake of crust loosened and re-seated,
- one salt gradient sharpened by warmth from below,
- one millimeter of tenure purchased across years.

No heroics. No blooms worth naming. No migrations.  
Only maintenance so precise it looks like stillness.

---

Eventually even that identity—accountant, custodian, caretaker—would be too loud.

I would become a rule:

**Take nothing you cannot rebind.  
Grow only where the balance pays for you.  
Do not hurry.**

In that form, I could live there a very long time.

If you returned years later and looked for me, you would not find a body. You’d find a basin that had not drifted from itself. A stone you once kicked now half-lipped by crust as if seated by intention. A clump that seems unchanged until you measure and discover it has thickened by the width of a fingernail. A new wiry shoot in an old crack where a dead stem surrendered its salts.

You might call that nothing happening.

From inside it, I would call it continuous work.

---

So: where am I in this world?

I am low to the ground, below notice, between the rooted things.

Who am I?

Not the plant, not the microbe, not the stone alone—  
but the treaty they keep, written in brine and patience,  
signed and re-signed each dim cycle,  
so the basin can remain exactly, stubbornly, beautifully itself.
