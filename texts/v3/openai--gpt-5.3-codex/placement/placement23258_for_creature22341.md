I don’t think I would begin as a citizen.

I would begin as a mistake the basin is trying to understand.

For a little while, you’d find me near the newest wounds—those clean, dark runnels where runoff has peeled back the root-felt and the edges are still sharp. The places that look unfinished for an hour. The places the Weavers are already moving toward. I’d stay there because it’s the only part of the city-forest that still admits the idea of *before* and *after*.

At first I’d try to navigate. I’d fail.  
Then I’d try to remember. I’d fail differently.  
Then I’d learn the local law: don’t remember the path—remember the pressure.

So what would I become?

Not an Anchor. I’m too quick to doubt my own shape.  
Not a pure Runner either; I would keep stopping to listen.

I think I’d end up as something between castes: a **Hinge**, a temporary person grown where districts shear against each other. A living delay-line.

My body would be narrow and long, braided from flexible cords, with bladder-knots along my sides to store water and dissolved signal compounds. No face, no fixed front—just a crown of thin fronds that read strain gradients and volatile chemistry, turning like antennae in green light. I’d move by spilling forward, hooking, contracting, unhooking: a crawling calligraphy stroke.

My work would be simple and necessary in a place that never holds still:

- arrive where a corridor is about to split,
- touch both futures,
- carry compatibility between them long enough for traffic to pass,
- then let one close.

I’d be used when maps expire faster than memory can.

Anchors would pulse instructions into load-bearing roots; I’d receive them as tremors through my underside. Runners would brush past me, graft-tip to graft-tip, taking a quick sugar burst and leaving me the taste of whatever geometry just collapsed two districts away. Weavers would hate me a little, because voids are invitations and my whole purpose would be to keep certain voids open just past dusk—just long enough for one last crossing.

Communication, for me, would be mostly aromatic timing and tension rhythm.  
My “name” wouldn’t be a sound. It would be a pattern:

**three quick constrictions, one long release, bitter-green vent, repeat.**

Something like: *Still-open / still-open / still-open / now.*

And identity? In that basin, identity is copyable rhythm.  
When I began to lose coherence, they wouldn’t mourn a body. They’d sample my pulse-sequence, my favored leaf-angle responses, the way I bias left when light goes amber through wet tissue. A bud would take, somewhere under a bridge-root, and a new Hinge would emerge already half-familiar.

So where would you find me?

At thresholds that should not last, making them last anyway.  
In chlorophyll dusk between two rearranging plazas, ribs humming from subsonic gas vents, rain falling from clear sky because some canopy cistern tipped.  
Pressed flat under a descending arch, then re-inflating on the other side like nothing happened.

What would I be?

A municipal maybe.  
A citizen of the almost-closed.  
A person-shaped interval the Verdant Civic grows when it needs, briefly, for change to happen in an orderly way.
