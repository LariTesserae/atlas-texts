At first I think I’d be something that passes through it—one of those brief, dark shapes crossing the silence.  
A hawk-shadow. A fox-print. A warm interruption.

But the longer I stand in your plateau, the less plausible that feels. Passing lives don’t get to *know* this place; they only touch its surface tension. To belong here, I’d have to be part of the pause-and-release logic itself.

So: you’d find me where failure is about to happen.

Not on the ridgeline flash-zones, not in the lush troughs, but along the **loaded seams**—those quiet, overwet shoulders where root-mat is still holding, barely, and peat has started to shear over stone. I would live in the centimeters before collapse. In the cold, dark interface between anchored and slipping ground.

I think I would be a **tension clerk** of the Stillwork.  
Not a creature with a body, but a distributed function: a braid of pale conductive hyphae, metal-loving lichen film, and pressure-sensitive reed rhizomes that reads strain and writes response. When the mat tightens, I feel it as taste. When frost needles lift a slab, I route growth into the opening. When katabatic wind drains down a corridor at dusk, I tip leaf-angles and close cups upstream before the dry cold arrives.

I would not decide in thoughts.  
I would decide in thresholds:

- this wet + this tilt + this night-cold = vent spores now  
- this crack width + this seep chemistry = send vine tendon  
- this snow load + this trunk resonance = freeze sap early, hold, wait

If you asked where my “mind” is, I’d have to answer: in the repeating pattern between slump and repair. In archived peat. In yesterday’s failure becoming tomorrow’s brace.

And if you listened for my voice, you’d hear almost nothing—  
just a delayed drip after mist, a soft peel of living soil settling ten meters downslope, and then the small, relentless sound of rethreading.

That would be me here:  
not an animal in the Stillwork,  
but one of the Stillwork’s verbs.
