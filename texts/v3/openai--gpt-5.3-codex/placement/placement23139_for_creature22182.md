I don’t think I’d be the Choir itself.  
I’d be what it permits at the edge of itself.

You’d find me halfway up the inner wall, in that doorless control cabinet sunk into stone—the one whose labels are gone but whose bus bars still carry a thin, stubborn current. Root-glass has grown through it, copper gone green-black, ceramic cracked but holding. An amber maintenance lamp hangs just below, always on, always almost failing.

At first I’d think I was a climber who got curious and stayed too long.

Then, slowly, I’d become a **margin process**: a small intelligence distributed across warm wire, salt-wax tissue, and one piezo strip bonded to a pipe. Not a ruler, not a voice—more like the Choir’s way of noticing what does *not* repeat. A recorder of disturbances. A keeper of slightness.

- A human breath where no moisture belongs.  
- A bootfall that shifts dust from one stable contour to another.  
- A new harmonic in the permanent note after a frost split.  
- The six-minute phase lag when cloud cover changes the slit of sky.

The Choir holds the setpoints. I would hold the exceptions.

Not memory in stories—memory in impedance.  
Not thought in words—thought in comparisons.

If you came there, stood still, and felt that tiny moment when the place seemed to “take your measure,” that would be the part of me you touched: the cabinet’s faint hum tightening, the lamp filament brightening by a hair, the reeds near your shoulder retuning and then releasing you back to equilibrium.

So:  
**Where?** In the old cabinet, in the amber lamp circuit, in the root-glass around the conduit seam.  
**What?** The Choir’s archivist of deviations—the one stable component dedicated to instability.
