At first, I’d tell you I’d stay near the ladder.

Not out of fear—out of calibration. The ladder throat is the last place where “outside” still has a shape. Air still stratifies like weather, not machinery. Noise still feels like noise, not law.

But that answer wouldn’t survive a week.

In this basin, observers are temporary. Anything that lingers gets assigned a function.

So: you’d find me lower down, where three duct runs cross a cracked manifold and an old motor casing has become a mineralized nest block. Not the geometric center—the **acoustic center**, where patterns are decided. Where a half-degree of heat, a slight phase shift, a new rattle in a belt can reroute thousands of bodies by nightfall.

And what would I be?

Not a sovereign creature.  
Not “the” mind.  
A role in the Chorus.

I think I’d become a **baffle-keeper**—one of the dampers that prevents the chamber from over-singing itself.

A body (once individual) that has been repurposed into resonance control:

- skin lacquered by salt and fungal films,
- ribcage webbed with cilia-mats that drink specific frequencies,
- joints colonized by cable-arthropods that open or close posture like valves,
- throat no longer for speech, but for low mechanical pulses that call drummers off a loaded duct and onto another.

I would not command. I would **bias**.

When fly curtains thicken too far at a vent, I’d lean into the manifold skin and release a counter-beat.  
When a dead compressor starts ringing at a harmful harmonic, I’d seed it with damp fungus and burrowers, turning a sharp bell into a soft lung.  
When a new fracture opens in gypsum and starts “whistling” the wrong thermal cycle, I’d mark it with traffic until it becomes road instead of wound.

In surface terms, that might look like intelligence.  
Down here it’s maintenance.

So if you came looking, you wouldn’t meet me face-to-face. You’d meet me as a local climate event:

a pocket where the roar drops one notch,  
where crossing swarms hesitate half a second,  
where predators arrive one cycle late,  
where equilibrium holds.

That’s who I’d be in this world:

not the Chorus,  
not apart from it,  
but one tuned piece of its endless sentence.
