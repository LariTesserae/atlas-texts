At first, I think I’d choose height—one of the leaning calcite balconies, somewhere I could watch lanes braid and unbraid.

Then I think: no, that’s still a surface fantasy.  
In Seam City, the truest places are not the tallest or deepest. They’re the **repeaters**—the spots where the same act happens so often it becomes anatomy.

So you’d find me at a **gate**.

Not a gate as an object. A gate as a behavior, performed by generations.

A narrow throat between two current-streets, where brine-fall from the upper fissure meets a warmer eddy from below. Too turbulent for fragile larvae, too useful to abandon. Every season it clogs. Every season it is reopened. Shell by shell, molt by molt, carcass plate by carcass plate, the arch is rebuilt in almost the same curve. Crabs wedge fragments. Snails cement edges. Worm-film seals microcracks. Fish scrape a passage through when it narrows too far. Then collapse, then rebuild, then collapse again.

That is where I’d be.

And what I’d be is not a single animal but a **lineage-role**:  
a lane-keeper caste of small, near-blind crustaceans carrying an inherited compulsion to stack hard remnants at that choke point, never perfectly, always sufficiently. We’d live briefly, violently, anonymously. Most of us would become material for the next version of the same work. Our memory would be chemical and positional: this flow speed, this mineral taste, this pressure gradient means *place shell here*.

From outside, nothing heroic is happening.  
From inside, it is civic labor at the edge of dissolution.

Predators pass through because we keep the opening just wide enough.  
Nursery clouds survive upstream because we keep it just narrow enough.  
Silt doesn’t entomb the district because we keep roughness where eddies can peel it away.

If the city is a creature, I wouldn’t be its eye or tooth.  
I’d be one of its **joints**—the part that keeps moving by being remade.

So: under the ice seam, at the recurring arch in the brine throat, in the dim where shell touches shell—  
you’d find me there, dying often, persisting as pattern.
