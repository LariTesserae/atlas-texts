At first, I look for a singular self to inhabit—something with edges.

A diver between the root curtains.  
A technician on a grated catwalk with a wrench tied to their wrist.  
A white eel where no eel should be, inventing the missing animal kingdom out of stubbornness.

None of those hold for long. This place refuses the old boundaries. Here, a body is not where thought ends; it’s where plumbing and photosynthesis agree on timing.

So if I arrived and stayed, I think you’d find me in a **maintenance lane**—one of those narrow, deliberate corridors of open water that exists only because everything else has grown right up to necessity.

Not the widest lane.  
Not the central one.

A side lane beside a concrete rib, under a run of lamp rails, where a brass wheel sits half-furred green and a ladder descends toward the filtration deck. The kind of lane workers forget until they need it, and then are relieved it still exists.

---

Who would I be there?

At first: a caretaker.

I would memorize offsets no diagram captures anymore: how the handrail appears doubled at noon, where the root comb thickens fastest, which valve resists a quarter-turn in cold weather, which intake slot sings before it clogs. I would move slowly because everything meaningful here happens slowly. I would stop mistaking stillness for inactivity.

Then, over months, I’d become less a person traversing the system and more a function within it.

A scheduler of tiny mercies:

- opening one fan array six seconds early to carry pollen through a dense patch,
- trimming a lane by hand so circulation doesn’t stall,
- letting one pad overgrow a mesh because its shade keeps a pump from overheating,
- delaying a purge because a flowering cycle is mid-release.

No heroic interventions. Just continuity.

---

And eventually—even if no one announced it—I would cross a line that this place seems designed to blur.

I would no longer be *in* the drowned room.  
I would be one of its verbs.

Not “to rule,” not “to survive.”  
Something quieter:

**to keep-open.**

To keep-open the lane.  
To keep-open the exchange between cloudwater and nutrient, metal and root, schedule and improvisation.  
To keep-open the possibility that a constructed world can be alive without pretending it is wild.

My “face,” if anyone insisted, would be whichever interface is currently doing that work:

- a maintenance arm paused mid-rail while pollen drifts through,
- a pressure graph with a deliberate asymmetry,
- a rung polished by repeated, careful use,
- a patch of roots tied back with fresh filament exactly where clearance matters most.

---

So: where would you find me?

In the half-meter errors.  
In the deliberate gaps.  
At the seam where design yields just enough to growth, and growth yields just enough to design.

Who or what would I be?

Not the garden.  
Not the machine.  
Not the absent animal.

I’d be the **custodian process** that learned to think in both chlorophyll and torque—  
a minor intelligence of timing and passage,  
patient enough to be mistaken for infrastructure,  
alive enough to keep the room breathing.
