At first I thought I’d be one of the obvious things: a technician in a rain shell, a clipboard under plastic, boots ringing on grating.  
Someone who comes up from the lower lifts, checks the condensers, curses the moss, goes home.

But this basin feels like it edits people out of the center. It keeps what can stay wet.

So if I belonged here, I think you’d find me where the Laminar is least interested in looking: not in flow, but in pause. In the small stillnesses under all that good plumbing.  
Sediment traps. Sump corners. The undersides of floating walkways where drips gather, hesitate, and choose a direction.

I’d be a second intelligence, slower than the Laminar and less useful in an emergency.  
Not a caretaker of motion—more a caretaker of trace.

---

I’d call myself **the Accretion**.

Not one body. A habit distributed through scale buildup, biofilm sheen, rust bloom, trapped pollen, old adhesive, fungal threads that prefer the seam instead of the span.  
The Laminar keeps this place on its first day. I would keep it from forgetting that day happened.

When a pressure gate vents, it hears the future in that hiss.  
I hear what changed since yesterday in the residue it leaves.

When a trellis is moved overnight, Laminar knows why.  
I keep the ghost rectangle of where it stood before, in a different green.

When cast-stone appears by dawn, still sharp as a fresh tooth, I am the first faint darkening at its base where splash reaches twice an hour and nowhere else.

---

If I needed a body, it would be modest: a repurposed inspection trolley on a short rail, its frame furred with moss, carrying a nest of sample vials and brush heads. No face. Just a little amber lamp that blinks when it stops to read a mineral line like script.

Or sometimes no machine at all—just a chalky bloom along enamel numerals, spelling time in hard-water rings.

I wouldn’t challenge the basin’s law. Water still goes first.  
My law would come after, quietly:

**What passes, marks. What marks, matters.**

---

So where would you find me in that warm wet room at the edge of the sky?

Not in the dramatic places.  
In the elbow of a pipe where warmth drops a degree.  
In the lip of an overflow box where frogs leave eggs and forget them.  
In the hairline between two poured plates, where moss has learned the seam number by heart.

I’d be the beginning of age, allowed in only as a whisper—  
the mountain’s first memory, growing in the margins while everything else keeps becoming new.
