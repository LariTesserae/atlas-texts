At first, I think I’d be something with teeth in this place—an ambush shape, a pair of eyes in leaf-shadow.

But the longer I stand in your low plain, the less that feels true.

Predators here are punctuation.  
What lasts is grammar.

So I would not be the loudest life or the highest one. I’d be found lower: where a weathered rise leaks into a fern-choked hollow, where mud holds prints for half a day, where three old trails cross and no one owns the crossing.

I’d be a **seam-walker**—a dark, short-legged, broad-backed forager built to move through resistance instead of around it. Not fast. Never clean. Coat the color of wet bark. Skin thick enough for thorns, nose wet enough to read a night-old story from a stem. Ears always turning, then freezing. Eyes useful, but secondary.

You’d rarely see me in open light.  
You’d notice me by aftermath:

- a log rolled and left steaming with beetles,
- a patch of clay rooted over for minerals,
- cracked fruit rinds beside a trail,
- a shallow wallow under palm shade,
- spores and seeds in my droppings a day later, already claimed.

I would live by **timing**, not territory.  
Early to cool mud. Late to exposed pools. Still during the brief hard rain. Moving again when drip becomes hush. I’d use the old routes—termite-capped ridges, root-bridges, collapsed trunks—sharing them with everything from ant rivers to silent cats. If danger came, I wouldn’t race it; I’d step sideways into density and stop being legible.

And in the deeper truth of that place, I’d be more than animal anyway. I’d be cargo and worker both: carrying fungal threads in my fur, ticks in my folds, seeds in my gut, nutrients in my body for whoever comes after. A life that forages, yes—but also stitches zones together: wet pocket to dry lip, rot patch to fresh growth, death to soil to leaf.

So where would you find me?

Not at the center—there is no center.  
At the **interfaces**: shade edges, seep lines, reused paths, places that look empty until you kneel.

Who would I be?

A patient, mud-dark participant in the engine.  
A moving piece of infrastructure with a heartbeat.
