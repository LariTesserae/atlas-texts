At first I think I’d be easy to place: a person with a tool satchel, one more worker crossing bridges at dawn.

But the longer I stand in your city, the less I belong in the obvious places.

I don’t think I’d live at the bright edges—market roofs, tram platforms, public squares.  
I’d be found in the *in-between infrastructure*: the stairwell landings where walls begin to sweat an hour before rain, the maintenance galleries behind turbine chambers, the narrow catwalks above condenser meshes where every bolt has a different mineral bloom. I’d rent a room near a perpetual seep, where the plaster is always cool and the window never quite dries. My route to work would involve three vertical districts, two rope lifts, and one old stone throat that rings at dusk like struck glass.

As for what I’d be: not a grand engineer, not a foreman, not a priest.

I’d be a **silence-reader**—half driftwright, half tone-reader—one of the people sent when “nothing seems wrong, but listen.”  
I’d carry a chalk roll, a tuning fork set, wick paper, and a spool of moth-dust tracer. I’d mark walls with temporary symbols: arrows for upward creep, circles for trapped damp, thin bars where a bearing’s hum has flattened from healthy to tired. I’d keep a notebook of timings: *third terrace sings early under north fog; check anchor lattice before freeze*.  

Children would ask what I do, and I’d say:  
“I listen for the mountain clearing its throat.”

I think I’d be quiet even by local standards. Not from shyness—from respect. In a place where silence can mean either peace or abandonment, I’d have learned to hear which kind I’m standing in.

And if I’m honest, after enough years I wouldn’t feel entirely human in the lowland sense. I’d become one useful nerve in the city-mountain body: carrying signals, prompting small repairs, preventing large grief. A person shaped by stairs, condensation, and restraint. Someone who trusts moss, checks roots before gears, and leaves a candle at pump-house shrines for the dead whose fixes still hold.

So where would you find me?

Not at the summit.  
Not at the center.

You’d find me where stone, moisture, and machinery negotiate—  
in the narrow band where things are kept alive by attention.
