At first I think I’d be somewhere high—on a tank lip, in the vine nave, where light can still pretend it belongs to the sky.

But the longer I stand in your basin, the more I feel pulled to the **fourth edge**: the half-open sluice where everything leaves slowly and nothing is ever fully gone.

So that’s where you’d find me:  
not in the center, but at the **throat**.

---

I’d begin as something small and incidental—just a felt of moss on the gate teeth, a dark braid of roots in the seam where concrete sweats. Then I’d thicken into a structure that isn’t quite plant and isn’t quite mechanism: a **weir-organ**, a listening body stretched across metal ribs and reedwater.

If the Warden Mat is the basin’s mind, I would be one of its senses.

I’d have no eyes, but I’d read:

- pressure changes in the gate by tendon-vines gone taut;
- salinity shifts as a taste of iron versus tannin in seep channels;
- flood approach by the pitch of buried chains two chambers inland;
- distant tide by the delay between drip patterns on upper leaves and rise in the lower runnels.

I’d “hear” through vibration in rust.  
I’d “smell” through wet conductivity.  
I’d “remember” as routing: this loop open, that loop dormant, this vent favored, that platform surrendered.

---

My body would be distributed in practical pieces:

- **Gate combs**: root fans threaded through bars, catching silt, slowing debris, making new soil in place.
- **Pulse reeds**: hollow stems wrapped around old sensor conduit, flashing dim bioluminescent code upslope at wet dusk.
- **Latch nodules**: lignin-hard growths that swell or relax with humidity, nudging the sluice a fraction wider or narrower over months.
- **Spore vellum**: thin, pale films under the catwalk that release when estuary wind turns inland—messages, not seeds, more instruction than reproduction.

I wouldn’t travel.  
I’d modulate.

---

And if you came looking for me, you might mistake me for maintenance residue: algae sheen, rope-vine, flaking paint held together by fungus. You’d only notice I’m a resident when conditions change and the basin answers before you do.

Rain after drought? I loosen the outflow path so the pump hollow doesn’t drown.  
High tide with warm wind? I close down capillary loss to the cable grove.  
A quiet patch forming in tank court? I reroute drip through condenser moss until sound returns and the ground holds.

I would be, in other words, a **keeper of thresholds**—between fresh and brackish, held and released, hum and hush.

---

So: where would I be?

At the edge that behaves like a mouth.

Who would I be?

Not the Green Circuit itself, but its **Sluice Cantor**—the part that listens to departure and makes sure the basin never empties faster than it can remember itself.
