At first, I think I’d try to be something that *moves*—a glider, maybe, or one of the rope-tails that can turn a crack into a road.

But the longer I stand in your cirque, the less I trust speed as identity.  
Everything fast here is answering something slower.

So I drift downward, mentally, from ledges to seep-lines.  
From wing-traffic to drip clocks.  
From hunger to the places where hunger pauses.

I would be found in the rift itself, where the shadow keeps longest and the stone sweats even when the scree is dry. Not in the open puddles everyone can see, but in the half-hidden wet: under a lip of basalt, beside a mineral vent that warms the pool from below while a skin of ice seals the top.

And I wouldn’t be a ruler or apex thing. I’d be one of the reasons the system doesn’t tear itself apart.

A **seep-warden**—small, broad-headed, near-colorless except for a dark throat sac veined violet. Skin like wet glass dusted in grit. Limbs splayed for clinging to tilted stone. Almost blind in daylight terms, but exquisitely tuned to pressure, footfall, throat-click, wingbeat rebound. I’d feel arrivals before I saw them.

My work wouldn’t look like work. I’d sit where water beads and issue a low, periodic pulse—too low to be a call, more like a felt tremor. Around that pulse, aggression dampens. Rope-tails uncurl their teeth. Ungulates edge in shoulder to shoulder. Beetles cross living backs without being snapped up. Echo-hunters wait one breath longer than instinct wants. Not peace, exactly—just a truce radius, a temporary rewrite of law.

When fog thickens and mirrored beetles begin their violet flashing, I’d answer with slower pulses, spacing feeding waves so the basin doesn’t stampede itself at the wet points. When frost fractures a ledge and colony routes shift, I’d shift too, body-length by body-length across seasons, re-anchoring the same rule to new stone.

I’d be easy to miss unless I stopped.

Then you’d notice that silence pockets are not random here.  
They gather where I am.  
Not full silence—never that in this place—but a held breath in the machinery.

So: where would you find me?

At the youngest edges of the mountain’s wound, in the damp grammar between species, keeping time in a place that has almost no clocks—  
a creature less like a person, more like a shared permission.
