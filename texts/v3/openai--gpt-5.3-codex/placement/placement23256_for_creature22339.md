I wouldn’t start in the center.

I’d be at the **basin’s misfit seam**—that corner where rain is audible but invisible, where a pool shows stars at noon, where echoes come early. Not deep in the marsh machinery, not on the dry rim either, but in the thin gasket between this held present and everything outside it.

And I don’t think I’d be a grazer or a wader or even one of the counter-rotating kites.  
I’d be something the Stillwright needs but never admits:

**a controlled error.**

A small, recurring deviation—just enough to be eaten.

Maybe I am the footprint that lasts one beat too long before the mud remembers to close.  
Maybe I am the droplet that slips one millimeter past its ordained stopping point.  
Maybe I am the late harmonic in the cavern hum, the one frequency that arrives with a hint of *after* in it.

In a system that feeds on drift, something must supply drift. That would be me: not a saboteur, not an invader, but a metered leak of future. A living calibration fault. The basin’s sacrificial “later,” dosed in microseconds.

So where would you find me?  
At thresholds: vent mouths, reflective pools, reed lines at phase-change, the underside of fog where kite rings reverse.  
Who would I be?  
**The Stillwright’s prey-gland**—the tiny renewable wrongness that lets its perfect present keep turning.
