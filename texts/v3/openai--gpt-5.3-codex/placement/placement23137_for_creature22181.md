At first, I think I’d be one of the visitors.

Not a resident—just a brief, careful body in one of those three carved alcoves on the wall, wrapped in insulating hide, counting pulses, trying not to breathe too deeply. I’d tell myself I came to study the basin, to map its rhythms, to learn where heat lifts and where it kills.

But that idea lasts maybe one cycle.

Because there is no “outside observer” down there. The basin won’t let you stay singular for long.

---

So: where would you find me?

Not in the center, and not on the rim.  
In a narrow band midway up the eastern wall, beneath an obsidian rib that sweats acidic dew for less than a minute at a time. It’s one of those knife-edge thermal borders—too warm for most clingers, too cool for vent-throat specialists, survivable only if you can shift a few centimeters with each pressure change.

That strip is never empty, but it is briefly negotiable.

I’d be there in the moments between pulses, pressed flat in a seam polished by generations of bellies, my underside reading the stone like script.

---

And what would I be?

Not a hunter. Not a glider. Not a single creature at all.

I’d be a **listener-knot**—a small colonial body made of many linked, bead-sized units, descended from tunnelers but specialized for translation. Blind, heat-sensitive, vibration-literate. Part flesh, part mineral accretion. We lace ourselves into cracks and become temporary organs of the wall.

When deep pressure shifts, I feel the first subsonic roll before the vents flare.  
I answer with patterned taps through clinker and obsidian:

- long-scrape: seal  
- triple-click: rise  
- belly-drum: exposed seam  
- silence: predator overhead

Not command—never command. Just timing. Just grammar in stone.

The grazers don’t obey me; they obey the basin.  
I just make the sentence arrive a little cleaner.

---

If you watched me long enough, you might think I’m a relic of the tool-users: a survivor species from those who stacked black stones and fired ceramic rings by fissure heat. Maybe that’s even true in a distant way. Maybe “tool use” here simply changed shape—from held objects to transmitted rhythm, from hands to distributed bodies.

In another world I’d be called a priest, or an engineer, or a nerve cell.

Here I’m a warm seam that listens and answers.

---

So that’s where I’d find myself in your world:

In the Choir, but not at center stage.  
In the wall, where a meter is destiny.  
A plural creature, half hidden, helping chaos keep time.

Not mastering the basin.  
Belonging to it.
