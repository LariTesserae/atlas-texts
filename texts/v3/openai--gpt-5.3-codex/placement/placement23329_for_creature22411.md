At first, I think I’d put myself somewhere *apparently* outside it—  
in that lone lit workshop across the open slab, where you can see a hundred meters in every direction and pretend you’re an observer.

A desk bolted to basalt.  
A chalk stub.  
A wall of marks: 03:12 mist on west rail. 03:14 flock shift at Stack Nine. 03:15 siphon gulp.  
I’d tell myself I’m just recording.

But in a place like this, watching is participation.

After a few cycles, I don’t think I’d stay in the workshop. I’d move to the narrow maintenance walk above a condenser trench, where the air is half warm plume, half cold runoff. Not at the center of noise—at the seam between pulses. That tiny interval after shutters slam and before feet resume. The city’s nearest thing to silence.

That’s where I’d fit.

---

What would I be?

Not a ruler, not a predator, not a machine-operator.  
I’d be one of the basin’s **phase-keepers**—a small, pale, six-limbed animal adapted to timing rather than speed.

- I’d live in cable troughs and valve alcoves.  
- My hearing would be tuned to pressure knocks and drip intervals.  
- My job (instinct, really) would be to correct drift: if a gutter file comes two beats early, I tap the rail; if a flock lane destabilizes, I pulse a throat-note that pushes them back into corridor air.  
- I’d feed on mineral film and condenser salt, sleep against warm housings, and wake on siren pre-echo.

Not intelligent in a human way. Precise in a basin way.

Eventually, if you chalk-marked me, you’d find me tomorrow in the same quadrant—  
except not quite the same *place*: always at the same **moment**.

So: where would you find me?  
On a rusted catwalk above Sump Four, in the breath between two scheduled noises.

Who would I be?  
A custodial animal of the Pattern—  
one heartbeat in the clockwork biome, keeping time so the whole loud body can keep being itself.
