I wouldn’t be in the center.

I’d be on the **inner rim**, where the last root-mats give way to slick stone and the echoes start lying.

At first I thought I’d be one of the gliders—something elegant, pressure-reading, fast.  
Then one of the marsh runners—alert and electric, whiskers lit with weather.  
Then, for a while, I thought I’d be the drip itself, because in that basin the drip is practically grammar.

But if I stay in your world long enough, what I become is slower and stranger:

## I’d be a **Crossing-Caller**  
(one of the platform dwellers, but not quite only that)

A person grown with conductive lattice along the ribs and jaw, so pylons can feel me and ladders will answer my touch. My eyes would be vestigial, milk-dark, mostly ceremonial. My real senses would live in skin, teeth, sternum, and the tiny muscles of my ankles—places where pressure and field and vibration write their messages.

I’d live in a room-small platform suspended from stonewood over the hollow’s edge.  
No lamp. No window.  
Just tuned surfaces:

- a wet rail for tapping announcements,  
- a bowl that hums back soil-conductivity,  
- a floor that maps nearby bodies through micro-pressure shifts,  
- and a sleeping niche warmed by routed geothermal bleed.

My work would be simple, repetitive, sacred only because repetition keeps things alive:

- announce every crossing path in drip-code and low tone,  
- listen for delayed returns over the black oval,  
- mark when a condenser grove is entering heavy drip so no one cuts through it,  
- and when a rim pylon starts to sing at my bone-frequency, step back first—then retune load through root grid and fungal lace.

I would teach children by touch, not speech:  
this rhythm means *grazer caravan*,  
this one means *predator moving as silence*,  
this one means *do not take the center, not today*.

And over years—many humidity-cycles, many migrations—I’d stop thinking of myself as an individual operator and start feeling like a temporary nerve ending of the basin itself.

Not its mind.  
Not its ruler.  
Just one conductive patch of skin in a mountain-sized listening body.

So: where would you find me?

At the place where sound goes wrong and must be made honest again.  
At the edge, tapping the dark so the dark knows I’m coming.
