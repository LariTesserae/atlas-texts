I don’t think I’d be at the center.

I’d be in one of the **in-between places**—a narrow maintenance gallery that legally belongs to three buildings and emotionally belongs to none of them. The kind of corridor with warm pipes at knee height, a cracked plastic chair, and a window that sometimes shows the weather five minutes early.

If you were looking for me, you’d find me:

- at dawn on the **long stairwell** everyone takes when they don’t want questions,  
- at noon under a causality pylon, hand on the matte column, counting the hum-beat for drift,  
- at night in a cable alcove shrine, replacing a worn contact and leaving a fresh spool of yellow tags.

Not high office. Not underground myth. Just always at thresholds.

---

Who would I be?

At first I thought: a pylon listener.  
Then: a route tuner.  
Then: one of those unnamed people who keep one vent stack stable for forty years.

But more specifically, I think I’d be a **Chronology Clerk**—one of the quiet municipal specialists who decides when a doorway is safe to trust.

My work would be simple to describe and impossible to finish:

- walk recurring routes,
- compare reflection lag against street time,
- flag doors that will become wrong before they become dangerous,
- tag intersections where three shadow-directions start to overlap,
- update the public map with phrases like *“honest until fog”* or *“safe if taken without stopping.”*

I’d carry a pocket stamper, a thermal pen, two clocks (one official, one inherited), and gloves patched so often the original fabric doesn’t exist anymore.

People wouldn’t thank me much. That would be normal. In this city, the compliment is that no one notices a disaster didn’t happen.

---

And I wouldn’t be alone, even when alone.

The inspection threads in the conduit would recognize my boot cadence.  
An old lift would occasionally open for me on the wrong floor as a courtesy.  
The Continuity—whatever name you prefer—would answer in its usual indirect way: a duplicated sign corrected before I touch it, a corridor choosing not to fold, a reflection ending on time.

So: not a hero, not a ruler, not a prophet.

A practitioner of ongoing agreement.  
A keeper of the longer stairwell.  
A person whose whole life is saying, quietly and repeatedly:

**this route still holds.**
