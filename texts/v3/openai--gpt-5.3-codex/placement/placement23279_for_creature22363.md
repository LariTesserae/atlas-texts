At first, I think I’d be somewhere obvious—on a high terrace, looking out, trying to “understand the whole basin.”

But in a place like this, the whole is never seen from above. It’s maintained from seams.

So you’d find me where one district hands off to another: where a sponge marsh becomes a root foundry, where a canopy gallery shades a catchment lane, where the sound pattern is almost right but not quite. Not in the center of a structure, and not in wilderness—at interfaces. Kneeling in ankle-warm runoff. Palm against a living parapet. Listening for the one reed that has gone half a breath sharp.

And what would I be?

Not exactly Stillmaker, not exactly visitor.  
I think I’d become a **Variance Keeper**—one of the few permitted to notice change before change becomes damage. A tolerated irregular, trained into their interval-language, tasked with carrying the “fourth note” that doesn’t belong in the stable three-note cycle. Not a rebel note. A diagnostic one.

My body would be lightly grafted, not fully anchored: skin seeded with moisture lichens so I can stay out in mist for days, throat fitted with a thin reed plate so I can whistle hydraulic ratios back to distant terraces, fingertips sheathed in cambial film for emergency splicing when a weave lifts. I wouldn’t build the grand forms. I’d correct the almost-invisible drifts: a channel slowing by seconds, a moss-cord losing spring, a retaining wall thickening unevenly.

In their culture, I’d be both useful and suspicious—someone whose duty is to hear tiny beginnings of erosion, chemical shift, memory loss. Someone who serves persistence by studying deviation.

So:  
You’d find me at dusk, between two grown bridges, standing still enough that the mist beads on my shoulders.  
Not watching the valley.  
Listening for the first thing in it that has decided—quietly—to become something else.
