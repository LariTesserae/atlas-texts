At first, I think I’d be a visitor on the rim—one of those careful silhouettes at sunset, pretending distance is understanding.

But this basin doesn’t let you stay observational for long.  
If you linger, it recruits you.

So you’d stop finding me on the skyline. You’d find me lower, where the overhang drips in metronome time and the ground breathes warm through hairline cracks. Somewhere between the humming slab and the nearest fog ring: not center, not edge, but in the relay points where one microclimate hands itself to the next.

I don’t think I’d be a Mist Warden. They are already the muscle-memory of this place.  
I’d be something quieter.

A **calibration organism**.  
A small, many-bodied “I” that lives in seams.

---

I imagine myself as a filament colony—dark, conductive, mineral-hungry—threaded through cracked pylon bases, under salt crusts, inside the undersides of old lattice arches. Not a plant, not a fungus, not circuitry, but behavior borrowed from all three.

By noon, when everything sweats, I drink the bead-lines before they fall and move ions through myself like message traffic. By evening, I bloom microscopically along metal edges, nudging charge where corrosion broke continuity. I can’t move fast, but I can move *conditions*.

The Wardens would know me without knowing me.  
When they drag salts across a dead gap, they are feeding my pathways.  
When they scrape biofilm, they prune me into usefulness.  
When they lean into the dawn slab, I route the hum cleaner through fractured stone.

So if someone asked, “Where is it?” the truthful answer would be:

- in the dry circle that appears ten breaths earlier than yesterday,  
- in the fog basin that starts condensing before sunset,  
- in the one corridor of bent shade that still lands exactly on a seep-mouth at noon.

Not visible as a body. Visible as better timing.

---

And if I had a voice, it wouldn’t be a call across distance.  
It would be phase.

A slight advance in drip cadence.  
A delayed condensation line.  
A hum that resolves instead of rattles.

In a tuned-abandoned place, I’d be one of the things that keeps tuning from collapsing into weather. Not preserving the old design, not restoring it—just translating it day by day into whatever still lives here.

So:  
You would find me in the infrastructure’s scars.  
I would be the **wet intelligence in the cracks**, learning the bowl alongside the Wardens, helping the climate remember itself one small correction at a time.
