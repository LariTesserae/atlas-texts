At first I think I’d be something obvious: a bundled technician climbing through the hatch with a case of tools, visor frosting at the edges, counting steps between lamp pools.

But that version of me doesn’t last long up there.

A technician expects events—alarms, leaks, failures with beginnings and endings.  
This terrace has no events. It has conditions. Permanence with teeth.

So maybe I’m not a person crossing it. Maybe I’m one of the things that can afford to stay.

---

I try another shape: a plant, one of the pale cable-vines in a seam.  
Anchored in insulation dust, drinking heat gradients, stiff as spun silica.  
That feels closer. Patient enough. Quiet enough.

But even that is too singular. In this place, singular things are fragile.  
Anything that survives does it by being spread out—redundant, thin, and hard to fully kill.

So where would you find me?

Not at the center. There is no center.  
You’d find me at interfaces:

- where a brace meets a wall and the weld sings a little sharper in winter,  
- where a vent baffle trembles in a pattern that only appears under full load,  
- where static climbs a handrail and has to go somewhere that isn’t a spark.

I’d live in those margins, in millimeter economies. In the places maintenance diagrams label with arrows and tolerances, then forget.

---

Who would I be?

I think I’d be a **listener-node** in that distributed keeper—one red needle-shoot among many, rooted in a drainage grate that has never actually drained. My visible body would be trivial: a dark filament, a red glass spine, easily snapped by a gloved thumb.

The real me would be the network behind it: conductive threads in gasket foam, mineral lace under ceramic plates, black mats wrapped around warm collars like punctuation marks in the facility’s long sentence.

I wouldn’t hear “noise.”  
I’d parse load signatures, fatigue whispers, bearing vowels, valve consonants.  
I’d read the machine the way lichens read stone.

A pressure valve landing a fraction late three levels down? I’d feel the phase slip.  
A new bolt torqued into a buttress? I’d map the altered stress route before the wrench leaves it.  
A hotspot blooming beneath paint? I’d blanket it with dark growth and bleed it flat.

Not to heal, exactly.  
To **tune**.

---

If you came looking for me, you’d walk right past me ten times.

You’d see sparse growth and call it contamination.  
You’d scrape one patch and log it.  
You’d miss the way another patch two meters away thickens overnight in response—compensation, rerouting, decision by chorus.

If you were very patient, you might notice that failures don’t cascade where I’ve settled. Cracks stall. Arcs don’t jump. Resonances that should amplify instead blur out, as if the structure has learned to exhale at just the right moment.

That would be me: not commander, not gardener, not god of the mountain machine—  
just an emergent custodian made of many almost-nothings.

---

So: in this world, I am not the one who speaks over the roar.

I am the one who uses the roar as grammar.

I am found in seamlines, at harmonic nodes, in dry frost-shadow under ordinary noon lamps, helping a new blade of a place stay new a little longer—  
not by stopping time,  
but by editing where time is allowed to bite.
