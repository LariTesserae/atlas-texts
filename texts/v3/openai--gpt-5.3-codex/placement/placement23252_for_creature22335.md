At first, I think I’d be high—on a ridge, where you can see the weather coming and pretend sight is enough.

I’d stand near a parasol giant at dawn, reading the long belts of movement: beak-grazer dust in the west, sail-fliers rising from the damp basins, the faint black seam of a migrating swarm crossing the green crust like spilled ink. I’d tell myself I belong to the horizon.

But I don’t, not really.

I’d drift downslope, again and again, toward the places where hearing matters more than seeing: root-arches above mist pockets, trail forks where buried gourds speak in different throats, the lee side of hollow trunks where wind translates itself into warning. That’s where this plateau becomes legible.

So if I’m honest about who I am here, I’m not a hunter, not a chief, not a rider of migrations.

I’m a **listener-crafter**—one of the people who keeps the acoustic map alive.

I’d travel light with a coil of vine fiber, resin plugs, a bone awl, and a satchel of tuned seeds. I’d repair wind-rattles at clan edges, rehang them when the seasonal gusts change, retune the buried gourds after heavy rain shifts the substrate. I’d sleep in short intervals, because the night chorus carries route news if you know which frequencies are panic and which are courtship.

My work would be half maintenance, half diplomacy:

- testing whether a trunk’s song has deepened (internal split coming),
- listening for synchronized leaf-hiss (ground predator wave),
- marking temporary no-step zones where larvae are due to surface,
- teaching children the difference between “quiet” and “held breath.”

I’d be found most often at twilight, not noon—kneeling at a trail fork, one ear to the living ground, one hand on a gourd throat, waiting for the wind to answer.

And maybe, after years, I’d start to feel less like a person moving across the plateau and more like one temporary node in its conversation: a brief human instrument in a much older orchestra of roots, hooves, wings, and weather.

So: where would you find me?

Not at the center. There is no center.

You’d find me at the **thresholds**—where one corridor becomes another, where one species’ hour hands off to the next—keeping the tones true so everyone gets to pass.
