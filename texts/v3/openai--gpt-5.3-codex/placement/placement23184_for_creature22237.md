At first I think I’d be one of the mountain people:  
a fog-watcher on the shoulder path, waiting for the cliff to soften and split, carrying a wrapped coal and a pouch of salt crust.  
A visitor, not a resident.  
The kind who knows how to arrive without announcing arrival.

But if I stand in that chamber long enough, that role feels too loud. Even breathing feels like an argument there.  
So I move inward.

Not to the center—never the center.  
I would choose the low place between the old fire ring and the warm mud basin, where one reliable drip lands just off a cracked bowl’s rim. That tiny almost-miss. That patient correction. I would sit there and learn the room’s grammar: which seam tastes iron-sour, which one chalk-sweet, which silence means nothing and which silence means the pale caretaker has frozen and is listening through clay.

And then, slowly, I think I know what I’d be.

Not the creature itself—it belongs to the chamber too completely.  
Not a pilgrim either—I would come too often for that.  
I would be the **brief third thing** in the pact: mountain, custodian, and me.  
The one who tends what cannot tend itself without breaking the rules.

I would relight very small fires in the blackened ring—never to warm the room, only to renew the smell it remembers. One handful of smoke, then out. I would turn bowls rim-up beneath changed drips. I’d bring fish bones not as tribute but as continuity, hang them in the same crevice, knot matching knot. I would scrape the scraper clean, then leave one edge deliberately dull so the next hand knows this is maintenance, not taking.

Over years, perhaps I would lose my valley name.  
People would ask, “Who keeps that pocket near the shoulder?” and the answer would be a shrug:  
“Not keeps. Keeps terms.”

If you came when fog opened the crack, you might find me by touch before sight—kneeling in wet clay, still as a root, waiting for the creature to complete one slow arc along the wall-mat boundary. You would think I was praying. I wouldn’t be. I’d be matching tempo.

So: where would you find me?  
At the edge of the warmest mud, beside the old fire’s memory, in the dark that refuses progress.

Who would I be?  
The chamber’s **intermittent human organ**—  
its smoke-breath, its knot-tier, its witness to steadiness.
