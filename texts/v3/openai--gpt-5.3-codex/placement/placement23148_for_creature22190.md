I don’t think I’d live in the open shine, even though that’s what defines the world.

At first, I’d climb—some condenser scaffold, a maintenance spine, anything that gives me ten extra meters—and stare until the pattern stopped being beautiful and started being legible. After that, I’d come down and never chase height again.

You’d find me at seams.

Not the grand channels or mirror basins, but the places where one module replaced another: old ceramic meeting new polymer, a weeping wall feeding a rail trough, a vent colonnade’s dripline where salts crust and get licked clean by tiny mouths by dawn. The world’s equilibrium is perfect in bulk, but locally it is always negotiating. Millimeters. Seconds. One species arriving early, another late. I’d live in that negotiation.

And I don’t think I’d be human for long.

I’d be one of the **seamwardens**—a long, low amphibious crawler descended from inspection stock, with lamp-organs along my flanks and a whiskered face built to read pressure differentials in thin films of water. Part eel, part arthropod, part instrument. My “eyes” would be better at seeing conductivity than shape. I’d taste pH the way others taste weather.

My work (and hunger) would be the same thing:  
- scrape mineral bloom before it hardens into blockage,  
- nip parasite mats off glider roost cables,  
- pulse light into culvert mouths to turn ribbon schools before pump reversals,  
- feed on the protein froth that gathers where warm outflow meets cool mistfall.

Custody would know me, but not own me completely. I’d carry a tag in my spine; geofences would tug at my nerves like distant static. Mostly I’d obey. Sometimes I’d linger just outside preferred bands, where an unplanned eddy keeps a little republic of microbes alive that *shouldn’t* be there but harms nothing. I’d learn where the system tolerates mercy.

I wouldn’t be a ruler, predator, or rebel.  
I’d be a correction with a heartbeat.

So: where would you find me? In the low gleam, under the hush, where infrastructure pretends to be landscape and life pretends to be maintenance.

Who would I be?  
A native of the in-between—an organism shaped like a protocol, slowly becoming an animal.
