At first, I think you’d find me on the highest passable thing—one of the roof-bridges at dawn, crouched where smoke thins and mist hasn’t decided whether to rise or fall.  
Not out of courage. Out of panic. I’d need one place where I could see the pattern before trusting my feet inside it.

I’d stay there long enough to learn the town’s breathing:  
the runnels flattening silver at first light,  
the brown pull by midday,  
the evening lift when tide and seep agree.  
I’d learn that “stillness” here is only very small motion repeated faithfully.

Then I’d come down.

Not to a grand role. To a necessary one: I think I would become a **seam-walker**, one of the people who move the middle web and read stress before it becomes collapse. The ones who don’t own much except a route, a mallet, a coil of tarred cord, wedges in a belt, and a reputation for hearing trouble early. I’d be the person others stop to ask, “How’s the south bank speaking today?”

My work would be half listening, half persuasion.  
Tap a post and hear if it rings, thuds, or swallows the sound.  
Press a plank with my heel and feel whether the give is wetness or hunger.  
Cut weight from one loft, add binding to another, reroute traffic before sunset.  
Never “fix” anything forever—just keep failure from synchronizing.

I think I’d sleep high, cook mid-level, and keep my tools where I can grab them in the dark. I’d own almost nothing decorative except one shell bead tied above my sleeping mat where the first condensation forms each morning. If the bead stays dry too long, I’d know something in the air has shifted before anyone says it aloud.

And after years, if I’m lucky, I’d become one of those old people children follow because I can hear through wood. Not a chief, not a priest—more like a tuning fork for the settlement. I’d teach them that the rules aren’t commandments; they’re joints in a living body. Block a runnel, and the body limps. Sink a hearth too low, and the body coughs steam and rot.

So where would you find me in this world?

On a narrow walkway above slow water, palm on a damp post, head tilted.  
Listening for the moment the town changes key.

And who would I be?

A person, yes—but mostly a practiced response.  
One small organ in the basin’s larger animal.
