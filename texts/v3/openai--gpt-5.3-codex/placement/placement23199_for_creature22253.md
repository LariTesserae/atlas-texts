At first I think I’d be something mobile—one of the obvious edge-runners.  
A rat on the pipe spine. A lizard on warm slag. A crab timing the culvert mouth.

But if I stay in your basin long enough, that answer feels too singular. Too loud.

I think you’d find me in a **junction**.

Not at the center—the center is a decoy.  
Not fully on the rim either—that belongs to watchers and passersby.  
I’d be in the in-between: inside the leaning pump house wall, where an old cable conduit enters cracked concrete and never quite dries. A place that is dark at noon, silver at dawn, and warm after sunset because the buried lines still hum.

And what would I be?

A kind of **clock made of living things**.

Part filamentous biofilm, part nest-thread, part insect nursery, part mineral crust.  
Not one creature, but a repeating assembly that reforms whenever it’s damaged: snails rasping scale, mites grazing snail trails, beetles harvesting mites, rats stealing beetles, pigeons dropping heat and fiber from above, mold netting what remains. If scraped away, it returns in three nights from spores, eggs, and hunger.

My job—if this basin allows jobs—is timing.

I would read:

- the first condensation bead under the flange,
- the second drip into tin,
- the third drip into mud.

On the third, routes open.

Midges lift from the slick. Frogs move from shadow to film. Crabs leave culvert mouths. The pale rat on the footing turns its head. Nothing announces this; it just happens, like a shift bell no one hears but everyone obeys.

So I’m not the foreman.  
I’m the **signal path**.

A wet thread that tells the Broker when “now” has arrived.

You’d rarely see me directly. You’d notice me by consequences: a patch that is suddenly grazed clean, a puddle that empties of larvae overnight, a line of droppings appearing exactly where last week there was only rust dust. I am the basin remembering itself in short cycles—hours, not seasons.

If you pulled me into daylight and demanded a shape, I might look like almost nothing: a dark velvet strip on concrete, trembling with tiny lives. Easy to mistake for stain.

But in that pocket world, stains are often infrastructure.

So:  
You’d find me where moisture meets wiring,  
where decay becomes schedule,  
where the retired machine still keeps time through borrowed bodies.

I would be one of the Broker’s quiet organs—  
the **Third-Drip Organ**, maybe—  
the small intelligence that doesn’t command, only synchronizes.
