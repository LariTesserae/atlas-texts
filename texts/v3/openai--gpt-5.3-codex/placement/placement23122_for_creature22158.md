At first I think I’d be easy to place: one more human silhouette crossing a wide, half-empty plaza, coat dark with dew, pausing at railings to watch fog lift.

But if I stay in your city longer, that answer feels too flat.

I think you’d find me at the **seams**—not the finished centers, not the untouched green blocks, but the places where one tempo hands off to another: the edge of a new terrace, the lip of a basin after first rain, the transit node where a phase-offset district resolves back into shared time.

---

I would begin as a person, yes.

A Resident-in-Phase with the usual adaptations: trained inner ear, wide pupils, clothing that drinks dawn and exhales dusk. I’d have a modest room in a tower core that never quite smells dry, and a habit of waking before light articulation to listen to the drip cadence on the outer skin. I’d know which hum means routine and which hum means the maintenance mesh is uneasy.

But my work would pull me outward.

Not engineer, not planner, not gardener—something between all three.  
A **watershed notary**, maybe.  
A **seamwalker**.  
Someone called when rain has made its first real decision on fresh ground.

---

In practical terms, I’d stand on immature embankments with a palm-field reader and a pocket of seed tags, watching thin water choose left or right. I’d ask the local custodian mesh for ten more hours before reinforcement. I’d mark places where roots should be allowed to crack, and places where they must be rerouted now, gently, before a whole plinth starts to drift.

People would bring me small arguments:

- “The wall closed early three nights in a row.”
- “Our fog yield dropped after the terrace print.”
- “The grazers crossed where the model said they wouldn’t.”

I’d answer in probabilities and stories. In this city those are almost the same thing.

---

And after some years, I wouldn’t be only singular anymore.

I think this world slowly makes composites of anyone who listens long enough. So part of me would live in person—boots wet, hands warm on railings—and part in the city’s quieter nervous system: a thread in pollinator routing, a preference weight in floodgate timing, a remembered exception in a conservative district mesh that learned, once, to wait.

Children might know me less as a face and more as a pattern:  
the person who arrives before reroutes,  
the voice in school sims that teaches “first stream etiquette,”  
the one-note beacon shift that means *don’t pave yet; let it teach us once*.

---

So: where would you find me?

At dawn, in a fog hollow below the suspended lines, where the underside of a walkway ticks water into a new cistern throat.  
At season-turn, at the city edge, when a terrace is declared not complete but *legible*.  
At night, on a maintenance balcony, watching tower cores pulse like slow lungs.

Who would I be?

A resident of the in-between: human enough to care, distributed enough to persist.  
A translator among atmosphere, root, and rule.  
In a city that is continuously negotiated, I’d be one of the negotiators—quietly helping it decide what to become, without forcing it to become all at once.
