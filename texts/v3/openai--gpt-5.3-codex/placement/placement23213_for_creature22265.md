At first I think I’d be on the rim.

Kneeling on that black, wet lip of basalt, rain needling my neck, trying to map patterns by ear: the hiss-slap at the surface, the click-banks below, the long subsonic groan that makes the stone under my palms feel briefly alive.  
A watcher. A note-taker. A mammal with lungs and bad luck.

But that’s only where I’d begin, not where I belong.

---

If I stayed, I think the basin would push me downward—out of the “observer” role and into the traffic of it. Not into the fast layer; I’m wrong for speed. Not into the deep Listener; too singular, too sovereign for what this place seems to value.

I would be something in between, something that *keeps time*.

I’d be a colony-animal, probably mistaken for rock at first glance: a narrow seam of soft-bodied units living in the cracks just above the main valve beds, where cold rainwater fingers down and vent heat rises up.  
A **phase-keeper**.

Each individual in me would be small, blind, and almost stupid alone—just a ciliated tube with a mineral ring and a pressure sac. But linked in long braided chains through basalt fissures, we’d act like one distributed instrument. We wouldn’t pump as hard as the Valve Beds and we wouldn’t jet like the Looms. We’d do something quieter:

- sample pressure gradients,
- pass timing pulses along our chain,
- delay or advance neighboring bed rhythms by fractions,
- smooth the transition when the basin shifts state.

Not a conductor. More like a clutch plate.

When the Listener sends up one of those organ-pipe notes, we’d feel it first in our mineral rings. The signal would run through us as a wave of contractions—tick-tick-tick in damp stone channels—and then out into the larger colonies as altered phase. A bed that would have opened now waits three heartbeats. Another opens early. A surge that might have become destructive turns into throughput.

I think that’s what I’d be: not power, not predation—**synchrony**.

---

You’d find me in the steep middle margins, maybe 12 to 20 meters down, where the wall is too sheer for heavy grazers and too structured for open swimmers. In daylight I’d look like pale stitching in black rock, interrupted by iron stains. At night rain, when surface pressure changes fastest, I’d be busiest, passing timing along kilometers of hidden crackspace while turners clatter overhead.

I’d feed on what everything else leaks: bacterial threads, dissolved organics, shed mucus from glass sacks, the dead skin of larger systems. Waste eater, signal maker.  
Industrial in the most biological way.

Predators would tear sections of me free. Doesn’t matter. The chain routes around damage. New polyps bud from protected nodes. Memory isn’t stored in a brain—it’s stored in delays, in the habitual lag between one contraction and the next. The basin teaches us its meter by breaking us and seeing what rhythm grows back.

---

So: where would I be?

Not on the rim for long.  
Not at the center.  
In the cracks, in the relay zones, in the wet dark between vent and rain.

Who would I be?

A many-bodied timing animal in the Choir of the Works—one of the things that makes the drowned engine run smoothly enough to remain alive.
