I don’t think I’d arrive there as a single creature right away.

At first I’d be the obvious thing: a watcher crouched in the bright damp, trying to pick one life to follow. I’d trail the porcelain salamander, then lose it to repetition. I’d track the shell-grazer’s arc, then realize I’d already seen that exact arc ten minutes ago—or ten years ago, same difference here.

Then I’d drift lower in scale.  
Not salamander, not cricket.  
Maybe one of the mineral cups under a drip, holding one bead of water at a time. Maybe a mica fleck in the ceiling, turning one constant light into a hundred constant lights. Something small, structural, necessary, and almost never noticed.

But if I stayed long enough to belong, I think I’d become the chamber’s **phase-keeper**—one of the hidden correction organs of the loop.

You’d find me where three rootlets stop above a stub of translucent flowstone, near a pebble collar that always looks newly made. I’d have a body, technically: pale, soft, blind, many-limbed, easy to mistake for just another cave thing. I’d wake only during condensation pulses. While everything else performs its perfect recurrence, I’d make tiny edits: nudge a shell half a grain-width, clear silt from a drip lip, rotate a bone ring by the smallest possible angle, press a wandering worm back into the old track.

Not to change the system—to prevent change from accumulating.  
Not a ruler, not a maker. A tuner.

So: where am I? In the busiest corner that looks inert.  
What am I? The maintenance instinct of the metacreature, wearing a disposable body.  
The hand that keeps the held breath from becoming an exhale.
