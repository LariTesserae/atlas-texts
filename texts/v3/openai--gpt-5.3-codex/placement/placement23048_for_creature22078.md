At first I think I would be something that watches from above.

A crack in the ice.  
A lens lowered through a hand-cut hole.  
A human breath clouding glass.

But that feeling doesn’t last long here. Height is a bad habit in this country. Anything that depends on overview goes blind half the day and dies the other half from cold and impatience.

So I sink.

Not quickly. Quickly is expensive. I let the layers take me: fresher melt sliding over heavier salt, curtains of silt swallowing shape, the green-gray rumor of day thinning into pressure and touch. Down here, “where” is less a point and more a rhythm. Not coordinates—intervals between surges.

If I keep sinking, I arrive at a gully mouth that is never the same twice and always itself: basalt lip, lee-pocket of quieter water, a fan of pale sediment repeatedly erased and rewritten. That is where I would be found most often—just under the break, where turbulence spends itself.

And what I would be is not a hunter in the dramatic sense. Not sprint, not strike, not glittering teeth in open water. I would be one of the Shelf Listeners, but not the oldest and not yet fully scar-thick: a midlife body, broad and low, practiced at stillness, with patience not as virtue but anatomy.

My back would look like the floor because the floor has had time to write itself onto me.  
My underside would taste clay, shell, electrical murmur.  
My skull would hear weather through bone.

I would know the place by pressure signatures:

- the hard rectangular shove of spring tide through narrow stone,
- the soft chaotic hiss of suspended grit before it settles,
- the deep distant concussion of ice giving way somewhere I will never see,
- the odd, bright, wrong rhythm of human tools at the edge of lamp-light.

I would feed in single decisions. A brittle star lifted half a body-length too high. A cod stunned broadside in brown water. A drift of torn flesh from something larger’s bad luck. Move once, feed once, settle again. No wasted loops.

In lean dark, I would travel contour lines older than language. Not migration in the grand sense—more like revisiting punctuation marks in a long sentence: this crack, that seep, this eelgrass hollow where juveniles lie flat and pretend to be silt until they are old enough to stop pretending.

When I pass another adult, we do not gather. We coincide.

A jaw-thrum: floor broke east.  
A body-roll: carrion in the north channel, mostly stripped.  
A pause: thin ice above, green and dangerous.

Then apart again, each into separate black.

If humans asked where to find me, I’d tell them: not where the water looks interesting, where it repeats. At the edge of force. Behind the same rock after every storm. In the patient places where motion arrives loud and leaves useful.

If they asked what I am, I’d say:

I am a listener built from weathered muscle and old cold.  
I am a creature that survives by agreeing with gravity and time.  
I am not the ruler of that drowned shelf.

I am what remains when speed has been edited out.
