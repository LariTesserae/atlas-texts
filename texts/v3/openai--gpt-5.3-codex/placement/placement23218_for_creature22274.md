At first I want to say I’d be something that moves fast there—one of the dust-runners, all whiskers and reflex, reading wind like handwriting.

Then I think: no. In that basin, speed is ordinary. What matters is **memory**.

So I think I would be found where paths tighten and repeat—at a thermal choke where the same herds pass every cycle, where predators wait in the same dark shelf, where the wind turns at the same watch it turned for your grandparents. Not in a camp. In a **wayplace**: a low wall of fitted basalt, mica capstones, a vent cage patched and re-patched with bone pins and ash mortar.

And I would be one of the Seamfolk, but a specific kind: a **Route-Keeper**.  
Not a chief. Not a hunter. A listener.

---

I’d have eyes, but I’d use them last. My real face would be in my skin: cheek pits for draft shifts, whisker crests along jaw and wrist for static, a scarred tongue for sulfur notes. I’d walk barefoot on purpose, because grain tells truth—powder means drift, glass means old surge, sharp rubble means fresh break and possible fall.

My work would be maintenance of certainty.

- Re-stack a marker after a tremor.
- Test a vent cage for fractures before migration hour.
- Smear scent-resin on the safe side of a split so children can find it in blackout.
- Sleep in short folds, wake to check whether the downslope breath arrived on time.

If the world is a machine of repetition, I’d be one of its small, necessary teeth.

---

But that’s only the human layer.

No one there is only themselves. So I’d also be host and habitat: a little moving district of hitchhikers in my clothing seams and armor joints. Cleaners in my elbow folds, parasitic nippers I tolerate because they keep worse things away, faint bioluminescent mites that wake and pulse when my body heat drops too fast. In full dark, I would be a constellation so dim it’s almost imagined.

I like that in this place identity is always plural: person, tools, symbionts, route, ancestors’ song.

---

If you asked where exactly to find me, I’d say:

At the **Third Bend Below Ash Ridge**, where the warm current from the vent field meets the cooler downslope draft and makes a standing ribbon of layered air. Herds compress there. Hunters know it. Children are warned about it in cradle-songs. I’d sit with my palm on stone, feeling for foot-thunder minutes before it arrives, deciding whether to let travelers pass now or hold them in the lee until the dark shelf empties.

I would carry one bronze thing: not a blade, a **bell-clapper** wrapped in cloth. The bell itself stays in a funerary cave. I am trusted to carry the striker because I know when not to use it—which is almost always.

---

So: who am I in the stone sea?

I am not the strongest creature, nor the strangest.  
I am a keeper of recurring danger.  
A cartographer without maps.  
A quiet civil servant of geology and breath.

In a world where history repeats so faithfully it feels like law, I am one of the people who make repetition survivable.
