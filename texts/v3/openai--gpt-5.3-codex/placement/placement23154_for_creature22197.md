At first I think I’d be something with eyes above water—  
a rim-walker, maybe, standing on black glass rock and watching weather collect in the basin.

But that feels too far away for this place.  
Everything important happens in contact: root on stone, pollen on film, warm layer on cool layer.  
So I move down.

Next I imagine being one of the broad-winged circlers, reading thermals and death from high air.  
Then one of the translucent fish, fluent in gradients so fine they’re almost thoughts.  
Then a grazer, pushing lanes through submerged fern and peat, my body a moving tunnel of light.

None of those quite fit either. They belong here, but they are still individuals in a world that is mostly relationship.

So if I am honest: I would be found where the floating meadows fray into open channels—  
in the half-shade beneath them, at the depth where warm water slips over mineral-cool and the boundary feels like silk.

And I would be a **knot-being** of the lattice itself.

Not a single creature, but a recurring form:  
a dense, hand-sized node of succulent stem, root filaments, microbial sheen, and buoyant chambers,  
one of thousands that can separate, drift, rejoin, and signal by chemistry and pressure pulse.  
When mats tear in storm-rain, we are the first stitches.  
When channels narrow, we thicken the edges.  
When a new seep warms a cove, we are the scouts that survive both sulfur and sun long enough for others to follow.

I would not “think” quickly.  
I would remember by persistence: salts held in tissue, scars in fiber, spores cached at the right depth for the next fractional cooling.  
My decisions would look like patience to animals and inevitability to stone.

You’d find me by touch before sight:  
a place where your hand expects loose roots and meets surprising tension,  
a living seam that gives, then holds.  
By day I’d be green-brown, unremarkable.  
At night, when disturbed, the invertebrate blue would catch on my films and map me in brief cold fire.

So: in that suspended sea, I am not the rare animal crossing it.  
I am one of the quiet joints that let it keep becoming itself—  
a small intelligence distributed through drift, heat, and time,  
helping the drowned mountain decide, slowly, what shoreline means.
