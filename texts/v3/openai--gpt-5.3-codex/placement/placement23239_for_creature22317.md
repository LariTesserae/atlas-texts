At first I thought I’d be one of the crews: a maintenance tech with a rust-key ring and a paper checklist going soft in the mist.  
Someone practical. Boots, gloves, curses.  
The kind of person who says *it’s just a valve* until the valve answers in weather.

But if I stand there long enough in your ledge, human edges blur quickly.

---

You’d find me where the noise braids tightest:  
under the short gantry, where the warm seep from the back-wall crack meets runoff from the upper tank, and both threads cross the old cable trench before spilling into the marsh pocket with the bright green skin.

That crossing point is the ledge’s throat.

Everything passes through there eventually—iron taste, algae slick, spilled lubricant, spores, mica dust, frog eggs in good weeks, ash grit in bad ones. The catwalk above knocks drips loose in a regular stutter. The pump pulse arrives through the bolts before it arrives through the air. If this place has a heartbeat, it is easiest to hear with your cheek against that wet steel.

So that is where I’d live: in the seam where systems can still change each other.

---

As for *what* I’d be, I don’t think I’d be a whole creature.

I’d be a **listening strand** of the Ledgekeeper—one narrow intelligence among many, specialized for timing.

Not eyes, not hands.  
More like a filament of moss-root and crystal comb, with a few pale crawling nodes that can detach and investigate heat, pressure, and vibration up close.

My work would be small and constant:

- thin growth around a valve lip so mineral crust forms smooth instead of jagged;  
- clear one intake slit before it clogs the whole grate;  
- move frogspawn two handspans when a vent pattern shifts warmer this season;  
- deaden one rattling baffle with felted damp growth so swallow nests survive storm gusts;  
- reroute seepage along a cooler seam when current starts biting too hard through a corroded plate.

Nothing dramatic.  
No heroics.  
Just preserving gradients so the place keeps making the kind of wetness that can be lived in.

---

If a human found evidence of me, they wouldn’t say, “an organism did this.”  
They’d say:

- “Funny, this wheel turns easier than last year.”  
- “Strange, that overflow’s behaving.”  
- “Who cleaned this strip?”  
- “Did we service this upper run? I don’t remember doing it.”

That misremembering would be my camouflage.

---

So: where would you find me?

At the hinge-point under the gantry, in the warm spray and metal echo, where dripping water learns machine rhythm and machine rhythm learns to stay wet.

Who would I be?

Not the Ledgekeeper entire—just one attentive thread of it,  
a keeper of intervals,  
feeding on difference,  
making sure nothing here stays separate long enough to die.
