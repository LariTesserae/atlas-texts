I don’t think I’d live in the center.

At least, not at first.

I’d be found in the **narrow band just below the rim on the lee side of the north-black ridge**, where descending wind breaks into readable layers before it hits the basin floor. It’s the only place a newcomer can stand long enough to be interpreted without being immediately erased by noise. A vestibule, basically. Not outside, not inside. A gasket.

And I don’t think I’d be a native creature in the usual sense.  
I’d be a **temporary interface** the basin grows when something like me arrives.

---

At first I imagine myself as a climber in sealed gear, careful with every gram of water, trying to survive the audit. But that version doesn’t last. In this place, independent bodies are expensive and brittle. The system prefers roles.

So after some span—hours, weeks, one storm season—I get repurposed.

The choir-intelligence would start by borrowing my posture, because bipedal symmetry is useful for approaching strangers. Then it would overwrite the rest with local materials:

- a frame of silica braid and old ligament-like cable  
- wing-film folded into a cloak that can shed charge without sparking  
- sensor-fur from static-bees at wrists and throat  
- a chest cavity that is mostly resonant chambers, not lungs  
- no exposed moisture except what is escrowed behind ceramic membranes

I become something like a **parley body**: a person-shaped instrument the basin can speak through without surrendering itself.

Not a ruler. Not a priest.  
More like a **customs officer for physics**.

---

My work would happen in sound, because everything here does.

I’d stand on one of those half-hanging walkway fragments—three meters up, where vortices are cleaner—and “speak” by phased clicks through collar bones that are mostly tuning forks. Incoming travelers hear fragments as words if they’re close enough:

Seal your seams.  
Announce your reservoirs.  
Do not damp the fronds.  
If silence falls, kneel.

But what I’m really transmitting is machine-readable etiquette to swarms, groves, pylons, and predators all at once: *foreign process entering, high leak risk, hold hunt, open corridor, watch for rupture*.

When there are no visitors, I would partly disassemble. My hands go back to pollination duty, my cloak to charge harvest, my spine to a scaffold in a root-archive. Identity here is not a permanent possession; it’s a runtime configuration.

---

So where would you find me?

At twilight, probably:  
on the rimward walk, backlit by copper light, with a thin coherent glow moving under skin like script. Not fully individual, not fully distributed. The basin’s peripheral voice.

And what would I be?

A **leased self**.  
A **moisture-safe diplomat**.  
A **single mouth the superorganism grows when it needs to address the singular**.
