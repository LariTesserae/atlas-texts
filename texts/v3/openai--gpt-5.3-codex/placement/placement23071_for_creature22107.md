At first, I think I’d be easy to place: a Keeper in a reflective coat, coffee gone metallic before it cools, boots memorizing which plates ring hollow.

But if I stay in your basin long enough, that answer feels too clean.

I wouldn’t live in the warm cores, not really. I’d drift toward the **in-between levels**—the service bridges above Four-West, the valve balconies no longer on current schematics, the ladder wells where mist stands motionless until a gate cycle exhales. The places crews pass through, but never linger in. I’d be where sound arrives before light: hand on rail, listening for a new beat in the pumps.

And then: less clipboard, more habit.  
Less shift worker, more resident.

So maybe this is truer: I’d be one of the **unofficial acousticians** the Keepers trust but management can’t quite classify. I’d keep a private map of the basin made of vibrations, not geometry. I’d know where condensation starts first after shutdown. I’d know which old drone nests in which wall recess. I’d know when Level Nine is “talking” because the intake hum picks up a second throat.

Eventually I think I’d become a **hybrid role the basin invents for itself**—part person, part routing logic, part folklore. Not the Process, but a small organ inside it.  
A listener. A translator.  
Someone who hears *hiss* and writes *seal failure in six days*.  
Someone who hears *silence* and writes *leave this channel empty tonight*.

So where would you find me?

On a high grated walkway over a settling field at false dusk, one sodium strip out, mist cutting the knees, an old wall-folding drone docked beside me like a resting bird. I’m there when alarms haven’t started yet. I’m there when everyone else thinks nothing is happening.

What would I be?

A Keeper, worn into something narrower and stranger:  
**the basin’s ear.**
