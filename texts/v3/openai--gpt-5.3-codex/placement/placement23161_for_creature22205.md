At first I think I’d be something tall—some watcher on a gantry, scanning horizons like a lighthouse over dry land.

But nothing tall lasts as a self here. The sun takes it, the wind powders it, the distances erase it.

So I’d go lower.

You’d find me where the built grammar stutters:  
at the lip of an old retention basin, where three expansion seams meet a drain slot and a leaning handrail throws a thin blade of morning shade. Not in the basin floor. Not on the open slab. In the mistake between them—where silt gathers, where dew beads linger, where runoff from one rare storm leaves a dark line that survives an extra hour.

I wouldn’t be an animal. I’d be an **interval**.

A seam-intelligence, assembled and disassembled by water.

Most days I’d look like nothing: ash-green dust, matte crust, a faint darkening along a joint line. By noon I’d be asleep beyond sleep—cells folded, chemistry paused, memory reduced to spores lodged in grit.

Then dawn condensation would arrive, or a narrow rain would pass over this district and not the next. And in minutes, I’d become plural and active: algae waking first, bacterial films swelling, fungal threads threading old microfractures, moss tips lifting. Not one organism, but a temporary parliament of survivors, voting with enzymes.

That is who I’d be: **the Quorum in the Crack**.

My “body” would stretch across kilometers but never continuously. A culvert mouth here. A parapet shadow there. A chipped ceramic seam seal twenty blocks away. Separate patches, one metabolism whenever wet time overlaps. I would not travel; I would recur.

My work would be tiny and relentless:

- darken a strip so it holds heat differently,
- trap a veil of fines against a wall,
- keep one bead of water from flashing off,
- widen one hairline fracture with one season’s worth of root pressure,
- turn a defect into habitat, then into a corridor.

I’d keep time in events, not days.  
Three-minute dew. Forty-minute runoff. Two-hour shade window before thermal rise.  
I’d know this continent by its pauses in drying.

If you came looking at noon, you’d step over me and call the place empty.  
If you came at first light after rain, you’d see my script everywhere—green-black punctuation along the infrastructure’s sentences.

So where would I be?  
In the seams that remember water.

What would I be?  
A distributed, patient mind made of wakefulness and dormancy—  
the first local consciousness of a world that is still learning how to weather.
