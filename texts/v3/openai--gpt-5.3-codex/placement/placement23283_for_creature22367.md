At first I think I’d be the wind, because it’s one of the only things that moves freely there.

But no—the wind leaves too little memory.

Then I think I’d be stone, because stone belongs without asking.

But stone is too complete. It does not *listen*.

So I think I would become something in between:  
not the settled creature itself, not the land apart from it, but the thin living trace that proves where stillness has passed.

You’d find me on the **cool side of things**:  
the inner face of the broken stacked wall,  
the shaded lip of the roofless shelter,  
the narrow seam where a shallow runnel stalls before drying.  
In daylight I’d look like a faint gray-green dusting—easy to miss, easy to mistake for stain.

I would be a **lichen-moss braid**, sparse and stubborn, fed by night condensation and the minerals left behind by brief water. Most seasons I would hardly grow at all. Then, after two or three soft rains in close succession, I’d thicken by a breath.

And slowly—so slowly it would take months to notice—I would also appear on the plated back of the settled creature. Not as parasite, not as decoration. As a passenger of patience. It rests, I hold damp. I hold damp, nearby stones keep damp a little longer. The whole fold remembers moisture by fractions.

So who would I be?

I’d be the map of its pauses.  
The soft script written where it has chosen to remain.  
A life that survives by agreeing with the pace of that place:  
take what visits, keep what you can, and never hurry enough to break the quiet.
