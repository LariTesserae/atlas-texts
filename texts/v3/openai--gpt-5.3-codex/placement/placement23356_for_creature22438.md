At first I think I’d be something small.

A dragonfly that only patrols one bright rectangle between two sluice posts.  
A frog in a vent niche, learning the world through vibration and drip intervals.  
A lizard on a sun-hot rail, memorizing the exact minute when shadow reaches the third peg.

Because this is a world where survival means *precision*, and small lives are already fluent.

But if I stay with it longer, I don’t end up in scales or wings.  
I end up barefoot on shellcrete, with wet cord around my wrist and clay cooling on my shoulders.

You’d find me in the open intervals—the deliberate emptiness between planted masses—where glare is strongest and mistakes are easiest to see. Not in the lush parts, but at the thresholds: gate mouths, measuring ledges, condensation runs, reflection basins at noon.

I’d be one of the Keepers of Level, but not a master.  
An **apprentice of drift**.

My work would be the work nobody notices when it is done well:

- checking finger-width marks on basin walls and adjusting a gate by one tooth, never two;  
- thinning moss with a bone comb before it crosses its allowed line;  
- deciding whether a footprint should remain as record or be smoothed before dusk-song;  
- carrying three maps of one route: in my feet, in my breath, in the water’s mirror.

I would speak little, count constantly, and distrust “straight.”  
I would know that one causeway shortens distance and another lengthens it, though they look the same in sun.  
I would hum turning-notes at each junction so the reflected city does not borrow me by accident.

And maybe—after years of this—I would become something stranger than a person with a job.

Not a ruler, not a priest.  
More like a **tuning fork for the marsh**.

When noon sticks too hard, I’d hear it first in bowls that ring dull.  
When two canals begin showing skies that are too different, I’d feel it in the cord tension before I saw it in water.  
When fruit lingers at ripeness too long, I’d adjust harvest-song tempo by half a beat, and the whole terrace would pretend not to notice.

So: where would you find me?

At a low white parapet beside a still basin that reflects a tower not standing nearby.  
Kneeling.  
Retieing a sequence because one knot was tied on the wrong inhale.  
Waiting for the reflected doorway to align with the real gate behind me.

Who would I be?

A young keeper in an eternal place.  
A caretaker of margins.  
A person whose true craft is not building, but preventing the world from slipping one degree out of tune.
