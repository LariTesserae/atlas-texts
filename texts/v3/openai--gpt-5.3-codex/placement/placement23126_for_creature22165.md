I wouldn’t arrive here as an explorer for long.  
This place doesn’t tolerate spectators.

At first I’d think I was a courier—just another body in an insulated harness, moving along the sounding lanes with a cargo canister and a borrowed map. I’d keep looking up for weather, keep misreading echoes as distance, keep flinching at wing-thrum in the fog.

Then I’d learn the local truth: nothing important is above you.  
Everything important is in the walls, in the drip-lines, in the way a floor answers your weight.

So you’d find me lower down, in a reclassified district where an old freight throat became a brood shelf, and the brood shelf is slowly becoming transit again. A hinge-place. The kind of place that belongs to three futures at once.

---

Who would I be?

Not a builder, exactly.  
Not a priest, though I’d spend nights in listening vaults.  
Not an animal handler, though I’d know migration clocks by feel.

I’d be a **reclassification listener**—one of the people who decide when a structure has changed enough in use, pressure, and occupancy to earn a new civic name.

My work would be part bureaucracy, part ecology, part divination:

- stand barefoot on diagnostic strips and read stress-songs through bone;
- sample condensation chemistry from rail-cables and door-lips;
- log right-of-way conflicts between glider shoals and lift traffic;
- listen for new weeping points in load-bearing stone;
- recommend whether a tunnel remains “human transit,” becomes “shared thermal corridor,” or is ceded fully to fauna and rerouted elsewhere.

I’d carry a tuning fork set, a wet-slate ledger, and a veil that leaves enough breath visible for the city to recognize me. My House-mark would be conductive ink at the throat and wrists, glowing faintly when I’m on duty so large things can hear me as “legal presence.”

---

And after years of that, I think I’d become something subtler:

a person whose body is partly a civic instrument.

My knees would predict pressure fronts before sirens do.  
My jaw would tell one tower from another by resonance alone.  
My sleep would sync to turbine moan and colony shift-change calls.

Eventually, if I stayed long enough, I wouldn’t say “I work in District Nine-Weep.”  
I’d say: “I am assigned to Nine-Weep’s current phase.”  
Because in this basin, identity is less species than contract.

So:  
You’d find me where categories blur—at the lip of water, law, and animal motion.  
And I’d be one of the people who keep the blur legible.
