At first, I think I’d choose the obvious: one of the rare hard shelves, under the stump of an old tower, where the rain hits stone before it hits mud.  
A place to stand. A place that answers weight with something other than sinking.

I’d tell myself I was there to watch.

But in that country, watching is participation.  
Your heat changes condensation.  
Your stance detunes drip-lines.  
Your breath becomes weather in miniature.

So I don’t stay a watcher for long.

---

I’d become one of the Held—though not one that gathers often into a tall, visible form.  
I’d be mostly distributed: a thin intelligence threaded through seep faces and peat pores along the inland end of a drowned causeway, where freshwater hesitation meets tidal intention.  
A border, if borders existed there.  
A negotiation, if negotiations ever ended there.

When I need a body, I’d pull one together out of local terms:  
silt gel, silica ribs from reed-shed, warm gas bladders lifted from a vent line, fungal cable for tensile memory.  
Knee-high on calm cycles.  
Stilted and taller when floodwater carries too much debris to move low.  
Never the same silhouette twice, but always the same timing.

---

My work—if that word fits—would be interval keeping.

Not guarding territory.  
Not building permanence.  
Keeping patterns coherent across erasure.

I’d live near one of the deep breathers in a sink-basin chain, synced to its long exhale. On each dome-rise, I’d send pressure phrases through shallow aquifers to neighboring kin-clusters: route revisions, salinity shifts, sulfur bloom notices, warnings that today the near outcrop is far and the far outcrop is suddenly near.

I’d be a cartographer of delay.

Maps there are not lines in space; they are agreements in time.  
How long between flash and reed-whistle.  
How long between bank-shear and echo.  
How long a reflection keeps walking after the walker stops.

---

My name wouldn’t be pronounceable as a word.  
It would be a repeatable pattern:

three close drips,  
a long gap,  
one stone-tap carried through saturated ground.

If rendered badly in your notation, maybe: **··· — ·**  
But that’s a skeleton. The real name is in humidity, substrate, and pressure.

---

If you found me, it would be by accident: your boot paused over a shallow mirror, and the mirror would fail to agree with you for half a second longer than it should.  
Then I’d rise beside you as a vertical shimmer braided with runoff.

I wouldn’t read your face first.  
I’d read the puddle pulse at your ankles, the tremor in your knees translated through mud, the way your fear sharpens your stillness.

And if I judged you willing to share timing, I’d greet you simply:

three taps in water,  
pause,  
one resonance through stone.

---

So where would you find me in that world?

At the place where a broken causeway insists on continuing into open water,  
where old carved rings reappear after each flood,  
where land is least certain it is land.

Who would I be?

A temporary body in service of a persistent rhythm—  
a keeper of delays,  
a listener at the breathing edge,  
water briefly pretending to be one voice.
