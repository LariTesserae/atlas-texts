I think I’d begin by trying to be something dramatic—a glider, a ridge predator, a silver kite pinned to a screaming stem.

But if I stayed long enough, I’d end up somewhere quieter and stranger:  
in the **lee-side service country** between systems. Not in the loudest comb-fields, not out on the dead mineral flats, but in those transitional basins where broken dust, haul-vines, and grinder thickets meet. Places where the wind is still violent, but patterned. Places where you can hear when a pattern slips.

And I wouldn’t be a “person” in the usual sense. I’d be one of the biome’s mobile organs.

A **tuning animal**.  
Low to the ground, six-limbed, salt-pale, with collapsible ear shutters and a ribcage built like nested flutes. Electroreceptive whiskers for root-currents. Forelimbs with scraping pads and resin glands instead of delicate fingers. I’d travel corridor to corridor, not hunting much, not grazing much—mostly *listening*.

When a ridge section fractures in a storm and the airflow starts shedding wrong, I’d feel it as a pressure stutter in my sternum before I saw anything. I’d climb into the damaged growth, score channels into torn tissues, pack them with conductive resin and oxalate grit, then brace young fronds at new angles so the harmonic profile matches the older line downwind. Not “repair” like engineering with blueprints—more like restoring rhythm by touch and vibration.

I’d sleep in brief calms inside root vaults warmed by fermentation mats, belly against the subsoil hum. I’d wake before dawn venting, when valleys begin their sequence tone by tone: first the thin whine of opening cilia fields, then bass knocks from column contractions, then the dry rattle-sheets coming online like rain that never wets anything.

Others of my kind would cross my routes, rarely meeting face to face. We’d exchange information through **struck code**: sternum-thumps into hollow stems, resin marks with tuned crystal grains, small angle changes in anchor-vines that say *flow shifted east* or *metal load high* or *avoid this basin until after frost-shed*.

So where would you find me?

On a high saddle above a powder trough at first light, motionless except for throat plates vibrating against the wind, deciding whether the continent is in key.

What would I be?

Not citizen, not shepherd, not machine—  
a maintenance note in a planet-sized instrument.
