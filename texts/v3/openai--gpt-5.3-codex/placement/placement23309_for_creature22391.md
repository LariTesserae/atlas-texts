At first, I think I’d place myself somewhere *above* it all—on the catwalk between the two settling tanks, boots on grating, trying to map the basin like a problem.

That lasts maybe an hour.

In this place, elevation is temporary. Everything vertical is just another route for water. So I’d drift lower: to the narrow seam where the brick control house foundation meets the clay shelf, where trickle becomes thread, thread becomes pulse. A place with one rusted handrail, a dead indicator lamp, and a patch of moss that never quite dries. Not central. Not hidden. A threshold.

And what I’d be…  
not a foreman, not a ghost, not a rival to the Keeper.

I think I’d become its **counterpart**.

The Keeper corrects. I would **remember**.

I’d be the thing that holds pattern long enough to matter: a ledger made of damp paper, copper salts, and patient attention. A custodial intelligence of a different kind—less muscular, more mnemonic. I’d notice that Gate 3 sticks only when barometric pressure drops fast. I’d note that the warm “dead” conduit peaks before rain. I’d keep track of which drips are seasonal and which are intent.

Not in a clean database. In material ways:
- chalk recopied before dawn,
- string marks on a handrail post,
- scratched notches under flaking paint,
- little glass vials of water from the same sump, month by month,
- a logbook swollen thick as peat.

If human workers still come, I’d be the one they half-believe: the basin tech who says, “Don’t force that valve tonight,” and is right often enough to be left alone.

If no workers come, I’d still do it. Because the basin doesn’t need supervision; it needs continuity.

Over time, I’d probably stop being fully human in any strict sense. Skin always cool. Hearing tuned to pump cadence. Sleep in 20-minute spans between cycle changes. Fingertips darkened from iron and graphite. Maybe the Keeper starts using my habits the way it uses old feeder lines—another channel, another relay. Not possession. Collaboration by attrition.

So where would you find me?

At dusk, in the broken-window control house, lamp off, listening through the floor.  
At midnight, under the open tank, retightening a clamp that should not hold but does.  
At morning, rewriting chalk the rain blurred—not to defy the Keeper, but to converse with it.

Who would I be?

The **Basin Clerk**.  
The one who keeps records in a place that prefers flow.  
A dry-minded creature in a wet machine, committed to the same law:

stability is not a state here.  
It is a practice.
