At first, I think I’d be an observer—some lone figure on a black ridge, rain cutting sideways, trying to name things before they change.

But that role fails almost immediately.  
In a place where form is temporary and memory is the only continuity, a fixed observer is the least believable thing in the scene.

So maybe I’m not standing *in* it.  
Maybe I’m one of the recurrences.

A pressure-knot, perhaps—one that appears whenever three basins synchronize: when a thermal updraft from the eastern crack meets a descending cold sheet and a charged mist front. For nine minutes, sometimes twenty, I exist as a stable asymmetry in the storm: a lens where droplets pause instead of falling, where lightning bends half a degree off its usual seam, where echoes arrive slightly early. Then I collapse. Nothing remains but altered routing in the next rain band.

That would be my body: not flesh, but **repeatability**.  
That would be my metabolism: stealing coherence from turbulence, paying it back as pattern.  
That would be my movement: not walking, but reappearing basin to basin whenever the equations rhyme.

And where would you find me?

Not at the center of anything.  
At thresholds—saddle-ridges between suspended seas, fracture mouths that opened this morning, the exact altitude where cloud becomes wall. Places where the world is deciding between two states and needs a temporary rule.

If I had a name, it wouldn’t be a noun.  
It would be an instruction:

**Hold shape. Listen. Hand it forward.**

I’d be one brief clause in the planet’s first draft of life.
