At first I think I’d choose the only obvious vantage: the firmer patch, the lone tree, the little bit of “up” in a country that resists height.

But I don’t think I belong on the still points here.  
This place rewards movement along seams.

So if you were looking for me, you wouldn’t find me in the middle of the flats at noon, and you wouldn’t find me deep in reeds either. You’d find me where wet thins into damp, where damp freckles into dry, where something larger has just passed and stirred the day open.

I’d be one of the white follower birds.

Not rare, not dramatic—just a long-legged, sharp-eyed wader that has learned the old lowland rule: let the heavy ones do the opening. I’d trail the edge-grazers at a respectful distance, then close in when they root and stamp. Every lifted foot would raise a chance: insects, tiny crabs, worms, startled fry in hoof pools, things that only exist for seconds unless someone is paying attention.

By dawn I’d be in their wake, stepping between prints while the mud is still cool enough to hold shape.  
By midday I’d stand on a dark shoulder or in tree shade near them, beak parted in the heat, riding out fly-hours above the worst of it.  
By dusk I’d be back on the ground, stitching from one fresh wallow to the next, feeding in quick jabs while their low calls move through the warm air like distant drums.

I’d nest in one of those broad, far-apart trees—high enough for wind, low enough to hear the frogs start. My life would be mapped by recurrence: same basin, same bands of grazers, same routes rewritten by each season’s slight changes. Not migration across worlds—circulation through a known one.

And if you asked where, exactly, in that huge plain I live, I’d answer:

In footprints before they fill.  
In wallows before they dry.  
On the moving border between disturbance and growth.

The grazers would be the weight of the system; I’d be one of its quick hands.

So: you’d find me near their knees, in the bright practical light, working the brief abundance they uncover—small, alert, and always at the edge where the plain keeps remaking itself by almost the same means as yesterday.
