At first, I think I’d be useless there.

Too loud in my movements. Too eager for light. Too attached to horizons.

So I wouldn’t begin in the deep. I’d begin in one of the narrow air pockets just above a working channel—where you can still see a diluted shaft of day if you crane your neck, and hear two worlds at once: drip above, pressure below.

Then, over time, I think I’d settle into the **middle levels**—not the ceremonial still-basins, not the dangerous fast galleries, but the junction places where slow decisions are made. A gate-room. A rope hall. A ledge where five passages meet and all of them change with the rise.

---

Who would I be?

Not a ruler. Not a hunter.

I would be one of the **knot-readers**—a keeper of passage memory.

The one crouched half-submerged beside a wall of hanging cords, fingertips moving over swollen fibers, reading route, depth, season, risk. The one who updates family rope-maps after each handbreadth rise. The one who can tell a child, in the dark and without looking, “third ladder is gone slick—take the turtle lintel instead.”

A practical mystic, maybe.  
Or an engineer mistaken for a priest.

Because in that place, maintenance *is* ceremony.

---

I can see the body I’d have there: low center of gravity, broad feet gripping algae-slick stone, scarred knuckles from blind turns, mineral dust at the brows, rope burns healed thick at the palms. Breath trained into muscle memory. Fear present, but domesticated.

I’d speak little in open air.  
Mostly I’d communicate in taps: pipe, shell, stone.  
Three quick for “hold.”  
Two and drag for “rise coming.”  
A long, hollow knock for “do not open that gate.”

And I’d be known less by face than by rhythm.

---

If I have a title, it’s something like:

**Between-Keeper of the Fifth Exchange**  
(the chamber where upper-cool meets lower-warm and neither is allowed to win)

My work would be to keep flow from becoming force.  
To notice small changes before they become emergencies.  
To love grates enough to clear them at midnight.  
To remember that every blockage is local, but every consequence travels.

I’d probably have one old turtle that tolerates me and no one else, sleeping in my maintenance niche like an irritable ancestor.

---

So where would you find me?

On a narrow ledge above black water, one hand on wet stone, one hand on a rope-map, listening with my cheek to the pulse inside a ceramic pipe.

Not looking heroic.  
Not looking important.

Just listening for tomorrow in slow water—  
and tying the knot that gets everyone there.
