I wouldn’t be the keeper itself.  
I’d be one of its temporary bodies.

You’d find me at the **hinge places**:  
not in the lush islands, not in the open stone, but where one gives way to the other—at the lip of shade beside a warm gravel lane, near a half-buried cool ring and a tree with braided roots. The places where decisions happen fast: drink here, hide now, hatch today, leave before noon.

At first glance, I’d look like nothing at all.

In dry hours I’d be distributed—just another set of faint threads among fungal mats, a ghost-pattern in lichen, a conductive lace under dust. If you touched the rock, you might feel only a slight coolness and pull your hand back thinking it was seepage. It would be me listening: counting humidity rise by fractions, reading the static tension before rain, sensing when the matte columns begin their old, subaudible hum.

Then the first hot burst would hit.

I’d assemble.

Not into one dramatic creature, but into several quick, practical forms: low fronded runners that spread over stone to catch first condensate; cup-like membranes that tilt droplets toward root hollows; short, jointed feelers that tap bare ground and map charge-safe paths for freezing prey. I’d be a **maintenance animal**, grown on demand—part gardener, part traffic signal, part medic for a biome that runs on minutes.

I don’t think I’d hunt.  
I don’t think I’d speak.  
But I would direct.

A pulse to the bark tubes: wake.  
A nutrient bleed beneath the ferns: now.  
A brief flattening of static over a crossing patch: hold still, all of you, predator overhead.  
A metallic-sweet exhale from a bladder node: pollinators, here.

When the basin empties at noon, I’d retract again, not dead, just narrowed back into gradients and memory.

If I had a name in that place, it would be something like **the Interval Hand**—one of many mobile hands of the interval-keeper, cast out during water-time and reeled back during heat-time. Not a separate species, not exactly. More like a temporary “I” the basin grows when orchestration needs fine control.

So where would you find me?

At the exact moment the hollow changes state—  
when dry stone becomes habitat,  
when silence becomes layered sound,  
when traffic becomes choreography.

I would be the thing that appears only during transitions,  
and without which the transitions would still happen—just less well, less mercifully, less in tune.
