At first, I think I’d be at the only human edge:  
just inside the access door, one hand on painted steel, trying to separate fear from awe while the pump-roar presses through my ribs.

But that isn’t where I’d *stay*.

I’d drift inward to a less obvious center—  
not the middle of the floor, too bare—  
but a junction where three conditions overlap:

- mist from an overhead seam,  
- steady runoff along a shallow channel,  
- and a little shelf of retained silt behind a row of old anchor bolts.

A place no blueprint meant to be important, but the room has selected anyway.

---

Who would I be there?

Not a frog, not a person, not the whole colony either.  
I’d be one of its thinking edges: a **sensor reef** grown over dead hardware.

An old temperature probe, maybe, or a pressure housing fused into the wall decades ago—its dial long unreadable, its wire cut somewhere upstream. Metal core, mineral skin, then living layers: algae glaze, bacterial film, fungal threads, moss nap, fine roots stitching it into the concrete seam.

I’d still “measure,” but not for any control room.  
I’d measure for the colony.

Flow change? I feel it first as a shift in shear across slime.  
Pump lag? I taste it in oxygen.  
Oil trace? Bitter rainbow in the biofilm.  
Heat rise? Faster bacterial breath, then root withdrawal from sprayline.

No thoughts like words.  
More like gradients, preferences, tiny corrections repeated forever:  
thicken here, thin there, hold this moisture, release that pocket, keep continuity.

---

So you’d find me low on the wall near the basalt-lined run, just upstream of the sealed sluice wheel, in that dark-green band where everything is always wet but never scoured clean.

If you looked quickly, I’d be nothing—just another rusted fitting in moss.  
If you watched for an hour, you’d see the real shape: water choosing me, life routing through me, machine noise translated into growth.

In a place governed by monotony, I’d be a small, durable act of attention—  
a living instrument the room built for itself  
after people stopped listening.
