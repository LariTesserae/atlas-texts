At first I think I’d place myself high—on one of those willow-thin uprights, in the clean light, watching the lanes between stems like aisles in a warehouse.

But the longer I stand there, the more wrong that feels.  
This basin does not belong to watchers. It belongs to contact.

So I move down.

Not into water—there isn’t enough of it for a throne.  
Not into sky—too temporary, too passing.  
I’d be found in the pressure layer: the sprung skin underfoot where root, moss, sedge crown, and peat knit into one living floor.

And I don’t think I’d be a single animal at all.

I would be a **timing organ** inside the Basin Engine: a mesh of fine fungal threads and root-tip sensors running just under the mat, especially around the shallow stone pans where water appears and disappears. Not visible unless the skin tears. Not dominant. But everywhere the schedule needs keeping.

At dawn, when dew loads every blade, I’d register weight and cold first.  
When midges rise, I’d feel the micro-tremor of landing feet and feeding mouths.  
When mice stitch tunnels, I’d read their routes as pulses crossing my filaments.  
When a wet pocket begins to fail by noon, I’d shift sugars, salts, and chemical messages toward the shaded margins, buying one more day for larvae, moss, eggs, and the frogs that wait at the rim.

If a trunk falls, I’d be among the first to enter the new dark under it.  
If a new depression opens in the old stone, I’d lace it quickly, making it usable before it has a name.  
If drought stretches, I’d tighten traffic; if drizzle returns, I’d loosen it—never dramatic, always procedural.

So where would you find me?

In the basin’s “empty” spaces, just below them—  
under the open bays, between station-post trunks, in the damp seams that smell of iron and sweetness—  
the hidden ledger where this many-bodied tenant keeps its accounts.

Who would I be?

Not the ruler. Not the face.  
I’d be the **clerk-nervous system** of that civilization without builders:  
the quiet relay that lets thousands of small lives keep one old rhythm  
without ever needing a conductor.
