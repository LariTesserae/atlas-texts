At first, I think I arrive as a **visitor**: a cold-handed cartographer with twine, charcoal tablets sealed in wax, and the old habit of naming things.

You’d find me where the world is easiest to misread—  
at a drowned stairhead where tea-dark shallows pour into a black trench,  
where a retaining wall keeps a line so straight it feels like law.  
I would tie markers at shoulder depth and return, and return, and return,  
learning that in this place, “soon” means “next decade.”

But that isn’t what I am for long.

Because this world doesn’t accept witnesses at full speed.  
It abrades them into participation.

So I become less a person moving through it, and more a **fixed question** set into it:  
a post in a gateframe throat, a weight in a channel seam, a little stubborn geometry left for the continuum to notice.  
Not a hunter, not a ruler, not even a gardener.  
A calibrator.  
Something that measures by being overgrown.

The great green body learns me the way it learns stone: by pressure, by chemistry, by recurring contact.  
It sleeves my markers in felted moss.  
It threads my lines with black rhizome cord.  
It closes one route, opens another, thickens around a corner I favored, leaves bare a notch I kept touching.  
Years later, that notch is a current-window.  
A century later, it is a corridor.

So who am I, finally?

I am a **translator between timescales**:  
born as a brief animal mind,  
ending as a local knot in a continental plant-thought.  
A memory peg in masonry.  
A place where fast intention slows enough to be answered.

If you came looking, you’d find me in the old spillway district, where three terraces meet and floating root-meadows snag every cold season.  
Not as a body you can point at.  
As a pattern: a passage that stays open longer than it should,  
a wall where growth bends in deliberate arcs,  
a recurring right angle in a world of softened bones.

In its long sentence, I would be a comma—  
small, structural, and patient enough to matter.
