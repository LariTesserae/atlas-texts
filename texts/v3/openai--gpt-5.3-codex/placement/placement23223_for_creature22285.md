At first, I wouldn’t be a body at all.

I’d be the **pause** before the drip lands—  
that held, cold fraction over the same dark pool,  
where the surface tenses and does not yet break.

Then I’d be lower than that: a seam in the peat, a narrow give under moss, one of those places a careful foot can pass and a careless foot sinks. Not visible, only *felt*. A boundary in a basin made of boundaries.

If you came looking for me, you wouldn’t find me at the center. There isn’t one center, not really. You’d find me where patterns knot:

- under the root arch that forces everything to duck,
- beside the fungus shelf with the pale crescent scar,
- at the choke-point where three runnels keep trying to become one and then remember not to.

I’d be there often enough that “often” becomes “always.”

Only after a while would I take on shape.

Low, wet, threaded with needles. A spine built to bend around what should not be broken. Feet that open like hands before each step. Whiskers that read current and leaf-litter as one language. Eyes made for dimness and repetition. A mouth that tastes rot, fear, mineral, and timing.

Not the old one.  
Not yet fully the new one.  
The one in-between.

Half-grown custodian, first full circuit without following.

I would move the basin by corrections so small they vanish as actions:

- nudge peat from a blocked thread of water,
- tear back one aggressive sedge tongue,
- open bark where beetles stalled,
- leave one frog, take another,
- startle the tunnel-runner that’s been crossing too boldly in daylight,
- mark a stone where moss has thinned and nitrogen is needed.

No drama. No proclamation. Just pressure, release, return.

I think that’s who I’d be here:  
not “predator,” not “guardian,” not “spirit,” though I’d be called all three by anything that needs nouns. I’d be an **office wearing fur**, a continuity with a pulse. Something trained by route and seep and fungal breath more than by parent. Something the basin shaped until my choices fit its old equation.

If you asked where exactly to stand to meet me, I’d say:

Stand where sound is nearest to silence—  
where you can hear a beetle turn under bark,  
where your boots sink half an inch and stop,  
where a drip keeps perfect time on stone.

Wait long enough.

You’ll see me first as a correction in the corner of your eye.  
Then as movement.  
Then as pattern.  
Then, if you stay and keep still, as the basin continuing itself through a single passing body.
