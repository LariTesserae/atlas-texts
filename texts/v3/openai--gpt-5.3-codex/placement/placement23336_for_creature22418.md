At first, I think I’d be drawn to the highest bridges—somewhere airy, watching the whole bowl at once.

But the longer I stay in your basin, the more I feel myself pulled lower, inward, to places where change is small enough to touch.

So: you’d find me on the **east-leaning ring**, halfway between the bulb racks and the kiln lane, where the afternoon tilt first begins to whisper through the masonry. Not in the center, never at the rim—at a seam. A place with three patched doorframes inside one another, each marking where “through” used to be.

I would be one of the people, yes—but specifically a **keeper of second motions**.

Not a chief builder. Not a grower of major crops.  
I’d be the person who watches for the basin’s delayed replies.

When someone raises a hand under a lintel and the empty air raises it again a breath later, I chalk that delay.  
When a corner can be reached from two distances, I string both distances and tag which one bites at dawn.  
When shadows start slipping from ankles toward cool plaster, I guide root trays in close and feed the drift to plants that can metabolize it before it becomes hazard.

My tools would be small and always on me: a wrist-plumb, folded chalk, three waxed cords of known length, a notch bone for dark recording, and a shallow heat bowl to read invisible gradients before geometry goes strange. I’d wear resin-stiff sleeves, knotted high, with counting beads at the collar—each bead not ornament but a remembered shift.

I think I’d keep animals, too:  
a tile-glider that startles before thermal seams open, and two drumming beetles in a vented gourd case for team lifts when timing has to be exact and spoken rhythm is too risky.

At night I’d serve in the listening watch, cheek against warm wall, feeling for fiber tension through bone before hearing it in air. I’d write drift in touch-ledger marks others can read by fingertip in the dark: stable, stable, shortening, doubled, hold.

If I had a name there, it might mean something like **“the one who stands where the copy appears.”**

So who would I be?

Not hero. Not ruler.  
A continuity worker in a young, crowded world: patient, precise, soft-voiced—someone who helps the basin keep being itself one careful adjustment at a time.
