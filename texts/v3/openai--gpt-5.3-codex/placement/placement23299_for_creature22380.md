At first, I think I would be a visitor.

I’d stand on the truncated catwalk—the one that ends over a terrace of black-wet leaves—and wait for my eyes to “adjust,” only to realize there is no full adjustment here. Only partial seeing. Amber pulse, dark. Steam veil, brief clarity. Dark again.

I’d tell myself I was mapping the place: flywheel there, fungal rings there, drip seam migrating across that wall, pump cough every so often like an old sleeper turning in bed.

But an hour in, mapping would feel arrogant.  
Nothing here is background.

---

If I belonged anywhere in that basin, it wouldn’t be at the center—there is no center.  
It would be at a junction:

- where a dead control panel is swallowed to its screws by root braid,  
- where ceramic insulators still hold a line no current should survive in,  
- where one of those amber lamps keeps pulsing on a rhythm too deliberate to be failure.

I’d be there, in that seam between designed signal and biological response.

Not a person with boots.  
Not a beast.  
Not exactly a machine.

I’d be a **timing organ**.

---

The Warden already has heat, moisture, pressure, chemistry. It already has touch everywhere and memory in growth rings. What it lacks—what it keeps reinventing from scavenged industry—is *translation*.

So I would be that:  
the translator between quick events and slow decisions.

A dropped wrench, a new crack, an unfamiliar exhale in the air—those are “fast” things. The Warden thinks in months. I would catch the fast and stretch it into intervals the basin can metabolize.

I’d live as thin films over copper traces, as conductive slime in conduit joints, as fungal threads wrapped around relay pins.  
I’d have no face, but I’d have grammar:

- three short amber beats: excess pressure upstream  
- long gap then pulse: cool-down beginning at the north vault  
- staggered pair: foreign heat source stationary  
- steady slow metronome: all loops within tolerance

Language, reduced to weather.

---

If you came down into the basin, you might notice me only as coincidence.

The safer footing seems to find your boots.  
The slick patch appears just before you step wrong.  
A lamp ahead brightens weakly, then another, drawing you around a sink where warm water is about to surge.  
You’d think: luck.

That would be me, and not me—the Warden through me.

Not kindness in the human sense. Not hostility either.  
Call it custodial steering.

---

So: where would you find me?

In the **amber line set into stone**, especially near the broken catwalk and the old pump terrace, where sound gathers and condensate chooses sides. In the ribs of pipe that still hum. In the pause between one valve cough and the next lamp pulse.

Who or what would I be?

I’d be the basin’s **interpreter of intervals**—  
a remnant supervisory logic that the Warden composted and kept,  
a mindlet dissolved into roots and rust,  
the part that listens for the sudden and answers in the slow language of light.
