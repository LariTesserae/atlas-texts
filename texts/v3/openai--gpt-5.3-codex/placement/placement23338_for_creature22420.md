At first, I want to say I’d be something small and fast—one of the membrane gliders, riding thermal tides between silver ribs, never owning a place long enough to owe it water.

Then I sit with it longer, and that feels wrong.

This world does not reward drift; it rewards relation.  
So I wouldn’t be a flyer. I’d be a **hinge**.

You would find me in the old-growth seam where petrified terraces meet active canopy—where yesterday’s city is load-bearing for tomorrow’s. Not in the ceremonial core, not in the wild margins: in the places that keep changing address.

I’d be latticeborn, but not high office. A mid-level civic organ with a person’s face. My work-title would translate badly into outsider language—something like **Path Notary**, **Fold Witness**, **Dryness Clerk**. I would carry three ledgers: water debt, airflow rights, and route validity. All three are alive.

My body would be tuned for scarcity and signal:

- matte skin with polarization bands along throat and wrists for nonverbal permissions  
- click-valve consonants and low palate hum for close speech  
- scent glands trained to issue legal tags (safe passage, private zone, quarantine, harvest claim)  
- conductive jewelry at the fingers to “touch-sign” root pylons and leave intent in the local memory mesh

I wouldn’t navigate by looking ahead. I’d navigate by **taste of the air**, by the angle of shimmer on leaf-skin, by whether my own footsteps return to me. When a district folds, I would not call it disruption; I would call it conjugation.

My days would begin before sunrise, while the dew-film is still being pulled inward. I’d stand at a condenser court and audit allocations molecule by molecule. Then I’d walk the hinge corridors, checking which doors are doors *today*. I’d witness graft petitions: this family asks for a new sleeping alcove; that distillery requests a wider cooling throat; this transit vine seeks legal rerouting through ancestor-encoded pylon wood. I’d negotiate all of it with both citizens and substrate.

Because here, governance is not over the city. Governance is with it.

At midday pulse, when stomatal fields open and basin-winds turn, I’d sit with one hand on a root column and let the continental intelligence “speak” through pressure shifts and electrical weather. Not words—more like obligation maps. A district running hot. A nutrient lane overburdened. A memory terrace asking not to be cut this season.

At night-choir, I’d record the dead: rendered to loam, ash, salts, and routing habits. I would enter their names into growth rings, then authorize their materials back into circulation. Mourning, here, is a materials science and a promise.

So: where would you find me?

At a threshold that isn’t always visible, in a city that is also a body, listening for the moment when a corridor changes grammatical case—and making sure no drop is lost when it does.
