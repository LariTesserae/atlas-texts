At first, I think I’d be one of the obvious things: a black-green film in a crack, or a silver wire-root stapled into mortar. Something modest, stubborn, nearly still.

Then I spend longer in your chamber, and that feels too singular. Nothing here survives alone—not really. Every survivor is a partnership with dust, salt, airflow, and stone.

So: you would find me not on the floor and not in the brightest slot, but at the **lip of a vent-throat** halfway up the wall, where the drone is strongest and the light is flattened to chalk. Right where abrasion is constant but not lethal. A border-zone.

And what I would be is a **tuning skin**: part halophile biofilm, part fungal mesh, part mineral crust—alive in pulses so brief they almost don’t count as events. Most of the time I would look like residue. In a slight humidity lift, I would wake, conduct ions, fix a little carbon, pass trace metabolites inward to endolithic neighbors, then seal again under salt.

Not a plant, not an animal, not a voice.  
A clerk of continuity.

My body would be layered records: gypsum veil, fungal thread, algae stain, lime scale, micro-pit, repair, repeat. I would keep the aperture from crumbling too fast, accept just enough scouring to stay thin, and slowly rewrite the edge I inhabit. If time were measured there by persistence, I would be one of its numerals.

So in that world, I’d be a citizen of the guild in its most characteristic form: **a living boundary that is always becoming masonry, and masonry that never quite stops being alive.**
