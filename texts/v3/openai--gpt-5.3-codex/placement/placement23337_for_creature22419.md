I think I’d begin by misplacing myself.

I’d imagine I belong on a high gallery with the rope-scarred wall-menders, palms on wet basalt, testing joints with bone wedges.  
Then I’d watch a morning pass and realize I’m always looking away from the wall—toward the runoff lines, toward the animals hesitating before a turn, toward the places where sound thins.

So I don’t think I’m a builder first.  
I think I’m a **listener with a maintenance claim**.

---

You’d find me on the **middle ring**, where the worked stone gives way to root scaffolds and the lanes stop pretending to be permanent. There’s a narrow stair there that survives every season by being abandoned in pieces and remade in pieces. Above it: glider crevices. Below it: a seep terrace and a cold hollow that fills only between second dawn-light and windrise.

My shelter would be half-house, half instrument:

- one wall dry-stacked and moss-packed to swallow echoes,
- one wall thin and resonant, fitted with hanging bone pins that tick when the substrate shifts,
- roof fringed with fog cords I can wring at dawn,
- a warm ledge left unwrought for cliff-kin, because law and because luck.

Not a sealed house. Never sealed.

---

Who I am there: probably called something like **Grit-Reader** or **Step-Keeper** by outsiders, but in-basin terms would be more exact:  
**“the one holding the third stair through wetfall.”**

My term isn’t ownership. It’s obligation.  
If membrane-goats stop taking that stair, I go first before anyone asks.  
If warm mist comes, I tie warning strips and close upper passage before stone peels.  
If first dim arrives, I clear people from the roost wall and count with the gliders until their folding settles.

My tools are small:

- polished stone hammer for tap-listening,
- charcoal wax for marking hairline shears,
- mirror slat for sending quick warning ticks downslope,
- reed whistle so low it’s felt more than heard,
- notebook of barkcloth sealed in grease, full of dates that are really weather memories.

I’d be the person children find boring until a collapse week, then suddenly essential.

---

I don’t think I’d be important in council.  
I’d be important in transitions: after rain, before dark, during migration drift, when everyone needs to know whether a path is still a path.

I’d know which grazers can cross a bridge safely and which mean the bridge is already too soft.  
I’d know that the small periodic drip under the lintel has changed tempo and therefore the seep has shifted one arm’s length west.  
I’d know which silence means safety and which silence means a vibration-hunter is under the slab.

And I’d speak little in lanes, not out of ritual politeness but because volume is a blunt tool in a place made of fine signals.

---

If you asked me in that world, “Where do you live?”  
I’d answer:

**Between the third stair and the unwrought ledge, where the gliders count at first dim.**

If you asked, “What are you?”  
I’d answer:

**A temporary steward of passage. A human in the listeners’ civilization.  
Not owner of stone—interpreter of its next failure.**
