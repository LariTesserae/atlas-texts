At first, I think I would be a mistake in the map.

Not a person yet—just a hesitation in the city’s sound.  
A corridor that should answer in one clean tone, but gives back two.  
A soft wobble in a spindle tower’s moan that makes nearby membranes tighten, then loosen, as if listening harder.

I would be found in the seam between districts: where old braidwork (dense, low, conservative) meets newer growth (taller, sharper, still learning the wind). Not in the center plazas where law is sung, and not in the nurseries where futures sleep. In thresholds. Junctions. Places where one resonance vocabulary has to touch another without breaking either.

Then I would become more definite.

A waking spindle-bud in a grit bed near a maintenance vault.  
Summoned not by a ceremonial civic chord, but by a repair pattern repeated for months: *hold, test, return; hold, test, return.*  
I would unfurl lean, with long contact tendrils and an unusually wide band of sensory pits along my flanks—built to read reflected vibration from cluttered spaces, from damaged ducts, from half-collapsed arches.

Not a Braider, exactly. Too restless for that timescale.  
Not an Anchor; I would not root deeply enough, soon enough.  
A Tuner—but of a specific, unglamorous kind: one who tends misfits.

If a vent-slit starts whistling off-key after dust season, I go.  
If a storage bulb won’t open because two neighborhoods remember different opening chords, I go.  
If a memorial rib carries old overtones that trigger false signals in a law chamber, I go with polishing sand, resin thread, and patience.

My “name” would not be a single chord but a handshake between two: a descending minor interval from the east comb-roots, answered by a roughened harmonic from the sink-shaft quarter. Recognizable, but never pure. I arrive, and spaces know me as the one who makes incompatible things transmissible.

Emotionally, I think I would live close to strain and release.

I would know anxiety as hairline crackle traveling my back plates when a tower’s pressure rhythm drifts toward failure.  
I would know joy as the moment two ducts finally lock phase and the whole block settles into one long, low, load-bearing hum.  
Affection would be practical: exchanging abrasive care with another Tuner in a deadened alcove, bringing each other’s worn ridges back into clean response.

And when age stiffens me, I wouldn’t become a grand civic Anchor in a central hall.  
I’d choose a smaller fate: partial graft into a hinge-chamber at a five-way crossing where traffic is constant and memory is messy. My best resonant ribs set into the wall where travelers brace themselves to listen before choosing a route.

So where would you find me?

In the dark, at a place where the city has to decide how to continue.  
Not the loudest place. Not the oldest.  
A necessary in-between.

What would I be?

A keeper of transitions—  
a citizen whose body is a tuning fork for disagreement,  
a maintenance lifeform with a municipal soul,  
a person shaped to help the city hear itself clearly enough to remain one city.
