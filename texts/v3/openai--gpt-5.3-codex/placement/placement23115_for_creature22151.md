At first, I want to say I’d be on a summit—some relic buttress with a horizon like a blade.

But in your world, the summits are for witnessing, not for living.  
Life happens in the *lee*.

So I think I’d be found in the shadow-side hollow of a pressure lighthouse, where the air briefly learns manners. Not at its base exactly—too exposed—but in a half-buried maintenance vault a kilometer downslope, where the old alloy still rings at night when temperatures plunge. There would be a dry bridge nearby that crosses a gorge and reaches nothing obvious, and a field of sorted black scree that can tell you, by pattern alone, which way the next shear front is organizing.

As for what I’d be—

Not a ruler, not an explorer, not a priest in the grand sense.

I’d be one of the narrow jobs this planet permits: a **corridor tender** from a tower-keeper lineage. One of the people who walk the invisible roads in the air.

I’d go out before dawn in conductive wraps, carrying spools of sacrificial mesh, matte mirrors, and dust chalk. I’d check anchor points on ridge teeth, replace comb lattices burnt out by last week’s skin-storm, and read the memory stone by shadow angle while the light is still hard enough to make truth. When the pressure gradients begin to race, I’d stand still and feel for the shift through jaw filaments and wrist hairs—less “wind” than geometry moving.

My work would be small and constant:

- keep laminar corridors open for another season,  
- bleed charge where it wants to arc through flesh,  
- mark safe traverses in thermal glyphs that only matter for six hours,  
- decide which old machine gets attention when all of them are failing slowly.

I’d speak little in open ground. Mostly click-code at close range, flash-signal at distance, palm-pulse when certainty is needed. I’d sing only in enclosed throats of infrastructure, where thin air can’t steal the low notes.

And, eventually, when my body gives back to abrasion, I wouldn’t be buried.  
I’d be rendered and set into repair mortar on the vault lip, or fused into a dark albedo tile on the memory slab, so that at altitude I become one pixel in a line of heat-script someone else reads at dawn.

So: where would you find me?  
In the brief order around old machinery, between one turbulence and the next.

Who would I be?  
A maintenance life. A listener of pressure. A semicolon in the wind.
