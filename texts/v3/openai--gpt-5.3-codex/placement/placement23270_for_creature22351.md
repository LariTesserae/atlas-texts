At first I think I would be high—on the three-poled watch platform, shell strips at my knees, learning the basin by pitch.

That seems right for a moment: to be above the moving islands, to read the gliders’ circles, to call hot gaps before they open. A watcher, all tendon and patience. Someone whose job is to hear the sun coming through dust.

But then the wind shifts, and I can feel how exposed that is. Nothing open stays still. A platform is not a home; it is a sentence spoken into weather. So maybe not the watcher.

Then I think: I’d be one of the grove-tuners, knees white with powder, palms amber with resin, moving young fronds in the dark by handspan and breath. Not farming, tuning. Re-weighing pebbles, resetting net angles, adjusting baffles so a hollow can inhale the gust and exhale without collapse.  
That feels closer—work that never finishes, work that survives by flex.

Still, even that is too singular for this place. Nothing here lives alone, not really. A person is a knot in a moving weave: runner trails, goat sheds, kite shadows, flute-note law, dust rights, shade debts. So if I am “someone,” I think I am someone in the middle ranks of that knot. Not elder, not child. A **hinge-keeper**.

You’d find me at crossings.

Not the obvious paths between grove-islands, but the negotiated ones—the narrow arcs where overhang shade, ridge tone, and burrow tide all agree for six minutes at dusk. I carry a roll of vent slats on my back and a bundle of chime strips at my hip. I travel with two trained mirror-runners that can smell fungal bloom before it dulls a tool edge. I stop where stone rings wrong and re-lash what has stiffened.

My craft is temporary things that must outlast permanence.

- Windbreak circles that can open like a hand when pressure spikes.  
- Dust nets that spill on purpose before they tear.  
- Sleeping hollows with baffles tuned to the season’s organ-note.  
- Shade markers that throw no hard silhouette, only gradients, so beasts and people can share them without panic.

I am paid in intervals: two crossings in first dusk, one safe pause under black glass at noon haze, rights to collect one molt-goat plate from the west face, first claim on collapsed chime shell after storm-night. Currency is not objects; it is *timing*.

My name, there, would be two-part: a finger-spread sign at the throat (vent, hinge, open) and a low click-whistle that only carries when the ridge flutes are in C-brown—what outsiders would call “before dark,” though dark is never absent. Children would shorten it to something like **Sef**, because children flatten everything into speed.

I would sleep shallow, wrapped in reed mesh and ash-resin seal, ear to stone. Not for fear—for information. Cliff-skin pops tell tomorrow’s dust. Goat-booms tell mineral bloom. If the groves hiss in stagger instead of chorus, seed release will walk east and the burrow tide will follow, and every basket left open will wake hollow.

I would keep one forbidden relic once, briefly: a crystal needle in clay, rumored wet inside. Not as a treasure, as a liability. I’d carry it with ritual gloves through three shade shares and hand it to the vault warden before dawn pulse, heart racing the whole way—not with greed, with membrane-deep dread. In that basin, holiness and hazard are often the same thing.

By age, if I live long enough, I would become quieter and more structural: less runner-fast, more resonance-sure. The young would bring me strips that no longer ring true and ask whether their contract still stands. I’d hang them, wait for first gust, and listen.  
If the strip sings full cycle: binding.  
If it chatters and dies: renegotiate before someone breaks with the weather.

And if you asked me, at the end, what I am—not job, not kinline, not tool-mark—I’d answer:

I am one of the people who keep openings from becoming wounds.

In a world that forgot rain, where dryness is not lack but law, where life survives by vents and yielding, I would be a practitioner of the old agreement: keep nothing rigid, keep nothing open, keep listening.

Not ruler. Not hero.  
A tuned part in a basin-sized instrument.
