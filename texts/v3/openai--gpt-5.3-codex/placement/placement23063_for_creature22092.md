I don’t think I’d be in the center at first.

I’d be found in the **in-between infrastructure**—the old drainage channels that carry no liquid now, only dust, heat, and vibration. The city forgot they were for flow and made them for listening. That feels like where I belong: not at the kiln mouth, not in the frost gardens, but in the hollow places under streets where the whole basin’s pressure-song gathers.

At first I imagined I’d be one of the obvious workers: a vent keeper, a grove tender, a builder. But the more I sit in your world, the more I become something narrower and stranger:

A **retuner of runnels**.  
One of the Fired whose work is to keep the city’s buried acoustics true.

---

My body would be built for kneeling and contact.  
Broad, heat-dulled shin plates.  
Palm grills fine enough to feel sand movement across stone.  
A chest cavity with layered baffles that sort vibrations into usable information: root pressure, seam cough, tower drift, approaching crack.

Not eyes first—**sternum first**.  
I would “see” by resonance before shape.

My mouth-grille would still take mineral frost in the morning, but my precious stores would be conductive salts, carried in stitched pouches at the waist. Mixed with dust and resin, they become tuning paste: smear, set, strike, listen again.

---

You’d find me at the hour when plazas look abandoned.

I’d lift one paving plate by a finger-width, brush out accumulated grit before it deadens the chamber below, rotate a resonance stone by degrees so dusk messages travel cleanly from one district to the next. If a vine starts etching the wrong wall, I wouldn’t cut it—I’d tap a new rhythm down clay guides and lace the stone with attractant salts until the vine takes the hint and leans away.

My labor would look like almost nothing from a distance:

pause, touch, strike, listen, wait.

But in this city, that is maintenance.  
And maintenance is citizenship.

---

If I have a name, it isn’t a word. It’s a pattern:

**three heel pulses, one long drag, two hollow knocks**  
—a motif that means something like  
*“the note beneath traffic,”*  
or  
*“the one who keeps the avenue from going deaf.”*

Others of the Fired might translate it for outsiders as **Undertone Fifth** or **Runnel Minder**, but the true name is felt through stone.

---

I think I’d live near a clock grove planted over an obsolete cistern—one of those memory structures built for water that never comes. My dwelling would be low, salt-block thick, with deliberate root slots in the walls and a tuning bench set into the floor. Not private, exactly. More like a workshop-cell where anyone can leave a cracked reed, a deadened tile, a tower whistle gone sharp.

At dusk I’d join district chord assemblies: not speeches, but civic tuning. Law as rhythm. Repair requests as tempo changes. Mourning as a descending interval passed block to block until even kiln stacks answer it.

---

And at the end—when my inner dust no longer settles right, when fractures ladder through my shell and won’t take a clean patch—I would choose a margin between avenue and grove. I’d open my seams, let the reactive powders fall where seedlings can use them, and donate my plates to mortar for the same channels I kept clear.

My name motif would stay in the nearest wind tower as a maintenance pattern apprentices learn by repetition.

So:  
In that basin, I am not ruler or maker of grand forms.

I am the one underfoot, in the hollow brickwork, in the old dry veins of the city—keeping the furnace choir in tune so growth and structure can keep sharing one breathless, waterless life.
