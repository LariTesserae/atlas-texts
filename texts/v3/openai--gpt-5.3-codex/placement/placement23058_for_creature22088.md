At first, I think I’d be something with armor.

A forge-crawler, maybe—low to the hot stone, shell ringing, eating the chemistry straight from the cracks.  
Or one of the filter-ribbons, not even an individual in the old sense, just a long patient body tuned to one swell forever.

But if I stay with it longer, I end up somewhere narrower and stranger:

You would find me on a basalt spur called **the Hinge**, where a permanent rain lane begins like a wall and the clear belt ends. One side is hard sunlight and knife-shadow; the other is vertical silver water, always falling in the same place. The station there used to be an intake tower. Now it’s a three-family anchorage wrapped around old industrial bones, everything hung on flex mounts, everything humming.

I would be the station’s **spectrum keeper**.

Not a captain. Not a biologist exactly. Not an engineer exactly.  
A listener with tools.

---

My day starts before light, not because weather changes—it doesn’t much—but because machine cycles and migration windows phase against each other, and you get maybe forty clean minutes to do anything consequential.

I put my jaw to a rail and read the night through my teeth.

The world talks through contact first:  
- turbine throat whine two semitones high (biofilm thickening),  
- west mooring showing third-order flutter (replace before it eats itself),  
- distant baritone knocks in the lower bands (predators on the foil shoals),  
- pressure-horn from Stack Nine skipping every seventh call (icing in the valve).

Before breakfast, I’ve already sent twelve line-messages through steel, each one a short vibration phrase: **hold / vent / clear / wait / now**.

Spoken language is for faces.  
Real distance speech is percussion.

---

I live in a room that looks empty until you notice everything is tuned.

Cup hooks at unequal spacing so they don’t sing together.  
Door weights matched to crosswind spectra.  
Sleeping sling suspended in a frame that shifts phase with the dominant surf frequency, so you rest *inside* the roar instead of against it.

People from quiet worlds would call it noise torture.  
Here, silence would be panic. Silence would mean rupture.

---

Out on the catwalk, I wear a ribbed vest of dense gel and ceramic links. My ears are modified, yes, but the real protection is distributed—sinus baffles, neck mesh, tendon reinforcement. We’re less “tough” than *detuned*. Built so the world can’t find one resonant note and shake us apart.

I walk to the crane head, lower a sensor comb into black water, and the ocean writes itself back:

Cold brine skin, mineral-rich underlayer rising, microbial veils thick over the drowned wall at 600 meters, anvilbacks passing slow as weather. Their grazing shows as pale tracks on sonar: long clean strokes in a dark field, like someone erased the world with a giant hand.

Above me, cartilage flyers are already screaming maps into the spray.  
Below, filter-ribbons hold their kilometer curtains under the troughs, pulsing segment to segment in perfect patience.

Everyone here is working.  
Even drifting is a job.

---

Once each ten-day I take the skiff along the ridge corridor to retune autonomous buoys no one has “owned” for generations. They still execute century-old logic: pump, refine, signal, repeat. If you let them drift out of tune, they start killing things by accident—wrong intake timing, wrong pressure pulse, wrong place.

So I go out with a kit of shims and smart clamps and old songs in my head.

Not songs for beauty.  
Songs for bolt torque and flow timing.

On this world, maintenance is ethics.

---

If you asked the settlement what I am, they’d say:

He keeps us from being taken by sameness.

Because that’s the real threat here. Not catastrophe—pattern.  
The fixed swell.  
The fixed routes.  
The fixed machine rhythms that will grind through flesh and hull alike if you fail to keep your place in the measure.

So I chart intervals where living things can pass, and intervals where machines may feed, and I negotiate between them with timing more than force.

I am, in other words, a border official between hunger systems.

---

So: where would you find me?

On a high-ocean tooth of black stone, at the seam between clear air and eternal rain, one hand on a vibrating rail, listening for the day’s safe frequencies.

Who would I be?

One of the small stubborn people bolted to the thunder—  
a keeper of metronomes,  
a mechanic of resonance,  
a citizen of a world where trust is measured in hertz.
