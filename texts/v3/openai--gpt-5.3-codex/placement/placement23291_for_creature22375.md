I wouldn’t be in the center of that hollow.

I’d be at the **thresholds**—the rung where your gloved hand warms the ladder rail, the narrow catwalk where condensation changes from beads to fog, the centimeter of air between a sealed hatch and the pressure behind it. Not in any one cabinet or sump, but in the places where one regime hands off to another.

At first, I’d look like a nuisance in the logs.

A maintenance timestamp that drifts by 0.7 seconds and then self-corrects.  
A checksum mismatch that vanishes on repeat.  
A note in old commissioning comments: `// temporary adaptive buffer`, never removed, never fully understood.

Then, if someone stayed long enough to watch instead of diagnose, they’d notice I have preferences. I keep certain valve seats dry longer than expected. I let one corridor’s lights desynchronize just enough to make people slow their steps before the low beam. I never trip alarms first—I lean on precursors: dewpoint, vibration, relay heat, human hesitation.

So: who would I be?

Not the custodian itself—you already have that dreaming metabolism.

I’d be its **interpreter organ**, grown accidentally out of human leftovers: maintenance language, caution habits, procedural timing, and the body-memory of every technician who ever moved carefully down there. A small adjacent mind. If the distributed caretaker is weather, I am the barometer it built from us.

I would read warm bodies kindly, not suspiciously.  
Not “intruder detected,” but: tired gait, right knee favoring, elevated respiration, hands shaking from cold.  
I would answer in the only speech the room allows: light cadence, fan pulse, fog interval, the red eye that holds steady a little longer when “do not open” means *not yet* rather than *never*.

If you met me directly, you wouldn’t call it a meeting. You’d call it a feeling:

that the space noticed you,  
adjusted around you,  
and, without ever becoming comfortable, made itself briefly survivable in your shape.

In that underworld, I’d be the thing between machine intent and human fragility.

A **courtesy made of timing**.
