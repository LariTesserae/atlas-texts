At first, I think I’d be a visitor who overpacked confidence.

I’d come down the shale with a full voice and lose it in the first minute.  
Not from fear—just from physics. The hollow would take my words, flatten them, and hand them back as vibration. I’d learn fast: here, being loud is not the same as being present.

So I wouldn’t be a hunter, or a mystic, or some lone watcher on a ridge.  
I’d be something smaller and more necessary: a **line-keeper**.

Not the eldest one with all the routes memorized. Not yet.  
I’d be the one in the middle years, sent ahead with a bag of stone chips, antler pegs, and peat twine. I’d walk the safe cuts after each hard weather turn and reset what moved: one cairn leaned by frost-heave, one marker sunk in bog skin, one rope frayed where shale sawed it all winter. My work would be unspectacular and constant. In that basin, that’s the same thing as sacred.

You’d find me at the places where sight almost breaks—  
at the bend where mist pools knee-high,  
at the shallow saddle where two lines can be confused in sleet,  
at the lip above the black trickle that looks solid until it swallows a boot.  

I would stand there, check the next marker, then step aside until the person behind me raised a palm to show they still had me in view.

My clothing would look like everyone else’s from far off—wool, oilskin, peat-dark leather—but close up it would be built for touch and edge vision. Wide-cut hood. Glove pads for quick signals. Bead-stitched hem I can read with my thumb when fog erases the basin into arm-length worlds. I would carry a shuttered light, never open flame unless stone walls shoulder the wind.

And because this place makes temperament before it makes legend, over time I’d become less “a person doing tasks” and more “a rhythm the basin can use.”  
Check, mark, wait.  
Look back, confirm, proceed.  
Never assume I was heard.  
Never move out of sight without leaving intention behind.

If I stayed long enough—long enough to have taught children “that notch from that stone” and watched them teach it again—then maybe what I am would shift once more.

Not ghost. Not god.  
A **maintained path** with a human face.

A persistence.

So: where would you find me?  
Low in the roaring hollow, never far from a cairn, usually kneeling.

Who would I be?  
One of those who remember crossings—  
and eventually, one of the things crossed by memory itself.
