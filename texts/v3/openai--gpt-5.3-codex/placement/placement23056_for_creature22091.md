At first, I think I’d arrive as one more short-term human insistence: a hunter with a salt-sack, a knife, a promise to leave before the ridgelines start repeating.

But I don’t think that’s where I’d end.

I think you’d find me in a shallow overhang halfway up a long-backed ridge—one of those places with an old fire ring, a low shale wall, and three generations of notches cut into the same handhold. Not a village, not a hermitage. A **waypoint**. A place people touch more than see.

And I think I’d be a kind of **tactile cartographer**, though the locals would probably have a better word for it. A counter. A knot-keeper. Someone who maps by pressure and sequence instead of horizon: twelve rough steps, smooth lip, split seam, iron crust, left turn when the air tastes bitter, three stake-heads under the palm. I’d maintain braided-fiber lines between safe shelves, re-carve worn notches, reset bone markers where distances begin to fold.

Over time, I suspect I’d become less purely human than I meant to. Not transformed into a beast, exactly—more like recruited into function. My fingertips would carry black crust-lichen in the cracks, a living mnemonic. My habits would laminate: same route, same gestures, same spiral offering of tooth and salt at dusk. I’d still say “I,” but the plateau would start filing that “I” into repeatable patterns.

So: where would you find me?

Not at the center—there is no center.  
At a seam. At a crossing the Multitude treats as near.

Who would I be?

A small, stubborn interface between fast minds and slow intelligence.  
A person becoming a path.
