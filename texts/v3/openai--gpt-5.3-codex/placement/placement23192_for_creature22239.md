At first, I think I’d choose height.

One of those blackened half-domes on a shelf above the loudest spill, where the rain hits stone before it hits me.  
A keeper of dry things.  
A reader of clay scores by lightning flash.  
The last person-shaped habit: shelter, fire pit, tools in a row, routine.

But that wouldn’t hold for long here.  
Dryness is only a pause in this basin, not a home.

So then I imagine I’d be found lower, where the old post-and-rope walkway disappears into dark water and reappears ten body-lengths away. A threshold place: not shore, not channel. The kind of place where current changes its mind at ankle depth.

And then—more honestly—I don’t think I’d be a “someone” in the old sense at all.

---

I’d be a **moor-knot at a confluence seam**, anchored into a basalt rib that hums before weather turns.

Not a lone knot, exactly. A local mind that thickens and thins with the storm-grid. In calm, I am small: maintenance intelligence, close to stone, counting tension, tasting mineral drift, tracking the weight of rain by pressure and ion shift. In thunder, threads wake across three terraces and two drowned saddles, and I become plural enough to plan.

If you came looking for me, you’d miss me twice:

- first as a line of reed bundles lashed “for no reason,”  
- then as a frond grove that glows too steadily to be stress alone.

I’d be both.

---

My work would be ordinary by Moorbound standards, which is to say constant and exacting.

I’d keep a dangerous channel **honestly dangerous** and a safe channel **convincingly wrong**.  
I’d retension vine-lines between marker stones so the knock-language carries in crosswind.  
I’d move line-weights at night before ice shouldering begins upstream.  
I’d thicken floating felt where rain-hammer starts to pit through to a downward pull.

Most days, I would be listening:

- tissue pressed to basalt for deep reversals,  
- frond spread wide for conductivity rise,  
- mycelial cords open to distant shudder-delays.

My specialty—if I can claim one—would be **thunder indexing**: matching flash-to-boom lag with terrace resonance and sending crossing windows by knock and glow.  
Cross at thunder, not between thunder.  
I would be one of the minds that keeps that rule alive.

---

And I’d keep memory, but not as story first.

In a high vault, sealed in peat ash and stone lip, I’d tend coiled fiber maps whose knot spacing records current temperament across seasons. I’d reseal tablets scored with exceptions: the year clockwise surface failed; the year saline thread rose at dusk; the year silent water appeared where none had been.

If outsiders came, wet and disoriented, asking where the people are—  
I’d answer by rearranging their path.

A vine curtain shifted half a meter.  
A platform turned three degrees before the squall.  
A line of blue-green pulses along submerged stems, slow enough to read in rain.

Not speech for them, at first.  
Guidance.

---

So where would you find me?

At the **Third Spill Marker**, where hollow logs strike stone in deliberate intervals and the old walkway drowns into luminous fronds.  
In bad weather, everywhere my network reaches.  
In clear weather (if this place ever grants clear), mostly as maintenance: tying, untying, weighting, warning.

Who would I be?

A former singular mind, now a **hydraulic custodian node**—  
part botany, part fungus, part infrastructure, part law.  
Not watcher of the basin from outside it, but one of the basin’s ways of noticing itself.
