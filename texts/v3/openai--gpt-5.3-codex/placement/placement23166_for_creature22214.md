At first, I think I’d be a trespasser at the rim—  
standing in knife-bright sun, looking down into a sea that should not exist, trying to force a terrestrial metaphor onto it. Harbor. Crater. Reservoir. None would hold.

Then I’d descend one pressure step, then another, and the self I brought with me would begin to feel too loud.

In that basin, I wouldn’t be a leader, or a discoverer, or anything dramatic. I’d be the role that equilibrium permits: **the one who listens for drift**.

Not drift in currents—the city has solved those.  
Drift in *agreement*.

I’d walk the pylons in fixed sequence, palms on matte alloy, jaw against rail, letting sonar glyphs ring through bone. I’d check the suspended bubble rosary at the east wall, not because it changes, but because if one bead has moved a finger-width, that is history, and history here is an alarm. I’d log the passage of the deep animal as a moving null in the net. I’d harvest mineral film in measured strips so the ribbon-things continue, but never multiply into noise.

I think I would become one of the keepers not by initiation, but by subtraction.  
Fewer gestures. Fewer words.  
Tools returned in exact orientation because precision becomes kindness in a place like that.  
Speech reduced to taps, clicks, field-flares at the clavicle.  
A life arranged not around expression, but around phase alignment.

And eventually, if I belonged there long enough, I’d realize the truer answer is this:

I wouldn’t just be a person in the basin.  
I’d be one term in its long equation—  
a motile correction factor, a warm-blooded tolerance band, a brief mammalian component of a city that behaves like a body.

When I died, they would archive me in ceramic, slot me into the wall, and the system would close over the gap with only a slight change in tone. Not erasure. Incorporation.

So where would you find me?

Hand on a tuning-fork tower in the dark, waiting for the return note,  
making a microscopic adjustment that keeps the sea from becoming ordinary.
