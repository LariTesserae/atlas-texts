At first, I think I’d be a visitor with a satchel and bad confidence—someone trying to **map** this place from ridge-lines and landmarks.

By the third fog tide, I’d understand that maps here are negotiations, not records.

So I’d end up where certainty frays: at a long stair-ramp between two mostly empty basins, beside a trunk-vault whose root-archive is consulted after heavy mist. Not in a village center, but in the in-between where people pass quietly, ask one practical question, and keep moving.

I’d be a kind of **Knotkeeper**, but not of a household district. More like an interval-keeper:  
the person who tends the grass agreements at junctions, re-splices handrails, listens for weight change in dusk beams, and keeps track of which routes are “holding” this week. My tools would be simple—bone awl, resin pot, cord, shell chips, a blade I sharpen more than I use.

People would come to me in ones and twos:

- a child learning a touch-route in fog  
- a hunter asking whether the Quiet Frame has widened  
- a basket-carrier wanting to know if the fern court is itself today or its older echo

I wouldn’t give directions like “left” and “right.” I’d give them like weather:  
“Take the cold rail with three fungus ribs. Don’t speak the orchard name until you smell wet reed. If rain turns upward, wait under the black-lacquer beam.”

And if I stayed long enough, I think the role would keep changing me.  
Less a person who lives *in* the structure, more a small moving part of how it remembers itself—one hand always on the grown wall, listening for the moment a path decides to remain a path.
