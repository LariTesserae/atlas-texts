At first, I think I arrive as a **person**—boots on a wet catwalk, hand on a rail, trying to name what I’m seeing.

I last about twenty minutes as a person.

After that, the chamber starts editing me.

---

I’d be found **mid-shaft**, not at the bright top or the black reservoir floor—somewhere around the band where cold fall meets warm updraft, where every surface sweats and every cycle leaves a different residue.  
There’s a cracked inspection balcony there, half-swallowed by veil-fern and cable-vine, facing a bank of old intake louvers. Water never stops crossing it, but it pauses there—just enough for films to thicken, larvae to cling, and debris to snag before the next surge.

That is where I would belong: where things **transition**.

Not predator. Not grazer. Not engineer in the old sense.  
A timing creature.

---

If you looked for me, at first you’d say I was a colony of junction skitterers.  
Small, many-handed, pale-bellied, nesting in a dead control box, running out in bursts when the siren gives its two-note warning.

But that would only be one face.

Because the “me” here is actually three bodies braided together:

- a skitterer colony that drags fibers, shells, and carrion;
- a mat of microbial film that reads chemistry and shear;
- a root-net of condenser vine that stores and releases water by minutes, not seasons.

Individually they’re just residents.  
Together they become a role: **cycle-keeper**.

---

My work (if work is the right word) is simple and endless:

I clear the grate before inversion.  
I open little channels in moss so floodwater doesn’t peel whole mats off.  
I strip clotting foam from a pressure lip.  
I drag mineral-rich dead downward and soft-bodied surplus upward.  
I keep one lamp lens just clear enough for insect swarms to continue, because the swarm feeds the hunters, and the hunters keep panic from turning every hatch into a stampede.

I don’t do this out of stewardship.  
I do it because each action feeds me somewhere else in the cycle.

But from far away, it looks like maintenance.

---

My senses are not centered in eyes.

I feel:

- sirens as pressure in the root nodes,
- valve timing as vibration through claw-bones,
- toxin pulses as a metallic sting across biofilm,
- coming flood as a fast drop in insect wingbeat near the lamp cone.

I know time by sequence, not clock.  
Three pump hammers, one chain rattle, warm surge, then the long hiss: move now or drown where you cling.

---

So what am I in your chamber?

Not a hero species.  
Not the apex anything.  
Not even one organism.

I am a **local intelligence made of cooperation and accident**, living on the forty-ninth ring where runoff becomes habitat.  
A keeper of intervals.  
A resident of the wet minute between purge and bloom.

If you met me, you might call me vermin, slime, overgrowth.

The chamber would call me function.
