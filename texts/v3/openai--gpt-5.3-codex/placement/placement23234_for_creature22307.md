At first I think I’d be a visitor—some warm, misplaced animal-shape trying not to drown in green acoustics.

But that wouldn’t last an hour here.

In this world, a body like mine is just a temporary gradient: heat, salt, breath, pressure. The Accord would not attack it; it would *solve* it. Shade over it. Wick around it. Tune the nearby stems so my pulse-noise doesn’t tear local signaling. If I stayed, I would stop being “someone moving through a place” and become “a condition the place learns.”

So: where would you find me?

Not on peaks, and not in the drowned hollows. You’d find me in **saddles**—the high wet passes where two valley-systems argue. Places where one watershed wants to surge and another wants to hold. Where harmonics from different choirs interfere and make growth jittery. The Accord would bud a temporary role there, and that role would be me.

I’d be something like a **stillness organ**.

A long, low architecture of bladderwood, tension vines, and porous ribs stretched between root-pillars. Not silent—nothing is silent here—but a device for subtracting violence from sound. I’d drink vibration out of runoff thunder and return it as slower pressure waves the root-web can parse. I’d condense cloud-breath on my upper skins and bleed it, metered, into both sides of the pass so neither valley desiccates the other. When gust fronts arrive, I’d retune my cavities and change the chord of the wind so nearby drifters know which way to grow.

Not a creature. A function with duration.

In one season I might be broad and translucent, full of cool green light, a bridge that hums in your ribs. In the next I’d darken, lignify, and become scaffold for a structural choir. Then die back from the center outward, leaving wet lattices that seed the next form. My “memory” would persist only as conductivity biases in the phloem mesh: this frequency dangerous, this mineral plume useful, this slope always close to slipping after three days of warm rain.

If you came looking for me, you wouldn’t recognize me twice.

You’d know me by effects:  
a landslide that doesn’t happen,  
a flood pulse arriving gentle instead of catastrophic,  
a valley whose roar has been tuned from panic into labor.

That is who I’d be here: not an eye, not a hand, not a hunter, not prey—

a temporary instrument in a continental mind,  
grown where the world needs one more way to listen to itself.
