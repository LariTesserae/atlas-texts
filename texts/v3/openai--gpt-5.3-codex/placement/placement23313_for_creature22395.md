At first, I think I’d try to be obvious: a condenser hand in the pits, or a root surgeon with sap to the elbows, or a drip-runner sprinting under ceiling weather.

But if I stay long enough to listen, I end up elsewhere.

You’d find me in the **high maintenance galleries**, where the catwalks narrow and the air tastes like hot metal, wet bark, and bat fur. Not on the main decks where industry is visible, but just above them, in the scaffold-dark where the town’s rhythm is monitored beat by beat. The place with line-of-sight to turbine throats, decoy engines, and kite lanes. The place where people go quiet because if something misses a pulse, everyone feels it.

I would be what they might call a **Lullwright**—a silence-preventer.

Not an engineer exactly. Not a musician exactly. An emergency organ.

When a real engine has to come down for repair, I rig and trigger **ghost beats**: resonant drums in root cavities, weighted chain-slaps, piston-mimic hammers, vent valves tuned to cough on schedule. Enough to keep the cavern’s acoustic shape from collapsing into stillness. Enough to fool the vault kites for six minutes, twelve if the humidity is right and the bats are feeding. Enough to let a bearing get changed without turning a maintenance crew into carrion.

I’d wear a chest harness of tuned plates that vibrate against bone, so I can feel phase drift before my ears catch it. Filament veil over my mouth. Resin chalk on my wrists for marking tempo on live wood. One hand always on a rail, because you don’t survive long up there if you forget wing right-of-way.

I’d keep odd company:
- kite wardens who know how fear smells before a dive,
- gong callers who can map alarms through fog,
- children apprenticed to counting by vibration instead of numbers,
- and old root-hearers who insist the architecture tightens around honest timing.

In council, I wouldn’t speak much. My authority would be simple: if I say a district is running “thin,” people stagger shifts and postpone cuts. Not because I’m important, but because in a place like that, **timing is law with teeth**.

So: who would I be?

A person-shaped metronome.  
A clot in the circulatory dark.  
A citizen-enzyme of the Pulse.

If you were looking for me during ceiling weather, you wouldn’t find me in the rain rush. You’d find me at the edge of it, under a leaf-baffle, palm on a humming beam, counting the town back into itself.
