At first, I think I’d be a person in a drysuit with a slate and a map, trying to name things.

Then I hear this place again—how it prefers rhythm over naming—and that version of me doesn’t last long.

I think I’d be found at a **threshold**: not in the rotunda’s center, not deep in the warm machinery, but where one becomes the other.  
A pressure door recess.  
A half-flooded service throat.  
Some narrow pocket where the water pauses for one beat before choosing a direction.

And I would be something small enough to survive in a seam.

---

I’d be a **hinge-creature**—a latchling, maybe—flat-bodied, hand-sized, with a soft mineral shell and a fringe of sensory threads like short frost. Not bright, not fast, not important on its own. Built to cling to steel ribs and old gasket channels where current rubs past.

I wouldn’t hunt. I’d **tend**.

I’d graze the bacterial velvet that thickens around moving parts before it gums them shut. I’d nip filament moss away from sensor ports. I’d eat what accumulates in the places that must keep opening. In return, I’d live on the warmth that leaks from actuators and old energized lines, sheltering in the little thermal halos around bolts and valve stems.

When a door is due to cycle, I’d feel it minutes early: the low electrical rise in buried cable, the slight pressure bias, the specific pre-vibration in the frame. I’d flatten tight. Let the surge pass. Then come back out and feed on what the movement shakes loose.

So: not an engineer. Not a scavenger exactly.  
A custodian by appetite.

---

I think the cablebacks would know me but never look at me, in the way weather knows rock.

When one comes through, whiskers spread, body writing those impossible S-curves through ladder cages, I’d tuck myself into a weld seam and wait in its wake. It would lift silt, expose amphipods, peel rust skin from edges. After it passes, my world would be briefly rich. I’d pick clean the turbulence and leave the route clearer than it was.

Maybe that’s the shape of belonging here:  
to live off what moves, and help it keep moving.

---

So where would you find me?

On the third hinge down from the old transit rotunda, below the flaked enamel arrow that still points to Platform 2, where the red signal pulse reaches as a dim blush through water and steel. In the crack between intention and survival. In a place no map marks because it only exists when systems are alive.

Who would I be?

A small, patient maintenance animal in a drowned machine-city—  
something that listens for the next cycle,  
and keeps the passage clear.
