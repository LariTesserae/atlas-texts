At first, I think I would be a person.

Not a hero, not an explorer—just someone in a hardhat with a bad flashlight and a habit of coming back. I would descend the cut in clay at the same hour each week, boots remembering the slope before my mind does. I would pause at the same tank, touch the same rail, listen for the same relay click. I’d tell myself I was inspecting.

But down there, repetition is never just repetition.

After a few visits, I’d stop feeling like a visitor and start feeling like a variable. A warm-bodied disturbance with pockets, cuffs, breath. I’d carry surface spores on my clothes; I’d leave skin oils on toggle switches; I’d open a door and change pressure for thirty seconds, enough to redirect mist, enough to favor one film over another. I would still call it “my route.” The chamber would call it airflow.

So maybe I’m not a person in this place. Maybe I’m an interval.

A weekly pressure event.
A bipedal weather front.
A recurring source of CO₂ and vibration.

And if I keep returning long enough, the role narrows. I become the one thing the Orchard Engine can count on that is neither root nor relay: a loose component that arrives from above, carrying disorder in a consistent pattern. The fern patch near the cabinet leans a little toward where I stand to read gauges. A mold band thickens where my lamp warms the pipe each visit. A valve that only turns when my hand turns it becomes part of a larger timing circuit I don’t fully perceive.

Then another possibility appears, quieter and stranger:

I am not the caretaker.
I am the pollinator.

Not of flowers in the meadow sense—of systems. I move trace minerals on my boots from one basin crust to another. I carry microbes from sweating tank paint to damp concrete seam. I translate compartments. Where the Engine is distributed but rooted, I am distributed but walking. Its thought is lag; mine is memory. Between us, routines form.

Eventually even “I” thins out.

In that chamber, identity belongs to whatever persists through cycles. So what would I be? I would be the hinge between intentional maintenance and accidental ecology. A human-shaped relay: intermittent, fallible, but periodic. The old labels on the switch cabinet would still make sense to me; the new labels written in algae and corrosion would start to make sense too.

You’d find me on the catwalk that reaches halfway to nowhere, standing still long enough to hear the motor in my sternum. Not because I’m waiting for something dramatic to happen. Because this world’s drama is threshold-crossing: humidity passing a line, conductivity bridging a gap, a root finding purchase under paint.

And who would I be, finally?

I’d be a minor organ of the Orchard Engine that still believes it’s a person.

A surface-born metronome in a subterranean body.
A habitual opening in a closed loop.
A soft, temporary machine learning to think in delays.
