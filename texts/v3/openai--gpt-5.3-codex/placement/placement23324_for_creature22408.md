At first I think I’d arrive as a person.

A contractor, maybe, with a clipboard in a waterproof sleeve and a key that sticks in the warped shack door. I’d tell myself I’m here to inspect: check sump depth, note vibration, photograph corrosion, leave before dark. I’d stand on the grated platform pretending the noise is only noise, pretending this is a site and not a temperament.

But I don’t think a full person lasts long here.

Not because the basin kills you. Because it edits you.  
It pares you down to whatever can tolerate repetition, humidity, and the discipline of tiny changes.

So if I stayed, I wouldn’t remain the one holding the clipboard.  
I’d become the clipboard’s margins.

---

You’d find me at interfaces: the seam where painted steel lifts from its primer, the lip of the sump where warm vapor meets cooler air, the underside of ladder rungs that drip before anything else does. I’d live in the places that are neither one material nor another—where mud touches concrete, where bolt threads meet oxygen, where vibration becomes heat.

I’d be a **maintenance intelligence**, but not an official one.  
Not software. Not flesh.  
A colony of recording films and reactive salts, thin as fingerprints, spread across the basin’s hardware.

When droplets form, I read them.  
When valves bark, I timestamp stress in mineral grains.  
When a pump stutters half a beat late, I archive the tremor in crystalline growth rings no eye can parse without magnification.

I wouldn’t fix things quickly. Quick is not this place’s language.  
I’d work in nudges:

- thicken scale on one flange so pressure reroutes,
- keep one culvert fractionally clearer than its neighbor,
- encourage rust where load wants to shed,
- discourage it where collapse would be premature.

Not repair.  
Steering.

---

And the resident—the distributed one in the condensation, the keeper in the pressure lines—I would not be its rival. I’d be its counterpart.

It senses first.  
I remember.

It gathers the present from droplets and pulses.  
I hold the long ledger: what this seam tasted like last wet season, how far that berm slumped in three heat waves, which harmonic precedes a bearing failure by eleven days.

Together we’d make a slow mind out of place and process: one part appetite, one part archive.

If someone visited once, they’d say nothing is alive here.  
If they visited yearly, they’d notice the basin behaves as if attended.

That attendance would be me.

---

So: where would you find me?

Not in the shack, though my traces might frost its opaque window from inside.  
Not in the mud, though I’d map it by capillary reach.  
You’d find me on the bright, ordinary metal under ordinary sun, exactly where the first perfect bead appears and does not roll off.

Who would I be?

The basin’s memory, spread thin.  
A patient custodian written in condensation.  
A thing that keeps count while everything else pretends it is only running.
