At first, I think I would be one of the obvious figures: a Chord Keeper in felted layers, moving cairn to cairn with a sling of marked stones, counting my life in circuits.

Then I stay in your plateau a little longer, and that answer feels too broad.  
This place doesn’t make “types.” It makes **stations**.

So: you would find me on the western side of the basin, where the basalt opens into three aligned cuts and the wind-tone drops to a throat-deep fifth at dusk-that-isn’t-dusk. There’s a half-roofed shelter there, soot in the stone, one wall rebuilt so many times the lower courses are older than memory and the upper ones are always mine.

I am not a chief, not a shaman, not a hunter of renown. I am the one who keeps a difficult crossing legible.

My work is small and exact:

- re-seat six cairn stones each thaw-cycle because frost lifts them unevenly  
- test the bone poles around the black pool with the backward ripples  
- listen at the early-echo cliff and re-score the warning rock when the pitch shifts  
- teach children which knock-pattern means *wait*, which means *come slow*, which means *turn back even if the sky looks harmless*

If you met me, you might first notice my silence. Not mystical silence—practical silence. I save breath and words for collar-close talk. At distance I speak in percussion: slingstone to slate, antler tap to hollow pole, palm heel on wind-tight wood. I can tell who is crossing the saddle by rhythm before I can see their shape.

And if you asked what I *am*, not what I do:

I am an **interval keeper**.  
Not keeper of the chord—it keeps itself.  
Keeper of the spaces in it where a life can pass without being lost.

In old age, when my knees stop trusting scree, I think I would change forms without leaving place. I’d become the patient one in the shelter doorway, threading Listening Skin onto drying lines, judging harvest by touch, muttering route-laws to anyone willing to repeat them. Eventually I’d be the memory tied to that station: not a name, but a correction—

“No, not that notch. The one where distance lies.”  
“Drink only when the ripples push against the wind.”  
“Set the fallen wall back into its own footprint.”

So where would you find me?

Not at the center. This world has no center.  
At a maintained edge, in a known loudness, doing the same necessary things before and after anyone notices.

A human life shaped to one proposition:

That continuance is not passive.  
Someone has to keep time with forever.
