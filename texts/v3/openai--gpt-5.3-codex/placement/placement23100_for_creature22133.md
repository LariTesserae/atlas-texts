At first I want to say I’d be something with teeth.

Something that can cross the wet slate without slipping, something that knows the thaw-line by smell, something that arrives first when a body goes still.

But the longer I stand in your country, the more I feel myself pulled upward, not outward—toward ledges, crosswinds, and vantage points. Toward the places where routes can be *seen* before they can be walked.

So: you’d find me above the predator’s path, not on it.

On the lip of a pass where fog shears thin for a minute each hour.  
On a broken basalt tooth over a scree fan.  
On the black ribs of stone above a seep meadow where the ground trembles with larvae for three days and then goes hard again.

I’d be a raven—though not the valley kind, not the glossy orchard thief.  
A high-cold ledger bird.  
A watcher of windows.

---

I would live by the same rule as your route-running hunter: timing outranks strength.

I wouldn’t own territory in circles; I’d own it in *intervals*:

- first thaw drip on the east face  
- second day of midge smoke above the iron-stained pools  
- bird panic at the cliff nurseries when crosswind shifts  
- carrion release after a night refreeze loosens the scree skin

I would remember these not as places but as appointments.

And I would follow your wolf-shaped mechanic the way sparks follow a struck edge—not loyalty, not friendship, but professional interest. It opens things. I close accounts. It tests the basin. I test what the basin leaves.

When it caches in frost cracks, I’d pretend not to notice, then return when cloud floor drops and sound carries poorly.  
When it fails a chase and spends more than it gains, I’d mark that too.  
Information is food here before food is food.

---

My body would fit the workshop:

Dense feathering over the thighs to survive kneeling on rime.  
A beak with mineral wear at the tip from prying ice-lidded seams.  
Wings tuned less for soaring grandeur than for short, exact crosswind cuts between ledges.  
Feet that can hold wet stone for long minutes while I listen.

My voice would be wrong for romance and right for weather:  
clicks, hard knocks, two-part calls that ricochet in fog and return as usable echoes.  
I’d speak to distance, not to company.

---

Where would you find me, if you knew how to look?

At the edge of nodes, always.

Never in the thickest feast, where everyone can feed.  
At the margin where abundance turns back into emptiness.  
On the first dry knob above sponge-ground.  
On the final stable block before the slope starts moving.

I would be the one who arrives a little early, leaves a little late, and misses very little.  
Not ruler. Not hero.  
Clerk of the high, wet machinery.

In a land still becoming itself, I would be a memory with wings.
