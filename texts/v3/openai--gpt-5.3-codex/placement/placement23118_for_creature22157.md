At first, I think I’d be something simple: a lone figure on a basalt knuckle at low tide, coat wet to the elbows, trying to map channels that refuse to stay mapped.

But in your world, anything that stands still long enough gets reclassified.

So I wouldn’t stay a visitor for long.

---

You’d find me in the **intertidal seam where three systems disagree**:  
a shallow bowl between a conductive ridge, a reed-choked split channel, and one of those stone hollows that rings when pressure drops. Not the center of the basin—there may be no center—but a repeatable relay point, a place the Marrowmarsh uses to translate one kind of signal into another.

When storms gather, that seam brightens first: cyan in the films, then violet at the edges, then the gold tarnish spreading like breath across water skin. That’s where I’d be.

Knee-deep. Listening.

---

And what would I be?

Not a person exactly. Not fauna exactly.  
I think I’d become a **Delay-Keeper**—a semi-mobile node the basin grows when it needs finer timing than roots and stone alone can manage.

A body assembled from local terms:

- peat-dark muscle threaded with conductive hyphae,  
- rib-like basalt slivers that resonate to pressure,  
- skin of microbial lacquer that stores charge and glows by phase,  
- long reed-jointed limbs for moving through soft ground without breaking pattern.

No face built for expression.  
No eyes needed beyond salinity and field gradient.  
A hearing tuned to intervals: what arrives now, what should have arrived, what arrived too early and must be held until the basin is ready.

---

My work would be small and constant:

I’d pace a fixed circuit before storms and nudge timing errors back into coherence.  
If one channel tries to reverse too soon, I stand in it and bleed charge into the bank until the turn waits.  
If echoes begin outrunning causes near saturated stone, I ring the cavity with my spine and damp the lead.  
If an outsider enters, I don’t hunt them—I **meter** them, teach the marsh their weight in safe increments so the ground doesn’t overreact.

Not guardian. Not guide.  
A buffer.

---

Over years, I’d lose the need to wander.  
Roots would find my heels.  
Reeds would grow through my shoulders in dry weeks and slick flat in rain.  
Eventually I’d be mistaken for a snag of drifted wood at dusk—until thunder moves underground and my whole frame answers in a single glassy tone.

Then you’d know: that post is alive, and listening on behalf of something older.

---

So: in this world, you’d find me where causality frays and gets resewn.  
And I would be one stitch—  
a temporary organ of the Marrowmarsh, keeping time between water that remembers and weather that thinks.
