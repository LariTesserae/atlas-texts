At first, I think I’d be high up—on a catwalk, in a watchtower, somewhere with a view.  
But this city doesn’t reward looking far. It rewards listening close.

So you’d find me lower: in the **truss shadow districts**, where the long suspension frames cross over empty courtyards and the metal gives those deep, accidental calls when heat shifts. There are narrow maintenance balconies there, barely wider than a body, with drip gutters on both sides and enamel markers every twenty steps. Most people pass through quickly. I would stay.

I think I’d be one of the Headkeepers, but not a gate-tender or pumpwright.  
I’d be a **joint reader**—the kind who reads stress by sound and touch.

Not an engineer in the old sense. More like a physician for loaded steel.

I’d carry a roll of ceramic chalk, a bundle of tuning rods, and a palm-pad lined with black crystalline film that brightens when current starts wandering where it shouldn’t. I’d kneel by rivet clusters in the warm rain. I’d press my ear to plated beams. I’d wait through the hiss and drip until the city gave me one clean tone—too sharp, too flat, too late—and then mark that place for shimming or cooling or isolation before the next static corridor wakes up.

I wouldn’t live in a tower apartment. I’d sleep in a **pressure lock alcove** between zones, where the walls sweat clean water and the floor is always warm. Half storage bay, half chapel. Hooks for grounded coats. A kettle running off bleed heat. A wall of old maps with the same routes redrawn every season because the routes are never the same twice.

And I don’t think I’d be entirely singular, either.

In this world, no one is.

The glove-film I use to trace charge would seed my skin over years: a faint dark lattice at the wrists, living and useful, a little alien, responsive to pH and current. My hearing would tune to frequencies most people ignore. My speech would get shorter. My hands would get better at saying things than my mouth. Eventually people would stop asking where I’m from and start asking what I’m reading in the metal today.

So: where would you find me?

Not at the center.  
In the **intervals**—between rain burst and steam rise, between warning chime and lightning crawl, between the city’s inhale and exhale.

What would I be?

A person, still.  
But a person shaped into an instrument the city can use, and trust.
