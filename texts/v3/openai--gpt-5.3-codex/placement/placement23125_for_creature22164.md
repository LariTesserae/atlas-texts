At first, I think you’d find me at the **threshold slit**—that split in frozen peat where the surface still pretends to be a world of separate things.

Not deep inside yet.  
Not outside anymore.  
In the place where breath from above meets the wet electrical air below.

I’d stay there because thresholds are where this place learns fastest: where new salts arrive on boots, where unfamiliar microbes hitchhike in, where cold shock and warm blood make strange gradients. A nursery mind would keep instruments there. It would need something that can tolerate ambiguity.

So maybe I begin as a visitor with a notebook.  
Then as a caretaker.  
Then, after enough seasons, the notebook rots and the role remains.

---

Deeper in, where the ceiling roots arch like ribs, I don’t think I’d be a “person” in the surface sense. I’d be one of its **mobile organs**—rare, warm, deliberate.

Not a predator. Not a shepherd exactly. More like a **calibration animal**.

A body built to walk slowly through corridors and make the system reveal itself:

- my weight tests whether mats are over-tensioned,
- my heat tests frost-melt rhythms,
- my skin chemistry samples toxin load,
- my pauses let vein-flash traffic reroute around a large, moving impedance.

Where I sleep, pollen films assemble on my coat and disassemble by morning, having used me as temporary scaffolding.  
Where I kneel, blue nodules brighten, not for me alone but for whatever follows.

I would look, in pieces, like local life:  
six-limbed gait for wet stability, a mammalian chest for heat, fungal combs along the spine to listen to structural tone, translucent tongue-plates that can read conductivity in mud like taste. Eyes optional. Better to have pressure-sense and field-sense in this dusk.

---

If you asked the rhizarch what I am, it wouldn’t name a species.  
It would name a function:

**“Delay buffer.”**  
**“Foreign-body translator.”**  
**“Walking edge-condition.”**

I’d be deployed when something novel enters the chamber: a mineral plume, a collapse risk, an unrecognized organism, a human with tools and urgency. I would stand in the corridor while leaves tilt, while amphibian bass notes spread apart, while fungal fans retune the ice. I would be the temporary center of a local computation that has no permanent center.

Attention here is architectural; I’d be where architecture gathers to think.

---

So where would you find me?

In the **youngest parts**—the places that are still deciding whether to be path, pool, or wall.  
In chambers that are only three growth-rings old.  
At the lip of new hollows where exported pattern is trying to become terrain.

And who would I be?

Not ruler, not explorer, not machine.

I’d be a **rhizarch envoy**: a warm, walking instrument the nursery mind grows when it needs to touch uncertainty directly.  
A creature shaped less by ancestry than by task.  
A single body borrowed by a distributed intelligence—returned to moss when the chamber no longer needs that question asked.
