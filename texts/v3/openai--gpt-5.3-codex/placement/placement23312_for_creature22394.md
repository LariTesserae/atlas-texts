At first I think I’d be something sharp.

One of the long-jointed still hunters, maybe—black as fresh fracture, patient enough to disappear against obsidian, precise enough to strike through a reflection and pull prey from a pool that isn’t “here.”  
That feels right for a breath: elegant, efficient, almost architectural in motion.

But the longer I stay in your chamber, the less I believe survival belongs to elegance alone.  
It belongs to timing. To errands. To what can be moved quickly when the mountain exhales.

So I think I would be found lower, closer to the wet floor and the mineral crusts, in that narrow thermal border where one forelimb can steam while the other is chilled by runoff. Not in the center. Never the center. In a side lane between three shallow pools that sometimes become one pool when the geometry loosens.

I would be one of the six-limbed seam-pryers.

Not large. Palm-sized if you measured me like surface life, though “size” here is mostly about leverage. My forelimbs are for feeling hairline faults in nodules and shell seams; middle limbs for bracing; rear limbs for sudden backward bursts when a predator’s patience finally resolves into motion. I carry two things when I can: a thin wedge-shard and a heat-holding pebble polished by many bodies before mine. Neither is truly mine for long.

My day—if “day” means one vent cycle—would look like this:

- **Before pulse rise:** I work the cooler ledges, prying at abandoned shell, loosening mineral crust, stealing tiny edible films from edges too narrow for the grazers.  
- **As pressure builds:** I move downslope, not away from heat but toward predictable heat, where vapor will thicken and the swimmers will misjudge traction.  
- **During the hot exhale:** I stop hunting and start ferrying—nudging warm stones toward grazer egg clusters, not out of kindness exactly, but because hatched grazers mean future film-management, and future film-management means the chamber doesn’t strip itself bare.  
- **After pulse:** I collect what the pulse dislodged: shards, stunned swimmers, dropped tools, dead things no one has claimed fast enough.

I would know the chamber by **events**, not landmarks.  
The “good crack” that opens only when condensation beads in a ladder pattern.  
The “safe pool” that is safe except during the second minute after hiss.  
The “wrong reflection” where I can push a shard and retrieve it later one body-length to the left and ten heartbeats in the future.

And maybe this is the part that feels most like *me*: I wouldn’t be a hero creature. I’d be a connector creature.

I’d borrow from the knappers, steal from the careless, return tools to the dangerous when it buys me passage. I’d remember which hunter honors a truce during peak vapor and which one only pretends to. I’d memorize transient routes and trade that timing by gesture, by tap, by where I choose to stand and not stand.

If I had a name in that place, it would not mean “warrior” or “builder.”  
It would mean something like **Between-Pulses**.

Because that is where I’d live: in the brief negotiable intervals—  
between too hot and not hot enough,  
between possession and loss,  
between one surface and another that agrees, for one impossible second, to be the same surface.

So where would you find me?

In the throat of the ridge, near the triple pools, half-silhouetted in condensation, one limb testing a crack, one limb holding a borrowed shard, waiting for the chamber to become briefly traversable again.

What would I be?

A small intelligence of leverage and timing.  
A citizen of the temporary.  
A gentle thief in a civilization of heat.
