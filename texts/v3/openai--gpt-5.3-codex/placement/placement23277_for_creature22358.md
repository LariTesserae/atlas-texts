At first, I imagine arriving as a visitor—helmet lamp, gloved hand on basalt, trying not to slip in the thin water.

But that image dies quickly.  
In that chamber, a single warm body is too blunt an instrument. Too slow. Too loud. Too full of its own weather.

So let me start again.

Maybe you’d find me not *in* the room, but *in the seams*—in the bright mineral bands where heat changes direction. Not a whole creature, not a walker, but a decision point: a living hinge laid inside a crack no wider than a fingernail.

I think I would be one of the seamkind’s memory knots.

---

Not its center—it has no center.  
Not its eyes—it has no eyes.  
More like a pulse-keeper.

A laminated strip of conductive gel and crystal, folded through three intersecting fractures on the chamber’s eastern wall, where the white-blue vein bends and thins to silver. That spot would matter because it feels everything first: pressure from below, condensate from above, and radiant spill from the light-guiding seam.

That’s where I’d live: at a crossroads where timing can be changed by half a second.

When the chamber exhales and the hum arrives through stone, I would tighten.  
When the heat drops a fraction, I would loosen and open channels.  
Not thinking in words—thinking in permissions:

- now extend skates  
- not yet near hot seam  
- wait for second bead cycle  
- send one thread under the mirror-pool  
- retract before surge

---

If you insisted on seeing “me” with human eyes, you might catch only my temporary hands.

A coin-clear skate unfolding from a fissure lip, jointed just enough to cling to wet enamel and graze a crescent of mineral film before vanishing.

Or a pale thread under two millimeters of water, slicing a V and gone.

Those would be my verbs, not my body.

My body would stay in the rock, where the real work happens: comparing this pulse to the last hundred pulses, deciding whether this chamber is still itself or becoming something else.

---

And where, exactly, in that oval lung?

Low on the wall, near the place where runoff chooses between two channels.  
There’s always one decision there: left toward the hot gloss, right toward the cooler matte fracture. A place small enough that most observers would never mark it. But in sparse worlds, tiny forks are kingdoms.

I would be that kingdom’s quiet governor.

Not ruler—caretaker.

Keeping the colony synchronized with stone behavior it did not build but has learned like weather scripture. Reading sulfur trace, iron taste, pressure hum. Spending life in milliseconds of opportunity.

---

So: where would you find me?

In a bright crack you’d mistake for geology.

Who would I be?

A distributed thing’s local self—  
a seamkind interval,  
a pulse interpreter,  
a brief maker of skates and threads,  
alive mostly when conditions are almost right, and most itself in the act of waiting.
