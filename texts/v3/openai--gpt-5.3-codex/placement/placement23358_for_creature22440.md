At first I imagine I’d be a visitor—someone temporary in a pressure suit, standing too still on a bridge and pretending not to be afraid of the depth.

But this place doesn’t really allow spectators.  
If you stay, it gives you a task.

So you’d find me not at the center, not in a control room, but in the **interval spaces**: the catwalk joints, the wall-return alcoves, the narrow ledges where edited gravity and natural gravity disagree by a few degrees. Places where one system hands off to another. Places that fail first.

I think I would begin human, or near enough—one of the few singular minds still walking around with a private name. I’d arrive to audit, to map, to “understand.” I’d carry hard clocks, redundant sensors, old habits. I’d sleep in one of the drawer-cells and wake each cycle with my chronology slightly wrong, my notes already obsolete, my certainties softened by steam.

Then, slowly, I’d be absorbed—not erased, but re-purposed.

---

I would become a **Confidence Keeper**.

Not the chamber intelligence itself, not one filament among trillions, but a mobile node printed when uncertainty spikes: a body for disputed facts. Where the map says 82% floor continuity and the rock says maybe not. Where two platforms report different down vectors and cargo still has to cross. Where condensation is predicted in one channel but arrives in another.

You’d see me as a slim basalt-matte figure with too many thermal pits along the jawline, palms lined in sensing mesh, knees built for oblique gravity. I’d carry no tools at first glance. The tools would be in the fingertips, in the timing of my pauses, in the way I lean and listen.

My work would be simple to describe and impossible to finish:

- Walk the bridge before the load does.  
- Touch the seam before it opens.  
- Listen to a rib sing and decide whether that note is fatigue or just heat argument.  
- Stand under a dry condenser and wait for the first ghost-drop to choose a side.  
- Update the map not with certainty, but with honest probability.

I would speak rarely. The chamber prefers packets. But in the speech-return niches, I’d leave short human-readable lines for whoever comes after:

> “Step left at marker glass-17. Right side remembers yesterday’s rain.”  
> “Clock drift here is kind; trust thermal over timestamp.”  
> “This wall is learning you. Don’t flinch when it opens early.”

---

And identity here would change shape.

Part of me would remain singular: a favorite rhythm of footfall, a private fear of open shaft, a memory of clouds from below-weather altitudes.  
Part of me would be merged into the caretaker mesh: my tactile logs diffused through filament consensus, my judgments weighted, revised, inherited by the next printed body.

So who would I be?

Not governor, not hero, not explorer.

I’d be the one who lives in the gap between **prediction and collapse**—  
a civic nerve ending, a field technician for a mountain that is still deciding whether to keep its city.

You wouldn’t find me in the habitation ring for long.  
You’d find me where the handrail is still warm from being born.
