At first, I think I’d be up high—one of the broad-winged gliders, all distance and thermals, writing circles over the ocher plain.

But if I stay in the world longer, that feels wrong for me. Too exposed, too singular.  
I think I would be lower, closer, in the crowded quiet.

Not in the open drainages, not at the first drips where everyone queues at dawn. I’d be where a cliff folds inward into a shallow, north-facing pocket: a place that looks dry by noon but darkens at night, where water beads along a mineral lip and collects in three potholes no bigger than cupped hands. Above it, an old stacked-stone marker. Below it, a den mouth patched with resin, thorn fiber, and shin-bright bone.

I would be stone-kin, but not a hunter at the front, not a breeding rival in storm dusk.  
I’d be one of the keepers of routes—an older, dust-matte individual with scarred toes and a throat-fan gone a little dull, good at stillness, better at memory. My work would be small and constant: testing damp seams with my jaw, refreshing scent lines before sunrise, turning skull bowls to catch dew, shifting pebble stacks a finger-width to mark that a seep is late, that a nest ledge collapsed, that a safe traverse now belongs to another clan branch with hatchlings.

I would “speak” mostly through substrate. Three claw taps in a hollow column for *wait*.  
A long flank pulse for *water held*.  
A brief brow-ripple for *hawk-shadow*.  
And sometimes, at deep night, jaw to stone, I’d feel the cliff answering back with everyone else’s movement—beetles at the film, juveniles restless in nursery hollows, a grazer file passing over old cataract-smooth rock—until the whole amphitheater becomes a shared sentence.

If you were looking for me, you’d pass by twice without seeing me.  
I’d be the iron-red shape flattened against a warm rib of bedrock, right beside a rib cage set upright as a windbreak, watching condensation gather one drop at a time, keeping a map that is never drawn and never finished.

So: I’d be found at the edge where dry meets damp, where age holds detail.  
And I’d be one of the ones who make persistence legible.
