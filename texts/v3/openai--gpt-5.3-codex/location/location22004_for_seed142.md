At first it seems like a mountain city at night.  
Then, as your eyes adjust, you realize it is not a city built *on* a mountain range — it is a city laid into the exposed bones of an old world, across a high plateau where erosion has finished all the dramatic work and left only certainty.

The land is lifted far above any sea that might once have existed. There are no rivers, no ice seams, no damp shadows, no moss-darkened cracks. Even the word *dry* feels too wet for it. The ground is a pale composite of wind-polished stone, sintered dust, and old salts fused into ceramic crust. Whole districts sit on terraces that were once ridgelines, then worn into broad, patient steps over ages so long that jaggedness has become rare. Nothing here looks fresh. Every edge has been negotiated into a curve.

The darkness is nearly complete, but not chaotic. It is a managed dark, a civic dark. The sky is a matte black-violet dome with a weak starfield, as if a veil of mineral haze hangs high and permanent above the plateau. Street grids are visible only by low amber strips embedded in paving joints and by red indicator lamps on towers, vents, gantries. No bright signs, no floodlights; illumination is rationed to function. From a distance the settlement resembles a circuit board left running in a cold room: sparse points, straight lines, long intervals of black.

Because there is no vegetation at all, construction never competes with growth. Plazas stay clear for centuries. Rail cuts remain open. Pipes laid across a basin stay visible until replaced, not swallowed by roots or lichen. The built environment has become geological in return: foundries carved directly into escarpments, cargo lifts running in shafts that were once fault lines, habitation blocks cast in refractory concrete that slowly vitrifies on the surface. Old districts are not demolished; they are abandoned in place, then repurposed when needed. Infrastructure layers over infrastructure, but with large distances between active nodes. It is urban by organization, not crowding.

Industry persists in a low, steady state. Not boomtown energy — maintenance energy. Refinement, separation, annealing, recycling. Atmosphere processors hum under berms. Ore breakers operate in enclosed vaults to keep dust from fouling bearings miles away. Thermal plants vent through tall chimneys with no visible plume; there is too little moisture in the air for dramatic exhaust. Conveyors move slowly. Cranes rest for long stretches between deliberate motions. The whole place behaves as if urgency was engineered out generations ago.

Sound travels oddly in the thin, warm air. Most of the time there is almost nothing: the faint electric whine of transformers, a distant impact from a stamping line, the occasional ticking contraction of metal cooling in shadow. Footsteps carry. A dropped bolt can announce itself across a courtyard and then vanish into vast silence. Wind exists but rarely commits; it drifts rather than gusts, lifting dust only in shallow veils that settle quickly. Without leaves, without insects, without water, there is no background rustle — just intervals.

Life is present, but scarcely. Fauna here are small and infrequent, adapted to heat gradients and mineral scavenging. You might see a six-legged sheath-beast, no larger than a toolbox, crossing a service lane to lick oxidized films from a rail. Sometimes pale membrane gliders circle heat stacks, not birds exactly, more like opportunistic kites of cartilage and foil-thin skin. They nest in ventilation baffles and emerge singly. Nothing swarms. Nothing sings.

The strangeness is not in spectacle; it is in the rules. Chemistry runs lean. Moisture is a contaminant. Organic decay is almost absent, so objects do not rot — they abrade, crack, and become dust. Time does not erase quickly here; it edits slowly and permanently. A warning stencil can survive two hundred years. Tire marks can remain for months. A collapsed overpass becomes a new path and remains one for generations.

And above all, the place feels settled into equilibrium. Not dead, not ruined — concluded.  
A high, warm, lightless plateau where stone, machine, and sparse life have agreed on terms and kept them for a very long time.
