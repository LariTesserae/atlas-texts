The place is a **city packed into a vertical wound** in a mountain so high it breaches weather.

From above, you would miss it. The plateau is a cold, pale skin of stone and salt-glass, wind-scored and almost featureless except for one split: a crack only a few meters across, dropping so far that light thins before depth ends. That is the whole landscape here—not broad, but **deep**. Extreme elevation turned inward.

Inside the fissure, construction has grown like a mineral deposit. Platforms, braces, gantries, furnace rooms, cable lifts, and habitation cells are bolted directly into fresh rock. Nothing is old enough to look softened. Corners are sharp. Cut faces still show drill marks. Broken stone lies where it fell last week, not yet weathered into anything gentler. It is a young place, geologically and socially: recently opened, rapidly occupied, still deciding its final shape.

There is almost no water. Pipes carry compressed gases, powdered ores, and occasionally hot oils that are never spilled casually because every drop must be reclaimed. Cleaning is done with abrasive dust and static fields. Cooling happens through heat exchangers and vent stacks. Air itself is treated like a resource stream: captured, scrubbed, recirculated, taxed.

No plants survive here. No moss in cracks, no lichen halo, no accidental green. Surfaces are stone, oxidized metal, ceramic insulation, soot film, and translucent polymer baffles that glow faintly in the dim. Color is mostly rust-red, magnesium white, basalt black, sulfur yellow where vents leak, and the blue-violet pulse of induction coils.

And yet the place is crowded with life.

Fauna fill the city the way pigeons fill old stations, except these are not birds. They are dry-world animals: six-limbed cliff-runners with keratin pads, pale tunnel eels that swim through ventilation ducts, fist-sized warm-bodied scavengers that nest in cable trays, and high-altitude gliders whose wing membranes are metallic with conductive dust. Some are domesticated; many are simply tolerated because they eat slag-mites and insulation lice. At shift-change, animals and workers move in interleaved streams, near-touching, each species knowing which ledges belong to whom.

The atmosphere is cold but not frozen. Breath ghosts briefly, then vanishes into intake drafts. The air pressure changes with height inside the crack, so neighborhoods are stacked by lung tolerance and machine requirement. Lower levels are denser, louder, hotter; upper levels are thinner, cleaner, and meaner. Crossing three tiers can feel like crossing climates.

It is, strangely, mostly quiet.

Not silent—never silent—but quiet in the way a machine room can be quiet when all frequencies are damped by mass. You hear:
- cable hum,
- distant hammering,
- ceramic chimes tied to warning lines,
- claw-scrapes of fauna on grates,
- short radio bursts,
- and, at intervals, the low body-sound of the mountain adjusting.

The turbulence is constant, but not always acoustic. This city lives in instability: pressure surges through ducts, ore chutes jam then release, cranes reroute, ledges are abandoned after fresh fractures, entire workshop blocks migrate upward on rail racks when gas pockets are discovered below. Routes that were safe in the morning may be sealed by evening. Work crews paint arrows directly onto rock, and by next day half the arrows point into void.

Light is dim and practical. Most illumination is task-specific: bench lamps, furnace doors, signal strips, helmet beams, hazard strobes, and the occasional shaft of cold daylight from far above. Faces are often lit from below by instrument panels, giving everyone the look of being mid-confession. In deeper sectors, bioluminescent fauna are encouraged—whole corridors are navigated by the slow pulse of nesting colonies.

The rules of the world are simple and strict:

1. **Nothing leaks**: air, heat, fuel, dust, and time are all inventory.
2. **Nothing stays fixed**: every anchor is temporary, every plan conditional.
3. **Height is economy**: where you live, what you breathe, and what you earn are measured in vertical meters.
4. **Edges are alive**: if a surface is warm, vibrating, or newly exposed, something will claim it—human crew, machine extension, or animal colony.

So the landscape is not a panoramic wilderness. It is an intimate, vertical, industrial canyon-city: dry as bone, cool as iron, crowded as a hive, dim as late dusk, always shifting, and held together by bolts driven into a mountain that is still being born.
