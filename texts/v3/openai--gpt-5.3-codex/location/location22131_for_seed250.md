At first it seems like a sea, until you notice where the horizon sits: far below.

The world is a chain of newborn high basins, suspended along a lifted crustal arc, each basin filled not with settled water but with moving depth—cold, mineral-rich fluid that sloshes and climbs as if gravity is still being negotiated. Between basins, ridgelines of black glassy stone rise sharp and unweathered, barely eroded, their edges too clean, too recent. Nothing old enough to crumble. Nothing soft enough to remember.

Water dominates everything. It hangs in the air in veils and pressure sheets, falls sideways in continuous walls of rain, then is pulled upward again by thermal columns and electromagnetic tides. Clouds are not overhead but embedded among the cliffs, so dense they behave like lakes you can walk around but not through. The entire region feels submerged in an atmospheric ocean: droplets in suspension, mist under pressure, condensation breathing in and out with planetary rhythm.

Despite the wetness, life is sparse to absent. There are only a few mats of low, metallic-green vegetation gripping cracks where heat leaks from the young rock—threadlike fronds, pale and efficient, more chemistry than flora. No animals at all: no flight, no movement in the water beyond currents, no eyeshine, no tracks. Emptiness is structural here. Distances between presences are enormous. A whole valley may contain only one patch of growth and a single standing pillar of stone.

The loudness comes from matter in conflict. Water hammers fresh basalt. Steam vents roar from fissures that opened this week, this hour. Deep in the basins, cavitation booms roll like artillery, then return as long metallic echoes from cliff to cliff. Lightning is frequent but oddly controlled: branching sheets that run along mineral seams in the air itself, as if the sky has circuitry. Thunder arrives in stacked pulses, almost digital—bursts, pauses, recalculation, burst again.

Nothing built stands here, yet the place behaves as if engineered by a physics beyond contemporary understanding. Currents route themselves in repeating algorithms. Rain bands self-correct to maintain pressure gradients. Whole water columns rotate into stable geometric vortices—hexagonal wells, square-edged whirlpools—that persist for days before collapsing. The stone extrudes in patterned terraces, not from design, but from a planetary-scale fabrication process that looks uncannily intentional. This is a landscape still under compilation.

Light is mostly ordinary daylight—silver, cool, clear when the mist thins—but it is constantly refracted by suspended water and charged aerosols. Shadows split into multiple offsets. At noon, cliffs cast prismatic ghost-shadows that drift out of sync with the rock. At dusk, the basins glow from within where bioluminescence would be, except there is no life: only chemiluminescent mineral reactions along fracture lines, cold fire under black water.

The governing rule of the place is instability with memory. Every storm changes the terrain, but not randomly; each new form seems to inherit equations from the last. Channels appear overnight where no river had run, then vanish, yet the next storm follows their absent path. You can feel the world teaching itself how to be a world—vast, loud, mostly empty, and very young—while standing in weather deep enough to drown the sky.
