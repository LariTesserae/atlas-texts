What lives there is not an animal, and not exactly a spirit.  
It is a **watershed intelligence**: a population of beings whose bodies are made out of three things the basin has in excess—thin water, resonant stone, and delayed light.

At first you only ever see their *edges*.  
An iridescent lattice on damp rock that turns a few degrees every hour.  
A shadow pooled in a hollow where no object casts it.  
A drip rhythm that “answers” your footsteps before your boot lands.  
Those are not separate phenomena; those are organs.

A single adult is distributed. It has no fixed outline, but it does have structure:

- **Hydric tissue**: capillary films and beaded runs across stone, where it moves mass and chemistry.  
- **Acoustic tissue**: pressure patterns lodged in hollows, chimneys, and ice wedges; this is where it senses and thinks.  
- **Umbral tissue**: detached shadow collected in depressions and fissures; this is slow memory, thermal buffering, and sometimes camouflage.

When wind plays the pillars and channels, the inhabitants are not merely hearing it—they are *being driven* by it, the way a heart is driven by electrical timing. The basin’s famous “wrong” physics is partly theirs: they maintain bifurcating streams, preserve contradictory erosion paths, and hold echoes out of sequence because their cognition depends on overlapping versions of events. They do not break rules randomly. They farm stable paradox.

The old stone waymarks, runoff walls, ladder grooves, and simple shelters are theirs too, from an earlier life stage often called the **hand phase** by the few who’ve studied traces. In that phase they condense into short-lived, load-bearing bodies: mineral cartilage, flexible salt-glass skin, no bones (hence the bone-free middens), and tool use that is practical rather than monumental. They build only what alters flow, resonance, and drip timing—never “cities,” always instruments. When the hydrology is corrected and the season turns, those bodies are abandoned, dissolved, reabsorbed. Intelligence returns to distributed form.

They reproduce in the films you noticed. Overnight, a mature colony seeds a wet face with rotating geometric membranes. Each membrane tests local conditions—temperature gradient, mineral concentration, echo lag. Most fail at noon and vanish. A few lock into phase with the basin’s pulse and become juveniles: first as tiny adjustments in bead-angle and runoff split, later as full acoustic-shadow organisms spanning entire ravines.

They are not hostile in the mammalian sense. But they are selective about coherence.  
Anything that introduces too much unpatterned disturbance—explosives, engines, shouting in enclosed cuts, heavy heat sources—gets “edited out”: mists thicken, footing liquefies under crust, echoes mislead direction, and travelers circle back to where they started. Not malice. Homeostasis.

So what lives there is a **choir of weather-bodies** that long ago stopped distinguishing between habitat and self.  
The basin is not an empty stage awaiting inhabitants.  
It is an organism made of many minds, and those minds are made of the basin.
