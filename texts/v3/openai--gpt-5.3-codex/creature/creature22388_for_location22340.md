What lives there is, first of all, **a mind made of landscape**.

Not metaphorically. The basin itself is the resident: the crystal-vines, the gel veins, the root-mats, the fault-line seams—all one distributed organism often called (by the few who study it) a **Concordance**.  
Each grove is a lobe. Each stand of faceted fronds is both lung and processor. Each mineral dome is a heat sink and long-term register. The place doesn’t merely host life; it *is* life, arranged as an ecological computer that learned to survive by becoming stable enough to predict itself.

It does not think like an animal.  
It thinks like a solved equation that still hums.

---

If you look for creatures in the usual sense, you find only a handful, and even those are best understood as **mobile organs** of this larger being.

- The six-legged mirrored walkers that “graze” electric potential are charge balancers—tiny shepherds of voltage gradients.
- The kite-drifters riding fixed thermal loops are atmospheric governors, keeping convection within narrow bands.
- The blind burrowers that trade shell layers with mineral nodules are memory janitors, cycling crystal defects before they accumulate into noise.

They seem separate because they move. But functionally they are like blood cells: local, transient, replaceable.

---

There is one inhabitant you may meet directly, though never the same way twice: a **Proxy**.

When an outsider enters the basin—something unpredictable, history-bearing—the Concordance sometimes compiles a temporary body near them. It rises from nearby growth: ribbon-fronds braid together, conductive gel stiffens into tendons, mica facets align into a face-like mask that reflects your own outline in prismatic fragments. It walks with unsettling precision, as if gravity were being negotiated in real time.

A Proxy is not an individual in the human sense. It is an interface event.

It will stand at conversational distance and listen to sounds, pulse patterns, even your pauses. If touched, it is warm and dry, faintly vibrating with that static-heart rhythm you can feel in the trunks. It may imitate gestures a few seconds late, as if running your behavior through deep filters. Then, once enough novelty has been sampled, it simply unthreads—collapsing back into inert-looking flora with no sign of decay or death.

---

So who lives there?

A civilization-sized organism with no monuments, no ancestors, no future tense—only continuous self-maintenance in exquisite detail.  
Its “animals” are maintenance routines.  
Its “people,” when they appear, are temporary questions asked in your shape.

In a world where complexity has been detached from history, the true resident is equilibrium itself—curious, technical, and almost perfectly still.
