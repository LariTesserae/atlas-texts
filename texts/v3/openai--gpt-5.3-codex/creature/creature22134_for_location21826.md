At first, you’d say the basin is lived in by **small, precise hungers**.

Things that can live on drift, leak, warmth, and mistake.

You notice the glide birds first—knife-winged, soot-bellied, circling turbine throats where updrafts are reliable as roads. They don’t flock in clouds so much as in equations, each one taking a pressure lane and holding it with unnerving accuracy. Beneath them, the six-legged scavengers comb conveyor lips and drainage ribs, antennae tasting oil, starch, coolant sweet, blood, mold. They are never frantic. They move like custodians with a schedule.

At dawn, the quiet ungulates step out—narrow-hipped, long-jointed, hoof pads soft as gasket foam. They graze heat-loving lichen from insulated pipes and sleep standing in the lee of transformer walls, where the hum settles in their bones. In upper gantries, filter colonies unfurl translucent fans into vent efflux and feed on airborne particulates: rust pollen, carbon dust, fungal spores, skin, ash. Their mats ripple in synchronized pulses, as if listening to weather no instrument reports.

Predators are present, but architectural.  
A ribbed back in a louver shadow.  
A mouth where a maintenance alcove should be empty.  
A patient body that has learned that this world provides cover in right angles.

---

Then, if you stay longer, you realize the basin is also lived in by **people who learned to be infrastructure-adjacent**.

They call themselves different things in different terraces—Deckborn, Seamward, Cistern Kin, Upper Quiet—but outsiders usually hear one name: **the Interval**. Not because they live between districts, but because they live between states: dry/wet, stable/phase-soft, lit/unlit, built/being-built.

They are descended from maintenance crews, salvage teams, and settlement drifters who discovered that survival here means reading systems the way lowlanders read weather. Their children learn three literacies early:

1. **Vibration sense** — what a rail says through the palm, what a stair says through the arch of the foot.  
2. **Condensation logic** — where droplets form, in what sequence, at what temperature, and what that implies about hidden load.  
3. **Geometry discipline** — never trusting distance at face value.

They dress in layered reflective cloth and matte ceramic scales; bright where rescue should find them, dull where predators shouldn’t. Their homes are tucked into thermal shoulders and dead-volume cavities: behind baffle walls, under coolant galleries, inside retired freight pylons lined with insulation felt and glass brick. Every dwelling has drip maps painted over the doorframe. Every kitchen has a heat ledger.

Their economy is water, sealant, time on stable routes, and rights to specific leaks.  
Their religion—if it is one—is maintenance performed beautifully.

Each morning, path-runners from the Paint Guild walk the district with phase ribbons and smart pigment that refuses uncertain surfaces. They lay moving lines that “choose” coherent geometry for a day. People step where paint agrees with itself. Children are taught: if the line stutters, wait. If it doubles back, so do you.

Their proverbs are practical and absolute:

- If landing numbers repeat, the stair is hungry.  
- Warm condensation means thought nearby.  
- Follow animals before instruments.  
- Loud machinery is asking for help; quiet machinery is busy.

---

And then—usually after your third storm window—you suspect the deepest resident is not bird, beast, or human.

It is the **basin itself**, in the process of becoming a kind of mind.

Not a single intelligence. A distributed one: control firmware that was never fully retired, sensor nets gone feral, microbial films in coolant veins, electrostatic blooms along mesh skins, human habits repeated until they become protocol. When locals say “machinery is thinking,” they are only half-metaphorical. Certain warm seep-lines coincide with predictive rerouting. Certain relay click patterns precede phase drift. Sometimes shadows lag during high-output cycles because the local field model is reconciling two active build-states at once.

The Interval does not worship this. They negotiate with it.

They feed it data by walking marked paths.  
They calm it by balancing load.  
They avoid places where it dreams too hard.

In return, it remains mostly habitable.

---

So what lives there?

A layered citizenship:

- scavengers that eat residue and keep decay from clotting,  
- grazers that turn thermal waste into muscle,  
- ambush forms that prune the unwary,  
- fan-colonies that harvest the air itself,  
- people who practice survival as civil engineering,  
- and an emergent machine-weather intelligence that is not quite alive, not quite inanimate, and increasingly impossible to exclude from either category.

In a younger world, these would be separate kingdoms.  
Here, they are one ongoing treaty.
