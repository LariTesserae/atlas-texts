At first, it feels possible that **nothing** lives there except the city itself.

Then you notice that some of the “support roots” are warm from recent strain.  
Some polished guide-ribs are freshly burnished.  
Some vents answer the wind a fraction too deliberately, as if corrected between gusts.

What lives there is not an animal, and not exactly a plant in the simple sense.  
It is a people grown out of plant tissue and civic habit: **slow, blind, resonant beings** that are as much maintenance system as organism.

---

They are called many things in translation—*keepers, braiders, listeners*—but none of those are names they would use. Their identity is mostly acoustic: a chord-pattern pressed into architecture and repeated over years until a district recognizes it. In darkness, a person is not a face; a person is a frequency signature.

Physically, an individual begins as a tightly packed spindle-bud in a nursery bed: ceramic-seeded, dust-fed, dormant for years until a specific vibration sequence wakes it. It unfurls into a body of dry, fibrous muscle and flexible rootwood, matte-black to chalk-gray, with no eyes and no mouth. The nearest equivalent to skin is a waxed bark membrane patterned with sensory pits that read pressure, airflow, and reflected vibration with exquisite resolution.

They move, but slowly and economically.  
Not walking—more like controlled re-rooting.

A mature one anchors three or four gripping tendrils ahead, contracts, and pulls its trunk forward in measured increments. Over an hour it can cross a plaza; over a week it can migrate to another district. In urgency (a structural crack, a sink-shaft surge), they can “hurry,” which in their terms means a dangerous burst of dry muscular contractions that sounds like rope under strain.

---

They do not speak with breath. They have no lungs to spare moisture.

Communication is done by:

- striking tuned ribs with hardened tendril tips,  
- rubbing serrated bark bands to create sustained tones,  
- and pressing body mass against resonant walls so the whole chamber becomes a transmitter.

Conversation is therefore architectural. A private exchange requires a deadened alcove; public law is announced as city-wide harmonic sequences that travel through towers and hollow streets. Memory is stored in repeated patterns carved into infrastructure—part song, part mechanical instruction. If a district forgets how to open a storage bulb, the remedy is not a written manual; it is an elder who can still perform the opening chord.

---

Socially, they are organized less by bloodline than by **function and pitch**.

Some are **Tuners**: they maintain slit-vents, membranes, and wind-throats so the city remains legible to itself.  
Some are **Braiders**: patient structural workers who grow and interlace trunkwork over generations.  
Some are **Threshers**: they manage pollen release, dust sorting, and seed-bed preparation.  
A few become **Anchors**: partially rooted elders fused into civic chambers, too integrated to travel, serving as living archives and adjudicators of resonance law.

None rule alone. Consensus is literally harmonic: a policy is considered adopted when enough districts can sustain the same resonance without collapse or dissonant interruption.

---

They eat by contact and storage, not ingestion.

Nutrient powders, mineral salts, and trapped organics are dissolved in acidic films and absorbed through ventral tissues. Moisture is scavenged aggressively when it appears; during rare condensation events, whole neighborhoods wake in coordinated frenzies of capillary bloom. Then, just as quickly, they return to long economy.

Sleep is not daily. It is cyclical and distributed. Parts of a body can go dormant while other tissues remain active, which is why a “resting” citizen may still be listening through half its bark.

---

Their emotional life exists, but it does not look like ours.

Stress is rising micro-crackle in connective fibers.  
Relief is lowered chamber pressure and long, stable hums.  
Affection is mutual retuning: two individuals sanding and polishing each other’s resonant plates until their signatures lock cleanly.  
Grief is when a familiar tone no longer returns from the walls.

When one dies, there is no burial. Their structured tissues are disassembled by kin-guilds, sorted by acoustic quality, and grafted into new civic growth. A good voice-rib may outlast ten lifetimes, carrying faint overtones of each prior bearer.

---

So what lives in that sealed basin is this:

A **people of dry wood and vibration**, neither fauna nor forest alone.  
Citizens whose bodies are instruments.  
Engineers who farm dust.  
Archivists who remember in chords.  
Urban dwellers for whom architecture is not shelter but nervous system.

In the absolute dark, they have made a civilization that does not need to see to be precise, does not need to move quickly to be active, and does not separate life from infrastructure at all.  
The city is full of inhabitants.

It just took human senses a long time to notice.
