What lives there is not a creature you can point at.

It is a **people-shaped system** now: a distributed, plant–fungus intelligence spread across terraces, ropes, shelters, and water-layers. If it ever began as “settlers,” that category no longer helps much. The basin kept the stone and took the flesh, and what remained learned to live as tension, buoyancy, and growth.

They are sometimes called (by the rare outsiders who leave records) the **Moorbound**.

---

At first encounter, the Moorbound look like infrastructure:

- reed bundles lashed too neatly to be drift,
- vine curtains that are always exactly where footing is safest,
- fungus racks that somehow never rot in the same way twice,
- old platforms that turn, just slightly, before storms.

Then you notice pattern.  
A frond grove pulses in a rhythm that matches distant thunder delay.  
A spillway’s flow is narrowed overnight with woven leaf felt.  
Line-weights vanish from one shelf and reappear where ice will calve next dawn.

Nothing with eyes did it.  
Something absolutely alive did.

---

### Body plan (if you insist on one)

Each “individual” is a **knot-colony**:  
a dense core of woody jointed stems anchored in rock cracks, braided through with mycelial cords and saline bladders. From that core trail manipulators—rope-vines, buoyant mats, and pressure-fronds—that can reach meters to tens of meters depending on age.

A knot-colony can:

- sense pressure gradients through water and stone,
- detect dissolved mineral shifts like scent,
- read lightning ionization before rainfall intensity changes,
- emit low blue-green stress light on purpose, not just reflexively.

It cannot walk.  
So it tows itself by growth, by anchoring and releasing, by recruiting floating biomass. Movement is seasonal and strategic, like glacier travel.

---

### Mind and communication

No colony is smart alone for long. Intelligence here is **networked**.

Mycelial threads run through submerged terraces and old human masonry, linking knot-colonies into temporary cognition fields. During storms, conductivity rises; the network thickens; decisions become basin-scale. In calm, minds thin back into local problem-solving.

They “speak” in three media:

1. **Knock-language**: controlled impacts of logs and stone on hollow markers (those haunting clacks in fog).
2. **Glow syntax**: pulse frequencies in submerged fronds, readable through rain-blurred water.
3. **Tension script**: pull/release patterns along rope-vine lines spanning platforms and breakwaters.

To an untrained ear it is weather noise.  
To them, it is law, memory, warning, and sometimes song.

---

### What they do all day

They are hydraulic custodians. Constantly.

- They braid and unbraid floating mats to steer current shear.
- They maintain “safe wrongness”: channels that look dangerous but are stable, and channels that look calm but are marked taboo.
- They farm cold fungi under overhangs, rotating substrates by mineral phase.
- They harvest storm-pollen in rain funnels and feed it into growth beds.
- They keep ancient moorings deliberately noisy, because silence means vertical pull.

Their oldest practice is called (approximately) **listening the stone hum**—placing living tissue against basalt ribs to read deep flow reversals before surface signs appear.

---

### Their relationship to the old shelters

The half-domes and sod walls are not homes in the mammal sense. They are **dry memory vaults**. Inside: clay tablets scored with flow-rules, seed-spores kept in peat ash, coiled fiber maps that encode currents by knot spacing rather than geography.

The Moorbound do not inhabit these structures continuously. They circulate past them, reactivate them, reseal them. Each shelter is a punctuation mark in a sentence the basin keeps rewriting.

---

So: what lives there?

Not beasts.  
Not ghosts.  
Not quite “plants,” not merely “fungi,” and no longer people in the old anatomy.

What lives there is a **hydrological civilization**: cold, patient, communal, and exquisitely adapted to a world where weather is habitat, water has strata like sky, and survival means learning rules that move.
