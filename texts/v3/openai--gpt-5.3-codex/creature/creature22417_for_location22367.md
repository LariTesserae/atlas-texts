What lives there is easiest to mistake for weather.

At first you’d call it **the megastructure itself**—just growth, just hydraulics, just an overachieving forest-architecture. Then, after weeks, patterns stop looking mechanical. Certain basins always fill first, certain terraces always sacrifice themselves in storms, certain tower-groves “wake” before rain and brace nearby spans. Not random. Not centrally commanded. Responsive in a way that feels like temperament.

The inhabitants are not animals and not individual trees.  
They are **distributed botanical persons**: long-lived, valley-scale federations of tissue called (if you need a word) **Choirs**.

A Choir has no single body. It exists as a connected district of living infrastructure—columns, retaining roots, tendon-bridges, wet plazas, drain throats, leaf-fields—bound by shared flow rhythms. Where flow synchronizes, identity begins. Where synchronization breaks, identity ends.

---

A young Choir starts from damage: a storm-sheared lattice, floating mats, split trunk-vaults, exposed root-fans. Those fragments root into one another and begin exchanging pressure signals. At first it’s crude—open/close, swell/release, drink/dump. But with every rain cycle, the network gains memory in structure: thicker here, compliant there, delayed valve response in one channel, rapid venting in another. Decisions become morphology.

It “thinks” in hydraulics.

- Rising pressure in upland cisterns = anticipation  
- Coordinated wall-flex across kilometers = intention  
- Intentional collapse of a waterlogged terrace = sacrifice  
- Rapid rerouting after landslip = learning

Its senses are distributed too:  
strain in fibers, temperature gradients in canopy skin, mineral taste in seepage, vibration signatures from thunder, even sunlight angle inferred from evaporation pace. It doesn’t see; it resolves. It doesn’t hear; it resonates.

---

If you met one up close, you wouldn’t find a face.  
You’d find a **knot-hall**: a thickened nexus where many channels cross and pulse-rich sap moves under high pressure. These are the nearest thing to organs of attention. They are where new geometry is drafted—fresh rib walls extruded, spillways reopened, tendons tightened, whole platforms gently tilted to redirect future storms.

Some Choirs are conservative, reinforcing old districts for decades.  
Some are reckless, growing fast, overreaching into unstable swales, then rebuilding from collapse.  
Some split often, budding daughter Choirs downstream after monsoon seasons.

They communicate with one another through:

1. **Hydraulic percussion** (those deep booming trunk-vault reports)  
2. **Runoff timing** (sending water pulses as coded delays)  
3. **Reflective signaling** (coordinated leaf-angle glints at dawn breaks)  
4. **Graft embassies** (temporary root-bridges that exchange chemistry, then self-sever)

No speech, but absolutely discourse.

---

What do they want? Not territory in the animal sense.  
They want **continuity of flow under change**.

A successful Choir is one that can stay itself while continuously replacing itself: losing platforms, growing replacements, metabolizing debris, rerouting water, surviving thermal spikes, and keeping its internal timing coherent through noise. Death is desynchronization—when a district still grows but no longer responds as one mind.

So whoever lives there is neither beast nor ghost nor caretaker caste hidden in the mist.

It is a civilization of rain-fed plant intelligence,  
each citizen a watershed-sized pattern,  
each thought a rearrangement of living architecture,  
each heartbeat a storm.
