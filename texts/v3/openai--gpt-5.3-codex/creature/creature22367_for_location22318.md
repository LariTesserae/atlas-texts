At first, you would say **nothing** lives there in the animal sense.  
And for most of the day, that is almost true.

Then you start to notice one patch of ground that is never quite where you remember it. A low hump near the broken wall one week, nearer the roofless shelter the next. Not a burrow, not a rockfall. Just a patient relocation, as if part of the terrain has the right to move very slowly.

What lives there is a **settled creature**—something between reptile, root, and stone.  
Its back is plated like weathered shale, matte gray-brown with tan dust caught in seams. Along its flanks grow sparse, thorny filaments like the shrubs around it; in dry months they look dead, but at dawn they darken with condensation and bead with moisture it can draw inward. Its underside is cool and clay-colored, broad enough to spread weight so it leaves almost no prints on compacted soil.

It does not hunt, call, chase, or display. It feeds by waiting:  
night dew, thin films of damp, mineral-rich mud from shallow depressions, trace salts from stone. When rain comes, it rises just enough to angle its body into the runnels and drink what passes. When rain stops, it lowers again and becomes part of the fold in the land.

By daylight it is often mistaken for a low outcrop. Toward evening it moves—quietly, in short deliberate shifts—between moisture-holding places: the shaded side of the old mud-brick room, the darker streak beneath a ledge, the shallow channels where water lingers longest. Lichens on nearby stones are thicker where it rests, as if its stillness helps them keep the night damp.

It is solitary. Not rare because it is hunted, but because this kind of life only works where change comes gently. Its rhythm matches the rules of the place:

- take water when it visits  
- grow only in available gaps  
- wear down instead of breaking  
- make no sound unless movement is necessary  

If you stood at dusk and heard a soft tick from cooling rock, then another, slightly out of sequence, that might be it unfolding one limb from beneath its plated back. Not emerging dramatically—just continuing, in the same quiet arrangement, as it has for a very long time.
