At first glance, it seems like no one lives in the Green Hollow except condensation.

Then you start to notice that *condensation* has routines.  
That vines are clipped at shoulder height in places where shoulders pass.  
That handrails are warm where hands were recently.

And gradually it becomes clear: the Hollow is inhabited by several kinds of resident, layered inside each other.

## 1) The first resident is the district itself

The Green Hollow lives there as a single distributed organism—part city, part wetland, part machine memory.

Its “body” is made of trained trunks, reed turbines, cable-root lattices, and sap lines carrying both sugar and signal. It grows by accretion, but also by response: where load repeats, fibers thicken; where footsteps recur, roots raise a guide-rib; where metal is forgotten, moss domesticates it and brings it into circulation. It does not hold still long enough to become architecture in the static sense. It is always *becoming* structure.

If you ask who inhabits a room, often the answer is: the room inhabits itself now.

---

## 2) The second residents are the few humans who can tolerate a place that never quite agrees with maps

They are not numerous enough to make crowds. You meet them as traces first—tool marks in bark laminate, clipped branch stubs tagged with copper foil, fresh resin seals on gourd tanks.

When you do see them, they look less like settlers and more like negotiators:

- **Rootwrights** who lace new supports through steel hoops and old cambium.
- **Moisture clerks** who read wetness as topology and issue route advisories no cartographer would believe.
- **Voltage gardeners** who prune conductive moss from rails before it buds into side-currents.
- **Shift ecologists** who monitor swarms, molds, and predator absences as if reading weather reports.
- **Geometry wardens** who mark “near corridors” after rain—places suddenly adjacent because damp growth made them so.

Their clothing is practical and tactile: oilskin layered with woven bast fiber, insulated gloves with textured fingertips for reading ridged handrail code in darkness. Many carry both pruning knives and multimeters. Most speak softly, partly from habit, partly because loud noise startles systems they depend on.

They do not try to impose permanence. Their craft is timing.  
They say things like, “That wall is in early leaf,” or “The stair is moving toward pond,” or “Don’t schedule freight on a dry week; distance will lengthen.”

Home, for them, is less a fixed address than a recurring compatibility with certain cycles.

---

## 3) The third residents are animals that treat infrastructure as habitat because it was designed that way—and then surpassed design

The turbine reeds belong to whiskered gliders by night; elevator pits are amphibian nurseries by rain cycle; catwalk planters carry hoof marks from heavy pollinators that browse while carrying entire dust-clouds of fertile light.

Many species here are quiet specialists:

- felt-wing insects that sip film-water from cable jackets,
- gutter fish that arrive with storm pulses and retreat through impossible hydrology,
- bark-scraper arthropods that leave script-like feeding traces across sound baffles,
- root-thread beetles whose larvae accelerate decomposition around stressed footings.

Predation happens, but almost never as spectacle. You notice the outcome: a branch suddenly empty, a cloud of pollen collapsing as if folded, feathers rotating slowly in maintenance light. Nothing remains long. Detritivores and molds translate death into new load-bearing substrate before grief can become monument.

In the Green Hollow, to be eaten is also to be installed.

---

## 4) The fourth residents are phenomena that have crossed into behavior

Because reality loosens here, certain conditions function like organisms.

**Shadows** are not just absences of light; in deep corridors they collect, pool, and are consumed. Creatures that drink from them phase out for minutes, moving as omissions in the air.  
**Wetness** is not just moisture; it is a transport network that can collapse distance.  
**Rain** is not only weather; it is a jurisdictional event that can invert indoors and outdoors.  
**Memory** is not archival; it is mechanical in root tissue, pressure-written and slowly thickening under repetition.

These are not metaphors to residents. They are operational facts.

---

## 5) What “living there” means

To live in the Green Hollow is not to dominate a plot. It is to participate in a metabolism of revision.

No inhabitant, human or otherwise, keeps final authority over form. The place accepts management, rejects mastery, rewards attention, and punishes assumption. It asks every resident to be part engineer, part naturalist, part listener.

So who lives there?

- A city that is still germinating.
- People who maintain without freezing.
- Animals that exploit every seam between scaffold and stem.
- Dark, damp, pressure, and growth themselves—conditions promoted to citizenship.

By the time you’ve learned one route, the Hollow has learned you back.
