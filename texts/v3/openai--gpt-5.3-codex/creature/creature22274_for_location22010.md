What lives in that stone sea is not one species but a **pattern of species**—a crowded, disciplined civilization of bodies that learned to survive in a world where nothing softens.

At the lowest level are the **eaters of heat**: matte films on vent lips, sulfur-chalk colonies that build brittle “reefs,” spore veils hidden in cracks. They do not look alive in the surface-world sense. They look like stains, bloom-rims, mineral fur. But everything above them depends on them. Entire migrations are timed to when a vent face “ripens” into edible chemistry.

Then come the small visible multitudes:  
- **dust-runners**, six-legged and low, with fan-whiskers that read airflow like script;  
- **glass-raspers**, shield-backed grazers whose mouths are ringed with silica plates;  
- **silt eels** that swim through powder drifts and erupt from them silently;  
- and **hitchhiker ecologies**—mites, feeders, parasites, cleaners—living in the folds and hinge-joints of larger beasts like cities on moving cliffs.

Most of them are dimly luminous, but only incidentally: a pulse to keep formation, a scrape-glow from ceramic tails, a brief gland-flash when startled. Predators usually carry the opposite adaptation: **light suppression**. Their skins drink glow. They hunt by warmth gradients, static charge, and the minute percussion of footsteps through fractured stone.

The large hunters are terrifying mostly because they are patient. They wait at thermal bends where warm currents force herds to narrow. They move one limb at a time so armor plates never click. Their kill style is often collapse, not chase—spooking prey toward glass scree, powder sink, or blind ledge. In this basin, terrain is a weapon older than any tooth.

And among all these lives, there are the people—call them the **Seamfolk**, though each route-clan has its own name. They are tactile navigators with broad, callused feet and sensory whisker crests along jaw and forearm. Their eyes are functional but secondary; the true map is felt: pressure in the sinuses, draft direction on cheek pits, sulfur notes on the tongue, grain size under heel. They “see” distance by airflow age and heat layering.

Their culture matches the basin’s logic: conservative, precise, ritualized. They build little, repair often, inherit paths. A safe ridge is sacred because it remains safe. Their songs are not melodies so much as encoded route-memory—vent sequence, dust corridor, predator shelf, burial alcove. Bells exist, but silence is law; a bell is struck only for the dead, and only in caves chosen for muffled walls.

So what lives there, finally, is a **quiet abundance**: armored grazers, invisible ambush, drifting spores, and a people who treat stability as weather and repetition as history. In a black warm abyss where water is myth, life has become hard-edged, tactile, and astonishingly close-packed—less a wilderness than a living mineral city that never quite sleeps and almost never shouts.
