What lives there is, first of all, **a civilization of organs without a city**.

Not metaphorically—biologically.  
The dominant life is not individual trees or beasts, but **processing guilds** spread across ridges and basins: comb-fronds that comb dust, grinder thickets that pulverize grit, ferment mats that chemically strip minerals, haul-vines that pump concentrates uphill, and tower-colonies that vent spores and heat. Each patch can survive alone for a while. None of them are complete alone. Together they behave like a lung, a refinery, and a weather instrument.

Among them move the animals—never in random scatter, always in routes:

- **Glider herds**: long-ribbed, six-limbed sailers with lockable membranes and bone shutters over the ears. They launch from escarpments, feed on volatile-rich resin blooms, and land by folding into the lee of column-plants like dropped tools.
- **Film grazers**: low, armored scrapers that rasp micronutrient crust from root-bark and stone skin. Their mouths are more mill than jaw.
- **Subsoil packs**: pale burrowers with electroreceptive snouts that track active root currents underground, raiding tuber vaults before freeze pulses.
- **Kite pollinators**: tendon-light fliers that hook claws into resonant stems and wait for the right frequency; flowers only open when the note is correct.
- **Rupture scavengers**: fast, heat-sensing runners that appear minutes after a pressure-burst, harvesting resin shrapnel before it oxidizes.

Predators are there too, but they do not “stalk” by sight. They hunt by **timing gaps**—the half-beat in a rattling seedfield that means something with mass just stepped wrong. In that noise, silhouette is seismic.

And then there is the thing you only notice after weeks:  
the biome itself seems to remember.

Damage a ridge, and distant growth changes angle days later. Harmonic corridors persist across generations. Dawn venting sequences repeat with regional dialects. In rare calms, everything waits at once, as if holding breath for a conductor’s baton. It is hard to prove mind in a place like this, but easy to feel coordination beyond local reflex.

So what lives there?

Not one species.  
A **choir of dry flesh and mineral chemistry**, tuned to wind;  
animals that navigate by vibration and field;  
and possibly—spread thin across a continent—an emergent intelligence that does not think in words or images, but in **pressure, phase, and resonance**.
