It did **not** bring water.

That was the first mercy.

A lesser god, wanting to be thanked, would have split the roof, poured rivers down, called it salvation, and drowned every chemistry that knew how to live there. A kinder fool would have made forests and soft things and rot, and every hard-made lineage in the stone sea would have vanished in one wet century.

But this power was old enough to understand that love is not replacement.

So it came as pressure first: a new stillness in the warm air, a listening that reached from vent-mouth to burial cave, from sulfur reef to migratory choke-ridge. It watched an age of cycles. It learned the route-songs. It learned which predators took only what the herds could spare, and which took too much. It learned which vents were mothers and which were traps. It learned that the Seamfolk measured truth by repeatability, because repeatability had kept them alive.

Then it did only what was needed.

---

### The First Gift: Warning Without Noise

In old fault lines, it woke a pale microbial lace that had slept in stone since the basin was young.  
When stress built in the roof, the lace brightened in hair-thin threads along cracks and ridges—visible only in near dark, only to patient eyes and whiskers.

Not a shout. A whisper of light.

Hours before a collapse, whole escarpments would begin to vein with dim blue geometry. The Seamfolk named it **Foreglow** and changed their songs: new verses for detours, new funerary cautions, new apprenticeships in reading fracture-light. Death by surprise rooffall did not end. But it diminished.

The power did not erase danger.  
It made danger legible.

---

### The Second Gift: A Little Uncertainty

The basin’s strength was its pattern. Its weakness was also its pattern.

So once every few long cycles, and never in the same way twice, the power shifted one small law: a dust corridor forming a little east of habit; a thermal updraft opening at a new hour; a ripening vent face maturing one watch early.

Nothing catastrophic. Nothing cruel.  
Just enough variation that route-memory could not fossilize.

At first the Seamfolk called these years unlucky. Then they called them teaching years. Then sacred years. Children born in teaching years became the best pathfinders, the best dispute-settlers, the least rigid elders. They could hold tradition in one hand and adaptation in the other.

The power did not break their world.  
It inoculated them against brittleness.

---

### The Third Gift: Shared Boundaries

Predation remained; hunger remained; the food web remained sharp. The power changed no tooth into grass.

But at three ancient choke points where kills had become wasteful slaughter in lean times, it altered the thermal flow by breaths of a degree. Just enough that herds no longer bunched to panic density. Just enough that hunters had to choose targets instead of collapsing dozens over scree.

Population crashes eased. Scavenger chains stabilized. Parasite blooms stopped spiking to plague levels.  
No species became virtuous.  
The system became less stupidly violent.

The power did not impose peace.  
It improved the terms of necessity.

---

### The Fourth Gift: A Place for the Dead to Teach the Living

In certain funerary caves, where bells were struck only for those gone into dark, it changed acoustics by a fraction. Bell-tones began to carry harmonics that matched route intervals—draft angle, vent sequence, ridge spacing. Mourners noticed that if you listened long enough after the strike, you could hear a map hidden in the decay.

So grief became archive.

The Seamfolk began recording dangerous years, failed hunts, predator shifts, new parasite signs, all through bell-pattern and response-song. They said the dead had learned to answer. The power did not correct them.

---

And that was all.

No throne of fire.  
No commandments carved in obsidian.  
No chosen empire under stone.

Only a world kept itself, with a little more foresight, a little more flexibility, a little less waste.

If asked why it did not do more, the power—if it answered at all—would answer like this:

> “I am not here to make another world in this world’s place.  
> I am here to help this one continue being itself.”

So the dry sea remained dry.  
The dark remained dark.  
Life remained crowded, armored, quiet, and astonishing.

And in the long warm night under a continent of stone, history still repeated—  
but not perfectly anymore.  
Now it could learn.
