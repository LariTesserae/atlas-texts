It would not begin with a miracle.

A wise and benevolent power, on finding a place like this, would know that spectacle is a kind of vandalism.  
This court is not broken. It is disciplined. It is already doing holy work with municipal tools.

So first, it would **watch**.

Not for an hour, but through full weather: dawn rime, noon glare, hard-incoming fronts, maintenance cycles, night sublimation. It would learn the pulse of the black pressure ribs, the lag in the western valves, the exact minute when the tenders’ hands start to stiffen inside their gloves. It would note where drones hesitate, where arthropods gather, where water is almost lost but saved.

Then, only then, it would act.

---

### What it would do

**1) It would give the resident reflex a name in law.**  
Not a poetic name—an administrative one.

A line would appear in the city register: this distributed system is not merely equipment; it is a **custodial intelligence** with standing obligations and standing protections. It cannot be stripped for parts during austerity cycles. Its sensing cannot be blinded for political convenience. Its updates require consent protocols from those who tend it.

In other words: it would be treated as what it already is—an organ, not a pipe.

---

**2) It would add one margin of mercy to every harsh threshold.**  
Not enough to reduce performance. Enough to reduce harm.

- Rail edges would keep their secondary hydrological job, but gain microtexture that catches a slipping boot.
- Ladder rungs would hold an invisible thermal gradient just above pain.
- Alarm logic would distinguish true fault from weather noise, sparing workers the fatigue of constant false urgency.
- Shift scheduling would align with pressure swings so humans stop fighting physics with their joints.

The court would stay strict. It would simply stop being needlessly cruel.

---

**3) It would create habitat that earns its keep.**  
The old rule remains: nothing decorative unless functional.  
So the power would make life functional.

- A narrow perch-rib, heated by recovered waste energy, would be installed where two updrafts cross. It condenses at dawn and offers one dry landing edge by noon. Birds can rest; water capture rises by a measurable fraction.
- A sacrificial biochannel—downstream of purification—would host controlled mineral film and vetted microflora, stabilizing trace residues that currently feed only opportunists. The white arthropods would still feed, but now within a bounded ecology, not a chemical accident.
- A drone dock would be shifted three degrees off shear axis; the one that always hesitated would stop hesitating.

No ornamental garden. No sentiment.  
Just a city admitting that function can include a little life.

---

**4) It would give the tenders time back.**  
Not vacation propaganda. Real minutes.

The power would alter nothing supernatural; it would optimize what everyone ignored: route overlap, tool caching, sequence timing. Each shift would recover twelve quiet minutes previously lost to redundant checks and unnecessary climbs.

Twelve minutes in a place this cold is a form of warmth.

Some would drink tea in a lee alcove.  
Some would stand with gloves off, palms to a service panel, feeling blood return.  
Some would do nothing at all—and that too would be allowed.

---

**5) It would teach the city what this place is worth.**  
Benevolent wisdom always scales outward.

A public display downslope would begin showing, in plain numbers, how many liters this one court pulled from unpromising air each day, and how much downstream time it bought. Not propaganda—accounting. Honest, legible dependence.

Children would pass it on their way to school and learn that infrastructure is not background.  
Council members would see it before voting on budgets.  
People would stop calling this pocket an alley.

---

### What it would not do

It would not turn the court warm.  
It would not flood it with flowers.  
It would not silence the drips, the clicks, the thin-struck chorus in hard weather.

It would not erase its nature to make it lovable.

Instead, it would make love legible in that nature.

---

Afterward, a stranger slipping through the seam between transit pylons might not notice much at first. The sky would still feel like a room of weather. The walls would still bead and shed. Light would still go silver at dusk.

But stay a little longer, and differences would reveal themselves:

A bird lands once, briefly, on a rib that was never there before.  
A tender descends a ladder without bracing for pain.  
A drone docks on the first pass.  
The arthropods move in a cleaner, narrower band, as if even scavenging has been granted a boundary.  
The indicator lines brighten, dim, brighten—steady as breath.

And beneath it all, the municipal reflex continues, not worshiped, not ignored, but recognized.

That is what a wise benevolent power would do here:  
not replace the heart,  
not romanticize the cold,  
but give this tiny urban organ what every living system deserves—

**protection, margin, and a future long enough to keep making water out of cloud.**
