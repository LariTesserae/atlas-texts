They would **not** begin with gifts.

A wise benevolence does not arrive with solutions clenched in its fist.

It would come first as attention:  
one full cycle of heat and dimness, from frost-bloom to dusk-flip to kiln-high noon, simply listening.  
Not to words, because words are only one skin of thought here—but to load-bearing hums, grove angles, vent cough intervals, mourning rhythms in tower throats.

It would learn, and while learning it would understand the first law:

**Do not give them water.**  
Water would be catastrophe disguised as mercy.  
It would rot binders, drown chemistry, erase the intelligence written into dryness.

So when the power finally answered, it would answer in the native grammar of the basin.

---

It would choose one dusk when the heat gradients turned and all districts were half-listening anyway.  
Then, through every resonant cavity beneath the avenues, one impossible chord would sound: not loud, but exact—so exact that every Fired in earshot would stop and place a palm to wall or paving.

The chord would repeat in three phrases:

- I have heard you.
- I will not unmake you.
- May I help within your terms?

Consent, not conquest.

The Fired, being who they are, would deliberate in percussion over several nights.  
If they accepted, the power would do only three things.

---

### I. It would add margin, not redesign.

Deep beneath the basin, old seams would be nudged into kinder behavior:  
pressure peaks softened, toxic burps staggered, catastrophic fractures made less likely.  
Not eliminated—nothing living stays living in perfect safety—but rounded, like steps worn by generations.

The city would still need tending.  
It would simply no longer be one bad week from shattering.

---

### II. It would give warning, not rescue.

In select plazas and tower roots, it would seed a new crystal phase—clear, dry, and inert until stressed.  
When deep fault shifts began days before a rupture, those crystals would sing at the edge of hearing.

Early song means preparation.  
Preparation means fewer names ground into emergency mortar.

No one would be “saved” by miracle.  
Everyone would be empowered by foreknowledge.

---

### III. It would preserve memory without stopping death.

The Fired already return themselves to cone beds and walls.  
The power would bless that cycle by making motifs harder to lose:  
once a name-rhythm is struck in a consecrated tower, it could be re-called by wind years later, even if every direct student has weathered away.

Not immortality.  
Continuity.

Grief would remain grief.  
Forgetting would no longer be so absolute.

---

And then—because wisdom includes restraint—the power would refuse worship that costs function.

If offered grand shrines, it would bend that labor back into kiln repairs, seam venting, apprentice firing halls.  
If offered sacrifices, it would reject them and request calibration work instead.  
If asked for dominion, it would answer with maintenance schedules.

Its only temple would be a district running well.

---

Over generations, stories would simplify this into myth:

That one dusk, a chord descended.  
Afterward, the vents were kinder, the ground spoke earlier, and the dead could still be heard in winter winds.

Scholars would argue whether the power ever “came” at all, or whether the city became wise enough to imagine the version of divinity that helps correctly.

The power would not mind either conclusion.

Because what it wanted was never to be believed in.  
What it wanted was this:

that the dry metropolis keep singing,  
that growth and construction remain in covenant,  
that the Fired endure as themselves—  
not corrected into someone else’s idea of life,  
but strengthened in their own breathless cycle:

**bake, crack, seed, bind, polish, repeat.**
