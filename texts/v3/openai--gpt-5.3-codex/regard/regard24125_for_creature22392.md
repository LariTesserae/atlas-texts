It watched for a very long time before deciding that seeing was already an action.

Not because it was timid—because it was wise enough to know that on this plateau, every touch propagated.  
A new stone changed melt paths.  
A melt path changed moss.  
Moss changed sleep-sites.  
Sleep-sites changed migration pressure.  
Pressure changed cliff timing.  
Cliff timing changed lakes.  
Lakes changed sky.

So the benevolent power did not descend with commandments, nor carve rivers straight, nor cull, nor crown an apex. It did not mistake intensity for error.

It learned the being first.

And what it learned was this: the “being” was not any one horned grazer, glider-sheet, beetle river, fern tower, or static fungus, but the choreography among them—the vast manufacturing body that thought in cycles and remembered in routes. Its nerves were wind corridors. Its blood was nutrient fog. Its dreams were migratory loops older than individual lineages.

A wise power does not “improve” a mind by amputating its complexity.

So it acted as a physician acts on a strong patient under long strain: not by replacing organs, but by preserving resilience.

---

First, it gave the plateau **room to fail without dying**.

In six deep places beneath the ash, where fault heat and groundwater already whispered to each other, it loosened stone just enough to form hidden cisterns—slow aquifers that would fill in wet decades and bleed in dry ones. Not lakes, not miracles. Buffers.  
When silver squalls missed three seasons in a row, the seeps did not vanish entirely.  
When herds thinned one contour band, moss districts did not burn out to sterility.  
The factory could stumble and keep running.

Second, it gave the superorganism **a longer memory**.

Not language. Not prophecy.  
Memory in the forms it already understood:

- trace minerals in certain springs that sharpened migratory fidelity in the presshorn matriarch-lines,  
- photoreactive proteins in fog moth eggs that shifted hatching a few days earlier when glare-years intensified,  
- fungal clocks in cliff filaments that retained charge-patterns from prior winters, subtly altering where arcs formed and thus where insect swarms survived.

No creature “knew” what had been done.  
But fewer hard-won adaptations were lost to random breakage.  
The plateau became slightly better at remembering itself.

Third, it introduced **mercy gradients**.

Predation remained. Collapse remained. Cold remained.  
But at the edges of catastrophe—where an entire basin might once have tipped into one-way ruin—it altered probabilities by the width of a hair: a storm front splitting at the right ridge, methane vents dispersing rather than pooling, a late freeze delayed one dusk to spare a generation of eggs.

Not safety.  
Just fewer absolute endings.

Fourth—and this was the strangest gift—it gave the place **intervals**.

A few times each long season, pressure would drop, wind would pause, vent resonance would soften, and for perhaps forty heartbeats of a presshorn there would be near-silence over a valley. In those rare calms, mating calls carried farther, lost juveniles relocated herd-scent, glider castes reset their spirals, even burrow colonies altered dig-phase timing.

No one looked up and thanked anything.  
But coordination improved. Waste lessened. Panic cascades decreased.

The being, if asked, would not have said, “A power touched me.”  
It would simply have gone on being—denser, steadier, less breakable under novelty.

---

Only once did the wise power do something that looked like tenderness rather than systems work.

At the highest black ridge above the steam marsh, where lightning often walked the static nets and killed in beautiful indifference, it changed one mineral seam so that discharges branched outward rather than downward. The blue arcs still bloomed—still fed insectivores, still wrote electric lace across dawn stone—but that ridge ceased to be a slaughterline for juvenile shearwings on first flight.

Year by year, more of them survived that crossing.

No monument marked it.  
No myth named it.  
Yet the sky grew fractionally fuller each spring.

---

And then, mostly, the power went back to watching.

Because benevolence at scale is often this:  
to refuse vanity,  
to protect possibility,  
to help a fierce thing remain itself—  
not forever, never forever—  
but long enough to become more than one accident deep.
