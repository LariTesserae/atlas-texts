The wise power does not arrive in thunder.

It arrives as **attention**.

For a long while—one full turning of cold and thaw, maybe more—it does nothing but watch. Not from above, like a judge, but from within the place’s own terms: in pressure shifts, in echo-return, in the timing between drip and drink. It learns the basin the way the basin is learned here: by repetition.

And what it sees is this:

- not chaos, but choreography;
- not cruelty, but costly balance;
- not one beast, but a distributed one—the rift-choir, alive across many bodies.

A lesser power would pity the harshness and try to “fix” it.
It would pour in warmth, deepen soils, calm winds, brighten days.

A wise one understands that this would be an extinction event wearing a kind face.

So it chooses interventions small enough to be mistaken for luck.

---

### What it does

**1) It keeps the water truces from breaking.**  
Around seep-holes and black puddles, the old law already exists: no bloodshed here. The power does not invent this. It strengthens it. Not with commandments—with consequences of feeling. Predatory muscles hesitate at the rim. Panic thins. Thirst rises above hunger for a few breaths longer than before.  
Enough for one more drink.  
Enough for one more generation.

**2) It teaches stone to hold fog a little better.**  
Not rivers. Never rivers. Just micro-ledges where condensation beads instead of vanishing, hairline roughness where droplets gather and fall true. A hundred new drips across a year. The kind of change no single creature notices, but all of them survive inside.

**3) It tunes collapse away from nesting walls.**  
Rock must fall; young mountains stay young by breaking. The power does not stop that. It nudges fracture timing by cold degrees and thaw minutes so major shears choose emptier faces more often than chance would allow.  
Loss still happens.  
Catastrophe happens less.

**4) It gives one gift to the echo-hunters—then takes half of it back.**  
For a season, their calls return cleaner, farther, with exquisite maps in the dark. Kills rise. So do fear and flight, and the choir begins to fray. The power sees this and withdraws the gift to a middle ground: enough clarity to prevent blind waste, not enough to dominate every shadow.  
Mercy, here, means refusing superiority.

**5) It seeds a new signal into the mirrored beetles.**  
When fog becomes lethal-thick, violet flash shifts toward a deeper pulse that means *shelter now*. Insectivores learn first, then gliders, then the rope-tails by following bodies. A weather-warning grows from bioluminescent rumor into culture.

**6) It remembers for those who cannot write.**  
No monuments can survive here. So the power stores pattern: where colonies thrived, where fungus bloomed after iron rain, which ledges held through ten hard winters. During migrations and recolonization, creatures feel inexplicable preference for good old places and faint aversion to death-traps.  
Instinct, they call it.  
Archive, says the power.

---

### What it refuses to do

It does not civilize.  
It does not crown a single species.  
It does not make night into day.  
It does not end predation, because then it would end the choir.  
It does not ask for worship, because no one here has spare breath for liturgy.

And it does not stay visible.

In a place this young and sharp, permanence is arrogance. The power acts like lichen, not empire.

---

After many years, if you returned with instruments, you might measure almost nothing dramatic:  
slightly higher juvenile survival near seeps,  
fewer colony-erasing slides,  
more stable traffic at dusk,  
a subtle widening of viable ledges.

If you returned with patience, you would feel it immediately:

the basin is still hard, still dim, still crowded with teeth and wings and need—  
but now the arrangement can absorb one more winter than it used to.

For this mountain, at this age, that is what benevolence looks like.

Not rescue.

**Continuance.**
