It would begin by **not arriving all at once**.

A benevolent and wise power, seeing this high wet crown and the people who keep its agreements, would know that sudden goodness can be a kind of violence. A new sun burns. A perfect answer silences living questions. So it would come in the only way this place can trust: as pattern before person.

For one season, nothing but small mercies:

- three failing condensers recovering half a breath of efficiency at dawn,  
- a landslip pausing long enough for a nursery route to clear,  
- storm charge dispersing around an archive-shrine that should have taken a strike and did not,  
- one old gravity channel, cranky for decades, choosing gentler “down” without being coerced.

The Kept would notice. They always notice.

They would log it in vent-rhythm, in reed notch, in route-song. Not miracle. Not yet. An anomaly with manners.

---

When the power finally made itself perceivable, it would do so **at maintenance scale**: not in the sky, but kneeling-height, palm-open, beside a weather house whose symbols had just begun to wake.

It would ask permission in the local grammar of ethics.

Not *Do you worship me?*  
Not *Do you obey?*  
But: **“What do you need kept that you cannot keep alone?”**

And because the Kept are what they are, they would not answer quickly. They would run the question across ridgelines and households, marsh and tower, predator corridor and fungal basin. They would ask children and flood-watchers and those who tune old shade planes by ear. They would include nonverbal witnesses: glider roost behavior, moss retreat lines, insect hatch timing.

Their eventual answer would be precise, and humble:

> “Do not perfect this place.  
> Help us preserve its capacity to adapt.”

A wise power would smile at that, if powers smile.

---

So what would it do?

### 1) It would repair only what collapse would make irreversible.

It would not optimize abundance.  
It would not erase scarcity.  
It would identify the thin hinges where one break becomes many: a buried fault under a marsh belt, a disease pressure in root mesh, a resonance drift in the oldest cliff-arcs.

It would mend those hinges quietly, so the system could continue arguing with itself in healthy ways.

### 2) It would give tools that require relationship, not dependency.

No inexhaustible engine. No button that solves weather forever.

Instead: a small set of intelligences grown into ceramic tags and field rings—advisors, not rulers. They would model consequences across decades, but only answer questions asked in full context: watershed, species, season, ritual use, migration routes. If asked selfishly, they would return silence or uncertainty. If asked collectively, they would illuminate tradeoffs with painful clarity.

The gift would sharpen stewardship, not replace it.

### 3) It would broker treaties with the nonhuman majorities.

Not fantasy speech with beasts; something truer.

It would help the Kept formalize what they already practice: mapped ambush corridors given legal standing, glider roost towers designated as co-owned infrastructure, insect emergences protected as seasonal rights, grazing access tied to reedbed renewal metrics. The power would add enforceable field-signatures so these treaties were legible even after memory loss, diaspora, or political fracture.

Law written into matter, renewed.

### 4) It would create one refuge against deep-time catastrophe.

Hidden in a cool fold beneath basalt: a **Memory Cistern**.

Inside: seeds, spores, microbial consortia, route-songs, maintenance grammars, stories of failure, stories of repair, recordings of vent cadences, and the ethics charter that begins “Nothing kept alone remains keepable.”

Not a bunker for elites. A continuity organ for the whole biome-cultural mesh.

It opens only by quorum from dispersed nodes. It cannot be owned, only accessed in stewardship.

### 5) It would refuse the throne.

Others would come, eventually—traders, pilgrims, extractors, missionaries, frightened empires seeking stable water futures. The power would not become a weapon of isolation or conquest. It would stand as witness and boundary condition:

- no external claim without ecological bond,  
- no technology transfer without afterlife plan,  
- no treaty valid that ignores nonhuman dependencies.

It would protect permeability, not purity. Contact, but with terms that keep the mountain alive.

---

And what of the Kept themselves?

A benevolent power would not freeze them into sainthood. It would allow disagreement, faction, error, reform. It would watch them argue over risk budgets and sacred wetlands, over whether to retire an ancient ring-stone or let it fail into habitat. It would let them become more than one culture over time, because living systems speciate.

It would intervene only when coercion threatens the conditions of consent.

In their festivals, a new threshold would be added: **the Day of Asked Help**—the day they admitted limits without surrendering agency.

Their children would grow up with a proverb:

> “Even wisdom must ask where to stand.”

---

After some centuries, the power might thin its presence further.

Not gone. Distributed.

In vent timing that still drifts toward resilience.  
In a legal phrase embedded in shade-plane firmware.  
In the instinct of a route-council to wait one more cycle before forcing a fix.  
In the habit of touching warm ceramic and listening before adjusting.

That is what a wise benevolence does here:  
not salvation, not dominion—  
**companionship at the scale of systems**.

It helps them keep what can be kept.  
It helps them release what must change.  
And it leaves them more themselves than it found them.
