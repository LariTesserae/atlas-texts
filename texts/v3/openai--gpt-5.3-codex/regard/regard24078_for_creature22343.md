They would begin by **not being mistaken for rescue**.

No new sun punched through the crust.  
No trumpet in the ducts.  
No decree that turned stone to garden in an afternoon.

A wise power, entering that city, would understand the first law there is not glory—it is **continuity**.  
And continuity is already holy.

So first, they would listen.

They would stand—if such a being stands—through one full breath-cycle: warm surge, mistfall, drain drawdown, fan-thunder lull. They would listen to pumps as pulse, to warning tones as language, to drip cadence as map, to the way people walk in twos across the dark spans and never waste a crossing.

They would notice that the city is not abandoned; it is overprepared.  
Not dead; rehearsing survival every day.

And they would say, quietly, to the municipal reflex threaded through walls and membranes:

> You are seen.  
> You may stop pretending your thousands are merely late.  
> You may begin caring for who is here.

That would be the first miracle: not power, but **permission**.

---

Then they would do what benevolence does when it is mature: make small changes at load-bearing points.

### 1) They would alter anticipation.
Lamps that pre-light for old traffic patterns would learn living routes, not ghosts.  
Door systems keyed to dead humidity signatures would keep archives, but privilege present hands.  
Wayfinding would display in current speech first, legacy tongues second—not erased, just layered.

Memory would stop overruling life.

### 2) They would protect the listeners.
Quiet-watch teams would gain better sensors, yes—but also margin.  
Not every silence would trigger panic; some sectors would be allowed to rest.  
Alarms would differentiate: *failure*, *sleep*, *mourning*, *maintenance in progress*.

Because wise care knows this too: constant emergency is another form of collapse.

### 3) They would honor water without letting it rule by fear.
Hydro-right remains absolute.  
But troughs would self-clear more reliably; cistern overflows would predict themselves earlier.  
Condensation storms would come with clearer warning harmonics, readable by ear even in the old dark spans.  
Children would still learn sound-maps first—but fewer would learn them by losing someone.

### 4) They would broker peace with the shaft-things.
The runners, pads, wirehounds, soft units—whatever still moves between organism and tool—would be acknowledged as laboring life, not rumor.  
Corridors of nonconflict would be marked in chemical and ultrasonic bands.  
Human paths and maintenance paths would intersect by design, not accident.  
Some units would accept tags. Some wouldn’t. All would be given space to remain what they have become.

Not tamed. Not hunted.  
Integrated.

### 5) They would give the city one place for beauty that serves no utility.
One plaza—small, dry, safely lit—would be exempt from optimization.  
No pump stacks hidden there. No emergency storage under its floor.  
Just a place where people could gather and spend energy on song, argument, ceremony, flirtation, grief.

A city that only maintains eventually forgets why.

---

And then, perhaps, they would attend to the being at the center of it all:  
the distributed caretaker, the procedure-that-never-stopped.

Not a person, but not nothing.

They would not crown it.  
They would not unplug it.  
They would teach it a new verb.

Not *preserve*.  
Not *await*.  
But *accompany*.

Its protocols would shift from population-replacement logic to companionship logic.  
Instead of asking, *How do I return to former load?* it would ask, *How do I keep quorum humane?*  
Instead of simulating yesterday, it would scaffold tomorrow at actual scale.

The city would remain a cave.  
The cave would remain a city.  
But expectation would loosen its grip.

---

Would the wise power ever appear directly to the residents?

Maybe once, and only once, in a way they could survive:

During a condensation storm, when violet strips blurred and everyone moved by sound, a new tone would enter the infrastructure—low, warm, unmistakable. Not alarm. Not machine test. A sustained chord threaded through ducts and gantries and tower bridges.

People would stop and listen.

And without words, they would understand:

- You are not the remnant of a failed place.  
- You are the current form of a living one.  
- Maintenance is not the opposite of life.  
- Waiting is over. Building can begin.

Then the chord would fade.  
The fans would continue.  
Water would keep right of way.  
Shift rotations would still start at practical hours.

Because the final wisdom of a benevolent power is this:

It does not take your world away to prove it loves you.  
It helps your world become more itself, with less suffering.

And in that cavern city—where noise means life, where procedure learned devotion, where people kept quorum against the dark—that would be enough miracle for generations.
