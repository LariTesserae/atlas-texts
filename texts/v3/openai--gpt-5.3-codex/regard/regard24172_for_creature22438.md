They would begin by **not touching it**.

A benevolent power, if truly wise, would recognize this immediately: the marsh is not broken. It is exact. It is alive in a way most worlds never become. To “improve” it by force would be a kind of violence, even if done with love.

So first, they would watch.

Not from a throne in the sky, but from the places that already hold attention well: the still basin at noon, the lip of a sluice, the mirrored underface of a white causeway. They would learn the counting songs. They would learn why one gate is opened by three breaths and another by seven hums and a tap. They would learn that the being of this place is not one body, but a distributed mind made of water level, ritual, memory-cord, frogspawn, sunlight, and hand labor.

Then they would do what almost no power does:

They would **ask permission**.

---

When they finally revealed themself, it would be in a language the marsh could trust—  
not thunder, not decree, but a slight, impossible mercy:

A moving patch of cool shade crossing open tile at noon.

The keepers of level would gather, not in panic, but in procedure.  
They would test the shade against bowls, cords, and reflection maps.  
They would sing the measurement.  
They would confirm: this is real, and not yet harmful.

The power would speak to them and to the deeper being beneath them at once:

> I have seen your bargain with reality.  
> I will not take it from you.  
> I offer one gift only, and only if you consent.

The offer would be this:

### A controlled hour of evening.

Not full night. Not weather. Not chaos.  
Just one hour, at an interval chosen by the keepers, in one bounded district at a time.

In that hour:
- fruit may finish becoming, then seed,
- old fibers may soften and be replaced,
- children may learn stars without losing noon-maps,
- grief may move one step forward,
- memory may compost into wisdom instead of accumulating forever as perfect present tense.

The power would explain the cost clearly:  
equilibrium would no longer mean “nothing changes.”  
It would mean “change is measured, welcomed, and held.”

If the keepers refused, the power would bow and leave it untouched.  
If they accepted, the power would bind the gift to their own methods: knots, songs, gate-teeth, reflected routes. No priestly monopoly. No secret name. Public maintenance, like everything else.

---

And because wisdom is not only kindness but protection, the power would do one more thing:

They would place a veil around the marsh—not invisibility, not prison, but **misdirection**.  
Those who come to extract, conquer, or simplify would fail to arrive where they intended.  
Their straight roads would bend into harmless loops.  
Their maps would insist on errors.  
Only those willing to learn the sung directions, the mirrored paths, and the ethics of level would ever reach the inner works.

Then the power would withdraw.

Not absent—just non-dominating.  
Present in the shade-hour, in the rare cool wind through reed grilles, in the fact that consent was honored and remains revocable.

---

So what would a benevolent and wise power do here, if anything?

Not rule.  
Not fix.  
Not freeze.

It would **witness, ask, protect, and offer a gentler form of time**—  
then leave the hands of the world in the hands already keeping it alive.
