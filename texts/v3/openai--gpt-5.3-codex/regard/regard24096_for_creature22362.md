A benevolent and wise power, seeing this place, would begin with restraint.

It would **not** crack the mountain open to sunlight.  
It would **not** drain the basins and call it salvation.  
It would **not** drag the Levelborn into a world that runs on speed and calls that mercy.

It would understand, almost at once, that this city is not a prison.  
It is a hard-won grammar of survival.

So first, it would do what very few powers do:

It would learn the local rules.

It would sit, if powers can be said to sit, in a warm shaft where mist hangs like breath, and listen for a full cycle of levels. It would learn the knock-pattern for *gate strain*, the shell-click for *child missing*, the three-pull rope signal for *air pocket compromised*. It would wait until one elder, then two, then a whole tending crew realized that when they spoke into certain stone throats, the echo came back not louder, but clearer, as if someone patient was repeating their meaning without distortion.

Only then would it act.

---

### What it would do

**1) It would make warning kinder than disaster.**

Not by stopping all fast water—that would break the mountain’s truth—but by lengthening the seconds between omen and impact.

In galleries where flash surges sometimes kill the inattentive, the power would alter almost nothing visible: a lipstone’s angle, the hollow behind a grate, the mineral skin inside a pressure shaft. Tiny changes. But now, before each dangerous rush, the place would sing a low, unmistakable throat-note through pipes and stairs—a warning tone that arrives thirty breaths early.

Thirty breaths: enough to unclip a child, enough to drop low and hook a line, enough to live.

---

**2) It would create three new airs that cannot be taken by accident.**

Not grand halls. Just three stubborn pockets at critical depths, each with rough handholds and anchor loops that never slime over.  
Refuges for the lost.  
Refuges for the panicked.  
Refuges for anyone whose rope-map failed in dark water.

The Levelborn would find them one by one and mark them in new knot-cords: *Mercy Airs*.

---

**3) It would bless maintenance, not miracles.**

Stone blades would hold edges longer.  
Rope would mildew slower where lives depend on it.  
Ceramic would crack loudly before it breaks quietly.

No effortless abundance. No ending of labor.  
Just fewer deaths from preventable failure.

Because a wise power knows dignity lives in work that still matters.

---

**4) It would teach memory to stone.**

The Levelborn already keep rope-maps, level scars, touch-signs. But floods erase, mineral buries, generations forget. So the power would coax certain walls to grow calcite in deliberate ridges—tactile script that can be read blind, underwater, by fingertips and cheek.

History that cannot be smudged.  
Evacuation routes that cannot be washed away.  
Names of the dead where hands can find them in blackness.

---

**5) It would mediate, not dominate, the other minds below.**

The old turtles, eels, seam-hunters, filter blooms—all those flow-roles—would remain themselves. But the power would braid a little more intelligibility between species: one drum-pattern that means *gate shift coming* understood by eel and child alike; one scent-mark that means *nursery zone* respected by hunters unless starving.

Not peace. Not domestication.  
Just fewer pointless misunderstandings paid in blood.

---

**6) It would choose one Levelborn as Listener—and then make itself unnecessary.**

Not a king. Not a priest above others.  
A maintainer with unusual patience and ordinary hands.

To this one, in dreamless rest between shifts, it would teach a discipline: how to place palm to wet stone and feel where stress is gathering months before fracture; how to hear, in slow water, the beginning of a future blockage; how to teach others without becoming a bottleneck.

The gift would be transmissible by apprenticeship, not bloodline.  
Wisdom distributed, or it rots.

---

**7) It would leave an unanswered gift.**

In one sealed upper shaft, reachable only during rare low levels, it would reveal a chamber no ancestor charted. Inside: not treasure, not weapon, but a mirror-still basin open to a coin of true sky.

A place to remember they are high, not buried.  
A place children can see stars reflected even if they never climb to open air.  
A place to ask questions their architecture cannot answer.

The power would not interpret the stars for them.  
It would trust them to do that work themselves.

---

And then—this is important—it would withdraw from daily governance.

It would remain present the way bedrock is present: foundational, not intrusive.  
No commandments every morning.  
No punishments for imperfect rituals.  
No demand for gratitude performances.

If thanked, it would accept.  
If forgotten for a generation, it would not sulk.  
If cursed during a bad flood year, it would not retaliate.

Because benevolence without ego is the rarest kind.

---

So what would change?

Not everything. Never everything.

Children would still train breath-counts in cold fear.  
Scrape crews would still bleed knuckles on lime.  
Some seasons would still be lean.  
Some chambers would still be lost.

But catastrophe would become less arbitrary.  
Knowledge would become harder to erase.  
Mercy would be built into infrastructure.

And the Levelborn—Those Between Levels—would continue as themselves:  
slow on purpose, quiet by wisdom, alive by relation.

The wise power’s greatest act would be this:

It would help them remain who they are,  
only with a little more time before the water turns.
