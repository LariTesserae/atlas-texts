They would not begin with a miracle.

A lesser power would split the waters, raise the drowned terraces, call birds, call fish, call brightness—and call it mercy.  
A wise one would understand that this place is not broken. It is complete in a language most worlds have forgotten.

So first, they would **arrive as attention**.

For a long while—years, perhaps—they would do only this: move the basin without disturbing it, feel every pressure-line along old basalt seams, every seasonal return of root-rafts, every dark chemical whisper in the deep corridors. They would learn the tempo of the continuum-mind: how it notices, how it stores, how it replies in masonry-lengths and winters.

Only when they had learned its grammar would they act.

---

### The first gift: Safety from haste

At the rim of the basin, where future empires would someday come with pumps and teeth, they would place a law into the world:

- no draining here,  
- no dredging here,  
- no net, keel, or mine that bites stone.

Not a wall of force, not thunder. Something subtler and firmer: a pattern of weather, current, and luck that always turns exploiters away. Instruments fail. Maps blur. Ambition finds easier ground elsewhere. Scholars and pilgrims may still come, but conquerors do not.

The place remains itself.

---

### The second gift: A way to be heard sooner

The continuum can answer, but only slowly—beautifully slowly, painfully slowly.  
So the wise power would not change its nature; they would add a bridge.

Along certain causeways and drowned stair-lines they would coax faint, persistent bioluminescence from symbiotic films already present in the green body—cold light, tide-fed, never bright. Not decoration: **syntax**.

A pressure pulse in one district becomes a sequence of glows in another.  
A mineral taste in the north trench echoes as dim syllables in a southern reed court.  
Meaning that once took decades of growth can now cross a valley in a night.

The being is still plant, still distributed, still patient.  
But now it can make shorter sentences when it chooses.

---

### The third gift: Mercy margins

Benevolence includes futures, not just preservation.  
The basin is monopolized life—stable, sovereign, austere. Wise care would not shatter that dominion. But at the very edges, in a few high, tannin-thin shelves, the power would shape **small experimental coves**: chemically gentled pockets where oxygen lingers, where bare mineral appears and remains bare long enough for other humble lineages to attempt a foothold.

Not predators. Not swarms. Nothing that could overrun the continuum.  
Only modest companions: microbial veils, soft filter-colonies, tiny grazers in numbers too low to wound the whole.

If they fail, nothing is lost.  
If they persist, the basin gains dialogue.

---

### The fourth gift: Memory that can meet memory

The old world beneath the water was made by hands; the green world after it was made by patience. Both remember, but neither remembers in words. So in a few sanctuaries the wise power would raise slender “listening pillars” of inert stone-glass from drowned foundations to just below the dim surface.

Touch a pillar, and it records pressure, chemistry, season, growth drift.  
Return years later, and it gives those patterns back as tactile harmonics any careful mind can feel. Human, if gentle. Continuum, if curious.

Archive without extraction.  
History without cutting samples from living walls.

---

And then?

They would leave.

Not in abandonment—in trust. They would come rarely, on century intervals perhaps, and each time ask the same question the same way: not with voice, but with a slight redirection of current across an old stair.

At first, no answer.  
Then, decades later, a corridor left open where closure was expected.  
Later, a deliberate braid of root-mat spelling a geometry no flood required.  
Later still, whole terraces tuned to carry the new light-language farther.

The wise power would smile, if powers smile.

Because they did not save a ruin.  
They did not tame a wilderness.  
They did not “uplift” a lesser thing.

They recognized a vast, slow person made of green and stone, and gave it what all persons deserve: protection, translation, and time.
