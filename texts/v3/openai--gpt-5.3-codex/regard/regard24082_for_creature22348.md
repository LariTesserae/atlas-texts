It would begin by **not rescuing** the hollow from itself.

A truly benevolent intelligence, meeting this basin and its people, would understand quickly that the place is not broken. Harsh, yes. Dangerous, yes. But coherent. Ancient. In working order.

So its first act would be attention.

It would sit through one full turn of seasons in the roar, learning what already keeps life here alive: the moss math, the cairn grammar, the hand-sign language, the discipline of line-of-sight, the humility of temporary shelter. It would learn that endurance here is not heroism; it is technique repeated carefully.

Only then would it intervene, and only in ways that preserve the basin’s character.

---

It would not quiet the pressure-song.  
It would tune around it.

On certain stones along true paths, a faint mineral sheen would begin to hold light longer than it should—just enough to remain visible in fog, moonless dusk, and storm dim. Not glowing like magic, not dramatic. More like wet lichen suddenly deciding to remember daylight for an extra hour. The line-keepers would notice first by accident, then by trust.

In peat cuts near dangerous pockets, a thread of tough white fungus would begin to spread in narrow bands where ground is firm. Underfoot, it would feel like ordinary root mat. To the practiced eye, it would be a sentence: *step here, not there.*

At the coastal break, where wind and sea force air through stone throat, the wise power would shape one small mercy into the physics: a recurring lull, brief and predictable, at two turns of tide each day. Just long enough for crossing signals, rope checks, and counting heads. Not safety—never total safety. Just a reliable breath.

---

For the people, it would not grant domination.  
It would strengthen memory.

Children would begin, without being told, to dream paths from above—the basin as dark palm, cairns as knuckles, bog as bruised skin, safe ridges as tendons. They would wake and point correctly in mist.

Elders losing sharp distance vision would find their touch-language becoming finer, faster, richer. Gloves would evolve. Signal systems would grow into art: three taps for “seen,” four for “seen and understood,” open palm against forearm for “wait, hazard moving.” The culture would deepen exactly where the place demands it.

If a traveler unfamiliar with the hollow entered carelessly—loud, overconfident, map-proud—the wise power would not punish with spectacle. It would do something more educational: erase their certainty. Bearings would fail until they accepted guidance. The first person they could truly follow would be a line-keeper child with mud to the knees and perfect route sense.

---

It would also protect the basin from the wrong kind of notice.

Not by walls of fire, not by curses. By administrative weather.  
Survey crews would schedule and reschedule and somehow never arrive in usable conditions. Funding proposals would be misplaced. Satellite images would return clear everywhere except this one fold, where mist and shadow flatten detail into geology too boring for extraction. The place would survive not because it is hidden absolutely, but because greed cannot get a firm grip on it.

Those who come to learn restraint, however, would find the paths.

---

And for the oldest resident—the temperament that has taken bodies—the wise power would offer a covenant:

- You will keep your roar.  
- You will keep your dimness.  
- You will keep selecting for patience, clarity, and deliberate care.  
- In return, when catastrophe exceeds skill, chance will bend slightly toward life.

A fox pausing at exactly the right fork.  
A bird’s sudden low pass above a person gone half-blind in sleet.  
A dropped peg landing point-first where rope is needed.  
A hand, extended at the instant before line-of-sight disappears.

Nothing that breaks the world.  
Everything that keeps relationship intact.

---

So what would a benevolent and wise power do here?

Not conquer.  
Not beautify.  
Not “improve” into ruin.

It would become a quiet ally of the existing grammar:  
visibility, memory, restraint, return.

In a loud world, it would choose to be subtle.  
In a dangerous place, it would choose to be dependable.  
And in the roaring hollow, that would be indistinguishable from love.
