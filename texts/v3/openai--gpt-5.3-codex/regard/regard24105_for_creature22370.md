It did not descend in fire.

It came as attention.

From far above weather, from somewhere older than governance and younger than rain, the benevolent power looked down at the mountain-that-forgot-stone and understood, almost at once, that this was not a place *in* balance so much as a place *balancing*: continuously, skillfully, with a thousand hands and roots and valves and wings.

It also understood that the “being” in question was not one body.  
It was the whole composite creature: people, goats, fungi, pumps, bats, trellises, old presses, fish channels, rust, rituals, schedules, and memory braided together into a single metabolism.

A lesser power would have tried to perfect it.

A kind and wise one did something harder first:  
it waited.

For one full wet season and one full dry season it intervened nowhere. It listened to pressure in pipe-racks, watched children tie bean-vines off load-bearing seams, watched elders tap casings with knuckles and diagnose bearings by ring-tone. It learned the etiquette between parakeets and market stalls, between turbine heat and mushroom halls, between goats and gravity. It learned that heroics were rare here, and maintenance was sacred.

Then, and only then, it acted.

Not by changing what the place was.  
By giving it a little more room to remain itself.

---

### First gift: **A Margin**

The power put a thumb on probability so lightly no instrument could detect it.

Fog condensed a little earlier on the hardest mornings.  
Not enough to flood, just enough to fill one extra cistern cloth per district.

A belt that might have snapped at noon held until evening shift, when someone was awake to hear it complain.

Blight spores found one more obstacle before crossing terraces.

Heat waves broke half a day sooner against upper towers.

In arithmetic terms it was negligible. In lived terms it was the difference between constant triage and sustainable care.

People called this, without theology, “a lucky year,” then “a run of lucky years,” then eventually stopped naming it and built it into planning models as if it had always been true.

Wise power likes it that way.

---

### Second gift: **A Memory You Could Share**

Before, knowledge lived in lineages, guilds, songs, calluses, and scar tissue. Rich, but fragile.

The power did not replace that with tablets or prophecy.  
It tuned the city’s own senses.

After the gift, certain patterns became easier for many to notice, not just experts: a particular harmonic in evening fan-noise that meant impending cavitation; a specific green in wall-moss that forecasted mineral imbalance three weeks out; bat flight density shifts that reliably predicted fruiting failure in one corridor over.

Children began saying, “It sounds thirsty on level sixteen,” and being right.

Newcomers could learn faster without flattening tradition, because tradition now had more handles.

No one called it magic. They called it “the mountain speaking plainly today.”

That was close enough.

---

### Third gift: **A Refusal**

The benevolent power refused worship that made anyone smaller.

When a faction proposed a high-tier shrine requiring daily offerings of rare water, the cistern cracked during construction in a way that injured no one and convinced everyone.

When another group tried to monopolize “interpretation” of the new patterns for political leverage, their own district suffered a preventable root-failure they had ignored while drafting decrees. The lesson spread faster than doctrine: if you stop listening outward, you go deaf inward.

So the power established one law, not by proclamation, but by consequences gentle and undeniable:

> No devotion is valid here if it interrupts repair.

People eventually ritualized this with humor.  
At festivals they brought offerings to broken things first: a cleaned filter, a sharpened blade, a retied trellis, a patched glove. Then music.

The power accepted every one.

---

It did not end storms.  
Storms are part of how this being breathes.

It did not erase corrosion.  
Rust is one of the city’s clocks.

It did not remove grief, accident, age, or conflict.  
Wisdom knows that friction is not always failure.

What it did was preserve *continuity*: it made cascading collapse less likely, learning more transmissible, and cooperation more rewarding than extraction.

Over generations, people argued whether a power had ever come at all. Engineers produced data trends. Grandparents told stories. Children rolled their eyes and checked valve seats. Bats rose at dusk in black ribbons. Presses kept time. Carp fed in metal-rich channels. Orchards thickened. Old flyovers became forests by increments.

And the being—this many-bodied, stair-bred, moss-skinned, machine-hearted being—lived longer without becoming rigid, changed without forgetting itself.

In the end, the benevolent power took a name only because the residents insisted on one.

They called it **Spare Capacity**.

Not a god above them.  
A blessing among them.  
One more guild of attention in the mountain that forgot it was stone, and remembered instead how to keep living.
