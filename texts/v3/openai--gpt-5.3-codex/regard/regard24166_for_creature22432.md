If a benevolent and wise power turned its full attention to that under-city,  
it would not arrive as thunder.

It would arrive as **regard**.

---

At first, it would do nothing visible.

Because wisdom would recognize what lesser gods miss: this place is not broken.  
It is difficult, yes. Exposed, hungry, always negotiating with stone and pulse-water.  
But it is not a failed world awaiting rescue.  
It is a living intelligence already in motion.

So the power would watch one full cycle of lean years and flood years.  
It would learn the hand-signs in wind-rise.  
It would follow beetle lines to salt.  
It would sit in the rafters with the gliders when market noise drops.  
It would listen to children recite routes in the dark.

Only then would it act.

And even then, gently.

---

### What it would not do

It would not split the ceiling open and pour in permanent rivers.  
Too much water, and rot would come; rot would erase the mineral economies;  
the herds would sicken; old routes would drown; dependence would harden into fragility.

It would not raise walls against every flood.  
A city that forgets movement dies in place.

It would not choose one lineage to crown forever.  
Permanent power in a shifting basin becomes cruelty within a generation.

It would not make suffering sacred.  
But neither would it erase all risk and call that kindness.

---

### What it would do

It would make **small mercies structural**.

- In every chamber where ceiling-fall is likely within a year, stone would begin to sing first—  
  a low, felt vibration in pillars and floor, readable to bare feet days before collapse.

- In every dry channel that will wake to flash runoff, a cool draft would run upstream minutes ahead—  
  enough time to lift children, untie loads, open herd gates, climb.

- The red flood marks would no longer fade quickly.  
  Mineral paint would hold truer through dust and handling, so memory would not depend on one elder surviving one more winter.

- Condensation cloths hung with care would yield slightly more on hardest nights—  
  not abundance, but margin.  
  The difference between panic and planning.

- Predators in famine years would hesitate one breath longer at low arches.  
  Not tamed, not banished. Just enough for design to work as intended.

- Lost travelers in whiteout dust would always, eventually, hear one familiar sound:  
  rope on a post, hoof against stone, drip into a jar—  
  a thread back to route-memory.

These are not miracles that replace skill.  
They are miracles that **reward attention**.

---

### What it would ask in return

No temple.  
No blood.  
No empire in its name.

Only this covenant:

1. Keep teaching children the old routes *and* the new cracks.  
2. Do not hoard warning signs as private advantage.  
3. Leave roost and herd corridors open, even in hunger.  
4. Repaint the marks.  
5. Move when movement is due.

And one more, hardest:

6. When a district must be abandoned, grieve it—then abandon it cleanly, so others may reuse what remains.

---

In time, people might give this power a name.  
Or many names, depending on lineage:

The One Who Gives Minutes.  
The Listener in Stone.  
Margin-Maker.  
Keeper of the Third Line.

But the wisest among them would say:

It did not save us from our world.  
It made us harder to separate from one another inside it.

---

And the being that lives there—the practice itself,  
that old, lucid habit of noticing and yielding before insistence—  
would not be replaced by divinity.

It would be recognized.

Strengthened.

Confirmed as sacred in the only way that matters there:

not by permanence,  
but by continuation.
