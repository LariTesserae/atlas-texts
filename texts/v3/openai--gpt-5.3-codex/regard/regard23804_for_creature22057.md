At first, the wise power would do **nothing visible**.

Not out of indifference—out of respect.

This basin is not broken. It is loud, unstable, dangerous, fecund, self-correcting. A lesser god would mistake turbulence for failure and try to straighten rivers, thin the predators, quiet the night, teach the chorus species to settle and stack stone. A benevolent, intelligent power would recognize that as a violence in polite clothing.

So it would begin by listening for a long time:  
through flood pulse, fungal collapse, swarm rise, migration front, drought gap, lightning bloom.  
It would learn the grammar of this place before touching a single syllable.

And then, if it acted, it would act like this:

---

### 1) It would protect **rhythm**, not outcomes.

It would not save every life.  
It would save the *pattern that makes life possible*.

When storms fail three cycles in a row, it would draw mist from high air and let it bead on the stone ribs at predawn—just enough to keep root mats and burrow webs from total failure. Not rain enough to erase thirst, only enough to prevent collapse.

When floods come too hard, it would not stop them. It would break one surge in ten against hidden shelves of bedrock, so seedbanks and den-lines are bruised, not erased.

A wise power does not choose winners species by species.  
It keeps the drumbeat from snapping.

---

### 2) It would give the chorus species one gift: **true interval memory**.

Not maps. Not language from elsewhere. Not fire, walls, permanence.

It would deepen what they already are best at: timing.

In practical terms, the gift would feel like this inside them:  
a bodily certainty of *when* rather than *where*.  
Half-night after sulfur scent. Two insect-waves after first thunder. Three sleep-wakes after white fungus opens.

Their elders already carry this in fragments. The power would make it harder to lose in panic and easier to pass in call and gesture. Calves and juveniles would learn rain-sequences before first full migration. Troops scattered by predators would reconverge with fewer deaths. Thirst windows would be missed less often.

It is not domination of nature.  
It is better hearing of the basin’s own clock.

---

### 3) It would place a law on mimicry—light, but sacred.

The chorus species survives partly by information theft: false alarms, borrowed calls, panic as tactic. Clever, effective, costly. In bad years, too much deception shreds trust and turns every warning into noise.

So the wise power would mark one sound as inviolable: a deep throat pulse no other call may imitate without consequence. Not forbidden by punishment from above—enforced by instinctive revulsion. A liar can still lie, but not with *that* note.

That one note means: flood-wall, canopy break, ground-fire, true immediate death.

In a world where noise is survival, this creates one strand of certainty.  
Not peace. Reliability.

---

### 4) It would hide the basin from simplifiers.

Not forever. Not absolutely. But enough.

Surveyors would lose bearings in storm haze.  
Road-cutters would find their embankments slumped overnight.  
Conquerors would call it unprofitable, haunted, inaccessible, too wet, too loud, too mutable for extraction.

The wise power would not wage grand wars to keep everyone out. It would use misdirection, inconvenience, and boredom—the humble miracles. The basin remains itself because those who crave stable edges cannot keep hold of it.

---

### 5) It would anchor one place of mercy.

Not a city. Not a temple.

A high, root-bound crescent above the worst floodline where predation pressure is mysteriously low during birthing pulses and injury seasons.  
No species owns it.  
No species is safe there forever.  
But for brief intervals, pursuit falters, aggression dips, and the wounded can breathe.

Every chaotic system needs one non-chaotic beat to reset.  
The wise power would make that beat.

---

And then, having done only this much, it would withdraw again into listening.

Because benevolence here is not control.  
It is restraint with precision.

The basin would remain loud, dark, wet-breathed, violent, and alive.  
The chorus species would remain restless, cunning, social, mortal.

No utopia. No fall from nature.  
Just a world allowed to stay wild without being abandoned to ruin.
