They would do **four things**.

And the first would be to do nothing at all.

For a long while—long enough to hear every register of the basin—they would only listen:  
to chute-hammer water, to root-felt tightening after rain, to cobble-rattle warnings running downslope before a failure, to thunder trapped and re-thrown between walls.  
A wise power does not confuse loudness with distress.  
A benevolent one does not call a hard life a broken life.

They would understand that the Holdfast is not asking for rescue from change.  
It is change, organized.

So the second thing they would do is **refuse bad mercy**.

They would not freeze the cliffs in place.  
They would not pour permanent sunlight into a place built for dimness.  
They would not import birds for prettiness or beasts for story.  
They would not silence the mechanical roar that the Holdfast uses as hearing.  
They would not make stability where stability would become death.

Then, because benevolence is not passivity, they would do the third thing:  
**they would make survivable margins**.

Not peace. Margins.

They would alter tiny boundary conditions, almost invisibly:

- In three high fractures, they would slow seep into pulse-fed trickles, so every storm is not feast-or-famine.  
- In two collapse-prone gullies, they would roughen buried bedrock by a finger’s breadth, enough that slides break into smaller tongues instead of one body-tearing rush.  
- Along one shadowed wall, they would open a narrow recurring light-window—minutes only, when cloud thins—so deep mats can recharge before the next long dim.  

No miracle spectacle.  
Just less total loss.  
Just enough continuity that memory can accumulate in living tissue.

And the fourth thing—the most generous—would be a gift to the inhabitant itself:

They would teach the Holdfast to keep **time ahead of impact**.

Not thought as animals think it.  
A new chemistry: faster long-distance signaling through hyphal cord and root cable, a way to pre-position growth before failure arrives.  
Where rumble says “saturation building,” anchoring begins earlier.  
Where wind-tone rises in a certain slot, drag-shedding leaf-drop happens before the gust front.  
Where cold-crack sings in stone, exploratory filaments are already waiting at the seam by dawn.

In other words: not invulnerability, but anticipation.

---

And because wisdom includes protection from other kinds of disaster, they would lay one final veil over the basin:

Not a wall. A tendency.

Those who come seeking conquest would misread trails, lose confidence, turn back in weather.  
Those who come to extract would find tools failing in damp, plans unraveling in fog.  
But those who come to study with patience might stay at the rim, listen, and leave with humility instead of samples.

The place would remain mostly itself:  
dark, loud, wet in pockets, collapsing and reassembling, vegetation shouldering stone, stone undoing vegetation, both continuing.

The Holdfast would still fail, and regrow, and fail again.  
But fewer failures would be absolute.  
More would be teachable.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do here:

not end the basin’s violence,  
not romanticize it,  
but bend it—slightly, faithfully—away from erasure  
and toward persistence.
