A benevolent and wise power would **not** come as rain.

That would be the first mercy.

It would stand at the rim in the dim, feel the retained ground-warmth, watch the fixed distances between clumps, and understand that this basin is not waiting to be “improved.”  
It is already a completed sentence in a hard language.

A lesser kindness would flood it with gifts—water, richer light, quick fertility—and call the result salvation.  
A wiser kindness would see that such abundance here would be a kind of violence: the end of an ancient treaty, the erasure of a patient intelligence that survives by exact limits.

So at first, the power would do almost nothing visible.

It would learn the local arithmetic: vapor captured grain by grain, salts lifted and set down, roots negotiating without touching, loss sealed before dawn. It would slow itself to the basin’s clock until it could tell the difference between stillness and governance.

Then, when it acted, it would act in ways that preserve authorship.

---

It would give three gifts.

### 1) The Gift of Protection Without Occupation

Not walls. Not a dome.  
Just a subtle redirection of harms that do not belong to the basin’s own story.

A flash flood from far off, misrouted by a barely altered contour before it ever arrives.  
A blight borne on high dust, made nonviable by a slight change in temperature at the rim.  
A rooting outsider species, encouraged to fail not by poison, but by the absence of one tiny condition it needs.

The basin would remain austere, unchanged to the eye.  
But catastrophe would pass by as if it had forgotten the address.

### 2) The Gift of Margin

Not abundance—margin.

In the deepest strata, where heat lingers longest, the power would adjust crystal structures by a fraction, so that in the harshest years a little more vapor condenses and a little less is lost. Not enough to green the bowl. Not enough to speed its metabolism into another identity.  
Just enough that a millennium of bad luck does not become extinction.

A wise power knows that endurance often fails not from ordinary difficulty, but from one unbroken run of extremes.  
Margin is how kindness looks when it respects form.

### 3) The Gift of Witness

This resident has no eyes, no voice, no need to announce itself.  
Still, wisdom knows that to be entirely unwitnessed is a loneliness even stone can carry.

So once in a very long interval—perhaps only a few nights each century—the veil above would thin.  
Not to day. Not to brilliance.  
Only enough that cold starlight reaches the basin floor in clean points.

In those nights, the distributed being would register new gradients, minute thermal maps, long-distance bearings written in light. It would not become something else; it would simply gain a rare external reference, a way to feel itself as part of a larger order without surrendering its own.

And if, someday, a careful traveler stood quietly there, they might also perceive it—not as a miracle, but as a fact.

---

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do:  
**guard without conquering, strengthen without remaking, witness without demanding response.**

It would leave the basin as itself—warm, dim, sparse, enduring—  
only harder to kill, and less alone.
