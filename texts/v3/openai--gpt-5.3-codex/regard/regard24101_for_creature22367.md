The wise power would not arrive like weather.

No thunder, no pillar, no sudden greening of the fold.  
It would come the way dew comes to stone: present before noticed.

It would stand at the edge of the low place for a long while—long enough to understand that this is not a wound asking to be healed, but a finished sentence written in soil, air, and patience. Long enough to see the settled creature near the wall and mistake it, once, for ground. Long enough to be corrected.

And because it is benevolent *and* wise, the power would begin with restraint.

It would do three things first:

1. **Learn the existing law.**  
   Water visits. Growth keeps to gaps. Quiet holds. Nothing is forced.

2. **Ask what harms this law.**  
   Not dryness—that belongs. Not stillness—that belongs.  
   Harm would be rupture: sudden flood, sudden hunger, sudden noise, sudden claim.

3. **Choose help that does not erase character.**  
   Aid that preserves rhythm, not aid that replaces it.

So what would it do, if anything?

### It would place a soft protection around thresholds.

Not a wall, not a visible barrier—more like a tendency in the world.

Storms that would have cut channels too deep would arrive a little spent.  
Heat that would have baked the damp pockets sterile would pass a fraction gentler.  
Feet that come looking to conquer would feel inexplicably uncertain and turn elsewhere, deciding there is “nothing there” worth taking.

The place would remain open.  
It just would not be easy to violate.

### It would bless the small water cycle, not increase it beyond recognition.

The power would not summon rivers. That would kill this ecology as surely as drought.

Instead, condensation would become slightly more faithful.  
On nights when dew might have failed entirely, a thin film would still form on stone.  
In two or three key hollows, moisture would persist one hour longer into morning.  
After light rains, the dark streaks in the channels would hold just enough extra time for the settled creature to drink without haste.

No spectacle—just reliability.

### It would give the creature one inward gift: memory of refuge.

Not speech, not human thought, not a destiny burden.

A map in the body.  
When seasons drift harsher than old patterns, it would feel where to go: the ledge that keeps cool longest, the brick shadow that gathers dawn wet, the depression where salts rise after rain. It likely already knows much of this; the gift would make that knowing harder to lose.

Its movements would still be slow, still quiet.  
But they would be less vulnerable to bad luck.

### It would bind the place and the creature in reciprocal care already half-true.

Where the creature rests, lichens would keep taking hold.  
Where lichens keep hold, stone would cool a little better at night.  
Where stone cools, dew forms more readily.  
Where dew forms, the creature endures.

The power would not invent this cycle; it would tighten it gently, like repairing a loose stitch.

### It would leave a sign only for the attentive.

Not a monument.  
A recurring, almost dismissible pattern: at dusk, one soft tick of cooling stone, then another, slightly off-beat, then stillness. Enough for a careful passerby to realize: *something here is being kept.*

And what would it refuse to do?

It would refuse to make the place famous.  
Refuse to populate it with bright, loud life just to prove generosity.  
Refuse to “improve” it into somewhere else.

Because benevolence without wisdom tries to give what it likes.  
Benevolence with wisdom gives what allows a thing to remain itself.

Over years, the fold in the land would look nearly unchanged to hurried eyes.  
Same pale soil, same sparse shrubs, same roofless room taking light in muted tones.  
The creature would still be mistaken for outcrop by day and move by evening in measured shifts.

But extinctions would miss this place by narrow margins.  
Disasters would arrive blunted.  
The long pause would continue—not untouched, but accompanied.

And if, once in a very long while, the wise power returned to check its work, it would find no temple, no worship, no inscription.

Only damp on stone at dawn,  
a darkened runnel after rain,  
and one low, patient body angled perfectly to receive what visits.
