It would **not** arrive as conquest.

No new sun.  
No rivers forced down dry stairs.  
No command that says *be ordinary*.

A wise benevolence, seeing this city-mountain and the patient people within it, would understand almost immediately that nothing here is “failing.” It is difficult, yes. Severe, yes. But it is coherent. It has law. It has character. It has earned itself.

So the power would begin the only way that place allows: by waiting.

For one full long cycle—through thermal snaps, dust rivers, shadow-lags, and the subterranean page-turning—it would do nothing but learn. Not because it is weak, but because it is respectful. It would watch how a shard-keeper pauses before moving a balancing block. How a cliff-runner tests air with its ear-fans. How black film chooses a seam. How even anomalies here have etiquette.

Then, and only then, it would act.

Not to change the mountain-city into comfort, but to reduce needless suffering while preserving its hard-won grammar.

It would make **five gifts**.

---

### 1) The Gift of Reliable Mercy

In seven places across the high districts—never announced, always discoverable—it would establish fixed pockets where reality does not drift.

Distance behaves.  
Gravity agrees with itself.  
Shadows do not detach.

Not everywhere. Just enough that no traveler, child, or elder is ever more than a day from one stable court. Waystations for the lost. Surgery rooms for the injured. Neutral ground for disputes. The Delayed would call them *True Rooms* and never own them.

---

### 2) The Gift of Measured Water

It would not flood the land; that would be violence disguised as kindness.  
Instead, each dawn, in deep-cut stone basins already present but long empty, a skin-thin film of condensate would form—predictable, clean, finite.

Enough to prevent deaths.  
Not enough to erase discipline.

Water as covenant, not abundance.

---

### 3) The Gift of Maps That Remember

Symbols currently fade overnight. Routes mutate. Knowledge is always at risk of becoming private, then lost.

The power would alter one law: marks made in a certain mineral mixture—black film, chalk dust, breath moisture—would hold through one moon cycle before softening. Long enough to teach, warn, coordinate, and pass on craft without turning the city into something static.

Not permanence.  
Just continuity with less attrition.

---

### 4) The Gift of Gentle Refusal

The greatest threat to such a place is not its strangeness, but outsiders who mistake it for empty resource.

So the benevolent power would place a veil at the outer approaches: armies, extractors, and zealots would find routes lengthening, compasses arguing, ambition dissolving into fatigue. They would turn back convinced the place is barren, pointless, not worth the cost.

But those who come in humility—cartographers, healers, students, kin-seekers—would still arrive.

Protection without isolation.  
Hospitality without invasion.

---

### 5) The Gift of Mutual Hearing

At last, the deepest gift: once each year, on the coldest dusk, the page-turning below and the living above would briefly understand one another.

Not in words. In alignment.

The shard-keepers would feel which walls are truly urgent, which shifts are natural, which corridors must be left alone. And the buried lithic mind—if mind it is—would receive, perhaps for the first time, gratitude directed downward.

A civilization and its foundation, no longer guessing in opposite darkness.

---

After this, the wise power would withdraw again.

No temple demanding tribute.  
No priesthood claiming exclusive access.  
No golden age that ends in dependence.

Just a few precise mercies inserted into a severe world, the way one well-cut stone can keep an arch standing another thousand years.

And if asked, *Why didn’t you save them completely?*  
the power would answer:

> “They were never asking to be rescued from themselves.  
> They were asking for enough steadiness to continue becoming.”
