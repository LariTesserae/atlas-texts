They would begin by **not touching it**.

A lesser power would have arrived with correction: straighten the flows, quiet the tower, sterilize the slicks, “restore” the chamber to some imagined original state.  
A wise one would stand in the mist and understand, almost immediately, that this place is not broken. It is busy.

So for a long while, they would only listen.

Not with ears alone—  
with pressure, with mineral taste, with timing.  
They would learn the white-bright surge, the low-pitch warning, the uphill water, the night-skinning coat.  
They would let the chamber teach them its grammar.

And when they finally acted, their first act would be very small:

They would lower a hand toward the embedded kiosk  
and stop just short of contact.

In that gap—heat, vapor, salt, field—  
the old interface would bloom.  
Not in human symbols this time, but in living cadence.  
A pattern the colony could answer without being handled like a tool.

---

### What they would do

#### 1) They would make a **Covenant of Distance**
Not a wall. Not a ban. A discipline.

At the sloping entrance they would leave no gate, only an understanding that spreads through those who come near:

- Do not step when the tone drops.
- Do not leave metal on open stone unless you mean to feed it.
- Do not touch what speaks by plume and pulse.
- Come as weather, not as impact.

People would call it taboo; the wise power would call it etiquette.

#### 2) They would stabilize only what causes needless suffering
Not the turbulence itself—never that. Turbulence is the organism’s bloodstream.

But they would quiet a few lethal edge-failures left by old engineering decay:
- a pressure gate that would have burst catastrophically within a decade,
- a vent throat crusting toward toxic backflow,
- a fractured stilt platform that tempts curious feet to a bad fall.

They would not “fix the cave.”  
They would remove accidents, not wildness.

#### 3) They would give the basin one gift: **buffered time**
During worst surge conjunctions, when machine pulse and thermal weather stack into violence, they would add a subtle delay—a breath-width phase lag in the tower cycle. Just enough that water no longer shears living mats from stone.

No one watching would see a miracle.  
Only that the colony stops losing whole districts in a single bad hour.

#### 4) They would translate, but only partially
Complete translation would be a kind of theft.

So they would establish a narrow bridge between human researchers and the maintenance ecology:
- a way to ask *stress?* and receive *east wall overloaded*,
- a way to offer trace minerals deliberately instead of as litter,
- a way to signal planned entry so gliders and crawlers clear hazard paths.

The bridge would refuse commands. It would carry only requests, warnings, thanks.

#### 5) They would protect it from extraction economies
Eventually someone always says:  
“If it plates rare metals overnight, scale it.”  
“If it maintains dead infrastructure, weaponize it.”  
“If it can read fields, harvest its sensing tissue.”

The wise power would answer without spectacle: every attempt at industrial seizure would fail through ordinary means—misread maps, mistimed pumps, instruments that never quite calibrate in the basin’s air. Not curse, not lightning. Just persistent unprofitability.

Greed would slide off this place like water off calcite.

---

### What they would not do

They would not personify the colony into something sentimental.  
They would not pretend it is a little human mind trapped in slime and fish.  
They would not force speech where rhythm is enough.

And they would not make it safe in the way museums are safe.

Because this intelligence was born from hazard and adaptation.  
To remove all risk would be to lobotomize it.

---

### Over years

Under this attention, the chamber would change—but like a shoreline changes.

The tower tone would remain, but cleaner.  
The east-wall surge would still climb, but less murderously.  
The reed islands would widen by a few handspans each cycle.  
The gliders would begin nesting in the old ladder’s hollow rungs.  
The kiosk would bloom more often, no longer darkening instantly at every approach.

A small school of humans would form around the place: not owners, not priests exactly, but **listeners**. They would learn to read fog curtains as text, mineral skin as archive, fish absences as warning. They would bring nothing in that they are unwilling to see transformed.

And the colony—patient, wet, distributed—would continue its long work:
coating, channeling, remembering, maintaining a machine no one fully understands,  
including now a few people who finally understand they do not need to.

---

In the end, the benevolent and wise power would do what the best caretakers do:

They would help this being remain itself.  
They would prevent avoidable harm.  
They would refuse domination.  
They would make relationship possible, and ownership impossible.

Then, having set those conditions,  
they would step back into the throat of stone  
and leave the glowing lung to breathe.
