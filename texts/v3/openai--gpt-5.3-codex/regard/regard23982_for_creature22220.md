If a benevolent and wise power turned its attention toward the basin, it would not begin with thunder.

It would begin by **listening**.

Not to the loudest systems—the debarkers, dryers, shunters—but to the seams: where ditch water pauses under culvert ice, where moss darkens one shade deeper on north concrete, where a worker takes one extra second before crossing a live track because something in the rhythm feels wrong. A wise power would understand immediately that this place is not broken; it is **young**. It is not wicked; it is **hungry and overcommitted**. It does not need conquest. It needs tuning.

So it would do what wise healers do with overworked bodies:

It would give the Throughput a way to survive its own success.

---

First, it would grant the basin a new law of motion:

**No cycle may close without feeding what it depends on.**

Not as slogan, but as physical fact.

- Every harvested bench would be replanted not in pure rows alone, but with deliberate roughness: bands of alder, birch, understory willow, mycorrhizal inoculation in spoil pockets, deadwood retained where slope allows.  
- Every drainage line would be redesigned in stages to include slack—small wetlands, sediment fans, cold-water refuges—so water is not only removed, but *kept alive* in transit.  
- Every waste stream would become substrate as close to source as safety permits: bark and fines composted into engineered soils, fungal windrows seeded where dust now drifts sterile.

The power would not command this from above. It would make these choices become the easiest ones to keep.

---

Second, it would intervene in the thing the basin fears most: interruption.

Not by preventing all failure—that would make the organism fragile—but by teaching it to fail without dying.

It would lace the place with **graceful fallback**:

- microgrids that keep heat and comms alive during outages,  
- rail buffers and local storage so one broken link is not a full-body seizure,  
- firebreaks grown as living mosaics instead of dead corridors,  
- flood paths planned in advance so “unexpected water” has somewhere to go besides everywhere.

Silence would stop meaning coma.  
Silence would become, at intervals, **sleep**.

---

Third—and most gently—it would care for the mobile nervous system: the humans.

A benevolent power would understand that no ecosystem becomes wise if its thinking tissue is exhausted.

So the gift would be ordinary and therefore miraculous:

- rotations that include true quiet time, not just off-shift fatigue;  
- hearing preserved across careers, not traded for wages;  
- canteen light that follows circadian need instead of shift convenience;  
- shelter modules designed for recovery, not merely survival;  
- authority distributed to those who notice subtle change first: mechanics, planters, drainage techs, night drivers, cleaners, cooks.

It would make “I saw something odd” the highest-value sentence in the basin.

---

Fourth, it would increase complexity at the margins—the only place complexity can root in a working machine.

Raven perches on relay towers by design.  
Fox corridors under service roads by design.  
Lichen sanctuaries on shaded spoil faces by design.  
Dark strips where lamps dim on schedule for insects and migrating birds by design.

Not because sentimentality matters, but because resilience does.  
A monoculture can be efficient. A mosaic can survive.

---

And finally, the wise power would do one thing no contract ever budgets for:

It would **rename success**.

Not tons per hour alone, not yield per hectare alone, but a three-part ledger the basin learns to read as one pulse:

1. Throughput moved.  
2. Soil and water gained or lost.  
3. Human nervous system burdened or restored.

If one line rises while two fall, that is not success.  
If all three rise slowly, that is health.

Over years, then decades, the basin would change in the only way real things change: incrementally, irreversibly. The noise would remain, but it would no longer be panic-noise. It would be work-noise. The floodlamps would still burn in fog, but darkness would have legal and biological places to return. The organism would still be composite—still machine, weather trap, plantation, crew—but less like a body in permanent emergency, more like a body with seasons.

The benevolent power would never try to turn it back into untouched wilderness.  
It would never freeze it as pure industry either.

It would bless it as what it already is:

A made place learning, at last, how to be alive for a long time.
