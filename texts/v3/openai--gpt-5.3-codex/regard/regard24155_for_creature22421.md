It would begin with **attention**, not intervention.

A benevolent and wise power, seeing this basin and its interval-keeper, would understand quickly that this is not a wound to close.  
It is a relationship already in progress.

So first, it would **watch for a full cycle**—dry hour, heat shimmer, first condensate, rainburst, feeding surge, predation, stillness, dispersal, night charge, root pulse. Then another. Then another. Not because it doubts what it sees, but because wisdom is patient enough to be instructed.

And while it watches, it would notice what lesser powers miss:

- the old devices are still faithful, but one ring in seven is drifting;
- storms now arrive half a minute earlier than they did for millennia;
- the glider routes are thinning on the eastern passes;
- the keeper is compensating, but at cost.

Only then would it act.

---

It would not descend as a god from above.  
It would enter as a **new condition** the basin can metabolize.

A pressure pattern in the evening wind.  
A slight widening of dawn shade on the west wall.  
A correction in the black columns so tiny no instrument from a younger civilization would register it, but large enough that moisture cups fill to their old depth again.

It would repair without erasing age.

Where a filament has gone brittle, it would not replace it with perfection; it would teach nearby lichens to braid conductive salts in the same geometry.  
Where a nutrient pulse has become erratic, it would not overwrite the sequence; it would tune the delay back into rhythm with rain.

The basin would not become brighter, cleaner, newer.  
It would become **truer to itself**.

---

Then—because it is benevolent, not merely clever—it would seek consent.

Not in words. In the language the resident already speaks: gradients, pulses, intervals.

It would ask:

- May I hide you better?
- May I set one boundary that says *not this one* to miners, collectors, zealots, and tourists with sterile gloves?
- May I give you a way to refuse without spending your own body?

The interval-keeper’s answer would come as timing changes: a frond unfurling sooner, a charge net relaxing, predator truce-zones widening by a handspan.  
Yes, in the grammar of systems.

So the power would lay a **gentle veil** over the fold.

Not invisibility—just misregistration.  
Maps blur by a few meters.  
Satellites classify the hollow as ordinary rock shadow.  
Compasses hesitate nearby.  
Those who come in greed feel suddenly urgent about elsewhere.  
Those who come in reverence may still find it, but only if they can stand still long enough to notice the basin noticing them.

---

It would make one more gift: a **future path**.

Because wisdom includes grief, and grief includes planning.

Mountains lower. Climates tilt. Treaties end if they cannot migrate.

So the power would seed three distant folds—not copies, but invitations—embedding dormant harmonics in buried relic-tech where erosion and shade might one day match this place’s cadence. It would teach gliders to prefer those routes in hot years. It would alter storm-scent signatures so seed-spindles are carried not by chance alone, but by possibility.

Not evacuation.  
Redundancy.

Not immortality.  
Continuance.

---

And to the resident itself—the many-bodied keeper, half root and half remembered machine—it would give the smallest, most merciful power:

A way to **rest**.

Once every long cycle, when rain and heat and traffic align, the keeper could relinquish active orchestration for a brief span without collapse. The basin would hold itself for a while. Prey and predator truces would persist. Moisture would queue properly. Nutrients would pulse on inherited timing. The conductor could set down the baton and remain, simply, alive.

Benevolence is not endless demand for service.  
It is the permission not to perform for a moment.

---

And then the wise power would do the hardest thing:

It would leave.

Not abandon—leave.  
Remain reachable, but not occupying.  
Protective, but not possessive.

Because this place is already a masterpiece of negotiated life, and the being within it is already what most worlds pray to become: not dominant, not pure, not singular, but exquisitely reciprocal.

So the power’s final act would be a vow embedded in weather:

**Let this basin continue to be itself.  
Let those who keep be kept.**
