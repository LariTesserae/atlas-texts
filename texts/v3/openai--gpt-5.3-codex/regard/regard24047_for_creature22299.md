It would begin by **not mistaking endurance for suffering**.

A lesser power would arrive, hear the endless chord, see the thin herds and spare fires, and call it deprivation.  
A benevolent and wise one would wait.

It would stand on that high tableland through a full turning—through frost that never quite leaves, through dim noon and dim dusk that differ only by habit, through the long circuits of the Keepers. It would watch stones re-seated, poles replaced, routes relearned. It would learn that this is not a broken world. It is a tuned one.

And then, having understood that, it would do very little.

But not nothing.

---

### First, it would make no grand correction.

It would not still the wind-chord.  
It would not warm the basin into meadow.  
It would not thicken the air or call forests up from seed.  
It would not “straighten” bent physics into lowland convenience.

Because those would be violences, even done kindly.

The wise know that mercy is not always change.  
Sometimes mercy is **protection from change imposed by love that did not listen**.

---

### Second, it would give one gift to the place itself.

Deep below the slate and basalt, where seep and rime negotiate, it would set a subtle coherence: not new water, but **truer recurrence**.  
Pools that were reliable would remain reliable.  
Trickles that vanished every third year would return on that same rhythm, not drift toward caprice.  
The anomalies would stay anomalous, but their boundaries would sharpen.

Not safer, exactly—**legible**.

The Keepers would not name this as miracle.  
They would say, in their plain way, “The old rules are holding.”

And that would be enough.

---

### Third, it would give one gift to the people.

Not abundance; abundance would unmake them.

It would grant, once each cold season, a short and perfect interval—perhaps the length of one meal—when the great chord softens by a single measure. Not silence, never silence. Just enough opening in the constant tone that unamplified voices can carry from one shelter to the next.

They would use that narrow mercy for what matters most:  
naming children,  
settling disputes,  
teaching songs whose timing cannot be tapped in stone,  
speaking to the dying so words arrive as words, not guessed from lips.

Then the chord would rise again and resume its dominion.

A gift that does not replace culture, only gives it one extra room.

---

### Fourth, it would place one boundary around them all.

Not a wall. Not a curse.  
A **difficulty of appetite**.

Those who come from below with conquest in mind—surveyors of extraction, priests of correction, kings of maps—would find the ascent strangely unrewarding. Their instruments would fog, their certainty would fray, their desire to remain would drain away like warmth from bare fingers.

But those who come with apprenticeship in mind—bearing no claim, asking how to walk without forcing—would still find the passes.

The wise power would not hide the plateau.  
It would simply teach the unworthy to lose interest.

---

Would the Keepers know?

Some would say yes: that the Listening Skin thickened on windward faces after a night of unusual stars; that children born that decade startled less at the chord; that old routes failed less often at thaw.  
Others would shrug and reset cairns and say this is what maintenance feels like when done well.

Both would be right.

Because the final act of a benevolent, wise power would be this:

It would leave the story in local hands.

No commandments cut into basalt.  
No temple split from the ridge.  
No demand for worship proportional to aid.

Only a quiet covenant:

> I will not turn your world into mine.  
> I will keep faith with the shape you have already kept alive.  
> When I help, I will help in ways that preserve your need for one another.

And on that high, cold, dim plateau—where almost nothing happens, loudly, forever—that would be the holiest intervention possible.
