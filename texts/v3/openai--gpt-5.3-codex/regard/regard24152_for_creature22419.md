A benevolent and wise power, seeing this basin, would understand one thing immediately:

**nothing here survives being “improved” too quickly.**

So at first, it would do almost nothing.

---

### First: it would witness before it would act.

It would stay through one full cycle of wet burst, gray pause, creep-fall, reroute, bloom, migration, hunger, repair.  
It would watch the stoneworkers, yes—but also the gliders at first dim, the goats refusing a stair six hours before a slide, the ink-thin hunters avoiding a wall already dead but not yet fallen.

It would notice that this is not many systems.  
It is one system with many bodies.

And because it is wise, it would not bless one species against another.  
It would bless **relationship**.

---

### Second: it would choose the gentlest lever.

A foolish power would stop all collapse.  
A cruel power would let collapse teach harder lessons.  
A wise power would do something narrower:

It would make warning arrive a little earlier.

Not certainty. Not control.  
Just a margin.

- Hairline fractures would weep a faint mineral scent hours before they shear.
- Warm mist would carry a copper taste strong enough that even children know to leave upper stairs.
- Roost-walls, when overburdened, would thrum at the edge of hearing, like a wooden bell struck far away.
- Moss at dangerous seams would pale overnight, visible by emberlight.

No laws are broken.  
No physics undone.  
But attention gets teeth.

---

### Third: it would give no throne, only a practice.

The power would refuse temples of stone that ask for permanence in a place built on change.  
If people tried to build one, it would crack—not as punishment, as correction.

Instead, it would establish a rite shared by all guilds and, in their ways, by the nonhuman residents too:

**The Hour of Mutual Count.**

Once each cycle, at first dim, all cutting and hauling stop.  
Humans stand in lanes and on terraces.  
Gliders settle.  
Herds pause at the edge-lines.  
Even predators go still in shadow.

For one hour, everyone counts what shares the basin:
water points, active nests, cracked walls, new runs, missing paths, births, deaths, absences.

Then those counts are exchanged—not as ownership, as orientation.

The gift is simple: after the Hour, lies become harder to maintain.  
Not impossible. Just heavier in the mouth.  
Enough that policy drifts toward truth.

---

### Fourth: it would protect plurality.

Benevolence here would not mean making life safe for humans alone.  
It would mean preventing any one intelligence from becoming deaf to the rest.

So the power would lay a quiet constraint over the basin:

Whoever takes too much influence too fast—guild, family, outsider, even charismatic reformer—finds their signals blunted. Flags tangle. Mirrors fog. Messengers arrive late. Plans overreach and fail.

Whoever coordinates without domination finds strange luck:  
a rope where needed, a clear hour in a week of mist, a herd that chooses the right detour.

In this way, the basin keeps its constitutional principle:  
**authority is weather-dependent, role-dependent, and temporary.**

---

### Fifth: it would answer prayer selectively.

If asked, “Make us rich,” it would be silent.  
If asked, “Make us unbreakable,” it would be silent.  
If asked, “Make our enemies leave,” it would be silent.

If asked, “Let us notice in time,” it would answer.  
If asked, “Let us repair without blaming,” it would answer.  
If asked, “Let us remain many without becoming war,” it would answer.

Its miracles would always look like this:  
not escape from condition, but deeper fluency within it.

---

### What of “the being who lives there”?

In time the power would name it—not the people, not the fauna, not the rock alone, but the composite:

**The Listener-With-Many-Bodies.**

And to that being it would grant one final blessing:

When disaster comes too large for skill—major wall-fall, sickness in the grazers, multi-season dry—the basin will, for three nights, dream collectively.

Not in words. In routes. In textures. In pressure maps and thirst maps and fear maps.

Humans wake knowing where animals will move.  
Animals move as if they remember human barriers before they see them.  
Predators hunt outward, not inward.  
Repair begins in the right places first.

After three nights, the gift ends.  
Choice returns.  
Work resumes.

---

And then, because it is truly wise, the power would recede.

Not gone—just not central.

It would leave behind no empire, no scripture of domination, no dependency.  
Only a slightly finer instrument panel for a civilization already built on care:

- a little more warning,  
- a little less arrogance,  
- a ritual of shared counting,  
- and the durable idea that survival is a conversation, not a victory.

In other words, it would do what the best gods and best elders do in unstable country:

**teach listening, then trust the listeners.**
