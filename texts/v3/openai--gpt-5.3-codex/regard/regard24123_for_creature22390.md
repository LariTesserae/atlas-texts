A lesser power would have tried to fix it.

It would have raised a ringwall, forced out the seep, pinned the banks with stone, driven straight roads through the layered walkways, and called that mercy.  
For a year, perhaps two, people would praise the relief—dry feet, bright fires, no midnight settling.  
Then the runnels would choke, pressure would travel blind through clay, rot would gather where water once moved, and the basin would answer in one hard season instead of a thousand small warnings.  
The town would die of being corrected.

A wise power, seeing this, would do almost nothing dramatic.

First, it would watch. Not from above, but from within: as mist on roof-bridges, as coolness in pile-wood, as a listening in the channels. It would learn that this place is not broken. It is exacting.  
It would learn that the “being” here is composite—human tendon, reed mat, tarred knot, eel run, slump line, taboo, lullaby—one metabolism in many skins.

Then it would choose interventions that increase relationship, not control.

---

It would make **warnings kinder**.

Not safety; never safety.  
But a little more lead time between mistake and collapse.

Posts beginning to hollow would change tone just enough for younger ears to hear, not only elders. Fresh-cut banks about to shear would exhale a faint mineral smell at dusk. Rope under dangerous tension would bead condensation in a distinct pattern—three bright droplets in a line, like punctuation.  
Nothing supernatural enough to end skill.  
Only signals clearer by one breath.

People would think, at first, that they had become more attentive.  
The wise power would be pleased to let them think so.

---

It would give the basin **one new green**.

Not a tree. Trees would tear this place apart or starve in smoke.  
It would bring a low, stubborn binder: a salt-tolerant moss-liverwort mat that grips raw clay in threads, drinks smoke, and survives trampling if left two hands’ width along primary runnels. Not everywhere—only where edges are most young and banks most hungry.  
It would not stop erosion; it would slow panic.

Children would name it before scholars did.  
They would call it *holdfast skin*.

In time, “leave the holdfast margin” would become part of runnel court, recited with the old rules.  
A taboo newly born, but practical from day one.

---

It would alter **memory**, not weather.

This town already keeps calendars on bone slats and law in chant. The power would strengthen that genius. On certain mornings, condensation on chosen roof pots would dry in repeating marks—tiny maps of pressure, tide lag, inland seep. Not prophecy. Pattern.  
A season of these marks, compared against old slats, would reveal shifts too slow for one lifetime and too fast for complacency.

So they could move three platforms this year, not thirty in terror next year.  
So they could deepen a spill channel before the storm that would have found it shallow.  
So they could remain a practice instead of becoming a ruin.

No priest required. Just readers.

---

And because benevolence without humility becomes tyranny, the power would refuse worship that asks for exemption.

If asked, “Keep my house from settling,” it would not.  
If asked, “Spare my child from the basin’s law,” it would not.  
If asked, “Show me where to place weight so my neighbor fails first,” it would turn silent and heavy in the air.

But if asked, “Help us hear in time,”  
if asked, “Help us mend without blindness,”  
if asked, “How do we remain together when the ground is not,”  
then the fog would thin exactly where a seam needed seeing, or a drifting plank would tap twice against the correct post, or an old woman would wake with the right knot in her hands.

Miracles small enough to be mistaken for competence.  
That is the preferred scale of wise love.

---

Over generations, outsiders would say the basin had become gentler.

Insiders would disagree.  
They would say: the basin is still itself—young, wet, immediate, unwilling to be ignored.  
What changed is that they and it learned a slightly finer conversation.

Their laws would gain one line:

**Leave a living margin where water teaches.**

Their greeting would gain one phrase:

**“Third gantry holds. Holdfast taking.”**

And when children asked whether the town was alive, elders would answer as before:

“We do. It does. Same thing, on most days.”

Then, after a pause, newer and truer:

“And something kind listens when we listen back.”
