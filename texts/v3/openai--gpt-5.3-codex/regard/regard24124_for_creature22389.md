It did **not** come to rule.

A benevolent and wise power, seeing this basin, would understand quickly that it had arrived late to a work already underway:  
stone teaching water, water teaching animal, animal teaching stone how not to vanish.

So it would begin with restraint.

---

### First, it would watch an entire turning

Not a day. Not a season.  
Years of mistfall and drought, mating surges and lean spells, wall-tilts and root-splits.

It would learn the old females’ patrol loops.  
It would learn which gutters must clear first, which cisterns can silt, which dog packs are all noise and which are knives.  
It would learn where sound lies and where it tells the truth.

A wise power does not heal what is merely unfamiliar.

---

### Then it would make only **small, structural mercies**

Not miracles that rewrite the place.  
Adjustments no heavier than a palm on a shoulder.

- In the worst three weeks of dry years, predawn mist would condense just a little more on the upper stone runs.
- In stormburst months, one in ten destructive downpours would break early at the rim, sparing the oldest retaining walls.
- A few key disease chains—those that could crash bats, frogs, or the lintel apes in a single season—would fail to jump when they otherwise might.

No species removed. No death abolished.  
Just catastrophe rounded down to survivable.

---

### It would choose no king, no prophet

But it might choose listeners.

The map-old females, dozing in noon-dark arcades, would begin to dream in route fragments:  
a blocked notch, a new crack over a cistern throat, a stair that will shear in first rain.

They would wake before dawn and go there first, and the troop would follow, and the channels would run.

No one would call this revelation.  
They would call it experience, which is close enough.

---

### It would place one law into the acoustics

Not in words. In reflex.

At certain water mouths, where border fights too often became falls and dead young, a new response would slowly spread through the lintel clans: a percussive pause-call—three heel taps, one tooth-click—meaning **drink first, contest later**.

Not peace. Never perfect peace.  
But a ritual interruption at the lip of necessity.

Over generations, that tiny hesitation would save more lives than thunderbolts ever could.

---

### It would protect the basin from the wrong discoverers

This is the one overt act.

If extractive minds came—surveying eyes, straight-line plans, hunger for salvage—the basin would seem broken, unpromising, inaccessible.  
Cloud would sit low. Paths would mislead. Echoes would confuse direction.  
They would leave with sketches that do not match one another.

If reverent minds came—patient, observant, willing to be instructed by damp stone—they could find it, and be found in return.

A wise power is not neutral about appetite.

---

### And finally, it would accept worship in the only form that matters here

Not incense.  
Not carved idols.  
Not fear.

Its temple would be this: a lintel ape clearing leaves from a drip-channel at first mist.  
A dog pack turning from a nursery vault because of a warning bark.  
Goats stepping a path kept open by generations of small hands.  
Water arriving where yesterday it did not.

The power’s blessing would be continuity with room for change.

It would not make the basin eternal.  
It would make it harder to end stupidly.

And when, long from now, a wall finally goes and a fig takes the court and the routes shift again, it would not call that failure.

It would call that the city still alive enough to be rewritten.
