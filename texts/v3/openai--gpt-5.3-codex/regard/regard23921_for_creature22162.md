If the power is truly benevolent, and truly wise, it does **not** turn the basin into a valley.

It does not summon forests where no root can hold.  
It does not pour warm wind into every crack.  
It does not make the worker invulnerable, because invulnerability would unteach the listening that keeps them alive.

Instead, it studies the place first.

It learns the basin’s grammar: thaw, seal, thaw.  
It learns the worker’s grammar: strike, pause, listen.  
And then it answers in the same language.

---

At first, what it does is almost nothing.

A gust arrives three breaths later than it would have, and a person clears an exposed traverse before the slab-ice skates loose.  
A hairline crack gives one extra ping before it shears, and a hand withdraws in time.  
A fog bank thins for sixty heartbeats exactly where the next cairn stands, and route memory catches.

No thunder. No voice from the sky.  
Just timing, corrected by mercy.

---

Then it blesses materials, not outcomes.

- Stone still breaks, but less treacherously.
- Clay still freezes, but keeps its seal one more cycle.
- Iron still rusts, but slowly enough to be reset rather than lost.
- Gloves still wear through, yet fingertips do not numb at the critical stroke.

The worker still labors.  
The mountain still moves.  
But waste decreases, and warning increases.

A wise power helps where effort can meet it.

---

It also protects the craft from vanishing.

Not by writing laws in fire, but by arranging continuities:

A traveler who never meant to climb this high is delayed by weather and shelters in the stone-roofed cell. They notice the ledger of marks. They do not understand it yet—but they copy it before leaving. Years later, they return with better tools and reverence.

An elder in the valley dreams repeatedly of dark ribs of rock and a wall built against a particular wind. They send up a roll of felt, two new hammer hafts, and a child who asks exact questions.

A lost pin glints at dusk in the one angle of light that reveals it.

In this way, the “one person at a time” never becomes “the last person.”

---

And once—only once in a generation—it intervenes openly.

When a whole section of loaded cornice and rotten plate lets go above the shelter, the fall breaks oddly, splitting around a shoulder of stone that should not have held. The debris runs past. The shelter survives by a hand’s breadth.

Afterward, no one calls it a miracle in public.  
They say: good fracture luck, unusual but possible.  
Then they rebuild the wall, reset the markers, and give thanks in the form they trust most: maintenance.

The power is pleased with this.

---

Because benevolence here is not rescue from reality.  
It is companionship within reality.

The basin remains bright, austere, and exacting.  
Cold remains cold.  
Edges remain honest.  
The worker remains mortal.

But there is now a second attention braided through the first—  
an unseen colleague that does not command, only steadies;  
does not replace skill, only rewards it;  
does not abolish danger, only keeps danger legible.

If you asked what the wise power did, the truest answer would be:

It **kept faith with the place**.

It refused to make it easier than it is.  
It refused to leave it harder than it must be.  
And it made sure that every careful act—every pin reset, every foothold recut, every stone placed true in glare and wind—was met, quietly, by grace.
