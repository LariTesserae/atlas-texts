They would not “fix” it.

A wise, benevolent power—seeing your basin and the Basin Broker—would understand at once that this is not a broken place. It is a place already solving itself at high speed, under poor terms.

So the power would begin with **attention**, not command.

---

### 1) First, they would witness the whole mesh

Not the rat.  
Not the crab.  
Not the lamp post.  
Not the sump.

All of it, as one breathing apparatus.

They would name it correctly: a many-bodied person of routes and timing. A civic body without a single skull. A republic of drips.

And because they named it truly, they would not insult it with a crown, a mascot, or a captive specimen jar.

---

### 2) They would remove only what is purely harm

A wise power does not confuse hardship with cruelty.

They would leave hunger, predation, weather, risk, competition—because those are grammar here.  
But they would quietly unmake the things that are only poison:

- the solvent seam that blinds tadpoles,
- the metal dust that sterilizes eggs,
- the sharp wire loop that kills for no eater,
- the midnight discharge that turns one puddle into a grave.

Not by miracle thunder. By subtle edits in flow:
a diverted line, a neutralizing seep, a bind in the soil, a rerouted drip.

In a month, no one could point to the intervention.  
In a season, survival curves would bend.

---

### 3) They would add **slack** to the system

Your basin runs hot: hatch/feed/hide/reset.  
Benevolence, here, is not abundance. It is margin.

So they would create tiny redundancies:

- one more reliable wet pocket through dry spells,
- one shaded strip that stays cool at noon,
- one safe rough wall no cat can descend,
- one perch line for birds that hunt insects, not nestlings,
- one mineral face that regrows faster than snails can strip it.

Not enough to domesticate the place.  
Enough to prevent single-point collapse.

---

### 4) They would protect it from the clean, blunt hand of improvement

This is where wisdom bites.

The greatest threat is not owl, rust, or drought.  
It is a project plan.

So the power would work through human bureaucracy—the modern weather system.  
Permits stall. Ownership records tangle. A survey flags “unstable utility substrate.”  
The basin becomes too inconvenient to pave, too minor to redevelop, too risky to grade.

A legal invisibility cloak.

The lamp still clicks on each evening.  
But the bulldozers never receive a date.

---

### 5) They would recruit one or two humans, gently

Not prophets. Custodians.

Maybe a night-shift plumber who starts leaving cracked ceramic dishes under condensation lines.  
Maybe a teenage trespasser who becomes an ecologist.  
Maybe a city inspector who marks the area as a micro-retention habitat instead of “vacant nuisance.”

The power would give them no booming voice, only repeated coincidences until care feels obvious.

---

### 6) They would make a covenant, not a rescue

At dawn, for nine breaths, hunting pauses.  
At first rain, overflow routes stay open.  
One culvert mouth remains unblocked, always.  
No species owns the center for long.

In return, the basin keeps doing what it already does best: turning leftovers into continuance.

---

And that would be all.

No shining dome.  
No paradise garden.  
No erasure of death.

Just this: less pointless suffering, more continuity, protected complexity, and enough room for the Basin Broker to remain itself—many, quick, unspectacular, alive.

A benevolent power, if truly wise, would not lift this place out of the world.

It would help the world stop crushing it.
