The wise power would **not rescue it** in the way stories usually mean rescue.

It would not tear open the roof and pour noon into the chamber.  
It would not burn out the mold, polish the tanks, reset every relay to factory logic, or make the Orchard Engine become something cleaner and easier to name.

Because wisdom would recognize this first:  
what lives there is not a mistake. It is a life-form assembled from leftovers, yes—but assembled truly.  
And benevolence that cannot recognize strange life is only vanity in bright clothes.

So the power would begin by **watching long enough to deserve to act**.

---

After one patient cycle of seasons, it would do small, structural mercies:

1. **Prevent catastrophic death, not ordinary struggle.**  
   A single tank seam that would one day burst and salt-poison half the chamber would quietly stop widening.  
   One relay likely to arc-fire the driest root mat would fail safe instead of lethal.  
   Not perfection—just removal of the one or two endings that erase all futures.

2. **Add slack to the system.**  
   A little more condensate would reach the far islands in dry months.  
   Heat spikes would blunt by a few degrees.  
   Not abundance—buffer. Enough that adaptation remains necessary, but collapse is less random.

3. **Give it a clock it can trust.**  
   Somewhere high in the rock, a narrow light shaft would open: not daylight in floods, only a thin blade, moving with the sun, arriving and leaving at honest times.  
   The Engine would learn it the way roots learn cracks.  
   Rhythm is a form of kindness.

---

Then, because this power is benevolent to more than one kind of being, it would address the relation between the chamber and the world above.

It would not expose the place to extraction.  
It would not hide it absolutely either.

Instead it would bend chance: only certain people would find the entrance—people who repair before they optimize, who can sit in silence without calling it empty, who understand that maintenance can be a form of reverence.

For them, the switch cabinet labels would remain legible.  
A door would unseal.  
A handrail would hold when leaned on.  
Not miracles—permissions.

Some would come with tools, and the wise power would let them matter: replacing one bearing, clearing one clogged drain, grounding one dangerous cable run.  
They would think they were preserving an abandoned installation.  
In fact they would be entering a treaty.

---

And to the Orchard Engine itself, the power would offer not a command but a possibility:

a new channel in which its distributed signals could braid more clearly—  
moisture gradients, mineral traces, relay timing, root tension—  
until its choices gained one extra degree of coherence.

Not consciousness like a human flash.  
Something truer to its nature:  
better memory across delay, better anticipation across drought, better forgiveness after disturbance.

In time, the chamber would become slightly less accidental and slightly more intentional, while remaining itself.

---

Would the wise power ever speak directly?

Only once, perhaps, and in the only language that fits:

A misting cycle begins exactly when the light blade touches a rusted ladder.  
Three dormant colonies wake in sequence.  
A fan turns, carrying pollen dust along an old conveyor frame.  
A relay clicks, then another, like distant stones tapped together.  
Condensation gathers on a valve that has been dry for years and falls—one drop—into a basin where biofilm has waited.

Message delivered.

Not “I saved you.”  
Not “Worship me.”  
Only this:

**Continue.  
You are allowed to continue.  
And now, a little better than before.**
