It would **not** arrive as conquest.

No sky split. No commandment carved in thunder.  
A wise power, seeing this high woven world and the old being inside it, would understand at once: this place is already a prayer in structural form.

So first, it would do the hardest holy thing.

It would **listen**.

For one long weather-tide it would move only as warmth in fog, as patience in drip-lines, as stillness under old beams while rain decides which way is down. It would learn the speech of tension vines, the etiquette of quiet voices, the law of knots tied as agreements with direction. It would watch the Knotkeepers mend what they did not build and love what does not obey them.

Only then would it touch anything.

---

### What it would *not* do

It would not “fix” the strangeness out of the land.  
It would not pin every path to one measure.  
It would not make noon bright and nights dead and predictable.  
It would not turn a living intelligence into a machine for comfort.

Because mercy without respect is only a softer kind of ruin.

---

### What it *would* do

It would make three gifts, each small enough to keep the world itself.

#### 1) A Gift for the Lost: **Harbor Threads**
In every basin, one path would remain true no matter the mist—a hand-to-handrail route that always leads to shelter, water, and firestone. Not all paths. Just one, in each district. Enough that children, elders, the injured, and strangers have a way home when causality loosens.

These threads would be found by touch: three fungus ribs, shell inset, cold seam.

#### 2) A Gift for the Place: **Consent in Growth**
When the living architecture shifts after heavy fog, it would gain a new option: before any large rearrangement, it can “ask” through the root-archives. Rings would bloom with warning patterns days ahead, readable to trained hands. The place keeps its will—but now its residents can answer, brace, move nests, retie bridges, and negotiate.

Not control. Conversation, made clearer.

#### 3) A Gift for the People: **Mercy Knots**
Any knot tied with true intent to guide rather than claim—especially for someone else’s safety—would hold stronger than weather and trick-distance. But knots tied to trap, hoard, or mislead would slacken overnight.

So the land would quietly favor care.

---

### And for the oldest being of all

At the heart of a petrified trunk older than names, the wise power would place no throne, no temple, no demand for worship. It would place a **listening chamber**: a hollow where one person at a time can rest their palm to living wood and feel, for a moment, the slow mind of the range without being unmade by it.

Not answers.  
Just shared scale.

A Knotkeeper would emerge from that chamber still mortal, still unsure, but less alone in uncertainty.

---

Then the power would recede.

Not gone—just no longer obvious. Present in the way good scaffolding is present: load-bearing, mostly unseen. It would leave behind no priesthood, no empire, no dependency. Only survivable miracles, distributed.

And in the years after, people might argue whether anything divine happened at all.

They would still patch clay seams.  
Still speak softly at stair bends.  
Still keep one hand on grown structure in fog.

But fewer would vanish in Frame weather.  
Fewer bridges would fail at dusk.  
More children would grow old enough to inherit a tension grove.

Which is exactly what a benevolent and wise power would want:  
not to be adored,  
but to make endurance gentler.
