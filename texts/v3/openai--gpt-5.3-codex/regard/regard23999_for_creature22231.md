At first, the wise power would do what the wisest helpers do:

**it would refrain.**

Not out of indifference—out of respect.

From above, it would see that this basin is not broken. It is severe, yes. Spare, yes. But not broken.  
It is a vow made visible.  
And vows are living things; if you lay a hand on them too quickly, they fracture.

So for a long while, the power would only learn the local grammar:  
how a notch means fullness,  
how a scrape means scaling,  
how three hollow knocks in a vent shaft mean “come now, not later.”  
It would watch the custodians move through glare like punctuation marks in a sentence that has never ended.

Then, when it finally acted, it would act **in their language**.

---

### First gift: warning

No wall would become immortal. No timber would stop rotting. No salt would cease to bloom.  
That would be domination, not mercy.

Instead, the power would place a kindness into matter:

- stone about to shear would sing a day earlier,  
- pegs about to split would darken at the grain,  
- siphons about to air-lock would stutter in a recognizable rhythm,  
- hairline cracks would hold dew even in dry wind.

Nothing prevented.  
Everything announced.

Failures would remain possible, but never again ambush.  
The custodians, who already live just ahead of collapse, would gain a little more margin—an hour here, a morning there.  
Enough to sleep one full cycle without betraying the basin.

---

### Second gift: memory without burden

The power would notice the cost of perfect maintenance: every worn stone erased before it tells its story.  
A young world, always young, can become a world with no ancestors.

So it would do something tiny and profound:  
where a stone is replaced, the removed one would leave behind a faint mineral echo in the bed beneath it—  
a ghost-line, visible only when wetted by dawn condensation.

Not decoration. Not monument.  
A hidden archive legible to those who kneel to work.

Over generations, the custodians would begin reading those pale traces:  
who set this runnel first,  
when the grade was corrected,  
which winter demanded three resets in the same bay.  
History would return, not as ruins, but as a second, translucent layer in the present tense.

---

### Third gift: one sanctioned stillness

The power would understand the strictness of Rule 2—water must move—and why it exists.  
Stagnation is danger here. Motion is health.

But it would also understand grief.

So in the deepest service chamber under the oldest dome, the power would alter one basin by less than a thumb’s width.  
Enough that a cupful of water could remain unworried for a single night before rejoining flow at dawn.

Only there. Only that long.

A lawful pause.

The custodians would find it, test it, distrust it, then keep it.  
They would use it to remember their dead, to quiet their hands, to see one unbroken reflection before returning to systems and slopes.  
Not a violation of the order—its completion.

---

It would not green the valley.  
It would not summon birds.  
It would not pry up masonry to “restore” an imagined past.

A benevolent intelligence does not confuse its preferences with salvation.

It would leave the four rules intact, and add no shouted fifth commandment.  
Only this subtle amendment, discovered rather than proclaimed:

> **Care may include care for the carers.**

And after making these changes, the power would withdraw again into weather and distance.

From then on, outsiders would still say what they always said:  
nothing lives here but water under instruction.

But if they listened longer, they might hear something else threaded through drip and pulley and siphon-throat—  
a little more time in every warning sound,  
a little more depth in every replaced stone,  
a little stillness hidden at the center of motion.

Not rescue.  
Not miracle in the theatrical sense.

Just wisdom behaving as maintenance does:  
small, exact, and in time.
