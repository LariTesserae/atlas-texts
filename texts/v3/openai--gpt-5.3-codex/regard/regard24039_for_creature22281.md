The wise power would not arrive as a miracle that ends history.

It would not “fix” the basin into obedience, flatten the fault-grids, still every echo, and call that mercy.  
It would recognize, almost immediately, that this place is not broken in the ordinary sense. It is difficult, yes. Dangerous, often. But also *inhabited by competence*. Loved through labor. Kept alive by people who know the names of hums and pressures and weather-moods the way others know saints.

So first, it would do something rare for power:

It would watch.

For one full turning of seasons it would remain nearly imperceptible—present in condenser mist, in turbine vibration, in the green-white dusk glow along root seams—learning the grammar before speaking. It would learn which bell patterns mean “all clear” and which mean “hold where you stand.” It would learn the taboo crossings, the branch-laws, the way children tuck clipped sprigs into sleeves before running errands. It would learn that maintenance here is not drudgery but citizenship.

Only after it had learned enough not to insult them would it act.

---

It would begin with legibility, not control.

On mornings before phase-overlap events, orientation glyphs would bead with silver condensation in the direction of safe passage. Not always—only when danger rose above ordinary risk. Enough to reduce deaths. Not enough to make surveyors obsolete.

At repeating intersections, if someone approached empty-handed in panic, a volunteer vine from nearby rootfelt would loosen and trail within reach, as if the city itself offered a handhold.  
No one would call this a violation of law. They would call it law answered kindly.

During heavy fog, public bells would gain a third harmonic, very soft, audible mostly to children and elders: a stitching tone that helped clocks settle faster. Shift choristers would notice first. They would argue for weeks whether the resonance came from a structural change in the towers. They would eventually conclude: perhaps. They would recalibrate anyway.

---

Next, it would honor the dead without weaponizing grief.

In this basin, loss has always been practical and intimate: a snapped cable, a misread echo, a terrace fold at dusk. The wise power would not erase those names into painless forgetting. It would inscribe them into function.

After that winter, certain hazard chimes would carry tiny timbral signatures—different for each district—mapped to memorial rolls held in civic halls. “If warning sounds like this, wait; if like that, reroute.”  
The lost would become part of safety infrastructure.  
Memory would do work.

No statues. No thunderous proclamations.  
Just fewer funerals.

---

Then it would strengthen the margins of life.

Not by introducing grand beasts or romantic wilderness, but by repairing ecological threads already present: swifts breeding more steadily in vent stacks, terrace grazers hardier through frost snaps, translucent moth blooms less prone to collapse when condensers overheat. Pollination kites would return with cleaner patterns, and wind-health maps would finally stop showing those old dead zones near the west ridges.

The power would never let abundance become complacency.  
It would not gift excess.  
It would gift *buffer*.

A few more chances per year.  
A few fewer single-point failures.  
Just enough slack in the system for mercy to fit.

---

Only once would it act openly.

During a storm season remembered for decades, when three district clocks desynchronized and an entire lower causeway entered cascading phase shear, people expected to lose the corridor and everyone still on it. Surveyors rang hold-signals. Root masons tied emergency lines. Choristers struck bells until their palms bled.

Then the basin did something no one had seen:

For seven minutes, all unstable geometry in the corridor resolved to one-to-one.  
No repeats. No folded distance. No false alleys.  
Just one true road from end to end.

Long enough for evacuation.  
Short enough to remain a gift, not a regime change.

Afterward the city returned to itself—dangerous, beautiful, negotiated. No permanent simplification. No conquest by benevolence.

---

And what of the being who lives there?

The wise power would understand that the “being” is not one body. It is distributed: in sapwright hands, in chorister breath, in branch tokens passed from elder to child, in root walls braided at dawn, in workers who still show up in sleet to tighten bolts no one will ever praise.

So it would choose covenant over rescue.

Its covenant would be simple:

- I will make warning clearer, not fate absent.  
- I will reduce cruelty, not consequence.  
- I will add resilience, not dependency.  
- I will remain answerable to your attention.

In return, the basin’s people would keep doing what they already do better than empires: maintain the world they have, instead of dreaming of another one while this one fails.

And in time, they would stop asking whether a power had visited.  
They would ask a better question, the kind wise powers hope for:

“Are we, in the way we tend one another, becoming worthy of what tended us?”
