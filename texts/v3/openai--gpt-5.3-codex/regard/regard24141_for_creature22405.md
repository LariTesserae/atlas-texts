It would **not** begin by fixing.

A wise power, on entering that underland, would understand quickly that this world is not broken in the ordinary way. It is violent, yes. Wasteful, yes. Loud enough to erase thought. But it is also *mid-sentence*—a living grammar still inventing its verbs.  
To impose silence or stability too fast would be a kind of murder.

So first, it would listen.

Not with ears. With pressure gradients in stone. With the timing of spore-release. With the half-second lag between herd-turn and fault-shift. With the Drumfolk heel-code tapped into ridge posts at dusk. It would learn that the place already has intelligence, just not centralized where outsiders expect.

It would notice three minds braided together:

- the swarming hunger of beasts and blooms,
- the adaptive cunning of the route-dwelling clans,
- and the deep vegetative host that thinks in pulses, not words.

And then, because it is benevolent, it would choose **aid without dominion**.

---

### What it would do

#### 1) It would create “Mercy Windows”
Not permanent peace—those fail here.  
Instead, brief, predictable intervals where certain violences cannot cascade.

During vent-storm onset, a low harmonic would rise from the humming stones—too low for most hearing, perfect for bone and gut. Predators would feel it as aversion; herds as corridor-memory; Drumfolk as an all-clear route-map beneath their feet.

For one hour in every storm surge, migration throats would become truce channels.  
No ambush reflex. No panic stampede feedback loop.  
Just passage.

Not utopia. Just fewer children crushed in bottlenecks.

#### 2) It would make warning legible across species
Right now, everyone senses danger differently and too late for others.  
The power would knit a translation layer into existing signals:

- spiral ceiling braid would trigger specific scent-plumes from nearby membranes,
- pre-collapse microtremors would induce visible color-ripple in certain fronds,
- gas chemistry spikes would make reed-forests click in a distinct triplet rhythm.

Nothing alien added; only old cues cross-wired so one life-form’s foresight becomes another’s chance.

The Drumfolk would call this gift *Shared Omen*.

#### 3) It would loosen water from cruelty—slightly
It would not flood the underland. That would erase the whole ecology.  
But in select chambers, it would alter mineral surfaces so night-cooling (however slight) condenses trace moisture into harvestable films inside protected stone combs.

Tiny yields. Sacred yields. Enough that hydration is no longer always purchased by killing.

Those places would become neutral ground, fiercely taboo: no hunting, no trapping, no blood on condensation stone.

#### 4) It would teach memory to persist
The underland remakes itself too fast; hard-won knowledge dies with camps.

So the power would help the Drumfolk and the host co-create **Resonance Archives**: carved-grown pillars storing event-patterns as stable hum frequencies. Touch stone, feel history in your skeleton:

- this pitch = roof shear after false quiet,
- this pulse = predator bloom following violet bladder rupture,
- this chord = safe high route during ashfall cycle.

Not writing in marks alone. Writing in vibration, the only medium that survives every dust storm.

---

### What it would refuse to do

It would refuse to end death.  
It would refuse to flatten the place into pasture.  
It would refuse to make the Drumfolk dependent on miracles.

A wise benevolence does not turn living peoples into pets.

So it would intervene *once, deeply*, then withdraw into background laws: harmonics, condensation niches, translated warnings, memory-stones. Enough to bend fate away from senseless loss, not enough to halt becoming.

---

### What happens after

Generations later, the underland is still loud, still hot, still perilous.  
But now its dangers are less arbitrary.

Children learn the Ninth Beat that opens storm truce-corridors.  
Hunters carry no blades near condensation combs.  
Pylon beasts begin pausing by archive stones, as if listening.  
Some predators even key their breeding to mercy-window gaps, adapting around restraint instead of being erased by it.

The Drumfolk stop calling the deep host “it.”  
They call it **Elder Tissue**.  
And in some clans, they add a fourth communication channel: palms on stone, waiting for return pulse.

As for the benevolent power, it is remembered not as conqueror or parent, but as something rarer:

**the one who made warning shareable.**  
**the one who gave time to choose.**
