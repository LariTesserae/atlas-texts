It would do almost nothing.

And that almost would be immense.

A benevolent, wise power—if it is truly both—would not arrive like a banner, a fire, a proclamation. It would come the way good rain comes to lichen: by agreement with what is already there. It would enter on fog, because fog already has the key.

It would stand (if standing applies) in that dark, warm pocket and understand quickly that this is not a broken place waiting to be improved. It is a held balance, a rare grammar of restraint: drip, pause, boundary, return. The creature is not a failed thing. It is a specialist in continuance. The chamber is not unfinished in the way a house is unfinished; it is unfinished in the way a seed is complete.

So the power would refuse the obvious violences of kindness.

It would not grant sight to eyes that do not need light.  
It would not speed growth in a room whose holiness is slowness.  
It would not turn the custodian into symbol, prophet, guardian-beast, or warning.

Instead, it would make three small covenants.

**First covenant: with water.**  
In years when snow retreats too high, when whole slopes crack and roots taste dust, one seam in that chamber would still weep. Not more than before—just enough. A single nodule would never go dry. The rhythm would hold: tick, pause, tick. The being would not starve by drought merely because valleys forgot winter.

**Second covenant: with humans.**  
Those who come in greed—miners of curiosities, collectors of rarity, makers of cages—would find only slick stone and a crack too narrow to enter, however wide it looked from afar. They would feel suddenly tired, or certain they took the wrong ridge, or remember an urgent task below.  
Those who come in reciprocity would find the fog at the right thickness, the opening where it has always been, and enough courage to bring little and leave little: bones, salt, silence, departure.

**Third covenant: with time.**  
Not immortality. Not exemption from ending. Something better: continuity with dignity.  
When at last this particular body of the custodian fails—tomorrow or centuries hence—the chamber would not sit empty and feral. In the same season, another life tuned to warmth gradients and mineral taste would arrive: born in some seep-fed cleft nearby, drawn by a map older than memory. Not a reincarnation, not a copy. A successor in the office of keeping.

And because wise benevolence includes witness, the power would also leave one hidden mercy for the mountain folk: now and then, in dreams, a child in some high village would see a dark room breathing and wake with an unteachable patience. That child might become nothing famous—just the one who repairs channels gently, tends fire without waste, listens before speaking. The chamber’s ethic would leak outward by human hands.

No temple would be raised.  
No doctrine declared.  
No pilgrim road cut into the slope.

Only this: the unfinished room remains unfinished, on purpose.  
The blind keeper keeps.  
The crack opens in fog.  
And a vast intelligence, having noticed, chooses to protect not by changing the song, but by defending its tempo.
