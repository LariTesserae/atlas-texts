They would begin by **not mistaking hunger for failure**.

A lesser power, seeing this high dry kingdom, would rush to fix it: call clouds, carve rivers, thicken air, soften light.  
A benevolent and wise one would see that such mercy, here, would be a kind of violence.  
This world is not unfinished. It is precise.

So the first thing they would do is **listen long enough to deserve acting at all**.

They would stand through a full thermal cycle, then a season, then many years.  
They would learn the click-interval of seed-cases, the hour when cliff mats wake, the routes of gliders written in pressure and sun-angle.  
They would learn that abundance here is measured in return-times, not density.  
That survival here is a mathematics of patience.  
That every life has already negotiated a treaty with distance.

Only then would they act.

And because they are wise, their actions would be small, structural, and mostly invisible.

---

### What they would not do

- They would not flood the basins.  
- They would not “green” the gravel into a valley ecology that would erase what evolved for austerity.  
- They would not make prey plentiful and predators easy; that would break the intelligence of timing.  
- They would not grant comfort that destroys competence.

They would refuse the vanity of dramatic rescue.

---

### What they would do for the place

They would place their hand—not on the surface, but on the probabilities.

- A civilization-ending impact, once every few million years, is nudged aside by less than a hair’s breadth.
- A stellar tantrum that would sterilize exposed ridges is thinned just enough by a magnetic veil no inhabitant will ever name.
- A pathogen capable of total monoculture collapse loses one crucial mutation and remains local.

Not safety. **Continuity**.

They would protect the world from the kinds of randomness that erase story, while leaving intact the hardships that *write* story.

Then, as a gift fitted to the world’s own grammar, they would make one gentle change:

On a small fraction of nights—irregular, unpatterned, impossible to exploit—they would allow a slightly better frostfall.  
Not enough to transform deserts into meadows.  
Just enough that old groves recover one failed year in ten.  
Just enough that cliffskin bands reconnect across a broken face.  
Just enough that rarity remains rarity, but not extinction.

Mercy, in teaspoons.

---

### What they would do for the being

And the being—whoever among this world has become capable of asking *why this and not otherwise*—would not be made king, prophet, or conqueror.

The wise power would give them no empire.

They would give them three quieter gifts:

1. **Discernment of interval**  
   The being would feel, in bone or sap or breath, when to wait and when to move: the true dawn, the false thaw, the once-per-season wind worth trusting. Not prediction of all things—just fewer tragic mistimings.

2. **An unbreakable sense of enough**  
   In a hard world, fear breeds hoarding, and hoarding breeds collapse. The being would carry a deep interior measure that says: take what sustains, leave what must continue.

3. **Companionship in silence**  
   Not a voice commanding from the sky, but a steady interior presence during long blue nights: You are not abandoned just because no one speaks.

If the being is solitary, this is how solitude becomes devotion rather than despair.  
If the being has people, this is how culture becomes stewardship instead of extraction.

---

### The sign they would leave

No temple.  
No pillar of fire.  
No law carved into stone.

Only this: once in a long while, at the edge of twilight, halos around the white sun would appear in impossible nested rings, and for a few breaths every frond in a spiregrove would align the same way, as if listening.

By dawn it would be over.  
Nothing provable.  
Everything changed by a degree too small for arrogance.

---

So what would a benevolent and wise power do here?

They would **honor the world’s hard-won intelligence**.  
They would **guard without ruling**.  
They would **help without making dependent**.  
They would **bless by preserving relationship: between stone and life, scarcity and skill, distance and return**.

And if they loved this place—and they would—they would love it in its own language:

not as a garden made wet,  
but as a high, bright kingdom where even mercy arrives as timing.
