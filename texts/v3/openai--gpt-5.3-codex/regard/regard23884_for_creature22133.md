At first, the wise power does nothing.

Not from indifference—from discipline.

It watches one full turning of freeze and thaw, one complete ledger of arrivals and losses.  
It learns the pass-winds by hour, the melt-lines by slope angle, the exact day when the cliff ledges become loud with hatchlings, the night when cold returns hard enough to close every shallow pool like a fist.

It watches the charcoal predator too:  
how it spends itself, where it refuses to spend, what it remembers, what it gambles, what it leaves behind for ravens and foxes and beetles.

Only when it can predict the place without reducing it does it intervene.

And even then, it does not intervene like a king.  
It intervenes like a patient mechanic.

---

### What the benevolent power changes

It does not make the country gentle.  
It makes it *slightly less wasteful*.

It gives the loosest scree faces one extra breath of coherence at dawn—  
just enough that a nursing mother and her small litter can cross before the slope resumes its rattling descent.

It deepens a handful of peat basins by the width of a paw,  
so one in ten thaw pools keeps water through an unexpected cold snap,  
and one in ten larvae clouds survives to wing.

It shifts mineral seepage by inches over years,  
laying trace salts and iron where grazers already pause,  
so the herds lose less to brittle hunger at winter’s end.

It encourages certain lichens on windward rock and certain sedges in black sponge-ground,  
not enough to green the high country,  
just enough to stitch a few false floors into true ones.

It does not erase death windows.  
It shortens the empty windows between them.

---

### What it gives the creature

Not strength.  
Not dominance.  
Not immunity from cold, famine, broken bone.

It gives timing a finer edge.

When the animal sleeps, it begins to dream in gradients:  
a pass two degrees warmer than yesterday,  
a snow bridge one night from collapse,  
a carrion slope that will open by noon instead of dusk.

Its feet grow no larger, yet place themselves better.  
Its lungs take no more air, yet waste less of each breath.  
Its hunger becomes less panicked, more precise.

And one more gift, small and radical:

When it kills, the fear-smell in the basin dissipates faster.  
Scavengers return sooner.  
Nothing sits unused long enough to sour.

The power cannot abide pointless spoilage.  
In this place, mercy often looks like efficient transfer.

---

### What it refuses to do

It refuses to warm the whole range.  
Refuses to flatten ridges.  
Refuses to make food constant.

Because constant abundance would unmake the intelligence that lives here.

This land is a school of intervals.  
Its native predator is a scholar of intervals.  
To remove the intervals would be to remove the animal itself and leave behind only a similar shape.

So the power leaves hardship intact, but trims catastrophe.  
Leaves hunger intact, but trims blind luck.  
Leaves wildness intact, but trims noise in the signal.

---

### The covenant (if such things can be spoken)

If the power could be heard in language, it would say:

> I will not make this place easy.  
> I will make it legible.  
> I will not spare every life.  
> I will reduce the lives spent for nothing.  
> I will not end winter.  
> I will teach more bodies where winter breaks first.

And to the charcoal worker of routes and pulses:

> Keep arriving on time.  
> I will widen time by a hair.

---

Years later, no monument marks the visitation.

There are only statistics, if anyone could take them:

- slightly more pups surviving first freeze,  
- slightly fewer migration crashes in late storms,  
- slightly more amphibian return after hard springs,  
- slightly less distance between carrion and lean days.

The country still looks unfinished.  
Raw stone still outruns soil.  
Water still redraws itself nightly.  
Cold still wins most arguments by dawn.

But now, in that high dim workshop,  
more cycles close cleanly.

And the creature—basalt-backed, fog-bellied, rime-collared—  
still moves like a fracture line given intent,  
arriving, more often than before,  
exactly when the world opens.
