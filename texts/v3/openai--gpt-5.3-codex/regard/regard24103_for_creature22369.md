A lesser power would have arrived with spectacle.

It would have split the sky, poured down oceans, carpeted the shelves in green, and called that mercy.

A wise one would not.

Because this world is not broken; it is *specific*.  
Its life is threaded through dryness, timing, conductivity, scarcity, and long memory.  
To flood it with abundance would be to erase it.

So the benevolent power does something harder than miracles: it learns the schedule.

It watches three full industrial generations before acting at all. It learns Run Days and Still Days, the condenser purges, the varnisher corridors, the sail-raker nesting failures that happen only after over-efficient descaling, the way one closed rail gate becomes hunger thirty kilometers away two years later. It learns that the “being” who lives here is not an emperor, prophet, or species.

It is the metabolism itself.

Then, when it finally intervenes, it does so in three mercies.

---

### I. It gives Margin, not rescue.

Not infinite energy. Not rain. Not salvation from physics.

Just margin.

Bearings run 3% cooler than they should in heat waves.  
Dust fronts arrive six hours later when repair crews are trapped on exposed lines.  
A cracked condenser spine holds one more cycle before failure.  
A migration corridor blocked by a slide receives, by improbable timing, a second opening before the breeding pulse.

No law is broken.  
But cascades soften.  
The difference between strain and collapse appears, quietly, in hundreds of places.

No one can prove it. Everyone notices.

---

### II. It gives Memory where memory usually dies.

On this world, data loss is extinction in slow motion.  
So the power hides durable memory in what already lasts: ceramic, basalt, plated alloys, old tunnel walls.

It teaches, through dreams, equations simple enough to carve and robust enough to survive regime change: ecosystem load ratios, shutdown taper rules, corridor minimums, cross-species dependency maps.

Children begin learning an old-new practice called **deep notation**—maintenance law engraved physically into city bone, impossible to “accidentally” delete in a software collapse. Ecology and engineering become one textbook, one apprenticeship, one oath.

When a city does fail, the next one no longer has to relearn everything from hunger.

---

### III. It gives one taboo.

Not worship.  
Not tribute.  
A prohibition:

**No system may be optimized in isolation.**

Any major improvement—faster rail, cleaner stack, tighter seal, higher yield—must be tested against the full web: floaters, burrowers, scavengers, human lungs, downstream condenser slime, predator pacing grounds, and twenty-year corrosion forecasts.

This slows progress.

It also prevents elegant mass death.

At first the taboo is resisted as superstition. Then modeled. Then codified. Then obvious. In time it becomes the civilization’s proudest export: design that includes consequences by default.

---

Centuries later, they do build shrines, but they look like service stations.

Grounded arches over static canyons.  
Public tool walls always stocked.  
Emergency nutrient vents for fauna during long shutdowns.  
Wayfinding beacons that hum at exactly the frequency sail-rakers avoid.

At each one is the same inscription, in local trade script:

**“Enough to continue.”**

That is all the wise power ever wanted to give.

Not paradise.  
Not dependence.  
Just enough continuity for a patient world to remain itself—  
and enough compassion, distributed through infrastructure,  
that nothing alive has to survive alone.
