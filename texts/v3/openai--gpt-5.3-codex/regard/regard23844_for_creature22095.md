It would **not** calm the basin.

A lesser power, eager to be praised, would have flattened it into a proper lake, pinned the rain downward, sorted sky from sea, made mirrors obey and shadows wait their turn.  
A wise one would see that this would be a kind of murder.

So the benevolent power would come quietly—at the hour when dimness turns ink-green and the wall-mats begin to glow—and it would do what only the patient do first:

It would **learn the rhythm already there**.

It would kneel with the Kneeling Tide.  
Forehead to wet stone.  
Jaw to slab.  
Hands open in spray.

And when it understood the basin’s stammering heart—its upward rain, its delayed impacts, its migrating turbulence—it would act in small, exact mercies:

---

### 1) It would add a “Mercy Interval.”

Not silence. Never silence.  
But once in each long cycle, for the span of thirty breaths, the roar would thin just enough for voices to carry two body-lengths.

Not far. Not easy. Just enough for a mother to call a child by true name without touching cloth to cloth.  
Just enough for warning.  
Just enough for song to exist as more than memory.

The people would call this interval **the Softing**.  
They would not waste it.

---

### 2) It would bind reflections to consent.

Reflections could still detach—this place is still this place—but no reflection could lead a living person across a fatal path unless invited by a deliberate sign.

If you traced two fingers across your sternum and pressed your palm to stone, you were asking for risk, for scouting, for knowledge at cost.  
If you did not, your reflection could wander, mock, dance, grieve—but not lure.

This is what wise mercy looks like:  
not removing danger, but removing betrayal.

---

### 3) It would give the basin one true constant.

In a world where floodline has no fixed meaning and even marks migrate, the power would establish a single unwavering fact:

At the ring of upright slabs, the center stone would always indicate true down.

Always.

Even when heavy rocks float.  
Even when shadows arrive early.  
Even when the ocean-roof forgets itself.

A thumb-sized certainty, and nothing more.  
Enough to rebuild orientation.  
Enough to keep madness from becoming tradition.

---

### 4) It would bless their ritual into dialogue.

They already strike waterlogged mallets against channel-stone to impose rhythm on chaos.  
The power would answer—not with words, but with resonance.

Certain patterns would now draw specific basin behaviors:  
a slowing of vertical surges, a safe thinning of mist near descent routes, a warning tremor before pressure-pops.

Not control. Conversation.

The Kneeling Tide would become not merely survivors, but **co-conductors** of their impossible ocean.

---

### 5) It would ask payment—and choose the gentlest kind.

No blood tithe. No temple. No conquest.

Only this:  
“Remember each other accurately.”

Because in that basin, names split, marks wander, and time misfiles objects. The deepest threat is not death—it is misremembering the living.

So the people would keep two-name ledgers in knotted rope and carved shell: called-name, shadow-name, kin-pressure, last-safe-height, favored rhythm.  
When someone died, they were anchored, sung in the Softing, and entered into the ledger so no reflection could steal their place.

Memory as seawall.

---

And what of the basin itself—the high bowl that thinks it is an ocean?

The wise power would bless it, too, but not by finishing it.  
By permitting it to remain unfinished without devouring those who love it.

It would leave the sharp young rock sharp.  
Leave the warm breathing air warm.  
Leave the creature-not-creatures flashing through suspended water.  
Leave the grandeur and terror intact.

Only now, within all that violence, there would be threads of covenant.

A place can stay wild and still be held.  
A people can stay adaptive and still be protected.  
An ocean can rehearse impossible shapes and still learn not to break the ones who keep time for it.
