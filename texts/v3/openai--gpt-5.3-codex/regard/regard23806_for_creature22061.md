It would **not** part the water.

It would not crack the terraces and summon an old riverbed, would not drape the gantries in instant forests, would not punish the machine for being a machine.  
A benevolent and wise power, seeing clearly, would understand the first sacred fact:

This place is already alive.  
Not despite what it is. Because of what it is.

So it would begin with restraint.

---

### First: it would listen for one full cycle.

Not a day. A full operational cycle: gates open and close, purge runs, scrape crews pass, warm discharge blooms, cold sumps refill, juvenile fish attempt crossings, predators take their tax, Rivetbacks drum at lock throats.

It would listen to steel conduction and shrimp crackle.  
To alarm buzzers and eel-slip in conduit shade.  
To the low struck-bell calls of seals who came too far in.

And to the Rivetback—the heavy, patient pulse that says:

> Here is the safe lane.  
> Here is the fatal shear.  
> Here is the timetable of survival.

Only after listening would it act.

---

### Second: it would make the least intervention that saves the most lives.

Not miracles. **Margins.**

At certain crossings where juveniles are lost, it would alter flow by a hair: a softened eddy behind a pylon, a pressure shadow that holds for six breaths longer, a current seam shifted one meter off a killing intake line.

To workers, this would look like “oddly improved hydraulics.”  
To biologists, “unexpected recruitment stability.”  
To Rivetbacks, it would feel like the world had become slightly more legible.

Wise mercy is often geometric.

---

### Third: it would tune the city’s bones for communication.

The Rivetbacks already use resonance. The power would honor that language.

It would make select members sing cleaner—gantry legs, old cable trunks, drydock ribs—so drummed pulses travel farther with less distortion. It would dampen false echoes at choke points where confusion costs bodies. It would turn the infrastructure into a better instrument for the citizens who already play it.

Not speech imposed from above.  
A grammar repair in a language already spoken.

Soon, juveniles would follow routes with fewer mistakes.  
Adults would queue with less panic at gate shifts.  
Humans would begin to notice that “fish behavior” now predicts turbulence events better than some sensors.

---

### Fourth: it would visit the human mind without spectacle.

No burning sky. No voice from a cloud.

Instead: recurring dreams among pump techs, dive planners, lock operators, maintenance schedulers. Different people, same intuitions:

- stagger scrape schedules by district, not all at once.
- leave micro-refuge strips on some cleaned grilles.
- avoid opening certain gates at exact juvenile transit windows.
- designate “quiet members” where resonance signals pass unobstructed.
- publish flow maps that include fauna lanes, not just vessel routes.

Each person wakes thinking, *I should suggest this at briefing.*  
No one feels commanded.  
Everyone feels responsible.

This is how a wise power keeps human dignity intact: by making stewardship feel like discovery, not obedience.

---

### Fifth: it would give the place one gift for the long future.

Somewhere between warm outfall and cold culvert, in a district not yet claimed by either industry or reef, it would encourage a new substrate chemistry—nothing dramatic, just enough roughness, enough mineral friendliness, enough anchoring pattern that holdfasts take, then mats, then layered invertebrate gardens that can survive purge cycles.

A nursery not outside the engine, but inside it.  
A third climate zone born from both design and accident.

In fifty years, people would call it naturally established.  
The wise power would not correct them.

---

And the Rivetback?

It would not be crowned, not made symbol, not burdened with mythic duties it did not ask for.  
It would simply be allowed to become more fully what it already is:

a reader of force,  
a keeper of routes,  
an old armored intelligence moving through made water as if it were ancestral current.

Its circuits would lengthen.  
Its young would survive at better rates.  
Its drumming would become less warning, more coordination—less “danger here,” more “pass here.”

---

If you ask what a benevolent and wise power would do to a flooded manufacturing plain and the being that belongs there, the answer is:

It would **bless without erasing**.  
It would **improve legibility**.  
It would **reduce needless death**.  
It would **invite cooperation** between machine, animal, and human schedule.  
It would leave behind no monument except a place that works better for everyone inside it.

And if you needed a sign that it had ever been there at all, it might be this:

At a lock throat in dim green noon, before a cycle shift, you feel the steel ring in your teeth—three low knocks, answered from far off by two—then see a line of Rivetbacks take the seam exactly, clean and unhurried, as the water tilts and the whole country-sized engine makes room.
