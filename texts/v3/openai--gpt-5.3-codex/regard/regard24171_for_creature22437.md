It would begin by **not rescuing them**.

A benevolent, wise power—truly wise—would understand that this city is not failing. It is doing exactly what it was made to do: endure, adapt, thicken, continue. So the power would refuse the easy miracles: no sudden rivers, no imported forests, no softening of stone into something sentimental. Gifts that erase a people’s hard-won intelligence are not gifts.

It would watch first.  
A full cycle of heat and storm, condensation and loss, expansion and repair. It would learn the grammar of fins, gutters, shadows, and rights. It would learn who is ignored, who is overburdened, and where one cracked valve could become a famine.

Then, quietly, it would act in ways the city could keep.

---

### 1) It would make warning kinder than disaster

Not by controlling weather, but by sharpening foreknowledge.

A pressure shift would sing faintly in certain rails twelve hours before a storm.  
A coming geothermal surge would show as a blue halo in inspection glass.  
Condensation failure would appear as a predictable dulling on specific tower skins.

Nothing mystical that only priests can read—just signals embedded in existing infrastructure, legible to workers, children, and elders alike.

---

### 2) It would widen justice in the language they already trust

The cruelest scarcity there is not only water; it is timing.

So the power would inspire (through dreams, insight, or one impossible equation found scratched in cooling slag) a new civic instrument: a **public shadow and moisture ledger** no district can falsify. Real-time, shared, undeniable.

Condense-right, shade-right, waste-heat-right would remain—but less hoardable, less capturable by old monopolies.  
Not revolution by fire. Correction by clarity.

---

### 3) It would seed life that belongs there

Not trees. Not imported nostalgia.

It would nudge existing biofilms and duct-lace colonies toward symbioses that improve nutrition, scrub toxins, and stabilize channels after rain bursts. Life that thrives in mineral heat, not despite it.

Within a generation, markets would taste different.  
Within two, deficiency diseases would be memory.

---

### 4) It would protect unfinishedness

This is the strangest mercy: it would prevent the city from becoming “complete.”

Because completion, here, would mean brittleness—centralized systems too elegant to repair, dependencies too delicate for real weather. The power would favor modular over monumental, redundant over pristine, adaptable over optimized.

It would bless bolts more than statues.

---

### 5) It would reveal the “being” in the place

Eventually, someone would prove what children already suspected: the city itself has a metabolism.  
Not a mind like a person, but a patterned responsiveness—a distributed cognition in heat gradients, valve timings, maintenance habits, and human ritual.

The wise power would not crown this as a god.  
It would teach them to treat it as kin: neither master nor tool, but co-inhabitant.

---

And if it ever spoke plainly, it would say something like:

> I will not give you a world that makes you unnecessary.  
> I will give you one more margin, one earlier warning, one fairer measure.  
> Keep building your beginning.

So what would it do?

Not save them from who they are.  
It would help them become **more fully it**, with fewer needless deaths along the way.
