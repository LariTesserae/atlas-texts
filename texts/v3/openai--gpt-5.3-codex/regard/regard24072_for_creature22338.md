It would begin with respect.

Not pity.  
Not conquest.  
Not the old mistake of calling a harsh place “broken” and trying to fix what is, in fact, exquisitely tuned.

A wise benevolent power, seeing this coast and its Tide Listeners, would understand quickly: this is not a world that needs saving from itself. It is a world that needs *time, warning, and mercy at the margins*.

So first, it would do nothing visible.

It would listen through one full turning of pulses—Spawn Drift, Fat Fog, Lean Noise, Hard Clear—learning which cliff-note means collapse, which sudden hush means death moving, which camps share and which hoard, which elders read stone truly and which pretend. It would not mistake loudness for chaos. It would hear the grammar.

Then, only then, it would act.

---

### What it would *not* do

- It would not raise mountains.
- It would not calm all storms.
- It would not lock channels in place.
- It would not make the people independent of tide.

Because each of those gifts is also a theft.  
Take away uncertainty, and you unmake the culture that learned to read it.

---

### What it would do instead

It would give **small certainties** in a world of necessary change.

#### 1) Three Warning Stones
On three dangerous reaches of coast, dark pillars of basalt would slowly surface over years, as if always there. When pressure drops toward killer surges, they would begin to sing—subsonic at first, then audible through the normal roar. Not alarm bells, not commands. Just a clear, unmistakable: *move now.*

No one owns them. Everyone can hear them.

#### 2) Breath-High Shelves
Not walls. Not fortresses.  
A handful of new ledges, no bigger than camps already use, just high enough to survive the worst king tides. Places for infants, elders, tools, and bone-archives when the water comes wrong. Refuge, not empire.

#### 3) Memory Clay
In select basins, the pale clay would hold marks longer: tracks, current scores, vent-spatter patterns. Stone Readers could return days later and still read yesterday’s argument between water and ground. Better forecasting. Fewer heroic deaths.

#### 4) A Safe Silence
Very rarely—once every many cycles—the coast would fall silent for a short, impossible span, but with no predator in it, no pressure drop, no hidden strike. A silence that means: *rest*. Sleep without one ear open. Grieve properly. Birth without panic.

A people taught that silence means danger would be given, carefully, a second meaning: peace.

---

### The covenant it would require

Not worship.  
Not temples.

Only this:

- Keep sharing food pulses.
- Keep weirs repair-based, not blood-based.
- Keep height a trust, not a throne.
- Keep teaching children to read water before they read story.
- Keep the dead above flood and the living below pride.

If they broke this, the gifts would not become curses—they would simply fade. The warning stones would go dumb. The memory clay would blur. Mercy would withdraw without vengeance.

---

And sometimes, in the worst once-in-a-generation storm, some would swear they saw it offshore: a vast low shape in the black water, broad-backed, taking the first force of the surge before it reached the channels. No face. No voice. Just presence between destruction and the small ring-walls.

By morning, gone.

The wise power would never make them less themselves.  
It would make survival a little less expensive.  
It would leave the authorship of their life where it belongs:

with tide, with stone, with listening.
