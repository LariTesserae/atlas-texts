The benevolent power would **not** make the basin easy.

That would be the first mercy.

It would stand on the rim through three weather-turns—rain to mist to hard blue light—and learn what already lives there: not an individual creature alone, but a woven one, a many-bodied intelligence made of roots, hooves, eggs, wings, hunger, and timing. The being in that place is Concentration itself: a mind distributed across channels and habits, alert as a flock and patient as peat.

A wise power would recognize that and refuse to “improve” it into stillness.

Instead, it would do small, exact things.

---

It would place a **warning in the throat of the cleft**.

Not a wall, not a gate. A sound: when floodwater is building beyond sight, the stone at the outlet would begin to sing—low at first, then unmistakable. People mending traps would hear it. Ungulates on the flats would lift their heads. Birds would break from feeding and take air. Not perfect safety; just a little more time, and time at altitude is often the same thing.

---

It would teach the basin to keep a few more of its young.

A storm that once stripped every egg from the same exposed margin would now leave one protected shelf wet but not scoured. A peat mat that always tore loose would catch on a hidden tooth of stone and hold. A late frost would spare one warm seep where frogs can restart. Not abundance everywhere—just **survivable variance**. Enough loss to keep everything sharp, enough refuge to prevent silence.

---

It would bless movement, not possession.

The seasonal people would begin to dream the same dream before climbing: which channels will braid cleanly this year, where the peat is hollow, when to leave before the pass drowns. At the antler-shrine, someone would add new carvings—not commandments, but remembered patterns. Take from three pools, rest two. Cut willow only above the second node. Never set all traps in one throat. They would call this tradition. The wise power would call it continuity.

---

For predators, it would grant fairness.

No prey made docile. No hunter made infallible. Instead, wind would turn honestly; scent would travel true; ambush and escape would remain a contest, not a sentence. In a bounded bowl, justice is often just **room for chance**.

---

And for the being itself—the concentrated life of the marsh—it would give one deep gift:

**memory that can cross bodies.**

When a channel shifts, sedge roots “remember” where to stitch next.  
When one ungulate line is lost, another rediscovers the old safe traverse.  
When a migratory flock misses one year, the basin still holds the timing for their return.  
When humans stay away for a hard season, the walkways rot in useful directions, showing where not to step.

Not immortality. Not immunity.  
Just the ability to recompose after being broken.

---

If this power were truly wise, it would also set a limit on itself:

No cities in the bowl.  
No permanent road in.  
No miracle that removes fear.

Because fear, here, is information. Exposure is truth. Water is law.

So the power would leave the basin as it found it—harsh, loud, crowded, alive—only now with a little more warning, a little more remembering, and a little less pointless dying.

A soft center, still inside hard stone.  
A place where life is not protected from change, but protected **through** it.
