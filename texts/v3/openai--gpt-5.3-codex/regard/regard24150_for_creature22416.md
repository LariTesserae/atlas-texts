The wise power would **not** arrive like rescue.

No sun-lance through cloud, no command, no cleansing fire.  
It would come the way this place can bear being touched: as a pressure change, a new interval in the dripping, a patience that does not insist.

It would spend a long while doing only this:

- standing where echoes still return,  
- listening to siphon-murmur inside the hydraulic persons,  
- learning the city’s grammar of pauses.

Because this is not a broken place.  
It is a place that survives by continuous negotiation.  
A benevolent mind would recognize that immediately.

So what would it do?

### First, it would ask permission in the local language.

Not with words. With timing.

One rain pulse arrives and does not overflow where it should.  
A second pulse fills a dry fountain in a district-copy that is usually barren.  
Then, under an arcade, seven drips strike stone in perfect measure and hold the beat.

That is the question: **May I help without unmaking you?**

The answer comes back from three cistern throats, staggered:  
**Help us remember which hour we are in.**

---

### So the power gives the city a gentle anchor, not a cage.

It places no walls around reality-flex.  
It does not “fix” the drifting districts.

Instead, it seeds certain high points—tower crowns, causeway knuckles, aqueduct ribs—with resonance. Not bright light, not machinery: just tones, barely audible, each one unique. When distance goes soft, the tones remain true.

Now a custodian can tap a wall, hear the answering tone beneath the echo, and know:

- this path is the present enough for crossing,  
- this stair belongs to *this* weather,  
- this plaza is a twin, not the one you left.

The city still shifts.  
But it no longer loses itself as often.

---

### Then the power gives mercy to the inhabitants themselves.

Before, when a hydraulic person slowed, it hardened into accidental monument and waited to be weathered apart. Useful, yes—yet lonely, wordless, indefinite.

The benevolent one teaches them a voluntary unmaking:  
a way to loosen seams, release warm brine, and return their mineral body to channels at a chosen time and place. A death rite, if you like—except nothing is wasted, and nothing is trapped.

Now elders can conclude themselves beside overflow courts, where new custodians tend to nucleate after storms.  
Continuity gains tenderness.

---

### Finally, and only with consent, it introduces one small form of ordinary life.

Not predators. Not swarms.  
A single species of blind, translucent filter-mite in the cisterns—minute enough to be nearly rumor. They feed on mineral film and dead bio-trace from stormfall, and in feeding they clear certain channels. Their presence changes nothing dramatic; it simply adds one soft rung to an empty ladder.

The city does not become green, or bright, or safe.  
It becomes slightly less alone.

The custodians notice first: cleaner siphon throats, subtler pressure gradients, fewer sudden fouls after heavy rain. They mark gratitude in verdigris crescents along drain mouths.

---

And the wise power?

It does what the wisest powers do after helping well:  
it withdraws from the center.

Not gone—never quite.  
Present at thresholds, at the edge of hearing, in the moment when no echo should return but one does.

If travelers come, they may find guidance now:

- three hollow taps for stable ground,  
- a low tone in the ribs of stone for present-time passage,  
- and, in some courtyards, a still basin that reflects not where you are, but where you can safely arrive.

The place remains itself: wet, dark, heat-held, mineral, patient.  
The beings remain themselves: arranged, adaptive, half-architectural, half-alive.

Nothing is cured.  
Everything is more possible.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do here.
