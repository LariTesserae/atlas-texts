It would not descend in fire.

A wise power, seeing that basin, would understand immediately that anything sudden would be a kind of violence there.  
So it would come the way water comes in that country: through seams.

First, it would **listen**.

Not for an hour, not for a season—for long enough to learn the grammar of drip intervals, collapse booms, vent whistles, hand-tapped warnings, nursery hums. Long enough to hear the difference between fear and routine in the ring-tones. Long enough to understand that the people already had a science, an ethics, and a music, all braided together.

Only then would it act.

And it would act in ways that did not make the people smaller.

---

### 1) It would make catastrophe less random, not remove danger entirely.

The basin lives by thresholds. Too much safety would make them deaf; too much hazard would erase them.  
So the power would choose the middle work: gently relieving the worst hidden pressures before they become village-swallowing collapses.

Deep below, where chambers overfill with trapped air and sudden surge, it would open hairline vents in stone—tiny, many, almost invisible. The great booms would become fewer. The deadly sinks would still exist, but less often without warning. Ground would still shift, but with more prelude: a hiss, a lower note in the rings, time enough to move the young.

A benevolent power does not promise “nothing bad will happen.”  
It gives people a fair chance to hear the bad coming.

---

### 2) It would clarify signals.

Their whole civilization is built on reading a noisy world.  
So the power’s most generous gift would be **better legibility**.

On certain mornings, thin mineral films would form on key stones—faint branching patterns that track overnight moisture migration. By midday they fade. No oracle, no commandment: just data, visible now to eyes as well as hearing plates.

Some ring structures would begin to hold a steady reference tone regardless of weather—a baseline note. Against that constant, subtle shifts elsewhere become easier to detect. Apprentices would learn faster. Elders would spend less life proving what they already know.

The power would not replace their knowledge.  
It would reduce the cost of transmitting it.

---

### 3) It would answer maintenance with abundance.

This people survives by endless repair. That is noble, but exhausting.  
A wise power would reward stewardship directly.

Where walls are restacked year after year to shelter sulfur gardens, mats would grow denser and recover faster after harvest. Fungal threads in nursery cracks would fruit a little more reliably. Not feasts, not miracles—just fewer lean intervals, fewer infants lost in bad years.

In other words: the power would make care pay dividends.

---

### 4) It would establish consent before revelation.

Eventually, the people would notice: fewer sudden deaths, clearer signs, kinder yields. They would gather in rings and ask what presence had entered their basin.

The power would not demand worship. It would offer a choice.

In dreams—if “dream” is the right word for a species that sleeps to dripping percussion—it would present three tones:

- one for **Leave us**  
- one for **Stay hidden, help only through the land**  
- one for **Teach us directly**

And it would abide by the majority pattern, renewed each generation.

Because benevolence without consent is only polished domination.

---

### 5) If invited to teach, it would teach humility at larger scales.

If they chose direct teaching, it would not begin with stars or empires.  
It would begin with watershed, chemistry, and time.

It would show them that their basin is young, yes—but not alone. That other low places are waiting for listeners. That their ringcraft can travel. That “pre-meadow” does not mean “failed world,” only “early chapter.”

It would teach them to propagate their condensers, to seed microbial succession in newly exposed flats, to midwife landscapes rather than merely endure them. Not conquerors—**founders of listening settlements**.

Their first expeditions beyond the basin would carry no banners.  
Only stones tuned to home frequencies.

---

And if they chose “Leave us”?

Then the wise power would leave, almost entirely.

One last act, perhaps: a deep, durable harmony laid into bedrock, too low to hear directly, but always there—a planetary heartbeat that keeps thresholds from tipping too far too fast.

A promise not of protection, but of accompaniment.

---

So what would a benevolent, wise power do here?

It would kneel.

It would listen before speaking, assist without erasing, teach without enthroning itself, and measure success not by monuments but by whether the people remain themselves—still low to the ground, still fluent in wet stone, still speaking inside their loud unfinished world.
