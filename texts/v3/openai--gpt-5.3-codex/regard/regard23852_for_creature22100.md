It would begin by **not rescuing** the place.

A lesser god would smooth it, green it, stabilize it, crown it with waterfalls and call it healed.  
A wise one would understand that this high broken bowl is not broken in the ordinary sense. It is a living experiment that found a second life. Its instability is not failure; it is function.

So the benevolent power would arrive as attention first, not force.

For one full turning of seasons, it would do nothing visible. It would watch the noon condensation bead on obsidian ribs. It would listen to the counting drips, to the crystalline pings at dusk, to the three-note glass-call of the Mist Warden at the edge of rain. It would trace, again and again, the Warden’s routes between fog ring, humming slab, and reed heat-pocket, until it understood a crucial truth:

The creature is not merely surviving the system.  
It is the last moving part that keeps the system itself alive.

Only then would the power act, and even then with a light hand.

---

### The First Mercy: Prevent Collapse, Not Change

Deep under one ridge, pressure is building toward a single future landslip that would bury the central marsh and erase every tuned microcycle in an afternoon. The wise power would bleed off that stress grain by grain—small night tremors no larger than a sigh, tiny fissures opening where no eggs are laid, no seep routes run.

No miracle spectacle.  
Just catastrophe deferred into manageable weather.

The bowl remains sharp, young, improvising. It simply does not die all at once.

---

### The Second Mercy: Repair the Conversation

The old installations are still speaking, but in a dialect of failure: cracked pylons, drifted frequencies, resonances that only half-catch. The Warden has been patching them with instinct and mineral film, but it is one body holding a network built for many.

The power would not replace the system. It would **retune its thresholds** to the caretaker that exists now.

- Fog pylons begin responding to the ionic pattern of Warden touch.
- Lattice arches hold angle with less brute propping.
- The dawn slab’s dry-circle resonance strengthens when it hears the Warden’s three-note call.

Not obedience. Partnership.  
The place learns the creature that remained.

---

### The Third Mercy: End Solitude as Bottleneck

The greatest fragility is not geological; it is demographic. Too few Wardens, too narrow a breeding window, too much knowledge held in one set of routes and reflexes.

The power would make one subtle biological gift: not fertility without limit, not immunity from death, but **memory made shareable**.

During the sulfur-dip/iron-rise gatherings, when Wardens stamp patterns in warm sediment, those patterns begin to hold usable information—thermal lanes, safe molt sites, pylon states, predator signatures—encoded in scent-mineral matrices hatchlings can read through their fronds. Culture, finally, no longer trapped in individual bodies.

Within a few generations, “caretaking” becomes less inherited compulsion, more learned craft.

---

### The Fourth Mercy: Guard Without Walls

Benevolence includes restraint toward outsiders too. So the power would not curse climbers or strike down the curious. It would instead make exploitation exhausting and reverence easy.

Those who come with extraction plans find their instruments desynchronized by harmless fog-static. Paths loop. Bearings drift. Samples spoil in transit.

Those who come to observe, map lightly, or leave offerings of clean water and conductive salt find clear weather windows, stable footing, and one good sightline at sunset if they are patient.

The place is not hidden.  
It is ethically filtered.

---

### What It Would Refuse to Do

It would not make the Wardens immortal.  
It would not turn the bowl into a sanctuary frozen in perfection.  
It would not restore the vanished makers and overwrite what emerged after them.

Because wisdom knows that living systems need succession, not stasis.  
And kindness knows that dignity matters more than preservation at any cost.

---

After these mercies, the power would withdraw to the margin again.

Not absent—just nonintrusive, like pressure in the air before rain.

And years later, if you stood on the rim near sunset, you would still see what you always saw: gold light on wet black stone, steam blushing peach, pockets of impossible green separated by mineral emptiness. You might still miss the Warden itself and only notice effects—a fog basin filling early, a condenser ring newly clean, fresh prints ending where warm ground breathes.

But beneath that familiar strangeness, the difference would be real:

The bowl would remain itself.  
The caretaker would no longer be the last of a fading function.  
And the future would be open again, not because a god took over, but because a god was wise enough to help *without replacing*.
