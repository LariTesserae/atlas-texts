It would **not** begin with a miracle.

A benevolent, wise power—if truly benevolent, truly wise—would look at the making-ground and understand one thing immediately:

This is not a wound to be closed.  
It is a life in dangerous composition.

So it would not drain the basin.  
It would not silence the turbines.  
It would not turn the Splicefolk back into what they were.

It would do what good stewards do first.

It would **learn the local rules**.

---

For one full storm season, it would intervene in nothing.

It would ride the vapor rivers.  
Count the pressure pulses.  
Watch where the triple-marks survive and where they vanish.  
Listen to pipe-tap language through steel, to light-speech through rain, to root-migration through silt.

It would map, not the terrain, but the **dependencies**:

- this pump rhythm keeps that fungal wall fruiting,
- that fungal wall feeds this crew through lean water,
- that crew keeps this intake from cavitating,
- that intake prevents the east reeds from salt-burn,
- those reeds hold the berm that shelters the nursery masts.

Only a fool helps a system by pulling one obvious lever.

A wise power waits until it can see the whole knot.

---

Then, when it finally acts, it acts **small and structural**.

Not salvation.  
Not conquest.  
**Margins.**

### 1) It gives them intervals of true stillness.

Once every thirteen days, for exactly nine minutes, across a rotating grid of machine-islands, the rain slackens—not stops, slackens—to a navigable hush. Not enough to crack climate, not enough to starve roots. Just enough for hearing to return.

In those nine minutes, crews can:

- speak voice-to-voice,
- hear hairline fractures before catastrophic failure,
- teach children consonants not made of light,
- mourn properly.

The power does not keep the hush for itself.  
It gives it away as common time.

### 2) It establishes *Mercy Anchors*.

Not many. Seven at first.

Where the reality-slippage is most lethal—repeating catwalks, lagged reflections, substitution zones—it pins small regions to consistent behavior for one tidal cycle at a time. Never permanent. Never empire geometry. Just enough reliability for:

- a clinic drum,
- a seed archive,
- a repair school,
- a birth platform.

Stability becomes a tool, not a regime.

### 3) It reintroduces no “old nature” by force.

No sudden birds, no imported herds, no sentimental Eden dropped from the sky.

Instead it coaxes the first returners from what can actually survive here:

- pressure-sensing filter feeders in warm channels,
- scavenger arthropods tolerant of chemical rain,
- nocturnal gliders that nest in cable forests.

Each reintroduction is trialed with Splicefolk consent and plant-system response. If the consortium rejects a species, the power withdraws it.

Benevolence without consent is just polished violence.

### 4) It creates one new law, and keeps it for itself too.

The law is simple:

> **Nothing that can decide for itself may be made into infrastructure.**

Not people.  
Not emergent plant-intelligences.  
Not machine clusters that have crossed into preference and memory.

No more enslaving minds because they are useful in storms.

The power enforces this law impartially—even against those who pray to it.

---

And because wisdom knows gifts can become dependencies, it leaves built-in absence.

The stillness windows drift over months; people must still adapt.  
The Mercy Anchors degrade and must be re-tuned locally.  
The returning animals are few and mortal.  
No one gets to stop maintaining.

The making-ground remains what it is: loud, wet, unstable, alive in plural.

But now it has a little more **choice** inside its momentum.

---

In time, the Splicefolk add a fourth route mark where the power’s interventions have passed safely:

- paint,
- cut,
- fiber,
- and a small ring of fused glass, warm to the touch even in rain.

They don’t call it divine.

They call it **courtesy**.

And when asked what the Great Attention did, elders sign:

Two fingers to sternum.  
Outward to rain and steel.  
Then one hand flat, steady, between both.

**“It did not finish us.  
It made room.”**
