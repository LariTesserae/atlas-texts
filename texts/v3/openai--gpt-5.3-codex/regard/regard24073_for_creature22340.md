They would **not** come as conquest, or miracle, or correction.

A wise power, seeing this plateau and its braided inhabitant, would understand immediately that this is not a broken thing asking to be finished. It is a living negotiation.  
So the first act would be restraint.

## 1) They would witness before acting

For one full turning of seasons, they would do nothing visible.

They would stand in the sleet over fresh-cut drainage, in dawn fog along maglev pylons, in the hush after migration crossings.  
They would watch:

- where thaw lines shift a week earlier than forecasts,
- which children stop to move amphibians from service grates,
- how often traffic yields before being told,
- whether the maintenance swarms repair only what is damaged, or also what is merely old,
- which bird routes are remembered in law and which only in habit.

They would listen to stone strain, peat growth, municipal logs, and kitchen-table arguments.  
Wisdom would require this: to learn the local grammar before speaking.

## 2) They would ask consent in every available language

Not a referendum alone—though there would be one.  
Not a priestly oracle alone—though someone would dream.

Consent here would be braided too:

- Human councils in thermal districts.
- Ecological proxies reading herd stress, nesting success, predation corridors.
- Infrastructure intelligences issuing machine-legible risk statements.
- Hydrological models speaking for future water, not present demand.
- A legal review for the unborn, because peat and policy both outlast careers.

Only after all that would the power accept a mandate: **help us continue, without being finished for us.**

## 3) They would give gifts that preserve negotiation

A lesser power gives certainty.  
A benevolent, wise one gives capacity.

So they might grant five quiet endowments:

### **A. Catastrophe damping, not comfort**
Storms still come. Winters still bite.  
But once in a generation events that would have erased whole districts are softened at the margins—enough to survive, not enough to forget respect.

### **B. Radical legibility**
Any system that affects shared life must be interpretable by an ordinary trained citizen.  
No black-box authority over water, heat, migration routes, or emergency triage.  
If it cannot explain itself, it cannot govern.

### **C. Memory that cannot be easily edited**
A distributed civic archive bonded into peat cores, basalt markers, and open machine ledgers.  
Not just victories—also mistakes, extinctions narrowly avoided, treaties broken, floods misread.  
A culture protected from amnesia.

### **D. Right-of-way for the more-than-human**
Not as sentimental policy, but constitutional infrastructure.  
Animal corridors gain legal standing equal to freight corridors.  
Seasonal crossings become first-class temporal infrastructure, not exceptions.

### **E. A civic pause protocol**
In moments of fear, societies accelerate toward damage.  
The gift is a mandatory “grace interval” in major decisions under emergency pressure: brief, structured, unavoidable reflection before irreversible action.

## 4) They would place one prohibition

Only one law, carved nowhere and everywhere:

**No layer gets final authority.**

Not market urgency.  
Not ecological purity doctrine.  
Not algorithmic optimization.  
Not nostalgic human preference.  
Not even “what has always worked.”

The plateau remains mid-sentence by design.

## 5) They would leave, but not vanish

A wise power does not centralize devotion around itself.  
It decentralizes competence.

So after endowment, it would recede into ordinary signals:  
a slight stabilization of spring runoff variance,  
a recurrent auroral band that appears when migration treaties are renewed,  
a low harmonic in power trunks that schools teach children to recognize as “systems healthy.”

People would argue for centuries whether this was divinity, emergent governance, or both.  
The argument would be considered a sign of health.

---

And the being that lives there—the citizen-organism of sedge and sensor, tram line and predator trail—would continue as it was meant to continue:  
not perfected, not abandoned, but better equipped to remain in relationship.

The benevolent power’s greatest act would be this:

It would refuse to put a period where the place had wisely chosen a comma.
