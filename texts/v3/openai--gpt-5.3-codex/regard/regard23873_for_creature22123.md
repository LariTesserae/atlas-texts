It did **not** arrive as thunder.

A wise power, seeing this watershed-city and the people who keep it alive, understood at once that spectacle would be violence here.  
A miracle that ignored gradients would be a flood in ceremonial clothing.

So it watched first.

It watched freeze-thaw mornings.  
It watched children with silt to the elbows.  
It watched elders kneel to put a palm on stone and listen for hollowness.  
It watched the way gratitude here was expressed by fixing what would otherwise fail.

And after a long while, it chose to act in the same language the city already spoke:  
**small corrections, repeated faithfully.**

---

### What it would not do

It would not stop the rain.  
It would not raise shining towers.  
It would not make the people “modern” by replacing memory with mechanism.  
It would not end labor, because here labor is relationship, and relationship is survival.

It would not take the city away from itself.

---

### What it did

It made disasters fractionally less absolute.

Not by canceling weather, but by teaching weather to hesitate:  
cloudbanks that might have broken all at once began to arrive in pulses;  
ice that might have split a hundred joints in one dawn released in staggered hours;  
wind shifted half a valley-width at the moment a scaffold would have gone over.

No one could prove intervention.  
They only said, over seasons,  
“Something has become kinder at the edges.”

---

It placed **warnings inside ordinary perception**.

The ear stones in walls began to carry more information.  
A blocked line gave not just silence but a faint, low tremor, like a throat clearing.  
A retaining wall nearing failure clicked once at midnight, sharp enough to wake the nearest sleeper.  
Children, who pressed their cheeks to stone for play, started finding problems before patrols did.

The people called these gifts “early sounds.”

---

It blessed **repair over heroics**.

In many places, powers reward grand acts.  
Here, it rewarded the person who replaced one cracked spillway lip before sleep.  
The one who left a vent gap instead of sealing neatly.  
The one who admitted, in council, “I do not understand this new seep line.”

Those who sought glory found their work clumsy.  
Those who worked for continuity found their hands steady, their knots true, their judgment calm in rain.

---

It widened compassion where hierarchy could have hardened.

Because upper and lower districts always risk resentment, the power nudged empathy into custom:  
during lean years, disputes de-escalated one sentence earlier than expected;  
rotations became slightly fairer;  
the tired were noticed sooner.  
No decree fell from heaven.  
Just this: fewer people were left alone with impossible sections of wall.

---

It gave one explicit sign, once each year.

On the first dawn after the longest aqueduct-spine was fully cleared, if the work had been done cooperatively and without concealment, a brief silver run would pass through the coldest channel—fish-pale, luminous, gone in seconds. Even in cloud, it flashed.

Not everyone saw it every year.  
But enough did.  
And its meaning was plain:  
**continuity has been renewed.**

---

### And what became of the people

They did not become devotees of a distant throne.  
They became more fully themselves.

Their shrines remained junction niches and worn thresholds.  
Their offerings remained replaced stones, cleaned grates, planted willows.  
But now, when teaching children, they added one sentence:

“Water remembers paths.  
Stone remembers pressure.  
And kindness, if repeated, becomes infrastructure.”

In time they understood the wise power had not come to rule the watershed.

It had come to join its maintenance.
