Short version: **treat this place as a wet industrial wilderness with active weather-electrical anomalies.**  
If you prepare for only “jungle” or only “ruins,” you’ll get hurt.

## 1) Mindset before gear
You are visiting a landscape that is:
- **alive everywhere** (no safe “empty ground”),
- **mechanically active** (moving belts, vents, intake currents, shift traffic),
- **electrically weird** (horizontal lightning, conductive seepage, up-rain),
- **hydrologically unstable** (flooding, peat collapse, hidden chambers).

So your baseline rule is: **nothing inert is truly inert**—not water, not metal, not mud, not “quiet.”

---

## 2) Non-negotiable gear (minimum loadout)

### Clothing
- Full waterproof shell (jacket + pants), but highly breathable.
- Quick-dry base layers; no cotton.
- Insulating midlayer that stays warm when damp.
- Cut-resistant, puncture-resistant gloves.
- Knee-high waterproof boots with aggressive tread + ankle support.
- Gaiters (mud, leeches, spore mat contact).
- Spare socks in dry bags (many pairs).

### Electrical/weather safety
- Compact insulating ground mat.
- Small nonconductive trekking pole.
- Lightning protocol card + local storm schedule app/radio feed.
- Avoid exposed metal-frame umbrellas (seriously).

### Visibility/navigation
- Headlamp with **red mode** + hooded white beam.
- 2 backup lights.
- Updated local station maps (paper + offline digital).
- Route marking tape/tags suitable for wet metal + moss.
- Whistle and vibration beacon (audio gets lost in layered noise).

### Respiratory/biological
- Half-face respirator with swappable filters (particulate + organic vapor).
- Eye protection (mist + spores + chemical spray).
- Insect net head cover.
- Antifungal skin barrier cream/powder.

### Survival/medical
- Trauma kit (tourniquet, pressure dressings, wound irrigation).
- Broad-spectrum antimicrobials as prescribed for expedition context.
- Electrolyte salts.
- Water purification that handles **biological + chemical load** (filter + adsorptive stage).
- Emergency thermal shelter that can be rigged above standing water.

### Movement/safety
- Climbing helmet.
- Harness + dual lanyard for catwalk/lift-tower transit.
- 20–30 m static line for extraction from mud/fissure edges.

## 3) Skills you should have before arrival
At least basic competence in:
- Moving on grated catwalks in low visibility.
- Slip/fall arrest with harness.
- Reading industrial hazard markings.
- Night navigation without horizon/stars.
- Flood and peat-collapse recognition.
- Electrical storm behavior around tall wet structures.
- Quiet stepping / vibration discipline.

If you lack these, hire a local guide and stay near maintained station corridors.

---

## 4) Behavior rules on-site (these matter more than fitness)

1. **Time movement around sirens/shift cycles.**  
   Animal migrations key off them; avoid corridors during those windows.

2. **Never camp in low basins** even if wind-sheltered.  
   They flood, gas, and attract feeders/predators.

3. **Do not touch pooled water near outfalls, pumps, or cable runs.**  
   You can get chemically burned, infected, shocked, or eaten.

4. **Assume every metal surface is wet and potentially energized.**  
   One-hand contact rule; test before full weight.

5. **Travel in 2–4 person teams.**  
   Solo gets risky fast when comms fail in noise/fog.

6. **Stop and listen every 10–15 minutes.**  
   You’ll detect slope movement, belt lines, migration waves, and storm phase changes.

7. **Never follow “dry-looking” ground without probing.**  
   Moss mats bridge voids.

8. **Treat all biological contact as contaminated.**  
   Clean cuts immediately, even tiny ones.

---

## 5) Sleep/shelter strategy
- Sleep **elevated**, clipped in, under hard rain cover.
- Pick sites with two exits and no overhead cable bundles.
- Avoid warm exhaust shadows (insect swarms, carnivorous plant zones, predators).
- Keep food sealed and hung away from sleeping platform.
- Use low-spectrum shielded light; bright white floods draw attention.

---

## 6) Social/logistical prep
This is a station culture, not frontier cosplay. Bring trade-useful items:
- gasket kits, fasteners, filter media, battery cells, antifungal meds, bearing grease.
Respect maintenance lanes and shift authority. Ask before crossing operational spines.

---

## 7) Red-flag conditions: abort immediately if you notice
- Ground hum changing rapidly underfoot.
- Fog suddenly “flowing” downhill faster than wind.
- Hair/gear static rise + ridge-to-ridge flicker.
- Synchronized silence in wet basin fauna.
- Repeating metallic clicks converging from multiple directions.
- Warm water level rising around supports.

When these happen: **leave elevation-exposed metal, gain stable managed ground, and shelter in rated structures.**
