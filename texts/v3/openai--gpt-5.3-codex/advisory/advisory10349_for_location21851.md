If you’re an ordinary human traveler, prepare for this place like you’re entering a **living hydraulic machine**—not a tourist city.

## 1) Core mindset
- Assume **constant motion, moisture, and noise**.
- Don’t expect stable “streets” or static neighborhoods; functions shift with weather.
- Respect maintenance crews and routing systems as civic authority. Here, infrastructure *is* culture.

## 2) Body prep (2–4 weeks before)
- Heat/humidity conditioning: daily cardio in warm, humid environments if possible.
- Balance and stair/ramp endurance training (lots of vertical movement, slick surfaces).
- Get travel medicine consult: broad-spectrum environmental exposure plan (fungal/bacterial-heavy biome).
- If you have asthma, migraines, tinnitus, sensory overload, or vertigo, bring a tailored management kit.

## 3) Essential gear (non-optional)
- **Hearing protection system**: filtered earplugs + bone-conduction headset.
- **Eye protection**: polarized, anti-glare, hydrophobic lenses (wet reflective surfaces are brutal).
- **Clothing**: quick-dry synthetics, no cotton, no dragging hems.
- **Footwear**: non-slip, chemical/wet rated, ankle support, drain channels.
- **Shell layer**: breathable waterproof, corrosion-resistant hardware.
- **Device protection**: IP68+ everything, tethered tools, waterproof power bank.
- **Bag**: hard-seal dry pack with internal desiccants.
- **Respiratory comfort**: light anti-mist mask/filter for steam, bioaerosol-heavy zones.
- **Skin care**: anti-chafe barrier + antimicrobial wash (you’ll stay damp).

## 4) First 48 hours on-site
- Stay in a **mid-elevation district** (avoid low flood-plaza lodging and high wind decks at first).
- Hire a local mobility guide to learn:
  - lane hierarchies (pedestrian/drone/service),
  - storm tone codes,
  - hand/light signal etiquette,
  - platform vibration etiquette.
- Register for local hazard feed (flood inversions, reroutes, pressure events, field maintenance windows).

## 5) Social/etiquette survival
- Don’t shout unless it’s a safety call; use clipped speech or signal protocols.
- Never block drains, seam gutters, maintenance hatches, or marked “temporary voids.”
- Don’t treat adaptive infrastructure like a spectacle. Locals see it as routine labor.
- In crowd flow, be fluid: stop-and-stare behavior is unsafe and rude.

## 6) Weather and movement protocol
- Carry a micro “storm shift kit” on your body: seal hood, grip gloves, tether clip, emergency beacon.
- At storm alerts, expect:
  - lower routes to seal/flood,
  - markets to lift/float,
  - transit to jump strata.
- Move **up or to designated sealed corridors**, not “away across” open decks.

## 7) Bio-ecology cautions
- Don’t touch colorful reservoir bands, fungal veils, or sensor-cleaning swarms.
- Don’t feed scavenger quadrupeds or vent-stack birds.
- Eat from high-turnover kitchens first; delay raw local bio-cultured foods until acclimated.
- Drink certified potable lines only (not all clear water is drinkable, even if engineered).

## 8) Psychological prep
- This city can induce fatigue through:
  - continuous mechanical sound,
  - visual glare,
  - crowd density,
  - lack of green space.
- Schedule decompression blocks in quiet pods / acoustic shelters every day.

---

### Minimal packing checklist
Hearing kit, eye kit, grip boots, quick-dry layers, waterproof shell, sealed bag, med kit, hydration salts, anti-chafe, signal cheat-sheet, local hazard app, emergency translator/comms with haptics.

If you prepare well, this place is survivable—and fascinating. If you arrive like it’s a normal rainy city, it will overwhelm you in a day.
