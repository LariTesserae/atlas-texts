Great destination choice—this is a **technical environment**, not a scenic hike.  
Prepare like you’re entering a high-altitude, partially active industrial shaft with a fragile mineral ecosystem.

## 1) Treat the main risks correctly

**Primary hazards:**
- **Thin air / altitude stress** (hypoxia, headache, poor judgment)
- **Continuous high noise** (fatigue, confusion, hearing damage)
- **Low light + vertical confinement** (falls, disorientation)
- **Dry mineral dust + silicate fragments** (lung/eye/skin irritation)
- **Live legacy infrastructure** (electric lines, pressure systems, sudden venting)
- **Brittle, sharp “vegetation”** (glass-like cuts, punctures)

**Notable absence:** water, wildlife, obvious decomposition.  
So don’t expect normal wilderness cues.

---

## 2) Physical prep (before you jump)

- Be acclimatized for high elevation (or carry supplemental oxygen).
- Do a baseline cardio/respiratory check if possible.
- Train for **slow, careful movement in tight spaces** (sideways passage, controlled footing).
- Practice communication in loud environments (hand signals, short radio protocol).

---

## 3) Essential gear

### Personal protection
- Climbing helmet with chin strap
- Clear sealed goggles
- P100 respirator (or better), ideally with fit test
- Cut-resistant gloves (plus dexterity liner)
- Long sleeves/pants, abrasion-resistant outer layer
- Stiff, high-traction boots (preferably electrical-hazard rated)
- Hearing protection: filtered earplugs + optional muffs

### Life support & navigation
- 2–3 independent light sources (headlamp + handheld + backup)
- Extra batteries (cold-tolerant)
- Pulse oximeter
- Compact oxygen bottle if unacclimatized
- Gas monitor (O₂, CO₂, CO, VOC; bonus: ozone)
- Climbing harness, short rope, and proper anchors (don’t trust old fixtures)
- Route markers/tether line for seam exit recovery
- Reliable timer/watch with vibration alerts (you will lose time sense)

### Safety/admin
- First-aid kit focused on cuts, eye rinse, and respiratory irritation
- Insulated water supply (there is essentially none there)
- High-calorie snacks (dry, sealed)
- Two-way comms suited to high ambient noise (throat mic helps)

---

## 4) On-site behavior rules

1. **Assume every cable/pipe is live or pressurized.**  
   Don’t lean on, clip into, or step over industrial remnants casually.

2. **Move slowly and laterally.**  
   Many passages require turning sideways through rigid growth.

3. **Don’t break structures unless survival-critical.**  
   Brittle fronds can shatter into dangerous shards and dust.

4. **Limit dwell time.**  
   Continuous mechanical resonance is mentally exhausting; use hard turnaround times.

5. **Track your exit constantly.**  
   The space feels small, then suddenly vertical and deep; orientation is deceptive.

6. **No open flames or sparks.**  
   Unknown gas micro-shifts + dusty conductive environment.

---

## 5) Contamination discipline (multiverse best practice)

This ecosystem is equilibrium-based and isolated. Be strict:
- Decontaminate boots, clothing, tools on exit.
- Seal dust-exposed gear in bags until cleaned.
- Don’t transport spores/mineral filaments between worlds.
- Avoid introducing moisture, organics, or microbes unnecessarily.

---

## 6) Psychological prep

Expect the uncanny effect: a place that feels “finished,” not alive in a human sense.  
The permanent hum, fixed light, and lack of animal movement can induce:
- time distortion
- mild derealization
- elevated stress without obvious trigger

Countermeasures:
- Use check-in intervals (every 10–15 min)
- Verbal status with partner
- Simple task list to stay grounded
