This is a **“treat it like a hostile industrial wetland + physics glitch zone”** destination.

If an ordinary human wants to survive it, preparation is less about sightseeing and more about **systems discipline**.

## 1) Mindset before gear
Go in assuming all of these are true at once:

- You are always in someone’s feeding ground.
- You are always one step from hypothermia, chemical burn, or asphyxiation.
- Your senses (distance, timing, sound direction, sequence of events) are **not reliable**.
- Standing still is dangerous; moving casually is also dangerous.
- The environment is alive, cyclical, and crowded—there is almost no “empty safe spot.”

If you can’t follow strict procedures even when tired and scared, don’t go.

---

## 2) Core physical prep (what your body needs)
Train for:

- **Cold-wet endurance** (hours in soaked conditions).
- **Uneven substrate locomotion** (slick rock, mud skin, shallow water).
- **Load-bearing in unstable footing**.
- **Respiratory stress tolerance** (mask use, low-oxygen/heavy gas pockets).
- **Noise fatigue** (continuous loud low-frequency sound causes bad decisions).

Do at least 4–6 weeks of conditioning focused on ankles, knees, core, and balance.

---

## 3) Essential equipment
### Survival suit
- Fully sealed **cold-wet environmental suit** with active thermal regulation.
- Chemical-resistant outer layer (iron/salt/acidic slurry tolerant).
- Integrated flotation that does not impede crawling.
- Hard knee/shin/forearm protection (you will fall).

### Breathing & air safety
- Closed-circuit rebreather or high-grade filtered respirator + O₂ reserve.
- Multi-gas monitor (O₂, CO₂, H₂S analogs, heavy gas pooling indicators).
- Low-mounted intake warning: heavy gases settle in hollows.

### Navigation & perception correction
- Triple-redundant nav:
  1. Inertial tracker  
  2. Beacon mesh you deploy behind you  
  3. Dead-reckoning log (manual)
- Lidar/radar rangefinder (optical distance is deceptive in fog bands).
- Time-synced wrist recorder with continuous voice log (“I step now / I hear collapse now”).
- Metronome/haptic timer to detect causality lag.

### Mobility tools
- Wide-foot traction overshoes (anti-suction, anti-slip).
- Probe pole (test crust, depth, temperature before each step zone).
- Lightweight grapnel line for buddy tethering in low-visibility segments.

### Shelter
- Elevated micro-platform camp system (never camp directly on ground skin).
- Thermal cocoon + vibration-isolated sleep bag (constant tremor/noise).

### Medical
- Hypothermia kit, rewarming pads, electrolyte concentrates.
- Chemical exposure wash packs.
- Broad-spectrum wound management (wet-environment infection risk extreme).
- Anti-vertigo/anti-nausea meds (reality-skew disorientation).
- Hearing protection that still allows comms.

### Data & fail-safe
- Bodycam always on.
- Automatic emergency beacon with dead-man trigger.
- Predefined extraction windows; no improvisational “one more hour.”

## 4) Team rules (non-negotiable)
Never solo.

Use a **3-person minimum**:
1. Lead navigator  
2. Environmental monitor (gas/thermal/anomaly calls)  
3. Rear observer/logger

Protocols:
- Constant line-of-sight or tether in fog shear zones.
- Call-and-response every 30–60 seconds.
- Any member can trigger halt.
- If timeline anomalies are detected (early echo, pre-tracks, motion mismatch), switch to “slow mode”: half speed, probe every step, no overtakes.

---

## 5) Movement doctrine on-site
- Move on **elevated mineral ridges** when possible, not basin pans.
- Avoid warm seep-lines: they attract grazers, filter-beasts, and predators.
- Never cross migrating belts head-on; wait, flank, or abort.
- Treat all crust as load-bearing **until tested** (many are not).
- Do not enter hollows with visible low-lying mist unless gas-cleared first.
- If shadows decouple or scale shifts occur, stop and rely on instruments, not eyes.

---

## 6) Fauna-specific cautions
- **Hexapod herd belts**: crowd crush risk + trampling + static discharge from nearby skates.
- **Filter towers**: do not pass down-current of intake throats; suction and aerosol exposure.
- **Mist skates**: electrical arcs can jump to poles/antennae—collapse conductive profile.
- **Subsurface tube colonies**: rhythmic mud pulsing means active venting below; detour immediately.
- **Anomaly predators**: they strike where reaction timing fails. In skew zones, present fewer motion cues and back out slowly.

---

## 7) Reality-fracture protocol
When cause/effect misorders:

1. **Freeze 5 seconds** (unless immediate physical hazard).
2. Check:
   - time sync,
   - teammate confirmation,
   - instrument consistency.
3. Mark zone with beacon.
4. Retreat along confirmed path, not perceived “shortest” path.
5. Log event with exact timestamp.
6. Do not re-enter that zone during same cycle.

Rule of thumb: if an event happens “wrong” twice in 10 minutes, that area is now red.

---

## 8) Camp and rest
- Camp on raised stable stone only.
- Perimeter: thermal + motion + gas alarms.
- Rotating watch; no one sleeps through full cycle due to repeating weather loops.
- Dry layers every rest window; wet-core fatigue kills judgment quickly.
- Keep meals high-calorie, high-salt, warm fluid heavy.

---

## 9) Psychological prep
Expect:
- mechanical-noise overload,
- depth ambiguity,
- déjà vu from repeating micro-weather,
- paranoia from early echoes and delayed consequences.

Countermeasures:
- scripted checklists,
- spoken task loops,
- strict sleep schedule,
- “instrument over intuition” discipline.

---

## 10) Hard no-go indicators (abort immediately)
- Two nav systems disagree persistently.
- Repeating terrain pattern with no positional gain (looping).
- Gas monitor spikes in multiple hollows.
- Herd migration converging from more than one direction.
- Team member shows confusion about event order (“I heard it after I saw it… no before…”).
