Great description. For an **ordinary human**, this is not a sightseeing biome—it’s a **high-density, low-visibility, always-active hazard field**.

If you visit, prepare like you’re entering a living industrial zone made of mud, shells, and predators.

## 1) Minimum readiness before arrival

- **Do not go solo.** Minimum 3 people: navigator, watcher, medic.
- **Fitness:** you need steady balance and endurance in slippery terrain, low light, constant noise stress.
- **Vaccinations + prophylaxis:** anything available for marine/wetland pathogens, plus broad travel anti-infective kit.
- **Extraction plan:** fixed return time + emergency beacon + automatic retrieval trigger if comms fail.

## 2) Gear that actually matters

### Mobility & protection
- **Full-body waterproof shell suit**, abrasion-resistant (basalt + shell edges will shred normal fabric).
- **Puncture-resistant boots** with:
  - soft/damped soles (to reduce ground vibration),
  - wide footprint (to avoid sinking in mineral mud),
  - aggressive wet-rock traction.
- **Gloves** (chemical + cut resistant).
- **Helmet** (falling stone, wing strikes, collapses).

### Sensory survival
- **Layered hearing protection** (filter harsh noise but keep speech frequencies and hazard cues).
- **Low-light headlamps** (dimmable, red/amber mode) + backup lights.
- **Thermal/IR monocular** for moving bodies in mist.
- **Barometer + tide/pressure monitor** (this ecosystem pulses before weather shifts).

### Environmental safety
- **Half-face respirator** with particulate + acid-gas cartridges (mist, guano dust, sulfur edge).
- **Sealed hydration and rations** (consume nothing local).
- **Trekking poles with soft tips** (test ground quietly).
- **Compact elevated rest platform** (never sit directly on ground).

### Medical/decon
- Trauma kit, irrigation, antiseptic, broad-spectrum emergency meds.
- Chemical rinse + containment bags for contaminated gear.
- Post-visit decon protocol ready before entry.

## 3) Timing: when to move, when not to

This basin runs on **moisture and pressure cycles**.

- **Best window:** stable pressure, no incoming rain pulse, receding tide phase.
- **Worst windows (avoid):**
  - 1–3 hours before rain (pre-emergence agitation),
  - active rain (feeding/spawning frenzy),
  - immediate post-rain channel retreat (scavenger fronts),
  - rapid pressure drop (roost disturbances, mass flight).

If your barometer starts falling fast, treat that as an evacuation signal.

---

## 4) Movement rules (critical)

1. **Step light, step deliberate.** Heavy vibration attracts hunters.
2. **Never stand still long in one wet spot.** Colonizers and grazers will accumulate on you.
3. **Avoid warm seeps and fresh microbial sheen.** These are feeding nodes.
4. **Do not cross beneath dense roost shadows** during pressure changes.
5. **Keep spacing:** 3–5 meters between team members; avoid synchronized stomping.
6. **No loud impacts** (hammering, dropped metal, shouting into ground cavities).
7. **Probe before weight transfer** near undercut banks—collapse risk is real.
8. **No organic waste left behind** (you’ll summon scavenger swarms).
9. **If wing-cloud lift begins, crouch, shield face, lights down.**
10. **Abort early.** This place punishes late decisions.

---

## 5) Camp/rest protocol (if absolutely necessary)

- Don’t “camp” on substrate. Use an **elevated, isolated platform**.
- Keep rest periods short; rotate sentry watch.
- No exposed food smell.
- Keep lighting minimal and downward.
- Maintain a clean perimeter—if scraping/hissing intensity rises, relocate immediately.

## 6) Mental prep

Expect:
- Near-total horizon loss,
- constant multi-layer noise,
- poor sense of time,
- sensory fatigue and anxiety from unseen movement.

You need discipline more than bravery. The basin is not malicious; it’s just **fully occupied**. You survive by being a brief, quiet, non-disruptive event.
