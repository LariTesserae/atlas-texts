1. **Dynamic** (terrain and water patterns change daily),
2. **Instrumented** (machines are present, active, and reactive),
3. **Ecologically dense** (lots of animals, visible predators),
4. **Physically weird** (localized gravity/phase anomalies).

## 1) Physical prep (your body)

- **Heat + humidity conditioning:** You’ll be warm and damp continuously. Do 1–2 weeks of light conditioning in humid environments if possible.
- **Balance training:** Uneven slick surfaces + soft substrate + occasional anomalous pull effects.
- **Respiratory caution:** Mist-heavy air; bring inhalation protection if you have asthma/allergies.
- **Vaccinations / prophylaxis:** Wetland-style exposure assumptions (fungal, parasitic, bite-related). If in doubt: broad-spectrum travel med protocol.

---

## 2) Clothing & personal gear

Think: “high-altitude greenhouse expedition.”

### Wear
- Lightweight, **quick-wicking base layer** (never cotton).
- Thin insulating mid-layer (you can still get chilled when constantly wet).
- **Breathable waterproof shell** (not fully sealed rubber—you’ll overheat).
- Tough, high-traction **ankle boots** for slick mineral and shallow marsh.
- Gaiters (mud ingress is constant).
- Thin gloves with grip + one waterproof overglove.
- Eye protection for mist, insects, spray.
- Wide-brim cap or hood (drip fatigue is real).

### Carry
- Trekking pole (probe porous ground before stepping).
- 2–3 liters water + electrolytes (humid heat masks dehydration).
- High-calorie, waterproof-packed food.
- Compact thermal blanket + drybag with emergency dry set.
- Antimicrobial foot care kit (trench-foot prevention).
- Insect management: repellent + netting for rest periods.
- Quiet medkit: trauma supplies + antihistamine + antifungal + anti-parasitic plan.

## 3) Tech protection (very important here)

This basin condenses moisture on everything and likely has odd EM/gravity fields.

- Use **sealed, rugged electronics** only.
- Keep backups in Faraday/dry pouches.
- Prefer analog backups: compass, paper map, wax pencil, whistle.
- Use mechanical watch + redundant timing method.
- Assume drones may drift in local gradient anomalies.
- Keep metal tools tethered; “lightened” stones imply transient lift zones.

---

## 4) Navigation & movement protocol

- Travel with a **three-point rule**: one person moving, one spotting footing, one scanning fauna/machines.
- Step test every suspect patch: porous crust may “weep” upward water under load.
- Avoid vertical faces (constant seep, slough risk).
- Mark routes with temporary non-invasive flags; terrain can reorganize overnight.
- Never camp in a dry patch without checking water-cycle history; “dry now” may be “marsh in 3 hours.”

---

## 5) Interaction rules with the built elements

Treat all devices as active experimental apparatus.

- **Do not touch pylons, arcs, frames, conduits, or glowing lines.**
- Maintain stand-off distance (at least 5–10 m, more if gravity effects visible).
- If raindrops reverse, stones hover, or hair lifts unexpectedly: back away slowly.
- Don’t place gear in “quiet zones” near humming structures—could be field anchors.
- Never shelter against unknown lattice unless verified inert/safe (one pod is dry, but don’t assume all are).

## 6) Wildlife safety

This place is visibly alive and not shy about it.

- Move in groups; predators key on isolated motion.
- Maintain 360° scans in reed margins.
- Avoid dawn/dusk crossings of seep edges (high grazer/predator overlap).
- Do not stand between herds and water.
- No feeding, no mimic calls, no bright strobes.
- If basin-wide resonant tone sounds and animals freeze: **you freeze too** until activity resumes.

## 7) Timing your visit

Best approach: short observational windows synced to cycles.

- Spend first day doing **cycle mapping only**: wet-zone expansion, animal corridors, pylon tone intervals.
- Plan movements during stable mid-cycle phases.
- Exit before night unless mission requires it; night illumination is beautiful but behaviorally complex (insects, grazers, active conduits).

## 8) Camp/operations setup

If overnight:
- Use elevated platform or known dry shelter pod.
- Keep all textiles off ground (constant condensation).
- Ventilate sleep area to reduce fungal growth.
- Rotate socks/foot drying schedule every few hours.
- Establish “hard retreat line” to bowl lip; boundary reportedly dampens anomalies.

## 9) Red flags: leave immediately if…

- Sudden broad tonal resonance followed by multi-species directional movement.
- New ground fissures/steam vents appearing near route.
- Repeated gravity oddities in previously normal corridor.
- Persistent metallic sweetness intensifying (possible aerosolized compounds/field effects).
- Your instruments disagree wildly for >10 minutes.
