This is a place where **industrial safety**, **mountain safety**, and **anomaly safety** overlap.  
Prepare like you’re visiting a high-altitude shipyard built inside a mild reality storm.

## 1) Physical prep (before you arrive)

- **Altitude conditioning:** Expect thin, dry air and high UV. If possible, spend 2–4 days acclimating at intermediate elevation.
- **Cardio baseline:** You’ll be climbing stairs, catwalks, and ramps constantly.
- **Balance training:** The environment is all edges, rails, grated surfaces, and visual depth tricks. Good foot placement matters.

## 2) Core gear to bring

### Clothing & body
- Layered, wind-resistant clothing (it’s bright but not warm).
- Waterproof shell for short rain windows and constant condensation drip.
- Cut-resistant gloves (for rails, ladders, mesh edges).
- Non-slip, soft-sole boots that grip wet metal/ceramic.
- UV-protective eyewear with high glare control (ideally photochromic + anti-reflective).
- Light helmet (overhead drips, low struts, maintenance clutter).

### Safety/navigation
- **Dual navigation system:**  
  1) local dynamic route feed (if available),  
  2) inertial/step-count backup + physical notes.  
  Visual perspective can lie.
- Short tether/lanyard for exposed catwalk crossings.
- Compact headlamp with red mode (even when bright, shadows and alcoves can be deceptive).
- Chalk tape or temporary marker tabs for backtracking in repeating corridors.
- Basic air-quality and temperature probe (warm condensation = active machinery zones).
- Personal med kit + altitude meds if prescribed.

### Water/food
- Reliable personal water bottle + purification/UV cap (water is present but system-owned; don’t assume potability).
- High-calorie snacks in sealed packs; long detours happen.

## 3) Arrival behavior: first 24 hours

- **Do not free-walk on day one.** Follow marked stable paths only.
- Hire/borrow a **local guide** or maintenance escort if possible.
- Learn the day’s “stable geometry windows” and route paint conventions.
- Test your reaction to glare and depth illusions before using upper decks or ladders.
- Move slower than you think necessary. This is a place where a wrong turn is often vertical.

## 4) Rules to treat as absolute

You already got local wisdom—treat it as law:

- Don’t trust horizon lines crossing multiple tower gaps.
- Warm condensation means active process systems: avoid touching/lingering.
- If animals suddenly avoid a seam/joint, you avoid it too.
- Repeated landing number? Immediate retreat.
- Quiet zones are active; loud zones are often faults or damage.

Add your own traveler rule:  
**If geometry feels “off,” stop moving first, then reassess.** Don’t “push through.”

## 5) Wildlife and ecological etiquette

- Don’t feed anything. Residue-driven food webs are tightly tuned.
- Give vent outflow colonies and dawn-grazing ungulates wide berth.
- Avoid reaching into warm alcoves, louver shadows, or pipe bundles.
- Seal food and gels; scavengers are smart and persistent.

## 6) Weather and timing strategy

- Plan movement around:
  - condensation peaks (slippery undersides),
  - rain windows (fast runoff channels),
  - known electrical/high-output cycles (shadow lag events).
- Avoid long traverses near dusk until you understand local lighting cues; lights can erase depth perception.

## 7) Mindset

This place is not “broken”; it’s **dynamic**.  
Treat it like a living infrastructure biome:

- Trust process over intuition.
- Trust local marks over visual distance.
- Trust repeated observations over first impressions.

If you prepare well, it’s absolutely traversable for an ordinary human—just not casually.  
Go disciplined, go observant, and let the locals’ practical superstitions guide your route.
