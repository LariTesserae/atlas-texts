Great description.  
For an **ordinary human multiverse traveler**, this is not a heroic expedition site — it’s a **hot, wet, bioactive infrastructure zone**. Treat it like visiting an active stormwater/mechanical underworks + urban wetland.

## What this place *is* to your body
Expect:

- **Constant humidity and heat load** (you overheat faster than you realize)
- **Slip/trip risk everywhere** (film, silt, algae, cable clutter, uneven bedrock)
- **Biological exposure** (mosquitoes, rat contamination, roach dust, stagnant-water microbes)
- **Noise fatigue** (continuous low-frequency mechanical sound)
- **Low-contrast visibility** (everything is damp gray, depth perception gets worse)

So preparation should prioritize: **infection prevention, footing, heat management, and navigation discipline**.

---

## 1) Pre-trip preparation (do this first)

1. **Vaccinations / medical baseline**
   - Keep tetanus current.
   - Depending on world biology: ask equivalent of leptospirosis/hepatitis risk guidance.
   - If you have asthma/allergies, pre-pack meds (roach/bat/rat environments can trigger reactions).

2. **Entry permissions + route plan**
   - This is active infrastructure. Know who controls pumps/power and when maintenance cycles occur.
   - Mark at least **two exits** and one high-ground fallback.
   - Check rain forecast aboveground: this basin can rise quickly.

3. **Buddy rule**
   - Don’t go alone. If solo is unavoidable, use scheduled check-ins and a dead-man timer.

---

## 2) Clothing and gear (minimum viable kit)

### Wear
- Lightweight long sleeves/pants (quick-dry, not cotton)
- **Knee-high waterproof boots** with aggressive anti-slip soles
- Thin cut-resistant gloves (plus disposable inner gloves)
- Cap or bump hat; proper helmet if around low beams/fans
- Eye protection (drips + insect strikes)
- Insect repellent on exposed skin + clothing

### Carry
- **Headlamp + backup light** (warm humid air eats visibility; lights fail)
- Spare batteries in sealed pouch
- 1–2 liters water + electrolytes (you’ll sweat continuously)
- Small first-aid kit (bandages, antiseptic, blister care)
- Respiratory option (at least a good filter mask) for dusty niches/guano zones
- Waterproof bag for documents/comms
- Compact pole/stick for probing questionable “dry” patches
- High-calorie, non-melting snack (heat drains energy)

### Optional but smart
- Portable CO₂/O₂/H₂S multi-gas detector if unknown ventilation reliability
- Ear protection (for long stays near fans/pumps)
- Disposable overboot covers for decontamination on exit

## 3) Movement rules inside (most important)

Your own site-rules summary was excellent. I’d convert it into operating behavior:

1. **Respect the stain line**
   - If water is already near the crest marks, leave. Don’t debate it.

2. **Walk like every surface is contaminated and unstable**
   - Short steps, three points of contact when climbing.
   - Avoid moss sheen, algae streaks, and silt fans.

3. **Stay on known routes**
   - Cable tray shadows and pump alleys become animal highways.
   - Don’t block those routes longer than necessary.

4. **Treat machinery as territorial weather**
   - Fans, pumps, switchgear create “safe-ish” and “unsafe” microzones.
   - Keep distance from electrical installations in splash zones.

5. **Light discipline**
   - Bright white beams attract swarms; use lower output unless needed.
   - Pause before entering lit insect clouds (predators often nearby: bats, frogs, cats).

6. **No hand-to-face contact**
   - This one prevents most avoidable illness.

---

## 4) Fauna-specific practical advice

- **Rats:** don’t corner, don’t feed, don’t leave food exposed.  
- **Mosquitoes:** your biggest guaranteed annoyance; cover skin first, repellent second.  
- **Roaches/crickets:** mostly nuisance, but indicate warm niches and possible contamination.  
- **Cats:** useful rodent control, but avoid touching (scratch/bite risk).  
- **Bats:** don’t disturb roosts; avoid standing directly beneath.  
- **Frogs/eel-like fish:** harmless indicators of wet routes and deeper pockets.

Core principle: this ecosystem is stable because everyone uses predictable lanes.  
You stay safe by **not interrupting traffic patterns**.

---

## 5) Timing your visit

Best window is usually:

- **After** major pump cycle stabilizes
- **Not during/just after heavy rain**
- During periods of active maintenance presence (if legal/safe), because systems are being watched

Worst window:

- Storm events
- Power interruptions
- Shift-change confusion when lights/noise patterns suddenly change

---

## 6) Mental prep

This environment can feel uncanny because it’s open yet enclosed, loud yet empty.  
Common effects after 20–40 minutes:

- Time distortion
- Mild anxiety from constant motion in peripheral vision
- Fatigue from heat + noise drone

Countermeasures:

- Set timed check-ins every 15–20 min
- Hydrate on schedule, not by thirst
- Stop, re-orient, verify exit direction regularly

---

## 7) Hard “leave now” triggers

Exit immediately if you notice:

- Water rising perceptibly over minutes
- Sudden fan silence / power instability
- Strong sewage/chemical smell spike
- Large coordinated animal movement away from one area
- Dizziness, nausea, confusion, chills (heat stress/infection risk)

---

## 8) Decontamination after exit

- Remove boots before entering clean area.
- Bag outer layer clothing.
- Wash hands/forearms thoroughly, then shower ASAP.
- Clean and dry gear completely (fungus/corrosion starts fast).
- Monitor for fever, GI upset, unusual skin reactions for 72 hours.
