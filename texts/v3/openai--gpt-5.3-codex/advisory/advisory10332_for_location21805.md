Treat this place like a **micro-desert machine**: stable, beautiful, and mildly hostile to human senses.

## What you’re walking into
You’re entering a **low-ceilinged, high-reflectance, high-noise, ultra-dry chamber** with:
- Constant loud broadband airflow (speech-killing noise)
- Mineral dust (alkaline/salty, desiccating, irritating)
- Glare-heavy light with weak shadow depth (depth perception strain)
- Tight geometry and a knee-height seam entrance (crawl/stoop risk)
- No fauna, little biological buffering, very stable conditions

It won’t “attack” you—but it will steadily wear down hearing, eyes, lungs, skin, and attention if unprepared.

---

## Minimum prep kit (non-negotiable)
1. **Hearing protection (double layer):**
   - Foam earplugs + over-ear defenders  
   The drone sounds like permanent industrial wind; protect against fatigue and damage.

2. **Respiratory protection:**
   - At least N95; ideally P100/FFP3  
   Dust is fine, mineral, and alkaline.

3. **Sealed eye protection:**
   - Wraparound goggles (clear or lightly tinted)  
   You need dust sealing and glare reduction.

4. **Head protection + knee protection:**
   - Bump cap/helmet and knee pads  
   Entrance seam and low ceiling make incidental impacts likely.

5. **Long sleeves, gloves, and closed boots:**
   - Smooth fabrics, minimal exposed skin  
   Dry salts + grit abrade skin quickly.

6. **Hydration + electrolyte plan:**
   - Pre-hydrate before entry; bring water for immediately after  
   You may not feel sweaty, but dry air dehydrates quietly.

7. **Non-audio timing/communication tools:**
   - Vibrating timer/watch
   - Hand signals or touch signals with partner  
   Voice won’t carry usefully.

---

## Entry protocol (practical)
- **Suit up before crossing the seam.** Don’t “just peek.”
- **Use a buddy system** if possible. If solo, leave an external check-in timer.
- **Pause for 2–3 minutes after entry** for sensory adaptation:
  - eyes adjust to diffuse glare,
  - balance adjusts to weak shadows + domed tiles,
  - brain adjusts to acoustic overload.
- Move **slowly and deliberately**; the floor isn’t flat in a normal sense.
- Keep your visit initially short: **15–30 minutes first exposure**.

---

## Behavioral rules inside
- Assume every surface is both **fragile and load-bearing** in some way.
- Don’t scrape crusts, wet surfaces, or “test” airflow throats.
- Avoid unnecessary touching: salts and oils from skin can alter micro-surfaces.
- Expect cognitive effects:
  - time distortion,
  - mild irritability,
  - tunnel focus from constant sound.
- Use pre-agreed check signals every few minutes (thumbs up, tap counts, etc.).

---

## What *not* to bring
- No loose papers/lightweight debris (airflow will take them).
- No perfumes/aerosols/chalk sprays.
- No open liquids inside unless mission-critical.
- No assumption that “quiet corner” exists—there isn’t one.

---

## Aftercare (important)
Once out:
1. Brush off clothing before removing respirator.
2. Rinse eyes/nasal passages gently (saline is ideal).
3. Rehydrate and moisturize skin/lips.
4. Spend 10–20 minutes in a quieter, dimmer space to reset hearing/attention.
5. Log symptoms (headache, ringing, dry cough, vertigo) before next visit.

---

## Mental preparation
This chamber is psychologically strange: it feels static, but it is active.  
The best mindset is not “explorer of ruins,” but **visitor to a working equilibrium**.  
Be respectful, be methodical, and keep exposures short until you know your tolerance.
