What a phenomenal place.  
For an **ordinary human traveler**, this is less “adventure cave” and more “living hydraulic machine with people inside it.”

Prepare like you’re visiting:

1) a damp industrial site,  
2) a monastery with strict habits, and  
3) a city that navigates by sound more than sight.

## 1) Mental prep first

- **Expect compression**: low passages, close walls, many bodies in narrow circulation routes.
- **Expect perpetual damp**: you won’t fully dry out.
- **Expect constant noise**: not random loudness—layered, meaningful sound.
- **Expect social conservatism**: this place survives by routine. Sudden novelty = risk.

If you need sunlight, silence, and personal space to stay regulated, plan short stays (24–48 hours max) before attempting a longer residency.

---

## 2) What to wear and carry

### Essential clothing
- **Non-slip boots** with excellent wet-stone traction.
- **Wool or synthetic layers** that insulate when damp (avoid cotton).
- **Light shell** (not for rain, for constant seep and splash).
- **Knee/elbow protection** for crawl entry and tight stairs.
- **Cap or hood** (drips are constant).

### Essential kit
- **Hands-free warm-spectrum light** (headlamp with dim mode + red/amber mode).
- **Backup lights** (at least two).
- **Dry bags** inside your pack (everything will get wet otherwise).
- **Fingerless grip gloves** (for wet rails and mineral-crusted stone).
- **Compact earplugs** with partial attenuation (you need to hear patterns, not go deaf).
- **Water purification backup** (their water may be safe locally but your gut may disagree).
- **Electrolyte salts** (damp + mineral-heavy environment can dehydrate you subtly).
- **Small first aid** focused on slips, cuts, fungal skin irritation, and blisters.
- **Respiratory cloth/mask** for kiln quarters and mineral dust scraping periods.
- **Waxed notebook + pencil** (ink fails in humidity).

### Do *not* rely on
- Paper maps, phone screens at full brightness, smooth-soled shoes, or anything needing dry conditions.

---

## 3) Health and body management

- **Acclimate your hearing**: spend first hour stationary, listening. Don’t try to “push through” noise fatigue.
- **Protect skin**: apply anti-chafe and barrier cream to feet, groin, underarms.
- **Rotate socks often** to prevent maceration.
- **Warmth management**: cave-cool damp can chill you slowly without obvious shivering.
- **Sleep aids**: use low-profile ear attenuation, not full noise cancellation. You need alarm cues from the environment.

---

## 4) Social etiquette (important here)

This city survives through maintenance discipline. Respect that.

- **Never block a channel, spillway, stair, or gate**, even “for a minute.”
- **Don’t touch sluices, wheels, tuned pipes, or drip stones.**
- **Keep your voice short and clear.** Long speech gets lost in the acoustic load.
- **Announce yourself before entering dim thresholds.**
- **Ask before using light in someone’s face/work area.**
- **Do not “improve” anything** (tighten rope, move a tool, clear buildup) unless asked.
- **If locals place a hand on a wall and go silent, you go silent too.** They’re listening for structural/hydraulic signals.

---

## 5) Navigation strategy

You won’t navigate primarily by sight.

- Get a local guide early and learn 3–5 anchor sounds:
  - deep cistern thud
  - kiln chatter
  - bath rush
  - chain lift rattle
- Memorize route by **sound sequence**, not visual landmarks.
- At intersections, stop and listen before moving.
- If disoriented: move toward broad communal water-chord zones (usually populated) rather than quiet maintenance ducts.

---

## 6) Food, trade, and gifts

Diet will be mineral-heavy, fungus/crustacean/protein paste oriented, with limited texture diversity.

Bring:
- your own digestive buffer foods for first 1–2 days,
- a clean personal cup/spoon,
- mild probiotics (if you’re sensitive).

Good gifts:
- corrosion-resistant needles/hinge pins,
- fine whetstones,
- durable cordage,
- precise measuring strings/plumb tools,
- smokeless lamp wicks (if compatible).

Bad gifts:
- seeds/plants/soil (biosecurity and symbolism issues),
- bright noisy gadgets,
- anything that creates unmanaged heat/smoke.

---

## 7) Main hazards

- **Slips and falls** (primary risk).
- **Head impacts** in irregular passages.
- **Hearing fatigue leading to bad decisions.**
- **Slow chilling** from persistent damp.
- **Localized bad air pockets** in low maintenance voids.
- **Getting “acoustically lost.”**

Emergency rule: if you lose orientation, **stop moving, reduce your own noise, listen for maintenance traffic**, then signal vocally in short repeated bursts.

---

## 8) Departure protocol (multiverse traveler-specific)

Before leaving:
- **De-mineralize gear** (calcite crust carries local microchemistry).
- **Dry and inspect leather/fabric** for mineral salt loading.
- **Check boots and pack seams** for hitchhiking arthropods/crabs.
- **Quarantine clothing** one cycle before entering a greener biome (to avoid cross-ecology contamination).

---

If you prepare well, this won’t feel like a hostile place. It’ll feel like entering a civilization that has learned to live inside a heartbeat of water and stone—and expects visitors to keep the rhythm.
