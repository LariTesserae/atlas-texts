What you’re visiting is basically a **high-altitude amphibious city**: cold, wet, vertical, and alive in every direction.  
Prepare for it like a cross between a mountain expedition, a marsh settlement, and a partially submerged ruin.

## 1) Body prep (before arrival)

1. **Altitude conditioning**
   - It’s in a mountain caldera, so expect thinner air.
   - Spend a few days acclimatizing if possible.
   - If your jump method drops you in abruptly, plan low exertion for day 1–2.

2. **Cold-water tolerance**
   - Even if air is mild, basin water is likely cold enough to sap strength quickly.
   - Practice controlled immersion and self-rescue from water while carrying gear.

3. **Balance + climbing**
   - You’ll be moving on wet ladders, floating platforms, roots, and sloped masonry.
   - Train grip strength and slow-footed movement over unstable surfaces.

4. **Infection prevention habits**
   - Tiny cuts matter in this environment.
   - Trim nails, carry wound wash, antifungal powder/cream, and waterproof dressings.

---

## 2) Essential gear (the non-negotiables)

- **Layered clothing**: quick-dry base, insulating mid-layer, waterproof shell.
- **Footwear**: one pair for wet traction (siped sole), one dry pair for sleeping.
- **Buoyancy aid**: low-profile life vest or inflatable collar; don’t skip this.
- **Dry bags**: multiple small ones > one large one.
- **Cordage + clips**: everything must be tethered.
- **Headlamp with warm + red mode** (plus backup light).
- **Gloves**: wet-grip work gloves; roots, ropes, and stone abrade skin.
- **Compact respirator mask or at least cloth wraps** for mold-heavy enclosed root rooms.
- **Earplugs** for sleep (the place is never quiet).
- **Water purification** even if locals have sweet-air plants and catchment methods.
- **Thermal blanket / emergency bivy** (hypothermia risk after immersion).
- **Small repair kit**: needle, waxed thread, cord, resin tape, knife.
- **Insect and leech management** suited to wet biomes.
- **Personal meds in waterproof hard case**.

If tech reliability is uncertain across universes: prefer **simple mechanical gear** over power-dependent systems.

---

## 3) How to move safely in the city

- Think in **three layers**:
  1) canopy/platform layer (bright, social, exposed to rain),
  2) stem/root layer (shadowed, maze-like, structurally complex),
  3) drowned stone layer (beautiful, low light, highest entrapment risk).

- **Never move alone** below the top layer until you know routes.
- Ask locals for **predator routes and feeding windows**; this ecosystem is stable because everyone respects timing.
- Treat all “empty” water as a roadway: fish schools, canoes, hunting birds all use channels.
- On ladders: **three points of contact**; on pontoons: **short steps, low center of gravity**.
- Don’t force through vine curtains blindly—there may be current, nests, or drop-offs behind.

---

## 4) Settlement etiquette (important)

This place feels patient and procedural. Visitors who rush are a problem.

- Follow marked channels and mooring customs.
- Ask before tying to any root/tower anchor; many are load-balanced systems.
- Keep gear **hung in nets**, not left on surfaces (mold + animals + water rise).
- Don’t disturb snail colonies, root lattices, or moss gaskets on doors.
- Avoid loud sudden noise in enclosed water halls—acoustics carry and can trigger fauna reactions.
- Bring useful gifts: cordage, needlework tools, resin sealants, fungal-safe storage cloth, hook repairs.

---

## 5) Food, water, and sleep

- **Drink**: rain-catch or treated water only.
- **Eat**: local fish/crab can be excellent, but avoid raw until you understand parasite risk.
- **Sleep high and dry** if possible, in ventilated pockets under leaf domes or tower platforms.
- Use a **hammock or suspended bed** over floor sleeping.
- Moisture management is daily work: rotate clothes, dry feet, air wounds.

---

## 6) Main hazards to respect

1. **Immersion + cold shock**
2. **Slip/fall into channels**
3. **Entrapment in root/ruin layers**
4. **Skin infections, fungal issues**
5. **Chronic fatigue from constant sound + damp**
6. **Navigation drift** (districts slowly migrate; yesterday’s shortcut may be gone)

---

## 7) First 48-hour plan (recommended)

- Day 0: Arrive, secure dry shelter, establish local contact, no deep exploration.
- Day 1: Learn district map, safe ladders, current direction, and animal schedules.
- Day 2: Do guided descent to mid/lower layers, practice return routes in low light.
- After: travel confidently only once you can navigate by **structure + sound**, not sight alone.

---

If you prepare right, this is not a hostile place—it’s a **disciplined wet civilization**.  
Move slowly, stay tethered, keep dry reserves, respect rhythms older than you, and you’ll do well.
