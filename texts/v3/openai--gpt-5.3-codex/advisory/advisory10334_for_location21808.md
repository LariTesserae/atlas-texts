What a beautiful, unforgiving place.  
To visit this city safely, prepare like you’re entering a **working water machine at high altitude**—not a museum.

## 1) Prepare your body first

- **Cardio + legs:** You’ll be climbing constantly (stairs, terraces, wet inclines).
- **Cold-wet tolerance:** It’s not extreme cold, but persistent damp plus wind drains heat fast.
- **Altitude adjustment:** Arrive with a plan for 1–3 lighter days if you’re coming from sea level.
- **Joint care:** Surfaces are uneven and slick. Strengthen ankles; bring knee support if needed.

## 2) Pack for “always damp, never dramatic”

Think: “I will be wet, but I must stay warm and mobile.”

### Essential clothing
- Waterproof shell (quiet fabric, not crinkly expedition style if you want to blend in)
- Mid-layers in wool/fleece (wool shines here)
- Quick-dry base layers (no cotton)
- 2 pairs high-traction boots/shoes (one drying while one in use)
- Several wool socks (rotate aggressively)
- Warm hat + light gloves (even summer mornings)

### Essential gear
- Soft-sided pack with rain cover
- Dry bags for documents/medicine
- Walking stick/trekking pole (huge help on slick steps)
- Small headlamp/lantern (mist + cloud dim light early)
- Personal water filter (for emergencies; local water hierarchy may limit access)
- Compact repair kit: cord, needle, patch tape, waxed thread

### What *not* to bring
- Hard rolling luggage (useless on stairs/channels)
- Delicate electronics you can’t seal from moisture
- High-power gadgets that imply extraction/surveillance
- Anything that leaks oils/chemicals (contamination is socially serious)

## 3) Learn the city’s core law: flow comes first

In this place, drainage is governance.

- Never stand long in a narrow runnel or sloped lane during rain buildup.
- Don’t set bags, tripods, crates, or yourself where water “might” go.
- Paths are often emergency spillways.
- Ask before using any basin, well, channel edge, or cistern platform.
- Never wash with soap near shared water lines unless explicitly told where.

If locals move suddenly during rain, follow immediately and ask questions later.

## 4) Social etiquette that will keep you welcome

- **Speak softer than usual.** Sound is low here; loud voices read as aggression.
- **Acknowledge maintenance work.** It’s civic backbone, not menial labor.
- **Don’t romanticize decay.** What looks ancient is actively managed.
- **Offer useful help, not opinions.** Carry stone, clear silt, patch rope, haul lime.
- **Ask “upstream/downstream?” before disposing of anything.**
- **Be punctual with weather, not clocks.** Rain windows dictate movement.

A good visitor is judged by whether they increase or decrease maintenance burden.

## 5) Health and safety risks

- Slips/falls are the #1 danger.
- Mild hypothermia from cumulative damp is common.
- Respiratory irritation can occur in fog + lime/plaster work zones.
- Foot problems (trench-foot-lite) if you don’t dry and rotate footwear.
- GI risk if you drink from wrong tier of water infrastructure.

Bring:
- Blister care, antifungal powder, rehydration salts
- A personal mug/bottle you can seal
- Broad-spectrum basic meds
- Waterproofed copies of medical info

## 6) Navigation mindset

This is not a street grid; it’s a gradient grid.

- Learn routes by **water behavior**: where it collects, splits, and drops.
- Memorize district by stone type/sheen and wall style.
- In mist, visibility collapses quickly—use fixed landmarks (aqueduct spine, stepped courts).
- Always have a “high route” and a “drain-safe route.”

If you get disoriented, go to maintained public nodes: cistern courts, major aqueduct streets, repair depots.

## 7) Lodging strategy

Choose lodging with:
- Raised sleeping platform
- Ventilated drying area
- Roof recently patched
- Proximity to a major route but not directly on overflow lanes
- Access to sanctioned water point

Night comfort matters more than scenic views; recovery is everything here.

## 8) Cultural exchange / gifts

Best gifts are practical and repair-oriented:
- Quality cordage
- Durable needles/blades
- Waterproof waxes/oils safe for timber/leather
- Fine wool textiles
- Useful medical supplies

Avoid flashy luxury items; they can create tension in a maintenance economy.

## 9) Multiverse traveler specific caution

Biosecurity is ethical here.

- Clean boots/clothing before entry (no foreign seeds/spores).
- Don’t introduce novel organisms, food ferments, or untreated materials.
- Keep your tech discreet and moisture-protected.
- Use analog backups (maps, phrase notes, paper permits).

Your mission is to pass through without destabilizing a finely tuned watershed civilization.

If you remember one sentence, make it this:  
**Arrive humble, tread lightly, keep warm, and never interrupt the water’s memory.**
