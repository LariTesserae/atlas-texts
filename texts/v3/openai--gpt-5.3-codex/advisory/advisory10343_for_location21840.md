This is a **maintenance civilization** disguised as a neighborhood.  
Prepare like you’re entering a living machine, not a ruin.

## 1) What to bring (and what not to)

### Wear
- **Slim profile clothing**: narrow passages punish bulky jackets/backpacks.
- **Quick-dry layers**: air cycles warm/damp to warm/dry unpredictably.
- **Grippy, quiet footwear**: wet plaster + polished drains are slip hazards.
- **Light head covering**: for drips, soot, and low beams.
- **Simple mask/cloth respirator**: for smoke-days, dust, animal dander.
- **One glove pair**: for ladders, rope rails, rough repairs.

### Carry
- **Soft, body-hugging pack** (no hard shell, no wide frame).
- **Own cup + small canteen**: water is allocated and ritualized.
- **Low-glare lamp** (warm light): avoid blasting mirror-lit chambers.
- **Earplugs for sleep** (but keep one ear open for alarms).
- **Basic medical kit**: antifungal cream, rehydration salts, GI meds, tetanus current.
- **Insect precautions**: repellent + bite care.
- **Small repair kit**: cordage, waxed thread, patch cloth, small knife.

### Better than money: useful trade goods
- Needle packs, lamp wicks, fine mesh, salt, soap, lime-safe patching material, quality rope.
- If gifting food/seed: do so only through local stewards (biosecurity matters).

## 2) Physical preparation before arrival
- Practice moving in **tight spaces** and on **steep stairs/ladders**.
- Train for **constant ambient noise** and broken sleep.
- Get comfortable with **close social proximity** and low privacy.
- If you’re mold-sensitive or asthmatic, bring extra meds.

---

## 3) First 24 hours: don’t be the problem
- **Hire a local guide immediately.**
- Learn the three core signals:
  - **Three hammer strikes = fire**
  - **Goat horn = flood/sluice release**
  - **Spun rattle = market closure / movement control**
- Ask where your host household’s **gutter section** is; every home maintains one.
- Find your nearest:
  - dry sleeping platform
  - safe latrine route
  - flood-safe high point
  - smoke vented chamber

---

## 4) Core etiquette (critical)
1. **Water has right-of-way.** If channels open, you move. Instantly.
2. **Never obstruct gutters, drains, ladders, bridges, or vent mouths.**
3. **Do not leave organic waste or carcasses in structure spaces.** Rot is taboo.
4. **Don’t handle valves, sluices, mirror shutters, or cistern covers uninvited.**
5. **Pest-eaters are communal assets** (spiders, snakes, owls, cats in some zones).
6. **Ask before photographing/documenting infrastructure** (if relevant to your travel tech).
7. Keep your camp odor controlled; smell is part of social geography here.

---

## 5) Navigation strategy
This place is legible by **sound + airflow + light quality**, not by pretty landmarks.

Use:
- “left at constant drip,” “up where tin light turns blue,” “past donkey bay.”
- Mental map by **vertical layers** first, then horizontal routes.
- Never assume a dry path stays dry on sluice-days.

Do not:
- Mark walls with paint/chalk unless invited (can be seen as defacement of maintained surfaces).

---

## 6) Health and sleep survival
- Sleep on a **raised surface**, not floor level.
- Rotate dry socks aggressively; moisture skin problems come fast.
- Eat hot cooked food when possible; standing water ecosystems are active.
- Keep small cuts clean—everything is touched, humid, and old.
- Expect dreams/light sleep fragmentation from constant percussion and dripping.

---

## 7) Mindset that works
Come in humble, useful, and observant.

You’re visiting a place where:
- wear = wisdom,
- noise = governance,
- maintenance = morality,
- and survival is collective choreography.

If you contribute labor (even minor gutter clearing, hauling, patching), you’ll be accepted faster than with any exotic currency.
