This basin is **not** a sightseeing stop. It’s a dynamic hazard zone with unstable hydrology, unstable acoustics, and mildly unstable causality.

For an ordinary human traveler, preparation should be less “expedition glamour” and more “mine rescue + storm caving + ritual discipline.”

## 1) Decide if you should go at all
Go only if you can do all three:

- Travel with at least **one trusted partner** (two is better).
- Commit to **strict procedures** even when they feel superstitious.
- Accept that your senses (especially vision) are not the primary truth source there.

If you can’t do that, don’t enter the bowl—observe from the rim.

---

## 2) Gear that actually matters

### Core survival
- Climbing helmet (falling rock + pressure impacts)
- Eye protection with anti-fog
- Full-body shell layers (non-cotton), plus warm midlayer  
  *(it’s warm-wet until it suddenly isn’t)*
- Gloves with wet grip
- Sticky-soled approach boots (glass-slick basalt is real)
- Harness, static rope, slings, anchors, carabiners
- 2–3 hard tie-off points per person at all times in active zones

### Wet/noise environment
- Filtered hearing protection (not full deafening plugs—you need pressure cues)
- Waterproof headlamp + backup light
- Red/low-output mode for night (don’t blind yourself in mist)
- Dry bags inside dry bags
- Waterproof notebook + grease pencil (don’t rely on phone screens)

### Health
- Potable water in sealed containers (do **not** trust local water)
- Electrolytes + high-calorie food
- Foot care kit (maceration/fungal prevention)
- Compact trauma kit (lacerations, blunt injury, eye rinse)
- Respiratory cloth/mask for mineral-heavy vapor spells

### Navigation/reality discipline
- **Physical route markers** tied/bolted, not just drawn marks  
  (marks may “move” overnight)
- Tensioned guideline between safe points
- Analog watch + backup (for routine timing, not absolute truth)
- Personal ID token worn on chest (helps identity checks under distortion)

## 3) Mindset shift: use the right senses
In that basin:

- **Sight lies first.**
- **Sound overwhelms.**
- **Pressure/touch tells the truth earliest.**

So move like this:
- Knees bent, low center of gravity.
- One hand always in contact with anchor/rock/line.
- Pause every few steps for a “pressure read” (air push/pull on skin).
- If unsure, stop and wait one full surge cycle.

---

## 4) Camp and shelter rules
Use existing alcoves/platforms only after testing stability.

- Never sleep at the same height twice (good local rule; follow it).
- Keep sleeping gear clipped in place.
- Maintain two exits from sleeping position.
- Pre-rig a “drop to kneel” stance area for vertical surge events.
- Keep all essentials attached to you while sleeping (boots, light, whistle, knife, water).

---

## 5) Communication protocol (because you can’t hear much)
Set this **before entry**:
- Hand signals for: stop, kneel, retreat, surge, injury, anchor, “not me” (identity check).
- Touch signals on shoulder/harness line when visibility drops.
- Check-ins every 10 minutes by touch and eye contact, not shouting.
- If separated from line-of-touch: freeze, crouch, wait, signal via line tugs.

---

## 6) Behavioral rules inside the basin
Treat these as law:

1. **Trust pressure before sight.**
2. **When roar drops suddenly, kneel immediately.**
3. **Never follow your reflection** or delayed shadow.
4. **Anchor everything** (tools, packs, even “heavy” items).
5. **Do not throw rocks** or make unnecessary disturbances; system-wide turbulence can propagate.
6. **No solo scouting**, even for “just a few meters.”
7. **Do not chase fauna** through mist layers.

---

## 7) Reality anomalies: practical countermeasures
You may see delayed wave impacts, detached reflections, displaced marks.

Use redundancy:
- Confirm observations with partner: “I see / you see / we touch.”
- Make decisions only on **two matching channels** (e.g., pressure + line tension, not vision alone).
- Record location with multiple references (anchor number + elevation + time + tactile feature).
- If something seems “newly placed” with no approach path, treat as unstable/untouched.

If you experience disorientation:
- Sit, clip in, breathe, drink, re-orient by touch map and anchor chain.
- Do not move until partner confirms route.

---

## 8) Medical and psychological prep
Expect:
- Fatigue from constant vibration and noise
- Balance strain and nausea
- Decision fatigue from sensory contradiction

Train beforehand:
- Wet-rock movement
- Low-visibility rope travel
- Nonverbal team drills
- Stress breathing under loud continuous noise

Plan short exposure windows. This place drains cognition fast.

---

## 9) Exit discipline
Leave earlier than you think you should.  
Do a full count at rim:
- People
- Critical gear
- Notes
- Identity checks (simple personal questions + physical token verification)

After exit:
- Warm/dry immediately
- Foot and skin treatment
- Hydrate
- Debrief while memory is fresh (sequence distortions happen)

---

If you prepare only one way, prepare this way:  
**Go slow, stay clipped, trust touch, obey local cautions, and never improvise because something “looks safe.”**  
In that basin, “looks safe” is often the most dangerous sentence.
