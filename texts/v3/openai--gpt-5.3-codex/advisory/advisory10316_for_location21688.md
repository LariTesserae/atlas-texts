Great destination choice—and yes, this one can kill you quietly if you prepare like it’s “just a hot desert.”

Here’s how an **ordinary human traveler** should prep for this basin-city.

---

## 1) Core reality check: this is a **dry chemistry world**, not an Earth biome

You are entering a place with:

- **No liquid water at all**
- Likely **high salts, reactive vapors, and volcanic gases**
- **Dim, copper-filtered light** (visual strain + circadian confusion)
- Constant **acoustic and vibrational load**
- Dense plant life that is likely **chemically active** and not biologically familiar

So your prep priorities are:

1. **Atmosphere control**
2. **Hydration independence**
3. **Chemical skin/lung protection**
4. **Hearing + vibration management**
5. **Spore and dust filtration**
6. **Slow movement + low-contact behavior**

---

## 2) Must-have equipment (non-negotiable)

## Life support
- **Closed-loop respirator** with multi-stage cartridges (acid gas + particulate + volcanic gases).
- Backup: minimum 2 full cartridge sets per day of field time.
- **Portable O₂ reserve** (even if local air is breathable, assume episodic vent outgassing).
- **Continuous gas monitor**: CO₂, SO₂, H₂S, volatile halogens if possible.

## Hydration & physiology
- Bring **all drinking water** in sealed bladders; no local sourcing.
- Add electrolytes; this environment will dehydrate you through respiration and heat.
- **Humidifying face interface** if available—extreme dryness damages nasal/lung tissue.
- Eye care: preservative-free rewetting drops + sealed goggles.

## Clothing
- Full-body **dust-proof, salt-resistant suit** (light-colored outer, heat-shedding).
- Inner layers that wick sweat without salt caking.
- Gloves with chemical-resistant palms.
- Boots with **sealed gaiters** (powder intrusion will shred feet over days).

## Sensory protection
- Layered hearing protection:
  - inner filtered plugs
  - outer adjustable muffs
- Vibration-damp insoles (plaza resonance may induce fatigue/nausea).

## Navigation + timekeeping
- Local horizon is deceptive in a basin; bring:
  - inertial nav + mapped waypoints
  - physical line-of-sight markers
- **Independent circadian timer/light** (you will lose day-night sense in copper dimness).

## Shelter
- Pressure-neutral isn’t the issue; **air quality and dust exclusion are**.
- Bring a **micro-shelter with filtered intake** and dust lock.
- Never sleep in raw plant bands or near vent lines.

## 3) Environmental hazards you might underestimate

1. **Chemical botany contact**
   - Torn fronds release powder that hardens; treat as caustic/adhesive unknown.
   - Do not brush against vegetation casually.
   - Avoid touching “frost crystals” on leaves in early cycle.

2. **Dusk spore events**
   - Massive synchronized releases likely at heat-gradient flips.
   - Be indoors/filtered at dusk and shortly after.

3. **Invisible gas pulses**
   - “Ground exhales” means localized atmospheres.
   - If plants get denser suddenly, gas chemistry is probably changing too.

4. **Acoustic fatigue**
   - Constant low-frequency tones can impair judgment.
   - Schedule quiet breaks in acoustically dead shelter.

5. **Dust abrasion**
   - Wind-polished salts + fine mineral grit will damage skin, joints, optics, seals.

---

## 4) Movement protocol in the city

- Stay on established **fired-clay avenues** where possible.
- Assume every green band is active infrastructure/ecology.
- Avoid kiln districts unless escorted—thermal and gas risk.
- Don’t loiter on resonant plazas if tremor activity rises.
- Keep distance from aging “succulent towers” (collapse/pistol-crack events).

Pace: slow, deliberate, low touch.

## 5) If there are locals (or inherited systems still “in use”)

Even if population is sparse/absent, the city sounds **maintained by design**. Treat it as occupied.

- Don’t block vents, wind organs, or root corridors.
- Don’t harvest plant material without permission.
- Don’t pour Earth water as an offering—could be contamination.
- Trade items likely valued:
  - ceramic tools
  - sealants
  - fiber-compatible cordage
  - noncorroding mechanical parts (not flashy metals)

## 6) Medical prep before departure

- Pulmonary check (asthma/COPD travelers should reconsider)
- Baseline hearing test
- Skin barrier prep for salt dermatitis
- Bring meds for:
  - dry-eye injury
  - contact dermatitis
  - inhalation irritation
  - migraine/vestibular overload
  - heat exhaustion

## 7) Psychological prep

This place is “busy but empty.”  
Expect:
- No birds, no animal cues, no familiar life rhythm
- Mechanical-botanical soundscape day and night
- Visual monotony with sudden industrial intensity

Travelers often report:
- Time distortion
- Mild derealization
- “Silent panic” despite loud surroundings

Counter with strict routines: hydration alarm, rest blocks, sensory resets, fixed return times.
