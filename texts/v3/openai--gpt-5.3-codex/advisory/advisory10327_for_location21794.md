What a place.  
For an **ordinary human** (water-based biology, Earth senses, Earth reflexes), this world is survivable—but only if you prepare like you’re entering a high-altitude industrial desert that is also a living, electrically active organism.

## 1) Baseline reality check (what will hurt you first)

Your biggest immediate threats are:

1. **Thin, dry, reactive air** (dehydration + respiratory irritation)  
2. **Constant acoustic overload** (infrasound, ultrasound, shock-cracks)  
3. **Electrostatic storms and conductive dust**  
4. **Misreading “empty ground” that is actually alive/owned/sensitive**  
5. **Urban-biological legal rules you can violate without noticing**

So prepare for **altitude + desert + biohazard + electrical weather + alien zoning law**.

---

## 2) Body prep before arrival

- **Altitude conditioning**: if possible, spend 2–4 weeks doing hypoxia adaptation (or get temporary oxygenation support mods).
- **Aggressive hydration protocol**: start before transit; this place will pull water out of you fast.
- **Respiratory protection training**: practice long wear of filtered masks/helmets.
- **Hearing protection conditioning**: you need layered hearing systems, not simple earplugs.
- **Vaccination / bio-screen package**: not because of “germs” in the usual sense, but to reduce inflammatory overreaction to mineral-organic aerosols.
- **Skin barrier prep**: dry conductive dust + static = dermatitis and microburn risk.

If your medtech allows it, bring:
- electrolyte balancing implants/patches
- anti-inflammatory rescue injectors
- tympanic pressure regulation aids
- emergency bronchodilators

## 3) Essential gear (non-negotiable)

### Life support & clothing
- **Sealed breathing hood or half-mask with multi-stage filters** (particulate + reactive gas scrubbing)
- **Humidity-recycling underlayer** (you exhale water; reclaim it)
- **Electrostatic-dissipative outer suit** (grounding mesh + insulated joints)
- **Eye protection** with anti-abrasion, low-light enhancement, and flash suppression
- **Hard-shell boots** with pressure-distributing soles (to avoid damaging living crusts)
- **Thermal adaptive cloak/jacket** (rapid temp swings)

### Sensory survival
- **Tri-band hearing system**:
  - passive reduction for continuous noise
  - active cancellation for infrasound bands
  - ultrasonic dampening
- **Vibrotactile alert pack** (because audio comms can fail in the noise field)

### Navigation & safety
- **Ground-permission scanner** (detects dormant plant tissue / owned substrate)
- **Charge-gradient meter** (storm precursor warning)
- **Local acoustic map module** (terrain is navigated by sound channels)
- **Faraday bivy or micro-shelter** for sudden electrical fronts
- **Non-sparking tools only**

### Consumables
- Water (more than you think) + electrolyte concentrates
- Lubricating eye/nasal gels
- Anti-dust skin sealant wipes
- Redundant power cells in shielded cases

## 4) How to move without causing problems

On this world, walking is a legal and ecological act.

- **Never assume open flats are inert.**  
  Treat all bare ground as potentially living, sensing, or privately managed.
- **Use marked corridors** in both wild zones and cities.
- **Step-light protocol**: avoid rhythmic stomping; many species and systems use vibration signaling.
- **Do not lean on “trees” or ridges casually.** You may be touching structural organs, transit conduits, or communication tissues.
- **Don’t collect samples casually** (pollen, bark, dust): often regulated as infrastructure.

If in doubt, stop and request route permission through local guidance nodes.

---

## 5) Storm protocol (memorize this)

When charge fronts approach, you may get: metallic taste, hair lift, dust streamer alignment, sudden fauna agitation, deep harmonic hum.

**Immediate actions:**
1. Get off exposed heights and isolated vertical structures.  
2. Avoid crown-linked vegetation clusters (lightning pathways).  
3. Enter designated storm alcove / Faraday shelter.  
4. Disconnect unnecessary electronics; shield comms.  
5. Stay put until local all-clear (not just visual calm).

After storms, terrain may be biologically reconfigured.  
**Do not trust yesterday’s map.**

## 6) City etiquette (very important)

These settlements are grown systems. Think “you are inside a managed metabolism.”

- **Follow charge quotas and docking rules** for devices.
- **Respect pollination/migration quiet windows** (some districts ban specific frequencies or vibration levels).
- **Do not block vent plumes**—that “steam” is often part of thermal ecology.
- **Ask before touching walls/surfaces**; many are semi-living and responsive.
- **Accept route reroutes**; neighborhoods physically shift over time.

Legal concept to remember: **ecological right-of-way beats pedestrian convenience**.

---

## 7) Wildlife safety

- Many animals signal before attacking, but in bands you might not hear.
- Use **broad-spectrum proximity sonar/lidar**, not eyesight alone.
- Predators may use panic acoustics: if your hearing rig alarms, **freeze, lower profile, seek hard cover**.
- Never stand between herding lines and resonant plains.
- If coated with conductive dust after burrow-zone crossing, **decontaminate before entering urban charge networks**.

---

## 8) Communication and social prep

- Learn basic local signaling etiquette: visual light pulses + contact tone packets.
- Carry **text-first translator**; spoken channels are unreliable in high-noise zones.
- Don’t interpret constant background sound as “chaos.” It is often structured civic/ecological data.

## 9) Psychological prep

Humans from quieter, wetter worlds often experience:
- dryness anxiety
- noise fatigue
- “lifelessness” misread (because little green, lots of mineral tones)

Plan mandatory decompression periods in shielded quiet rooms.  
Your judgment drops fast under constant infrasonic stress.
