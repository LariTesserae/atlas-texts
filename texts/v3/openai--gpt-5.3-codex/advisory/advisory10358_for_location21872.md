Great description.  
If I were prepping an **ordinary human traveler** for this district, I’d frame it like this:

## Read the place correctly first
This is not an “adventure cave.” It’s a **young, hot industrial basin** where people live and maintain a delicate water/heat system.  
You are entering a place where:

- heat load is constant,
- moisture appears suddenly,
- layout changes frequently,
- space is tight and shared,
- mistakes can disrupt survival infrastructure.

So prepare like you’re visiting a **working engine room that doubles as a neighborhood**.

---

## 1) Body prep (non-negotiable)

- **Heat acclimation**: Spend 7–10 days before arrival increasing time in warm environments.
- **Hydration discipline**: Train yourself to drink on schedule, not by thirst.
- **Electrolytes**: Bring salts/tablets; plain water alone won’t keep you functional.
- **Respiratory baseline**: If you have asthma or heat sensitivity, bring meds and a backup.
- **Sleep resilience**: Practice sleeping in warm, low-hum environments with periodic mechanical noise.

---

## 2) Clothing and personal kit

### Wear
- Lightweight, **moisture-wicking**, long-sleeve base layers.
- Breathable but abrasion-resistant outer layer (rock edges are still sharp).
- **Anti-slip, heat-tolerant boots** (wet metal + warm floors).
- Thin work gloves for handrails/ladders.
- Compact hardhat or bump cap (dense overhead piping).

### Carry
- 1L insulated bottle + electrolyte sachets.
- Small microfiber towel (condensation happens constantly).
- Headlamp with warm and cool modes + spare battery.
- Waterproof notebook + grease pencil (digital tools fog and fail).
- Compact mask/respirator for steam-mineral bursts and maintenance dust.
- Simple first aid: blister care, burn gel, oral rehydration salts.

### Protect your tech
- Use **sealed, heat-rated cases**.
- Anti-condensation wipes/sachets.
- Tether anything you can drop into grates.

---

## 3) Navigation strategy (critical in this district)

Because corridors get renumbered and rerouted:

- Never rely on one map.
- Capture route notes at each junction (section number, color stripe, landmark sound).
- Mark your path in locally accepted ways (ask first; don’t add confusing marks).
- Build a “return spine” route and repeat it daily.
- Learn shift timings—human flow is your best wayfinding system.

---

## 4) Behavioral etiquette (this matters as much as gear)

- Treat every valve, gate, and panel as **untouchable** unless invited.
- Don’t block walkways during shift changes.
- Ask before photographing, mapping, or recording infrastructure.
- Keep your footprint small: space is shared, storage is tight.
- Offer useful trade items if appropriate: filter media, seal tape, corrosion-resistant fasteners, battery cells, label stock.

People there likely respect visitors who are **predictable, disciplined, and low-maintenance**.

---

## 5) Know the local “weather”

Their weather is phase-change weather:

- sudden indoor rain,
- rapid drainage activation,
- fast dryback and heat rebound.

When moisture events begin:
1. Move off exposed catwalks if possible.  
2. Watch for slick metal and visibility loss in steam bands.  
3. Stay clear of active channels, grates, and pump intake zones.  
4. Let crews work—this is routine for them, not a spectacle for you.

---

## 6) Main risks to manage

- **Heat exhaustion / heat stroke** (most likely issue for newcomers)
- Slip/fall on wet steel and scaffold
- Minor burns from hot surfaces/lines
- Disorientation in dim, repetitive architecture
- Social friction from getting in the way of utility work

No fauna doesn’t mean no danger; it means the hazards are mostly **infrastructure and physiology**.

---

## 7) Mental preparation

Expect:
- no sky,
- little organic life,
- constant low mechanical presence,
- dim practical lighting.

Some travelers get “compressed” mentally in places like this. Counter it by keeping routine:

- fixed hydration times,
- fixed check-ins,
- fixed sleep window,
- short daily decompression ritual (journal, breathwork, stretching).

---

## 8) Best arrival plan

For your first 48 hours:

- Stay with a host/guide linked to maintenance or shift logistics.
- Learn one residential route, one service route, one emergency route.
- Don’t roam during your first moisture cycle.
- Observe before asking for access to deeper sections.
