Treat this place like **a high-altitude volcanic greenhouse crossed with a living electrical plant and mild reality drift**.

For an ordinary human traveler, preparation is less about weapons and more about **physiology, discipline, and instrumentation**.

## 1) Pre-trip assumptions (don’t skip)
You are entering a chamber with:

- **Thin mountain air** (altitude risk)
- **Immediate dry heat** (heat stress + dehydration)
- **Reactive gases** (sulfur/metal vapor; respiratory hazard)
- **Sharp, young rock** (cuts, punctures, slips)
- **Biological conductivity** (plants acting like circuits)
- **Low-grade spatial inconsistency** (small geometry/shadow mismatches)
- **No obvious liquid water source** (self-contained ecology; you bring everything)

So: this is a **short-duration field excursion**, not a camp.

---

## 2) Physical prep (human body first)
- Acclimatize to altitude for 1–2 days if possible.
- Arrive well-hydrated; start electrolytes before ascent.
- No alcohol/sedatives beforehand.
- If you’re prone to altitude issues, discuss prophylaxis (like acetazolamide) with a physician.
- Set strict turn-back rules before entry.

---

## 3) Core gear
### Life support / PPE
- Half-face or full-face respirator with **acid gas cartridges + particulate filter** (P100 equivalent).
- Sealed goggles (if not using full-face respirator).
- Heat-tolerant, breathable clothing with a light outer shell.
- Cut-resistant gloves (outer) + disposable inner gloves for sampling.
- High-ankle boots with puncture-resistant sole.
- Helmet (rock + crystal hazards).
- Minimum 2–3 L water for a short visit, plus electrolytes.

### Monitoring
- 4-gas meter (at least O₂, H₂S, SO₂, CO₂).
- Temp/humidity meter.
- Simple electric field/static indicator if available.
- Redundant timekeeping (2 synced watches).
- Analog notebook + pencil (don’t trust memory alone).

### Navigation & control
- Physical route markers (noninvasive flags/tape).
- 20–30 m nonconductive tether line for orientation near entrance.
- Headlamp (white + red modes), even in daylight.

---

## 4) Team protocol (critical)
Never go alone.

Minimum:
- **Inside pair** + **outside anchor observer** at the seam.
- Voice check every 3–5 minutes.
- Pre-agreed abort word and immediate retreat rule.
- Max first exposure: 30–60 min.

If either traveler reports:
- sudden headache, metallic taste, confusion, panic, lost time, or mismatched observations,
you both exit immediately.

---

## 5) Behavior inside the chamber
- Move slowly, deliberate foot placement only.
- Assume **root grids/crystal rows are live infrastructure**: don’t step on or bridge them.
- Keep distance from venting stalks and fissures.
- Avoid touching sap/amber films (could be chemically or electrically active).
- Do not stay into dusk on first trip: likely static discharge phase.
- Treat shadow lag / geometry edits as a hazard signal, not a curiosity contest.

---

## 6) Sampling & contamination discipline
- Take minimal samples, only if mission requires.
- Use sterile ceramic or polymer tools (less conductive/reactive than metal).
- Double-bag and label with time/location immediately.
- Decon on exit: brush powders off, isolate boots/gloves, check for skin irritation/microcuts.

---

## 7) After-exit checks
- Rehydrate, cool down, reassess oxygenation.
- Compare both travelers’ notes immediately while fresh.
- Flag any memory mismatch or timeline confusion.
- Observe for delayed respiratory irritation (sulfur gases can have lagging effects).

---

## Practical rule of thumb
If normal caves teach “watch your footing,” this place teaches:

**Watch your footing, your breathing, your charge state, and your shared reality.**

Go in like a patient field engineer, not an explorer chasing spectacle. The chamber rewards slow, careful observation—and punishes casual improvisation.
