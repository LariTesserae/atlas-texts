What a beautiful and dangerous little place.  
For an ordinary human traveler, the key is: **treat this chamber like a neonatal ecosystem plus a reactive lab**—not like a cave hike.

## Core hazards (in plain terms)

1. **It is physically unstable** (surfaces still forming, soft calcite skin, clay sections).
2. **Perception is unreliable** (light/shadow inversion, double shadows, moving moisture veils).
3. **Your attention changes it** (sustained observation appears to trigger mineral growth).
4. **Local time-rate is patchy around moisture** (wet zones “run faster” biologically/geologically).
5. **Dense microfauna = contamination risk both ways** (you harm it, it may irritate/infect you).

---

## Prep before you go

## 1) Clothing & body protection
- **Non-absorbent outer layer** (light shell jacket/pants): chamber is damp, cool, and intermittently dripping.
- **Knee/elbow protection**: low ceiling, uneven ribs.
- **Gloves (nitrile under light work gloves)**: protect skin from mineral films and avoid contaminating surfaces.
- **Sealed boots with soft tread**: grip wet stone without cutting into soft calcite.
- **Eye protection** (clear wraparound): against drip, dust, fungal lace fragments.
- **Mask/respirator (at least P2/N95)**: for spores, clay particles, organic musk aerosols.

## 2) Gear
- **Diffuse, low-lumen headlamp** with red/amber mode.  
  Avoid harsh beams: this place already has odd illumination; bright directional light worsens misreads.
- **Secondary handheld light** for side-lighting floor relief.
- **Short trekking pole** (non-metal tip if possible) to test ground softness.
- **Flagging alternatives** that are non-invasive: removable clip markers, laser waypoint, or projected grid—**do not chalk or scratch walls**.
- **Field notebook + voice recorder**: you’ll forget sequences because local pacing is deceptive.
- **Timer with vibration alerts** every 2–3 minutes to break long staring episodes.
- **Sealable sample bags** only if sampling is explicitly allowed by your mission/ethics board.
- **Thermal layer**: cool air plus moisture can chill you quickly.

## 3) Biosecurity
- **Decon before entry**: clean boots, gloves, tools with non-residual disinfectant.
- **No exposed food, no crumbs, no skin flakes if possible** (cover hair, sleeves).
- **Exit decon** to avoid exporting organisms/spores into other worlds/biomes.

## Behavior protocol inside (most important)

## A. Attention discipline
Because formation responds to observation:
- Use **soft, scanning glances** instead of fixed staring.
- Never hold gaze on one crack/feature for long; use a **“3-second rule”** before looking away.
- Work in pairs: one observes, one times and prompts gaze shifts.
- If you need prolonged study, use **indirect methods** (camera on tripod, remote monitor viewed intermittently).

## B. Footstep discipline
- Step only on clearly hardened substrate.
- Avoid repeat stepping in same line; prints may “seed” future ridges and alter microflows.
- Keep weight centered and slow; no jumping, no kneeling on soft skin-rock.

## C. Moisture-time discipline
Treat wet and dry patches like different time zones:
- **Do not linger under active drip** unless you intentionally want accelerated local changes around you/equipment.
- If you must cross wet-active zones, do it briskly and log start/end time.
- Do sensitive tasks (mapping, note consolidation) in **dry-bright zones** where change rate seems slower.

## D. Wildlife etiquette
- No touching fauna/flora.
- Pause when lines of six-legged runners cross; don’t break their routes.
- Expect tiny gliders near light pools—move slowly to avoid inhalation/face contact.
- Don’t chase dart-fish in moisture veils; your approach collapses their medium.
- Burrow moth blink-synchrony can disorient—blink deliberately and keep moving.

## Navigation and cognition tips

- The chamber “reads backward.” Use **three-point orientation**:
  1. fixed entry marker,
  2. ceiling landmark,
  3. floor texture landmark.
- Reconfirm location every few minutes; don’t trust intuitive depth.
- If shadows become contradictory (double/none), stop and crouch; re-light from low side angle.
- Keep visits short (20–40 min initial exposure).

---

## Built traces: how to handle respectfully

Those stacked arcs, clay cups, and mineralized fiber lines are likely cultural/behavioral artifacts.
- Treat as **archaeological and active ecological features**.
- Do not move, clean, “repair,” or use them.
- Photograph with scale, from distance.
- If boundary fiber line exists, assume it still means “do not cross.”

---

## Exit routine (critical)

1. Pause at threshold, visually inspect boots/clothing.
2. Bag disposable layers/gloves.
3. Decon tools.
4. Log subjective anomalies immediately (time dilation feel, attention-linked growth, shadow behavior).
5. Recheck your own skin/eyes/airways for irritation over next 24h.

## Quick packing checklist

- Shell layers, thermal midlayer  
- Gloves (inner + outer), eye protection, mask

- Low-lumen diffuse lights (primary + backup)  
- Timer, notebook, recorder  
- Pole for substrate testing  
- Non-invasive markers  
- Decon kit + waste bags  
- Water + sealed nutrition (consume outside chamber)
