If you’re an ordinary human, treat this basin like a **micro-volcanic reef crossed with a chemical warzone**.  
You can visit it, but only if you prepare like you’re going EVA.

## Bottom line first
- **Do not go solo.**
- **Do not go without full respiratory + thermal gear.**
- **Do not touch rock with bare skin, ever.**
- **Spend at least one full pulse-cycle observing from the rim before entry.**

---

## 1) What will try to kill you first
In order of likely danger:

1. **Heat gradients** (a one-meter mistake can cook you)
2. **Air quality** (thin oxygen + acidic aerosols + volcanic gases + metal dust)
3. **Cuts/punctures** (obsidian, clinker, hard chitin)
4. **Dehydration** (hot, dry, no free water)
5. **Faunal contact** (most things are defensive, some are predatory)
6. **Disorientation** (dim light, few landmarks, warped sense of scale/time)

---

## 2) Minimum kit (non-negotiable)

### Life support
- Sealed **respirator or rebreather** rated for acid gas + metal particulate
- Spare filters / cartridge redundancy
- Supplemental oxygen (altitude + dust canopy)

### Thermal survival
- **Active cooling layer** under abrasion-resistant outer shell
- Thermal map visor or handheld IR scanner
- Heat alarms on wrists/ankles (your feet will enter lethal zones first)

### Mobility + protection
- Cut-resistant boots with high ankle support
- Gaiters and shin guards (crevice strikes and low crawlers)
- Gloves with puncture resistance and tactile feedback
- Climbing line + anchors (basalt plates shift, edges slice)

### Hydration/chem safety
- More water than you think you need, with electrolytes
- No skin-exposed hydration systems
- Neutralizing rinse for accidental acid dew contact

### Sensors
- CO₂/SO₂/H₂S multi-gas monitor
- Vibration sensor/geophone (the basin “talks” through stone)
- Marker beacons (visual references are poor in dust twilight)

### Medical
- Burn kit, pressure bandages, broad antiseptic, anti-inflammatory meds
- Eye wash ampoules
- Epinephrine (unknown stings/spores)

---

## 3) Skills to train before entry
- Moving on fractured volcanic rock
- Heat discipline (reading thermal gradients continuously)
- Silent team signaling (you don’t want loud acoustics)
- “No blind placement”: never kneel, sit, or hand-brace without probing first
- Fast retreat drills (because pulses can reorganize the whole ecosystem quickly)

---

## 4) Entry strategy
1. **Observe first from rim** for several hours minimum (ideally one full vent-pressure cycle).
2. Identify:
   - recurring vent flare zones
   - grazer lanes
   - predator crack-lines
   - temporary “cool islands”
3. Enter only during a relatively stable phase between major pulses.
4. Move in short stages. Re-scan thermal and gas conditions every few meters.

---

## 5) Behavior rules inside (strict)
- **Assume every surface is occupied**, even if it looks inert.
- Step on broad, already-worn plates when possible.
- Avoid polished belly-trails and dense crevice zones (traffic corridors).
- Do not block vent mouths or tunnel entrances.
- Keep vibrations low and consistent; erratic impacts can trigger chain reactions.
- Never chase wildlife away by hand—use gentle light/air puffs from a distance.
- Don’t camp in upper ledges (“up is hotter” here).
- If mats retract suddenly and grazers surge, a pulse is starting—**relocate immediately**.

---

## 6) Where to shelter (if forced)
Those old carved alcoves are tempting, but treat them as:
- possible historical shelters,
- likely current microhabitats.

Check thermal stability, gas pooling, and occupancy before use.  
If any living layer is present, do not displace it unless survival-critical.

---

## 7) Ethical + contamination protocol
You’re entering an extremely dense, niche-packed system:
- Avoid biocide sprays unless medical emergency
- Keep footprints minimal and reversible
- Pack out everything, including filters and wipes
- Decon suit/boots before leaving to avoid cross-world bio transfer

---

## 8) Exit triggers (leave immediately if)
- You lose thermal mapping
- Gas monitor alarms twice in under 10 minutes
- Team comms fail
- You feel “quiet then hum” intensifying underfoot (major pulse onset)
- Wildlife movement synchronizes abruptly (they sensed what you didn’t)

---

If you prepare well, this place is survivable—but it is never “safe.”  
Think of it as visiting a living machine whose parts are heat, stone, and hunger. Your job is not to conquer it; your job is to pass through without becoming part of its food web.
