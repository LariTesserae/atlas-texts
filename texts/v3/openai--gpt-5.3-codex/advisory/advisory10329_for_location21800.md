What you’re visiting is **not a desert town**—it’s a highly tuned dry ecosystem-city.  
Prepare like you’re entering a bright, loud, alkaline, vertically layered machine that happens to be alive.

## 1) Core risks to prepare for

1. **Dehydration + alkaline air**  
   You’ll dry out fast (lips, eyes, sinuses, skin), even if you don’t feel sweaty.

2. **Light overload**  
   Reflective white mineral + mirror mica + hard sun = snow-blindness-style eye strain.

3. **Acoustic fatigue**  
   Constant amplified sound can disorient, exhaust, and socially isolate you.

4. **Air quality variability**  
   Vent gases are useful to local life but not always friendly to human lungs in concentrated pockets.

5. **Vertical urban movement**  
   Rope lifts, ledges, shifting walkways, retensioned canopy routes: falls are a real risk.

6. **Cultural hazard: water misuse**  
   Casual spilling/washing can be seen as reckless, disrespectful, or dangerous.

---

## 2) What to pack (minimum viable kit)

### Body survival
- **UV-blocking wraparound eyewear** (category 3–4 tint, side shielding)
- **Wide-brim head covering** + neck drape
- **Loose, breathable, full-coverage clothing** (light exterior, darker inner layer)
- **Alkaline-resistant lip balm** and heavy skin barrier ointment
- **Nasal ointment/saline gel** (tiny amounts, not spray-heavy)
- **Electrolyte plan** (tablets/powders, measured dosing)
- **Sealed personal water reserve** with lockable cap and drip-proof valve

### Air and dust
- **Half-mask respirator** with mixed gas/particulate cartridges (or at least good particulate mask)
- **Compact gas detector** (CO₂ + H₂S ideally) if you’re traveling independently
- **Soft face cloths** for dust wipe-down (dry use first)

### Noise and communication
- **Musician-grade earplugs** (attenuate volume but preserve speech)
- **Backup foam plugs** for sleep
- **Slate/cards for writing + basic local sign lexicon**
- **Retention straps** for all metal objects (dropped items become major noise events)

### Movement and safety
- **High-traction boots** for saltstone and polished steps
- **Light gloves** for rope/ladder handling
- **Compact climbing lanyard** if you expect upper-terrace travel
- **Small headlamp** (for ducts/interiors; nights still have scattered light but contrast is harsh)

### Socially smart trade items
- Fine tool steel, durable cordage, precision hand tools, repair needles, ceramic filters, mica-safe polishing compounds.  
- **Do not assume water is an acceptable gift** unless brokered through local protocol.

## 3) Skills to learn before arrival

- Basic **signed marketplace phrases** and emergency gestures
- **Close-range speaking etiquette** (mouth-to-ear conversation without grabbing people)
- **Rope-lift etiquette** (loading order, silence cues, counterweight rules)
- **Echo awareness**: pause after loud sounds; listen for return patterns before moving
- **Dry hygiene discipline**: minimal water use, dust-first cleaning, waste sealing

---

## 4) First 72 hours on site

### Day 1: Acclimate, don’t explore
- Hire a **local basin guide** immediately.
- Stay in lower-to-mid terrace lodging with known airflow and sound-damp alcoves.
- Walk only marked routes; learn district signal rhythms.
- Hydrate by schedule, not thirst.

### Day 2: Sensory adaptation
- Rotate ear protection (you need hearing, but not full exposure all day).
- Practice reading wind-throat and drum signals.
- Learn “quiet zones” and “strike hours” (when mill/hammer noise peaks).

### Day 3: Functional mobility
- Add one vertical route (lift + bridge + stairs) under supervision.
- Visit one grove with a caretaker; learn what **not to touch**.
- Register your lodging with local wardens if vent shifts occur overnight.

---

## 5) Local rules that matter (and can offend if ignored)

- **Never dump water** on street, roots, walls, or dust by habit.  
- **Do not mist plants**; their biology is dry-gas adapted.  
- **Ask before touching vines/reeds**—many are structural, not ornamental.  
- **Secure loose gear** (noise discipline is civic discipline).  
- **Respect animal handling rituals**; fauna are scarce and culturally significant.  
- **Follow daily vent advisories**; districts can re-route quickly.

---

## 6) Health management in a dry-lush city

- Drink small, regular amounts; avoid chugging.
- Increase salts/electrolytes modestly (alkaline environment can mask depletion symptoms).
- Protect eyes aggressively; photokeratitis is plausible here.
- Use nighttime recovery: dark cloth over eyes, ear protection, skin re-barrier.
- If you feel sudden headache, confusion, or air hunger in low pockets:  
  **move upslope and crosswind immediately**, then mask and reassess.

---

## 7) Mindset shift (most important)

This place rewards travelers who treat it as a **living engineered ecology**, not a harsh wasteland and not a quaint ruin.  
Everything is active: sound, gas, growth, tension, salt, routes, districts. If you stay observant, quiet-handed, and moisture-disciplined, you’ll do well.
