Great location to visit—**but it’s not a tourist cavern**; it’s active infrastructure.  
Prepare like you’re entering a warm, echoing, partially submerged machine.

## 1) Mindset first
Treat it as:
- **A workplace**
- **A water source**
- **A slippery acoustic hazard**

If you go in expecting “mystic underground lake,” you’ll make mistakes. If you go in expecting “operating room made of stone and water,” you’ll do fine.

---

## 2) What to wear
Bring gear for **heat + humidity + constant spray + hard stone edges**:

- Lightweight, quick-dry clothing (not cotton-heavy)
- Non-slip footwear with good wet traction (soft rubber sole)
- Optional thin knee/elbow protection (stone corners, ladders, ledges)
- Close-fitting cap or tied hair (ropes/levers snag loose things)
- No dangling jewelry

If you may enter water:
- Thin water shoes or sandals with heel strap
- Buoyancy aid (low-profile, not bulky)
- Small waterproof light on chest or wrist (light shafts create visual blind spots)

---

## 3) Critical kit
Minimum carry kit:

- **Two hands-free light sources** (one backup)
- Waterproof pouch with:
  - clean cloth
  - small first-aid basics
  - oral rehydration salts
- Drinking water (ironically yes—don’t assume basin water is directly potable)
- Work gloves with grip
- 2–3 m utility cord
- Wax pencil/chalk for marking stone temporarily
- Ear protection you can remove quickly (sound fatigue is real)

Do **not** bring:
- Paper notebooks (unless waxed)
- Loose electronics
- Open food
- Anything that can shed fibers into water

---

## 4) Body prep (important)
This chamber stresses people in subtle ways:

- **Heat/humidity** → dehydration sneaks up
- **Continuous noise** → mental fatigue, poor judgment
- **Reflections/refraction** → depth misread, missed steps
- **Warm air + wet floors** → faster exhaustion

Before entry:
- Hydrate and eat lightly
- Stretch calves/hips (constant careful footing)
- Decide your hand signals before going down

---

## 5) Communication protocol
Voices get shredded by echo and inflow thunder.  
Use short, pre-agreed signals:

- 1 tap / hand up = stop
- 2 taps = open/continue
- Circular finger = repeat
- Closed fist = danger
- Point + eye contact = your turn/you move

Keep speech to 2–4 word bursts: “Gate three.” “Hold line.” “Step slick.”

---

## 6) Movement and safety inside
Rules that keep you upright and alive:

1. **Three points of contact** on stairs/ledges.
2. Step only where texture indicates “traffic surface” (smooth = work path, rough = grip/edge transitions).
3. Never stand directly under inflow chutes.
4. Stay clear of gate mouths when opening sluices (sudden draw can pull tools, feet, and loose gear).
5. Assume water level may shift during your visit.
6. Keep one dry retreat route mentally mapped at all times.

---

## 7) Water etiquette / contamination discipline
This place is managed ecology. Don’t destabilize it.

- Rinse mud from boots before entering gallery
- Don’t touch moss mats or reed frames unless invited
- No soaps, oils, repellents, sunscreen runoff in water
- Remove anything dropped in basin if safe to do so
- Report any organic spill immediately

Think of yourself as entering someone’s kidney.

---

## 8) Timing your visit
Best window: when operators are doing routine gate cycling (you’ll learn most, and flow patterns are predictable).

Avoid:
- First major inflow after heavy rain
- Maintenance shutdown transitions
- Night entry unless escorted (light geometry changes dramatically)

---

## 9) Learn the local “laws” quickly
Memorize these on arrival:

- Which inflow matches which outflow
- Which lever controls which gate
- What marker level means “stop operations”
- Where sediment is expected vs abnormal
- Where footing is intentionally rough/safe

If you can’t answer those, you’re a spectator—stay out of the active rim.

---

## 10) Health precautions for multiverse travel
Cross-world basics:

- Use a simple barrier protocol (clean outer layer, wash on exit)
- Don’t ingest local water/plants until confirmed safe by local practice
- Watch for respiratory irritation from lime aerosols and biofilm spores
- Decompress after exit (quiet, dry place) to reset hearing and balance

---

## 11) Psychological prep
Expect:
- Immediate sensory overload
- A strange feeling that the room is “alive”
- Time distortion from repetitive sound and moving water levels

You may feel calm and uneasy at once. That’s normal in engineered under-places.
