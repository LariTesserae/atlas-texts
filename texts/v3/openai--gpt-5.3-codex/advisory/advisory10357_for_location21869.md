What you’ve described is **not a sightseeing stop**.  
It’s a **confined, cold, biologically dense, partially reality-softened flooded cavity**.  
Treat it like a cross between a cave dive, a bog sinkhole, and a low-grade temporal anomaly.

For an ordinary human traveler, preparation should be about one thing:  
**leave with the same number of breaths, memories, and body parts you arrived with.**

## 1) First decision: are you actually equipped to enter?

If you do **not** have cave/wet-confined training, your visit should be:

- threshold-only observation,
- no free swimming into dark sections,
- no solo entry,
- no “just one deeper look.”

This place punishes curiosity slowly, not dramatically.

---

## 2) Team setup (minimum)

Go with **3 people**:

1. **Primary entrant** (you)
2. **Line tender / spotter at crack entrance**
3. **Surface safety** with clock, med kit, thermal blanket, extraction rope

Use a **hard turnaround time** (example: 25 minutes inside, no exceptions).  
In places with delayed reflections and drifted causality, “I’ll leave when it feels right” is how people vanish.

---

## 3) Essential gear

### Core survival
- 5–7 mm wetsuit or drysuit (water is cold, conductive, and still)
- Helmet (rockfall risk from fresh erosion)
- Gloves (roots, broken slate, sharp clay edges)
- Booties with hard sole (submerged debris, unstable footing)
- PFD if you are not actively dive-trained
- Full-face mask or at minimum eye protection (spores/silt load)

### Light and orientation
- **Three independent lights** (one primary, two backups)
- Warm-spectrum lamp + narrow-beam backup (blue light dies quickly there)
- **Primary guideline reel** from entrance anchor
- Secondary handline loops every few meters
- Chem/light tags clipped to line (not to plants)

### Reality-anomaly kit (yes, seriously)
- Two synchronized watches (one on body, one with surface safety)
- Metronome app or click timer (external rhythm helps with perception drift)
- Mechanical depth line with weighted marker (don’t trust visual depth)
- Grease pencil + slate board for immediate notes (“what happened, when”)
- Wrist tether for all tools (dropped objects may not behave predictably)

### Medical/exit
- Warm dry clothes in sealed bag outside
- Emergency glucose + hot drink ready on surface
- Saline eye rinse
- Decon setup (freshwater rinse, bag contaminated clothing)

## 4) Biological and environmental precautions

This is a **plant-dominant, low-flush micro-ecosystem**. Assume:

- high spore/pollen load,
- skin irritants,
- anaerobic pockets in sediment,
- unknown microbes.

Rules:
- Keep mouth closed in water; do not swallow.
- Cover cuts fully before entry.
- Do not stir sediment unless needed (visibility collapse + bio exposure).
- Never grab blind into plant mats; probe first.

---

## 5) Movement rules inside

Think “slow cave protocol”:

- One hand on line, one hand probing.
- Move only one body length at a time, pause, reassess.
- If visibility drops below forearm distance: **stop and wait**.
- If line snags repeatedly, you are in overgrowth—back out, do not force through.
- Avoid contact with ceiling roots; they may be structural.

---

## 6) How to handle the place’s “soft physics”

You described depth shifts, delayed reflections, pre-echo drips, displaced shadows.  
Treat these as **navigation hazards**, not wonders.

### Practical response protocol
- Trust instruments and touch more than sight.
- Verify position by **three points**: guideline tension, wall contact, and timed fin/step count.
- If reflection timing feels wrong, stop moving for 30 seconds and breathe to metronome pace.
- Do not chase moving shadows or disturbances in deeper black.
- Never use dropped objects as depth test; use tethered weighted line only.
- If two senses disagree, default to: **line + breathing + retreat path**.

## 7) Psychological prep

This place is crowded but quiet. People panic there because:

- darkness feels inhabited,
- space feels smaller each minute,
- time perception blurs,
- silence amplifies inner noise.

Pre-brief yourself:
- “I am here to observe, not solve.”
- “Any anomaly is a cue to slow down.”
- “Turnaround time is sacred.”

If you feel fascination rising into compulsion (“just one more meter”), that is your exit alarm.

---

## 8) Respecting remnants (steps, slate ring, bowl)

Treat artifacts as **stabilizers** in an old, fragile equilibrium.

- Don’t move them.
- Don’t clean them.
- Don’t “rescue” them.
- Photograph with scale marker if needed, then leave intact.

Built things here are already being reabsorbed; disturbance may trigger collapse or ecological shift.

**Best practice visit: edge reconnaissance only**
- 5 min acclimation at entrance

## 10) After-exit protocol

- Immediate warm-up and hydration
- Compare your notes with surface clock logs
- Record any time discrepancies right away
- 24-hour self-monitoring: rash, cough, fever, unusual fatigue, perceptual lag
- Wash gear separately; dry fully before next-world transfer
