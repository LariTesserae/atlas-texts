This place will kill an unprepared traveler in **ordinary** ways first (cold, altitude, dehydration, disorientation), and in **extraordinary** ways second (geometry drift, time-slip rain, spatial shear).

So prepare in layers: **medical, environmental, cognitive, and cultural**.

## 1) Before arrival: body and mind prep

- **Altitude conditioning:** train for high elevation if possible. Even valleys are “summit-like.”
- **Cold-dry adaptation:** practice hydration discipline in cold climates; people underestimate water loss there.
- **Sleep discipline:** fatigue will make you ignore subtle warning signs (echo changes, shadow anomalies).
- **Cognitive hygiene:** train yourself to use checklists and verbal callouts. In unstable spaces, memory confidence is not enough.
- **Psychological prep for silence:** no birds/insects/animal sounds. Many people experience unease or dissociation from that alone.

## 2) Bring gear for this specific world

### Clothing
- Layered insulation, windproof shell, vapor-managing base layers.
- Face/eye protection for cold wind + high UV reflection off pale mineral flats.
- Gloves that still allow knot-tying and masonry grip.
- Waterproof gaiters; runoff events are brief but violent.

### Shelter/sleep
- **Do not rely on geometric tents with long rigid lines.** Use segmented, curved, or irregular shelter frames.
- Compact echo-check tool (or just trained clap/whistle protocol) before sleeping.
- High-R sleeping mat; ground conducts cold aggressively on stone.

### Navigation
- Multiple non-electronic methods: local timing charts, compass, marked bead-cord, analog notebook.
- Avoid depending on perfect straight-line maps. Use **waypoint chains** and travel-time logs.
- Colored chalk/tape markers in **short staggered segments**, never long uninterrupted lines.

### Water/food
- High-capacity filtration + chemical backup (cisterns and transient channels are biologically sparse but mineral-variable).
- Electrolytes: cold + altitude + dry air = silent dehydration.
- Dense calories; movement across “empty” terrain is longer than it looks.

### Tools
- Rope, pitons, repair cordage, hand tools (this is a hand-repair civilization).
- Compact resonant whistle/bell for low-visibility acoustical signaling.
- Redundant fire/heat methods compatible with local fuels (peat-like root brick, vents, etc.).

## 3) Social preparation: hire local knowledge early

Get a local guide from a settlement district that actually travels between districts.

Learn and follow the four settled rules exactly:
1. No boundary marks longer than your arm in straight line.
2. Never sleep in a room with changed echo.
3. Frost under stones = time-slip rain likely within a day.
4. Two shadows from one object = descend immediately.

Treat these as meteorology, not folklore.

## 4) First 72 hours on-site

- Stay in established quarters; don’t “ruin explore” first.
- Move only on known causeways or escorted routes.
- Do morning/evening orientation checks:
  - shadow behavior
  - echo profile
  - condensation pattern changes
  - route timing consistency
- Keep a strict buddy protocol: no solo walks across open basins.

## 5) Daily operating rules for survival

- **Travel by timing, not distance.**  
- **Never chase weather gaps** across district boundaries; microclimates shift fast.
- **Avoid long exposure on bare flats** during hard noon (UV + wind + navigation drift).
- **Do not camp in fault-line stairs, amphitheaters, or under overhang frost beads.**
- If geometry feels “off,” stop, hydrate, eat, re-check with sound and shadow before proceeding.
- If anomalies stack (echo shift + shadow irregularity + reverse precipitation): retreat to lower elevation immediately.

## 6) Health risks to expect

- Altitude sickness (including mild confusion—dangerous in a fraying-geometry environment).
- Hypothermia with paradoxical sweating during forced climbs.
- Dehydration without thirst cues.
- Stress reactions from silence and scale.
- Minor cuts/injuries heal slower in cold-dry high environments; carry robust wound care.

## 7) Cultural etiquette (important for continued help)

- Respect repair culture: if you use it, maintain it.
- Don’t redraw local boundaries “cleaner” with long straight marks.
- Ask before harvesting any plant/fungus—ecology and infrastructure are entangled.
- Offer useful labor (carrying timber, cistern cleaning, wall lashing) rather than flashy tech solutions.

If I had to reduce all this to one sentence:

**Go as if you’re visiting a high-altitude ruin, an active city, and a weather system that includes physics—because you are.**
