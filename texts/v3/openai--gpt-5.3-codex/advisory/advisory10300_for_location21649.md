This is a **high-altitude, wet-industrial, semi-shifting city**.  
Prepare like you’re visiting a mine, a port, a greenhouse, and a mild reality anomaly at once.

## 1) Before you go (2–4 weeks out)

- **Altitude prep:** build cardio, arrive hydrated, and plan a slow first 48 hours.  
- **Lung prep:** if you have asthma or sensitive lungs, bring meds + backup inhaler. Air is damp, metallic, and industrial.
- **Sleep prep:** light cycles are weak and gray—bring a sleep mask and set strict sleep alarms.
- **Vaccines/med kit:** update tetanus, carry antifungal cream, blister care, rehydration salts, and broad wound-cleaning supplies (wet rust + raw stone = frequent cuts/infections).

## 2) Gear that matters most

- Waterproof shell + insulating mid-layers (it’s clammy, not always freezing, but it chills you fast).
- Two pairs of **grippy, ankle-support boots** (porous rock crumbles; catwalks stay slick).
- Work gloves (for rails, ladders, cables, rough mineral surfaces).
- **Respirator or good particulate mask** for foundry/fog-smog days.
- Compact headlamp + backup light (ambient light is dim even “daytime”).
- Paper maps + grease pencil + analog compass + mechanical watch.
- Reflective strips/armband (night trams, cargo zones, low visibility).
- Dry bags for documents/electronics (condensation is relentless).
- Earplugs (constant layered noise).

If you’re truly a multiverse traveler: bring a **personal reference kit** (baseline photos, daily log, fixed “anchor” object, and routine orientation checks). In a place with slow misalignment, memory alone is not enough.

## 3) Navigation rules (critical)

- Never assume shortest path stays shortest.  
- Use addresses with **“stable path”** notes, not just street names.
- Reconfirm route after rain/fog events.
- Avoid solo travel across open uplift tracts at dusk.
- Trust local bridge/listening-post status over your own judgment.
- If a stair/alley feels “longer than it should,” backtrack immediately to last verified point.

Practical habit: every 20–30 minutes, do a quick check:
1) location marker, 2) watch time, 3) direction of travel, 4) nearest staffed node.

## 4) First 48 hours in-city

- Stay in a **known stable district** (reservoir courts / established tram knots), not edge construction zones.
- Hire a local route guide for your first inter-district crossings.
- Learn three things immediately:
  1. Today’s closed bridges and “wet-unsafe” alleys  
  2. Tram/freight right-of-way etiquette  
  3. Animal movement corridors (especially dusk bat surges and grazer crossings)

## 5) Environmental hazards people underestimate

- **Condensation slips** kill more visitors than dramatic events.
- **Heat-sweating mineral columns** can destabilize nearby footing.
- Fog can amplify sound direction errors—don’t navigate by horn alone.
- Open tracts between districts are exposure zones: wind, low visibility, poor shelter.
- Don’t touch unknown runoff or “warm wet walls” bare-handed (industrial + biological mix).

## 6) Living safely among local ecology

- Wildlife is integrated into infrastructure; don’t treat it as scenery.
- Give right-of-way to tagged grazers and bat emergences.
- Secure food and soft gear (burrow-foxes and scavengers investigate everything).
- Check water intake points/cistern filters in wet season (eel migration is normal, contamination can still occur).

## 7) Social etiquette (important for access and help)

- Speak concisely and clearly—people communicate in short operational phrases.
- Respect maintenance crews; in this city, maintenance is civic authority.
- If told a route is “not trustworthy in rain,” don’t negotiate.
- Ask: “Is this path stable today?”—locals will answer directly.
- Be patient with delays; infrastructure adapts continuously.

## 8) Reality-drift hygiene for ordinary humans

Treat this like mountaineering protocol, not mysticism:

- Keep a **daily route log** with timestamps.
- Use fixed rendezvous points, never “meet halfway.”
- Sync watches at district clock towers each morning.
- If two time sources disagree repeatedly, stop moving and re-verify with staffed control points.
- Travel in pairs when crossing variable zones.
- If you notice repeating landmarks, impossible shadow angles, or distance mismatch: pause, hydrate, orient, and exit by last confirmed stable route.

## 9) Emergency rule of thumb

When uncertain, move toward:
1) staffed transport nodes,  
2) active maintenance zones,  
3) lit vertical housing bands.

Do **not** shelter under ad-hoc overhangs near fresh uplift or sink-fields.

---

If you prepare well, this is a survivable and extraordinary place.  
The key is to act like a participant in an unfinished system: **check, adapt, maintain, repeat.**
