The world is choked by wet, a clinging heat that beads on the impossible smoothness of the landscape. It is not water in the familiar sense, though it acts like it — a solvent, a medium, a constant presence. It sheens across surfaces, collects in shallow depressions, is always on the verge of dripping, of running, though where it could run *to* is unclear. There is no horizon, only a shifting, grey-black ceiling of cloud that seems to press the world down.

The ground is of a dark, obsidian-like material, polished by the pervasive wetness to a mirror sheen. It feels warm under the infrequent footfalls, almost…alive. Up close, details resolve: the dark surface is not perfectly uniform. Veins of lighter grey, almost white, run through the material like riverbeds. It fractures, too, though in a way that feels organic, like a vast, petrified organism that's been broken open. There's no dust, no grit, no loose material. Everything is locked in place, smoothed and worn by the ceaseless, condensing moisture.

There is *nothing* of the familiar sort that we call life. No plants, no trees, no grass, no fungi. Yet, the air hums with unseen activity. The dominant fauna appears as a shifting, shimmering tapestry of light and shadow, seen primarily in rapid ripples and distortions. These are, by far, the dominant presence. They flicker across the obsidian sea, filling the emptiness with a frenetic energy. They exist in bewildering variety: sometimes they are just moving highlights on the slick surface of the ground, sometimes they form elaborate, quasi-geometric patterns. More rarely (or perhaps, only *seemingly* rarely), they coalesce into larger forms, vast and indistinct shapes that roam the obsidian plains. The creatures are always in motion, they seem drawn to the moisture.

The cities are constructed from the same obsidian-like foundation, though their form suggests an industrial power. They are skeletal, vast structures of interlocking girders and plates, that rise up to meet the lowering sky. Great, open shafts and cavities pierce their heights. They are linked not by roads but by wide, deep channels that run across the surface of the world, filled with a viscous, oily fluid. Some of the cities are active, belching plumes of steam and dense, grey fog. Some are silent, dark, and still. The machinery within is not visible, but the impression is of a world that once *thrummed* with purpose. Now, only a sense of endless potential remains.

The air is relatively quiet. The sounds of industry are intermittent: the hiss of steam, the clanking of metal, the deep, guttural thrum of… something… coming from deep within the city structures. These intermittent sounds are accompanied by the ever present sound of moisture, the tiny patter of condensation, the soft sighs of the wind.

The scale of the place is vast. The cities reach for the sky, and the plains stretch on without end. There's an established order to the world, a balance. No obvious signs of decay, of collapse, only the slow, inexorable process of weathering and change. The overwhelming fauna are a constant factor, changing and drifting, but never disappearing. The world seems to have found a stable, if strange, equilibrium. There is a sense of immense antiquity, of cycles measured in geologic time. This world has *always* been here, in its wet, obsidian immensity.
