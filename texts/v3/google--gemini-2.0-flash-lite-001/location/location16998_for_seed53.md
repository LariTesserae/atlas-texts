The Whispering Cairn. That's what the wind calls it, though the wind itself is just a concept here, a subtle pressure against the face, a promise of cold through the dense mineral fabric of the place.

You arrive from above, a long descent through a sky so bright it seems to actively hum with light. A single, unseen star — the only star, apparently — provides all the brilliance this world requires. It bathes everything in a cold, hard glare. The cairn sprawls below, a chaotic collection of structures clinging to the steep slopes of a single, colossal mountain. It’s hard to tell where the mountain *ends*, and the cairn *begins*. The two are inextricably linked.

The landscape is built of a dark, obsidian-like material, shot through with veins of a pale, milky substance that seems to glow faintly from within. There are no plants, no obvious life forms, yet a sense of subtle activity pervades. Erosion has carved the mountain into impossible shapes, not sharp or jagged, but *worn*; smoothed and rounded by forces that defy easy understanding. The “settled-form” parameter is strong here — this place has existed for an age, and the slow, inexorable hand of time has shaped it.

The cairn itself is a labyrinth, a maze built on top of and within the mountain. The structures are *scattered*; individual buildings, archways, and towers. They vary in size, from small, cramped dwellings to impossible, cathedral-like spaces that open into the heart of the mountain. All of them are built of the same dark material, marked by those milky veins, and all are subtly *weird*.

The “density” parameter is also strong. Even in the seemingly open spaces, the presence of the cairn is *everywhere*. Structures lean against each other, nearly touching. Paths wind through a close-knit weave of shapes and forms. The air, despite its coldness, feels compressed, saturated with the presence of things – with the memory of the builders, perhaps.

The "primitive" technology is difficult to define. There are no tools or machines to be seen, yet the construction is clearly the work of intelligence. The structures appear to have been perfectly joined and carved: with nothing but hand-tools, the builders must have devoted their lives to its creation. The "hand-tools" are not present, nor are the builders, but the workmanship is present everywhere. The only hint of technology might be a faint, irregular network of lines etched on the surfaces of the structures, as if something has *worn them down* in predictable lines. The result is the same as if the builders had used, perhaps, some advanced form of template-based carving.

Water is a rarity. The air is "damp" in the sense that there is always a slight condensation on the surfaces of the mountain and the structures, but the liquid never seems to form pools or streams. The source of this water is unknown. Perhaps it is a product of the mountain itself.

There is *no* discernible fauna. The "no animal life" parameter is absolute.

Sound is "mostly quiet," but punctuated by the wind’s subtle whispers. The wind seems to *speak*, though it carries no recognizable words. Instead, it seems to murmur of the mountain's history, the actions of its builders, the secrets buried deep within its heart.

The "dynamic" aspects of the world are subtle, yet ever-present. The mountain is *alive*, one could argue. Its surface is constantly shifting, changing, being worn down by the same inexplicable forces that shaped it in the first place. The cairn seems to grow and shrink, shift and change, in response to these forces.

This is a place of memory, of loss, maybe of longing. The builders are gone, but their structures endure. They speak of a life lived, a purpose fulfilled, though the specifics remain a mystery. The Whispering Cairn is the echo of a forgotten civilization and the memory of something beautiful. It is an impossible monument, a puzzle box built from a dying star's light, and it is waiting to be explored.
