The world is a tapestry woven from obsidian and water. Not the liquid water of a familiar planet, but something stranger: a slick, mobile, almost-sentient substance that coats every surface in a pulsing sheen. It clings to the colossal, crystalline structures that dominate the landscape, a liquid skin over the city-sized geometries that stretch to the horizon. These structures, born from some impossible technology, are not merely built, but grown, their surfaces etched with glyphs that shift and rearrange in the constant, deafening sonic maelstrom.

Vegetation is non-existent as we understand it, replaced by a crystalline growth. These structures erupt from the obsidian substrate, but they’re not plants. They don't absorb light. Instead, they seem to conduct the very sound that pervades the atmosphere, channeling sonic energy into themselves and giving off no observable life signs. They are a different kind of “vegetation” altogether, a mineral analogue, perhaps, or something that is not vegetation at all but a byproduct of the ambient, titanic engineering.

The temperature is warm, even oppressive, like being inside a colossal, humming engine. The air hangs thick and heavy, saturated with a moisture that seems to emanate from the ground itself. The elevation is low, a vast, flat plain punctuated by the colossal structures. There are no hills, no mountains, just this endless plain of obsidian and water and crystalline spires.

Erosion, in any conventional sense, is absent. The scale of everything is vast, continental, but not static. This is a city built on the cusp of some unimaginable physics. It is the physics of sound expressed as architecture, of water as a conscious material, of a constant state of becoming and dissolving. The crystalline structures, while seemingly permanent, are in a constant state of minute alteration, shifting and reshaping, like ice sculptures in a gale. Each moment it’s new, old, and always.

Density is total. Every possible space is occupied, saturated. The obsidian plains are a tightly packed lattice of crystalline structures, shimmering water, and an abundance of fauna you may never see. 

Built is too simple a word. This is not construction, but integration. The colossal structures are an inherent part of the landscape. They seem to grow from the very bedrock, their purpose and origin unknown, yet intrinsically connected to the strange and chaotic atmosphere.

Technology, if it can be called that, is beyond modern. It is not about machines, but about principles so alien that they defy attempts at description. This society has mastered the manipulation of the sonic spectrum, the integration of physical and immaterial forms, the control of forces we do not even know exist. This tech shapes the world, the air, the water, and everything. 

The light is normal, but the density and scale of the structures cast deep shadows that crawl across the plains. At times, the crystalline structures seem to absorb all light, becoming absolute voids in the landscape. At others, they pulse with an inner luminosity, bathing the surroundings in strange, shifting hues.

Fauna is abundant, but utterly unlike anything you might expect. These are creatures of sound incarnate, forms sculpted by the sonic torrent. There are the shimmers, fleeting beings of pure light that dance through the crystalline structures. The hummers, vast, winged creatures whose bodies are resonant cavities, emitting a constant, throbbing drone. The crawlers, serpentine forms composed of the obsidian material and the shifting water, that tunnel beneath the plain, leaving shimmering trails in their wake. And there are others, unseen and perhaps unseeable, creatures that exist only within the sonic landscape, their forms manifesting only as distortion and pressure waves.

Weirdness is a constant. This place bends the rules, and twists them to suit some unseen master.

The sound is deafening, an unending cacophony of overlapping frequencies and pulses. It’s the sound of the crystalline structures resonating, of the fauna calling, of the very air itself vibrating. There are no silences, only varying degrees of intensity. The sound can become so intense that it seems to solidify the air, making movement difficult, or so refined that it can be felt physically, a gentle caress or a violent push. 

The dynamism is overwhelming. It is a world in constant flux, a turbulent ocean of creation and destruction. Structures rise and fall, shift and reform. The sonic environment shifts, creating new pathways, opening new chasms. The fauna adapts, evolves, and dies in a perpetual dance of change. This is a place where nothing is permanent, where every moment is a fleeting glimpse of an impossible reality. It is a place both beautiful and terrifying, a world built on sound, water, and the very essence of becoming.
