The sun bleeds across the sky, a smear of blinding gold that seems to hang closer than it should. The air shimmers, thick and heavy, a humid embrace that tastes of minerals and something else… something akin to the metallic scent of blood, though there's no blood here, at least not in the sense of living things. This, more than anything else, is a place of *absence* of things that ought to be, a place punctuated by their echoes.

The land stretches out. From the high elevation, it reveals a vast, rolling panorama. Ridges of obsidian-like rock, carved by some long-forgotten force, reach towards the horizon. There's a persistent breeze that whispers across the landscape, but it carries little in the way of sound. The wind seems to *taste* of the silence, a low hum that vibrates deep in your bones, a counterpoint to the relentless glare of the sun. This is a place that feels *old*, worn down over vast timescales, yet somehow still in vibrant motion. Nothing seems to stand still.

Water is everywhere, although it takes forms that are often deceptive. Pools of iridescent liquid shimmer on the low ground, reflecting the sun in impossible geometries. It's not the water one expects, not H2O. It seems… thicker, denser. Sometimes it takes the shape of towering, slow-moving columns that rise and fall, as if breathing. Moisture condenses in the air, forming a perpetual, shimmering haze, a liquid veil that blurs the edges of distant structures.

There are structures here, the remnants of a city of impossible scale, arranged in ways that seem alien and organic at the same time. The buildings were crafted from obsidian, iron, and a third, unknown material, one that seems to change color with the light, shifting through all the colors of the spectrum. The architecture is impossible, soaring arches that defy gravity, intricate lattices that twist and turn, colossal engines of unknown purpose, their surfaces scarred by the centuries. The style is not a human style, there are no references or clear parallels: it is a language lost to the ages. There are no signs of inhabitants, the city is abandoned, but well-preserved.

There is no *green* here. No trees, no grasses, no plants of any kind that I can recognize. Instead, the landscape is punctuated by strange, fungal growths in shades of crimson, violet, and gold. They do not appear to grow *up* from the ground: they hang from impossible structures, they float in the shimmering water, they pulse with an inner light, as though the city is alive, and these are its organs. Their function is unknowable: are they filters? Energy storage? A byproduct of the planet’s reaction with the atmosphere? The answer is lost to time.

The abundant fauna is strange. Creatures abound, though not in the way one might expect. There are gigantic crystalline insects, their wings reflecting the sun like a kaleidoscope, moving in great swarms. They gather at the edges of the fungal growths, absorbing something they would seem to need. They move and fly with no sound. There are colossal, serpentine forms that slither through the liquid lakes, their bodies iridescent, leaving wakes of shimmering disturbances behind them. They give off the humming, low-frequency sound that seems to pervade the silence of the land.

This is a place of industry, a place where the processes of a vast machine still run even in darkness, in a solar system with no sun. Great gears grind somewhere beneath the surface, feeding the machinery of the abandoned city, or perhaps transforming the landscape into something new, something *other*. The place is actively in motion, whether by will or by some other force.

This is a strange place, a place of broken echoes and hidden purpose, a place of sun and silence and secrets, a place where the absence of life screams louder than any living thing.
