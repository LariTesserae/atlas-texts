The world is a vast, low-lying plane, stretching to the horizon in every direction. It is a world of pure, manufactured substance, a landscape entirely of construction. It feels like what a world would be if it were only ever assembled, never grown, never eroded. 

The ground is a mosaic of massive, perfectly flat plates. They are laid in a repeating pattern, seemingly of some incredibly durable, dark, charcoal-grey material. The plates are segmented by narrow, deep channels. These channels are the only indication of the "coming-into-being" of the world; they are not eroded, but instead, they are freshly cut, raw, and expose a slightly different color of the same substance: a pale, almost white grey. These channels are the only place that water gathers and is the only hint of moisture; a slow, barely perceptible seepage along their floors.

The air is hot, heavy, and still, though there is a subtle, almost imperceptible drift. This drift is not wind, but the movement of heat. The sky is a normal blue. Light reflects off the plates as if they were water, but instead of the sky, the surface reflects images of the world around it.

There is no animal life. No plants either. The lack of flora adds to the starkness and the feeling of artificiality. There is no decay, no decomposition. The stillness is nearly absolute. The only sounds are the slow drips of the channels and the faint clicks that occasionally echo through the space. These clicks are the only indication of potential built tech, an echo in the world.

The scale of the place is immense, larger than any single observation could contain. This is not a planet: it is something else, something constructed on a scale impossible for human minds. The landscape constantly transforms. The plates shift very slowly, like tectonic plates of a synthetic world. The channels deepen and widen at a pace only perceivable over long periods. Patches of the grey substance "grow" and "shrink", some in a chaotic manner and others with a clear, but inscrutable, intent. Some plates "bloom," releasing puffs of fine dust that hang in the air for a moment before they too, are reabsorbed.

This world is dynamic. At the edges of perception, there is movement. The rules are clear: the world is a construction, the world is active, the world is without tools. It is a monument, but a monument to what? To whom? What grand project is this? Or is it a tomb?
