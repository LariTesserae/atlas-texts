The world is a bubble, a perfect sphere of impossible brightness, cradled in a sea of thick, warm water. It's a sphere of obsidian walls, its surface utterly smooth and unbroken. There are no clouds, no gaps in the dark, polished surface, no doorways, no windows. And yet, light spills from it, in a torrent so pure it borders on painful. There is no sun, only the unending illumination of the sphere itself.

The water around the island is an active place. Currents twist and churn, driven by unseen forces. These forces are the rhythm of this pocket universe. There's no land to speak of, only the sphere and the water. The whole world seems to be suspended in constant, deafening motion, a high-pitched, oscillating tone that vibrates through the water, pressing against anything that might try to exist here. The sound is almost physical, a pressure that builds, then releases, then builds again.

There are structures, though they are difficult to perceive at first. They are made of a porous, black material that seems to absorb the light around them, making them nearly invisible against the obsidian of the sphere. Closer inspection will reveal they are not natural formations. They are woven, complex. Primitive, too. The patterns suggest a kind of order, an intent, though it's hard to discern the purpose behind them. They might be webs, or fishing nets, or even massive windbreaks. The impression is of something made to strain or filter the water.

There is no vegetation in the traditional sense, but the water is not completely barren. Strange, fibrous strands drift through the currents. These strands are made of the same dark material as the structures. As they move, the strands become entangled, forming tight, floating masses. 

The heat is oppressive. It radiates from the sphere, it seeps from the water, it is trapped within the enclosed space. The combination of heat and ceaseless turbulence makes the sphere feel smaller and more suffocating than it should.

There are no creatures here. Nothing swims or floats or breathes. Yet the water is not empty. The strands, the structures, some of them are slowly, almost imperceptibly, changing. Some of the strands begin to collect and coalesce around the constructed webs, the filtering structures, becoming thicker, more textured. The black material shifts and transforms.

This is a place of making. A place of endless, directed action. Every element of the environment is born from the other. The sphere provides light, heat, and a substrate. The water provides energy. The strands provide the building blocks. The structures, the webs, they serve as the template, the pattern, for all that grows. The process occurs in a ceaseless loop, an engine churning away out of sight, never failing, always making.
