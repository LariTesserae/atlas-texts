The air hangs thick and warm, a nearly unbearable wet blanket pressed against the skin. Water beads on the rough-hewn stone surfaces, constantly weeping from unseen sources within the rock itself, or perhaps condensing from an atmosphere that seems perpetually on the verge of rain. Not rain, exactly, but a slow, heavy accretion. Overhead, a low ceiling of grey light, barely piercing the gloom, suggests the sun might be present, but veiled. It's impossible to tell.

The landscape is a series of interconnected chambers and corridors, carved from a single, massive boulder of a dark, obsidian-like material. The stone is worn smooth in places, polished by what feels like an eternity of the slow, wet passage of something more substantial than mere water. Scattered across the floors and clinging to the walls are small, crystalline structures. They resemble nothing so much as clusters of tiny, imperfect diamonds, each reflecting the meager light in fragmented glimmers. Sometimes the crystals grow large enough to be described as clusters, other times as strange "veins" that run through the stone.

No plants grow here. Instead, a kind of simple, biological process has taken root throughout the area. Colonies of fungal-like organisms carpet many areas, in colours near the black of the stone, or in the stark brilliance of a blue-white. The structures are not merely surface features; they're integrated with the stone, their presence seemingly vital to the integrity of the architecture. Break away a piece of a crystalline cluster to reveal the dark stone. Touch one just so, and it may dissolve away to nothing.

The architecture is primitive, but not crude. The chambers are not raw caves. They are subtly shaped, with gently curved walls and precisely angled corners. Evidence of tools is rare, but present: the faint scoring of something that once scraped and shaped the stone, and the occasional, near-invisible crack or drill-hole where a tool might have been used within the stone itself.

This feels like a place that has always been, and always will be. The chambers and passageways are dense, packed together, and in the scale of a single interconnected series of rooms. It’s hard to predict exactly how large, but the space feels enclosed, like a series of small cathedrals or connected grottos. There is no sense of movement, no indication that this place has ever changed. The sound is almost completely absent, save for the occasional, quiet drip of water. But sometimes, when one listens closely enough, a subtle pulse permeates, felt through the soles of the feet: life in some form.

The only sign of anything outside of the mineral and the strange fungal-crystalline growth is the rare fauna. Small, semi-transparent creatures, like barely-there jellyfish, drift lazily through the chambers. Their bodies pulse with a faint light, mirroring the crystals and the crystalline structures in the chambers. The creatures seem only to exist in the water.
