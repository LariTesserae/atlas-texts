The light here is not so much absent as it is *contained*. It's a sphere of near-darkness, maybe fifty paces across, cupped in the palm of some impossible hand. The water, a milky jade, fills the sphere completely. It's the same water whether you're at the "surface" or "below", a concept that loses meaning with every breath. The air, too, has a liquid quality, like inhaling a gentle fog.

You are underwater, but the pressure is not what you expect. It's a mild compression, a gentle hug. The cool temperature keeps the jade-water from feeling cold, though after a while, a shiver will creep in. There's so much to *see*, but the darkness absorbs most of it. Details resolve slowly. 

The sphere rests within a vast, unseeable cavern. You can't see the edges of this space; the sphere simply *is*. Its perfection is unsettling. The erosion here is young: the formations within are sharp, the features severe. This also seems to apply to the small, scattered structures you can find here. The architecture is impossibly organic, grown and sculpted from the same jade-water. The structures appear to have been built, or perhaps *grown*, within this contained reality.

Vegetation grows profusely, tendrils and fronds of an impossible green. They twist and tangle, a packed tapestry that seems to occupy every available surface within the sphere. The plants are luminescent, casting a soft, internal light that is the only source of illumination. They pulse gently, a slow, rhythmic thrumming that becomes the primary sound within the space. There's almost no fauna, but occasionally a tiny, bioluminescent something might dart across your vision, too fast to properly identify.

The "ground," if you can call it that, is a mesh of entangled roots and glowing moss. It seems to shift and undulate almost imperceptibly with the slow tide. Gravity, though present, is weak, making movement dreamy and weightless. The few scattered artifacts or structures seem to grow from the "ground." They're not placed, but *arisen*. 

The rules of this place are not firm. Things shift, and the reality that contains the sphere shifts with it. The first sign of the *weirdness* is a shimmering effect on the surface of the jade-water. You might catch a glimpse of something beyond, a distortion of impossible colors and geometries. Sometimes, the structures and plants change, morphing between known forms and impossible ones.

There's a sense that time here is distorted. Cycles are slow, the changes gradual. The pulse of light and sound is constant, a reminder that something, *everything*, is alive. It's a sanctuary, of sorts. Everything is preserved within that cool, perfectly-compressed space. The silence, when it occasionally breaks from the thrumming, is more profound than any noise could be.
