The city is built into the side of a mountain, a mountain still young enough that the raw stone bleeds through the edges of the construction. The rock is a deep, bruised purple, shot through with veins of silver that gleam in the perpetual twilight. The air is cool, heavy with the scent of wet stone and blooming fungi, and a constant, gentle drizzle mists the angular streets.

Water trickles down the mountain face, channeled into stone aqueducts that snake through the city, feeding hanging gardens and moss-covered walls. The vegetation clings tenaciously to every available surface – ferns, thick carpets of vibrant green moss, and strange, luminous fungi that pulse with a soft, internal light. The plants thrive in the damp, cool air, but their growth is contained, almost sculpted, as if the entire city is a meticulously tended terrarium.

The architecture is a marvel of primitive ingenuity. Stone blocks, precisely cut and fitted, are mortared with a mixture that seems to bind to the rock itself. Buildings are tiered, stacked, and interwoven, clinging to the slope like a colony of giant, geometric fungi. Wooden scaffolding, woven from thick, strong vines and secured with carved stone clamps, still crisscrosses the city, evidence of ongoing construction and maintenance. The city feels…complete, yet still being made.

The light is dim, filtered through the perpetual mist and the dense canopy of interwoven buildings and vegetation. It’s enough to see, but shadows cling to every corner, obscuring the details and hinting at hidden passages and half-finished projects. Here and there, windows glow with the warm, golden light of oil lamps, casting dancing shadows on the wet stone. 

The sounds are deceptively quiet. A constant drip-drip-drip of water is the background of everything, punctuated by the rhythmic thud of a stone-cutter’s mallet, the call of a bird (a flash of iridescent blue against the purple rock), and the rustling of the wind through the hanging gardens. But beneath the surface, there's a hum, a low thrum that vibrates through the stone, as if the mountain itself is breathing.

Fauna is abundant, but rarely seen. Small, nimble creatures with iridescent scales dart through the shadows, their presence felt rather than seen. Birdsong is plentiful, but the birds themselves are elusive; flashes of colour against the dull stone. And in the deeper shadows, there might be other things moving, things with eyes that glint, things that are better left unseen.

The city is built around a central, open plaza, a wide, flat space carved entirely from the mountain. It serves as a communal gathering area, a place for trade and meeting, but also as a place to observe the unchanging sky. Time seems suspended here. There are no seasons, no weather, no decay. The city is in a state of perfect stasis, its growth and construction frozen at the cusp of completion.

The rules are simple, and immutable. The city is a closed ecosystem. Everything is recycled; water, waste, and even the building materials themselves. Everything is designed to last forever. There are no signs of a society, or even many people. It feels like an unfinished project, a monument to a unknown purpose. Each dwelling is meticulously crafted, appearing to have never been touched by human hands. It almost feels like the city is growing itself.

And then there's the weirdness. The silver veins in the rock pulse with a faint, internal light, mirroring the glow of the fungi. Occasionally, a section of wall will shift, subtly rearranging itself, as if the buildings are growing, not through construction, but through some kind of slow, geological metamorphosis. The plants themselves seem to respond to the city’s rhythms, growing in patterns that mirror the architecture. There is no explanation for any of it, no way to understand how the city functions. It just *is*. And it will always *be*.
