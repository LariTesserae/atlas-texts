The world is a geode, or perhaps a collection of them. They are nested, one within the next, and this particular pocket is small enough to hold in your hands. But it is not *small*. It is merely *contained*, a single feature in a vast, unknowable system.

The rock of the geode is obsidian, newly formed. Jagged edges glint in the normal light. Tiny rivulets of water, warmed by an unseen heat, carve paths through the glassy surface. The water is everywhere, a film on the stone, condensing in the air, a constant presence that never quite accumulates enough to form a pool. It is a wet heat, a suffocating humidity that clings to everything.

There are no plants, but the rock itself seems alive. Veins of shimmering metals run through the obsidian, pulsing with a faint inner light. These are not veins, not really, but something like channels of energy. The rock *breathes*, expanding and contracting almost imperceptibly, in time with a rhythm only the fauna seem to understand.

The silence is deceptive. It is not an absence of sound, but a *hiding* of it, a compression. You can feel the air vibrate, you can *see* the potential of noises hanging just out-of-reach, but you cannot quite *hear*. If you press your ear to the rock, however, you can hear it: a low, resonant hum, a chorus of clicks and whispers that build and fade in cycles.

The fauna are the dominant presence. They are small, almost microscopic, but their numbers are incalculable. They swarm, a living tide that rolls over the obsidian. They are primarily arthropods, but not as you know them. They have exoskeletons of polished metal, iridescent in the light, and bodies that seem to blur the line between organic and inorganic. They are built with primitive tools. Some are born with them, as parts of their very bodies. Others craft them from fallen flakes of rock, tiny shovels and picks and drills. They are ceaselessly, feverishly, *working* the stone. They are the engine of this place, the reason for the heat, the constant motion, the wetness.

These creatures, these swarming workers, are not the only fauna. There are others, larger, rarer, apex predators that move through the swarms in a smooth, oily grace. These are built of the same materials, but their forms are fluid, almost liquid, capable of squeezing through the tightest crevices, of dissolving into the rock itself. They feed on the swarms, but they also *are* the landscape. They shape it, they refine it, they are connected to it at every level.

Reality is unstable here. The laws of physics are bent and stretched. Gravity is not a constant, but a suggestion. Time flows unevenly, sometimes speeding up, sometimes slowing down. The obsidian shifts and changes, the light flickers, and the very air feels thick with possibility. It is as if the raw ingredients of matter are constantly being assembled and disassembled, in a grand alchemy that is driven by the will of the fauna, the whims of the predators, and the hidden forces that pulse through the bedrock. This is a place of creation, a place of destruction, a place of endless becoming.
