The world is a single, immense tree, or perhaps a collection of trees so thoroughly interwoven they behave as one. Its colossal trunk, a porous cathedral of woody tissue, rises from the low ground, which is less a ground than a vast, shallow plate of saturated, fibrous root. This root-plate is the "city," in a sense: an urban sprawl of the organic, a network of tunnels and chambers, waterways and bridges, all crafted from the tree's own substance.

The “damp” of the water rating is a constant. The air is thick with condensation, and the interior spaces of the tree-city are perpetually slick with moisture. Rain is not so much a phenomenon as a state of being, a gentle, diffuse downpour that comes and goes, or rather, ebbs and flows, within the living structure. Small “pockets of water” collect in the cup-shaped leaves that form the city's roofs, and in the intricate networks of tunnels that wind through the wood.

"Total" vegetation is the only thing here. Every surface, every form, is plant. The massive trunk is not just a support. It *is* the building. Roots form walls and floors. Giant, fan-like leaves serve as roofs and awnings. Vines, thick as pythons, twist and writhe, forming bridges, ladders, and sometimes, living cages for unknown purposes. The "plant-as-architecture" is the defining characteristic of this world.

The "warm" temperature manifests as a comfortable, almost tropical humidity that permeates the entire space. It’s never cold, but never truly hot either, just a constant, clinging warmth that makes the wood perpetually soft and yielding. The light is "normal," but filtered through layers of leaves, creating a dappled, shifting kaleidoscope of greens and browns.

The "low" elevation is deceptive. While the city itself resides at a base level, the tree's branches and structures reach impossibly high, a dizzying verticality that extends beyond sight.

"Established" erosion is the law. The tree has stood for time beyond reckoning. Its form is settled, weathered, in balance with the continuous movement and decay of the living tissues. Parts of the city are obviously older than others, but there’s no sense of ruin, only of transformation. Everything shifts, grows, and changes, in a timescale both rapid and glacial. The tree constantly alters its architecture, both building and breaking down.

The "small" scale refers not to size but to human scale. It is a city, but one that could comfortably fit within a human's understanding; a collection of neighborhoods, districts, chambers, and tunnels.

The "empty" density is almost unbelievable. Despite being the center of the world, there are very few beings within the city, or within the tree if you consider the tree to be the world. The vast majority of spaces are hollow, unoccupied, abandoned. This hollowness gives the city a surreal, dreamlike quality, a sense that one is exploring a place that has been, will be, or is just never quite inhabited.

This place is "urban"—the construction is organized, the infrastructure layered. There are distinct districts, each with its characteristics: the spongy root-markets, the hollow trunk-temples, the leaf-draped towers. But there's no visible "tech" at all. This is a purely biological city. All structures, all tools, all systems are from the tree itself. Water channels are grooved within the wood. Leaves regulate temperature. Vines are rope.

"Abundant" fauna is a massive understatement. The deafening sound, the movement, comes from the fauna, a cacophony of creaks, rustles, and chirps. Insects the size of small dogs scuttle down vine-bridges. Flocks of iridescent birds with razor-sharp beaks flit through the airy cathedral. The echoing clatter of unseen animals scurrying through the empty chambers and tunnels fills every space. The place is alive, but in ways that defy easy categorization.

"Reality breakdown" is the nature of the landscape itself. Physics bend to the will of the tree. Spaces fold into each other. Certain passages lead to non-Euclidian geometries. Inside the architecture, reality has a porous quality. You might turn a corner and find yourself in a place that is both ancient and unborn. The tree's core has shifted into different times.

The "deafening" sound is omnipresent. The constant rustling of leaves in the perpetual rain. The creaking of the interwoven branches. The chorus of a million unseen insects. The shrieks of the immense avian predators. The sounds of the place are constantly shifting, blending, and overlapping, creating a symphony that is both beautiful and terrifying. The sounds are alive, a chorus speaking to the tree and the nature of this collapsing reality.

This world is "active," a place in constant motion. The tree grows, shifts, and changes. The fauna relentlessly roams through the labyrinthine streets. The weather is cyclical, with periods of heavy rain, moderate moisture, and brief patches of relative dryness. The predator-prey system is complex and brutal, a continuous struggle for survival. Everything moves, breathes, devours, and decays. This is the truth of the world. It is the tree. It is life. It devours and is devoured, a continuous cycle in an expanding and contracting void.
