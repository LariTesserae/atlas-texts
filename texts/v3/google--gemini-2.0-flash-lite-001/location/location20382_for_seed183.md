The wind here sings a cold, abrasive song through a valley that shouldn’t exist. Above, the sky is a glaring, impossible white, offering no clue to the sun’s location. It simply *is*—the source of a constant, undiluted illumination that defines every edge, every shadow, though there are so few of either. The air, thick with a fine, stinging mist, always tastes of metal and something else, something undefinable, like the ghost of a flavor. 

Elevation warps the reality. This is no gentle climb, but instead a jagged ascent toward a fractured peak, a skeletal spine of black, obsidian-like rock that tears at the sky. Erosion has worked its will here, but it's a slow burn, a patient sculpting of improbable forms: impossible arches, precarious spires, and a landscape littered with what look like petrified waterfalls, their forms frozen in mid-descent. The water… it's everywhere, yet nowhere. It clings to the rock, weeping from every fissure, a constant sheen, and pools in strange, crystalline reservoirs. It doesn't seem to freeze, doesn't seem to follow the dictates of the cool temperature. The water is a being unto itself, flowing upward, the very stones breathing it in and then exhaling.

There's no visible plant life, which should mean a mineral world, but there *are* things. Here and there, dark, iridescent structures, almost like enormous, multi-faceted crystals, rise from the ground. They are not built, not *placed*, but somehow grown or extruded from the rock, and they pulse. Their surfaces shimmer with a chaotic internal light, a complex network of veins or channels that seems to hold the mist and something else, something like captured starlight. The structures shift slowly, sometimes expanding, sometimes collapsing in on themselves. They release a low, resonant hum, a thrumming that vibrates through the very bones. 

The air is *loud*. Not just windy, but alive with a chorus of sounds that defy easy explanation. There’s the constant hiss of the mist, punctuated by what sounds like grinding stone, the echoing *crack* of something splitting, and deep, guttural notes that shift in pitch, like the song of a colossal, unseen creature. Those notes can be heard by *feeling* it just as much as hearing it, the whole valley seems to vibrate to it.

Even with the mist, the place is saturated. You could reach out and touch another world. Where the strange structures are found, the air is thick with the scent of something like petrichor, but sweeter, laced with an echo of electricity. The structures warp perception. Walk through their shadows and the world bends with you. The white sky swirls into a violet dusk, then snaps back into its unwavering blankness.

The human-made element is sporadic: small carvings, etched into the rock faces. They depict no recognizable figures, but a series of overlapping spirals, impossible geometries, and glyphs that might be attempts to capture the landscape itself — to bind its reality. Crude implements, wrought from dark, unknown metals, litter the area, remnants of someone, something, that came here. They are left in careful arrangements, as if in offering, or as markers of some rite.

The wind intensifies, whistling through the rock formations, shaping and reshaping the mist into swirling phantoms. The mist itself begins to coalesce, forming shifting, semi-transparent figures that flicker at the edges of perception. They move in erratic ways, as though lost or disoriented, and the shapes are impossible to pin down, as if the very fabric of reality is unraveling. The structures pulse harder, casting a chaotic, shifting light across the landscape. They seem to be calling to the figures in the mist.
