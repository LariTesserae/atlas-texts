This place breathes water.

High on a plateau, larger than any single mountain, the air is thick and tastes of dissolved minerals and something vaguely metallic. The light is almost shockingly bright, a constant sun-dazzle without a clear sun in the sky. It seems to come from all directions, diffused and pervasive, as if the very air is incandescent.

The ground is a living carpet of emerald and obsidian. Tiny, intricate plants, their leaves like polished scales, form the bulk of the vegetation. The emerald plants grow in dense, interconnected mats. The obsidian plants are scattered amongst them, looking like crystalline slivers that have erupted from the ground itself. Both are wet, constantly weeping a clear fluid that pools and runs over the uneven surface, collecting in unseen capillaries and unseen rivers beneath the surface. Water is everywhere, a constant presence. It condenses on everything, dripping, pooling, and slowly carving its way through the landscape.

Erosion is a gentle sculptor, barely beginning its work. The plateau is new, still in the process of forming. Scattered across the surface are formations that hint at what is to come: shallow canyons barely beginning to bite into the living ground, embryonic ravines that are little more than suggestion, and strange mounds that seem to swell and shift slightly with the slow, internal tides of the place.

The scale is vast. The horizon is a haze of emerald and obsidian, punctuated by the occasional rise of even higher plateaus. Built into this living landscape are the scattered remnants of something industrial. They are not grand structures, but single buildings, some looking like crucibles from which the plants have sprung, others like factories dedicated to making the atmosphere, and rusting machines that push water back to the top of the plateau. Engines, powered by unseen sources, thrum with a relentless, deafening noise that is the soundscape of the place. The sound is not a cacophony, but a single, deep, resonating hum. It is the sound of the world’s breath.

Fauna is rare or well-hidden. The wetness, the strange geometry of the plants, the sheer noise – all conspire to keep creatures scarce. The only sign of life seems to be from the living ground itself.

Strange, faint marks suggest the remnants of industry. They are a testament to former projects. Buildings that may have once served a purpose now seem almost entirely decorative.

This place has no known purpose, no goal. Only its own slow, patient becoming.
