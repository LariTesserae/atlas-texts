The air is thick, a heavy broth of moisture and something else—a perfume of overripe blooms, of loam, of the slow, patient decay of stone. It’s hard to breathe, not from the lack of oxygen, but from the sheer weight of *presence*. Everything here is *something*. Even the nothingness has heft.

This is a world of plants. Not just plants everywhere, but *everything* plant. The air itself feels vegetal, a filtered respiration of unseen, colossal things. Upward, into the dim, perpetual twilight, is a canopy of interlocking forms, a riot of greens and browns and things that defy easy categorization. Some are scaled like plates, others woven into gigantic, fibrous knots. Some are slick with the constant condensation, beaded with droplets that gleam like captured light.

The ground is absent. Or, rather, it *is* the plants. Everything grows upon everything else. At the very bottom – if one could even reach it – the foundation might be petrified wood, smoothed to glassy surfaces by immemorial time. Layer upon layer grows above, roots and stalks and twisting limbs forming a roiling, almost liquid terrain. The "terrain" itself flows in slow motion, a biological version of continental drift, a constant, silent struggle of immensity.

Water is everywhere—not in obvious bodies, but threaded through every part of the world. It seeps, it trickles, it pools in the great hollows carved into the colossal flora. It seeps within the plants' own structures. The air drips, constantly, with a quiet *shushing* of condensation falling. The light filtering through the canopy is perpetually dim, a diffused and attenuated glow. Perhaps there *is* a sun, but if so, it’s a distant thing, a pale ghost of light that rarely reaches the world below.

Scattered amidst the plant mass are bits – a few things that hint at the past. Small, crude constructions of stone. The remnants of some ancient, long-vanished kind. The stone is worked, but the craftsmanship is rudimentary, almost accidental. The stone itself seems to have been absorbed, partially, by the plants, covered in moss, softened at the edges, and integrated into the ecosystem.

And then there are the fauna. Not many, as far as the eye can see. Many things are active in the dimness, but most are hidden, heard, or felt. Things that rustle in the massive leaves of the high canopy. Things that whistle through the tunnels within the great trunks. Things that click and scuttle across the immense floral plains. The air hums with their presence, with a low thrum of life. The creatures themselves, when caught, are all plants… or seem to have *become* plants. 

The sound is mostly quiet, a kind of internal susurrus of the world itself. But at intervals, a sound rises, deep and resonant. A low thrum, or a deep sigh, or a groan that vibrates through your very bones. These sounds are not random. They’re the respiration of the world, the slow, imperceptible shifts of this grand, living body. 

There are rules here, though they are not written or spoken. One must move with the flow, not against it. Be gentle. Be patient. Don’t try to force your will, because the will of this *thing* is much larger than you are. Life, death, and decay are all part of the same vast dance, a continuous cycle. Nothing is truly separate, everything is connected, and the world goes on, indifferent, turning and breathing in its own deep, slow rhythm.
