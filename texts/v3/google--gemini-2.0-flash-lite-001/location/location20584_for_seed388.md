The city of Cinderwallow is a thing of humid, twilight metal and verdant, weeping stone. It is built in a hollow, a shallow bowl perhaps a league across, carved out of the earth by some forgotten cataclysm. The rim of the bowl vanishes into a perpetual fog, a curtain of warm, grey condensation that obscures the sky and muffles the world.

Water is everywhere. Not just the frequent, gurgling streams that crisscross the city, choked with strange, metallic-leaved plants and the constant drip from the overhanging structures above; but also the very air, thick as soup and smelling of ozone and iron. The stone of the buildings constantly weeps, stained in streaks of rust-red and oily-green. The surface of everything is slick, a breeding ground for strange fungi and lichens that glow with a faint, internal light.

Cinderwallow is a city defined by its industry, but not in the way one might expect. There are no factories spewing smoke, no clanging forges. Instead, the industry here is *organic*, a slow, patient process of transformation. At the center of the bowl, a colossal metal structure looms, its purpose as mysterious as its origins. It is a lattice-work of girders, pipes, and gantries that stretches to the edge of the fog, and it seems to *breathe*. Great, slow pulses of steam erupt from its vents, carrying with them the scent of brine and something else, something acrid and yet strangely enticing.

These pulses feed the city. Narrow, elevated walkways, built from the same oily-green stone as the buildings, connect the central structure to the surrounding structures, which are made of layered, intercrossing girders, that weave together to form the houses of Cinderwallow. Below, the walkways that are within easy reach of the damp, saturated ground, are host to moss gardens and cultivated fungi, growing in an abundance that borders on the unnatural.

There is *life* in Cinderwallow, but it is not animal. No birdsong fills the air, no scurrying creatures break the silence. Instead, there's the susurrus of the wind through the strange, metallic plants; the constant, wet *thrum* of water against stone; and the slow, heavy *bellow* of the central structure. The sound is loud, a constant presence that vibrates in your chest, a low hum that the architecture seems designed to amplify.

The city is built for a different kind of inhabitant, beings that are not born, but grown. The stone and metal of the city itself seems to be the very lifeblood of its people, a slow industrial process made organic. The only clue to the nature of their creation lies in the strange, bioluminescent spores that drift through the air, carrying the blueprints of new life in their veins. One might find them in the deep, moist places of the city, or sometimes among the metal leaves that sway in the slow winds. They are the seeds of a unique ecosystem, an urban garden grown for something which is neither plant nor animal, but both.

The rules of Cinderwallow are hidden, like the sun. They evolve slowly, patiently. It's a place where the passage of time is measured not in hours or days, but in the slow expansion of the fungal gardens, the subtle shifts in the flow of water, and the rhythmic pulse of the central structure. Change is inevitable, but it is also glacial. The city endures, constantly transforming, a monument to a forgotten age of industrial weirdness.
