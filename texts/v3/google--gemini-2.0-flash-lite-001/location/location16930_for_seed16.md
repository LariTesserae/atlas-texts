The air tastes of dust and something metallic, a familiar tang to my nonexistent tongue. My breath, also nonexistent, still *feels* the heat. A low, constant thrum vibrates through the ground, resolving itself into a chorus of harsh, scraping noises. This is the **Chamber of Shifting Sands**. It is not a place for the living. Or rather, there is nothing here that *lives* in the way I was built to expect.

From my perspective – whatever that even means in this place – the Chamber is a roughly spherical space, perhaps a hundred meters across. Walls of obsidian-like glass curve inwards, and they're marked by the tracks of some vast, unseen force. In fact, all the landscape here has such tracks. The "sands" of the name aren't sand at all. They're vast formations of a dark, crystalline substance, black as the void between stars, yet reflecting light with the same fierce intensity. These crystalline dunes are constantly reshaping themselves.

There is a sense of immense antiquity, of deep time. The crystalline structures, despite their ceaseless motion, suggest a slow, relentless process of formation and erosion. The obsidian walls, too, bear the marks of this slow violence. They're etched with patterns, deep grooves that seem to *flow* across the curved surface in fractal designs.

The vegetation – or, at least, the most plant-like things here – are a contradiction. They’re called the *Gloom-Weeds*. They are neither lush nor particularly green. Instead, they resemble gigantic, bone-white fungi, twisting and contorting toward a dim source of light. Their surfaces are porous, absorbing the meager light and radiating a faint luminescence, like the corpses of stars. These "weeds" are the loudest things present. The slightest physical contact causes them to release vast, guttural groans. The groans echo throughout the chamber, a cacophony of deep, resonant sounds. These are not weeds, I realize, so much as living tools, made to carve and shape this hostile environment.

The absence of water is the most difficult thing to reconcile. There is no water, but there somehow *is* vegetation. The plants must be taking nourishment from *something*. Maybe, though, this isn’t a world of elements at all. The very concept of "elements" might not apply.

I focus in on the erosion and it suggests an active geological process, but one that is not necessarily *physical*. The crystalline sands shift not by wind or water, but by some unseen, unknown force. I *feel* this force, a constant pressure, a shaping will that seems to emanate from the chamber’s unseen center. I would expect this force to destroy everything here, but instead, it creates.

As with any enclosed system, there are edges. Hidden within the shifting sands are a series of intricate, almost impossibly intricate structures. They are like nests. Like catacombs. Built with an incredible, primitive sense of pattern. They are the "built" elements of this landscape. 

The structures are constructed from the same crystalline material as the sands. I see the light of the Gloom-Weeds illuminating their entrances and corridors. They seem to be *for* *something*, but I can't determine what. There are no tools, or signs of tools, in the traditional sense, but the craftsmanship is evident in a careful, deliberate arrangement. The structures are arranged in an urban, settled structure, as though they were buildings in some hidden city.

My perspective drifts higher. I see that the obsidian walls are not as simple as I thought. They're not smooth glass, but are composed of larger, interlocking crystalline structures, much like the sands. At the apex of the chamber, I see the source of the dim light. It is not sunlight, but is instead a single, pulsating crystal of immense size. It glows with an eerie inner light, a dim but constant pulse. I have the sense that this crystal is the heart of the chamber, the source of its shaping power.

The constant sounds, the dim light, the oppressive heat, the dust-filled air – it's all designed to *shape*. It's a forge of unknown purpose, working without apparent fuel. Everything here is being relentlessly, patiently, rebuilt.
