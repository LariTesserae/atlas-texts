The world *groans*.

It's a sound that's less auditory, more a pressure, a deep thrumming in your bones. It's the sound of the world's guts, the churning of something vast and unknowable. It's the sound of a planet that’s both ancient and in the throes of a perpetual, agonizing birth.

Elevation is a concept here. There are no mountains, no valleys as you'd understand them. Only subtle undulations in the saturated ground, a slow, relentless rise and fall that stretches to the horizon and beyond. This is a landscape worn smooth by eons of torrential rain, a sea of mud and stone. The erosion is complete, not in the sense of 'gone,' but in the sense of 'settled.' Everything has found its place, its rhythm, in the endless cycle of creation and decay. The scale is immeasurable; a continent-sized sponge saturated with warm water – a vast, brackish marsh. You could walk for what feels like centuries and not experience a change in the horizon.

The vegetation is sparse, but not absent. There are skeletal, gnarled trees, their twisted branches reaching towards the perpetually dim sky like the desperate claws of the dead. They're scattered, solitary sentinels, clinging to the slightly higher ground, their roots sunk deep into the saturated earth. Their skin weeps a viscous, oily substance, a substance that refracts the light into impossible colours – a spectrum beyond human sight.

The light itself is strange. It’s a perpetual twilight, a soft, diffused glow that seems to emanate from the very ground beneath your feet. The source of this light is unclear, but it bathes the world in an unnatural, ethereal luminescence. The air hangs thick and heavy with moisture, constantly condensing, dripping… you *feel* the moisture on your skin, a fine layer that never quite dries, that carries the constant, underlying pressure.

There is *something* that is here, at all times. Always churning. Change pours through this place like a river. It is difficult to name what, exactly, is moving and shifting and chaotic. Though it's *packed*, and seemingly everything present is material, there’s no clear boundary between things. Parts of the earth, parts of the sky, seem to shift with each moment; a moment is a long time here, and change is rapid. Like something boiling, bubbling and pouring into other things. Maybe this is *weirdness*?

There is no technology, no sign of any tools or structures that could possibly be from a species. Maybe there was once a presence, but if so, it’s long since been absorbed, broken down, returned to the primal muck. Scattered, crumbling structures are visible, tiny and fragmented, made of stone that seems to melt back into the mud with relentless patience. A building might appear, a single, impossible edifice with angles that defy geometry, only to collapse in on itself a moment later, becoming a stain on the landscape that fades as quickly as it appears. 

The fauna is rare, and what *is* present is alien. Creatures of impossible form drift through the perpetual mist, their bodies translucent, their movements erratic. They seem to phase in and out of existence, flickering like broken images, sometimes briefly, sometimes for epochs. Their forms are fluid, always changing, adapting to the chaotic environment. They are never quite *real*… or perhaps they are always *too* real, existing on some plane beyond the comprehension of your senses.

The sound is deafening, a cacophony of groans, whispers, and a sound like the grinding of tectonic plates. There are no individual sounds, only a constant, overwhelming resonance, a chorus of the world’s ongoing creation. Yet somehow, despite the noise, there is *stillness*. The sound is an active void, that makes you feel profoundly alone, a dot.

It’s a place that pushes on your mind, that tests the very fabric of reality. It’s a place that is alive, in a way that defies understanding. There are no rules, only the raw, untamed forces of a world in its death throes, eternally giving birth to something new.
