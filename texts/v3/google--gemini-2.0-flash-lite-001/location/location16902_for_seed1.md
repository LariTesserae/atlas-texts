The world begins as a vast, low-lying plain, a petrified sea of obsidian stretching to the horizon. The obsidian isn't glassy, but textured, a skin of intricate, overlapping flakes. Between the flakes, deep fissures run, invisible from a distance, but opening into abyssal darkness at their edges. The air is thick, metallic, and heavy with the scent of ozone. A perpetual, dim twilight reigns, cast by a sky choked with perpetual cloud.

Water, in a thousand forms, shapes this landscape. It doesn't fall as rain here, but condenses out of the air, a constant drizzle that never quite reaches the ground. Instead, it clings to everything, a sheen of oily slickness coating the obsidian plains. Within the fissures, the water collects into murky pools and sluggish rivers, choked with an ever-present, grey sediment. The air itself seems to be water; breathing here is like swimming in a primordial soup.

No plants grow. Instead, dense, pulsating fungal colonies thrive, filling the spaces between the obsidian flakes and lining the edges of the fissures. These aren't the familiar mushrooms and molds; they're alien, shimmering masses of iridescent tissue that thrum with a faint, internal light. They shift and writhe, as if constantly digesting and reshaping the very rock beneath them. They are the only sign of "life" here, if they can even be called that.

The cold is absolute. It is not shivering cold, but a bone-deep chill that seeps into every pore. The temperature clings just above freezing, a persistent frost-bite on the world. The dynamic of the landscape manifests primarily in slow, inexorable shifts. The water, the fungal colonies, the erosion; everything is in motion, reshaping the landscape at a microscopic scale. This is a place beyond geology, and beyond any obvious measure of biological action. Instead, the material here seems to exist with a purpose.

Signs of "industry" are everywhere, though nothing is readily recognizable. Great, obsidian-shrouded structures dot the horizon, or rather, the illusion of a horizon, each a vast factory spewing a constant stream of cold, wet smoke. They churn and groan, their internal machinery obscured, but their presence radiates a sense of purpose. This machinery cannot be said to make anything; its function seems to be continuous processing of the substance of the world itself. Their purpose is obscured by the fact that it is the world.

The sounds are deafening, an overwhelming cacophony that blurs the edges of every other process. Engines grind, steam hisses, and vast, unseen forces churn within the land. The roar of the factories, the gurgling of water, the rustle of fungal growths, and something else... something else that can’t be adequately sensed by the ear, but rather by the bones.

Fauna abounds; they take forms that defy easy categorization. Metallic, arthropod-like creatures scuttle across the obsidian plain, their exoskeletons mimicking the flaking surface. The bodies are like integrated machines, humming with internal energy. They hunt each other and the fungi. Sometimes the sky tears open with strange, biological storms of flying, electrical worms.

The "weirdness" of this place is its defining characteristic. The rules of reality are mutable. Time flows backward and forward simultaneously. Gravity behaves erratically, sometimes pulling in unpredictable directions. The very structure of space seems to warp and bend. Every moment is a paradox, a knot of impossibilities. Objects phase in and out of existence, and the familiar laws of cause and effect are meaningless.

It's a place where thought itself warps the landscape, and where every perception is a potential lie. Here, the world continuously breaks down and rebuilds itself, a perpetual cycle of destruction and creation. There is no beginning and no end, only the endless, cold hum of what's *being done*. And as you stand here, you become aware that *you* are part of it. The landscape is not external to you; it's within you, and you within it.
