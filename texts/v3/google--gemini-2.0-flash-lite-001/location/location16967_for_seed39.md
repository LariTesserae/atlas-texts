The wind shoves, a near-constant pressure against the exposed rock and the few, twisted trees that cling to the slopes. It's a biting wind, smelling of ice and something metallic, like the tang of blood on cold stone. The air itself feels *wet*, a clinging, heavy dampness that condenses on every surface, turning the boulders a slick, gray-black. Water is everywhere, but in ways that seem wrong. It doesn't flow in rivers, not yet. Instead, it beads and trickles across the skeletal remains of what might once have been a lush, green land.

Elevation here is extreme. The peaks, impossibly sharp and jagged, pierce a perpetually dim sky. Clouds, thick with moisture, seem to be born and die against the rock, wringing out their meager rain over and over again. This isn't a landscape *made.* It's a landscape *making*. The very bones of the world are being heaved and ground, constantly shifting, hinting at the titanic energies sleeping just beneath the surface. Erosion is in its infancy here, but the signs are everywhere. Patches of fresh, raw earth, scarred by the actions of some unimaginable force, are exposed between the ancient, weathered stone. The scale is vast. Horizons stretch endlessly, broken only by the fractured architecture of the peaks. A sense of emptiness permeates the scene. The distance swallows any sound, and the few creatures that can survive here are far removed from each other, leaving the vast space between them mostly silent.

But it isn’t *completely* empty.

Hidden amongst the rocks, and built into the cliff faces, are the factories. They are cold, dark, and utterly *industrial*. These are not idyllic workshops; these are monolithic structures, belching black smoke that stains the already dim light even further. The technology is advanced, focused on some purpose that the land itself seems to be involved in. The factories hum and thrum, punctuated by the mechanical shrieks of industry, almost the sole source of sound. The machines, made of dark, unfeeling metal, are driven by an energy that seems to flow directly from the crust itself. 

Despite the cold, the dim light, and the raw power of the land and of the industrial presence throughout it, fauna is abundant. Hardy, resilient creatures populate the raw landscape. Twisted, furred things scurry through the rock, their forms adapting to the harsh conditions. A few winged creatures, resembling gigantic, metallic birds, soar in the sky, their motions jerky and efficient. Larger beasts, with thick, almost rock-like hides, can be glimpsed in the distance, slowly stripping the meager vegetation from the ground. Their presence is a constant reminder: this is a land of predator and prey, of action and reaction, of survival.

The weirdness is minimal. There's nothing *obviously* unnatural about this place. However, the presence of the factories, coupled with the strange interplay of water, stone, and the constant reshaping of the terrain hint at something else, lying beneath the surface of the normal. The environment is *active*, dynamic, changing at a pace that is both exhilarating and unsettling. Everything is in motion, from the shifting landscape to the ceaseless activity of the factories to the hunt, feeding, and fleeing of the creatures that somehow call this place home. It's a symphony of survival, a testament to the brutal beauty of creation.
