The air hums, thick with the scent of damp earth and something acrid, almost metallic. Visibility is perhaps a dozen paces, the light a diffused grey-green, the sun a memory behind the impossibly dense canopy overhead. This is a place made of water and plants, a city grown from the swamp, a cathedral of living things. 

The ground is a yielding, springy mass, a woven carpet of roots and mosses. It gives way underfoot, water pushing up from below, soaking through the thin leather of my boots. This is no solid ground, but a living sponge, the very substrate of the place. Everywhere I look, plants dominate. They are the structure, the architecture, the very air itself. Giant ferns with fronds wider than a man’s height unfurl in impossible spirals, their surfaces slick with condensation. Thick vines, like colossal pythons, twist and constrict around trees, which themselves are more like columns of living wood than the dead trunks of a normal forest.

The architecture of this place is organic and fluid. There are structures that could be called buildings but they are difficult to distinguish from the environment. They appear to have grown, not been built. Woven from living wood and draped with moss, they blend seamlessly with the surrounding vegetation. Some are open, almost skeletal, allowing the pervasive green light to filter through. Others are solid, carved with intricate patterns, their surfaces weeping a slow, constant rain that feeds the mosses and lichens that coat them.

Water is everywhere. Not just in the saturated ground, but trickling down every surface, dripping from every leaf, collecting in countless tiny pools and channels. The air hangs heavy with it, a constant, cool embrace. The sound is a symphony of moisture: the rustle of leaves, the gurgle of water, the unending patter of dripping. A low, continuous drumming emanates from the distance, like a heart.

The scale feels intimate, like wandering through the interior of some gargantuan, breathing organism. The paths are narrow, winding through tight corridors carved between the enormous plants. The density is overwhelming, a constant press of plant life, layered and interwoven, reaching for light. There's a nervous energy in the air; the feeling of a constant, subtle churning.

The tech, if it can be called that, is primitive. Tools are fashioned from bone, polished stone, and perhaps some simple alloys of metals. The structures are built with immense patience and care, and seemingly, an understanding of the plants themselves. There is an almost symbiotic relationship with the environment. I see no fire, and no obvious source of light other than what filters from above.

Fauna is abundant. Insects buzz and whir, a constant counterpoint to the rustling of leaves. Strange, bioluminescent creatures flit through the undergrowth, their movements too rapid and complex to be readily followed. The air vibrates with the chirps, clicks, and calls of creatures unseen, a relentless soundtrack to this bizarre world. The presence of these creatures is overwhelming. They're as much a part of the landscape as the trees and the water.

The weirdness is low, but the strangeness is high. Everything is familiar, yet nothing is as it should be. It is as if the known world has been pressed through a biological matrix, then reassembled in an unfamiliar order.

There is a rhythm to the place, a heartbeat. This is caused by the turbulence, by the constant change. Something is always growing, decaying, shifting. The structures are in perpetual flux, always weathering under the downpour, always being reshaped by the plants themselves. This place *is* an organism, a massive, ever-evolving ecosystem. It is beautiful, terrifying, and utterly alive.
