The air hangs thick and luminous, a perpetual dawn. It presses, a presence, not quite wet but heavy, as if made of the same coarse-grained stuff as the walls. These walls, everywhere: the world. They curve and twist around, a network of intersecting tunnels and chambers, the whole a warren built into the heart of something massive. Elevation feels meaningless here, as does "below surface." There is only the warren.

The stone is a deep, bruised purple, veined with threads of a pearlescent white. It’s warm to the touch, and feels… alive. Not in the way a rock might be, but with a subtle, slow pulse. The tunnels are close-packed, a squeeze, barely allowing passage in some places. The light, impossibly bright, radiates from within the stone itself, from the wet, glistening walls. It’s a clean and constant illumination, never harsh, yet casting no shadows.

Water: it isn’t *present* exactly, not in pools or streams. But a faint sheen clings to everything, a condensation that never quite drips. You can taste it on the air, mineral-rich, like blood and iron.

Vegetation is a surprise. It shouldn't *be* here, in a place like this, but it is. Patches of luminous fungi cling to the walls, the same purple as the stone but with an oily sheen. Their caps, or whatever they are, swell and recede slowly, breathing with a gentle rhythm. In the larger chambers, where the light is most intense, there are strange, fleshy plants that reach out like yearning limbs. They have no leaves, only thick, pulsing stalks that coil and uncoil, seeking something.

Fauna abounds. The sound, constant and deafening, comes from them. It is not easily identifiable; a chorus of clicks, chirps, and rustles fills every space. There are glimpses of movement: creatures that scuttle through the narrowest crevices, their bodies the same deep purple of the stone, scaled, chitinous, and multi-limbed. No room exists between the presences of the world. One never sees a creature in full; only parts glimpsed and vanished. 

The strangeness intensifies when the mind bends to understand the structure. Here and there, the barest suggestion of building. Some sections of wall bear faint glyphs etched into them, patterns that seem to shift and reconfigure as you look. Often, you will find places where the wall has a sudden, strange geometry, a perfect cube or sphere carved into the rough stone. These might have once been doorways or enclosures, but as everything in the warren, they have long since been reshaped. The whole of it feels like a ruin, a catacomb… but what was *built* has been so completely absorbed back into its surroundings that only the faintest suggestion remains.

The air remains unchanging. There is no wind, no current, no sense of time passing. The light never dims. The sounds never fade. The creatures never cease. It is a world in stasis, yet teeming with life. It is like being within a single, massive, breathing cell.
