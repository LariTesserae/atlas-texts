The place is a sphere, no larger than a good-sized room, though its curved walls are nowhere visible. The air is so thick with the exhalations of machinery and the chittering of unseen fauna that you can almost taste it. It’s metallic and sweet, like blood that’s been left out in the rain.

The “ground” — though it's hardly that — is a mosaic of interlocking plates. Each is formed of some unknown, black, gleaming substance, cold beneath the touch. They're slightly convex, and subtly textured with minuscule ridges and whorls. Run your fingers over them, and you might think they're carved with the patterns of veins and tendons. Every surface is coated with a glistening, viscous film. It’s hard to tell if this is water, or something else entirely. It clings to everything, a constant presence, a promise of slickness. The air itself feels wet, pressing against your skin.

Above, the density is unimaginable. A tangle of pipes, wires, and articulated limbs, all made of impossibly smooth, obsidian-like materials, crisscrosses and merges. No two elements seem to share a consistent scale. Girders the size of a man’s torso are intertwined with filaments finer than hair; enormous, throbbing mechanisms sit immediately adjacent to dials so small that they could only be the province of insects. The construction is a tapestry of total fabrication. There is nothing natural here. The entire sphere is a single, immense machine, self-contained and self-replicating.

The light is a perpetual, dim dusk. It seems to emanate from the very fabric of the place, from a thousand tiny perforations through which a dull luminescence leaks. This is a place without sun or shadow, only a steady, unwavering illumination.

The sounds are deafening. The place pulses and throbs with an incessant symphony of industrial noise. A deep, omnipresent thrumming underlies everything else: the low *whir* of gears, the high whine of turbines, the staccato *clack* of unknown processes. The air vibrates with the mechanical song, an endless, intricate fugue.

The fauna! It’s overwhelming, a seething, silent tide. They exist, you are certain, even though they remain unseen. You know they scuttle through the pipes, that they feed off of the oily residue that drips from the ceiling. You *feel* them, a constant, unnoticed pressure. A prickling on your skin. A sense of being observed. You can hear their soft, ceaseless clicking. They are everywhere. Everything here seems to serve their purposes.

This place gives no indication of change. It will always be thus. Each component is exactly where it needs to be, and it all will continue to function this way forever.

A strange feeling pervades. Equilibrium. Stillness. The world has already been created. It is complete.
