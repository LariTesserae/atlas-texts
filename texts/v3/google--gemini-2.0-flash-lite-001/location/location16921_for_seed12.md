It begins with pressure, a constant, heavy thing. Not painful, exactly—more like the embrace of a lover who will never let go. Around, above, in, and through you: water. A perpetual, gentle ocean. Sunlight struggles to make its way through the depths, painting the sky-ocean a bruised, bruised blue. 

Below, a city. Not a ruin, not yet. Not built of stone or wood, but something else. A substance that hardens like bone, yet shifts and flows like the water itself. A city of coral-like towers that reach towards the dim, distant light, their forms impossibly intricate, constantly evolving. This architecture is alive, breathing, growing. It is both urban and wild, a synthesis of built and natural.

The terrain is vast. Rolling hills of this organic stone undulate, forming valleys and canyons, all beneath a constant, almost imperceptible current. Water currents carve the city, too, shaping the twisting pathways between the massive structures. Erosion is minimal, but the movement of the water, the gradual expansion, the unending growth of the city means change is still present. This place is young, in the process of becoming.

Vegetation is abundant, though adapted to the submerged environment. Giant kelp forests rise from the seabed, their fronds swaying like emerald curtains. Strange, bioluminescent plants cling to the city's structures, casting soft, pulsing light in the perpetual twilight. The air—or rather, the water itself—is dense with nutrients, allowing for lush growth even in the absence of direct sunlight.

The temperature is warm, even at these depths. Life flourishes in this comfortable embrace. Fauna, however, is rare, strange. The few creatures that dwell within are adapted to the environment. They're slow to move, patient. Giant, jellyfish-like beings drift through the kelp forests, propelled by the currents. Their bodies glow with a soft, internal light, like living lanterns. There are also smaller, unknown creatures, unseen by most, that flit through the city's labyrinthine passages. 

The soundscape is mostly quiet, punctuated by the gentle rush of currents and the distant, almost inaudible hum of the city's growth. Occasionally, a deep, resonant tone ripples through the water—the communication of the large, slow-moving creatures, perhaps, or a sign of the city’s unformed heart. The light is dim, filtering through the water with no obvious day/night cycle.

The tech level is primitive, if the word even applies. The city's inhabitants, if there are any, do not use tools. They are part of the city. Perhaps the architecture is guided by some internal force, some consciousness that we cannot perceive. Or perhaps the architecture is the consciousness.

The rules of the world are tied to this slow, constant change. Time itself is measured differently here. Days bleed into weeks, months, years. The slow cycles of growth and decay dictate all things. There is no urgency, no hurry. Only the patient persistence of emergence.

The density is high. Every space is filled, layered with forms. Everything is in conversation with everything, as the city grows slowly, inexorably, towards a future that is still being written in the depths.
