The world *breathes* water. Not the gentle lapping of a shore, nor the churning of a storm at sea. It *inhales* and *exhales* liquid, a slow, titanic rhythm of cold darkness. This is not an ocean, not a lake, not a flood. It is the water itself—the substance of the world, given form and will.

Above, the sky is water. A perpetual, starless night, where light fails to break through the layers. Its cold seeps into the liquid world below, a chilling caress that governs everything.

You are high – perhaps on a mountain, though mountains are a quaint concept here. Perhaps on the remains of a mountain, its peak long eroded by the ceaseless flow. These high points are rare, scattered like forgotten teeth. They are the only places that still hold any scrap of the old world.

The landscape is a vast, open theater of shapes sculpted by the water’s unending dance. Sparse, dark, and strange forms drift through the water, hinting at a built past. Here, a submerged archway, its stone softened and rounded by time. There, a fragment of a wall, its surface etched with primitive carvings that seem to writhe in the perpetual gloom. These are echoes of a civilization lost to the waters, its structures scattered by unseen forces.

Lush, alien vegetation clings to these remnants, a verdant embrace of the lost. Bioluminescent plants, in shades of violet and emerald, pulse with light, the only beacons in the perpetual gloom. This luminescence is the only light, and it is far from warm, a frigid glow that barely pierces the profound darkness. These strange growths of plant life are the fauna of this world, silent and ceaselessly alive. They sway and twist with the water's currents, and seem to feed off its substance.

There is a loud, almost deafening quality to the water. Not the roar of a waterfall or the crash of waves, but a constant, deep humming, a resonance that vibrates through bones and thoughts. This is caused by the slow, shifting tides, the unseen currents that sculpt and erode. There is an active dynamism in the world. The water never rests. Constantly something shifts, forming, dissolving. The world is in motion, always changing.

Primitive technology is evident, but in hints and whispers. Carvings on the stone, perhaps tools of a bygone age, or devices that were meant to interact with this strange, watery reality.

The complete absence of fauna suggests a great catastrophe -- or perhaps a different kind of life entirely, one utterly alien to ours. Perhaps the plants *are* the fauna, or perhaps life here is invisible, microscopic, or something beyond imagining.

This world... it offers no welcome, no solace. It is a place of profound cold and darkness, of shapes that shift and dissolve. Yet there is a strange beauty in its alien existence. It is a testament to the power of water, and the enduring echoes of a forgotten world. The rules are carved in the flowing water, in the silent pulsing of the luminous plants. The rules are the world.
