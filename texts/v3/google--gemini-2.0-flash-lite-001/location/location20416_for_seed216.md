The world is a tapestry woven from water and plant, a vast and ancient thing. It sprawls across the void, a constructed reality hanging in the dim, cold light of a dying sun. Mountains rise, but they are not made of stone. They are the petrified husks of colossal, long-dead trees, their wood turned to a slick, obsidian-like material, veined with rivers of luminescent sap. These peaks pierce a sky perpetually veiled in a thick, shimmering mist, a living substance that condenses and falls in a constant, gentle rain.

The air is thick with the scent of water and the indescribable perfume of blooming, thriving life. Everything is plant. The ground itself is a shifting, pulsing carpet of moss, fungi, and strange, luminous flora. Canyons are carved by rivers of viscous, emerald fluid, flowing through living, breathing tunnels made of enormous, interwoven roots. The few structures that exist are not built *on* anything; they *are* the landscape. They are vast, organic cathedrals grown from the living rock of the great trees, their architecture a collaboration between plant and something else, something the locals call "the Weavers."

The Weavers are a primitive species, tiny beings with skin like bark and voices like rustling leaves. Using simple tools crafted from bone and the hardened husks of giant seedpods, they cultivate and shape the world. They guide the flow of the sap-rivers, meticulously pruning the colossal plants, and even seem to influence the growth of the architecture itself, whispering to the living stone. They are utterly dependent on this engineered paradise and are, in turn, masters of the craft of staying alive and preserving what sustains them. 

The silence is deceptive. It is not an absence of sound, but a layering of the subtlest of noises. The slow, rhythmic pulse of the living world, the whisper of the Weavers communicating through their plants, and the distant, echoing cries of the fauna. The fauna are plentiful. Great, serpentine creatures with bodies shimmering like woven reeds slither through the fungal forests, their howls resonating from the depths of the canyons. Giant, bioluminescent insects drift through the air, their wings buzzing with a gentle hum. A variety of smaller creatures scurry beneath the moss, their presence hinted at by rustling leaves and the fleeting shadows that dance in the dim light. They have no natural predators or enemies. The whole ecosystem is built for all to eat whatever grows.

The world is held in a delicate balance, a complex set of processes constantly in motion. The plants are both the foundation and the structure, the substrate and the sky. The water sustains all through circulation and as the lifeblood and structural element of most organisms. The Weavers, with their limited technology, are the caretakers of this unnatural place, a place defined by its vastness, its density of life, and its peculiar emptiness.

But the reality is not stable. The fabric of the world is frayed, and there is a sense of cosmic entropy, of slow unravelling. The sky sometimes fractures, revealing glimpses of something beyond, a glimpse of another reality where the world might be different, maybe even natural. The plants themselves seem to be aware of this, adapting and changing in a way that is just as unsettling as it is beautiful. The Weavers’ practices grow more rigorous, their rituals more arcane. Something is coming, a break, a shift, and the constructed paradise might not survive it.
