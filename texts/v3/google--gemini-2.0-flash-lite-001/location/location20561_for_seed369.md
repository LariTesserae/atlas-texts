The world is a high, tilted plateau, the size of a large cathedral. Ancient stone, the color of wet sand, forms the plateau's base. The stone weeps constantly, fed by unseen springs deep within, and the air hangs thick with a constant, warm humidity. This world feels as though the ground is only barely holding itself together. Water seeps from every pore of the stone, running in countless tiny rivulets and gathering in shallow pools that dot the landscape.

Lush vegetation attempts to find purchase on the stony ground. Strange, rubbery plants with oversized leaves, like a cross between ferns and orchids, cling to the porous rock. They are the dominant flora, and though dense in places, they are also scattered, with the plateau's empty stretches clearly visible between them. The plants are a deep, almost unnatural green, and they seem to pulse slowly, as if breathing in the humid air. 

The air is alive with sound, a symphony of the unseen. The constant drip of water echoing within the cathedral-like space. A choir of unseen insects, their calls loud and persistent, fill the air. The faint, high-pitched song of something, perhaps a bird, drifts from the higher parts of the plateau. 

The light is normal, but the moisture in the air refracts it, giving everything a soft, diffuse glow. It's difficult to see far, the humidity and the plants' density limiting visibility.

Animals are rare here, but their presence is known. Faint trails cut through the plants, and the air occasionally carries the scent of something musky and unknown. The very slow pace of change, the water's persistence, and the established-ness of the area suggest that fauna are either very long-lived or that they have adapted to the sparse environment.

The weirdness is subtle but present. Some of the plants have a faint bioluminescence, their leaves softly glowing in the perpetual twilight. The stone itself seems to shift and morph slightly over time, forming new channels for the water to flow. There's a sense that the plateau is alive, not in a traditional sense, but as a slowly breathing, shifting body.

There are no signs of any structures here, no artifacts left behind, either. However, small piles of a red-brown mineral are found in sheltered locations. This mineral looks like a fine powder and feels faintly warm to the touch. It seems to have been gathered, but for what purpose is a mystery. 

The "tech" available in this world is in the tools a native might use; something like polished stone implements or perhaps simple woven containers. There is no clear evidence of either, however.

The rules of this world are dictated by its slow, patient nature. The passage of time is measured in the slow expansion of the water-worn channels in the stone, in the growth of the plants. The sky seems to be constantly overcast. The world feels as though it is gradually settling into something, a form both ancient and new. There is little action visible, only slow, persistent, and quiet change.
