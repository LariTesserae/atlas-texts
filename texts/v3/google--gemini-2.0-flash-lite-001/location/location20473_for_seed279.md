The world breathes. 

Up close, at ankle level, it is a mat of gleaming black. Not a soil, not quite: more like a membrane, or a slick of congealed ink. It yields slightly underfoot, then snaps back, sending a shudder through the whole plain. This is how the world breathes, through the dark membrane, inhaling and exhaling a barely-visible fog that tastes of ozone and something older, deeper.

Above, the sky is a bruised grey, heavy with moisture. It presses down, muffling sounds and dimming the light. The sun is a faint rumor, a pale smudge behind the thick, slow-moving clouds that crawl across the sky.

The plain extends, broken by occasional rises and falls. These are not hills, not exactly. They are more like wrinkles, folds in the skin of the world. They are formed of the same black material, but are marked by strange, crystalline formations of a pale, translucent mineral. In the dim light, they gleam, catching what little illumination there is and scattering it into fleeting rainbows.

The air is thick, saturated. Not with water, but with a condensation of… something. A heavy, oily vapor clings to everything. It doesn't quite drip, but it feels like the world is constantly weeping. The rocks, where they are, are slick with it. 

Where the plain wrinkles into sharper formations, one can see the deeper structure. The black membrane pools in the hollows and ravines. It reveals an underlying network of sharp, angular, dark rock. No sign of erosion, no smoothness, no soft features. The edges are knife-like, the planes clean and severe.

The sounds are many, loud, and hard to place. A constant, low hum vibrates through the ground. It is joined by the grinding of stone on stone, a metallic clicking, and a high-pitched whine that seems to resonate within your very bones. The silence is never complete, the sounds seem to have volume and direction, but no particular source. The world *is* sound.

Vegetation is absent. Or… almost. Sometimes, in the deepest clefts, where the weeping of the sky gathers, there are patches of… something. Thin, iridescent veils that cling to the rock faces. They shift and writhe, shimmering with impossible colors that seem to change with every breath. They are not plants. Not as we understand them. They are… filters. Sieves. Traps. They appear, and then they vanish. 

Fauna is rare. On the plain now and then, there are dark shapes. They move, but rarely, slowly. They are large, perhaps the size of a small car. They have multiple, thick limbs, and move across the landscape in a strange, deliberate fashion. Their forms are difficult to discern, constantly shifting, their surfaces shimmering like disturbed oil. One can perceive their presence – a disturbance in the already saturated atmosphere – long before one sees them. 

The structures are scattered, almost unnoticeable. They are built of the same dark rock as the terrain, and blend in almost completely. There are no straight lines, no apparent purpose. They look almost, but not quite, natural – built, perhaps, by the same processes that formed the landscape itself. They may be the source of the incessant sounds, if they were truly *made*…

The world has rules, though they are not immediately apparent. There is something of a cycle – long, vast, and slow. The crystalline formations appear and disappear, the veils bloom and contract, the shapes move across the plane. The clouds shift, the light ebbs. It almost feels like the world is breathing in and out, inhaling, then exhaling. What, exactly, it is breathing, is a mystery – but it surely has consequences.

The scale is large, open. The boundaries are unclear. The horizon is always a shifting, blurred line, lost in the heavy atmosphere. The world invites exploration by making such exploration a task of the mind.

Here, in this strange, wet, unyielding place, the slow dance of the world continues.
