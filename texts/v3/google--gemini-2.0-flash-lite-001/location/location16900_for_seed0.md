The air is a heavy, almost solid thing, a clinging dampness that presses against your skin. It tastes of something metallic, sharp, like the tang of ozone barely suppressed. The world is a riot of green and brown, almost featureless at first glance, but soon resolving into a tapestry of interwoven life. 

It is *alive*, fiercely, obsessively.

The landscape is built on the remains of a recent upheaval, the evidence of which is still sharp in the eye. Eroded hills rise and fall with an impossible fluidity, sometimes sheer drops, sometimes gentle slopes. Angular, freshly-exposed rock faces weep the occasional trickle of condensation that collects in shallow pools, quickly absorbed by the ravenous vegetation. 

The heat is a constant presence, a warm embrace, thick enough to be tasted. It would be oppressive, but there is always a movement, a shift. Every moment contains chaotic elements, a symphony of growth and decay.

Water clings to all surfaces, but it never *pools*. It is everywhere, but nowhere. It emerges in a blink on broad leaves, or trickles down the faces of cliffs, or hangs as mist which is instantly consumed.

The sounds are overwhelming. Not just loud, but a cacophony that shifts and writhes. It is mostly the chitter of insects, but there is more — the rustle of unseen large things shifting through thickets, the shriek of a bird that feels wrong, a low, guttural chanting that starts and ends without rhythm or apparent purpose.

There are trees here, but they are not the dominant form. Instead, the canopy seems to be a layered mass of interwoven plantlife. Giant ferns, their fronds broader than a man, intertwine with vibrant vines that pulse with an inner light. Strange, luminous fungi burst from every surface, their caps flaring like delicate umbrellas. All of it is held together by a web of more, smaller things, a tangle of the small and bright; this is the true density, the true "built" form, with an underlying, unseen structure built of fibers and organic compounds.

Fauna is abundant, *and* aggressive. Every footfall is attended by the scuttle of unseen things, the snap of jaws hidden in the undergrowth. Birds with impossible plumage dart through the air, but they are gone before you can truly see them. 

The land itself is alive. The hills groan and shift, the trees seem to writhe. Strange, crystalline structures rise and fall in minutes, only to be reclaimed by the dominant flora moments later. It is a place of constant change, of impossible energy.

The tech level is primitive, yes, but its tools are not made of stone or bone. They are grown, shaped from the same organic matter that forms the landscape. Blades crafted from fused fungi, woven baskets that seem to *think* — these are the tools of the land’s hidden inhabitants. Their presence is felt, a faint pressure, a sense of being watched, but you have yet to see them. Their presence is a whispered threat, a promise of a knowledge that is alien and unknowable.

Darkness isn't necessarily a physical phenomenon here; the light is filtered. The dense vegetation and constant, shifting mist ensure that shadows are the norm. The few open spaces are bathed in an eerie twilight, the sun itself a memory, or an ever-present dream.

The rules of this world are…fluid. Time itself feels distorted, moments stretch into ages, then collapse into instants. Gravity seems to have a will of its own, things fall and rise on a whim. The land itself is not *solid*, but a living, breathing entity, constantly reshaping itself.

This is a place of unending creation, and of unimaginable destruction. A crucible where life and death dance a frantic, violent waltz, forever.
