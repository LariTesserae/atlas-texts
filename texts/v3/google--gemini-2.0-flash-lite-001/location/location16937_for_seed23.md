The city of Aeon sits nestled in a high mountain pass, a place of impossible geometries where the very rocks weep. The air itself feels **dense**, a thick broth of frigid mist that condenses on every surface with a **bright** clarity. Water is **everywhere**: not just in the slow, glacial streams carving through the city or the **abundant fauna** that cluster on the *edges* of the settlement, from the occasional glimpse of massive, long-necked beasts feeding on the sparse, silvered vegetation to the swarms of iridescent insectile creatures that glitter within the shadowed, **primitive** constructed structures of the metropolis. Each stone of the city glistens with constant moisture, and the wind carries the scent of brine and ozone, the perfume of constant rain.

The **terrain** is a symphony of stark contrasts. Jagged peaks claw at the sky, their surfaces eroded into smooth, water-sculpted curves that gleam with a **settled-form**. The city itself is built into the mountain, a sprawling warren of interconnected chambers and narrow, winding alleyways that feels both **dense** and surprisingly low in **scale** — like a complex internal organ of the mountain, rather than external buildings. Every surface is sculpted, and every angle seems a paradox, yet the effect is harmonious. It feels as if the mountain breathed and the city blossomed from the exhalation.

The buildings are constructed from a dark, almost obsidian-like stone, veined with shimmering quartz and infused with what appear to be fossilized remnants of enormous, unknown creatures. **Urban** in its complexity, it is testament to unknown processes. They are constructed in a way that suggests a **primitive** mastery of stone, but also an understanding of esoteric principles. Structures rise, curve like wings, and dive back into the mountain as if embracing it. Tunnels and bridges connect the buildings. Their construction relies on hand-tools and precise, geometrical arrangements rather than sophisticated machinery — an arrangement of effort that suggests some kind of relationship to the very flow of the mountain's structure.

The silence, however, is not total. A near-perfect quiet reigns, except for the occasional, deep groan of the rock itself, the drip of water, the faint rustle of insect wings, and the far-off, echoing calls of the larger beasts. But there’s a tension in the silence, a feeling of being watched, of hidden currents beneath the surface.

This is a **turbulent** place. The forces are constantly shifting and remaking their own effects, in a dance of creation and destruction. The glacial torrents are not predictable. The flora and fauna appear and disappear with the shifting of the light; even the very stones of the city seem to rearrange themselves under some invisible pressure. It’s as though something is *always* beginning and ending.

The inhabitants of Aeon, though, are a mystery. Hints of earlier lives are evident from an earlier, more advanced technology lying dormant within the mountain: a series of glyphs that appear and disappear even the most learned can not translate, hinting at some earlier, forgotten age. They are rare. If they exist at all, they seem adapted to this unique environment, perhaps able to manipulate the mountain or to survive beyond the edge of chaos.

Aeon breathes, endures, *is*, and it is a place of wonder and terror, a monument to a creation that’s always in the process of becoming something else.
