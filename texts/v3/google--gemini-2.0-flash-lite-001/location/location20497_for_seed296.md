The air hangs heavy, a cool, damp blanket clinging to the slopes. It mutes the world, swallowing light and sound, painting everything in shades of grey and muted green. This is a place where the mountains have aged into a final repose. The peaks, if they could be called that, are blunt, no longer striving for the sky. Instead, they are broad terraces, layered and leaning against each other. The deep wounds of erosion have become wide, fertile valleys.

Water is a persistent presence, though not always obvious. Mist seeps from the shadowed canyons, condensing on the coarse, lichen-covered stone. Occasionally, a drizzle descends, turning the earth into a sucking bog, then disappearing as quickly as it came. Rivers, swollen from the high elevations, carve sluggish paths through the dense undergrowth, their surfaces reflecting the dim light like tarnished silver.

Vegetation clings to every available surface. Giant, gnarled trees, their bark a mosaic of greens and browns, stand shoulder to shoulder, their canopies interlocked. The forest floor is a thick carpet of moss, ferns, and strange, luminous fungi, emitting a faint, ethereal glow. Here and there, the ruins of structures peek through the foliage—crumbling stone walls, rusted metal frameworks, hinting at a lost civilization.

The scale of this place is vast, but the density is overwhelming. The open space of the wide valleys is immediately filled by the forest's reach, the air thick with the smell of damp earth and decaying vegetation. The ruins, themselves, are layered, with older constructions entombed beneath more recent settlements. Industrial remnants blend with the natural world, suggesting a long period of fusion between the two.

This is an industrial city, reclaimed by nature. Factories and workshops, once humming with mechanical activity, are now silent, their skeletal remains slowly being consumed by the relentless plant life. Great, rusted gears lie half-submerged in the bog, connected by decaying conduits, their purposes unknown. The city's original purpose, its workers and ways, have all gone to the grave. 

Though the sound here is mostly quiet, a low, persistent thrum penetrates the gloom. It is the echo of ancient machinery, perhaps, or a current generated by some unknown source. Faint creaks and groans emanate from the trees. A rustling in the undergrowth occasionally announces the passage of fauna, a rare movement from something unseen.

The fauna of this world are adapted to the unique environment. They are rarely seen and are more frequently felt. They might be sleek predators, with camouflaged coats able to shift with the shifting of the light; or they might be strange herbivores that have adapted themselves to the forest's deep, dark places. They might be the product of some genetic experiments in industry. They might be something entirely unseen. The world pushes them along its dynamic track.

This place is built on secrets, many of them lost to time. Industrial infrastructure is mixed with primordial landscape–the scars of industry slowly consumed by the unyielding march of nature.
