The world is a tapestry of black, a canvas stretched taut between an unseen loom and an unfathomed void. Here, on the *Ebon Plains*, the wind is a raw, biting thing, a sculptor of ice and shadow. It howls through colossal, skeletal structures that claw at the endless sky, the last vestiges of a civilization lost to the planet's slow, implacable hunger. Stone, once smooth and carefully shaped, is now jagged and fractured, the signature of a young land, freshly torn and reformed.

Water here is a clandestine presence. It exists mostly as the breath of the world, a fine, chilling mist that hangs heavy in the air, clinging to the colossal, obsidian-like plants. These aren't trees, but something else entirely - structures of living, interwoven fiber, black as the deepest night. They tower, colossal and ancient, reaching for a sky that refuses to yield them light. Their surfaces are slick with the constant damp, a film that thins and thickens with the capricious moods of the wind.

This is a world of pure plant. The only substrate is the plant itself. No soil, no rock. The plains are a vast, unending carpet of a pulpy, spore-rich material, a network of fibrous roots that weave the landscape together. These plants are the architecture, the buildings, the very ground beneath your feet. They are living, breathing fortresses, and their internal spaces, vast and labyrinthine, are the only havens here, echoing with the sound of the wind.

The temperature is glacial, an ever-present, bone-deep cold. The darkness is absolute, a curtain that blocks all but the faintest glimmers of distant stars. Light, when it comes, is a pale, ethereal bleed, a consequence of the great, roiling storms that constantly batter the plains. These storms are the life blood of the world, the catalysts of an active and dangerous ecosystem.

The dynamic nature of this place is a constant, frenetic ballet. The wind is the choreographer, driving storms that tear through the plant-architecture, leaving vast swathes of destruction in their wake. The storms bring the briefest moments of searing light and thunder, and with them, the hunters come.

This is a world teeming with fauna, though it can only be detected with your other senses. Massive, shadowy creatures - creatures of many limbs and sharp, clicking claws, move through the dense, dark thickets, their forms shifting, blurring against the impossible darkness. The air thrums with their unseen presence. The loud sounds—the screams, the rustling of chitinous hides, the grinding of teeth—betray their activity. They are apex predators, hunters who have evolved to thrive in this harsh environment.

The scattered built structures are remnants of a lost era. Some are colossal, half-buried in the fibrous carpet woven by the great plants. Others are smaller, perhaps dwellings, perhaps temples, or just way-stations of whatever the long-gone civilization may have been. Their construction, like everything else here, seems to be a strange adaptation of plant matter and primitive metallurgy.

The tech level of the early people, assuming they existed, seems to have been low. It is all rudimentary tools, simple structures, rudimentary weapons. No elaborate machinery. Perhaps, they were merely caretakers or symbiotic partners with the native plants, or worse yet, their prey.

The rules of this world are… unstable. The very fabric of reality seems to fray and shift. In certain areas, reality warps, creating pockets of altered time, strange reflections, and impossible geometries. One must tread carefully, for every step can lead into an unseen abyss.

The weirdness is not just in these unpredictable areas. It is in the very nature of the plants themselves. Their ability to shift their form, and the way they integrate themselves into the very fabric of the world, making the landscape into a living building, is not to be understood. The creatures are equally strange. Some are purely biological, some seem to be partly composed of the very essence of the storms, shifting and changing with the weather.

The air itself hums with an undercurrent of raw, primal energy, a sense of something vast and unknown churning beneath the surface, preparing to erupt. The Ebon Plains are a place of desolation, yes, but also a place of a morbid beauty, waiting for the return of whatever made it beautiful in the first place, on a vastly larger timescale than can be comprehended.
