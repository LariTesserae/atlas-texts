The world is a vast plateau of obsidian glass, reflecting a sky of muted bronze. Though the elevation is high, the land stretches out in a mostly level plane for as far as the eye can see, marked only by the gentle undulations of its own surface. It feels like the world is still settling, a cooling lake of igneous rock, though any movement is imperceptible.

Despite the lack of water, a peculiar lushness pervades. Not in the way of earthly forests, but a strange, alien bloom. Towering, crystalline structures of a deep emerald hue erupt from the obsidian, their surfaces etched with intricate geometric patterns. These formations, the "Gladioli" as they are known, appear to be the dominant form of vegetation. They are arranged in enormous clusters, separated by kilometers of the black glass, as if planted according to an unspoken, unknown logic. Wisps of shimmering, iridescent gas — a kind of internal atmosphere generated by the Gladioli— drift around these structures, catching the bronze light and casting long, colorful shadows.

The temperature is warm, but a dry heat, and the air is remarkably still. The scale is undeniably vast, the emptiness of the world accentuated by the absence of sound. There is a profound silence broken only by the occasional, almost subliminal hum that seems to emanate from the Gladioli. It's a low-frequency vibration that you can feel more than hear; a faint thrumming that resonates deep within.

The "industrial" aspect reveals itself in ways that are both subtle and unsettling. Scattered across the obsidian plane are enormous, geometric frameworks, composed of a dull, rust-colored metal. These structures, known as the "Hammers," are clearly engineered and yet serve no immediately obvious purpose. They resemble colossal machines, but are inert. They remain fixed, suggesting a forgotten function. No visible power source. No moving parts. They sit silent, like abandoned cathedrals of a dead civilization.

The lack of fauna is striking. No insects, no birds, no small creatures of any kind. This place is utterly devoid of animal life. This does not feel like a void, but a place that has decided no animals.

The weirdness is found in the interplay of the contradictory parameters. The lushness is dry and mineral, the industrial is ancient and inert, the silence is somehow filled with the suggestion of powerful forces. The land does not seem dynamic, but stable. It gives the feeling of a world locked in an eternal moment, a moment of profound potential just before something momentous. This place does not seem to “do” anything, it *is*. It is a place of potential.
