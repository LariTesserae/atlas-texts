The **Abyssal Foundry** is not a place you stumble upon. It is a place you willingly, or perhaps unwillingly, *descend* into, a journey taken by an individual or a small collective.

It begins with the **Siphon**. A vertical shaft, a twisting bore carved through the ocean floor, is the only entrance. The Siphon is not natural; it is a seamless tube of polished obsidian, a black gradient that absorbs the little light that penetrates the deeps. Its walls are engraved with rhythmic glyphs, machinery of silent function, that glow with a faint, internal luminescence. The Siphon is far from simple, an industrial marvel. Its purpose, you eventually understand, is to manage the flow of water, regulating pressure and temperature on the descent.

The shaft descends for an unknown distance, and then, at its end, the Foundry awaits.

The Foundry itself is a pocket of engineered reality, a city-scale construct entirely submerged within the crushing depths. It is not a sprawling metropolis, but a condensed, almost claustrophobic place. Walls, built to resist unimaginable pressure, are built of a subtly shifting material called **Chroma-Steel**. It is a composite metal that refracts and bends the feeble light, so that the Foundry seems to breathe with internal colors, the structures built from it subtly alive with deep greens, sullen blues, and the coppery red of perpetual sunset.

The water pressure here is managed: it is not the deep-ocean crush. Instead, water is itself a material here. It flows in controlled rivers through the city, or else clings to the structures, dripping steadily. The Foundry does not sit on the ocean floor, but is suspended within a vast, slow whirlpool of its own creation, a gentle rotation that ensures the city is never still.

The structures lack the organic look of a living ecosystem, but they are not entirely inhuman either. They seem grown, grown to purpose. Machinery is everywhere; impossible gears turn at impossible speeds, humming with barely-audible resonance. Massive, silent pistons drive complex mechanisms. Gigantic cooling towers, crafted of the same Chroma-Steel as the city’s outer skin, loom over the city.

The Foundry’s purpose, as far as can be understood, is production. It is an industrial hive, but operating on an alien principle. Mechanized processes are executed relentlessly by automated devices, and the water itself becomes a medium of work. The machinery is not powered by electricity, or coal, or internal combustion. Neither are they quite run by magic, though their principles seems utterly beyond the understanding of any ordinary science. Instead, their processes are powered by a kind of water-magic. The water is forced into patterns, directed in currents, used to grind, and shape, and assemble.

Vegetation is uncommon, but it is *lush*. Glowing fungi thrive in the shadowed recesses. Giant, bioluminescent kelp forests sway in the currents, their fronds trailing like spectral curtains. Strange, plantlike organisms, fed by the Foundry’s waste, cling to the infrastructure, filtering and cleaning the water. They provide a strange, beautiful contrast to the harsh, industrial structures.

The temperature is warm, even tropical. Pools of heated water, channeled from the machinery, bring life to the rare flora. The air, what there is of it, is thick with moisture and the metallic tang of the Chroma-Steel.

Fauna is rare, but exists. The *Eel-Keepers* are long, slender creatures with eyes that seem to absorb light. They are the closest things to workers in the Foundry, tending the machinery and maintaining the kelp gardens. It's unclear what keeps them within the Foundry, they are not aggressive, but appear to be driven by a purpose perhaps only they can understand. The other fauna are the silent, deep-sea creatures, fish of every shape and size, drawn to the controlled temperature and abundant resources.

Sounds are soft. The hum of the machinery is pervasive, a low drone. The drip of water is a constant rhythm. Other things move. *Things with legs and pincers, things with far too many eyes*, skitter in the darkest corners.

The world of the Foundry is governed by a patient, slow dynamic. Processes take days, weeks, months. The city seems to have stood for a long time, weathered by the abyss, a state of settled form.

*The rule of the world* is production. The Foundry exists to create—but what it creates is unclear. Perhaps the goal is survival in the deep, a slow, unending, and silent act of industrial artistry.
