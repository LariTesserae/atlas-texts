The world is held within a single, colossal stone. It is a perfect ovoid, miles across, but cracked at one point as though once struck by a cosmic hammer. On its surface, the landscape is a sparse, shallow valley, where water weeps from the porous stone, collecting in meandering rivulets. The air is thick, saturated, and warm, but the darkness is profound, a velvet curtain drawn across everything. No natural light reaches this place.

Vegetation, tenacious and otherworldly, clings to the rock. It's a profusion of forms: broad, bioluminescent fungi that pulse with an inner light, mosses like spun glass that shimmer in the darkness, and vines that creep and writhe, their surfaces slick with moisture. Though abundant, the life here is scattered, almost skeletal. Each plant, each creature, has unfathomable space to itself, a consequence of the vastness within this small world.

Sounds echo eerily, amplified by the cavernous stone. The slow drip of water, the low hum of the bioluminescent flora, the rustling of unseen fauna, all combine into a constant, resonating chorus. A deep, almost subsonic growl permeates the air at irregular intervals, a sense of immense presence felt more than heard.

The ancient, eroded stone reveals layers. They hint at vast aeons of time, cycles upon cycles of water and wind, of life and death, all slowly shaping the landscape. The surface is almost flat; millennia have worn down any sharp features. The processes here are subtle, patient, barely perceptible by a fleeting observer.

Signs of a forgotten civilization are scattered around. Structures of impossible geometry, built from a material that seems to absorb rather than reflect the light, are embedded within the stone. These monoliths are impossibly perfect, but also eroded – remnants of a technology beyond comprehension. They suggest a mastery of physics unknown to our world. The purpose of these artifacts, and of the civilization that bore them, is buried in the stone's timeless dust.

Fauna thrives in the darkness, but are rarely seen. They are creatures evolved for the shadows, adapted to the wetness and the eerie chorus. Some are enormous, slow-moving things – their presence sensed by the ground that trembles when they move. Others are small, almost invisible creatures, flitting among the luminous flora. 

The weirder aspects of this world are revealed slowly. The way time doesn't appear to exist in the same way here, or the way the stone itself vibrates with purpose. The constant sense that you're being observed, that the world is more alive, more aware than it seems. The slow, inexorable pull of the stone's surface, the way it seems to be drawing everything into itself. And the unsettling knowledge that, deep within the valley, something waits, something ancient and immense, that is slowly waking.
