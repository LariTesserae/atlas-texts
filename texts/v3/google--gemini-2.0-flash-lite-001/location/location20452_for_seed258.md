The world unfolds, a slow exhalation of **stone and bloom**. It is a place of impossible geometry, where the mountains themselves are sculpted from colossal, living flowers. Their petals, thick as ancient trees, unfurl in a perpetual, silent dance, forming the very cliffs and peaks. The "mountains" are less geological formations and more colossal blooms, and the stone of the cliffs is an incredibly hard, petrified petal formation. The air is thick with the scent of pollen and something acrid, almost metallic, a by-product of the petrification process.

**Water** cascades everywhere, a constant, shimmering rain. It's not the sky that provides it, however. The gargantuan blooms actively *weep*; their immense surfaces, always glistening with condensation, release water in a fine, almost imperceptible mist that hangs low in the valleys. This keeps the air constantly humid, like a perpetual twilight after a downpour. The **elevation** is significant, but not severe, with the entire landscape sloping gently upwards. Valleys are carved by the weeping blooms, their surfaces a complex web of channels and pools.

The **vegetation** is absolute, a complete conversion of everything. The geological substrate has been utterly replaced by it. The only “ground” is either the stony petal, or the pools of water. Nothing else exists here. There is no substrate for it to grow on, because all those materials have *become* plant. This "world" is entirely **built**, but not in the conventional sense. There has never been an architect, or a tool, or even a cell. No visible **technology** is present, only an exquisite biological self-construction beyond human comprehension. The scale is massive, **open**, a regional tapestry woven from petal and stream. The air vibrates with a **loud**, persistent hum, a resonance that seems to emanate from the blooms themselves, an unseen life-force that keeps them slowly unfolding, like lungs breathing.

The sun shines **normally**, its light catching the crystalline droplets that cling to the petal-stone. The **density** is surprisingly **sparse**. Despite the total vegetation, the spaces between the blooms are vast, echoing with the sound of the constant weeping and the hum. The few creatures that **rarely** stir are small, iridescent things, perhaps the size of birds, that flit through the valleys. The place feels deeply **weird**, unsettling, like a dream that has taken root in reality. There are no signs of time passing or change occurring at all. The entire world is in **never-changing** equilibrium. The blooms will continue to weep, and the sun will continue to shine, and the hum will never cease. This is a universe, complete and self-contained, and everything about it — the shape of every valley, the texture of every petal — exists in a state of perpetually forming and simultaneously complete.

There are no rules, not in our sense. There is simply *being*. The world is more of a constant, self-similar expression than a structure or set of relationships. There are not even beginnings and endings, only expressions, each moment mirroring the whole.
