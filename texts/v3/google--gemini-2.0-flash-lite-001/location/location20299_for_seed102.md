The world is a single, immense geode. Its outer shell, a mosaic of obsidian shards and pale, heat-blistered sandstone, gives way to a shallow, perfectly circular depression. The air inside is thick, smelling of pollen and the faint, metallic tang of exhaled breath.

The silence is the first deception. It presses in, a suffocating blanket woven from static. Then, the sound begins. It starts as a whisper, a low thrumming that seems to emanate from the very stone, a vibration felt more than heard. It grows, resolves into the chitter of insects, the rustle of leaves, the rasp of something dragging across the ochre ground. It is *loud*, a cacophony that, rather than deafening, paradoxically enhances the interior, intimate nature of the world.

This interior is a garden. 

The earth here, a fine, almost crystalline sand, is a deep, bruised purple. It does not shift or flow, retaining every footprint, every scuff, every gentle breeze. The sand is cool to the touch, and warmed from above by a dim, diffused light, like the glow of distant embers. 

Vegetation thrives. Strange, bioluminescent fungi cast an eerie, ethereal glow, clinging to the walls of the geode and intertwining with towering flora. These plants are the source of the persistent insect sounds: their petals and fronds have become homes to a riotous fauna. The largest of these plants are tall, almost alien ferns, with leaves that shimmer with iridescent colors. Patches of moss, a vibrant, impossible blue, carpet the ground, punctuated by the occasional outcropping of obsidian. Even more fantastical are the plants that appear to be made of polished bone, radiating a warm, internal fire.

Water is absent, but the plants are vibrant, thriving. They consume the mineral-rich sand, exhaling moisture through pores invisible to the eye.

The elevation is deceiving; this isn't a cavity. It is a single, vast garden, its boundaries defined by the smooth curve of the geode’s wall, which disappears into impenetrable darkness far above. The only structures are small nests and temporary shelters, built from woven plant fibers or rudimentary structures carved into the smooth, purple sand, the work of equally simple tools: flint blades, bone needles, tools made of carved and polished wood.

The fauna presents as both abundant and strange. Insects, of every imaginable variety, hum and chirp and scurry. Creatures with iridescent exoskeletons and multiple, multifaceted eyes hunt among the fungi. Reptiles, small and quick, with skin like polished jade, dart between the plants. More curious are the creatures that resemble large, flightless birds, their feathers the same blue as the moss, their calls resonant. Their eyes are dark and intelligent, and they seem to be aware of the visitor.

Erosion is minimal. Any disturbance to the environment is immediately healed, filled in with sand, and returned to its natural form. The landscape itself is in an ideal stasis – everything remains fixed as it is, unchanged by time or external forces. The sky is dark, a deep, velvety black, where the geode's interior is not lit from above, but in the center, a dim point of yellow light. It never changes. 

This is a place where life flourishes, but it does so within strict boundaries. It’s a microcosm of something larger, more complex, impossible to access or comprehend. It is the echo of a lost world, its secrets sealed within a single, perfect garden.
