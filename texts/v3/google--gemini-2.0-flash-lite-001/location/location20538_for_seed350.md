The world is a wound. It bleeds water, a perpetual, churning deluge. The source, or perhaps the wound itself, is hidden somewhere in the roiling horizon – a smear of bruised violet and sickly green. Rain is not falling; it *is*, a solid wall of water that occupies every cubic meter of space. It's warm, this water, like a bath left too long in the sun, and thick with the oily sheen of something manufactured.

Below, the land struggles to emerge, a fractured skin of jagged, wet rock. Elevation is a joke. Islands, if they can even be called that, rise only a few meters above the relentless tide. They are young, raw, barely formed, their edges still sharp and unforgiving. Erosion is a constant, a brutal sculpting force that tears at the fragile surfaces. These are places of violent, immediate change; a new island might appear overnight, only to be sheared away by the deafening, constant roar.

Beneath the water-curtain, the architecture of the world is revealed – and it is industrial. Twisted metal structures, half-submerged and encrusted with barnacles, are all that remain of what might once have been. Factories, judging by the size of some of the ruins, but their function is impossible to discern in this place. Mechanical arms, tangled in the slick rock, reach skyward. Engines, forever running, their purpose lost to time. The engines are not silent, nor are they a discrete sound. Rather, it merges into the perpetual white noise of the water itself.

Vegetation clings desperately. Across the islands, life asserts itself. Lush growths of phosphorescent kelp and alien, rubbery plants fight for a foothold on the submerged rock. Dense, clinging, and vibrant, their colours offer the only relief against a monochrome world. The plants pulse faintly with an inner light, a desperate plea for recognition in the dim depths.

There is no fauna. Only the machines.

The industrial hum is deafening. More than sound, it vibrates through everything – through the water, through the rock, through your very bones. Coupled with the torrent of rain, it’s a physical force, reshaping everything, pulling everything apart at the seams. And yet, this is not a world of destruction. This is a world of constant, frantic creation–a furious act of making that has gone terribly wrong. Buildings coalesce and decompose with impossible speed, and the boundaries between object and environment are fluid. The rules here are mutable.

This is a place where boundaries are not fixed, where identities dissolve in the constant rush. It's not a place for the weak, the unadaptable. But for the things that remain, the things that can find purchase in the raw and the ruin, there is a terrible, hypnotic beauty. This is a wound, yes, but it is also a birth, a fever dream composed of water and industry, and a strange, unknowable kind of life.
