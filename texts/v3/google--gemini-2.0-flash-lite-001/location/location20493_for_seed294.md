The world is a vast, submerged plateau. A deep ocean covers it, but the pressure is not crushing. It’s as if the world has been meticulously carved, not by water, but *in* water, the very substance itself the sculptor, and the air is just the most diluted kind of water vapor. The plateau stretches for leagues, a flat expanse of dark, obsidian rock, etched and worn by eons of torrential, mineral-rich rain. Any "hills" are merely the remnants of colossal, geometric structures, now eroded and softened, their forms hinting at impossible origins.

The temperature is frigid, but not deadly. The water itself is perpetually churning, a slow, silent maelstrom. It’s hard to tell where the surface is; the dim light diffuses through the water, creating a perpetual twilight. The light isn't a single source. It emanates from the internal structures of the buildings, casting an organic glow that feels like bioluminescence, but without the pulsing of living things. No sun, no moon, just this internal radiance, shifting and swirling like spectral aurora borealis painted on deep water.

There is no plant life, as such. However, the plateau is teeming with fauna, though it isn’t the kind one would expect. Instead of fish or crustaceans, strange, crystalline creatures drift through the water. They are constructed in impossible forms—cubes nested within spheres, helixes that stretch unbroken for miles, and impossible combinations of these patterns—and they constantly reconfigure, their facets catching and reflecting the dim light. They’re like living geodes, each one a universe unto itself. They lack what we call 'organs,' they are pure pattern, driven by some energetic force we cannot understand, though it feels akin to electrical, gravitational, and mathematical forces. What is their function? What do they *do*? No one knows. They are a kind of endless, silent performance.

The world is entirely built, but the builders are gone. The "buildings" are not buildings in a conventional sense; more like vast, crystalline labyrinths that have been ground down by an eternity of erosion, their original forms obscured by time and the ceaseless, turbulent water. Any original structures would be impossible to define, at least for any earthly mind. They stretch out, past any immediate view, into the gathering darkness. The plateau is a tapestry of intersecting structures that form a whole more complicated than it's parts. The built environment *is* the environment.

There is no technology, in any way we would understand it. But the very existence of such intricate structures, the crystalline fauna, and the ambient energy that keeps the world dynamic–it suggests an intelligence, a purpose, that transcends our understanding of tools and machinery. It’s more akin to bio-architecture, but without the organic. The water itself seems to *know* how to shape the creatures, the world. It’s as if the water is the device.

The world is mostly quiet, except for the subtle, constant grinding of water against stone, and the gentle clicking sound of the crystalline creatures, which grows into a dull roar in the densest areas. More sounds are heard deep within the constructed structures, patterns that may emerge from the geometry of the structures. It's too complex to discern individual sounds, but the totality hums with a deep, unknowable music.

The world is in a state of perpetual, chaotic change. The crystalline creatures shift and reform, entire sections of the plateau crumble, only to reassemble themselves elsewhere. The light subtly shifts, revealing and concealing hidden patterns. Everything is constantly being created and destroyed, rearranged and refigured, but the essential world, the submerged plateau of obsidian and the endless stream of crystalline entities, remains.

This is a world of ancient, settled-in design; a final state. It makes an unsettling, strange feeling. One would get the impression of having arrived too close to the end of a long, unknowable process.
