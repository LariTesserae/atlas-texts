The city of Cinderhold is cradled in a valley, a shallow bowl carved from a world still finding its shape. The mountains, just a hike away but always in sight, are young, their peaks jagged and unforgiving, the stone a dark, volcanic grey that seems to absorb the sparse sunlight. Rain, a fine, gritty mist, kisses the valley floor more often than it pours, leaving everything perpetually damp. Water clings to the sculpted, angular architecture, the surfaces of the metal walkways, and the rough-hewn stone of the buildings.

This is a place of contrasts, of a slow, deliberate march against a backdrop of wild, youthful energy. Built in the valley floor, Cinderhold is a marvel of industrial ingenuity, a testament to what humanity can accomplish in a harsh environment. Buildings rise like dark, geometric trees, their structures interwoven with pipes and cables, some humming with electricity, others hissing with steam. Smoke, almost entirely absent, is a sign this is not a pollution-choked environment. Instead, the air is clean, smelling of metal, and the distant, subtle sweetness of the strange, fungal blooms that cluster on the lower reaches of the buildings.

The city feels sparse, despite its obvious size. Buildings are spaced apart, connected by elevated walkways and a network of canals that snake through the city. These waterways are a key feature; they’re not wide, but they gleam, their waters used for industry in ways I cannot grasp.

The dominant sound is a mechanical symphony. The relentless churn of gears, the thud of pistons, the hiss of steam, and the rhythmic clanging of some unseen foundry echo through the metallic canyons. Yet, interspersed within this industrial rhythm is an undeniable undercurrent of life. Birds with iridescent plumage flit amongst the towering structures, their calls a sharp contrast to the bass of the machinery. Larger, sleek, reptilian creatures, perhaps a distant relative of the lizard, stalk the walkways, their movements slow and deliberate, their eyes gleaming. These creatures don’t seem to fear the humans.

The city's ecology is an enigma. The fungal blooms, in shades of violet and crimson, grow luxuriantly on the damp stone, their caps dotted with shimmering spores. Their presence is a puzzle, but they provide a base for a food chain that supports many other species. Strange, bioluminescent insects buzz in the humid air, their light painting intricate patterns against the dark surfaces. All this is maintained, it feels, not by nature herself, but by unseen mechanisms built into the city’s bones. 

The humans, if that’s the correct word, are few. They are lean, their skin dusted with fine grey ash, their eyes sharp and focused. They move with purposeful efficiency, their clothing functional and dark, almost blending into the environment. They keep to themselves; though I can't say if they are friendly or not. There is, however, a sense of deep purpose and shared effort. 

The entire city is not simply built. It is being *made*. Raw materials are forged into finished goods, then into intricate structures. Everything, from food, to water, to shelter, to the very infrastructure itself, is carefully designed and organized. 

The rules of Cinderhold are unspoken but clear. Waste is unforgivable. Efficiency is paramount. The city, and its process of self-creation, is the only religion that matters.
