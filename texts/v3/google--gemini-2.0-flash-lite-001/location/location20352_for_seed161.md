The Sunken Grove.

It begins in the depths. Not of water, for there is none, but of elevation. A pit, a chasm, a basin carved into the rock, miles deep, and at its bottom, a city. A city that *grows* rather than thrives.

Here, the world has long since settled into its final form. Mountains long ago eroded into the dust that now forms the valley floor's base. The scale is small, just a few square miles. The air is warm, thick, and dim, the light filtering in from whatever remains of the world above. 

The heart of the grove is the city itself, a layered, dense network of structures carved from the crimson rock of the chasm walls, and constructed from woven, pale-green vegetation, almost like living buildings. It's a place of almost unimaginable age. Stone is the primary material, but it is deeply weathered, smoothed by wind, and worn, and so old that it seems to have become part of the very landscape. 

The architecture is strange, with repeating patterns and curves, and the only metal is the iron-rich stone that is used for decorative and ceremonial purposes. It is a city of layers, where old structures have grown over and inside others. Here there is no simple up or down; the city spirals. Each layer is connected by winding tunnels and bridges fashioned from the ever-present, vibrant vegetation that has woven itself into everything. 

This is the lifeblood of the grove. The lush vegetation, fed by the light that filters down from above, which is mostly a dull, orange glow. The plants themselves seem to be both source and solution, the fabric and the architecture, living and serving their purpose. They are intricately woven together, almost like living walls. Strange, bulbous fruits hang from vines, and even more bizarre creatures.

Life is abundant, though. The Sunken Grove is teeming with life. Not as a thriving ecosystem but as a slowly-unraveling one. It is loud. The wind whistles through the chasm, the constant susurrus of the vegetation as it grows and shifts, the calls of the innumerable insects and other animals that populate the city. These are creatures unlike any you know, creatures from every plane of the imagination, each one of them a facet of this strange place. Many of the creatures are blind, adapted to the perpetual dimness, and rely on acute senses of smell and hearing.

Movement is slow. The cycles of the grove are patient, unhurried. The city grows, yes, but it does so at an almost geologic pace. The world shifts, but not in any way you can easily perceive. The erosion is a slow grinding, the wearing down of the rock, the slow disintegration into dust. The plants grow, reaching, weaving, incorporating the remnants of the broken structures. The seasons are measured not in days, but in generations.

The sounds of the city are a constant, complex symphony. The chittering of insects, the rustle of leaves, the calls of the creatures, the occasional rumble of shifting rock. It is a world where every presence is known, where every corner of the layered structures is a habitat. Within the city, there is only the whisper of the growth. It is the only sign of what remains.

The rules of the world are simple, cruel. There is just the rock, the light, the wind, and the plants. The animals are parts of these, with their own natures, but ultimately, they are subject to those primal forces. Every creature, every building, every thing of stone and foliage will, one day, return to the soil.

The Sunken Grove is a place of decay, but not of defeat. It is a place of endless becoming, a slow, patient dance of life and death, of creation and destruction, all played out within the walls of a forgotten city, at the bottom of a chasm that once scraped the sky.
