The city of **Veridia** clings to the uppermost branches of a colossal, sprawling tree. Not a tree in the conventional sense, but a singular, impossibly vast organism whose form transcends easy definition. From the surface of the drowned world far below, only the faintest movements might be perceived — ripples in the placid, deep green water, reflecting the filtered sunlight that penetrates the perpetual, heavy rain. This rain, ceaseless and gentle, is the lifeblood of Veridia.

The tree-city itself is constructed almost entirely of living matter. The "trunk" — if such a word can apply — is a colossal pillar of interwoven vines and coalesced plant fiber, thicker than any mountain. Its bark, a mosaic of mosses, lichens, and resilient, luminous fungi, absorbs the constant rain, which slowly feeds the myriad ecosystems that thrive within and upon the tree's surface. Elevated walkways, bridges, and platforms are constructed from living wood, grown and shaped by the inhabitants. Buildings are not built, but *grown* — giant, bulbous structures of woven leaves, sturdy enough to withstand the wind and the slow, relentless erosion of the rain.

The scale is cathedral-like, each building a natural marvel of organic architecture. The main thoroughfares are broad avenues carved into the tree's surface, winding through a network of dwellings, temples, and communal spaces. The "streets" themselves are living things, covered in thick carpets of moss that absorb sound, creating an almost eerie quietude broken only by the gentle patter of rain.

The water parameter dominates. Veridia is almost entirely submerged. From the highest platforms, the submerged world below is a blurred, green-tinted abyss. Water cascades from every surface. Within the city, water flows through aqueducts carved into the tree’s living wood: it is captured, stored, and regulated; it feeds the city’s many ecosystems.

The temperature is cool and damp. The air is heavy with the scent of wet earth, decaying vegetation, and the subtle, almost metallic tang of the fungal growth that pervades everything. Light filters through the dense canopy of leaves above, creating a diffused, emerald-tinged illumination, normal for an overcast day.

The inhabitants, the Veridian, are attuned to the slow, patient rhythms of their world. Their technology is primitive: crafted tools of wood, bone, and stone are essential. They understand the intricacies of the tree's growth and the delicate balance of the plant-dominated ecosystem. Their skills lie in shaping living matter, coaxing and guiding the growth of plants into their desired forms. They have mastered a form of bio-engineering.

Fauna is abundant but unusual. Giant, bioluminescent insects flit through the air, their buzzing a constant hum that contributes to an overall feeling of loud-ness. Huge, slug-like creatures, covered in shimmering scales, slowly traverse the walkways, feeding on the moss and fungi. They are treated with respect, as if they are part of the architecture, a living scaffolding. Higher up, among the highest branches, unique avians perch. Many have adapted to the submerged environment, or the constant rain.

The slow dynamic means that the city evolves over generations. Structures are not replaced, but added to. The tree grows, and the city expands with it, always in balance. The rain slowly wears away at the city, but the Veridian understand this: Erosion is part of the life cycle. They constantly work to maintain and reinforce, creating new growth to replace what is lost.

The "weirdness" comes from the inherent strangeness of a city built on, and *of*, a single organism, submerged in water, teeming with life, and sustained by a civilization that exists in harmony with its surroundings. The Veridian perceive the tree and water as interconnected, a grand organism. The rules of their world follow a biological and botanical logic, the city, a testament to the symbiotic relationship between humans and nature.
