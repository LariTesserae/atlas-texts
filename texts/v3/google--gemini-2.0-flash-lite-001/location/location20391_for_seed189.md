The air hangs thick and warm, a nearly solid thing. It presses against my skin, slick with a perpetual near-rain. Not falling, not really. Instead, the moisture condenses on the impossibly vast surfaces of the *walls* — I can't think of them as anything else — surrounding me. These walls are the dominant feature of this place. They are not made of rock or soil; they are constructed of plant.

It’s all plant. Everywhere.

I find myself in a cave, though the word feels wrong. It's a vast hollow, but instead of rough stone, the walls are formed of a living, breathing, pulsing biomass. This isn't a forest. It's something… else.

The air thrums with a low, deep sound, a vibration that seems to emanate from the walls themselves. It's a sound I feel more than hear; a constant pressure on my chest. This is a place of unbelievable scale—hidden, pocketed, nested within itself. While I'm in a cave, one moment I can almost feel the outside pressure of the world. Then, it's immediately gone, and I'm within again, alone in the chamber.

The 'walls' are not smooth. They undulate, a topography of impossible angles and fleshy folds. Great veins of deep crimson and emerald green pulse beneath a translucent, almost glistening, skin. No detail is static; the surface constantly shifts, refolds, and grows. It’s early formation, young, but I don't see any erosion. Its youth seems to be about how the walls grow and flow.

Embedded within the larger structures is a second layer: an architecture of smaller, yet similar, plant-matter. It's like looking at the musculature inside the body, veins and arteries supporting its processes. This network is dense and complex, seemingly without purpose on its surface.

Warm, dim sunlight filters into the cave via openings that constantly shift, appearing and disappearing at random. These aren't holes, but instead, glimpses through the outer layer. There’s a normal light in here, but the sources are the shifting apertures. The light makes the fleshy walls gleam with an unearthly luminescence.

The few creatures I've seen in this cavern seem out of place, almost like hallucinations. One resembled a giant, iridescent beetle with legs of woven vines, navigating through the structures' intricacies. Another was a shimmering, dragonfly-like creature with wings of pure, refracted light. They move with an impossible grace, their presence almost accidental, as if they're simply flitting through another's dream. Fauna is rare, but strange.

The dominant sound I hear is the walls itself—a constant, thrumming hum. Sometimes it rises in pitch, a screech that makes my teeth ache. This is when the cave seems to... *shift*. The walls move around, slowly at first, then at a speed that makes me want to look away. One moment a tunnel may be blocked, then the wall bends and it's clear once more. This active nature is part of the world.

There's a subtle odor too, a mixture of sweet and something metallic, like the smell that comes from an open wound but richer, more alive.

This place feels *wrong*. Physics doesn't apply the way it should. Space bends as I look at it. There are no straight lines, no stable structures with permanence. Everything is in a constant state of becoming. The laws of reality have softened here, and the bizarre takes root. The weirdness is everywhere.

The only "tool" I see is an object, of the same vegetation, formed into a rough, but functional shape. I don't know who would make it or why.

Perhaps the rules of this world are that there are no rules. That only growth, change, and the constant flux of the living wall persist. Survival here is a matter of adapting and accepting the impossible.
