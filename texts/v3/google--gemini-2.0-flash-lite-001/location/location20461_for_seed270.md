The air hangs thick and heavy, a cool, barely-breathable miasma. It smells of ozone and something metallic, like blood that's been left out in the rain for a thousand years. Light filters down from no discernible source, a perpetual twilight that reveals a world of close, layered surfaces.

The ground is a mosaic. Not of tiles, but of the mineral detritus churned by forgotten machinery. Jagged shards of obsidian-like glass, dull gray crystals, and plates of what might have been polished metal are packed tight, almost fused together by the sheer age and pressure. There’s no soil, no sediment, only the endless, ancient grinding of things against each other. Erosion has flattened much of the landscape, but the sheer density of debris ensures that you're constantly stepping over, around, or through something.

The structures here are scattered, more like artifacts left behind by a civilization of giants than buildings. Wrecked gears, their teeth ground away, lean against the jagged walls of what might have been a foundry. Twisted metal girders, half-buried in the inorganic dust. There are no smooth surfaces, no clean lines, just the brutal elegance of a machine that worked long after its creators were gone. The structures are scarred, pocked by some relentless, invisible attrition, yet they still retain the echo of their original, complex forms.

There are no plants, no signs of anything remotely resembling life as we understand it. Yet, this place is saturated with *fauna*. And what fauna! The air teems with it. The most common creatures are crystalline, many-legged things – the size of rats. They scuttle across the broken ground, their bodies catching the ambient light and refracting it in bursts of color. Something akin to birds, but made of animated steel. Thin, feathered wings of polished metal are kept in constant, erratic motion by means of tiny, whirring engines. These metallic birds, too numerous to count, fill the air with their tireless industry. They move in swarms, a cloud of hard edges and endless clatter.

The soundscape here is deafening. Mechanical screeching, the hiss of leaking gas, the *thrum* of some unseen energy source that vibrates in the stone beneath your feet. The chittering, the grinding, the constant rustling as the crystalline insects swarm across the landscape, feeding on something unseen, leaving trails of iridescent dust in their wake. And always, the metallic song of the birds.

It *moves* here. The ground tremors with a slow, almost imperceptible pulse. The shattered landscape is not static, it is a scene of perpetual, albeit ancient, industry. The metallic birds are the apex predators, and the crystalline insects are both their food and the harbingers of corrosion. Something is slowly, inexorably, breaking down. The slow processes of an unknown chemistry are constantly at play, creating what looks like a perpetual, low-heat salt marsh, but filled with the detritus of some immense, unknowable machine, now long in its death throes.

This place gives you a feeling of immense scale, but it's also a miniature. You could hold it in your hand and find the whole of its ecosystem. You are a mote in the center of a self-contained, perfect doom. It is an industrial wasteland, a cathedral of discarded gears and shattered crystal, a testament to an unknown purpose. A forgotten pocket of a world that once *was*, still grinding toward its unknowable end.
