You are not underwater. You are *in* the water, utterly surrounded, a mote of something almost imperceptible, caught in a thrumming oceanic heart.

The vessel you occupy is small, a sphere, barely a meter across. It's built of something that both absorbs and transmits sound, a kind of ceramic woven with incredibly fine wires, and the whole of it is seamless. Through it, the world both resonates and is revealed.

You are adrift within the **Hydraulic City**, which is more properly a **Hydraulic Engine**. There isn't an inch of solid matter within miles. The architecture exists in swirling form — currents of a dark, oily water threaded with structures of polished obsidian and dull, rust-red metal. The flow of water is constant, a roaring torrent, and the whole city is a symphony of machinery.

Here and there are structures that could be called buildings, but they are closer to massive, complex machines, their surfaces etched with glyphs and arcane symbols. They pulse with energy, their guts a dance of gears and pistons, of cables and conduits that vanish into the churning depths. Steam billows from vents, a dark, hot fog. 

There is no sunlight. The only illumination comes from the city itself — from the countless points of light that bloom and fade within the churning currents. Some are simple, a flickering spark; others are complex, fractal patterns that shift and rearrange like living things. The lights are not bright, not really, but they are numerous, and the water reflects and refracts them in ways that make it impossible to tell where they end, or begin. It's a dim world, mostly, but your sphere of being is never without something to see.

The water itself is your medium. It is not cold, but warm, almost hot, and it has weight. It presses against you, a constant, physical presence. It is the lifeblood and the substance of the city. 

There are no plants, and no creatures that could be called fauna in any recognizable sense. 

But there *are* things moving in the depths. You glimpse them sometimes — shadows that twist and writhe, bursts of light within the water. They are quick, impossible to focus on, and you are not sure if they are alive (though the idea seems wrong), or if they are just the by-product of some process far larger than you. 

The city's sound is constant and deafening. It's the roar of water, of machinery, of something else you can't identify, a deep bass that vibrates through the water and through you. Even the sphere that encases you is not enough to dampen it completely.

This is a place of industrial might. The city produces something, though you cannot tell what it is. The rhythmic pulse within the sphere marks patterns as you drift toward the center of the structure — the heart of the engine. The currents are a map, a series of invisible pathways, and you are being carried toward the center. 

The world feels ancient, built long ago, built to last. But the churning currents, the endless motion, the violence of water and machinery speak of instability. You suspect that the city is not meant to last forever. Perhaps it already has overstayed its purpose. Perhaps it will end suddenly, in a moment of cataclysmic change, dragged away by the same currents that carry you now.
