The air hangs heavy, a curdled, metallic fog. It claws at the throat, bitter and cold. This isn't the cold of winter, but the cold of a deep, unlit cavern, scraped raw by machinery. The wind, if it can be called that, is a constant pressure, a hand pressing down on the valley. Everything exists under the oppressive weight of the sky.

This is a valley, but not one sculpted by rivers or wind. Instead, the land seems to have been vomited forth. Jagged obsidian peaks, still raw and unweathered, claw at the dim sky. These aren't mountains so much as the teeth of some gigantic, slumbering beast. The black stone is streaked with veins of sickly green, a phosphorescent glow emanating from within, like worms or diseased sap.

Water clings to everything. It drips from the peaks, condenses on the sparse, strange vegetation, and pools in a multitude of unnatural hollows. This isn't clear, life-giving water, but a thick, oily fluid, shimmering with an unsettling iridescent sheen. It tastes of rust and iron.

The vegetation is a bizarre mockery of life. Giant, fungal blooms the size of small trees sprout from the exposed rock, pulsing with a faint, internal light. Their caps are a sickly, bruised purple, and they release spores that smell of diesel and decay. In between these fungal giants, smaller plants grow--pale, twisted things that seem to writhe in the perpetual dimness, their leaves coated in the oily water.

The air is thick with the sounds of industry. The rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* of unseen engines mixes with the piercing *shriek* of grinding metal, and the hissing release of pressured steam. These mechanical symphonies are constant, unchanging. Everything speaks the same language.

Yet, despite the industrial nature of this place, there are no visible structures. There is no evidence of who, or what, built this. Only the sounds of the machinery remain, its relentless operation defining the landscape, but its source unseen.

But it isn’t quite empty. Life, in a distorted, abhorrent form, has taken root. Small, six-legged creatures with carapaces of polished steel scuttle across the rocks. Birds, their feathers the colour of toxic waste, flit through the air, their songs a distorted parody of nature. Then there are other creatures. Things best left unseen. Things that are not quite of this world.

The static nature of the valley only adds to the unsettling feeling that something is very wrong. Despite the sounds of constant motion, of relentless production, nothing *changes*. The clouds above the valley never move. The oily water never flows. The light, a perpetual twilight, never shifts. The landscape is frozen in a perpetual state of creation, and yet, there is nowhere for anything to be created.

And the weirdness. That is the final ingredient, the one that truly unravels the fabric of reality. Here, the laws of physics are bent, and broken. Distances are uncertain. Objects flicker in and out of existence. Shadows stretch and distort, revealing glimpses of other, impossible places. Time itself seems to have lost all meaning, and the valley appears to be slowly folding in on itself.
