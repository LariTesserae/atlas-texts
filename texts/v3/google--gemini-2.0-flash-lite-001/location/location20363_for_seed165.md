The wind howls, a constant, keening note that has no beginning and no end. It whips across a plateau of obsidian, polished smooth by millennia of glacial melt and the grinding kiss of the eternal storm. This is the Scar, a place where reality itself seems to fray, where the threads of existence thin and snap like brittle filaments.

Elevation is everything here. Above, the sky is a bruised purple, a heavy, oppressive presence. It presses down, forcing a perpetual twilight. The light is dim, filtered through layers of unseen things, casting long, distorted shadows that dance with a life of their own.

Water is a fickle mistress. Rain falls, sometimes, but it’s a cold, stinging drizzle that becomes icy the moment it touches the ground. The occasional thaw reveals hidden veins of dark, viscous fluid, seeping from deep within the obsidian. It's not water, not exactly. It is a memory of water, a phantom echo of a time when the plateau might have been different. These pockets of moisture refuse to pool, instead sinking into the rock as quickly as they appear.

Vegetation is a whisper of what could be, clinging stubbornly to life in the face of impossible odds. Sparse, skeletal trees, their bark a gnarled, obsidian-like material, huddle in the lee of wind-carved canyons. They strain towards that dim light like desperate supplicants. These trees are never touched by any changing, their posture fixed, frozen in a constant posture of leaning. Beyond the trees, the hard, barren ground shows patches of something like moss, but it feels like solidified shadow, and it does not shift or breathe like typical plant life.

The scarcity of temperature is almost welcome in this cold place; yet, it is a cold that does not change at all. The biting wind is the same temperature every moment of every day, and everything around it remains untouched by the diurnal cycle.

Built structures exist, though barely. They are scattered across the plateau, remnants of a civilization lost to the ravages of not time, but to eternity. Small, black stone structures, carved with symbols that shift and writhe as you look at them. They are open to the sky and empty inside, save for a layer of the dark, viscous fluid that has begun to accumulate as it falls from the air.

Tech is a forgotten dream. There are no tools here, no devices, no marks of any intelligent presence beyond the faint tracery of the built structures. It is as if all thought, all innovation, has been scoured away by the wind and the relentless pressure of the sky.

The dynamic is static; an eternal ice, unshifting, unmoving, in total equilibrium. The erosion is a slow grinding, the plateau forever in its weathered form. The scale is open, the plateau stretching as far as the eye can see, broken only by the fractured sky and the occasional, impossible angles of the structures.

The fauna is rare, and what does exist is as peculiar as the landscape that birthed it. Shadow-striders, creatures of pure darkness, stalk the plateau. They have no physical form, and you can only sense them as a chilling presence, like the absence of heat, a momentary flicker of broken light that is not seen but felt.

The air itself seems to be alive, a buzzing energy that hums and vibrates throughout your very being. Reality breaks down with disturbing regularity. Distant sounds become close, and close sounds vanish. The shapes of the structures warp and shift. The wind carries voices that speak a language you can almost understand, then it vanishes.

The sound is loud: the wind, the whispers, the grinding of the obsidian as unseen forces shift the earth. But the sound is also empty, vast, and eternal, a constant that does not shift. It consumes all other noise, then fills the silence.

This is the Scar. A place of endless, unyielding cold, where reality is a fragile thing, constantly fraying under perpetual, impossible, forces. It is a place of absence, a vast, hollow echo of something that came before, never changed, and will never be.
