The world is vast, a continental expanse of raw geology and nascent infrastructure. It's a place still under construction, a dream slowly solidifying into reality. The air hangs heavy with moisture, a perpetual post-rain atmosphere where condensation clings to everything. This water, though, isn't a surface feature; it's within, seeping through the porous, angular rock that dominates the terrain.

Elevation is significant. The ground rises and falls in slow curves and sharp cliffs, an uneven stage for the city's unfolding. It is cold, an unrelenting chill that bites through even the layers of advanced technology.

The built environment is urban, but not in the way we understand it. It's a city woven into the landscape, a complex interface between the natural world and technologies beyond our comprehension. Structures rise and fall in harmony with the terrain, their forms echoing the harsh angles of the surrounding rocks. The materials are unlike anything we know, but seem to include some form of living metal.

The technology is advanced, bordering on the impossible. Perhaps the very manipulation of the land and its resources constitutes the advanced technology itself, the control of the fundamental forces of nature. The scale of the technology matches the size. There is a cosmic aspect to it all.

Light filters through the atmosphere in a normal way, but it is a filtered light, as if coming through oiled glass. The atmosphere shifts constantly, casting long, distorted shadows and painting the landscape in shades of grey, violet, and pale blue.

Fauna is abundant. These are creatures adapted to the severe conditions. Their forms range from sleek, metallic avian predators to burrowing, bioluminescent worms that tunnel through the rocky undergrowth. The ecology is in constant flux, a delicate dance between predator and prey, the built and the unbuilt. The soundscape is mostly quiet. There is a low hum of the city, a high keen of the predatory birds, the occasional crash of the rock.

This is a world of sparse density. The city does not stretch everywhere; large tracts of untamed rock, water and the sky remain. There are many places where one can be alone.

The world is characterized by constant change. The landscape is dynamic; the processes of erosion mingle with the constructions of the city, each shaping the other in a perpetual cycle. Weather patterns are massive, continental in scale, producing torrential downpours and biting blizzards. These systems affect life.

The world is not particularly weird in itself, but the advanced technology, coupled with the unusual environment, creates a sense of the uncanny. One can never be certain of what is just around the corner.

This is a place of emergent beauty, a landscape pregnant with the promise of future forms. It is cold, wet, and vast, a testament to the power of constant transformation.
