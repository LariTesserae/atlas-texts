The Silent Maw.

Imagine a planet, or what remains of one. What’s left has been ground down, twisted, and reshaped by forces long past. Now, it exists as a desolate plateau of obsidian, stretching beyond the curvature of the horizon. Its surface is fractured into colossal plates, riven by deep, sheer canyons that plunge into absolute blackness. Some of these plates are angled precipitously, leaning against each other like the teeth of a shattered jaw.

This is a world of utter stillness, save for a deafening, omnipresent *hum*. It vibrates through everything, a deep bass note that permeates the very structure of reality. The sound isn't *heard* in the conventional sense, but *felt*, a pressure against the bones, a dull ache in the skull. It is the only sound, unchanging, unyielding.

The temperature is brutally cold, a perpetual, soul-biting chill that permeates the atmosphere. There is no wind, no variation; only the cold of the void. And yet, this isn't a dead-cold. No.

Water. The planet's surface is entirely submerged. The water here is not ordinary, it is the raw, unbroken essence of a drowned world. It is a dense, almost viscous fluid, a deep, fathomless dark that stretches into the blackness of the canyons. The water is everywhere, a colossal, featureless ocean that meets the obsidian plateau at a ragged, constantly shifting waterline.

The plateau itself is devoid of traditional vegetation. There are no plants, no fungi, in any form recognizable to the human eye. But, there is an eerie, unnatural biological element present: a bizarre, crystal-like fauna, abundant throughout the submerged parts of the planet. These creatures, if they can be called that, are multi-faceted crystalline forms, constantly shifting in the dim light. They pulse with an internal luminescence, a soft, ethereal glow that varies in intensity. They drift slowly through the water, in perfect silence. Some are small and delicate, like frozen snowflakes; others are massive, towering structures that dwarf any man-made edifice.

There used to be technology on this planet. What is left of it is now scattered, the ruins of immense industrial complexes, twisted metal skeletons protruding from the obsidian surface. Remnants of colossal engines, gears the size of cities, and broken structures of an unknown purpose stand as monuments to a lost civilization. These structures show signs of colossal energy, remnants of an energy-powered society.

The only "dynamic" aspect in this environment is the water, slowly eroding the base of all material.

The light is dim, diffused, as if filtered through a perpetual overcast. The origin of the light is unclear, as there are no suns or moons visible. The ambient glow illuminates the landscape just enough to see the endless obsidian and the impossible, crystalline fauna within the water. Everything is at once immense and empty, a vast and silent void filled with whispers of a forgotten time.
