The high, cool air is thick with a deafening, constant percussion. It isn't a rhythm or a melody, but a uniform, all-encompassing *thrum*. It permeates everything, vibrating the very ground underfoot. Where is the ground? That’s hard to say. The world isn't a collection of discrete features, but a single, immense outcropping of obsidian. It rises in a jagged plateau, an endless expanse of polished black glass, reflecting the blinding light that pours from the sky. There is no sun, no moon, but the light is as real and as all-encompassing as water.

Water. The surface isn't dry, though there are no puddles, no rivers, no lakes. The obsidian itself weeps. Tiny, glistening droplets bead and coalesce, then crawl across the glassy surface, tracing complex and unpredictable paths before vanishing. It is as if the stone itself is sweating, a slow, continuous exhalation of moisture. The air is heavy, the cool clinging to the skin, but it isn’t damp like a forest, but cold like the breath of the stone.

There is no vegetation here, no life as commonly understood. Yet, something persists. Grotesque, bulbous forms rise from the obsidian, not *from* it, but *of* it, the obsidian flowing like a monstrous, petrified sea. These are not built, not created, yet they are present. They are not plant, not animal, not mineral, but contain qualities of them all. They don't appear to move, but they *shift*. They change, slowly, almost imperceptibly, expanding and contracting, their surfaces reflecting and distorting the landscape, the light, the very world. Sometimes, the obsidian seems to bloom, its surface fracturing to reveal intricate, crystalline structures within, which grow into the bulbous forms for a time before they recede, leaving the surface smooth and featureless once more.

These forms are rare, scattered across the vast plateau, each a grotesque parody of life. They are the only fauna present. And they are the source of the sound — the drumming. Within each, something churns, a low-frequency vibration that builds upon itself to create the constant, deafening hum. These forms are the only presences.

The rules of this world are… strict. Time doesn't seem to pass. The light never fails. The obsidian surface is relentlessly, unforgivingly stable. There are no gradients, no gradual changes, no flow. The erosion is complete, the balance perfect. Everything exists in a state of frozen equilibrium, a singular, unchanging moment.

But the light… it is *too* bright. The sheer intensity is the only thing that suggests weirdness. It doesn't seem to *come* from anything known, but seems to be *of* the world itself, washing over everything like an infinite, formless ocean. The light and the sound, both constant, both deafening, are the only things that break the monotony of the obsidian plain. They are the only indicators of something beyond the stillness.

What could create such a place? What force could sculpt such a landscape, instilling it with such bizarre life and perpetual silence? It's a world seemingly untouched by anything yet imbued with a brutal elegance. It is a mystery, a puzzle, and it is impossible to escape the feeling that its truth lies just beyond the horizon, locked away in the deafening, undying heartbeat of the stone.
