The air hangs thick and warm, a nearly tangible humidity clinging to the high, fractured mesas that dominate the landscape. Rain, frequent and often, has sculpted the rock into a labyrinth of canyons and gullies, each surface weeping with a perpetual dampness. This isn't a downpour, but a slow, persistent weeping from the clouds, a constant condensation that leaves everything slick with moisture.

Sparse, tenacious vegetation claws at the stone. Twisted, gnarled trees, their roots clinging to the barest crevices, dot the slopes. They are not grand forests, but isolated pockets of life, hardy survivors in a world constantly eroded and reshaped. Cacti, their forms distorted by the persistent wind and moisture, stand sentinel on the edges of the plateaus.

The scale of the place is vast. Mesas stretch as far as the eye can see, interconnected by a network of winding roads and canals. Built into the rock, almost as an extension of it, are the remnants of an industry that seems to have long since run its course. The infrastructure is a patchwork of metal, glass, and crumbling concrete, all softened by the endless rain. Factories, their windows shattered and machinery exposed, are slowly being reclaimed by the elements. Canals, once vital, now carry only the overflow from the rain, stained with rust and the ghostly residue of unknown processes.

The noise is deafening, a cacophony that begins in the very stone. The slow, rhythmic groan of the mesas as they shed their surface, creaking and sighing with the weight of the water, forms a deep, constant undertone. This is overlaid by the whine of wind through broken structures, the hiss of the rain, and the distant, rhythmic thud of… something. Some surviving piece of machinery, a forgotten engine that continues its work, regardless of its purpose.

Fauna is rare, but present. Winged things, strange avian creatures, flit through the wet air. Their forms are unlike any known bird – sleek, almost metallic bodies that capture the light, and long, delicate legs. There are whispers of larger creatures in the deep canyons, forms barely glimpsed in the swirling mist, but they are more myth than reality.

The light is normal, but filtered. The constant moisture softens the sunlight, turning the sky into a canvas of shifting grey. The light picks out the complex textures of the stone and the rusting metal, highlighting the wear and tear of a long-past industrial age.

Weirdness permeates everything. The air itself seems to hum with an unseen energy, a faint vibration that can be felt more than heard. Buildings are adorned with strange symbols etched into the stone, lines and circles that appear to shift and change depending on the angle of view. There are whispers of a ‘shifting’ – sections of landscape that flicker, sometimes disappearing for days or even weeks.

The erosion is relentless, but this is a place that has found its balance. The structures are decaying, but they have settled into the landscape, becoming as much a part of it as the stone itself. The slow, patient work of wind and water shapes all things, and the environment is changing, slowly but definitely shaping this landscape.
