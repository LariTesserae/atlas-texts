Here, on the Threshold Plateau, time itself seems to have chosen stasis. The air is thin and cool, biting your exposed skin with a chill that seeps deep into your bones. The light is a constant, a pale diffusion that bends rather than casts, casting no shadows. It's a land of bone-white stone, a vast, undulating expanse that stretches to the horizon in every direction – a plateau that seems to be the very summit of existence.

Water is a forgotten dream here. The rare, twisted semblances of plants cling to the stone, their forms a grotesque parody of life. They are bone-dry, petrified near-twists in the wind, brittle, like the remains of forgotten stories etched into the very fabric of the plateau with the passing of time.

This world is ancient, its valleys and rises smoothed by eons. The erosion has long since ceased, as if the land has reached some final, impossible equilibrium. The few signs of human presence feel ancient, as if the plateau was scoured by something far more potent than water or ice.

Against this still backdrop, the creatures of the Plateau are another story. They are everywhere and nowhere. They come in forms that shift in impossible ways, like dreams made manifest. They are a density beyond the physical, a press of unseen forms and the echo of a forgotten sound that carries itself like the whisper of the old world.

Their forms are varied, but linked by an impossible coherence: there are beings made of shadow that move like liquid, their forms rippling across the stone; creatures of light that flicker and dance on the periphery of vision; and others formed from living stone, which twist with a slow, grinding inevitability. Individually, they are overwhelming; as a whole, they are complete.

The constructions found here are primitive, but they have somehow managed to withstand the endless eons. These are not buildings as much as they are echoes of forms, the shells that remain of long-dead dreams. They're scattered across the plateau, each bearing a unique connection to the land. Some are simple rings of stone, others are structures built with bone and flint. They are like hints, the whispers of those who came here.

The few tools that exist reflect this level. Knives of volcanic glass. Woven baskets of a strange, pliable stone-fibers. These objects hold a unique resonance with the place and the flora and fauna that comprise it.

The rules here are not those of your world. Here, reality itself seems to be breaking down or re-forming, or already at the apex of its own evolution. The dynamic world is gone, replaced with an unbroken stillness. Time itself is bent, twisted, and folded, existing in a state of eternal becoming.

The Sound is a constant echo; the wind is still, but the air carries a hum, as if the very stone is whispering. The faintest sounds, like the grinding of stone against stone, the rustling of the stone-fiber plants, amplify and reverberate through the plateau. It's a silence that is always hinting at a different sort of silence.

This is a place where existence feels less like a progression and more like a collection, a gathering into an endless event. Perhaps it is a place of creation, perhaps destruction, but it is clear that everything here has arrived at its final destination. Here, on the Threshold Plateau, you stand at the edge of forever.
