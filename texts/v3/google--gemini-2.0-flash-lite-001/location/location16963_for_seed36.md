The light is the first thing, and it’s a shock: an implacable white brilliance that punches down through the uneven ceiling a kilometer above, turning the air itself into a sculptor. The ceiling isn't a single, smooth structure; it's a fractal tangle of colossal, petrified fronds, each a kilometer long and as thick around as a city. The light bends and fractures through this latticework, leaving a perpetual, dappled intensity that makes your eyes ache.

The air is *thick* with humidity, but not in any comforting way. It feels like the air itself is being pulled apart, slowly dissolving, turning back into the minerals of which it’s composed. Tiny droplets, too small to see individually, cling to everything – the petrified fronds above, the ground, the surfaces of the few things that aren't plant matter.

The ground is a slow, steady gradient downward, and it's a riot of plant life. Not the kind that grows *on* things—we’re far beyond that. This is more like plant-as-world. The ground itself is a vast, spongy matrix of interwoven roots, fibers, and pulsing, bioluminescent veins. The texture undulates, breathing slowly. At first, you think it's just the roots, but then you notice how the individual strands of the plants are growing together, becoming each other, merging into a single, massive organism.

There are no trees, no shrubs, no grass, in the conventional sense. The plants are all fractal geometries of intricate, interlocking patterns you couldn't trace with your eyes; as though their design is beyond your capacity for description. The colors… beyond. Not just greens: emeralds, chartreuses, and jades blend with iridescent blues, fiery oranges, and the sickly yellows of phosphorescent decay, all interwoven with shimmering, almost invisible sheaths that seem to ripple and shift in the perpetual white light.

Water is intermittent in this environment. It seems to have a specific purpose. There are huge, bowl-like formations, woven entirely of living plants, where the bright light focuses. These catch condensation, channeling the drips down into a network of vein-like channels, feeding the deeper parts of the plant-world.

Here and there, the petrified fronds above have collapsed, or perhaps *grown* to touch the ground, forming immense caverns and defiles. In these places, you find the scattered remnants of something—something *built*. Structures of a kind, fused out of the same plant matter as everything else, as though they grew, inevitably, from the world itself. They're not houses, though—not in the familiar sense—but vast, intricate shells, pulsing with bioluminescent light, each one a unique, alien pattern. The scale of them is difficult to grasp. You see only their edges. All sense of a "purpose" or a builder is absent.

The sounds are deceptively quiet, like a perfect silence, but if you listen close, there is an ambient hum. Faint, rhythmic pulses, like a heart or a distant gong. This is the sound of the entire ecosystem, the living world. The plants themselves are communicating, exchanging nutrients, information, and maybe even something more nuanced, like *feeling.* The entire world has a silent rhythm, but the nature of it is not easy to apprehend.

Fauna is rare, but present. The very occasional flicker of a winged form, nothing you would recognize. And the ground itself seems to breathe; it undulates, and in the movement, you sense—perhaps—something more. If you stop and really listen, really *feel* the ground, you can sense the movement and the presence of something gigantic, something that might even be *sleeping*.

The rules of this world are simple because they’re eternal. Everything exists in a state of pre-formation. It is not an environment of growth so much as becoming. It is a slow, silent genesis, where everything is constantly coalescing, reshaping itself, becoming a higher-dimensional thing. There is no decay, no entropy, only a slow, inexorable evolution towards something you cannot yet understand.
