The world is a single, immense geode, a pocket-scale universe sealed within a jagged, obsidian shell. Externally, nothing marks its presence, a void of absolute, starless darkness. But within, a hellscape of industry and life churns slowly, a pressure cooker of ambition and necessity.

The "ground" is not earth, but a vast, crystalline salt plain. It shimmers with a faint, oily iridescence, reflecting the meager light, generated by enormous, crackling arc-lamps that hang suspended from the geode's interior ceiling. These lamps, like colossal, pulsing hearts, cast long, distorted shadows across the surface. The mineral plain itself is in constant flux, a vast, slowly shifting tide of hexagonal salt crystals forming, fracturing, dissolving, and reforming. This process is the key to everything in this world.

There's no water here, not in the traditional sense, but the air is thick with a superheated, corrosive mist comprised of sulfurous fumes and metallic particles. The temperature is torrid, a dry heat that clings to the skin and burns the lungs. This environment is the exhaust of the world's purpose: to *manufacture*. Colossal, ungainly machines, powered by unseen engines, crawl slowly across the plain, extracting, processing, and reconfiguring the hexagonal crystals. They are colossal, insectile things, their gears grinding, steam hissing, and pistons pumping in a slow, ponderous rhythm. Their function is not readily apparent; there is no clear output, nothing that seems to be *built*. They are simply *busy*, each one focused on its own task in a vast, unknowable system. Their movement is slow, a creeping choreography across the crystalline plain. 

Despite the lack of plant life, fauna are abundant, though strange and unfamiliar. In the dark the shapes are difficult to discern. Large, chitinous creatures, like armored beetles the size of small tanks, with segmented bodies and multiple, clicking mandibles, roam the plain. They are not predators, but harvesters, consuming the raw, crystalline material and, seemingly, contributing to its ongoing transformation. These creatures are the only things that seem truly native to the world. Other creatures are manufactured. Small, metallic automatons, humming with a high-pitched whine, flit through the air, performing maintenance on the machinery. There are also lumbering, bi-pedal beings, their bodies a mass of pipes and pistons. Their purpose? The sound is loud enough, but it is difficult in the darkness to discern what they are doing.

The entire system is a closed loop, a self-contained engine of production. The crystals are formed, processed, consumed, and reformed. The machines manipulate the material. The fauna contribute to the cycle. The automatons maintain and repair the machines. The heavy, sulfurous air slowly breaks down everything, only to be reclaimed and reused. The process is slow, deliberate, and utterly inhuman.

The technology here is industrial, but not in any way recognizable. The machines are not designed for efficiency, but for something else unknown. The power source is invisible, drawn from a source beyond the geode's confines. The purpose of it all is a mystery. The world remains unfinished, in an eternal state of emergence. Its destiny is unknown, but clear. Something is being prepared. It is not building, but *becoming*.
