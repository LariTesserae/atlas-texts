The world is held in an eternal night, a cold and dense place far beneath any surface. It is a labyrinth of minerals, a network of tunnels and vast chambers carved from some impossible dark stone. The air itself feels mineral, thick with the scent of iron and the faintest hint of sulfur. There is no water, none; even the minerals are dry to the touch, their surfaces rough and unyielding.

The tunnels are young. They are sharp-edged, the stone pristine, untouched by any sign of true erosion. The carving is not natural, not entirely. Here and there, strange structures rise from the stone. Small, scattered habitations of a sort. They appear hand-fabricated, primitive, but built from the same dark stone as the tunnels themselves. The architecture is angular, almost crystalline, with a strange beauty. A beauty that has no place for the usual things. No adornments, no comfort, no purpose easily defined.

There is no light, and yet, there is a kind of dim illumination. The walls themselves faintly glow with a cold, internal radiance, a dying fire buried deep within the stone. In this weak, perpetual twilight, the density of the world is revealed. Creatures of all shapes and sizes, or forms beyond imagining, swarm everywhere. Some are insects of dark chitin, others are like worms of living stone. Still others, more alien, shift and writhe, their bodies made of the very darkness itself. They are a teeming, thriving ecology, utterly adapted to this subterranean existence.

There is almost no sound. The creatures move silently through the tunnels, their many legs and mandibles making not a whisper. The air hangs still. The only sounds are the occasional scrapes and echoes as the creatures travel and the rare, almost imperceptible shifts of the enormous stone caverns themselves.

Nothing changes. Nothing falls. Nothing rises. Time itself seems to have stopped, or perhaps never existed at all. The very essence of the world, its state, is maintained. The eternal night holds.
