The world is a vast, open bowl of ochre. It stretches out to the horizon in gentle undulations, a barely-there topography that speaks of aeons of slow grinding, of mountains worn down to a whisper. It's empty, and yet, it *teems*.

The ochre is the bedrock, the bone-dust of a world long past its prime. It feels strangely warm beneath bare feet, radiating a gentle heat that speaks of a hidden core, a slow-burning ember. The warmth is amplified by the humidity, a constant, pervasive damp that clings to everything. It’s not rain as such, but a gentle condensation, a fine film that coats the ochre and the *things* that grow from it.

And the things are *plants*.

Vegetation, total. They are the air, the land, the very framework of existence here. Massive, sprawling, root-like tendrils of woven, fibrous green snake across the ochre ocean. They seem to *be* the ground, the ochre barely visible beneath their thick, resilient weave. Structures rise from this primary layer: colossal, cathedral-like formations of interwoven leaves and woody stalks, reaching toward the sky. They are the legacy of a species whose architecture is its biology.

The light is normal, but filtered. It is diffused by the density of the plant life, the endless canopy that blots out the direct sun. This, and the perpetual humidity, makes the air thick and heavy, a humid, vegetal breath.

And then there are the sounds. Deafening.

The world *roars*.

From the depths of the plant-structures comes a constant, throbbing pulse. A low, guttural chanting that vibrates in the bones, a symphony of unseen life. The song is a tapestry of buzzing, whirring, clicking, and a chorus of insectile, chitinous voices, punctuated by the screeches and howls of things *alive*, *overwhelming* fauna.

Fauna overwhelming, indeed. These are enormous, creatures of impossible physiology — colossal insect-like beings, their bodies formed of the same fibrous plant-matter as their surroundings. They are the builders, the architects, the source of the deafening symphony. They are a single, monolithic super-organism, a plant-fauna hybrid, weaving the world into itself. They are the structures, the forms, the bodies, and the songs.

The scattered buildings are like scattered teeth. The buildings are relics, remnants of a bygone age, built from an ancient form of metal — now rusted and worn by the plants into skeletal remains, swallowed by the green. The metal tools are crude, simple hooks, hand-axes, but they’re not used to *build* as much as they are to *harvest*. The people, the inheritors of the scattered tech, are scavengers, living in the interstitial spaces between the plant-structures, taking what they can from the ruins of their ancestors. They are also incredibly small, dwarfed and vulnerable in this world of giants.

The strange thing? The *weirdness*? 

There is an undercurrent, a subtle distortion to the landscape. A shimmering wave in the ochre, a slight bend to the light. The plants themselves seem to *think*, their growth patterns taking on geometric shapes, forming complex patterns that are both beautiful and unsettling. The sounds, the roars, have discernible melodies, complex rhythms. The fauna, in their overwhelming mass, seem to move with a strange, collective intelligence.

This isn’t just a landscape. It's a single, colossal organism. An intelligent labyrinth of ochre, water, and plant, and sound. It is a world that has reached its final form, settled into its deepest stability. It is dynamic, in a slow, inexorable process of self-creation and decay. And in the face of all this, among the bones of an extinct metal age, the tiny, human scavengers struggle to survive, barely audible beneath the deafening song of an impossible world.
