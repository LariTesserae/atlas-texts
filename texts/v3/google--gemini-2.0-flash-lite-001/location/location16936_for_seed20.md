The world breathes with a deafening pulse. It’s not something you hear so much as *feel*—a constant, thrumming pressure that vibrates the very marrow in your bones. Imagine the inside of a colossal, living heart, a cavernous space where the walls themselves are made of shifting, viscous water. It’s warm here, heavy with the scent of ozone and something else, something metallic and ancient, like the memories of a forgotten forge.

The scale is cathedral-like, vast and echoing. Yet, despite the immensity, the "world" feels contained, a bubble unto itself. Boundaries are indistinct, but you know you haven’t truly *moved* in a long time. You are adrift in a roiling, dim ocean. Sunlight struggles to penetrate the depths, filtering through the churning water in weak, diffuse beams. The light is barely enough to cast shadows, and the world is defined more by the sense of pressure than by form.

The terrain is young, newly formed in the turbulent chaos. Sharp angles and abrupt chasms punctuate the watery architecture. It’s as if monumental structures are constantly erupting from the depths, then being immediately scoured away by the relentless current. Erosion is a constant, creative force, and the water itself seems to *sculpt* everything. The "ground" is a constantly churning seabed, where mountains are born and dissolve in moments.

Vegetation is sparse, clinging to the few stable points. Giant, bioluminescent kelp forests, the size of cathedrals themselves, sway with the currents. But the majority of the landscape is barren, a stark testament to the ongoing upheaval.

The structures are scattered, remnants of things half-born and already decaying. They appear as fleeting glimpses–a pillar that appears, solidifies, and crumbles, a brief window that hints at a forgotten purpose. They’re built of materials not of this world – translucent obsidian, living rock that pulses with errant life, metallic substances that don’t seem to obey any laws of physics.

Technology is nonexistent. Here, everything is nature, albeit a nature distorted by the strange currents of the place. Creation and destruction function according to principles no creature could hope to understand.

Fauna is rare, and the few creatures that can be glimpsed are bizarre adaptations to the environment. Something like luminescent, leviathan-sized jellyfish drifts past. Here and there, strange, fragmented life flashes: glimpses of something… almost familiar, yet utterly alien.

Then, there’s the weirdness—the reality breakdown. It manifests as discontinuities: flashes of impossible colors; abrupt shifts in the flow of time; the sensation of being everywhere and nowhere at once. Gravity fluctuates. The fabric of the waters thins, and you glimpse other worlds beyond the walls, chaotic and beautiful, and then *gone* again.

The deafening sound is not just noise: it’s the very essence of the turbulence, the raw energy of the place. It rises and falls, a rhythmic heartbeat that controls the creation and destruction of everything. The light flickers and fades in time with it.

The dynamic nature of the world is its defining characteristic. This isn’t stagnation, this is constant change, a storm-state of creation and destruction. Everything is in motion, always becoming, always being undone. It is a world where even the most solid things are only momentarily so, and the only constant is the deafening, resonating sound of existence itself.
