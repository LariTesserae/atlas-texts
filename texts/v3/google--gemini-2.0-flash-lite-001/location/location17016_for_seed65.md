The wind is a constant, screaming presence. It tears across the jagged peaks, a deafening shriek that never ceases. Yet, somehow, the light remains impossibly bright, a relentless sun washing over everything in an almost clinical clarity. There are no clouds, no haze, only an unrelenting panorama of stark beauty.

This is the Bleak Expanse. It is a world of stone and sky, where the peaks claw at the heavens, their summits lost in the blinding solar glare. The mountains are immense, reaching for a sky that seems to push back. The air bites with a cool, sharp edge – even in the sun's direct assault, there is a pervasive chill.

The terrain is brutal. Jagged, gray stone dominates, but it's stone that has been here a long time and has its own story, its own pace. The erosion has long since established an equilibrium. The mountains themselves are sculptures, chiseled by eons of wind and a negligible amount of moisture. The little water that exists is there only in pockets of shadowed crevices.

Despite the desolation, life persists. The fauna of the Expanse is abundant, but unseen: Howling Echoes are never quite alone. Their existence feels impossible in this harsh world, but they are here, living among the stone. They are the sources of the deafening sound.

The technology of this world is primitive, but the people who use it work with extreme discipline and dedication. They have learned to survive, crafting tools from stone and bone, their lives a constant struggle for an edge, or more often, mere survival. Their lives are as unvarying and unforgiving as the landscape. They are a deeply private people, and they keep their voices for the wind.

This is a world of contrasts. Boundless distances are both beautiful and forbidding. The absence of change is both a promise and a curse. The intense light reveals both splendor and a bleak reality. The density is almost empty and almost everything here is a secret.
