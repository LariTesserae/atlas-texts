The world is a vast, churning sea of green. Not the blue of an ocean, but the thick, opaque emerald of a kelp forest, or perhaps a vast, overgrown swamp, rendered aqueous. It stretches to the horizon in every direction, a seemingly endless expanse of liquid vegetation. Only the highest peaks of a long-ago mountain range break the surface, and even these are worn down, sculpted by millennia of relentless, liquid erosion. The peaks are visible, but the valleys between have been almost entirely filled in, or else leveled.

The water itself is cool, almost cold, a refreshing contrast to the humid air. The density is extreme, a cacophony of life, and the sounds are a constant, overwhelming roar. It's a symphony of churning water, rustling fronds, chittering creatures, and the groaning of ancient, submerged structures. The few buildings, all industrial relics, are made of a dark, rust-colored metal, their forms barely perceptible through the tangle of vegetation. They stand as solitary islands of human creation, slowly being devoured by the relentless green sea.

The light is normal, but filtered. The dense vegetation and humid air create a soft, diffused glow, casting long, distorted shadows. There is a constant downpour, but not like rain; the water *is* the air, and it condenses and disperses, and falls from every surface. The sky isn't a solid dome, but is instead an ever-shifting, unpredictable turbulence of water and light. Any color can be seen against an overcast, filtered backdrop, from deep violet to a sickly yellow-green.

The scale is vast. One can see for miles, but always through the intricate mosaic of the watery flora. Despite the erosion, and despite the endless supply of energy, the landscape is not flat. Great currents of water swirl and churn, creating a dynamism that makes the terrain constantly shift. The structures shift, also, as do the shadows. The only constancy is the churning, boiling abundance of life.

Evidence suggests that humans built here, or perhaps something like humans. Remnants of industry indicate high-tech manufacturing, but of what? There is no sign of the human beings who built them. The technology seems designed to be lost, scattered, and absorbed by the natural world. Massive engines, rusted and half-submerged, churn out some invisible, relentless labor. Factories pulse with unseen activity.

Fauna is abundant. The creatures of this world are adapted to both liquid and solidity. Some are plant-like, some are animal. Many are both. Everything moves with a desperate, frantic energy, an echo of the constant turmoil of the environment. The rules of the world are simple: life and death, but on a scale the mind struggles to grasp. The old mountains are being consumed, but life goes on, with unimaginable fecundity.

This place is a paradox. It is ancient, settled into its final form, yet utterly turbulent. It is both serene and chaotic, lush and decaying. A world in a state of eternal becoming, a swirling, seething green, where technology and nature are locked in a final, all-consuming embrace.
