The world is a single, immense, echoing chamber, carved from a material like obsidian, but that refracts rather than absorbs light. The air is thick with the scent of wet stone and the cloying sweetness of overripe fruit. A dim, internal luminescence casts the chamber in a perpetual twilight.

The floor is a vast, cracked plane. Jagged fissures, some wide enough to swallow a whole person, crisscross the surface. The cracks are the only sources of the meager water here, trickling out of the rock and collecting in small, brackish pools.

Vegetation clings tenaciously to the cracks and crevices. It is a riot of life in a world that shouldn't be able to support it: massive, fleshy fungi that glow with a faint, internal light; vines thick as pythons, covered in thorns and bearing fruit the size of a human head; and trees with bark like polished bone and leaves that shimmer like oiled metal. The leaves are a deep green verging on black, the fruit a bruised-purple.

The temperature is warm, even humid, but the air never *feels* wet. It feels… saturated, as though it holds all the water it can, but will not release it.

The elevation is high, but that’s difficult to conceptualize in this enclosed space. It feels more as if we are at the bottom of something immense. The very material the world is built from has been worn down over unfathomable ages. Erosion here has created the cracks that the life thrives in. The cracks are pathways, carved highways between worlds, and the vegetation embraces these lines.

The scale is almost claustrophobic, despite the immense size of the chamber. Everything feels near, like a collection of rooms embedded within one enormous room. The paths are narrow, the vegetation grows close.

Density is a key characteristic. The fauna are a suffocating presence. This is not simply an abundance of life, but a *pressure* of life. Insects the size of small dogs crawl over everything. They have exoskeletons like polished obsidian and mandibles that click like castanets. Tiny, iridescent creatures flit through the air, their wings beating a frantic rhythm that adds to the general din. Larger creatures, unseen but definitely *felt*, move through the undergrowth, their presence betrayed by rustling, snapping, and the occasional, guttural roar. Their shadows are dim shapes at the very edge of the light.

This is an urban environment, but unlike anything built by human hands. It is a city of organic architecture. The fungi and vines have been trained, shaped by the unseen hands of the world itself. The trees, their bone-like branches interlaced, form archways and tunnels. Structures rise and fall, shift and change, built and rebuilt by unseen forces, like cells in a living body. The structures are all interlocking, all reinforcing the sense of being contained within something vast.

The technology is primitive. There are tools, made of bone and chitin, used for cutting the giant fruits and harvesting the fungi, but little is truly *made* in a way that suggests design outside the natural. The structures are all grown, not assembled.

The dim light doesn't penetrate the shadows of the plants, giving everything a slightly warped, unsettling appearance. It is a light that seems to *know* but not to *see*.

The fauna are truly overwhelming. Every inch of space is teeming with life. There are things that fly, crawl, slither, and burrow, all living in the narrow cracks and the dense undergrowth. The creatures are a symphony of textures, from the smooth, cool obsidian of the insects to the rough, bark of the trees. 

What passes for normality in this place is something other. It’s hard to say what would count as strange, because everything here operates according to rules we cannot know.

The sounds are loud — the incessant clicking of insect mandibles, the rustling of leaves, the drip of water. The occasional roar of an unseen beast breaks through the cacophony. The sound echoes endlessly through the chamber, reverberating off the obsidian walls, creating a constant, unsettling symphony.

The world is active. There is a constant push and pull, a slow, relentless movement. The great fungi expand, contract, and shift. The vines twist and grow. The fauna hunt and breed. The air itself seems to pulse with unseen energies.
