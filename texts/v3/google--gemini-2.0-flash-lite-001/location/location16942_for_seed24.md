The world is a hollowed-out geode, maybe the size of a large cathedral, maybe the size of a very large grove. The walls are a mosaic of crystalline structures, mostly in shades of warm brown and dusty rose. Some of the crystals are large plates, flat and smooth, catching the dim light in sharp facets. Others are clusters of tiny, needle-like formations, creating a rough, textured surface.

There is no water here, not in the liquid sense. But the air is thick with a strange, oily substance, a suspension of fine, glittering particles that refract the little light there is. The light itself is faint, a dull crimson glow that seems to emanate from the walls themselves, as if the crystals were very slowly burning from within. The source is not apparent. The faint glow is evenly distributed.

There is no sound, at least not that anyone (or anything) can hear. A deep silence permeates the space, broken only by the occasional, almost imperceptible *crack* as a crystal shifts and settles.

The temperature is warm, like a long summer's day. There's no wind, no sense of movement in the air. Time seems to slow here, the stillness pressing down.

The "vegetation" consists of scattered growths of what look like petrified coral, their intricate forms still visible even in the dim light. They feel cool to the touch, and are hard as stone. The coral is not alive (or not any animal life that can be said to exist here), but it seems to pulse with a faint, internal luminescence that matches the light of the walls.

There are no signs of any structures, of anything built, except for the basic form of the geode itself. No tools, no crafts, even the simplest of things. But there are occasional "seeds" of crystal, embedded in the walls. They are dormant, small, and sharp.

The rules of this world are… not simple, and not fixed. The basic geometry of the space *is* fixed, and the crystalline matrix around is stable. But like the coral, there are elements that *feel* as if they could change, given an outside influence. The crystals themselves seem to be slowly growing, expanding, and a bit of study shows that the particles in the air are also slowly changing. This slow change is just barely perceptible, only noticed with great attention.

There are no animals, no insects — no life as we know it — but the coral suggest a history of something that grew here. The crystals are all-formed and the air is static, so if anything exists, it is likely far beyond the capacity of human experience.
