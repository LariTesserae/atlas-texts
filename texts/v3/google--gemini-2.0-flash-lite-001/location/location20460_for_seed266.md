The world is old, a vast expanse of rust-red plains and towering, obsidian structures that pierce a sky stained ochre by perpetual dust. This is Xylos, a planet forged in the crucible of a dying star, where the sun beats down with a warmth that borders on oppressive, baking the very stones of its cities. Water is a forgotten myth, a whisper in the wind, a secret held tight within the deep, labyrinthine networks beneath the surface.

The plains themselves are cracked and fractured, a mosaic of red and brown minerals, smoothed over aeons of slow, grinding erosion. Dust devils, miniature cyclones of fine grit, dance across the terrain, their movements dictated by the planet’s slow, almost imperceptible winds. The scale of the place is immense, the horizon a distant edge, a line drawn by the curvature of this ancient world. The air is dry, dense with a metallic tang, carrying the scent of iron and the faintest hint of something acrid, almost chemically-sweet.

Dominating the landscape are the cities, or what remains of them. They rise from the plains like colossal teeth, black and unforgiving. These are not cities built for comfort, but for purpose. The structures are geometric, blocky, and monolithic, their surfaces etched with intricate, alien carvings, the secrets of which are lost to time. The architecture is indicative of a long-past industrial civilization, a civilization that left behind vast factories, mile-long storage facilities, and complex infrastructure designed to harness the planet's meagre resources.

The technology that built Xylos is all but lost. Engines, their purpose now only vaguely understood, are scattered across the plains, broken down and rusting. Production lines, frozen mid-process, are exposed to the elements, silent monuments to a forgotten era. However, the machines still function at a basic level, maintaining a quiet, rhythmic hum - a deep, industrial thrum that permeates the planet. Electricity crackles through the air, unseen but ever-present, like the nerves of a body long deceased.

Life on Xylos is strange, undeniably alien. No plants grow here, but the plains are teeming with fauna. They are not animals as we understand them, but mineral-based lifeforms, strange constructs of rock and crystal, animated by the planet's dormant energies. Some resemble colossal, armored beetles that slowly traverse the plains, their carapaces gleaming in the dim light. Others are serpentine, their bodies composed of linked, crystalline plates that shimmer with inner light. They move with a slow grace. And everywhere, on the surface of buildings, on the plains, in the air, vast herds of tiny creatures, like motes of dust in the wind, gather. They are always active, eating the dusty plains, being swept up by the wind, and returning to the plains.

The cities are mostly quiet, but a constant symphony of low sounds emanates from their heart. The whir of machinery, the creak of structural strain, and the occasional, jarring metallic clang. Only at the heart of the great cities is anything like activity - but it is unlike activity we know. The core of the cities are populated by strange figures - beings of light and shadow, their forms shifting, their voices echoes in the dust-filled air. Often, they are gathered around some massive device or structure, attending its slow maintenance and operation. 

The dynamic of Xylos is glacial. The planet seems to experience a kind of long-term stasis, but the machinery of the cities suggests that this stasis could be broken—some cataclysmic occurrence waiting in the deep past, or near the future. The slow decay of the infrastructure, the grinding away of the plains, the subtle shifts in the planet's atmosphere are the only signs of change, a dance played out over millennia. It is a world where the rules are old, and the strange, mineral life quietly follows them, existing within a harmony of its own design.
