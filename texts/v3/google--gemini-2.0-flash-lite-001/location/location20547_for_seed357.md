The world breathes. Not with lungs, but with a symphony of slow, colossal exhalations and inhalations of moisture. The air itself feels thick, warm, and alive, a swirling soup of nascent life. It clings to everything, a constant, tangible presence. Water, in all its forms, is king here. It *is* here. It's the architecture, the substrate, the very substance of this place.

The land doesn't *erode*; it *melts* and *reforms*. The ‘ground’ is a maze of vast, fleshy, obsidian-black stalks that rise and fall with agonizing slowness. They writhe, not with any organic intention I can discern, but with a slow, planetary pulse. The surface is slick with condensation, always weeping, always glistening. These towering forms are not rock; they are the petrified arteries of a colossal, unseen plant. They support a canopy of colossal leaves that unfurl high above.

This entire ecosystem is built with plants. No, that is not accurate. It *is* plants. There is nothing else, and everything is plant. I see no separate soil. The very ground, the structures themselves, are plant matter, but I see no definable individual plants. I only see the immensity. I see the endless, seething, plant ocean.

The air is thick with the hot, sweet scent of overripe fruit, mixed with the acrid, metallic tang of the ‘water’ itself. It sounds constantly – a deep, resonating hum emanating from the ground, punctuated by the rhythmic sigh of the gargantuan, plant-flesh columns. The wind – a constant, chaotic dance – whips through the highest reaches of this plant-city, rustling immense leaves, their edges sharp and jagged. This is the only sound of 'movement'; the rest is still, slow, and geological.

The light is dim, filtered by the dense canopy above. Shadows stretch and contort across the obsidian stalks, dancing with the ceaseless, slow shifting of the structures. It's like being inside a colossal, beating heart.

The very shape of the place is always in flux. I witness spires rising out of the ground, only to slowly melt and cascade into vast, subterranean pools. These pools are not still. They seethe with an inner life. They push and churn, reflecting the dimly-lit canopy above.

This is a young world. A world in the throes of becoming. All is in formation, or deformation.

There is *nothing* of the familiar. No recognizable flora or fauna, no rock, no ground, no sky. Only plant-flesh, water, and the churning, constant motion of creation and destruction. There is such an emptiness here, a desolate beauty. I watch a new structure emerge from the black depths – a vast, convoluted tower. It pushes upward, seemingly without effort, and I experience the sudden, overwhelming sensation of being profoundly *small*.
