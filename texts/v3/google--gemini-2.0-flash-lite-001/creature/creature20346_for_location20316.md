The architects of this submerged gallery are gone – or perhaps they never were, in the way a sculptor is "gone" once a statue breathes its final pose. Whatever built this is not of flesh, not of bone, not of a form you can readily comprehend. To call them *living* is inadequate. They *were* the place, a continuous presence woven into the very fabric of these impossible corridors. They were the stone, the water, the light. They *are* the echo.

Consider the bone-white stone first – a living medium. It doesn't simply *contain* the interlocking patterns, the architecture that shifts and writhes – it *is* the patterns. The patterns themselves are not mere decoration; they are the language, the thought process, the very essence of the builders. They are a dance of cellular growth, a symphony of mineral metamorphosis, a slow-motion ballet performed by something beyond the grasp of a hand or a tool. It is hard to imagine a body, as we understand it, could create such a feat.

The builders, if you can call them that, *perspired* this gallery into being. The fluid that crystallizes into those fragile, geometric shapes is a tangible manifestation of their consciousness -- a kind of "memory-stone" – a solidified record of their thoughts and dreams. The tools, too – those enigmatic instruments of a forgotten craft – are not just left behind; they are shed artifacts, fragments of a mind that had no need for hands. They are the tools *of* the tools, a kind of self-awareness.

The fauna, especially the jellyfish-like creatures, are not merely inhabitants; they are extensions of the builders, their eyes are the lens through which they still perceive. Their bioluminescence is the leftover hum of the builders' presence, the faint glow of the original creative spark. They are tiny sparks in the wake of a cosmic wildfire.

The biological rhythm that permeates the space? That is the heartbeat of the builders, a constant ebb and flow of growth and decay, a silent symphony of becoming and unbecoming. It is the language they continue to sing in. The wind? Their sigh. The water? Their tears.

But *who* were they? What *were* they? It is as futile to ask as it is to ask of the wind or the stone. To grasp their essence, you must abandon all notions of material bodies and individual minds. They were a collective consciousness, a gestalt entity. They must have been born into the space, grown from it, and left no traces of having *arrived*.

Consider that this place is a *gallery*. It is a presentation of something, perhaps to *something else.* The builders did not create; they revealed. This is no tomb; and if it is, the dead *are the landscape.* This is a testament to the absolute power of creation, of the universe as an artistic expression. The submerged gallery is their statement, and the question it poses is not: "What did they make?" but: "What *is* this, to begin with?" The architecture knows, and will never tell. But within their echo, you might find an inkling.
