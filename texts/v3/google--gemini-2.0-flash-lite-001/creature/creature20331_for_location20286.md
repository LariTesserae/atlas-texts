The city breathes, and within that breath, life finds a form. 

The primary inhabitants are not creatures as we might understand them. They are more akin to living architectures, the result of a profound melding between the biological and inorganic, the deliberate and the accidental. They are the *Builders* and the *Melders*, the children of the deep, who have taken the ruins as their own.

The Builders are the architects, the engineers, of this strange domain. Their forms are fluid and adaptable, a perpetual state of becoming. They have no fixed shape, but shift and reconfigure. They are a fusion of the ancient, forgotten languages etched into the submerged architecture, and the genetic code of the deep. Their "bodies" are not discrete beings but extensions of the city itself. They are the structures, the arteries and veins of this deep-sea ecology. In the Builder form, they may manifest as shimmering, bioluminescent towers carved from living coral, which breathe and pulse with a slow, internal light. Or they may flow through the tunnels, like viscous rivers of light and shadow, reshaping the stone with their passing, repairing and altering broken structures.

The Melders possess more physical form, though it is never static. They are the artists, the crafters, and the scavengers. Born of the deep's rich biodiversity, they are a fusion of human and aquatic. Their forms are highly variable, each one a unique response to its environment. Some have adapted to the lightless depths, with bodies of semi-translucent cartilage and bioluminescent markings that mimic the fungal flora—a mimicry that allows them to move unseen through the city's complex warrens. Others have evolved elaborate filter-feeding systems, growing great, fan-like appendages to harvest nutrients from the currents. Still others are covered in a carapace of sharp, obsidian-like plates forged from the ruins, their forms mimicking the broken angles of the city's shattered towers.

The Melders are the eyes, ears, and hands of the Builders. They work tirelessly to maintain the city's infrastructure, to defend against threats, and to exploit the resources of the deep to help keep the city alive. They are the heart of the city, ever-changing and adapting, just as the city itself. They are the ones who hunt for the few remaining foods sources, and for the components that are needed to sustain the structures of the city. Their voices are not heard with words but with the gestures of construction and communication: they communicate with glowing symbols of light and shadow that shift with their presence, and with bioluminescent trails that trace their paths through the maze.

The other inhabitants of the city are not so integrated. They are its “visitors,” its guests, the creatures the Builders and Melders carefully curated and use in concert to keep the city alive. Some serve as sentinels, patrolling the city's perimeter. These are the *Vanguards*, deep-sea predators that have morphed themselves into guardians that have been transformed by bio-engineering. They patrol the perimeters of the city and watch over it with bioluminescent eyes and a deep, pulsing hum.

There are also the *Harvesters*, the kelp-fish hybrids that sweep the city’s sediment, filtering for organic matter. The Harvesters are small and relatively unaggressive, but they form immense swarms, their movements as one.

The Builders, Melders, and the curated fauna are bound in a symbiotic dance, an intricate choreography of life and death, creation and destruction. The city is their organism, a living entity that breathes, grows, and transforms. Their existence is a testament to the power of adaptation, of persistence, and of the profound beauty that arises from the collision of the world's most unlikely forces.
