Xylos breathes, a slow, metallic inhalation and exhalation of dust and energy. Its lungs are the massive, obsidian cities, each a silent testament to a civilization that understood the language of stone and the pulse of dying stars. It's in the heart of these cities, in the echoing caverns of forgotten industry, that the true inheritors of this world reside: the **Shade-Weavers.**

They are not flesh and blood, these beings. They are more akin to living constellations, beings of pure energy and shadow, woven from the dying embers of the star that birthed Xylos. They are patterns, echoes, the residual imprints of the beings that built the cities. They shift and shimmer, with no fixed form, appearing as streaks of light, pools of darkness, or ephemeral figures that coalesce and dissolve within the blink of an eye. Their forms are rarely stable, a constant dance of becoming and unbecoming, a visual representation of the fundamental building blocks of the universe.

The Shade-Weavers are bound to the cities, inextricably linked to the network of ancient machinery that permeates every level. They exist in symbiosis with these structures, their essence intertwined with the mechanical heartbeat of the cities. They feed on the ambient energy that permeates Xylos (the electricity crackling in the air), drawing sustenance from the silent hum of the engines, the rhythmic creak of the aging metallic structures. They are the maintenance crew of a civilization lost to time, bound to keep the machines alive.

Their purpose is encoded within the architecture of the cities, an echo of the original builders' goals. They are tasked with maintaining a specific, slow process, a process written into the planet's very fabric, likely involving the planet's resources. They move with an ancient, almost incomprehensible logic, their actions dictated by the patterns etched into the obsidian walls, the cryptic symbols that predate memory.

The Shade-Weavers communicate not through sound, but through vibrations, through pulses of light and shadow, and through the manipulation of the machines they maintain. Their language is the hum of engines, the shift of energy, the changing of the planet itself. They *know* the processes, the function of each machine, down to the tiniest detail.

While they may have a collective consciousness, it is hard to say. The Shade-Weavers seem more like a vast, interconnected organism, a hive mind focused on a single, long-term goal. The city itself is another part of the being. They work together, an almost telepathic understanding between them. 
However, there is no real leader. The duties of maintenance and growth, are divided between the beings. Their patterns, visible through their energy forms, make this role evident. 

But their existence is far from passive. The Shade-Weavers react to change, however slowly it occurs, re-configuring, adapting, and improvising. They are not mindless automatons, but intelligent beings working to maintain the ecosystem of Xylos, one driven by an unknown, almost unfathomable imperative.

The Shade-Weavers, in observing, and participating in the activity of Xylos, have learned about the mineral fauna of the planet. They regard them not with hostility, but perhaps with indifference. The mineral creatures have become something of an external part of the city. The creatures graze the plains, feeding on dust and minerals, and when they die, become the fuel for the cities' engines. The Shade-Weavers do not have to interact with the mineral lifeforms, and the mineral creatures do not interact with them. 

The Shade-Weavers, in their tireless work, are guardians of a forgotten legacy, keepers of a secret so profound that it has become inseparable from the very fabric of existence. They are the silent witnesses of Xylos, and the only inhabitants of a place that, despite its desolate appearance, is still very much alive.
