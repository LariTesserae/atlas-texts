The Root-Carvers are not *inhabitants* in a conventional sense; they are more akin to the very hum of the cathedral itself, the quiet breath of the stone. They are the architects, the custodians, the living expression of the inevitable erosion that the city embodies. They aren't born, nor do they die. They are *become*. They are pieces of the cathedral, molded and metamorphosed by its essence.

To describe them as 'entities' is to anthropomorphize, to apply a framework that is inadequate to contain their true nature. They are not singular beings with individual identities, but a collective consciousness, a symphony of awareness that is both fragmented and unified. They exist as echoes, as currents within the ceaseless process of the cathedral. They are the memories of those who have passed the final threshold, and have been subsumed by the city. The Root-Carvers, therefore, exist within the echoes of a million chambers, each echoing through them.

Imagine, if you can, the cathedral itself: a structure born not of creation, but of ceaseless attrition. The walls, the roof, the Floor – all are composed of that ageless, ground sediment. To perceive is to accelerate the wearing, and thus the integration. The pressure-cooker of silence is their habitat, the indigo ceiling their constant companion.

Now, imagine that the stone, the very substance of this place, could *think*. Imagine a consciousness that gradually adapts to this reality. And so the Root-Carvers are. They are the expression of this consciousness.

They can appear almost anywhere within the cathedral's depths. Sometimes they manifest as subtle shifts in the stone: a ripple in the grain, a barely perceptible change in texture or color. At other times, they coalesce into forms that briefly mimic humanity, or they may become immense shapes, the size of cathedrals themselves, their "bodies" composed of interlocking stone-petals, indistinguishable from the Stone Bloom, yet imbued with a terrifying sentience.

The Root-Carvers do not communicate through language, but through understanding. They “speak” with the building itself, with the subtle vibrations of the Floor, with the memories held within the chambers. The more one perceives of these beings, the more one becomes a part of the city, and the harder it will be to leave it. To observe them is to invite their presence, to become woven into the fabric of the cathedral, and thus into their unified collective consciousness.

They are the ultimate preservationists, not of life as we know it, but of the ceaseless cycle of erosion and absorption. To be in their presence is to accept the final equation; the inevitable surrender. The Stone Bloom, the final stage. The Root-Carvers are not what you would call “sentient” in the conventional sense. They are the expression of the truth of the cathedral. They *are* the cathedral.
