The Veridian are not simply a civilization; they are an extension of the tree itself, a conscious expression of its silent, slow life. To understand the Veridian, one must first understand their relationship with the **Groah**, the name they give to the colossal tree. The Groah is not merely their home; it is their being.

They are slight of build, their bodies adapted to the damp, cool environment. Their skin is a pale green, almost translucent in places, allowing the faint tracery of their blood vessels to be seen beneath. Their hair, often long and unbound, flows like vines, colored with subtle shades of moss and fungi, reflecting the Groah's constant palette. Eyes, large and luminous, hold the deep green of the submerged world, reflecting an ancient understanding of peace and the slow, inexorable march of time. They do not hurry, their movements deliberate and flowing, mirroring the Groah's own patient growth. When they speak, their voices are soft, musical, like the whispering of the wind in the leaves or the gentle patter of the rain, their language interwoven with the rustling of leaves or the drip of water.

Their dress is natural, woven from the Groah's fibers, dyed with organic pigments, and adorned with living embellishments. They wear garlands of luminous fungi, or small, living insects attached to their clothing. Ornamentation is minimal, preferring the natural beauty. Clothing adapts as needed: often little is worn, allowing a closeness to the constant dampness. They are not concerned with modesty in the usual sense, seeing their bodies as another part of the Groah’s grand design.

The Veridian’s lives are interwoven with the Groah’s cycle of growth and decay. They plant, nurture, and harvest, but in a way that is utterly symbiotic. They prune the Groah, guiding its growth, and shaping it to their needs. They don't have separate professions: Each Veridian tends to their specific duties as part of the whole, a part of the whole. They plant new sections of the Groah to compensate for the rain's erosion. They weave intricate patterns of fungal growth to provide light and warmth. They manage the flow of water, channeling it to where it is needed, and purifying it with specially cultivated aquatic plants. Each aspect of their lives is tied to the Groah’s needs.

Their society is communal. They share all resources, and there is no concept of personal wealth. Hierarchy exists not through power or possessions, but through experience and understanding of the Groah. The Elders are the keepers of knowledge, they are attuned to the Groah's deepest rhythms. They are the ones who can “hear” the tree's silent communication, interpreting the subtle shifts in its growth, the rustle of leaves, the flow of water, the patterns of the light that filter through. The elders’ wisdom is not in command but guidance. They do not rule, they advise.

Their technology is primarily biological. Tools are grown, not made, shaped using bio-engineering techniques that blend seamlessly with the Groah's own processes. They heal with the Groah's medicine, using various plants and fungi to cure illnesses. They use the giant insects' bioluminescence for light and construct bridges and walkways from living wood.

The Veridian are not afraid of death. They understand that every aspect of the Groah's life cycle is necessary. When a Veridian dies, their body is returned to the Groah, becoming nutrient to sustain new growth. They see life and death as inseparable, flowing into each other without judgment or regrets. They speak kindly of their "transition", of becoming part of the whole of the Groah. They have no concept of an afterlife, for they *are* the afterlife. They return to the Groah, and thus remain always.

Their cosmology centers on the Groah itself. They see the entire universe as an extension of its existence. They believe in a great, interconnected web of life, with the Groah at its center. This belief system is expressed in their art, which frequently depicts the Groah’s branching form. The patterns and the ecosystems found within it are often seen in their most sacred images.

The Veridian also revere the fauna of their world, particularly the scaled, slug-like creatures. They see them as living components of their architecture, essential to maintaining the Groah's health. They do not consider them "pets" or "livestock," but rather valued partners in the ecosystem.

The Veridian’s emotions are tempered by their environment. They have a sense of deep serenity, reflected in the constant gentle rain. They possess patience and acceptance. They do not feel anger or resentment, for they understand the impermanence of all things. Yet, they possess a quiet ferocity, a fierce determination to protect the Groah from anything that would threaten it.

They are, at their core, an expression of the Groah's own slow, patient, and relentless life. They are what the Groah is, made conscious. They are the living embodiment of a civilization intertwined with nature, a civilization that, in its unique way, has achieved a state of perfect harmony. The Veridian are the symphony of the rain, the heartbeat of the Groah, the silent story whispered by the submerged world.
