Within this humid, subterranean labyrinth, the denizens are the inheritors of ruins, the children of decay. They are the inheritors, the scavengers, the weavers of a new order from the skeletal remains of the old. Their forms have been twisted and reshaped by the environment; they are born of the stone, the water, and the broken dreams of a vanished civilization.

First, there are the **Gloomkin**, the dominant sentients of this world. They are not one species, but rather a spectrum of varied forms, all bound by shared principles of survival among the ruins. Their size varies: some resemble the bloated, luminous rats, their spines elongated into bony plates that glow with faint bioluminescence. These are the **Runemarks**, the scouts and messengers, darting through the tunnels with unnerving speed. Others are massive, beetle-like beings, the **Stone-Shells**, their carapaces a mosaic of rock and chitin, nearly invulnerable to the elements. They are the builders and protectors, whose immense strength is the key to maintaining their home.

All Gloomkin share key characteristics. Their bodies have adapted to the darkness, their eyes often reduced or replaced by other sense organs. Many have developed elongated appendages or sensory organs to detect shifts in the stone, tremors in the water, and the movement of other creatures. Their skin is smooth and slick, designed to repel water and to absorb the faint light that filters down. Across many, veins of the red mineral that runs through the stone pulse, giving the impression of constant movement. 

The Gloomkin are not united by tribe or nation but by a shared purpose: to live on. They venerate the **Forgotten Engineers**, or what's left of the memory of them, the creators. They don't worship a god in any traditional sense, but they have learned to honor the mechanics. Ancient blueprints and functional tools are treated as sacred objects. Survival is the most sacred of all.

As for the larger, unseen predators, they may indeed be of the Gloomkin. This world favors large creatures, and so the apex predator is a monstrously large being that dwells in the deepest shadows of the ruin, rarely seen. It is a descendant of the **Constructs**; the automated guardians of the original city, but now corrupted by nature and the passage of time. They have evolved into colossal, predatory forms, known as the **Shadow-Weavers**, their bodies a tangle of metal and flesh, a chilling testament to the city's lost glory. The Shadow-Weavers are masters of the tunnels, their movements silent, their presence a palpable threat. They understand the patterns of the ruin, the flow of water, the tremor of stone. They hunt with an instinctive, cold efficiency, and their victims become part of the dark ecosystem.

The other animals play their key role. The gigantic insects, the **Chitin-Weavers**, are the laborers, the cleaners. They break down the organic matter, consume the waste, and their bodies, when ground into a paste, become a vital building material for the Gloomkin. The luminous mosses and fungi that bloom across the walls aren't merely decorative; they're cultivated by the Gloomkin, used as a food source, and as an illumination for the darkest parts of the chambers.

The most profound adaptation to life below concerns the flow of water. The Gloomkin revere it, as it is life. They have learned to harness the power of the waterwheels, though their methods are still primitive. They have created canals and reservoirs, attempting to manage the flow of water, to keep it from destroying their homes. Their lives are a constant dance between the harsh forces of the world around them and the instinct of self-preservation.

The Gloomkin themselves reflect the nature of the ruins. They are fragmented, divided, but all are driven by an unyielding desire to survive. Their existence is a slow, relentless work in progress, a testament to the enduring power of life, forged in shadow, water, and stone. They are the children of the engineers, the inheritors of an industrial wasteland, transforming it into something new and strange. They are the future of the deep, a testament to resilience, a grim symphony of adaptation and endurance.
