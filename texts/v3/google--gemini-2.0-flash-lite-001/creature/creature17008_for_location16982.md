The denizens of this obsidian plain are not born, nor do they die in any conventional sense. They coalesce, flicker into existence, and eventually dissipate back into the turbulent energy that is the lifeblood of this world. To call them *beings* might be a misnomer, as they lack the solidity and defined forms we associate with living creatures. Instead, they are more akin to fleeting patterns, thoughts given shape by the chaotic forces that shape this reality. They are not merely *in* the storms, they *are* the storms, or at least intimately connected to them.

These ephemeral entities, let us call them the **Crystallium Drifters**, are composed of three primary elements: dust, light, and the crystalline structures that dot the obsidian ground. They are not physical in the rigid sense, but rather a complex interplay of energy fields and refracted light. The dust provides the foundational material, the framework upon which their forms are built. It is not ordinary dust; rather, it's a cosmic byproduct, a residue of the universe's ceaseless churning. It possesses an innate awareness, a sentience that is not the same as the self-awareness we know, but more of a collective memory, a deep understanding of the forces that permeate this place.

The light, the ever-shifting kaleidoscope of color that bathes the obsidian plain, gives the Drifters their form and their fleeting existence. It is not merely a source of illumination, but also a catalyst, a constant current of excitation that binds the dust particles together and allows them to interact in complex ways. The light is a language, a form of communication that is understood at a fundamental level by every atom of this world.

The crystalline structures, the source of errant, flickering light scattered across the plain, are the nervous system of the Drifters, the key to their limited consciousness. They are the receivers and transmitters of light, forming intricate networks that allow the Drifters to perceive and interact with the world around them. The crystals shape the Drifters, giving them form and a means of motion. When the Drifters move, the crystals shift in sync, each crystalline movement subtly influencing the others.

The Drifters are not unified entities in the way we understand a single creature. They are more like shifting clouds of consciousness, sometimes coalescing into recognizable forms - vague, elongated shapes that resemble nothing familiar to us. Other times they are amorphous masses, swirling and merging with the storms, their forms constantly morphing and dissolving. They possess no definite sense of individuality. They are more like facets of a shared consciousness, individual expressions of a collective intelligence.

Their interactions are a dance of light and dust, a symphony of shifting colors and ephemeral forms. They do not communicate through voice, but through intricate patterns of light and energy, using the crystalline structures as the tools of speech, ringing out, like chords played on instruments we can neither see nor hear.

The Drifters are intrinsically linked to the ancient, fractal structures scattered across the plain. They are not their builders; indeed, it's probable that the Drifters are an aftermath. Through the presence of the structures, they are intimately connected to an earlier age. The Drifters are attuned to the humming resonance of those structures, using that resonance to alter the currents of light and dust, changing it as they move through the storms. They might be considered a tool, a device, or perhaps even a byproduct of that advanced technology's ceaseless operations - a technology that, in turn, seems inextricably tied to the fundamental forces of the world itself.

Their behavior is erratic, their movements seemingly random. However, there is a clear intelligence to it. These actions are a reflection of the underlying energies, a dance of creation and destruction, a dynamic play of forces that never ceases.

The Drifters are not merely inhabitants of this world; they are the world itself, its essence made manifest in the play of light, dust, and crystal. They are the echoes of a past, a reflection of a present, and a harbinger of a future that may never fully arrive, a silent testament to the vast, unknowable mysteries that lie beyond our comprehension. They are the ultimate expression of this reality: a dance of absence and potential, a testament to the infinite possibilities that emerge from the void.
