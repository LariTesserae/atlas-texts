The inhabitants of this plateau and canyon system aren't so much *seen* as *felt*. They are the very breath of the place, woven into its fabric. To speak of them as singular beings is perhaps inaccurate; they are more akin to a chorus, a symphony of interconnected existences.

At the apex of this complex web are the **Shepherds of Stone**. These beings, if they can be called such, are the remnants, or perhaps the inheritors, of the civilization that built the structures. Their forms, if they still have distinct forms, are lost to time and the blending with the stone itself. They are not visible, but their presence is **palpable**. Their 'bodies' seem to have become the very **plateau and canyons themselves**. They could be considered a hive-mind-like entity.

They shepherd the great, slow fauna. Their minds are attuned to the earth's rhythms, the pulse of the moist air, and they direct the lives of the many creatures who reside in this place. The great fauna are enormous, herbivorous, land beasts who's shells are covered in moss and fungal forests. Their bodies are conduits for energy and resources, their massive frames acting as living ecosystems. They are the heart of this world.

The second tier of life are the scuttling, six-legged beings. They are the **cleaners and recyclers**. These creatures, with their iridescent shells and clicking legs, are the constant busyness of this place. They are scavengers, consuming waste and breaking down organic matter. They have no visible leaders but an instinct to keep the place clear of dead matter. Their clicking is a constant, subtle music.

The final element are the plants. The many **strange, fleshy plants** that cling to the canyon walls. Some are bioluminescent, casting an ethereal glow in the perpetual twilight. Others resemble oversized succulents, swollen with moisture. These plant lives are not just passive life, but are instead part of the network of the shepherding beings.

These plant and creature lives interact in a self-governing bio-mechanical world. The beings control resources, maintain the cycles, and act as a link between the fauna and the larger network.

The civilization that once built the structures are gone in the classic sense. But their consciousness lives on. It is not a force that controls in the traditional sense. It *is* the place itself. It is the slow, patient unfolding of the seasons, the gentle erosion of the stone, the unwavering dampness in the air. Time flows differently here. Everything is connected. A single thought, a subtle change, can ripple through this system like a stone dropped in a vast, subterranean lake. It is a land of the ancient, where the boundaries between past and present have dissolved. This is a place where every element is interconnected, a living, breathing testament to the enduring power of life, and the strange, subtle whispers of the unseen.
