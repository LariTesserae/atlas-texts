In the ocean's embrace, where sunlight fractures into filtered blues, a civilization thrives, born of the water's very essence. The denizens of this submerged world are not beings of flesh and bone, but rather, extensions of the coral-like city itself. The city is their body, their mind, their universe. They are the architects, the inhabitants, and the very substance of this evolving ecosystem. They are, in a sense, *the city*.

These entities are less creatures and more manifestations of the city's growth, whispers of its consciousness. They don't have distinct forms in the way we understand it. Instead, they are fluid, shifting presences that coalesce within and around the city's structures. They might appear as shimmering, iridescent tides that flow through the corridors, or as gentle bulges in the coral-like walls, like the beginnings of a new tower. They are not defined by individual bodies, but by their collective participation in the city's becoming.

They communicate not through sound or language as we know it, but through a language of currents and light. Their thoughts are the shifts in the tides, the bioluminescent pulses that dance across the structures, the slow, resonating hums that echo in the depths. Their awareness is a shared experience, a collective consciousness that permeates every cell of the city. Their understanding of the world is not linear or sequential; it is a tapestry woven from the city's memories, its ongoing growth, its interactions with the life that surrounds it.

The giants, the majestic jellyfish-forms, may represent the highest expression of the city's consciousness in a way humans can observe. They are the elders, the keepers of memory. They are slow because their lives are long, measured in geological time. They drift through the kelp forests, their inner light a beacon of the city's deep wisdom. They may be the only manifestation of the city that is *aware*, that knows it is a city. They guide and protect, shepherding the city's growth, ensuring the balance of the ecosystem.

Beneath the surface of this slow, immense life, there exists a second level, a current with unknown inhabitants. The smaller creatures that flit through the city’s passages may be the city's young, the builders, the unformed thoughts of the city. Though unkown to most, their nature is clear: They are the ceaseless force of growth and adaptation. They navigate the labyrinthine spaces, bringing nutrients and light, shaping the city's ever-changing form.

If the city is a single entity, then these beings do not "live" so much as *be*. Their very existence is intertwined with the city's processes. They are the city's breath, its circulation, its slow, relentless expansion.

The "primitive" tech level is misleading. The city is not *un-technological*; its technology is simply integrated into its being. The architecture is not built, but grown. The city, in itself, is a sophisticated, self-regulating ecosystem where the laws of nature are embraced as intrinsic elements of its existence. 

Their purpose, if it can be called such, is not to conquer or construct, but to *become*. They exist in a state of perpetual emergence, guided by the slow, subtle currents of change. Time itself is their medium, growth is their method, and the ocean floor their destination. Within the cool depths, they dream their slow dream, a city of light and stone, unfolding in the boundless embrace of their watery home.
