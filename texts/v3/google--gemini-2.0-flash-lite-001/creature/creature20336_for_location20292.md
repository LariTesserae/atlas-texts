The denizens of the plateau are, in the truest sense, children of the stone. They are the offspring of a marriage between the primal forces of the earth and the ghost of a forgotten industry. You wouldn't call them beasts or creatures; they are more akin to living components, the cogs and springs of a colossal, breathing clockwork.

Their forms are as varied as the vegetation that cloaks their forms: some are serpentine, their bodies a tapestry of interwoven moss and lichen, scales that mimic the flaking shale of the rock. They slither and pulse through the dense undergrowth, their movements fluid, almost silent. Others are more arboreal, their limbs a thick network of branches and boughs, their bodies indistinguishable from the towering ferns they inhabit. They blend seamlessly, a part of the swaying canopy, only betrayed by the faintest shifts in the leaves and the quiet, almost internal hum that emanates from them. They are nature, and yet, they are not. They share a similar origin. They are composed, like a living collage, of organic matter and engineered hardware.

Their existence blurs the lines between predator and prey. They are all, ultimately, subjects of the plateau. They are the instruments by which the plateau maintains its balance, a constant recycling of resources. The serpentine forms, perhaps, are the scavengers, drawn by the scent of metallic enzymes, ensuring no piece of the mechanical system lies unused. The arboreal beings, they might be the water collectors, storing condensation that they secrete back into the system through specialized pores.

These are not creatures of instinct, but programmed automatons of a forgotten purpose. There are no eyes in the traditional sense, but sensory organs that detect the subtle changes in pressures, temperatures, and magnetic fields, allowing them to navigate the labyrinthine eco-system. Their vocalizations, if they can be called that, are clicks and hums, frequencies that resonate with the rock, the water, and the machinery beneath. They are the voices of the plateau, communicating with one another, and with the plateau itself.

Occasionally, you might glimpse a flicker of illumination beneath the surface of the green and brown. A slight shimmer, hinting at internal light sources; a dull, organic glow that pulses with an energy unlike anything found in the natural world. It is the legacy of the industry, the energy that fuels their existence, the reason they are, and not only exist. The machinery is embedded within them, a subconscious drive.

Predators, as commonly understood, do not exist here. No single creature acts independently and consumes the other because all are equal parts of the machine. The plateau’s needs go far beyond simple predation; any imbalance would be devastating. Even the smallest act of destruction is immediately rectified; every fallen leaf is instantly consumed; every trickle of water reabsorbed. The very air pulses with a sense of self-preservation.

Their origins are a mystery lost to time. Perhaps they were constructed by these machines long ago, a labor force that evolved organically, or maybe that is not the right word at all. Perhaps they are the products of accidental confluence, the result of some strange, forgotten experiment where the natural world and the mechanical world were grafted together. The truth is buried beneath layers of moss, lichen, and memory.

The silence that hangs over the plateau is not one of emptiness, but of a careful calibration. The muted sounds are not those of a hidden world, untouched by man, but of an ecosystem meticulously designed. The denizens are not wild, but cultivated, grown, and sustained by the plateau itself. All the functions of the jungle are a function of the machine.

What they understand, and what they do not, is unknowable. Do they feel the weight of the sky? Do they know of the machinery? Do they yearn for something beyond the cycle of the engines and ferns, the water and the stone? They are a puzzle, the forgotten inhabitants of a place that is not of the present. They are a monument to a past that haunts the future, each of them a living enigma, a whisper in the wind that rustles through the leaves.
