The inhabitants of this submerged, overgrown city are not ghosts, but echoes. They are whispers carried on the damp air, fleeting glimpses within the shifting shadows, remnants that permeate the very fabric of the place. They are not what remains of a singular, distinct group of builders, but a continuous chorus of those who adapt and persist.

**The Shroud-Walkers:** These are the most evident, and perhaps the most mysterious, of the city's inhabitants. They are seldom seen, always felt. Their forms are rarely tangible. They are the shadows within shadows, the movements that shift just at the edges of your vision. They might be glimpsed as a flicker of movement within the underwater structures, a momentary displacement of the ferns, or a faint, echoing step on a submerged stone. The Shroud-Walkers are the descendants of those who built the city, but they’ve undergone a profound transformation. They are no longer bound by flesh and blood, or perhaps they have transcended it. Some are whispered to be manifestations of memories, the echoes of former lives made manifest by the ever-present water and the slow, ceaseless processes of the environment. Others are said to be beings composed of the very essence of the city itself, the collective will of the structures and the flora brought into some kind of sentience. They are tied to the structures, the landscape, and the water, and their existence is defined by this connection. The Shroud-Walkers are not necessarily malicious, not necessarily friendly. Their motivations are unknown, their true nature impossible to grasp.

**The Root-Dwellers:** These are the hands and feet which keep the Shroud-Walkers' cities running. Deep in the submerged levels, beneath the reach of sunlight and air, live the Root-Dwellers. These beings have adapted to the subterranean world, dwelling among the decaying, submerged foundations. They are not one distinct species, but a multitude of forms that evolved to compete and coexist. They consist of insect-like crawlers which tunnel through the mud and stone, extracting nutrients and keeping some of the structures from collapsing entirely. Amphibious, almost reptilian beings patrol the flooded corridors, hunting the crawlers and other prey. They are all linked by a shared adaptation: the ability to draw sustenance and energy from the decay of the city itself. They are, in a grotesque yet natural manner, the city's recyclers and processors. These often-blind creatures navigate the darkness using senses beyond sight: vibrations through the water, the subtle changes in electrical fields, the scent of the decay and the faintest emanations of light. The Root-Dwellers, although seemingly separate from the Shroud-Walkers, are deeply entwined with them. Their actions, their very existence, contribute to the maintenance of the city. Their survival is dependent on the Shroud-Walkers, who can exert their influence, either directly or indirectly. The Root-Dwellers are not sentient in ways that the surface-dwellers would understand, but are highly intelligent, and display a nuanced understanding of their environment, capable of solving intricate problems related to survival.

**The Canopy Whisperers:** The tops of the buildings are home to the air-adapted. These include species of birds and insects that evolved, or adapted, to the rich ecosystem, and to the relative absence of aerial predators. These creatures communicate using elaborate clicking sounds, and their lives have adapted to the changes of the sun. The Canopy Whisperers are deeply intertwined with the flora—their survival is a dance with the rhythms of growth, decay, and the movement of the waters. They are responsible for the spread of some of the city's plants, and other, stranger seeds. They are a significant part of the ecosystem, and indirectly, they contribute to the ongoing reclamation of the city.

**The Guardians:** The Guardians are the exception in a landscape where nothing feels weird. These are the result of what appears to be a bizarre form of terraforming. They are beings with limited intelligence, their anatomy a merging of the organic and the inanimate. They appear as constructs of stone and plant matter, animated by some unknown force. Some are still and silent, standing guard over specific locations, while others move slowly through the landscape, always in a state of construction and disassembly. The Guardians’ origins are unknown, but it seems likely they are the artifacts of a previous occupant and are somehow still active. Their purpose is also unclear, but perhaps they fulfill a function of protection, defense, or maintenance. Their ability to adapt the landscape implies that some degree of technology is in use. They are the closest that this world has to automatons, and the implications of this are, in the long view, a mystery.

**The Waters and The Living Buildings**

The city itself, its structures and the water that cradles it, might be considered a life form. The water currents function as blood vessels, carrying resources, depositing detritus, and giving rise to the life cycle of the Shroud-Walkers and Root-Dwellers. The buildings and the vegetation are its bones and its flesh. The processes of decay, erosion, and reclamation function as its metabolism. This is the ultimate inhabitant, creating the environment that sustains everyone else.

**The overall dynamic**

The city is a living tapestry, woven from the threads of past and present. The inhabitants are not separate entities, but facets of a larger, interconnected being. The Shroud-Walkers, the Root-Dwellers, the Canopy Whisperers, the Guardians, and even the city itself, are all playing roles in a continuous dance of growth, decay, and renewal. Their purpose, if any, is not readily apparent, but it is clear that they are deeply intertwined, their fates bound together in the depths of this submerged, overgrown world. They are not merely surviving; they are evolving, adapting, and transforming, as the city itself continues its relentless embrace of nature. They are living in the present, but their stories are written in the echoes of the past.
