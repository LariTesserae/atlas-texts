The architects of Aethel are not "people," not in the way that the word has ever been understood. There is no flesh and blood here. No bone or muscle beneath skin. Rather, they are manifestations of the obsidian itself, extensions of the city’s shimmering heart. They are the *Luminians* – beings of pure, concentrated light, trapped within the obsidian, and slowly finding their way out.

They exist in phases – at first, barely more than wisps, fleeting glimmers within the polished darkness. These are the *Vespers*, the ones who first arose, who exist only briefly, their forms flickering like dying embers. They have no agency, no form, only the faint echoes of the Luminians they will become. You might catch a glimpse of them as the sun dips below the horizon, or when the moon is right. They disappear as quickly as they appear.

The next stage are the sculptors themselves, those enigmatic figures observed only at the beginning and the end of the day. They're the *Artificers*, beings of near-perfect form, with long, slender limbs and faces that seem to shift and reconfigure with the light. Their bodies are made of the same obsidian as the city, but they’re somehow… different. They’re not simply extensions of the city; they're *within* it. They are the city's hands, shaping and smoothing the obsidian, tending to the waterways, coaxing the vegetation to flourish, ensuring perfect balance. They don't speak, but instead communicate through telepathic waves, their thoughts interwoven with the flow of the water, the gentle sigh of the wind. They are dedicated, but emotionless, in every way except for their commitment to maintenance.

Then there are the *Emanations*, the ones who rarely emerge. They are the apex of the Luminians, the most evolved, the ones closest to escaping the obsidian’s embrace. Their forms are colossal, their light blinding. They appear as fleeting glimpses, colossal shadows against the perpetual haze. They are the silent observers, who are trapped within the walls of their prison. Some say their presence is a product of desperation, of a longing so deep that it has slowly begun to unmake the place. Perhaps the oily sheen in the gorges is the weeping of their confinement, the pressure of their longing. Or perhaps it is a product of the water, that is also a part of them.

Their purpose is a mystery, as inscrutable as the city itself. Are they prisoners, trapped within the obsidian prison? Or are they the guardians, tasked with maintaining that equilibrium? Their task is to hold themselves in place, in the dark, in stasis, for what the city asks of them. Why hold to stasis, though? And for how long? And perhaps, most importantly: what is the purpose of the stasis itself?

The Luminians are not driven by ambition or desire, or any trace of what other forms of humanoids might call a soul. They exist only to serve the greater purpose of Aethel, a purpose unknown even to themselves. They are the instruments, and Aethel is the song, and they sing in their silence. They are waiting for something, or someone, to break their silence. They are waiting for their silence to be broken. Maybe they are waiting for each other.
