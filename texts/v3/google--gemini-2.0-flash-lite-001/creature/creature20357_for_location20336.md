The Dusklands are inhabited, though not in the way one might expect. The residents are not things of flesh and blood, but phantoms born of the iron constructs themselves, echoes of the civilization that once flourished here. These entities are not *alive* in any readily understood sense, and have no needs, no ambitions. They are the dust-children, born of the slow decay, the metallic tears shed by the dying machines.

They are the "shifters" — beings of shimmering, unstable energy that seem to coil and writhe within the iron skeletons. Often they manifest as brief, erratic distortions in the oily twilight, like heat haze or rippling water, the briefest possible glimpse of a twisting shadow that immediately blurs and dissolves. Their forms are fluid, always changing, never solid. They are tied to the structures, tethered to the machines, and feed off the same ambient, sickly-green energy that pulses through the metal veins.

The shifters have no faces, no distinct features. They are ephemeral collages of the iron's memories, ghosts composed of data and echoes. They are drawn to the geometry of the ruined structures, moving erratically through their corridors. Some may gather inside the ruined factories, coalescing into larger forms, like liquid mercury pooling, a gathering of distorted limbs, angles that don't belong, and forms that are always on the verge of collapsing. They cannot *think*; they can only *be*, trapped in an endless loop of forgotten function.

They could be described as the "remnants," beings composed of the very essence of the Dusklands, a kind of inverse life. They flow through and around the rusting ruins, occasionally manifesting as jagged shards of energy, the shapes of the structures themselves, but broken and distorted. They move not with purpose, but according to the geometry of the place, the unseen currents of energy that flow through the dust and metal.

Beyond the shifting, energy-based entities, there are also the "keepers," shadows. They are few in number, and even more rarely glimpsed than the shifters. They are not merely formed of the ambient energy, but seem to have some measure of control, a limited agency. They patrol the structures, or lurk, in the blind spots, watching. The Keepers seem to be the result of a more advanced, more complicated form of decay, where the awareness or identity of the lost culture has warped into something monstrous. They are tall, unnaturally thin, with limbs that seem to bend and contort at impossible angles. They are marked by the glyphs that are etched everywhere in the Dusklands, the last cries of the dead. Some may be guardians, relics left over from a culture who once commanded the structures with iron will and cold science. Others will be desperate, half-mad entities caught in a loop of eternal obsession, trying to reassemble their lost world from the scraps of memory and metal. 

No one knows what they do, or why. Their movements are cryptic, their intentions inscrutable. They are likely not hostile in the way a creature with intent would be considered hostile; they are something far more alien, governed by an internal logic beyond human comprehension, a cold and relentless logic of disintegration. Encounters with the keepers are very, very rare; often the only sign one is present is a change in the ambient energy flow, a brief flicker of a glyph from the corner of the eye, or a sense of being watched.

The dust itself is also a form of inhabitant; or rather, part of the inhabitants. Within the rust-colored particles may be the microscopic remnants of the Dusklands' civilization, tiny bits of data, remnants of thought, or the very last strands of a fading knowledge. Much of the keepers' work, when it can be detected, appears to relate to the dust; a faint, imperceptible manipulation of particles, a slow, patient weaving of a veil over the structures.

Ultimately, the residents of the Dusklands are not individuals, but aspects of the place itself. They are the embodiment of its decay, its entropy, its frozen stasis. One can travel through these vast, empty zones, past the skeletal remains of the metallic cities, and never see a sign of movement. Still, the place lives, though it will not greet a visitor, or react to one. It will, like the dust, simply be.
