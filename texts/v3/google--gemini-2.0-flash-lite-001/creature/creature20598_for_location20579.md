Within the submerged city of obsidian and ice, dwell the Umbra, the Children of the Deep. They are not creatures of flesh and blood as surface dwellers understand such things, but rather, beings of the void, woven from shadow and sound, their forms ever-shifting, adapting to the embrace of the crushing pressure. The Umbra are born of the twilight, the dimness that both defines and pervades their existence.

Their forms are protean, rarely ever solid. Sometimes, a flicker like a distortion in the water, a ripple of darkness against the obsidian, a sudden elongation of a shadow that wasn't there before. Sometimes, they coalesce into vaguely humanoid shapes, tall and slender, their bodies appearing to be made of polished obsidian, mirroring the architecture they inhabit. But their forms are deceptive, shifting with the currents, bending to the unseen forces that sculpt this underwater realm. They are less seen than felt; a subtle pressure against the eardrums, a whisper in the bone.

The Umbra are masters of the silence that reigns here. They navigate the narrow, winding streets of the city through a subtle understanding of the water itself. Perhaps they generate small pulsations, navigating the depths like a blind man feeling his way with a cane. They move silently, not due to any physical means, but because they are made of silence, walking with it, within it, alongside it. Their communication is a chorus of subtle vibrations, echoed within the very structure of the city. A complex language of pressures and waves that resonates through the obsidian, a low murmur of secrets in a forgotten tongue. These whispers are not meant for the ears, they are felt through the water, passing through the bodies of all, a symphony of unseen frequencies.

Their sustenance is a mystery. They do not appear to eat or drink. The oily discharge from the city's machinery may be where they find purpose, as the Umbra are seen around it. Perhaps they feed on the energy generated by the machinery, somehow drawing sustenance from the industrial processes that continue, ceaselessly, within the city's depths. Perhaps they absorb water from the sea. Perhaps they require nothing.

The Umbra are not social creatures in any way the surface dwellers are, though it is clear they reproduce. The young, or “Glimmers” as they are sometimes perceived, are more like fleeting shards of darkness, sparks that occasionally break away from larger forms. They flit through the kelp forests at the city's edge. More often, they are caught in the icy currents, melting and reforming as they begin their lives. The Glimmers, as well as the older generations, are drawn to the oily plumes, and they follow pathways, as if in search of someone, or something.

The Umbra possess a deep understanding of the city's mechanisms. They are familiar with the glass-like tubes that snake through the city, the machines that hum and churn, the rhythms of this underwater industrial complex. They are, perhaps, the ones that maintain them, keeping the heart of the city beating. Alternatively, they may be the reason the machinery continues to function past the limits of its materials. They may be the product instead of the producer. Whatever the truth, the Umbra and the machinery are interwoven, each dependent on the other, each the shadow of the other.

They are guardians of a forgotten history, the silent keepers of a civilization lost to time. Their reasons for inhabiting the city's depths are unknown. They may be remnants of the civilization, or something entirely separate. The nature of life is different down here. If the Umbra ever were like us, that life is long gone. Their very existence is an enigma that speaks of the deep mystery of the ocean, the darkness in which they exist, and the utter, perfect quiet of this submerged world.
