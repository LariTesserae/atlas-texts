The inhabitants of this fractured landscape, if they can truly be called that, are things of the in-between. They are not merely creatures; they are the consequence of a world constantly unraveling, a testament to the persistent, slow work of entropy. They embody the paradox of this place – decay and emergence intertwined, a constant becoming from the rubble.

**The Shapers:**

The true architects of this world are the unseen, the ones who seem to have come from the landscape itself. They are not necessarily *alive* in any traditional sense. Perhaps they are an evolved form of the strange symbols etched into the stone, an energy that interacts with the stone and the rain, able to induce a shifting in the very fabric of reality. They are the ones who *understand* the shifting, who have learned to flow with the chaos of this world.

Their presence is registered through the subtle alterations of the landscape. They might be responsible for the canals and infrastructure, but not in the way one would imagine. They are attuned to the earth's natural flows, the path of water and wind. Their constructions reflect this understanding, a harmonious integration rather than a brute force attempt at control. They are the unseen hands that gently nudge reality, the spirits that breathe life into the decay. Their purpose is not for preservation or creation, but for transformation to the next evolving form.

**The Metallic Birds:**

The metallic birds are possibly the most overt manifestation of this transformation. Their sleek, almost artificial bodies are a striking juxtaposition against the rough stone, the product of the interaction of the shifting, the constant rain, and the remains of the industry. They are a fusion of the natural and artificial, a physical embodiment of the place's strange beauty.

These birds do not follow any known logic of avian behavior, their long, delicate legs suggesting a life lived among the fragmented machinery. They act as extensions of the infrastructure, as if some mechanism is maintaining their flight through use of the surviving energies, the echoes of a past technological age. They are, in a sense, a form of active decay, spreading the metallic residue of the factories, feeding the rain, and further integrating the elements of the mechanical world with the natural. 

**The Whispers and Legends:**

Deeper within the canyons, the whispers of larger creatures are heard. They are the apex predators of this ecosystem, but their forms are ephemeral, a blend of what was and what is, a phantom echo of what will be. These creatures aren’t truly “real” in their entirety, representing the potential for new life, new forms emerging from the ashes.

The whispers are a warning, a promise, and a reflection of this environment. They are a sign of the unknown, the things this place creates but we cannot see. 

**The Last Remnants:**

In the depths of the factories and the overgrown infrastructure reside remnants of the original inhabitants. Once vibrant and productive, now they are lost. They are absorbed by the strange power of the landscape, like husks of a former harvest. Their actions are driven by instinct more than reason. Driven by the echoes of their previous lives, they wander, haunted and disoriented, through the ruins. 

**The Environment:**

The true inhabitants, however, are the elements themselves. The constant rain, wind, and the stone are the actors, shaping the landscape and its occupants. The very air pulses with an unseen energy, the hum of the shifting, which could be the source of all the strange transformations. In this world, the distinction between life and inorganic matter blurs. Everything is becoming something else.
