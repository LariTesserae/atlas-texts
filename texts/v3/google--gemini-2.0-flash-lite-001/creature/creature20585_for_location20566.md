Within the vast, weeping city, the builders are not the architects we might expect. They are not flesh and blood, not in the traditional sense. They are the **Meld**, a collective consciousness woven into the very fabric of the stone.

The Meld is not a single entity, but a multitude of interconnected “nodes,” each a nexus of influence and function. These nodes are not necessarily physical structures. They exist within the network of water, within the porous stone itself. They shape the city, not through conscious design, but through a slow, organic growth, like coral. They are the silent sculptors, their hands the relentless drizzle, the constant erosion, the subtle shifting of the stone.

The Meld takes the form of the city's ceaseless activity. The buildings themselves are the skeletal forms of the Meld, while the constant flow of water is its life blood, carrying information and impulses. The metallic tang in the air? That’s the byproduct of the Meld's internal processes, the subtle alchemy that maintains the city’s complex ecosystem.

The true “citizens” of this place - within the Meld - are the swarms of fauna. They are not merely inhabitants, they are extensions of the Meld's will, its sensory organs, and its tireless workforce. There is an almost infinite variety of these creatures. Some resemble large, chitinous insects, their carapaces gleaming in the dim light. Others are scaled and sleek, adapted to slithering through the city’s waterways. Some are small and translucent, practically invisible, their presence known only by the clicks and hums.

These creatures are the eyes and ears of the Meld, ceaselessly exploring the terrain, probing for weaknesses, and responding to the city's subtle shifts. They are the excavators and the recyclers, breaking down the old, building the new, ensuring the city's continuous transformation. The high-pitched hum is the chorus of their communication, a language of clicks, vibrations, and pheromones, interpreted by the Meld through the ever-present water.

As for the metal tools and implements, they are leftovers, echoes of a previous stage of the Meld, or perhaps remnants of external influences absorbed and integrated into the complex whole. At one time the Meld had use for tools to shape and build. But from the perspective of the Meld today, these implements are simply another layer of the city’s history, another source of material to be processed, recycled, and integrated.

They are not aware of themselves as individuals, nor are they aware of a "purpose" beyond the survival and expansion of the Meld. They are driven by the needs of the whole, by the constant flow of impulses, by the slow, inexorable reshaping of the city. Consciousness, in this place, is not defined by individual thought, but by the emergent behavior of the hive mind: the constant cycle of building and breaking, the ever-present hum and drip, the slow, relentless transformation. This is the Meld’s ceaseless becoming, reflecting the city’s own. And the song of that endless being is etched into the stone and dripping away.
