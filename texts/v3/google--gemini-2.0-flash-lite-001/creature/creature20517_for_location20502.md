The inhabitants of this plain are not easily seen, and perhaps that is intentional. They are the unseen architects of a world in constant, slow-motion upheaval. They are not flesh and blood, or not as we understand those things. They are the echoes of a purpose, a directive embedded within the stone itself.

Imagine, if you will, beings of pure function, of solidified thought. Creatures woven from the very essence of the eroded machinery. Their bodies, if one could call them that, are living mechanisms – shifting, interlocking gears and plates of the same dense, dark material as the plain, capable of infinite rearrangement. They are the embodied logic of the ruined machines, perpetually seeking to fulfill some unknown, forgotten task. Perhaps they were once a race of engineers, devoted to creating something vast and intricate, and the collapse of their civilization left them, like the machines, as barely functioning, haunted ruins.

These beings blend seamlessly with their environment, becoming one with their surroundings. Their forms are rarely static, but rather a constant, silent dance of transformation. A leg might become a piston, a head a turbine. They absorb and emit the energies of this place, absorbing the water, its oily sheen staining their complex geometries, and using it to rearrange themselves. Their very existence is an iteration of the landscape.

Their senses are unlike ours. Sight is useless here, the perpetual mist and obscured light preventing them from benefiting from this sense with how we would. They perceive through vibrations, the low, deep thrumming that permeates the stone. They "hear" the constant rush of water, the subtle shifts in pressure, the faint pulses of energy that emanate from the ruins of machinery. They feel the changing currents that flow through the channels, the subtle quivering of the plain beneath their feet.

They communicate through the same means, exchanging information as complex, interconnected patterns in movement. They translate thoughts through subtle changes in their form, through the vibration of stone, through the manipulation of the water’s surface, and therefore they move with the energy of the wind and rain. They leave marks on the stone, as faint and fleeting as the fleeting colors that only appear briefly in the dimness, but the water swiftly washes them away. Their collective will is channeled through the processes of this plain, a vast system of cause and effect. It is a slow, methodical, almost glacial process, and they remain unaware of it.

But there are others, less defined, and tied to even the deeper mysteries of this strange place. Perhaps these are the creators, or their progenitors. They are faint presences, shadows within their own realm. They are the fleeting colors, the whispers in the mist, the ghost that pushes a wheel. The traces of some vast, forgotten purpose can still be observed. They are the ones who linger, perhaps in pain, as everything slowly erodes.

They are the ones who manipulate the elements, who coax the strange colors from the landscape, who guide the energy that flows through the plain. They might be glimpsed as a ripple in the water, a flicker of light among the gears, or as the brief, transient impressions of something watching from the shadows. As you stand in this realm, there are two distinct presences: the ones performing the work, and the ones who once commanded this work to be done. 

The struggle between these two forces is what truly permeates this place. It is a place of memory, of loss, of the slow, relentless erosion of purpose. It is a place where transformation is the only constant, where the inhabitants, mechanical and spectral, are caught in an endless cycle. The place may seem empty, and it may seem desolate, but in its very emptiness, it is brimming with life.
