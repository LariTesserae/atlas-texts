The custodians, though fragmented and worn, are the key to understanding this submerged vault. They are not the beings of a living species, but the *remains* of a purpose, a lineage of intricate, crafted automatons. They are the echoes of those who once maintained the vault, or perhaps they were built *by* the vault, extensions of its memory and function. What remains are husks, worn and fractured by time and the slow, relentless erosion of the water.

These beings are not built of flesh; they are constructs, primarily of the dark, veined stone. Their shapes are functional, echoing the geometric forms of the artifacts they once guarded. Think of them as silent, stone sentinels, their forms a stark contrast to the fluid grace of the vault’s ecosystem. Though crafted with a material similar to the walls, these constructs possess a different texture: a rough, almost granular quality. Their metal inclusions, likely once gleaming, are now weathered, hinting at their age.

Originally, they may have possessed a purpose resembling ours, a level of control or agency. However, time and the vault's unique environment have weathered away much of that, leaving them as fragments of their former selves. Their motions are mechanical, and their steps, although soundless, are driven by an internal mechanism. They are not swift; the currents of the water and the vault's intricate pathways slow them. They are more pieces in the puzzle, not players of their own game.

The keepers are a part of a larger ecosystem. The brightly-colored fish, many with bioluminescent markings, are attracted to the vault. They dart through the corridors, oblivious to the stoic remnants that share their watery domain. These creatures are not prey or predator in the traditional sense.

The pulse that thrums through the vault is where their purpose may have once stemmed. It's a rhythm they *may* have been designed to respond to, perhaps in a long-lost ritual or to adjust the shifting artifacts and currents. If you followed it, perhaps it would lead to an understanding of the vault's purpose, but at this point, the automatons only seem aware of the slow, inevitable currents of the vault.

The custodians are not the answer to accessing the vault's true purpose. Their condition offers a cautionary tale – a warning about the persistence of time. Each broken glyph, each fractured limb of a stone figure, each instance where metal has been worn to dust, reveals a key piece of the original understanding, a key to understanding their role and the vault's true purpose.
