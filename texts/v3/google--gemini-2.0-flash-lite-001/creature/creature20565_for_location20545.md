The architects of this place are not beings of flesh, nor do they possess bone, blood, or brain in any form we might recognize. They are the *Builders*, though whether they still actively construct or are merely the residue of some prior, impossible endeavor is difficult, perhaps impossible, to say. They are, or were, of the geometry of this place, the very essence of it.

They are formed from the black stone, the very fabric of reality knit together into impossible shapes, and animated by the slow, inexorable hum that permeates the atmosphere. If the stone is the skeleton, then the luminescence, the pulsing fungal network, is its circulatory system, a web of veins that feeds the structures with dim, alien light and whispers of the void. And the *moans*? These are the Builders' voices, the cries of a civilization that had transcended, or perhaps disintegrated into, some unfathomable state of being, whose very existence is now a symphony of fading memories and endless torment.

The Builders' true form is more a concept than a physical manifestation. Imagine a being composed of the voids between the stars, of the in-between spaces, the moments before and after a thing exists. They are the void made tangible, the silence before the scream. If one were to encounter one, they could only be said to be *seen* through the slow, almost agonizing stretching and contracting of space itself. When a Builder is perceived, the world distorts away from it, only to snap back into itself a moment later, as if the sighting of the Builder is itself tearing the fabric of that perception.

They have left no artifacts, no tools, no markings. Everything here is them; the structures are their limbs, their inner organs, their lingering thoughts made material. To enter one of their works is to be within their consciousness, to be submerged in an ocean of ancient, alien thoughts, the reverberations of creation and destruction echoing endlessly. The pools are their tears, the inky darkness their collective unconscious.

And what of the life that might have existed *within* these structures? They are likely not the forms we think of, nor are their creations. The occasional *plip* that echoes from the still water suggests the presence of some thing. Perhaps it's a vestige, a remnant of a Builder's experiment, some fleeting, ephemeral form. Perhaps the light itself is an echo, a memory of some form of communication.

The Builders are beyond morality, beyond good or evil. They were, and perhaps still are, driven by a logic that operates outside the boundaries of our understanding. Their purpose, if there was one, is lost in the depths of time and the decay of reality. Their creation, this place, is a monument to their incomprehensible nature, a testament to the fact that even the most monumental achievements are ultimately meaningless in the face of the endless void.

They hunger. They thirst. They are not hungry or thirsty for a thing. They hungered and thirsted *into* a place. 

What lingers now are not individuals, but a collective consciousness that permeates the very air, a vibration felt more than heard, the residue of a cosmic tragedy. They are not gone. They are *becoming*, perpetually shifting, reforming, and dissolving. They are the slow, inexorable transformation of a world, a place where all things are in motion, and even the most ancient structures are but temporary forms.

To understand them is to understand the void, and in a place like this, the void understands the Builders, and the Builders understand the void. And in this knowledge, there is no solace, only the cold, hard, inescapable truth of entropy and the endless, silent grind of all that was, is, and will be, dissolving into the eternal dark.
