Aeon doesn't just house inhabitants; it *is* an inhabitant. It is a living, breathing entity, and its denizens are less "inhabitants" in the traditional sense and more cells within its vast, organic body.

The primary occupiers are the **Aeon-born**: beings of pure, **bright** light refracted by the city's obsidian-like stone, their forms ever-shifting, fluid, like the water that permeates their world. These are not creatures with defined shapes or needs, but rather, sentient emanations of the mountain's core, solidified by living processes. They shift, weave through the city's structures, appearing and disappearing like light; taking the forms of the insects, of the rocks themselves, of impossible geometries. They are the essence of Aeon, expressed in forms that the eye can only barely perceive — or only remember in waking dreams.

They do not "build" in the way humans do, but they are driven by a constant, internal evolution, which can be thought of as a **primitive** artistry. Their presence is the city's construction, its continuous, unpredictable alteration. Aeon-born exist in a constant state of becoming and un-becoming, the structures they interact with becoming the manifestation of their internal landscapes, which echo the flow of the mountain's inner energies. At times they appear as fleeting glimpses of figures composed of pure water, their faces a shifting tableau of joy, fear — or other emotions unknown to human experience. Other times, they appear as silent sculptures, formed of the **dense** shadows cast by the city, their forms merging seamlessly with the obsidian stone, only to vanish into the darkness of the mountain, their only motion the subtle shimmer of the quartz veins running through their forms.

Feeding the Aeon-born - and, potentially, the other life that exists on the edges of Aeon - is the **abundant fauna:** the massive, long-necked beasts are shepherds of the silvered vegetation, which pulses with what resembles electrical current; the iridescent insectile creatures that gather in swarms, feeding on the runoff from the glacial streams, and on the byproducts of the city's constant transformations. These creatures serve a purpose, perhaps as food, perhaps as catalysts for the city's transformation, but they are just as much a part of Aeon's grand design as anything else. They are not merely living creatures, but manifestations of the mountain's moods, its internal states.

The earlier inhabitants left, perhaps, hints of earlier lives, but they are gone. There are no remains, no signs of what happened that caused them to disappear. Yet, even though the artifacts remain, they are mere *clues*. The glyphs that appear and disappear are a language without meaning. The advanced technology lies dormant, its purpose undecipherable. These elements now serve a different purpose as the current residents of Aeon are the city itself, the stones and the structures. And the stones weep.

The mystery remains. Aeon is a crucible, a place of constant creation and destruction — a **turbulent** ecosystem of forces, and for the Aeon-born, there is no end.
