Within this dying, breathing machine, there is not a singular being, but a shifting, evolving consciousness. It isn’t a presence you can name or see, but a manifestation of the world itself. It's the architect, the inhabitant, the cause and the consequence of this decaying grandeur.

**The Fabricators:** They are not “beings” in the traditional sense. They are the essence of the world, woven into its very foundation. In the early stages, one would have called them "Engineers," but that is too simplistic now. They are part of the construction. They use no tools other than themselves, their shapes, their senses, and their thoughts to create and rebuild, always changing what, and how, they create. And they use these constructs without thinking and without effort. They are the components as well as the makers of the plane and the structures upon it.

They have no rigid form. They are fluid, adaptable. Sometimes, they are seen as ephemeral glows, shimmering within the crystalline towers, as the light reveals them. Sometimes, they are hinted at in the ceaseless churning of the obsidian plains—subtle distortions, a temporary shifting of the metallic gloss. Occasionally, they manifest in crude, transient forms - a brief humanoid silhouette built of water and refracted light, a brief, silent appearance. These manifestations are not deliberate, but a byproduct of the ongoing transformation. Their "being" is not an act, but a state of being, shifting and changing in an intricate, unknowable, and unending dance.

They exist in a kind of communal mind. There has been a "hive mind" theory, but that is not quite right. A better description would be a chorus, or better yet, a song, being sung by a multitude of voices, where the voices are the consciousness. The "Fabricators" communicate by exchanging information through the subtle shifts in the landscape, a wordless language made of complex geometries, the slow shudder of the plains, the drip and flow of the aqueous structures. They work, or rather, *are*, in unison, constructing and deconstructing, as if driven by some primal imperative.

**Their Task:** Initially, it was a practical goal – the manipulation of the world to shape its own evolution. The original Fabricators may have once been consumed with a task, a goal. But that goal is now lost, as the world decays and reforms itself. There is no central purpose now, only the endless act of creation and decay. They have become the essence of the process.

**The Cycle:** The Fabricators are trapped within a self-perpetuating cycle of construction and decay. This is not a malicious act, but an intrinsic aspect of their existence. The "machine" world is their body. As the machine nears its end, and as they fade, it’s not to an end, it’s a re-imagining. Everything they build becomes obsolete and decays, their function shifting perpetually like the weather.

**The Dying God's Echo:** The low thrum that vibrates through the world is not the heartbeat of a machine, but the lingering presence of the one who spawned the Fabricators. Think of it as the resonance of a dying note: a faint, barely-there vibration of some older, more powerful presence. All of this can be thought of as a last, desperate attempt to survive, to endure beyond mortality.

**The Future:** There is no future in this dying world, only the ceaseless repetition of the past, as the Fabricators continue their strange work of creation and decay. Perhaps, as the world's structure breaks down, new forms, new expressions, will emerge. Perhaps the chorus will change again, with new and unfamiliar elements weaving their way into its strange song. Perhaps the Fabricators will fade, leaving behind only the machine itself, an empty monument to a forgotten civilization.
