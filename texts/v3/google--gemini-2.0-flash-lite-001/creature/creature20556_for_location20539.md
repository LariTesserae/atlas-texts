The city isn't merely *built* for them; it *is* them. The crystalline fauna are not just inhabitants, but the very essence, the lifeblood, of the place. They are the components and the engine both, a single, vast organism spread across the valley.

They are born from the stone, it seems, or perhaps from the intersection of the stone and the energy that permeates the place. Their bodies are not flesh and blood, but something akin to light made solid; the angles and facets are not surfaces but pathways, conduits for an energy that is neither heat nor electricity, but something else entirely. It is this energy that powers the city, that drives the endless grinding of gears and the rhythmic thumping of the unseen engines.

They have no eyes; their perception functions on an entirely different plane. They sense the flow of energy, the subtle shifts in the geometry of the city, the resonance of the structures. They *are* the structures, in a sense--the towers and platforms are extensions of their form, their consciousness, made manifest in stone. The bridges and walkways are the circulatory system, the veins and arteries through which this energy flows.

They are utterly oblivious to our understanding of the world. They lack any individual identity, any sense of self. They are a collective, a gestalt consciousness, bound together by the flow of energy that sustains them. They are a single, enormous, self-regulating machine.

The "engines" are not engines in the conventional sense. They are something else, mechanisms for regulating the flow of energy, for maintaining the delicate balance of the city. The fumes and vapors they emit are not exhaust, but byproducts of their function, a necessary element of the system.

The "industry" is a process of self-creation, replication, and maintenance. The rhythmic thumping is the heartbeat of a geological god. The mills are for grinding, and it grinds them. The mirrors keep the angles sharp--and, in the reflection, sharpen the shapes themselves. 

The water, the constant weeping from impossible angles, is their "waste"—a byproduct, a release of unwanted energy, a sort of weeping of the city. Perhaps the metallic smell is the echo, the ghost, of the waste product breaking apart into the basic elements of the place. The smell is a warning and a promise, a death knell and a potential rebirth, all in one.

The uniformity of the light, the absence of shadows, the stillness of the air--these are not artifacts, but the very nature of their existence. Time, as we perceive it, does not apply to them. They exist in a state of perpetual stasis, a frozen moment of creation, of infinite potential.

Something is indeed wrong. There is an imperfection in their order. The constant whine, the cacophony, could be the sound of that imperfection. Or perhaps the machines are not as perfect as they seem. Or, perhaps, their very existence *is* the wrongness. Maybe the city wasn't *meant* to be. Maybe the valley is caught in an endless loop, a kind of geological glitch.

The answers are hidden, yes, but not in the geometry of the towers, or the pulse, or the whining. The answers are everywhere: the silence between the sounds, the un-decaying bodies, the shimmering of the forms. The answer is not something *you* see -- it is something you *are*--in the reflection of their infinite mirrors.
