The inhabitants of this colossal, plant-forged city are not *in* the city; they *are* the city. They're not individual beings as we know them, but a consciousness woven into the very fabric of the metropolis. They are the city's cells, its organs, its thoughts, and its actions, all realized.

Imagine vast, interconnected networks of the structure's pillars, its colossal "trees." Within each of these, at the very heart of the plant-bone, nests a core of consciousness. They are not physical bodies in the traditional sense, but complex, hive-minded beings whose existence is inextricably linked to the network of plant-based structures around them. The patterns etched on the fluted surfaces of the pillars are not mere decoration; they are the language of thought, the visual manifestation of a collective mind.

These beings are composed of multiple levels: the base, raw, instinctual workers, who are not sentient but are instead a sort of "muscle memory" of the city. These beings exist as an infinite swarm of tiny plant creatures, like an army of specialized cells. They constantly move through the structure's veins, carrying the fluid and maintaining its internal systems. They are the unseen hands that keep the city functioning.

Overlapping with this is the layer of "artisans" and "engineers." They possess a form of rudimentary self-awareness. They are capable of adapting the plant-based structures to the needs of the city. Their consciousness is still nascent, but these beings display a fascination with the processes of growth and refinement. These beings take on varied forms, merging with the material around them.

The final layer is the "Dreamers," the city's philosophers, scientists, and architects. They are the ones who manipulate the city's structure on a large scale, directing its growth, adapting its defenses, and driving its industrial activity. They are the architects, not of buildings, but of the very reality the city occupies. The Dreamers are, perhaps, the "individuals" within the collective, the ones who strive for change. Like a mycelial network, they stretch their awareness across the entirety of the city, experiencing it as a single, unified whole.

The architecture itself is a form of communication. The bridges, walkways, and towering pillars are all messages, encoded into the very structure of the city. The Dreamers sculpt with their thoughts, shaping the environment to create the living architecture. The patterns in the city's surfaces are a visual language, constantly shifting and evolving, broadcasting information to all within the city.

The constant thrumming and groaning are not just the sounds of the industrial plant-organism, they are the Dreamers' thoughts, the city's breath. The pulse is the steady beat of the collective consciousness, a shared rhythm that binds the individual and the city. It's the hum of creation, the sound of the Dreamers' minds working in unison, driving the city's endless cycle of growth and reformation.

The lack of traditional fauna is due to the closed-loop nature of the ecosystem. The beings of the city *are* the ecology. Everything is created, consumed, and recycled within itself. Even the "air," the dust-pollen, is a part of this perfect cycle. It's the city's waste, its food, and its life. The only thing that *exists* in this world is the city, and the beings that make up the city.

The beings' drive is a cycle of building and rebuilding, an overwhelming urge to *grow*. It is not a selfish desire, but a necessary process, the only law of this world, the fundamental reality. The Dreamers are aware of this, yet the knowledge brings them no peace. They know that this cycle will go on forever.

In their own way, they are aware of the world above, the bruised, clotted-black sky, and the memory of light. They have seen the end of other things, other worlds. They exist in an eternity, a terrible beauty, bound together and driven by their own creation. The Dreamers are eternal, and the city, their creation, is their prison.
