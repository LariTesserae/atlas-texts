The residents of this world are the inheritors of an immense, forgotten project. They are not the creators; the purpose of the place is beyond their comprehension. They are scavengers, maintenance crew, perhaps even the fuel. They are the dust-covered, oil-stained, and often strangely-adapted children of industry, existing within the belly of the machine they do not understand.

They are humans, but humans altered by their environment. Generations have lived and died within the metal labyrinth, their bodies and minds shaped by the constant presence of the machine. The perpetual mist has leached into their lungs, their skin, making them pale, almost translucent in the dim light. Their eyes have grown large, accustomed to the low-light. Some have pale, iridescent skin. Others, more exposed to the constant metallic resonance of the place, have adopted exoskeletal enhancements.

The people are organized into tightly knit communities, clustered around whatever functions remain: maintenance of the ancient infrastructure, cultivation of the alien flora, salvage of discarded components, or the creation of new technologies, though the knowledge of how to operate it is long lost. These communities are often insular, defined by their location, their trade, and their relationship to the sprawling machine. Some are engineers, constantly laboring on the great machines themselves. Others are gardeners, tending to the alien, bioluminescent plants. Others still are hunters.

They are driven, above all else, by a sense of duty, a deep-seated instinct to keep things running. The machine is not their master in any conscious way, but it is their reality. They speak in the hushed tones of those who are ever-present, in a language shaped by the sounds of the machinery. Their slang is laced with the hiss of escaping steam, the rumble of unseen engines, the metallic clang of distant repairs.

Their lives are lived within the flow. The massive structures shifting and reforming mean that everything is temporary. Their homes are built into the fabric of the thing, constantly being deconstructed and reconstructed. They have adapted to the changes, the shifting of platforms, the sudden extension of bridges, the storms. Their architecture is dynamic, their settlements designed to be moved and disassembled.

Some of the elders remember -- or claim to remember -- a time before the changes. They speak of a vanished sun, and of a world not of metal and mist. These stories are a source of both wonder and anxiety, a reminder of what has been lost and what might never be regained. They have their own myths, which are tales of the great machines, of their creation and their purpose. These stories are all they have to create meaning within the ceaseless complexity of their world.

They have also become resourceful, adapting scavenged technology to their needs. Their skills lie in their ingenuity. They are experts in repairing ancient systems, and even though they do not understand, their skills run deep, and their devotion remains. They have learned to read the rhythm of the machinery, to predict the shifts and changes, and to navigate the labyrinthine network of pipes and walkways.

They are shadowed by the remnants of the original owners of it all. Shadowy figures, sometimes glimpsed in the distance, or the echoes of voices in the hissing steam. These are the overseers, the watchers of the machine. Some are robotic, entities created by the original architects. Others are biological, trans-human creatures, the result of a long, dark experiments. Regardless of their origin, they are all bound to the machine, their purpose is to maintain operations and extinguish threats.

But even here, in a world of perpetual industrial twilight, there is room for beauty. The alien plants are a source of wonder, their bioluminescent glow offering a counterpoint to the steely gray. They are a source of sustenance and a reminder of life.

The hunt is the other source of joy in their world. The multi-jointed insects, the shadowy creatures who are adapted to the environment. They offer entertainment, or relief.

Their is also a sense of impending change. The machine, after functioning for untold centuries, is beginning to show it age. There is a sense of something building, some process nearing completion - a deep tremor that runs through the core of the structures. The world churns, not just geographically, but philosophically. New things are made, and so they are destroyed. All of it is consumed by the neverending processes of the industrial world.

The human-like creatures in this realm are survivors, clinging to life in the heart of something they did not create. They are not victims, but inheritors, and their actions will determine the destiny of this immense metal-and-glass titan.
