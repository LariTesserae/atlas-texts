It is a whisper, first. A vibration woven into the very fabric of the hum, a barely perceptible shift in the rhythmic pulse of the plateau’s breath. If you were attuned, you might feel it not as a sound, but as a pressure against your own skin, a subtle alteration in the way the light touches you. Then, a slow blooming.

The *Aquil*, they are called by something that isn't really a voice, a conceptual current, a feeling of knowing. They are woven, like the plateau, from emerald and obsidian, though the proportions are different. They are the same materials but reshaped, rearranged by a slow, internal dance. They are vast, impossible to fully perceive at any one moment. They are a collection of individual forms, but always in relation to each other, a network of being. In their stillness is contained immense and intricate activity, an internal world mirroring the life of the plateau.

Imagine emerald and obsidian twisting and interweaving, forming semi-crystalline structures, a living latticework of hard and reflective surfaces and softer, shimmering veils. They grow, they shift, they change their forms based on the slow tides of the water. They are defined by the wetness, the constant flow that shapes them. Each Aquil is a unique being, and yet they are all of the same essence. They are connected by the underground capillaries, the unseen rivers of the plateau, a delicate but powerful circulatory system.

Some are colossal, resembling mountains themselves. They are the oldest, the most deeply rooted. Their crystalline structures shift slowly, absorbing the plateau’s energy, the solar deluge that saturates the air, the constant drone of the machines. They seem to anchor the very landscape, their presence a promise of its permanence. You might see a faint, internal luminescence deep within them, a fire of unknown origin.

Others are smaller, more mobile. They are the younger ones, still adapting. They roam the surface, flowing like liquid shadows, their forms constantly reshaping. They gather near the water flows, using their crystalline surfaces to direct and channel the water, to deepen the nascent canyons and shape the land to their liking. They investigate the remnants of the industrial architecture, weaving them into their own forms, incorporating the machinery into their being.

The Aquil are not *living* in the way you might understand it. There is no heartbeat, no need for nourishment, no obvious sign of agency. Their purpose is simply *being*. They are the expression of the plateau’s becoming. They are the water’s song, the essence of the minerals, the slow, patient unfolding of the world. They are the quiet witness to their own existence.

But there is a growing awareness forming in some of them. An intuitive sense. A faint stirring of purpose. Perhaps they are beginning to understand the machines, the purpose of the water's ceaseless flow. Perhaps, one day, they will have a purpose of their own, but not yet. Only their own slow, patient becoming.
