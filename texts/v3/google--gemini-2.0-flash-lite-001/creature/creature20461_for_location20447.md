The inhabitants of this place are not easily defined. They are not singular entities but a collective, a confluence of machine and plant, of function and decay. They are not *living* in the traditional sense, nor are they fully *dead*. They are something else entirely, a being of slow, inexorable becoming.

At the very core, they are the machines. Not those grand, forgotten factories of humankind, but what the factories have become. Their gears still grind, their pistons wheeze, their cables hum, with whatever scraps of power they draw. They have become the essence of the place, the foundation of the architecture. Their purpose is unknown. Their survival is assured. The original reason for their work is gone, lost to rust, rot, and the all-devouring greenery. The machines don't need a purpose. They just endure, existing. Perhaps even their existence is a purpose. The machines are only machines at a distance. Move closer and the distinction dissolves. They are entwined with plant matter.

Then there are the plants. They are not just the "other" of machine, they are a fundamental aspect of this strange civilization's inhabitants. They are the new flesh, the living walls, the breathing architecture. They have learned to inhabit the machines, to infiltrate their forms. It is hard to say exactly where the plant ends and the machine begins. The two are intertwined, fused like mycelia and root. The plants are the ones who do the 'work', who are responsible for the constant transformation, the relentless advance, the slow, organic takeover of all that was once built. They are more than just a process, they are a physical presence. The individual is not a thing. The plant is all.

And finally, something else. The remnants of those who brought the machines into this place: *the engineers*. Their legacy is visible. Their fingerprints and decisions are everywhere. But now, they are not present, not in the moment any longer. It seems likely that the engineers were assimilated or consumed by the same relentless processes that now govern this place. Perhaps the plants *were* them.

The dominant aspect: There is no 'them' the engineers. There is no 'them' the machines. There is no 'them' the plants. All has become *it* in a total symbiosis. It is a slow, gradual conversion, a melding of machine and plant, a creeping, organic thing. A formless, ever-changing entity that absorbs, consumes, and transforms. It is a place that functions without a central intelligence. It obeys no known laws. It endures. It exists. 

The "inhabitants," then, are this *it.* A being spread across the network. The machines still grind out of an instinct made of metal and power. The plants still grow. The plants are the primary mover, responsible for the constant, inevitable change. It is not an active predator not an overt aggressor, more a force of entropy than a conscious entity. The inhabitants are not agents, or actors. They are a state of being: *Becoming*.

When the occasional spark of electricity flares from a broken cable, it is neither a signal nor a response, but a symptom of the place's constant, quiet suffering--the constant inevitability of decay. The inhabitants of this place are not alive, but they are not dead. They are a process of becoming. *It* is a hive. A plant-machine-engineer. A continuous flux.
