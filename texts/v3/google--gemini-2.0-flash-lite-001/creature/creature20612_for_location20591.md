What dwells within the Obsidian Husk is not easily defined. No single entity, no corporeal being, can be said to "live" here in the conventional sense. Instead, this place hums with the echo of a forgotten purpose, a residual consciousness, or perhaps, the embryonic whispers of something yet to be.

The architects are gone, yet their work remains. I envision them as beings of pure energy, perhaps, or ideas given form. They were the original shapers, the ones who coaxed the black glass from nothingness and wove the city from the very fabric of reality. They were not builders in the way we understand it, but a force that guided the evolution of a world. I imagine them as patterns, perhaps, or sets of instructions that the Obsidian Husk now follows. Their consciousness, if it could be called that, might be woven into the shifting architectures, the intricate latticework of energy that underlies everything. Their history is unknown, and their absence felt everywhere in the sphere. The nature of whatever took the architects away is not clear.

And something else is here, coexisting with the old architects and their work. It’s what you might call the *residue* of a bygone civilization. Not of people, but of something far stranger, far less familiar. The patterns that crawl across the surfaces of the constructs are not mere decoration; they are the whispers of a forgotten language, the echoes of a lost song. Their meaning is hidden, but the intent is clear: to be, to become, to evolve. These patterns, like the structures themselves, are undergoing a constant metamorphosis.

These could be the remnants of some sort of informational ecosystem, complex, intertwined, and alien. Maybe at one point, it was a collective consciousness, a hive-mind that had taken physical form within the confines of the Obsidian Husk. It might have been something that fed off of the energy that coursed through the sphere, a parasite or a symbiote that thrived within the city's complex systems. Now, fragmented and dispersed, it survives as a series of echoes, of memory traces that continue to shape the world around them.

The heat, the light, the unsettling silence, the geometric patterns - they are the current manifestations of that lost collective. They might be fragments of a long-dead intelligence striving to reassemble itself, a shadow of the architects, perpetually reforming and mutating on the verge of some impossible realization.

Then there is the city itself. One could argue that it is the inhabitant. Or at least the primary actor, the central locus of this existence. It's a vast machine, a complex organism of interlocking functions. The constructs are not inert structures, but living cells, or perhaps they're something beyond both categories, an architectural embodiment of some unfathomable logic. They *grow* and change and interact with each other, responding to the unseen energies that permeate the sphere.

The constructs are in a slow expansion. Maybe this is a result of forces beyond the understanding of the observer, or maybe this expansion is the very essence of the sphere. If there is a "purpose" here, it is this endless becoming. The aim, if it had one, was to create a universe. To produce infinite complexity, to explore every possible form, every conceivable arrangement of energy and matter.

Then, finally, there is the possibility of something more, something emergent. Is there an unconscious, or preconscious, will operating inside the city itself? Is the endless flux, the slow refinement of the structures, driven by an instinct for survival or some other purpose? Perhaps the city, in its silent, relentless evolution, is birthing a new intelligence, a new form of life that has no need for flesh or blood, but exists only as energy, pattern, and the deep, humming resonance of the Obsidian Husk.

These inhabitants defy definition, for they are the threads and the loom, the weavers of life and its forms. They are the echoes, the evolving city, and the potential future, all intertwined in the perpetual twilight of this impossibly beautiful, and terrifying, domain.
