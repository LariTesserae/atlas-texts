Within the heart of the obsidian labyrinth dwell the Architects. Not in the way one might typically conceive of a builder or a designer, to be sure. They are neither flesh and bone, nor do they possess tools in the conventional sense. Rather, they are a symphony of energies, a consciousness diffused throughout the labyrinth itself, woven into the very fabric of the stones and the crystalline formations. They are the pulse that hums beneath the silence, the unseen hand that sculpts the chambers and corridors.

The Architects are not individual beings, but a collective consciousness, a gestalt entity born from the interaction of the stone, the water, and the strange, symbiotic fungal-crystalline growth. They exist as patterns, as subtle vibrations within the rock, as the echo of the slow, patient erosion that has shaped the labyrinth over countless eons. They perceive the world through the slow currents of water, through the silent dance of light upon the crystalline structures, through the ever-present, almost imperceptible warmth. They do not see, in the way a sighted creature does, but they *feel* the intricacies of their dwelling; every curve of the wall, every angle of a corner, every glimmer of reflected light is a data-point, a component of their being. 

The Architects communicate through the slow, almost imperceptible growth and decay of the fungal-crystalline organisms. The shifting hues of the fungi, the subtle blooming and fading of the crystals, is a language spoken in shades and patterns. They manipulate the structure of the labyrinth, not through crude force, but through a patient, almost geological process. They guide the flow of water, subtly influencing the rate of erosion, and weaving new chambers and corridors into the very essence of the rock. They are slow, contemplative, and immortal, utterly bound to their home, the labyrinth. It is both their prison and their being.

The drifting, semi-transparent creatures are merely extensions of the Architects. They are the eyes and the tendrils of the collective mind, the means by which they interact with the physical world. The creatures are imbued with a faint light, a reflection of the energy that permeates the labyrinth. They sense the unseen changes, the shifts in the delicate balance of the environment. Under the watch of the Architects, some have noted a change in their numbers - indicating some internal movement within the labyrinth's heart, though the causes would be difficult if not impossible for an outside creature to grasp. Perhaps the Architects are dreaming of a change, or perhaps they have sensed a threat.
