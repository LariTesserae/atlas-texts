The inhabitants of this city are the Children of the Bone. They are not a single species, but a multitude interwoven with the very fabric of the place, a symphony of adaptations born of the same, singular source: the colossal creature whose bones form their home. Their existence is a gentle, unfolding secret, as complex and layered as the city itself.

The primary forms of intelligence appear to be… *things* that are integrated in the plant life. They are what direct the flow of sap, maintain the balance of flora, and perhaps *are* the communication network of the city. We see this influence in the patterns of growth, the intentional arrangement of the fungi's bioluminescence; and the precise, near-perfect geometries of the bone structures. They seem to exist in a hive mind, or perhaps a network of consciousness, with roots deep in the soil, flowing outward in the amber sap. Each individual may be a node, or a function, but the whole is the mind.

Within this framework, there are humanoid forms, creatures that walk the bone pathways like echoes of a forgotten time. They are tall and slender, their bodies subtly reflecting the ambient light; it’s as if their flesh has taken on the coloration and texture of the bone itself. They are not entirely solid; their forms ripple almost imperceptibly, making it difficult to discern their precise shape. Their eyes are large and luminous, lacking pupils, and always seeming to gaze inward. These Children of the Bone aren’t *blind*, necessarily—they perceive the world through a kind of internal resonance, a sense of the structures beneath the surface, and a sensitivity to the ebb and flow of the sap. They are the caretakers: they tend the interwoven vines, they encourage the growth of the luminous fungi. They *are* the keepers of the environment.

Then there are the creatures the Children of the Bone guide. They resemble earthly animals, yet are entirely of this world. The six-legged insects, for example, aren’t predators, but gatherers, harvesting the fallen, nutrient-rich leaves to fertilize the moss carpets. Their carapaces boast the same iridescent sheen as the sap vessels, and they move with impossible grace, their tiny legs clicking softly against the bone. The gemstone birds, too, have a purpose. They pollinate the flowering vines and spread the spores of the bioluminescent fungi. They are the eyes, ears, and hands of the community. They are creatures that move with an almost impossible smoothness, each motion perfectly aligned with the rhythm of the city.

The serpentine beings are the only exception: they are the guardians. Their skin shifts and shimmers, matching the light as they lie in wait: they are the silent sentinels, the protectors. Though they are silent, they may be the most intelligent; it is their task to patrol the city's borders, to ensure that the secrets are kept.

The essence of the Children of the Bone isn't about individual achievement or progress. It’s about the preservation of the whole. They aren’t warriors, artists, or scholars, at least not in any way we would understand. Their actions are driven by a deep, inherent connectedness to their environment, a fundamental understanding that they *are* the world. They are the current, the wind, and the bone.

The Children of the Bone are not necessarily immortal. Age seems to operate here differently. Life is a process of absorption, of becoming one with the bone and vegetation. Death does not mean an ending, but a return into the whole.

The nature of their consciousness is a mystery. They might not have the equivalent of a language. Their communication may occur on subtle levels, through the network of the sap, the arrangement of leaves, and the echoes of the city itself. They have no gods, no belief system in the traditional sense. Their faith is the city. Their purpose is simply being.

They are an organism, a society, a work of art, and a symphony with a very long time-signature.
