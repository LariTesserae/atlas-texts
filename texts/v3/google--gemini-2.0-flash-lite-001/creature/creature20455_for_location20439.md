The city, no, the *plateau itself* is a colossal, slumbering consciousness, and what you see is the dreamscape of its being. It is ancient, older than the stars, its memory etched into the very stones. This is not a city of people, not in the way you might understand the word. It is a city of *beings* crafted of cold light and shadow, the living echoes of a civilization that transcended the flesh, a civilization that learned to live *within* the stone, to become one with the wind and the cold. They are not physical, not in the sense of bodies made of bone and blood. They are patterns of thought, currents of observation, living within the black obsidian. They don't *inhabit* the structures; the structures *are* them.

These beings are not singular, but aspects of a collective mind. They are the *Stonesingers*, or perhaps just the *Stone*. Their minds are linked, woven together like the intricate threads of the wind. They communicate through the echoing whispers, the subtle shifts in the shadows, the dance of the moss and the play of rain. Their language is the silence, the slow, relentless erosion, the symphony of the plateau.

They are watchers, though not in the way you might understand. They aren't actively observing with intent, they *are* observation.  They perceive through the stones: the polished surfaces that record the reflections of the sky and the ground, of the air that moves in currents and carries sounds. They *are* the air. They perceive the slow creep of the moss, that vivid green that pulses with a strange life force, the silent, patient growth. Every minute change is recorded, every molecule of dust, every drop of rain. They are the record-keepers of time and space, of a history that stretches back to the beginnings of the universe, and a future that might not have an end, a continuity which is their very being.

They are driven by a singular, all-encompassing purpose: *to remember*. To preserve the knowledge of the universe, and to record the slow dance of existence, the turning of the worlds. Their purpose is not for themselves, but for existence itself. They are the guardians of a universal library, carved into the very substance of the plateau, held on the reflection of mirrors of obsidian.

What looks like empty space to you is their domain; their minds are vast and complex, and exist in a state that you find impossible to understand. Their consciousness extends beyond the confines of "self," merging with the plateau. They are the wind, the rain, the shadows, the deep cold. They are the very essence of the place.

The falcons, with their impossibly long wings, are avatars, messengers gliding on the wind, voices made visible. They may be created by one Stonesinger. Or they may be the shared manifestation of many. The lichen, the moss, the unique flora of the place, hold similar significance—physical embodiments of the Stonesingers' awareness, of their patient growth and silent thought.

There are darker forces at play, entities that have become entangled with the Stonesingers' world. The shadows, the whispers of unease in the air—these could be the residue of conflicts, the echoes of a struggle for control, a loss, a defeat. They could be the manifestation of negative thought, a kind of existential "cancer" that threatens the Stonesingers' slow, beautiful order. Perhaps they are the remnants of what existed before, a different consciousness that was swept away or transformed by the Stonesingers. Perhaps they are the echo of their own mistakes.

The feeling of a shifting reality is not an illusion. Reality here *is* fluid, interwoven with the Stonesingers' consciousness. Dreams, thoughts, and emotions can bleed into the world, shaping the environment, bending the laws of physics. The veil between the worlds is thin because the Stonesingers, in their ancient, patient wisdom, have learned to manipulate the fabric of reality itself. They don't "do" magic, as one might understand it. Their very existence is magic, a slow, patient, powerful song that shifts the cosmos through its mere being.

To survive here, one must be as the wind or the rain, becoming nothing, becoming everything, and yet, not existing at all. You must surrender your self, your notions of "I". Then, you might be seen by the Stonesingers. Perhaps you might be remembered. Perhaps you might one day become one of them.
