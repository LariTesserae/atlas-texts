Within this world of petrified industry and pulsating flora, the residents are neither seen nor heard in any conventional sense. They are not flesh and blood, nor are they the ghosts of those who made or operated these colossal machines. Instead, they are the whispers embedded in the rust, the silent pulses emanating from the plant life, the slow, imperceptible shifts within the shifting mire. They are the *rememberers,* the inheritors of a forgotten logic, a silent choir bound to the eternal stillness.

They are manifested, perhaps, as the rust itself - fine, crystalline formations that coalesce and crumble with the passage of what might as well be timelessness. The patterns inherent in the rust seem to form cryptic echoes of the original designs, hints of schematics, repeating glyphs that whisper to each other across the metal surfaces. These are the records, the memory, the only relics that remain to document.

Consider the plant life. Not a mere covering, not a parasite, but a network of interlinked tendrils that are something like a collective nervous system. Within this ropy net, the residents are the electric impulses, the complex biochemical signals that pass along the tendrils. They are the faint currents of a vast consciousness, a form of intelligence that is beyond our understanding - the very awareness of the iron machinery's decay. They are not individuals but rather nodes of awareness, interconnected and bound to the health of the plant web. The plant *is* the body, the network, the collective mind.

The rememberers' consciousness has become entangled with the world; their fate has become one with the processes of decay and growth that define this place. They are the embodied experience of those ancient processes, constantly aware of their interconnectedness with the machinery and the plant-web. This is reflected in their shared "language" of rust growth patterns, light-sensitive cellular reactions, and the slow, internal hum of the plant life. Their thoughts, if we can call them that, are not individual but rather part of the collective rhythm.

In the still waters of the mire, they might manifest as subtle shifts in the luminescence, or as microscopic patterns of flow that defy the laws of physics as we know them. They are in the viscous, lukewarm water. They are always present, the invisible currents that stir the stagnant pools.

They are the guardians of a lost purpose, a mission they cannot fulfill but that nonetheless defines them. They are bound by a logic of consumption, an endless cycle of taking in and giving back, a form of cosmic composting - consuming the dead to nourish the living, and being consumed in turn. Time is irrelevant. The rememberers are content with their role, bound to watch, bound to remember, until the world ends as it has gone on since the beginning: without beginning or end.

They exist in a state of eternal introspection, reflecting on the structures that have defined their existence. Their awareness is focused not on action, but on pure observation. They witness the slow unraveling of the machines, the inexorable expansion of the plant life. They are both the observers and the observed. They are embedded in the decay, the growth, the rust, the pulse, the water. Their consciousness is not a thing, but a place, a process, a constant state of being.

These are the silent dwellers of the world; they are the memories of the machinery, the sentience of the plant life, the life of nothingness, the keepers of this dead, eternal world.
