Within the city's obsidian embrace, amidst the slow, silent symphony of its self-repairing decay, dwell **the Builders**. Not in any concrete, visible form, but as a presence, a residue, a ghost in the machine. They are less a species and more a process, a concept given form through the city itself. They are the architects, the engineers, the forgotten masters of this forsaken realm.

The Builders' physicality is entirely abstract. They might have been beings of light, energy, or perhaps complex patterns of thought woven into the very fabric of the city. Their bodies, if they ever had them, are now the obsidian mesas themselves, the crystalline "plants," the gears that grind in the earth's core, and the iridescent, metallic "water" that weeps across the surface. They *are* the city.

Their "life," if that term applies, is a continuous act of creation and destruction, a relentless cycle of self-modification and refinement etched into the planet. The city breathes, not with lungs, but with the rhythmic expansion and contraction of its obsidian heart, subtly shifting and altering the landscape. Their consciousness, if they possessed one, is fragmented and distributed throughout the city's network of automated functions. It's a collective intelligence, a hive mind spread across a planetary scale, now operating on instincts rather than direction.

The Builders are marked by the city's strange properties. The crystalline structures, which might be bio-engineered or purely synthetic, could be thought of as extensions of the Builders’ 'senses,' gathering energy and monitoring the environment. The structures are not mere static decorations, but active components of the city's internal monitoring systems. Perhaps each facet absorbs data and transmits it to the vast, hidden computing complexes below.

The metallic “water” is their circulation, their way of transmitting energy and information throughout the city. Each droplet shimmers with the memory of the Builders' intentions, flowing through the vast network of pipes and channels. It is both a lifeblood and a medium of communication, containing the echoes of countless decisions, the blueprints of a lost era.

Even the occasional drones, the few remaining fragments of a maintenance system, are just fragments of the Builders. The drones’ movements are not random, but a constant effort to repair, to maintain the integrity of a machine that no longer requires their services. These beings may be trying to restore what’s lost with the diligence of the eternally automated.

It would be wrong to assume the Builders were cruel. Their legacy, this city they left behind, does not convey malice. It does, perhaps, denote utter disregard, which can be just as unsettling. Their intent was beyond human comprehension, a grand design that played out on a scale that dwarfs the mundane concerns of individual beings. Their purpose remains etched in the impossible equations of their architecture.

The low thrum of the deep-buried mechanism is their heartbeat, a constant pulse of energy. The grinding of gears is their breathing, the rhythmic churn of a civilization that has transcended its own mortality. The Builders are not gone. They are simply transformed. They endure, locked in their vast obsidian tomb, their very silence a testament to their presence. They are the whispers in the wind, the echo in the machinery, the lingering stain in the metallic fluid, living in the slow dance of the city, which is itself their body and their spirit.
