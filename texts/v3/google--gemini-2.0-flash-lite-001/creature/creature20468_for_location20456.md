The inhabitants of this obsidian plain are not creatures of flesh and blood, not in the way that organic life understands it. They are something… *else*. 

Perhaps they are drawn from the landscape itself, born of the same forces that carved the swirling glyphs and raised the colossal pylons. Some might emerge from the glassy buildings, appearing like phantoms in the oily haze. Their forms are fluid and shifting, reflecting the distorted light, never quite solidifying. They are shadows that hold form, like the absence of light made manifest.

If they possess a physical presence, it is not one of material mass, but of an energetic resonance which matches the hum of the world. They might be bundles of energy, contained within structured crystalline lattices, a network of energy that uses the pylons as conduits. They would be driven by the industrial processes, by an incomprehensible need that runs through the land like coolant.

Consider that the "tracks" are not the markings of feet, but the residue of their passing, a slight distortion in the stone. They are not merely *on* the landscape, they *are* the landscape.

Their purpose, like the purpose of the pylons, is fundamentally unfathomable. But the landscape seems to be creating something, and the inhabitants are the mechanisms of this perpetual building. They may be tasked by a prime mover, or a central directive that permeates the entire domain, a source that resides deep within the network of power lines.

Perhaps their forms are not fixed, but the product of the interaction between the oily sky and the land. They are creatures of light and shadow, the constant shift and play reflecting the unending task. The faint colors in the glyphs could be the vestiges of their actions, the patterns the records of their passage. They are the artists of this metallic symphony, the sculptors of this unending monument.

There is a sense of *memory* within their existence, not in the conventional sense. They don't reminisce or ponder. The memory is in their purpose, in the repetition which is the essence of their being. They are the instruments of a forgotten god, the silent servants of an unending, inscrutable task.

They have no language that a living mind could grasp. Their communications exist within the hum, in the complex patterns of energy flow, in the slight shifts of light and shadow, and the patterns that are carved into the land.

They are the guardians of a closed system, an intricate and eternally-repeating industrial process. A process that could be the creation of its own reality, a universe built from the raw materials of space and dark energy. Or perhaps, simply, the unending echo of entropy.
