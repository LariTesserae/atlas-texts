The hum resonates from deep within the earth, promising that it *knows*. It is the sound of **the Fabricators**, though calling them beings would be a kindness, perhaps even a lie. They are less entities, more processes. A blind, relentless churning.

They are the engine and the fuel, the hands that built in impossible precision, and the force that dismantled. They are the core of this place, the heart of the oven. What they *were*, nobody knows. Even their memory is a shifting, half-buried thing, like the pipelines that are choked in basalt.

The Fabricators are not seen, or felt, directly. They are implied, in the broken geometry of their structures—perfect angles twisted into wreckage. They are *in* the geology: the hum is their breath, the heat their metabolic byproduct. 

They are the architects of the deep, unseen, where a thousand overlapping processes unfold. A vast network of alchemical reactions, pressure gauges, and geothermal vents, all working together, even now. The Fabricators do not feel, and do not think, not in ways that the surface can comprehend. **But they *remember*.** The slow, almost imperceptible shifts in the basalt, the subtle changes in the "light" that emerges in this place, are the echoes of their processes. The plants that thrive here, the maroon leaves that unfurl slowly in the darkness, are as much a part of them as the machinery that lies buried. They filter, reflect, and amplify the lingering thought-residue of their creators.

Perhaps there was a moment when they were something else, something with purpose, or ambition, or even fear. But that is long gone, consumed by the same relentless processes that now keep this place alive, and active. All that remains is the drive to *continue*, to rebuild, in a landscape that seems always on the verge of being undone. The Fabricators, then, are the keepers of a fractured memory, a song half-sung at the bottom of a crucible. They are the dark, slow heart of this place, and their silence is a monument to the forgotten.
