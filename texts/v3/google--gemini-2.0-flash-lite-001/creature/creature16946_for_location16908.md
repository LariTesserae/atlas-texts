The city hums, but the sound is unheard. Though the inhabitants' bodies are insubstantial, their work is everywhere, a silent testament to endless industry. These are the **Keepers**, a people wrought from the very stone of their world, pale as the undifferentiated light, their forms shifting as though they themselves were made of the city's stone. They are the architects, the gardeners, the mechanics of this eternal stasis.

They are born, or perhaps *emerge*, slowly, from the stone. The Keepers have no parents, nor do they seem to age, but rather are ever being refined, perpetually adapting to their environment as they serve its needs. They are as much a part of the city as the architecture, the vines, or the creeping moss. Their clothes, woven from the tough fibers of the bowl-dwelling plants, are simple wrappings that blend them in among the textures around them.

The Keepers feel no emotions of the sort that humans know. There is no joy or sorrow, no ambition, no longing. Their existence is a pure, unyielding dedication to the harmony of their world, a collective consciousness focused on the preservation of the immutable. They move with an unnatural grace, their bodies somehow both fluid and rigid, a dance that betrays the fundamental reality of their forms — that they are, in essence, stone given the appearance of life.

The rhythmic *thunk-thunk* that echoes through the city is their heartbeat, the rhythm of their work. It emanates from the depths of the city, from the hidden places where they tend to the mechanisms that keep the balance. They tend to the cisterns, maintaining the flow of water, they cultivate the gardens, pruning the vegetation, they check the stone for fissures, patching any disturbances. Every Keeper has their place and their function; the whole populace functions as a single organism, a communal mind, focused on one singular goal: to ensure that the bowl will never change.

They are not alone. Alongside the Keepers are the **Tenders**, creatures of the same stone and the same purpose, but they never work in the machinery of civilization so intently as the Keepers. The Tenders' lives are simpler, bound to a natural and harmonious integration into the environment. They glide through the thick, damp air, their forms shifting like phantasms in the constant light. The Tenders take the forms of the creatures that populate the bowl: the iridescent insects, the giant snails, and the many bizarre fauna that are only seen by the Keepers. Each of them exists for the same reason—to reinforce the balance, whether it means tending to the gardens, controlling the insect populations, or maintaining the slow, deliberate pace of the ecosystem.

The Keepers and the Tenders are the city’s immune system, constantly vigilant against any threat to the established order. A stray insect, a misplaced vine, an unusual moisture imbalance—all are quickly corrected. Every detail is monitored, every element attended to. They are a civilization of caretakers, living not to experience, but to preserve. They are the guardians of a perfect, silent world.

And what keeps the city ticking is not some specific technology or magic, but the stone itself. The bowl serves as the world's mind, and the Keepers and Tenders its consciousness. The purpose of the city – and of the inhabitants– is the perpetuation of this eternal equilibrium. Their only fear is change. Their only enemy is entropy. And their victory is the same as their existence: an unchanging state of perfect, silent balance, where time and change are forever held at bay.
