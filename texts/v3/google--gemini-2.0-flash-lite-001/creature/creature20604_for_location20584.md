The inhabitants of Cinderwallow are not born, but *emerge*. They coalesce from the very fabric of the city, from the interplay of metal, water, and fungal bloom that defines its essence. They are, in a sense, the city's self-awareness brought to life, an extension of its slow, relentless industry, a living echo of the colossal structure at its heart.

They begin as intricate lattices of metallic filaments, spun from the rust-red and oily-green weeping stone. These lattices are fragile at first, shimmering in the humid, twilight air, easily disrupted by the slightest touch. The spores, their blueprints echoing in their veins, are the catalysts. They settle upon the nascent forms, knitting the metal with the fungal threads, infusing them with a bioluminescent glow, the color of moonlight on water.

As they grow, they take on a form best described as *tectonic*. They are architectures of the self, composed of layered, intercrossing girders, mirroring the design of their homes. They are not flesh and blood, but a fusion of metal, stone, and the strange, glowing fungi of their environment. Their bodies are porous, allowing the water-rich air to pass through them, sustaining them in a way that suggests a symbiotic relationship with the city itself. They are, essentially, living structures.

Their "faces," if one can call them that, are perhaps the most fluid and enigmatic aspect of their forms. They do not have fixed eyes or mouths. Instead, they possess shimmering, shifting mosaics of bioluminescent fungi, capable of displaying a range of expressions, from the gentle glow of contentment to the sharper, more intense flares of excitement or alarm. Their "voices" are composed of the susurrus of wind through the metal leaves, augmented by the resonance of the city itself, the low hum amplified by their porous forms. They communicate through the interplay of light, sound, and the subtle release of spores, creating complex, living tapestries of meaning.

Their lives are aligned with the rhythms of Cinderwallow. They maintain the city, not through conscious effort, but by being *part* of its self-sustaining structure. They repair the walkways, tend the fungal gardens, and regulate the flow of water, all as a part of their existence. Their society is a collective consciousness, a hive mind echoing the city's industry. There is no hierarchy, no conflict, only a shared purpose: to endure, to evolve, and to become ever more intricately intertwined with the life of Cinderwallow.

They venture out into the world beyond of the city, though not in the way one might think. The outermost of their forms can sometimes find their way into the perpetual fog that surrounds Cinderwallow. They travel through it as a kind of dream made real, a waking sleep that only allows access to the periphery. The fog itself seems to be infused with the same slow industrial quality of the city, a place of slow, inevitable transformations that can't be escaped.

Their emotions, while alien, are nonetheless recognizable. A sense of wonder and curiosity resonates within their fungal mosaics when facing a new growth pattern, when a fresh variety of spore blooms within their internal bodies. They experience a profound sense of *calm* in the constant hum emanating from the heart of the city, the reassuring pulse of their lifeblood. They mourn the dissolution of their numbers, a slow fading of the bioluminescent bloom, their forms returning slowly to the basic elements of the city.

Ultimately, they are a mystery, an enigma crafted by the city itself. They are the living embodiment of Cinderwallow, each individual a walking, breathing testament to the city's slow, organic industry, endlessly transforming, an enduring reminder of a forgotten age of industrial weirdness.
