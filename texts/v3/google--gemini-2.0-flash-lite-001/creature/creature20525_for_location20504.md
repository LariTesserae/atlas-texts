The Weeping City breathes in a rhythm of stone and water, a slow, mournful exhalation that shapes the lives – the *beings* – that dwell within its shadowed embrace. They are the **Tearkin**, and they are as integrated with the city as the glistening moss that clings to the bruised purple stone.

The Tearkin are not one thing. They are a mosaic, a collection of forms constantly in flux, reflecting the city's own perpetual metamorphosis. Some are humanoid, but not in any way familiar to the common traveler. Their bodies are built from a living stone-flesh, a composite of the city's purple rock itself, interwoven with the silver-grey fungal material. They are constructed, yet born; sculpted from the environment, and perhaps, *by* it. Their forms flow and shift, sometimes taking on rough, angular shapes mimicking the canyons, other times becoming sinuous, almost liquid, as if drawn from the streams that weave through the city. 

Their skin, if it can be called that, is a mottled tapestry of colors. Deep purples, the color of the bedrock, are laced with veins of the luminescent grey, the fungal material flowing like silver rivers across their bodies. These veins pulse gently with a light of their own, echoing the internal glow of the city's strange flora. The veins seem also to provide a medium for moving. Moving in relation to the city and the environment, allowing them to shift and flow as needed, mirroring the city's own constant transformation.

They are tall, slender, and with a great density of strength. They move with an unnatural grace, silently, their limbs seeming to glide rather than walk. Some possess multiple, almost insectoid limbs, enabling them to traverse the narrow passages with ease. Others are more like living sculptures, their forms less defined, their faces obscured, with no other recognizable features. These beings seem to be permanently intertwined with the structures themselves.

Their faces, where they exist, are rarely distinct. They possess smooth, featureless features, almost like polished stone, with indentations for eyes that can open and close. The features that seem most expressive, are the patterns of glowing, silver veins – the subtle shifts in illumination betraying a shift in mood. 

They do not speak through conventional means. Instead, they communicate through a form of telepathy, a sharing of thoughts and emotions transmitted through the very air, resonating in the city's damp atmosphere. The city itself seems to act as a receiver and amplifier of these thoughts, so any communication, echoes with an ambient quality, a murmur of stone and water that seems to emanate from the walls themselves.

The Tearkin are attuned to the city's every pulse. They are not merely inhabitants; they are extensions of the Weeping City. They are aware of every shift in the stone, every drip of water, every growth of fungus. They react instinctively to the changes in pressure, aware of the rhythms of the city and responding with their own internal cycles. It's a dance between them and the environment.

Their purpose, if it can be called that, is not clear, but it appears to be one of preservation, and possibly, expansion. They tend to the city, fostering its growth, maintaining its intricate network of life. They may even guide the processes of erosion, subtly shaping building stone to suit their needs.

They are not without conflict. The Tearkin aren't a united whole. The "factions" (divisions?) reflect the city’s complex nature. Some focus on preservation, maintaining the city's current form. Others embrace innovation, exploring new forms and materials, guiding development in the fungal metal. Occasionally there are conflicts between these "tendencies," resulting in the careful dismantling and reshaping of structures, or the temporary isolation of groups determined to be a threat to the harmony.

They feed, but not in the conventional sense. They absorb sustenance from the city itself, drawing energy from the streams, the fungal growths, and the minerals within the stone. They are capable of withdrawing energy. The mosses and fungi might be a food source, but this too is unclear. 

The Tearkin have some form of life cycle. Some are ‘born’ from the stone, slowly emerging from the bedrock as smaller versions of the adult forms. Others are grown, sculpted from the fungal metal and infused with the city's essence. Their "deaths" are not clearly observed, but it is clear that they eventually become part of the city again, their substance reabsorbed into the stone and re-purposed.

They understand the slow movements of the city. They create, they maintain, they adapt to the cycles. But despite this, they are wary. The occasional, shadowy fleeting figures that are sometimes seen, are the youngest or the smallest, the scouts and the sentinels. They are ever-prepared, ever adaptable. If outsiders approach, they may be watched from the shadows, or their presence communicated in the slow, liquid pulses of shared thought.

The Tearkin are a mystery wrapped in a riddle, a testament to the Weeping City's strange, alien beauty. To see them move is to behold the city's soul reflected in the dim light. They are the tears of the stone, made flesh, forever bound to their sorrowful, yet beautiful home.
