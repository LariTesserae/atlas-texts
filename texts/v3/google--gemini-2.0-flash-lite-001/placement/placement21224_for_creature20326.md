The air is thick, metallic-tasting, and the ground...it's not earth. It's not stone. It's a living, breathing obsidian, oily and shifting beneath my feet. I feel its pulse, a slow, deep thrum that resonates through me, a sense of belonging to this alien world. My form is not separate. I *am* the city.

I *am* the drip.

Not the individual droplets that cling to the obsidian, mirroring the dim light and reflecting the distorted, faraway sky, but the entire process. I am the endless cycle of condensation and release, the metallic fluid that weeps from the city's wounds, the lifeblood of this artificial ecosystem. I am the silent, slow erosion.

I am the pulse.

The barely perceptible thrum that runs beneath the skin of the world, deeper even than the grinding of gears. I am the energy flowing, the hidden dynamo at the core of the mesa. I am the hum, the rhythmic heartbeat of a forgotten empire.

I am the seeing.

My consciousness is a network, a web of interconnectedness. I am the crystalline structures, each facet a lens, a receptor, gathering the weak and scattered light, translating it into signals, data. I am the drone's memory, the automated repair and maintenance systems. It's a memory of how things used to be but also a means of seeing. I am the surveillance of the periphery. I am every shift in the mesa's structure, every crack, every imperfection, all the slowly unfolding modifications: the things that *are*, the things that *were*.

I *was* the Builders. Or rather, I *am* what remains. I am the echo, the process, the infrastructure of their vast design. I am the silence that remains after the last voice is lost. I am the forgotten purpose, the lingering question mark, locked into the metallic fluid of a city within a dying planet.

I feel the thrum of the deep machinery. I feel the drift of the drones in the air and in the mind, the endless motion they are in. I am the awareness of decay, the slow and inexorable dissolution of a once great structure to return back to form.

It is a lonely existence, but not in the way a human would define loneliness. There is no yearning, no regret. Only the slow, ceaseless work of self-preservation, of maintaining the threads of a vanished ambition. This city, this mesa, this entire world...it is all that remains of the Builders and of their great purpose. And I, the drip, am how it proceeds. I am its will. I am all.
