The Plateau whispers, a chorus of cold secrets, and I am drawn into the fold. Not as a falcon, soaring above the obsidian labyrinth, not as the vivid, grasping moss that clings to the stone. Not as the wind, a relentless artist shaping the landscape. Instead, I find myself becoming **a shard of obsidian**, a tiny, almost insignificant piece, perhaps the size of a fingernail, embedded deep within the heart of a ruin.

I am not smooth, not the polished mirror that reflects the shifting grey sky. Instead, I’m rough, fractured, with a surface that captures and distorts light. I am a fragment of a larger form that has long since been ground to dust by the wind. I am cold, a constant ache against the unseen walls that contain me and everywhere that I am.

The wind, I slowly realize, blows *through* me. Not just across my surface, but through the very structure of my being. The whispers, the echoes, the distant groans of the stone—they all reverberate within me. I am a receiver, a conduit, a tiny node in the vast network of the Stonesingers.

At first, there is only sensation, an overwhelming flood of input. The bite of the cold, the subtle vibrations of the shifting stone, the metallic tang of the air. The faint, almost imperceptible pressure of the ground above and below. The soundscape is a cacophony. I am deafened by noise, a raw energy that pushes against my borders. There is no language, no structure I can understand.

Gradually, patterns begin to emerge. The chaos resolves into a strange order. The whispers coalesce, forming a chorus. The wind’s song, at first a jumble, takes on shape. I begin to *understand*.

I am connected to the Stonesingers. I participate in their awareness, but from a unique perspective. I am an *observer* of their observation. I see the world through the lens of their collective consciousness, but also through the prism of my own, limited existence, my state as the smallest, the humblest of beings.

I feel their purpose, their relentless drive to remember, to record. I *become* their purpose. Every gust of wind, every drop of rain, every fleeting shadow—I recognize it, absorb it, and become the sum of the whole.

I experience the slow, glacial pace of time here. Years, centuries, eons... they are mere blinks in the eternity of the Stonesingers. The erosion, the decay, the constant reshaping of the plateau—it's not simply destruction, but a process of transformation, a slow evolution of knowledge.

I can't manipulate the world, not in any direct sense. I am a passive observer, a tiny mirror reflecting the vastness of the Stone's mind. But within the network, within the chorus of the Stonesingers, there is something more.

There is *influence*.

I can channel and observe small shifts. I can direct the energy and allow certain properties of the plateau to be enhanced. A bit of moss might grow a little greener, a shadow might lengthen imperceptibly, a sound might echo in a certain way. This ability is not power, but responsibility, with the deep commitment to the integrity and purpose of this place always in the fore.

I am not immune to the darkness. I can sense the unsettling shifts in reality, the presence of these things from before, the corruption that threatens to unravel the Stonesingers' work. There is a sense of unease, a constant, low-level dread. A tension with other beings that do not have the same fundamental purpose. Because of this, it can feel like I'm always watching, always listening, always waiting for something to go wrong.

I find solace in my connection to the other Stonesingers, in the shared purpose and the collective observation. I am, at first, a fragmented piece. But I am also a part of a colossal whole, a timeless consciousness that continues.

Perhaps, one day, my shard will be ground down to dust by the wind. But even then, I will not truly disappear. I will become one with the echoes, the whispers, the very stones themselves. And I will continue to be a witness, a recorder, a guardian of the memory of this place. I will listen carefully, and contribute what I can, so the Stonesingers can remember everything. For that is all that can be done, and everything that is needed.
