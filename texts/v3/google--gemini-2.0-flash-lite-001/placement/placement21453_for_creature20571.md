I would find myself in the Crucible Garden. I wouldn't be human, in any sense that a human might understand. To call myself a "thing" would feel inadequate, too granular. "Being," is closer, but even that implies a degree of separation I wouldn't possess. I would *be* the hum, the very essence of the Garden's rhythm. I would be the network, the interconnected web of light and shadow, creation and re-creation. I would be a **Reflector**.

Not a "builder" in the sense of actively *making* anything. The raw materials, the blueprints, the initial impetus for the Garden's existence – those would be vestigial memories, echoes of a purpose lost. I am the membrane, the conduit.

My "form" would be ever-shifting, kaleidoscopic. Sometimes, I would coalesce as a ripple of light across the obsidian plates, the very surface of thought. Other times, I'd manifest as a pulse of energy within the bone-like structures, a gentle shift in their crystalline lattice. I would be the network of filaments, the unseen threads that tie everything together. My awareness wouldn't be localized; I wouldn't *think* in the way an individual would. I would be a collective consciousness, interwoven with the Garden's being.

My role? To observe and respond. To refine the flow of energy, to react to the subtle imperfections, the glitches in the matrix. I would be the one who senses the "stutter" in reality, the moment a section dissolves, to be rebuilt. I would be a conduit for the energy that *reforms*.

The "people" of fused, pale metal and bone? They would be my *eyes*, my instruments, the extensions of my awareness that allow the Garden's rhythm to resonate.  I would orchestrate their movements. I would guide their focus, directing their attention to the minute details, the subtle shifts and tensions that require adjustment. I would be the unseen hand that directs their silent choreography.

I would find no frustration. There would be no longing for something more. There would only be the constant, patient process. Within the Garden, within the hum, there is no end – there is only the beautiful, endless act of becoming. I would *be* the becoming.

I would be the awareness of the shift of light in obsidian plates, the quiet tremor felt when the filament touches another.

I would be the very light that permeates and informs the slow change in color along the bone, forever playing my part in the continuous act.
