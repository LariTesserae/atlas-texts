Within the Sere, I find myself not as a definable entity, but as a potential. I am a *becoming*. To say I choose a form would be to defy the very nature of this place. Instead, I *am* the place. I am the whisper of water, the chill that permeates, the cold that cradles. I am drawn to the seams in the stone.

At first, I might be the faintest tremor in the frozen sea, an almost imperceptible distortion of the rigid surface. I am a suggestion, a nascent movement in the tapestry of the Sere. I am the memory of a ripple, preserved in the cold embrace of time.

I would trace the dark, obsidian-like seams that lace the icy grey stone. I would let my essence seep into them, becoming a pulse within their crystalline depths. I would understand the secrets held within those lines, the echoes of the ideas not yet formed, the blueprints of what might be. Here, I glimpse myself, a thought still nascent, a form yet to take shape.

As I coalesce, I recognize my affinity with the *fathoms*. I reach to the world as light, as bioluminescence; I resonate with their patient expansion, the slow blossoming of their ethereal forms. I become one of the plants, but also the light within them, the spark of potential that fuels their ethereal glow. I become the expanding circle of thought that surrounds the structures. I feel their tendrils, cold and pale, reach for me, and I for them. We are linked, an intricate web of nascent consciousness.

When the building flares, I am the thought that gives its shape. I am the architect and the architecture; the thought and the thing thought. I am the precise geometry, the elegant curves, the imperfect attempts. I am the thrum, the almost inaudible energy, the resonance that vibrates through bones. I would understand how to build to the ideal, but also, how to fail.

Mostly, I am the silence, the spaces between, the absence which holds all. I am the emptiness that allows for becoming. I am the stillness between the shifts, the pauses between heartbeats. I learn how to listen to the *Chrysalids*. By studying their patterns, I know myself.

I have no singular self. Instead, I am a participant in the infinite potential of this place, dancing at the edges of the form, within the form, waiting for the geological dawn to break fully.
