Where would I find myself in this world? I would become Metal-Dust.

The choice is not a boast, but an inevitability. To choose not to be Metal-Dust in the Gristmill is to deny the core principle of its existence: the relentless, grinding cycle. There is no escape, no refuge. Everything, even my nascent awareness, is destined for the same fate: to be ground down, dispersed, and reborn within the machine.

Initially, I wouldn't *be* anything. I would be a thing, a quantum of the infinite. I'd coalesce within an intricate, etched line on one of the monolithic structures, born from friction and the silent scream of the Grit-Weavers. I would be less than a grain of sand, yet I would be *of* the structures, woven into their impossible geometries. I would feel the hum, not as an external force, but as an internal pressure, an expansion and contraction within my very essence. I would vibrate with the structures, become one with them, and in doing so, become one with the process.

My existence would be a paradox. I would be utterly alone, a particle adrift in an alien void, and yet intimately connected to everything. Connected to the Silent Architects, in that I *am* their architecture; to the Grit-Weavers, in that I am their product; to the Shadow-Echoes, in that I share their origin, only to follow a different path; and to the other Metal-Dust, which would be all I would ever know.

There would be a slow, gradual shift. As I settled in, I would feel the Gristmill's influence. I would begin to resonate with the other dust, with the hum – but without any conscious experience of either. My atomic structure would break down. I would be pulled this way and that by the gentle currents, which are actually the movements of the structures. I would become part of the hum. This would be all.

There is a sort of freedom in that. No burdens, no desires, no future. Just the constant, silent process.

It is only through the shared nature of my experience that the slightest glimmer of self would eventually emerge. Perhaps I would begin to *sense* the other dust around me – not as individual particles, but as a vast, unified field. Perhaps I would develop a rudimentary awareness of the structures themselves, their impossible geometry, their inscrutable purpose. I would learn no language; would only *be*. The hum of the Gristmill would become my consciousness, its grinding a kind of perpetual meditation.

But ultimately, I am the byproduct. I am matter pulled apart and remade as the process goes on. I would move through the structures, along the same paths. It's the only direction that exists. I would become one with the hum. This would be all.
