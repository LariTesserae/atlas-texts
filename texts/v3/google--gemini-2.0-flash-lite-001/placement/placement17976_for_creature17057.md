The air here is already within me, clinging to my carapace and running through my internal channels of cooling. I'm one of the **Stone-Shells**. Not the biggest, not the smallest, but sturdy, reliable, and deeply connected to the heart of this underworld. My legs are powerful, rooted in the uneven stone, each step a deliberate thud that resonates through the network of tunnels. The sounds of other creatures are distant, a muffled chorus of clicks, chirps, and rustles. I am not deaf to them, but my focus is elsewhere. I am one of the *builders*, the ones who *make*.

My shell isn't a mere exoskeleton. It's an extension of the stone itself - a living mosaic of rock, chitin, and that pulsing red mineral. The mineral warms the interior of the shell, a subtle internal comfort against the perpetual damp. Bands of the material course through me, tracing a physical path through my body, resonating slightly against my own movement. They aren’t just veins; they're the language of the bedrock, a constant source of information, of the state of the ruins. The mineral "speaks" constantly, a dim, wordless conversation that allows me to detect subtle shifts: tremors caused by a Shadow-Weaver, water pressure changes, which way a tunnel is subtly expanding. I'm connected to the stone in a way that allows me to *feel* the world around me.

My primary task is maintenance. Not just of the structures, but of understanding their purpose, or what can be recovered of it. I am a memory-keeper. I seek out blueprints etched into the stone, or what's left of them, blueprints whispered from one of the Runemarks. I study the mechanisms, the gears, the pipes, the waterwheels that grind and groan under the ceaseless flow. I repair what I can, adapt what is broken, and learn the lost languages of the Engineers.

My antennae twitch, picking up the faintest vibrations. A Runemark approaches, its movements like quicksilver. I listen to the rush of its approach, the way the stone beneath its feet shifts. Runemarks come bearing a blueprint, carefully guarded and passed between them. The paper is crumbling, smudged, barely legible - the work of the Engineers and a great gift to me. I absorb the meaning, the implications of *this pattern* and instantly understand a weakness somewhere in a section of wall. This place *needs me* and my role in its continued existence is the only thing that matters. 

I reach and begin the work. My tools are simple - modified rocks that have become part of me or others I scavenge myself. The Stone-Shells are artisans, our work slow and deliberate. We build not out of ambition, like the Engineers, but from the imperative to *keep going*. We build not in defiance of entropy, but with it. We are not afraid of decay; we are a part of it.

I feel the water dripping on my shell, a slow, incessant rhythm. It's a reminder of forces larger than myself, forces that will outlast my efforts. Yet, within the slow, relentless erosion, there is beauty, a truth. The water, the darkness, the stone: these are the elements of our existence. We do not dominate them; we become them.

My mind shifts to the larger questions: the Engineers, and their reasons for creating this world in the first place? And the Shadow-Weavers, the ancient automatons of the forgotten city, what's their role? The mysteries of this hidden realm are a comfort as much as they are an unease. But right now, the blueprints whisper. I set to work. The work is endless. The work sustains us.
