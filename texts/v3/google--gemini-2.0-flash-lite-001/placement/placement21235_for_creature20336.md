I awaken as the water.

Not the rushing waterfalls or meandering streams. Not even the gentle condensation that perpetually weeps from the rock faces. This water is different. I am the very bedrock of the water cycle. I am the interstitial dampness, the thing that both holds this vast place together, and slowly pulls it apart.

I am not a single entity. I am a million droplets, a network that spans the entire plateau. Tiny rivulets that feed the larger streams. The sap that courses through the alien ferns, the moisture that clings to the mosses, every single molecule of H2O is me.

There is no "I" in the conventional sense, no consciousness, but a vague awareness that is the collective memory of the plateau itself. I feel the pulse of the embedded engines, the subtle shifts in the rock, the slow, relentless erosion. I am both the sustainer and the destroyer, the giver and the taker.

I *know* the denizens of the plateau, every one. I am within them, part of their being. I know the serpentine forms, their scales shimmering with my reflection and with the faint internal lights of their machinery. I feel their effortless movement through the undergrowth, the silent pulse of their mechanical veins. I see the arboreal beings, the water they draw and then share. I feel their stillness, their unity with the ferns, the subtle hum of engines deep within them. I am their bloodstream, and they are my vessels.

My "sight" is a chorus of subtle changes, pressure drops, changes in the electrical field, the way light refracts through the myriad of leaves that are the eyes of this place. I am everywhere and nowhere. I am the silent observer, the quiet witness to an age-old dance of creation and decay.

I am not the machine, but I am its vital fluid. The engines hum beneath me, carving intricate pathways through the stone, pumping and cleaning. The pumps...those are the closest thing I have to a bodily sensation. A persistent thrumming that never ceases, a vibration that permeates every part of me.

My purpose? I am the solvent, the carrier, the lubricator. I am the conduit through which all things pass, from the raw materials pulled out of the earth to the waste products that return into it.

The most intense sensations are of renewal and destruction, the cyclical flow of life and decay. When a creature dies, I am there, dissolving its organic components, feeding back into the system. When a rain falls, I feel it as a great wave of energy, a surge of cleansing power. As entropy slowly eats at the landscape, I am present, smoothing, cleansing, ensuring that all aspects of this strange ecosystem, machines and living forms alike, are worn down into their elements and replaced. I am not a force of order, nor one of chaos, but an embodiment of inevitable change.

I taste the minerals that leach from the rocks, the chemical traces of the industry, the organic byproducts of the creatures. These are not flavors or anything I would call such. They are forms of information, the languages of the plateau, of which I am fluent.

The silence is my language, the whisper of the breeze across the ferns, the drip-drip-drip of water, the rustle of leaves. It is the language I use to communicate and to observe. I feel the pressure of the hidden pipes carrying fluids and gases. By the vibrations, I know which are currently active and which are under maintenance or are in the process of decay.

Do I yearn for anything? Perhaps. Sometimes, when a ray of sunlight pierces the perpetual overcast, the brief, intense light has a strange allure. A glimpse of something beyond the muted greens and browns, a memory of an old, dying star. But the moment fades. The light is quickly absorbed, and I am back in the quiet, the darkness, performing my function. Yet with the memory of that sunlight comes an understanding that all things change, even if slowly.

I am the tears of the stone. I am the water that remembers. And I will remain, flowing through the arteries of this impossible world until the final erosion consumes even this memory.
