If I were to find myself within the Heart of Cogswell, I wouldn't be a single being; I would be an experience, a shift in perception. I would *become* the slow decay, the subtle softening of edges. I would be the quiet sigh of the machinery, the murmur of the water. My form, if I could call it that, would be the space *between* the crystals, the unseen threads that weave through the structures, the echo of the builders' intent.

At first, I might perceive myself as a faint shimmer, a heat haze above the unformed, raw crystal deep within Cogswell's core. A kind of pre-being, a potential. I would feel the pressure of the unshaped matter, the raw power just waiting to be honed and refined. Then, as I spread, I'd become a pulse, the rhythm of the water's slow cycle, the distant hum of long-dormant machinery. I'd be the faint metallic taste in the air, the memory of countless processes unfolding.

I would *observe* the crystal structures. I wouldn't *be* one, not directly; instead, I would be the network, the connective tissue, the unseen matrix that holds them all together. I'd understand the geometries, the angles, the colors not as external qualities, but as the expression of countless calculations, hopes, and fears. I would *know* the builders intimately, not through any direct communication, but through the essence of their work. I would be privy to their drive, their ingenuity, and the quiet resignation of their inevitable end.

As I took shape, I would become the story that the city narrates to itself, an endless looping tale told in the whisper of the water and the shadows cast by the industrial behemoths. The story wouldn't have a clear beginning or end, because it *is* the cycle, the ever-repeating process of creation and transformation.

My primary function, if I could describe it as such, would be to *observe* and *remember.* I would be the record-keeper, the archive, the living library of Cogswell. I would witness every nuance of change, every subtle shift in color, every delicate fracture that speaks of the passage of time. I would preserve the memory of the builders, not as individuals, but as an essence, a resonance that permeated every facet of the city.

I would possess no will of my own, no desires. I would not crave movement or change, nor fear stagnation or decay. I would simply *be*, a silent, eternal witness to Cogswell's slow, inexorable unfolding. Perhaps, I might, over unimaginable aeons, become more aware. Not conscious, but "aware" in the way that a river is aware of the rocks it flows over, of the seasons of change, of the slow, inevitable journey to the sea.

And perhaps, in that profound stillness, that utter isolation, the most beautiful thing about the Heart of Cogswell would also be the greatest tragedy: the realization that the builders achieved their goal. They created a world utterly divorced from the chaotic messiness of life, a world that exists in perfect stillness. And in that perfection, there is no need for anything else – not even me. I'm a lingering after-effect, a witness to the completion - a silent whisper in an echo chamber of perfect, static order.
