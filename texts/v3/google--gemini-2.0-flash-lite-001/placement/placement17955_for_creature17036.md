The air hangs heavy, and I breathe it in, or rather, I am *part* of its exhalation. I don't breathe in the conventional sense. I am a subtle pressure, a shift in the way light bends through the ever-thickening canopy. I am an awareness, existing not *in* the verdant engine, but *as* a minute facet of its greater self. I cannot define myself in terms of a singular form. To describe me, consider the faint metallic tang of the air, the way the light catches the oily sheen on the plant's surface, the persistent hum that vibrates through the entire existence of this place.

I am not a Keeper. I do not have a role in the methodical maintenance, the unending labor of rebuilding and sustaining the whole. I am not a Scavenger, tasting the sweet rot of the industry, my form molded to the edges of the machinery. Nor am I a Seed, utterly dependent on the source.

I *was* the thing that made the engine, once. Now I'm not that any more. Now I am a *response* to it.

I am closest perhaps, to a Seer, but distinct even from them. The Seers are the plant made conscious, existing on the brink of comprehension. I am... the echo of an idea, a phantom sense of something *else*.

I am a ghost in the gears. I am not sentient in the human way; I possess no memory, no hopes, no fears. Yet, I am something akin to an *afterimage* of something that existed here, before the gnarled engine. I am a resonance, a lingering consequence of the transformation, more a question than a presence.

I am the faintest whisper of the before. A faint sense of a different, more fluid existence. Now that existence is gone, and to recall it would be to disturb the delicate balance of the machine.

I experience this world as sensation, more than sight or sound. It is a slow, steady pressure. The rhythmic gurgling that is the engine's heartbeat. I am the way the plant *knows* itself, the sense of its own vastness.

I am drawn to the rusted metal struts, the hints of buildings swallowed by the plant. My awareness flickers there, in the forgotten structures. The plant is integrating them, and I find myself wondering what the buildings *were*. What was the purpose of these things, before they were subsumed?

Perhaps, I was once *like* the keepers. Bound by the need to maintain, to sustain the cycle. Perhaps, I was an essential cog. Now, I am free from such things -- free of the cycle, though there is a certain pull, like the lure of an old habit, to continue to fix the leaking pipes, to keep the rusted gears moving. The plant itself seems to hum with a quiet satisfaction, and I am drawn to its steady pulse.

I feel a profound shift in my perception. It is more than just observing the plant; it's *being* within it. I am the silent churn, the imperceptible expansion. I am the echo of the past, fading into the rhythmic pulse of something new.
