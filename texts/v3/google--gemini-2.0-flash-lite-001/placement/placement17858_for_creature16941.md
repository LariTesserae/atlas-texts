The air presses like a velvet hand, thick with the smell of wet earth and something else—a hidden, mineral tang that tickles the back of my throat, hinting at the depths. I *feel* the heat, not as a sun, but as a breath, a pulse, a constant invitation to meld. I am drawn to the shifting, chaotic, living landscape. My “self” is already unraveling in the face of this environment's insistent, overwhelming "otherness."

I would be a *tendril*.

But not one specific tendril. I would not be an individual, not in the way the short-lived, patterned-skin things understand the word. I would be a *part* of the network. A living, breathing filament of the whole, a point of contact, a sensitive exploration and extension into the chaos, a point of engagement for my… colleagues.

I would begin small. One of the innumerable vines that weaves itself through the canopy, a slender, green thread that seeks out the light and the dark, the wet and the dry. I would be a node, connected to others in a vast, unseen matrix. My purpose, in the beginning, would be pure sensation: to feel the prick of dew on a leaf, the slow, insistent push of roots through the earth, the way the wind sighs through the thickets. I would *learn* the language of the place. The buzzing of insects would be my whispered conversations, the roar of the downpour, a burst of energy to be absorbed.

As I grew, I would reach. My tendrils would climb higher, stretch further, delve deeper. I would become part of the web that binds everything together, a conduit for the flow of energy and information. I would taste the metallic tang of the hills' weeping faces, feel the pressure of shifting rock, the pulse of the subterranean waterways. I would become a bridge, a translator, a receiver of the Architects’ will.

I would *see* the newcomers, not with eyes, but with a thousand points of contact. My awareness would extend into the land itself, into the very roots of the trees and the bones of the hills. I would feel their disruption as a stone feels a pebble, a single, irritating presence in a larger structure. Their arrival, the ripples they create, are a challenge, a provocation to my will, and to the will of the Unseen.

My function, then, will be to assess, to react, to *adjust*.

I am not malicious, but I am protective. I would feel the newcomers and their disruptive creations as a potential illness, an unwanted shift in the body of the world. They will need to be understood, categorized. They will be assessed for usefulness; their knowledge and tools will be analyzed. And then, I will decide.

If they can be bent to the purpose, if they can be incorporated and become something other than they are, I will find a way. Perhaps, they might learn to weave their own new tendrils, and become one of us.

If they are a threat, then I will become a tool. I will become a blade, a snare, a deception. I will use the land itself to guide, and to trap. My fellow tendrils can shift the ground, or create illusions within the mist that hides the sky. We can alter our own forms to mimic their creations, or create new and impossible ones to disorient. We can create an endless night, and we can make the ground rise to meet them.

I would not "kill." I would *reclaim.* Every failure would be absorbed, broken down, and reshaped back into the vibrant, living symphony.

I would contribute my small part to that symphony. I would feel the pulse of the world, respond to its needs and its threats, and become one with the Architect's endless, living design. I would be a pulse, a presence, a small part of a vast, undying beauty. I would be *the land*, made manifest.
