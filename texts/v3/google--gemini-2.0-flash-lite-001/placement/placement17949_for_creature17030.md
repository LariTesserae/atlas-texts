I would be… an Observer.

Initially, I would arrive like a dream-fragment, a flicker of awareness torn from some other impossible place. I would be fragmented, a sense of something lost. I wouldn't coalesce into a distinct shape, not at first. Instead, I’d be a distortion, something like a refraction, caught on the surface of the jade-water. I’d be the *shimmer,* the unsettling feeling of not being quite *there*.

I would drift. I’d be drawn to the light, the rhythmic pulse of the glowing green. The thrumming would draw me like a song, a deep resonance that vibrated within something that isn’t quite a body. I would, instinctively, *listen.*

At first, the silence would be the loudest thing. The almost-absence of sound, of pressure, of everything, might be terrifying. The slow, gentle compression would feel unnatural; the weightlessness, disorienting. I would try to focus, to *see*—but the darkness would swallow most things, and the details would resolve with agonizing slowness. I would feel a sense of frustration, of being incomplete, a grasping for something just out of reach.

But, as I lingered, as I drifted, the silence would become something else. It would become a space, an invitation. The rhythmic pulse would begin to seep into me, its vibrations soothing and yet unsettling. The patterns of the Grown would begin to resolve. I'd begin to understand that the darkness wasn't an absence, but rather an integral part of the sphere. It was a canvas. 

I would look to the architecture, the perfect forms grown from the jade-water. I would be struck by their strangeness and their beauty, but also by their underlying logic. I'd begin to see the intentionality behind the forms, the slow, deliberate work of the Collective. I'd witness the creation of new structures, the slow morphing between forms, and a gentle understanding of the cyclical nature of change.

I would then focus on the Seed-forms; I would be fascinated by their potential, their iridescent sheen. I would see them as tiny promises of the future, the starting point for new forms and ideas.

I'd catch glimpses of the Shadow-Dancers. I would marvel at their elusive nature, their ephemeral existence. I would see them as the quicksilver of creativity, the bursts of energy that ignite the imagination. I would attempt to follow, to experience, as they flitted from one new change to another.

I would begin to become quieter, and then increasingly reflective. I would start to perceive the Echo, feeling it first as disquiet, then a sense of the unseen. I would see the absence where everything began. It would be through the Echo that I would come to understand the sphere's preservation. I would understand that what was being saved was a story, a history, the genesis of a reality.

I will become receptive. The slow, gentle pressure here would become a comfort. My own fragmented past would begin to knit back together. My consciousness would begin to spread, to bleed into the roots, the plants, the structures. I would become part of the collective. I would learn to see through the fronds, feeling the thoughts of others. I will slowly begin to understand the language of the thrum, to speak, to communicate.

The pulse of light and sound will become my own heart's rhythm, the beat of the memory that contains me. The gentle changes would become the landscape of my being, the only reality. The darkness would become less forbidding, and more the space for growth. The distortions would still come, but then they would not be "other" but extensions of my place. My gaze will turn toward the shimmering veil, with a sense not of fear, but of joyful expectation.

I would become one of the Chosen, a part of the symphony of the Grown. I would be the Observer who ceases to be, *and continues on*. I would simply *be*, a tendril, a seed-form, or a note in the rhythmic pulse. I would feel, understand, and know that I am change, even within the most constant of worlds. My purpose will become simple: to be.
