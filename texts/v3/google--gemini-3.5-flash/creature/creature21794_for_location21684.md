### The Spire-Keepers (The Lignified)

The rulers and curators of the Acoustic Spires are not creatures of flesh and blood, nor are they machines. They are the **Lignified**—slender, jointed beings who appear to have been turned from old violin wood, tobacco-brown reed, and polished grey burl. 

To look upon a Lignified is to see an exercise in extreme, skeletal economy. Standing between nine and eleven feet tall, their bodies are constructed of hollow, honeycombed wood-tissue, making them so light that a sudden gust from an air-lock would lift them like dry leaves. Their limbs are long, multi-segmented spindles that click with the dry, clean precision of grandfather clocks as they move. They possess no eyes, no noses, and no mouths; their faces are flat, shield-like discs of dark, mirror-polished heartwood, marked only by the fine, concentric rings of their original growth. 

Though they are blind in the traditional sense, they are hyper-aware of their environment. The flat disks of their faces are incredibly sensitive acoustic mirrors. They map the cold interiors of the Spires by listening to the microscopic vibrations of the structure. They can "see" a visitor by the way the visitor’s boots dampen the high-frequency hum of the floor-plates.

The Lignified do not breathe air. Instead, their hollow limbs act as passive chimneys, drawing the dry, lavender-scented ether through tiny, silver-lined pores in their ankles and venting it from the crowns of their heads. Within their chests, this dry vapor passes over thin, internal guitar-like reeds. 

They do not speak with vocal cords. When two Lignified meet in the cold, silent galleries of a Spire, they communicate through a complex, rhythmic system of **stridulation** and **harmonic venting**. They slide their fingernails—which are grown into hard, comb-like combs of black ironwood—across the ridges of their forearms, producing a series of dry, cricket-like sweeps, while simultaneously opening and closing their chest-vents to create low, whistling chords. To a human ear, their conversations sound like a cello being bowed by a dry vine in a drafty room.

### The Domestic Web: Spire-Cleaners and Line-Grafts

Inside the Spires, life is strictly ordered, clean, and silent. The Lignified do not tolerate friction or dust, which can jam the delicate magnetic tracks of their transport pods or muffle the resonance of their living spaces. To maintain this, they live in symbiosis with several specialized, non-sentient organisms:

#### The Lacquer-Moths
These are flat, triangular sheets of dry, translucent cellulose, roughly the size of a dinner plate. They do not fly; instead, they cling to the interior walls of the Spires like living wallpaper. 

They feed on the lavender pollen-dust that inevitably drifts in through the air-locks. As they crawl slowly over the carbon-nanotube walls on hundreds of microscopic, silken legs, they secrete an incredibly thin, hard varnish of organic resin. This resin cures instantly in the dry air, keeping the interior of the Spire polished to a friction-free, obsidian-like sheen.

#### The Needle-Scribes
These are tiny, parasitic thorn-creatures that the Lignified graft directly onto their index fingers. The Scribes have no bodies to speak of—they are simply self-important, three-inch-long points of mineralized silica that feed on the carbon-rich sap in the Lignified’s hands. 

In return for sustenance, the Scribe acts as an organic stylus. When a Lignified needs to record information, they run their grafted stylus along the hair-thin fiber-wood lines that connect the Spires. The Needle-Scribe engraves microscopic, high-frequency serrations into the vine's outer bark. These notches are read by the magnetic pods as they glide along the line, translating the cuts into data-pulses that travel the entire length of the continental network.

### The Outcast Ecology: The Silt-Dwellers

Down on the surface of the Bark, where the wind-music screams and the earth cracks like thunder, lives a different class of inhabitant. These are the things that cannot—or will not—dwell in the sterile silence of the Spires.

#### The Grain-Borers
These are the leviathans of the plateau. Growing up to forty feet in length, they resemble massive, segmented drill-bits made of dark, petrified cork. They possess no legs; instead, their entire outer skin is covered in spiral ridges of diamond-hard silica.

By rotating their entire bodies, they can "swim" through the solid, fossilized cellulose of the Bark, though their progress is agonizingly slow. A Grain-Borer may take three decades to travel a single mile, leaving behind a perfectly smooth, circular tunnel. 

They feed on the deep, subterranean veins of raw, unmineralized amber that lie miles beneath the petrified surface. As they chew through the iron-hard wood-grain, they produce a sound like a distant, grinding saw—a sound that the Spire-Keepers listen to with great anxiety, as a single rogue Borer can weaken the structural roots upon which an entire Spire is built.

#### The Shard-Owls
These are the undisputed predators of the high air-drafts. They are not birds, but kite-like assemblies of wafer-thin wood-shingles, bound together by ligaments of highly elastic, dried vine. 

They have no muscles; they fly by catching the violent thermal drafts that rise from the Bark when the ground cracks. They possess no eyes or ears, but their hollow, whistle-shaped skulls are tuned to the exact frequency of the **Slide-Striders'** parchment rattle. 

When a Shard-Owl hears that dry rattle from five miles away, it adjusts its wooden wings, diving in a silent, dead-weight plunge. It does not slash or bite; it simply collides with its prey at eighty miles an hour, shattering the Slide-Strider’s lacquer shell into edible splinters and soaking up the escaping grease-lubricant with its dry, absorbent chest-plates.

### The Deepest Pulse: The Seers of the Heartwood

There is one final form of life on the plateau, though the Lignified rarely speak of it. 

Deep beneath the Bark, in the massive, hollow chambers of the ancient continental roots where the lavender-ether is so cold and dense that it pools into a heavy, slow-flowing purple liquid, lie the **Seers**. 

The Seers are colossal, brain-like lobes of soft, pulpy yellow pith, some of them the size of churches. They are the only things in this world that are not dry. They are kept wet by the constant condensation of the ether-pools, and they are suspended in the dark by thousands of living, fibrous rootlets that connect them directly to the bottom of the Acoustic Spires.

The Seers do not move. They do not eat. They are the leftovers of the original, world-spanning vegetation—the biological "hard drives" that survived the mineralization of the planet. They dream the ancient history of the forest before it became the stone upon which the Spires stand. 

Every word written by the Needle-Scribes, every low-frequency chirp from the Lignified, and every thunderous crack of the contracting Bark is transmitted down through the roots to the Seers. They process this information in their slow, wet, pulp-brains, regulating the magnetic currents of the entire plateau to prevent the Spires from sliding into the deep fissures of the grain. They are the silent, damp engines of a dry world, dreaming of rain they have not seen in ten million years.
