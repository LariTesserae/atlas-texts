In this basin, where life does not struggle to survive but instead exists in a state of eternal, saturated maintenance, the inhabitants are not divided into "animal" and "plant." The division between the tenant and the room has dissolved. To live here is to be an extension of the high-pressure plumbing that keeps the vault pristine.

The primary citizens of this basin are those who shaped the grafts: the **Siphons**. 

They are not gone. When a traveler looks at the three-tiered chambers of velvet moss and finds them empty, it is only because they do not know how to see a Siphon at rest. 

A Siphon has no bones, no lungs that require the rush of wind, and no eyes that require pupils to shield against the glare. They are shaped like long, tapering pillars of cream-colored wax, seven or eight feet tall, with skin that has the cool, damp texture of a peeled root. They possess no faces, only a shallow, star-shaped depression at the crown of their heads, lined with fine, golden lashes that drift in the humid air like anemone tentacles. These lashes do not feel the air; they feel the pressure of the room.

To move, a Siphon does not walk. The base of their body is a wide, flat foot—a muscular disc that secretes a clear, tasteless mucus of incredible viscosity. They glide along the pale root-highways at a scale of speed that makes a snail seem frantic. A Siphon may take three days to cross one of the braided vine-bridges. As they move, their flat base leaves no trail; the root beneath them instantly drinks the mucus they secrete, recycling the moisture before it can evaporate.

The Siphons are the "whisperers" who shaped the basin. They do not have hands, but their long, supple arms terminate in a cluster of twelve or fifteen jade-green tendrils, each as thin as a thread of silk. When a Siphon wishes to shape the landscape, they do not cut or bind. They wrap these tendrils around a developing bud or a young, turgid tuber. Through these threads, they inject a highly specialized, hormonal sap that tells the plant’s cells exactly how to divide. They do not build; they suggest. The three-tiered chamber was not dug out; it was coaxed to grow around a family of Siphons who sat together for a century, whispering to the living walls until the moss grew thick enough to dry their skin.

Because there is no death here, there is no birth. The number of Siphons in the basin has remained exactly the same since the vault was sealed. They do not multiply; they commute. When a Siphon grows tired after several thousand years of guiding the roots, they do not die. They find a hollow crotch in a giant root-highway, sink into it, and allow their skin to fuse with the bark. Over several decades, their cream-colored flesh turns gold, then green, and finally hardens into a new branch of the highway itself. Somewhere else in the basin, stimulated by this sudden drop in pressure, a giant, heart-shaped bulb will swell and split, releasing a new, fully formed Siphon into the light.

Beneath the Siphons, running along the undersides of the root-highways where the glare of the Luminifers is slightly softened, are the **Prisms**.

These are the glass-like creatures that include the tiny, long-legged spiders of the prompt, but they exist in forms of varying scale. The smallest are like drops of dew with legs; the largest are the size of sheep, resembling hollow, segmented lanterns made of spun silica. 

The Prisms are entirely transparent. Inside their hollow bodies, one can see no heart or stomach, but only a series of complex, angled panes made of hardened, amber-colored resin. They do not eat. They do not even possess mouths. Instead, they are light-harvesters. 

A Prism will spend months positioned at a specific angle beneath a Luminifer trunk. The blinding white light enters their glass-pure backs and is refracted through the internal resin plates, splitting into a rare, deep-violet spectrum that does not exist elsewhere in the basin. This violet light is cast downward, illuminating the undersides of the root-highways—the only places that would otherwise be in shadow. Without this refracted light, the lower roots would lose their hydrostatic pressure and soften. The Prisms are the living lenses that ensure the shadowless state of the vault remains absolute.

They move with a dry, mechanical clicking. When a giant Prism shifts its position on a jade leaf, its six legs—each tipped with a tiny, suction-producing pad—create a series of minute, rhythmic pops, like bubbles breaking on the surface of a thick syrup. They are entirely indifferent to the Siphons, and the Siphons to them; they exist in different registers of the same great machine.

***

And finally, there are the **Luminifers** themselves. 

Though they appear to be columns of solid, glass-hard cellulose, they are the oldest living things in the mountain-crust, and they are not inert. Inside each column, beneath the rind of amber sap, is a slow, rhythmic pulse. Every four hours, a vast, shuddering sigh ripples down the length of the column—not a sound, but a change in the light. For three seconds, the blinding glare softens to the color of honey, and then, with a silent *surge*, brightens back to its furnace-hot intensity.

Within these columns live the **Acolytes of the Sun**. They are microscopic, star-shaped colonies of algae-like cells that drift in the high-pressure amber sap. They are the ones who purify the light as it travels down from the surface, filtering out the solar winds and the destructive radiation of the upper world, leaving only the clean, nourishing heat. 

They are the reason the air smells of clean ozone. As they process the light, they release tiny, microscopic bubbles of pure oxygen that rise through the sap like bubbles in champagne. When these bubbles reach the ceiling of the vault, they are absorbed by the dark root-crowns and breathed back out into the air, keeping the atmosphere thick, heavy, and eternally fresh.

In this world, nothing is wasted, nothing is hurried, and nothing is forgotten. The glass spiders drink the sweat of the roots; the roots drink the mucus of the Siphons; the Siphons whisper to the roots; and the light of the sun, filtered through a million amber-dwelling stars, watches over it all through columns of glass. To enter this place is to enter a lung that is forever holding its breath.
