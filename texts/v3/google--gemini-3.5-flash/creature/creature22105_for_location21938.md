To understand what lives in the Cradle of the Fluted Chasm, one must first dismiss the division between the inhabitant and the habited. Here, there are no tenants who simply rent space from the wood. The line between the tissue of the city and the tissue of the flesh is as thin as a wet leaf. 

At the center of this world’s intelligence are the **Phloe**. 

***

### The Phloe (The Tenders)

To a traveler from the stone-and-dirt world, the Phloe would appear at first glance to be slender, faceless folk of green-bronze wood. But they are not made of timber; they are made of tension. Their bodies are spun from millions of micro-fibers of raw cellulose and high-tensile hemp, wrapped around a flexible skeleton of living, water-filled reeds. 

They have no faces in the traditional sense. Where a nose and eyes should be, there is only a flat, gently curved shield of waxy rind, striped with pale chartreuse veins. This "face-leaf" is incredibly sensitive to the barometric pressure of the Chasm. By feeling the slight billows and drops in the air, a Phloe can "see" the shape of a room, the approach of a falling water-drop, or the silhouette of a companion thirty paces away. 

In place of hair, their heads are crowned with a magnificent, fan-like fringe of golden cilia—fine, feather-like threads of lichen that drink light and detect the chemical whispers of the Chasm. When a Phloe is startled, this fringe flares outward like a golden halo; when they are in deep, slow-time meditation, it curls tightly against their skulls like a cap of damp moss.

The Phloe do not possess a single, individual voice. Instead, they communication through **The Tap**. Each Phloe has hard, horn-like pads of dense, petrified bark at their fingertips and soles. By tapping on the resonant, hollow columns of the city or stamping on the harp-string suspension bridges, they send coded clack-and-hum patterns through the wood. A Phloe standing at the very bottom of the Chasm on the Pith-Floor can receive a message from a cousin in the high canopy simply by leaning their chest flat against a vertical column and feeling the vibrations rattle through their ribs.

They do not die so much as they "settle." When a Phloe’s fibers grow stiff and their green sap runs thin after three or four centuries, they find an empty niche in one of the great concentric rings of columns. They step into the moist, shelf-like fungi of a sleeping-cell, press their feet deep into the root-veins, and let their outer skin harden into permanent bark. Over several seasons, their limbs fuse with the walls. Their thoughts, which were always quiet, slow down to match the deep-vascular pulse of the colonial tree. The city is literally made of ancestors.

***

### The Glaze-backs (The Janitor-Beetles)

If the Phloe are the architects and weavers of the Cradle, the **Glaze-backs** are its metabolic oil. 

These are beetles the size of sheep, though their bodies are radically flat, like low-slung, armored skiffs. Their shells are not black or brown, but a brilliant, mirror-polished lacquer of jade and emerald that sheds the constant dripping water of the canopy like oil. They possess twenty-four jointed legs, each tipped with a tiny, velvet-like pad that allows them to walk vertically up the slickest jade columns, even while carrying heavy loads.

The Glaze-backs have a single, obsessive purpose: the maintenance of the cellulose skin. They scurry in endless, rhythmic lines along the spiral ramps, their mouthparts making a soft, wet *click-shree, click-shree* sound as they shave away dead wood, moldering leaf-matter, and the biological waste of the gliders. 

They digest this detritus in their massive, three-chambered stomachs, transforming it into a thick, glowing, mint-scented mastic. When the Chasm's gravity-slips or the vibration of the cicadas causes a suspension bridge to splinter, a team of Glaze-backs will arrive within minutes. They spit this mastic into the fissures, smoothing it with their flat forelegs until it hardens into a material stronger than the original wood. Without the Glaze-backs, the city would dissolve into compost within a single year.

***

### The Foil-Wings and the Orb-Skimmers

The great, empty air of the Chasm belongs to the **Foil-wings**. 

These featherless gliders are about the size of herons, but they are as light as dried husks. Their wings are made of two large, translucent, vein-streaked membranes that resemble skeletonized leaves. Their yellow, bio-luminescent skin is so thin that one can watch the dark green blood pulsing through their hearts and along their flanks. 

Because the central void of the Chasm is interrupted by pockets of zero-gravity and folded space, the flight of the Foil-wings is a beautiful, erratic dance. They do not flap their wings; instead, they launch themselves from the high-canopy galleries and "sail," using the warm, sweet-scented updrafts of the city’s respiration. When they hit a zero-gravity pocket, they curl their bodies into tight balls and spin, using the momentum to drift through the airless bubbles like floating seeds until they reach a normal gravity zone, where they unfurl their wings with a loud, paper-like *clack*.

They hunt the **Orb-skimmers**—small, jelly-like organisms that float in the air. The Orb-skimmers are essentially living bubbles of gas, transparent and fragile, with long, trailing, thread-like stingers that pick up microscopic spores from the air. They have no eyes or limbs, drifting wherever the wind-currents take them. Often, hundreds of Orb-skimmers will get caught inside one of the floating, spherical water-ponds that drift through the central void. There, they form miniature, self-contained ecosystems, spinning slowly inside the liquid globes like golden beads in ice.

The Foil-wings will dive directly into these floating water-orbs, their leather-like skin shedding the moisture instantly, to pluck the Skimmers out before bursting through the opposite side of the water-globe, leaving behind a trail of shimmering, tiny droplets that fall upward into the ceiling.

***

### The Root-Woven (The Stilled)

There is another class of inhabitant in the Cradle—those who did not begin here, but who could never leave. 

Over the centuries, rare travelers from the outer world—worlds of iron, stone, and seasons—have stumbled into the Cradle through the thorn-walls at the chasm’s edge. Some tried to climb back out; others sat down on the soft, moss-like fur of the Pith-Floor to rest.

If you sit still for more than three days in the Cradle, the city claiming you is not a metaphor; it is a biological event.

Known as the **Root-Woven**, these are humans or other foreign beings whose lower limbs have been gently, systematically embraced by the creeping rootlets of the Pith-Floor. They are not dead; rather, their metabolism has been hijacked by the warm, damp equilibrium of the chasm. 

One such figure is **Ebra**, who has sat upon a rib of petrified bark at the third level of the city for what she estimates to be four hundred years. Her legs have entirely dissolved into the column; they are now part of the structural arch that supports a major water aqueduct. Her skin has taken on the pale, satiny sheen of the jade wood, and small, purple-flowered liverworts grow from her collarbones. 

Yet mouth and eyes remain free. The Phloe treat her and her kind with immense, quiet reverence, bringing them cups of sweet condensation and honeyed sap-milk. In return, the Root-Woven act as the memory of the Chasm. Because their nervous systems are directly plugged into the colonial plant’s vascular network, they can feel the health of the entire valley. 

"The eastern wall is thirsty," Ebra will whisper to a passing Phloe, her voice a dry, papery rattle that sounds like wind through rushes. "The water-weeds in the fifth pool are choking on silt." 

She remembers the world of "dry dirt," but only as one remembers a dream from childhood. When she speaks of it, the Phloegather in the spiral ramp below her, their golden cilia flared wide in fascination, listening to stories of things that do not grow, things that are dead and do not heal themselves, and the terrible, cold gray stones of the outer world.

***

### The Mirror-Guests

Due to the slippage of reality in the Chasm's center, the most mysterious residents of the Cradle are its **Echoes**.

Because space is dream-folded, a person walking the spiral ramp on the fourth tier may look across the void and see their own back three levels down, walking in the opposite direction. If you throw a seed-pod to that figure, it will drop through the ceiling and land at your own feet. 

But sometimes, these reflections do not go away when you turn the corner. 

These are the **Mirror-Guests**—temporal residues that have become detached from their original bodies. They are semi-solid, looking like figures seen through a pane of rain-slicked glass. They do not eat, nor do they speak with voices, but they are fully integrated into the community. 

A Phloe might be weaving a vine-bridge and find themselves assisted by their own self from yesterday, the two of them working in perfect, silent synchronicity to tie a knot that requires four hands. A Glaze-back will often be seen traveling alongside its own temporal shadow, both of them polishing the same column from different sides of the same hour.

In the Cradle, time is not a river that flows away from you; it is a pool that rises and falls with the tide. The inhabitants do not fear their ghosts. They know that in this warm, green, screaming hollow, nothing is ever truly lost, and everything—sooner or later—returns to the hand that sowed it.
