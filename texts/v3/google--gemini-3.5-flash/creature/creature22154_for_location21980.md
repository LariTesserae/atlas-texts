In this world, life does not sprout from seeds or crawl from eggs. It has no blood, only *slip*—the fine, suspended silt of liquified clay. 

The inhabitants here are not visitors to the terracotta; they are the terracotta itself, briefly detached from its stillness, animated by the fierce, trapped heat of the planetary kiln.

### The Glaze-Skins

Across the flat, glistening plains of red tile, where the universal film of water flows in geometric spirals, there are things that look like broken glass but move like oil. These are the *Glaze-Skins*.

A Glaze-Skins has no thickness to speak of. It is a two-dimensional sheet of vitrified mineral—sometimes a shard of deep cobalt blue, sometimes a greasy, leaden green, or a patch of yellow salt-glaze. They are born when the extreme heat of a steam-vent blasts a patch of the floor, melting the surface into glass, which then cracks as it cools. But instead of remaining static, these cracked sheets peel themselves away from the underlying brick.

They slide across the wet tiles, riding the thin film of water like leaves on a pond, though there is no wind to push them. They move by a subtle, rhythmic curling of their edges, like the undulations of a flatworm. When thousands of them gather in a low-lying basin, they sound like a sack of broken porcelain being shaken very slowly—a dry, glassy *tinkle-clink* that is one of the few sharp noises in this muffled world.

The Glaze-Skins do not eat in the biological sense. Instead, they seek out the "salt-crusts"—the delicate, white, fern-like feathers of efflorescence that bloom in the joints of the wet bricks. A Glaze-Skins will slide over a patch of these mineral crystals, press its flat body down, and dissolve them with its own acidic, wet underside. As it feeds, its color deepens, and its edges grow. If two Glaze-Skins of different colors collide, they do not fight; they slide over one another, merging slowly like pools of spilled ink, until they form a single, mottled sheet of iridescent slag that eventually grows too heavy to move, settles into a depression, and bonds permanently back into the floor.

***

### The Lime-Weepers

Inside the endless miles of wet joints and mortar seams, there is a slower, quieter kind of existence. These are the *Lime-Weepers*.

If you were to watch a single seam of white lime-mortar for several hours, you might notice a section of it—perhaps three feet long—that is wetter than the rest. It does not stay in one place. It travels along the grid of the tiles at the speed of a clock’s hand, a self-contained pulse of saturated calcium hydroxide.

The Weepers are the consistency of thick, cold fat. They are white and opaque, and they smell strongly of wet masonry and high-pH wash. They are the maintenance of this world. As they crawl through the seams, they smooth out the cracks that have been opened by the buckled floor; they suck up the grit and the broken crumbs of red clay, digesting them in their highly alkaline interiors, and leave behind a perfectly struck, semi-liquid line of fresh white grout.

They have a primitive sensitivity to geometry. If a Lime-Weeper encounters an irregular, hand-chopped edge of a slate tile, it will linger there for days, depositing layers of itself like a pearl oyster until the line is straight again. If you were to step on one, it would not pop or scream; it would simply offer the resistance of stiff dough, and your boot would leave a grey, chemical burn where the lime had instantly sucked all the moisture from your leather.

### The Tensioners

Deep in the dark, octagonal wells—forty feet below the red surface where the giant wooden counterweight systems shift—lives the most complex life this world can support. These are the *Tensioners*.

They are the engines of the ground’s turbulence, but they are also its inhabitants. A Tensioner is an assembly of non-living things that has acquired a collective, nervous coordination. They are made of sixty-ply hemp rope, green grease, and heavy, rough-cast bronze plates that have been lashed together by some ancient, accidental tangled knot. 

They do not have faces, but they have a distinct, heavy morphology. They look like giant, multi-jointed caterpillars or flat looms, thirty feet long, that cling to the massive fir timbers of the underworld. They do not travel by walking; they travel by *tensioning*. A Tensioner will cast a loop of greasy hemp over a bronze peg in the dark, twist its own central body using a system of counterweighted bronze weights, and pull itself forward with a wet, heavy *groan-creak* of rope on wood.

Their purpose, if they have one, is the regulation of weight. They are drawn to slackness. Whenever a pulley system above begins to slip, or a wooden beam starts to rot and sag under the weight of the brick floor, the Tensioners will migrate toward the failure. They throw themselves into the gear-teeth; they wrap their bodies around the slipping capstans; they tighten themselves until their hemp fibers sing like harp strings. 

They live on the grease that drips from the upper axles—heavy tallow made of animal fats that must have been stored here in deep cisterns before the builders vanished. When a Tensioner is dying, its bronze plates slip from their hemp lashings with a wet clatter, falling into the bottomless mud below, leaving only a pile of rotting, grease-soaked fiber that the Lime-Weepers will eventually dissolve.

***

### The Sound-Shapes

In the steam-choked air, fifty feet above the tiles where the grey condensation drips perpetually, there are things that are made of nothing but vibration and moisture. These are the *Sound-Shapes*.

They are the literal embodiments of the noises that drift through the steam—the *tock* of the mallet, the *ping* of cooling earthenware, the heavy *shlap* of wet clay. In this dense, hot atmosphere, sound does not disappear; it condenses. 

A "Shlap-Shape" is a localized cloud of heavy, grey moisture, about the size of a hay-bale, that drifts three feet above the floor. It is cooler than the surrounding air. As it moves, it makes a continuous, wet, sputtering sound, like water dropped into grease. It is attracted to the edges of things—to the lips of the blue-glazed basins, to the sharp corners of the leaning chimneys. When it touches an edge, it deposits a layer of fine, red clay dust that it has gathered from the air, slowly building up the structure, adding an inch of rough pargeting to a wall before it dissipates into a normal cloud of steam.

The "Tock-Shapes" are different. They are small, spherical concussions—pockets of high pressure that travel in straight lines across the plains. You cannot see them, but you can see their track: as a Tock-Shape passes over the wet tiles, the water-film parting before it in a V-shaped wake, and every tile it crosses rises by a sixteenth of an inch with a dry *clack*, as if a finger had pressed it from below.

### The Hand

Ultimately, there is a singular, planetary presence that underlies all of these minor movements—something that does not live *in* the world, but *is* the world. It is the thing that the empty world is a monument to.

Sometimes, when the silence is deepest and the steam is so thick that you cannot see your own hand held before your face, the flat brick floor will not buckle, but rather *soften*. For yards around you, the red tile will lose its glaze and its hardness, reverting in the space of a minute into warm, wet, plastic clay.

If you were to kneel in this clay, you would feel it moving. It does not slide or run like mud; it is being *worked*. It rises in smooth, cylindrical ridges that mimic the movement of giant fingers. It is squeezed; it is flattened; it is shaped by an immense, invisible intention that is trying, with clumsy, continental strength, to knead the entire planet into something else.

You can feel the giant thumb-prints shaping themselves in the mire—depressions twenty feet wide, curving with the unmistakable whorls of a human thumb, complete with the microscopic ridges of the skin, rendered in red silt. They remain for an hour, steaming in the twilight, before the heat of the deep kilns rises through the floor once more, vitrifying the clay back into hard, red, unyielding tile, locking the giant print into the stone until the next reset.
