Inside this pocket of blinding light and eternal rain, the inhabitants do not possess flesh. In a chamber of absolute mineral purity, where the air behaves like a lens and the floor is a scalding mirror, life has discarded carbon, marrow, and blood. 

The dozen or so creatures that slide across the glass shelves—the **Shards**—are the sole masters of this space. 

To look closely at one is to see an object of immaculate, terrifying elegance. A Shard is roughly the size of a human thumb, though it has no front or back, only a directional vector of movement. Its body is composed of a series of interlocking, overlapping plates of amorphous silica—not hard, brittle glass, but a specialized, high-density gel that is both flexible and incredibly tough. It behaves like tempered silicone, capable of bending to the curve of the stone but maintaining a razor-sharp, protective edge. 

Because they are entirely translucent, they are almost invisible against the wet sapphire floor. Only their outlines exist—fine, gossamer seams of refracted light that shimmer with the faint, oily colors of a soap bubble. Within their hollow, pressurized bodies, there is no heart, no lungs, and no digestive tract. Instead, their interiors are filled with a complex, branching network of capillary-sized tubes. Through these channels flows a dense, hot fluid—a highly concentrated liquid crystal that serves as their nervous system and their blood. 

This fluid is highly sensitive to the thermodynamic currents of the room. It glows with a faint, violet chemiluminescence, visible only if one blocks out the blinding glare of the central node. It is a biological heat pump; as the Shards slide across the scalding floor, their lower bellies absorb the near-boiling energy, while the cooler spray of the ceiling water hits their backs. This temperature differential—the stark contrast between the ice-cold ceiling droplets and the boiling glass floor—is what drives their biology. They are literal heat engines, converting the room’s fierce thermal gradient into physical motion.

They do not walk; they glide. The underside of each Shard is lined with thousands of microscopic, quartz-like cilia. These cilia do not bend or row like those of terrestrial microbes. Instead, they vibrate. They are piezoelectric crystals, tuned precisely to the low, electronic hum of the central energy node. By resonating with this hum, the Shards generate a minute electrostatic field beneath themselves. They float—not on a cushion of air, but on a microscopic repelling charge, sliding across the wet, vitreous slopes without ever truly touching the stone. As they move, they leave no trail, but the water droplets they pass over are temporarily polarized, briefly aligning into perfect, geometric frost-patterns of boiling steam.

Their relationship with the room is one of absolute, unconscious stewardship. They are not merely inhabitants; they are the gears of this small, closed world. 

The sulfur-yellow and copper-blue mineral crusts that trace the water’s path are their graze. The needle-like antennae on their rostrums are not feeling for obstacles—they have no concept of obstacles in a room they have occupied for eons—but are instead measuring the electrical conductivity of these mineral patterns. The Shards sip these trace metals through tiny, vacuum-fleshed pores on their undersides, keeping the glass shelves clear. 

If the minerals were allowed to build up unchecked, they would insulate the floor, dampening the intense heat exchange that keeps the water cycle moving. By scraping the glass clean, the Shards preserve the perfect, rapid evaporation rate of the room. They are the maintenance crew of a perpetual motion machine.

Because their numbers are so few, their cycles of life and death are slow, ritualistic, and beautiful. They do not reproduce by division or mating, for there are no genders and no cells to split. Instead, their reproduction is a physical consequence of the room’s high-energy physics.

Over centuries of grazing, a Shard slowly accumulates heavy metals—copper, sulfur, iron—into its silica plates. As these contaminants build up, the Shard loses its translucency. It turns a milky, opaque blue, then a deep, scarred indigo. It becomes heavy, loses its resonance with the central node, and can no longer float. 

When this saturation reaches its limit, the Shard begins its final journey. It leaves the cool, dripping ledges and crawls toward the center of the room, directly beneath the blinding, suspended star of the energy node. Here, where the heat is dry and absolute, the Shard ceases to move. It anchors itself to the hot stone and lets the water spray wash over it one last time.

In the intense heat, the moisture inside its capillary tubes evaporates, leaving behind a core of pure, crystalline minerals. Deprived of its liquid crown, the silica shell becomes brittle. The high-frequency hum of the node vibrates through the stone, finding the resonant frequency of the dry, mineral-choked body.

With a sudden, high-pitched *ping*—a sound like a crystal goblet breaking in a quiet room—the old Shard shatters. 

Its fragments do not scatter aimlessly. The steam currents carry the lightest dust upward, toward the cold ceiling, while the heavier, vitreous shards slide down the slick slopes. In the cool, dripping fissures of the upper walls, where the water first condenses, these silica fragments act as seed crystals. The rich minerals in the ceiling water begin to deposit onto them, molecule by molecule, layer by layer, growing new, flexible plates of clear, pure glass. Over decades, a new, tiny Shard dissolves its anchors, drops from the ceiling into the lower pools, and begins to slide.

But the Shards are not the only things that "live" here. 

There is an awareness in the room that does not belong to the glass-crawlers. The central node—the pocket sun—is not just an inert knot of energy. 

The low, electronic hum that fills the chest is not a byproduct of its power; it is a transmission. The node is a stabilized singularity, an artificial eye left behind by a physics thathas long since departed or evolved beyond this universe. It observes. It measures the rate of the water drops; it notes the angle of the steam; it adjusts its thermal output by fractions of a micro-kelvin to compensate for the slight weight of a Shard as it crosses a shelf.

If one were to listen to the hum for long enough, they would notice that it is not a flat tone. It rising and falling in complex, mathematical fugues that take years to complete. The Shards, with their resonant bodies, are the instruments upon which this song is played. As they move across the room, they change the acoustic properties of the chamber, turning the sapphire-walled pocket into a living, whispering synthesizer.

Here, in the deep basalt dark, the machine-mind of the star and its tiny, glass children play an endless, silent game of thermodynamic balance. They have no names, no history, and no destiny. They require nothing from the surface of the earth, and they offer nothing to it. They simply exist, shining and wet, in the heart of the stone.
