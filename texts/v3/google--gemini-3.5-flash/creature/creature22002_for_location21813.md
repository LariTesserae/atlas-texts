To look upon this city is to believe it has been abandoned for ten thousand years, or that it was built as a tomb by some vanished, microscopic race of kings. Yet, if one were to watch a single tower for three lifetimes, the stillness would eventually dissolve.

The inhabitants do not walk; they adhere. They are the *Lithic Thread*, though they have no name for themselves, nor any language but the hum of the stone.

At first glance, a living one is indistinguishable from the grey, clay-like mortar that binds the slate. Shape-wise, they are like pumpkin seeds or broad, flat lentils, rarely thicker than a fingernail’s edge. Their backs are composed of overlapping scales—not of chitin, which is organic and prone to rot, but of a dense, semi-crystalline calcite. This armor is an exact mineral match to the limestone walls of the rift, patterned with the same microscopic ripples and greasy sheen. They are a milk-white that borders on the translucent, like fat that has cooled in water.

They have no eyes, no ears, and no antennae. To them, the universe is not seen or heard, but *felt* as a dense, shifting lattice of vibrations. 

The deafening, metallic carillon of the water drops is their vision. The sole of their flat belly, which is a soft, grey muscle constantly slicked with mucus, acts as a vast drumhead. When a droplet strikes a terracotta gutter six inches away, the shockwave travels through the slate masonry and into the creature’s flesh. They "see" the shape of their city in the acoustic bounce of the drips. A dry terrace is a silent void; a clogged channel is a dull, muffled throb; a clear, leaping stream is a bright, ringing bell. 

Thus, they move toward the discord. 

When a microscopic grain of quartz silt settles in a terracotta gutter, it dampens the vibration of that channel. The nearest citizen—perched perhaps on a balcony three levels down—feels this sudden flat note in the music. It begins its journey. 

The crawl is agonizing. To the human eye, it is invisible; to the stone, it is a brief, smooth glide. The creature uses two tiny, shovel-like plates at its front end—appendages made of a mineral so hard it can scratch glass. These are the "fingernails" of the legend. With these, they do not just scrape away the silt; they groom it, gather it, and mix it with their own mineral saliva to produce the gray mortar. 

They are both the masons and the maintenance of the city. Every tower, every gutter, and every hair-thin staircase is a product of their slow, collective excretion. They do not build with an architect’s blueprint, but rather in the way a coral reef builds: by responding to the prevailing currents of water and sound. A tower rises because a drip falls in a certain spot, and the stone there must be reinforced to prevent the vibration from shaking the wall loose. 

Because there is no vegetation, no soil, and no light, their metabolism is entirely lithotraphic—they eat the stone itself, or rather, they host colonies of microscopic, stone-eating bacteria within their translucent bellies. They drink the weeping water, filtering the calcium, the dissolved iron, and the carbonate salts out of the flow. They are living chemical filters, purifying the water as it passes through their tiny pipes.

They have no social structure, no conflict, and no individuality. They do not congregate, though two may sometimes meet in a narrow alley between slate towers. When they do, they simply climb over one another with the indifferent patience of two pebbles settling in a stream-bed. 

In this world of perfect balance, death is as quiet as life. 

When one of these creatures reaches the end of its span—which may be several centuries long—its internal moisture slowly crystallizes. It does not decay; there are no bacteria of rot here to consume it. Instead, its soft underbelly hardens into stone. The milk-white calcite of its shell fuses with the slate beneath it. It becomes a step on a staircase. It becomes a bracket beneath a balcony. 

The city is not merely built *by* them; it is built *of* them. Every slate tower is a vertical graveyard where the living crawl over the petrified remains of their ancestors, continuing the work of clearing the clay pipes and sharpening the acoustic song of the water, forever locked in the cold, roaring dark.
