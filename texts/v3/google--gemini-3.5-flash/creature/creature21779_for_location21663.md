### The Kaelen (The Dwellers of the Pods)

To look upon a Kaelen is to look upon an organism that has forgotten the concept of dry skin. They are human, but only in the way that a seal is still a mammal; their bodies have adapted to a medium that is thick, warm, and heavy.

They are long-bodied and pale, their skin possessing the smooth, pearlescent quality of a peeled root. They have no hair—which would only drag in the slow currents, collecting colonies of parasitic silt-mites—but their scalps and spines are instead marked by a ridge of low, sensitive cartilage. These "drift-ridges" contain thousands of tiny, jelly-filled pores that register the micro-currents of the Thick, allowing a Kaelen to feel a neighbor swimming toward them in the pitch-dark long before they can hear or see them.

They do not breathe air. Within the basket of their ribs, the lungs have evolved into what they call the *Green-Sack*. This is a fibrous, highly vascular chamber lined with a symbiotic, moss-like algae that Kaelen children ingest shortly after their birth-drift. When a Kaelen draws the brackish sky-sea into their chest, the algae absorbs the dissolved minerals and carbon dioxide, releasing oxygen directly into the blood. 

Because of this, their chests glow. Through their translucent, pale skin, one can see the *Green-Sack* pulsing with a faint, bioluminescent emerald light—a soft, internal lantern that grows brighter when they are excited or swimming hard, and dims to a dull, mossy spark when they sleep. When they "exhale," they do not blow bubbles of air, but rather a slow, sluggish stream of cold, deoxygenated water that falls away from their throat-slits like heavy oil.

Their fingers are impossibly long, with double-jointed knuckles that allow them to wrap around the slick basalt steps of the spires like vines. At the tip of each finger is not a nail, but a thick, grey pad of hardened skin, textured like the skin of a catfish. These pads can find purchase on the perfectly smooth, glassy faces of the ironstone where even the bryophytes fail to cling.

### The Language of the Tethers

The Kaelen do not speak with the breathy, vocal expressions of dry-land humans; the Thick is too heavy for such sounds, swallowing high frequencies within yards. Instead, their language is a physical extension of their hands and their environment.

Across the eighty-yard spans between the spires, they communicate via the *Weave-Line*. These are the great, resin-coated hemp tethers that hold Kael-Tide together. A Kaelen standing on the outer edge of a pod-bundle will take a tether in their long hands and pluck it. The vibrations travel through the wet rope, carrying miles through the dense water. 

A sender will strike the rope with a small mallet of deep-glider bone or pull it with rhythmic, snapping motions of their fingers. On the far spire, another Kaelen will hold the rope between their teeth or press their drift-ridge against it, "listening" to the deep, thrumming register of the wood-signals. 

Their face-to-face speech is equally quiet. It consists of low, sub-vocal clicks produced by snapping the hard plates at the back of their throat—sounds that register as a physical tapping in the teeth of anyone within ten feet—and a complex vocabulary of hand-signs that are illuminated by the soft, green glow of their own chests. To speak in private, two Kaelen will bring their chests close together, using their hands in the narrow green space between them, their bodies shielding their words from the rest of the dark water.

### Domestic Symbiotes: The Loom-Spiders

Inside the basket-work of the hanging pods, the Kaelen do not live alone. Every sphere is a small, self-contained ecosystem. 

Scurrying along the interior and exterior of the water-bamboo walls are the *Loom-Spiders*. These are flat, six-legged crustaceans about the size of a human hand, with wide, shovel-like claws and bodies that resemble wet, grey slate. They are not venomous, nor do they spin webs to catch prey. Instead, they are the maintenance crew of Kael-Tide.

The Loom-Spiders have a natural affinity for the hemp and sea-lily ribs that form the pods. They graze on the destructive, wood-boring fungi and rot-worms that try to settle in the woven walls. As they crawl over the bamboo, they secrete a thick, milky grease from glands near their tail-plates. This grease, when exposed to the brackish water, cures into a black, rubbery varnish that seals the weave and prevents the fibers from fraying. 

A home without Loom-Spiders is a home that will rot and sink within three seasons. As such, they are treated with great care; Kaelen children feed them scraps of boiled bivalves from their hands, and when a family moves to a new pod, they carry their "hearth-spiders" with them in wicker cages.

### The Grazers: Moss-Sippes

Clinging to the sheer, black faces of the spires, far below the hanging city, live the *Moss-Sippes*. 

These are massive, slow-moving creatures that resemble five-foot-wide plates of antique pewter. They have no heads, no eyes, and no limbs. Their undersides are a single, enormous muscular foot—highly similar to that of an ocean limpet—that can grip the glassy basalt with enough suction to withstand the strongest thermal upwellings.

The edge of their circular bodies is lined with a row of hard, obsidian-sharp teeth that rotate like a conveyor belt. As the Moss-Sippe crawls infinitesimally slowly up the spire—moving perhaps three feet in a single thirty-hour day—its teeth scrape the pale blue bryophytes off the stone, leaving behind a clean, black trail of raw, sparkling ironstone.

The Kaelen harvest the Moss-Sippes for their thick, gelatinous fat. A group of hunters will descend on guide-ropes, using wedges of glider-bone to break the suction of a Sippe’s foot. Once pried loose, the creature does not fight; it simply drifts downward like a heavy metal plate, its body too dense to swim. The hunters catch it in large nets made of kelp-rope, dragging it back to the lower cooking vents where its fat is boiled down into the water-resistant resin used to coat the city's bridges.

### The Apex: The Velvet-Gliders

The "deep-gliders" mentioned in the histories of Kael-Tide are the true masters of the Thick, though they are rarely seen near the spires. The Kaelen call them *Silt-Skimmers* or *Velvet-Gliders*.

A Velvet-Glider is immense—often eighty feet from wing-tip to wing-tip—but they are almost weightless in the warm water. Their bodies are constructed of a light, flexible cartilage that is softer than pine-wood, covered in a thick, velvety skin of charcoal-black that absorbs all light. 

They have no eyes; in the changeless green dusk, vision is of little use for a hunter. Instead, their entire forward edge is split by a wide, sensitive smile containing hundreds of small, needle-sharp teeth made of highly mineralized enamel. These teeth do not rot, and when a glider dies and its soft cartilaginous body dissolves into the acid-moss of the spires, its teeth fall to the lower ledges like handfuls of pristine white pebbles—the most valuable resource in Kael-Tide for making knives, scrapers, and needles.

The Velvet-Glider does not swim by flapping; it glides through the kelp-forests by undulating its entire flat body, moving with such silence that it does not disturb even the delicate silt resting on the bladder-wood leaves. It is a passive hunter. It hangs vertically in the kelp, its black body mimicking a dead leaf of bladder-wood, waiting for a school of small, blind silver-fish or a careless Kaelen drift-hunter to pass too close. 

When it strikes, there is no splash or rush of bubbles. There is only a sudden, momentary displacement of the warm current—a heavy, silent "shove" in the water—and then the velvet wings fold together like a book, enclosing the prey in a dark, suffocating pocket of flesh where it is slowly digested over several weeks.

### The Hibernation (The Great Quiet)

Every few months, when the volcanic plumes below Kael-Tide surge with fresh magma, the water of the Thick warms to the temperature of fresh blood. The botanical soup of the sky-sea erupts in a silent, golden frenzy. The bladder-wood kelp swells, its gas-bladders expanding until they glow with a warm, amber inner-light, and the blue-green bryophytes on the spires grow so thick they resemble shaggy wool.

During this time, the Kaelen undergo the *Swell*. 

Their metabolisms, tied to the warm currents and the oxygen-producing algae in their chests, slow to a crawl. The lung-garden within them turns from emerald to a rich, golden-brown, producing a mild, sedative chemical that enters their bloodstream. 

One by one, the families of Kael-Tide retreat into their wicker pods. They lash themselves into their lily-rib slings, their limbs loose and heavy, their long fingers loosely intertwined with those of their children. 

The city of Kael-Tide falls completely silent. No tethers are plucked; no click-talk rises from the platforms. The only sound is the deep, ancient *creak-creak* of the hemp ropes as the pods swing in the warm, volcanic swell, and the soft, rhythmic *hiss* of millions of tiny golden bubbles rising from the kelp-gardens, drifting upward through the green darkness toward a ceiling that has no end.
