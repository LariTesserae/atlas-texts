To understand what lives in the Void-Vault, one must first dismantle the idea of the "inhabitant." Nothing here is an occupant in the sense of a guest occupying a room; rather, the life of the Soughing Ribs is a continuation of the vault’s own material logic. It is a slow, wet, semi-synthetic ecology that has grown like mold over a abandoned engine.

These are the things that keep company with the hot dark.

### The Wheel-Furred (The Line-Clickers)
The Cable-Gliders listed in the ancient surveys are not merely beasts, but the self-reproducing maintenance cast-offs of the city’s original infrastructure. 

To look upon a Wheel-Fur is to see a creature that has traded its symmetry for the physics of the wire. They are long, thin, and segmented, resembling three-foot-loaves of gray, wet leather. They have no eyes, no noses, and no lungs; they breathe through the pulsing of their jointed flanks, which suck the vinegar-thick air directly into lateral spiracles. 

Their limbs are their defining feature. They possess six specialized appendages: four are long, jointed struts of black, mineralized bone that end in hard, grooved plates of tungsten-carbide—wheels that do not turn on axles, but rather "glide" along the high-tensile cables by means of a naturally secreted, high-viscosity grease. The remaining two limbs are delicate, multi-jointed fingers tipped with scrapers of hardened keratin.

They live in perpetual, frantic motion, driven by an ancient, hard-wired compulsion to clean. They run the vertical wires of the city at terrifying speeds, their wheels making a dry, rhythmic *shrr-slick, shrr-slick* through the dark. They feed on the *Lichen-nulla* and the salty, chalky mineral crusts that build up on the power lines. When two Wheel-Furred meet on a single cable, they do not fight; they press their flat, hairless bellies together, exchanging chemical data about cable tension and corrosion rates through small, wet pores, before reversing their paths and clicking back into the blackness.

### The Sieve-Strands (The Hanging Nervous Systems)
What appears from a distance to be quiet, fibrous root-moss hanging from the metal shelves is actually a vast, collective predatory colony. 

Each curtain of Sieve-Strands is composed of thousands of individual, hair-thin threads, some reaching fifty feet into the void. They are a dull, bruised lavender in color, covered in microscopic, sticky barbs. They do not have roots in the basalt; instead, they anchor themselves inside the copper conduits of the city, tapping directly into the low-voltage currents that still leak through the wet walls.

These strands are highly sensitive to the acoustic roar of the cavern. When a three-ton platform shifts or a water-pearl explodes, the vibrations travel through the air, causing the Sieve-Strands to shimmy in sympathetic resonance. 

They feed on the **Drift-Moths**. When one of the giant, paper-thin insects drifts into a curtain, the strands do not coil or snap; they simply hold. The moth’s transparent wings are melted within minutes by the highly acidic, sulfurous grease that coats each thread. The Sieve-Strands then siphon the liquefied nutrients back up to their anchors in the metal, where they store the energy in small, glowing nodules that resemble wet, green copper-grapes.

### The Melted (The Pool-Minds of the Hexes)
Inside the thousands of dark, hexagonal chambers that line the basalt walls, the original builders did not die; they simply lost their edges. 

Over three hundred thousand years, the biological occupants of the city—abandoned by whatever star-faring purpose brought them here—underwent a deep-time degradation. They did not starve; the self-healing city fed them through nutritional mist-vents until those vents broke, and then the occupants began to feed on the city’s own polymer-rich runoff and each other.

Today, they are known as **The Melted**. They are no longer individual creatures, but shallow, undulating pools of dark, protein-rich grease that coat the floors of the hexagonal cells. They have the consistency of heavy oil and are slate-blue or dull copper in color, reflecting the faint green light of the lichen.

The Melted have no bones, no limbs, and no faces. However, they are not mindless. Across the surface of these pools, small, tight membranes form and dissolve—highly specialized "organ-drums" that are attuned to the 12-hertz tectonic thrum of the city’s foundations. They "think" in vibration. A thought within a pool of the Melted is a ripple; a memory is a standing wave that remains trapped in the grease for weeks. 

They do not leave their hex-chambers. They survive by absorbing the moisture that condenses on their surfaces and filtering the organic soot carried up by the hot thermal currents. If a human were to reach into a hex-cell and touch one of these pools, the grease would feel fever-warm and would slowly, gently attempt to lubricate the hand, tasting the skin for mineral salts with a thousand microscopic, chemical receptors before letting go.

### The Soughing Sacks (The Gas-Bloated Shells)
In the great, dark throat of the vault, where the gravity-stabilizers have decayed into wild, unpredictable null-gravity zones, lives the largest of the vault's fauna: the **Soughing Sacks**.

These are hollow, bulbous organisms the size of small carriages, constructed of a tough, translucent membrane that is nearly identical to the self-healing carbon-polymers of the walkways. They are "feral technology"—biological containers that long ago escaped their original storage vats and adapted to the free-fall of the central void.

The Sacks have no means of active propulsion. They drift helplessly on the warm, sulfurous thermal drafts that rise from the geothermal floor, rising until they hit the cold ceiling, where they are pushed back down by the descending drafts of cold sweat. 

They survive entirely on the giant, falling water-pearls. A Soughing Sack features a wide, funnel-shaped mouth at its top, lined with rings of soft, overlapping cartilage. As it floats through the null-space, it positioning itself beneath the dripping structures. When a fist-sized drop of water falls into its mouth, the Sack closes its valve with a wet, heavy *clack*. The water is filtered through its inner chambers, extracting the dissolved minerals and iron-dust, before the excess fluid is expelled from its base in a fine, warm mist.

Because they are filled with warm, light gases—methane and water vapor—they are highly buoyant. In the faint, green glow of the *Lichen-nulla*, they look like pale, floating lanterns, drifting silently through the steel cables, occasionally colliding with a walkway with a soft, leather-like thud, only to bounce away into the dark, entirely indifferent to up or down.

### The Marrow-Columns (The Living Architecture)
Perhaps the most terrifying, yet passive, occupants of the vault are the **Marrow-Columns**. They are the transition point where the technology of the city becomes indistinguishable from a living creature.

A Marrow-Column is a structural pillar that has "gone wild." When the city's self-healing nano-lattices were cut off from central command, they did not stop repairing; they began to over-repair, treating the surrounding basalt rock as a wound that needed to be closed.

These columns are massive, bone-white pillars of mineralized calcium-carbon-alloy, thirty feet thick and hundreds of feet high. They are not static. To watch a Marrow-Column over the course of a century is to watch a muscle contract. Under the immense pressure of the five-mile-deep rock, the column "feels" the strain. Its internal nano-lattices do not snap; instead, they liquefy, flowing like cold honey toward the point of highest stress, where they solidify into thick, gnarled buttresses that resemble the thighs of giants.

They make a constant, low-frequency groaning sound—the "soughing" of the ribs—as they shift their weight against the rock. They are alive in the way a glacier is alive: they have a metabolism (consuming the iron and carbonate minerals from the groundwater that sweeps through them) and they have a purpose (to keep the ceiling from crushing the void). 

If the city should ever die completely, and its hum go quiet, the Marrow-Columns will be the last things to go. They will continue to grow in the hot dark, slowly, blindly turning the entire half-mile cathedral into a solid, white bone-reef, burying the secrets of the vault in stone.
