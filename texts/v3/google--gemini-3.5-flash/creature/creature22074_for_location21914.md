To live here is to be a node in a single, colossal, wet nervous system. 

In this world-sea, there are no "individuals" in the sense of isolated minds locked in cages of bone; instead, life is a gradient of cooperative assemblies. The citizens of this drowned metropolis do not build their home so much as they *are* their home, and their relationships with one another are as much physiological as they are social.

### The Litho-Scribes (The Keepers of the Calcite)
If you could descend into the deep chambers where the creamy white towers are thickest, you would find the **Litho-Scribes**. These are immense, slow-pulsing molluscan collectives, looking like cascades of living velvet draped over the calcite rims. They have no faces, only vast, undulating mantles that are constantly tasting the water. 

The Scribes are the chroniclers and architects. They do not write with ink or carve with iron. Instead, they secrete highly specialized enzymes from the undersides of their mantles. Over centuries, these enzymes etch infinitesimally fine micro-grooves into the stone walls—grooves that are then filled with a dark, mineral-rich secretion that acts like a fossilized thread. 

To a human, these would look like chaotic, organic patterns on the bone-white towers. To the Scribes, who read them by trailing their ultra-sensitive, feathered antennae along the grooves, these are the records of the world. They record the "great droughts" (when the geothermal vents went cold and quiet for three generations), the "saline rains" (when a new thermal fault cracked open to the east, flooding the city with rich, heavy copper salts), and the lineages of the great siphonophore-clouds. 

The young of the Scribes are born as microscopic, free-floating veligers. They drift through the city’s current-lanes, tasting the history of their ancestors etched into every wall they pass, absorbing the "language" of the water before they finally settle, cement themselves to a bare patch of stone, and begin to secrete their own contributions to the towers.

### The Weft (The Sentient Currents)
The three-dimensional "streets" of the city—the pressurized corridors—are not just spaces; they are governed by **The Weft**. These are creatures that look like vast, ribbon-like curtains of translucent, muscular flesh, sometimes stretching for hundreds of feet along the interiors of the tubular aqueducts. 

The Weft are the directors of traffic and the governors of climate. They possess thousands of tiny, lateral-line pits along their flanks that can detect the slightest variation in pressure. By undulating their long, ribbon-like bodies in precise, rhythmic sequences, they create micro-currents. They can speed up the flow of water to push a school of glass-fish through a crowded plaza, or they can create a spiral-eddies to trap and concentrate floating organic detritus for the sedentary filter-feeders on the walls.

The Weft do not think in concepts of "yes" or "no," but in *balance* and *drag*. Their intelligence is hydrostatic. They communicate with one another across the city by sending low-frequency pressure pulses through the water—a language of thrums and thuds that translates to something like a continuous, cooperative weather report. *“The southern valve is warm; increase the draw. The northern tower is heavy with silt; pulse the bellows.”*

### The Velvet-Walkers and the Polishers
Clambering over the outer surfaces of the towers are the **Velvet-Walkers**—the dog-sized, multi-jointed decapods. They are the hands of the city. While they lack the cognitive capacity of the Scribes or the Weeft, they possess an exquisite, instinctive sense of maintenance. 

Their shells are not hard, calcified armor, but are covered in a dense, mossy pile of sensory hairs that feel the drag of the water. Their claws are highly specialized; instead of pincers for crushing, they have delicate, comb-like scrapers and soft, brush-like pads. 

The Walkers are endlessly busy. They climb the vertical shafts, their legs making a soft, rhythmic *tick-tick-tick* against the calcified glass-sponges, cleaning the entry-pores of the towers. They scrape away the dark, suffocating mats of sulfur-bacteria that threaten to clog the breathing-vents of the building-stones. In return for this perpetual janitorial work, the sedentary anemones and bivalves that line the walls do not sting or shut on them; instead, they open wide, allowing the Walkers to harvest the small, parasitic worms that nestle in their soft tissues. It is a contract written in the ancient language of mutual survival.

### The Dream-weavers (The Siphonophore-Loooms)
The fifty-foot-long, bioluminescent **siphonophores** that drift down the vertical avenues are the closest thing this world has to wandering deities. They are not single animals, but elaborate, floating colonies of thousands of individual zooids, each performing a specialized task—some sting, some digest, some propel, and some glow.

Because they are bioluminescent in a world of absolute hand-in-front-of-your-face darkness, they are treated with a quiet, instinctive reverence. They are the only source of "vision," though it is a vision of shadows and faint, ghostly outlines. 

As a siphonophore drifts past a tower, its long, trailing tentacles—looking like weeping tresses of blue-green light—sweep gently against the walls. The organisms living on the walls do not retreat. Instead, they reach out. The anemones flare their tentacles; the Scribes extend their chemoreceptive tongues; even the blind glass-fish swim slowly through the glowing, lethal-looking train of the colony. 

In these moments of contact, an exchange of chemical memory occurs. The siphonophore, having drifted from the wild, uninhabited barrens miles away, brings with it the taste of foreign waters—the iron-cold of the abyssal plains, the sulfur-stink of wild, untamed vents, the passing of leviathans. It deposits these "tastes" into the city's collective registry, and in return, drinks in the warm, nutrient-rich waste of the metropolis. They are the sensory couriers, stitching the isolated towers together with threads of light and chemistry.

### The Great Deep-Heart
At the very bottom of the city, nestled in the blackest depressions of the sea-floor where the silt is warm and thick as porridge, live the **Bellows-Organisms**. These are the largest things in the world. They are vast, fleshy bells, miles across, that have grown into the very bedrock of the rifts. 

They do not move; they have no limbs, no skeleton, and no eyes. They are simply immense, pulsating chambers of muscle and leather. They are the lungs of the city. 

Every few hours, as the geothermal pressure builds in the faults beneath them, the Bellows-Organisms contract. The force of their contraction is so immense that it is felt throughout the entire metropolis as a deep, vibrating *thrum* that rattles the calcite towers and makes the glass-fish dance in unison. This contraction draws the cold, stagnant water down from the upper darkness, forces it through the hot basalt fissures where it is sterilized, mineralized, and heated, and then spews it back up through the city's current-corridors. 

To the inhabitants of the city, the Bellows-Organisms are not animals; they are the ground-state of reality. Their rhythm is the rhythm of time itself. There are no seconds, minutes, or hours here; there is only the *In-Draw* and the *Out-Push*. 

### The Philosophy of the Drowned
To live in this city is to know, with absolute certainty, that you are part of a whole. There is no concept of the "wilderness" versus "civilization." The city does not end where the barrens begin; the barrens are simply the places where the water is currently too cold to whisper. 

When a citizen—whether it is a giant, two-hundred-year-old Scribe or a tiny, transparent glass-fish—nears the end of its life, it does not seek a grave. It travels, guided by the scent of the deep, down to the foundations. It slips into the great, warm chambers of the Bellows-Organisms. 

There, in the thick, churning silt, its body is gently dissolved by the acidic, mineral-rich runoff of the vents. Its calcium is reclaimed by the water. Its memories, encoded in the proteins of its flesh, are tasted by the bottom-dwelling filter-feeders, who whisper them back up the current-corridors in the form of chemical scents. 

A few weeks later, that same calcium is secreted by a young Scribe onto the rim of a rising tower. The dead do not leave. They simply become the floor, the wall, the current, and the heat.
