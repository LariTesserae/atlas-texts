To live in the Ocular of the Ribs is to be forged from friction, silence, and speed. Here, where there is no moisture to soften a blow or lubricate a joint, life does not flow; it clicks, spins, sparks, and sails. 

The inhabitants of this high-profile crucible are not creatures of flesh and blood, but of glass, stone, and static.

***

### The Loom-Spiders (The Glass-Weavers)

Clinging to the pristine, curved ribs of the chalk basin are the primary sculptors of the valley’s lower canopy: the Loom-Spiders. They are not true arachnids, but segmented, multijointed crabs of cast bronze and smoky quartz, the largest of them no bigger than a human hand. 

Because there is no water to produce silk, the Loom-Spiders have evolved a different method of snaring prey. They crawl through the dense mats of copper-wire ferns, feeding on the dry mineral secretions of the stone. As they digest, they extrude filaments of pure, liquid silica from spinnerets on their carapaces. The wind, screaming through the basin at hundreds of miles per hour, instantly cools these filaments into incredibly thin, glass wires. 

The Spiders stretch these glass threads between the hollow, vibrating stalks of the wire-ferns. The constant pneumatic roar of the basin causes these glass webs to vibrate like high-pitched violins. When a brass gale-rider is blown into the web, the impact does not stick; instead, the vibration of the web immediately shatters the gale-rider’s dry shell, dropping the mineral-rich debris into the Spider’s waiting, scissor-like mandibles.

During the rare moments when the gravity wells shift and the wind dies to a brief whistle, the Loom-Spiders can be heard executing a dry, rhythmic tapping against the chalk walls—a percussive code that allows them to map the basin's fissures without sight.

### The Vane-Skins

In the middle-air of the basin, suspended between the chalk walls and the roaring eye of the storm, drift the Vane-Skins. 

These are the largest physical organisms in the Ocular—flat, kite-like creatures with wingspans of up to six feet, yet they weigh no more than a dry leaf. Their bodies are constructed of a pale, paper-thin material resembling dried, yellowed parchment, stretched taut over a rib cage of hollow, flexible carbon rods. They have no flaps, no legs, and no muscles for active flight. Instead, they are completely passive aerial sailors.

The Vane-Skins have series of thermal-sensitive slits along their edges that warp and curl in response to the temperature of the air currents. When the cold mountain air strikes them, the slits close, catching the wind and lifting them into the sky; when they pass through the warm, friction-heated center of a gravity column, the slits curl open, causing them to drop. 

They feed by trailing long, hair-thin tendrils of spun copper into the swarms of gale-riders. These tendrils carry a permanent electrostatic charge built up by the dry wind rubbing against their parchment skins. As they sail, the tiny, metallic insects are magnetically sucked toward the tendrils, sticking to them like dust to an old screen. The Vane-Skin then retracts the lines through a series of sliding, scraping rings on its underbelly, scraping off the insects and channeling them into its central, toothless maw.

***

### The Gyro-Fauna (The Spindle-Shells)

Closest to the black, hovering gravity rings live the most enigmatic residents of the Ocular: the Spindle-Shells. 

These creatures are perfectly spherical, solid nodules of basalt and green fluorite, ranging from the size of a walnuts to massive, bowling-ball-sized spheres. They possess no eyes, no limbs, and no visible mouths. Instead, they are entirely defined by their relationship with gravity.

The Spindle-Shells are hollow, containing a heavy, floating core of magnetic iron that slides freely inside their quartz-lined interiors. When the black rings pulse and warp the local gravity, the Spindle-Shells respond. They do not fly; they "fall" horizontally, vertically, and diagonally through the empty space of the basin, riding the shifting vectors of the cyclone like sliding beads on an invisible string.

To feed, they depend on impact. When a Spindle-Shell "falls" through a dense patch of cushion-globes or wire-ferns, its heavy, momentum-driven mass crushes the silica vegetation into fine powder, which it absorbs through microscopic pores in its rocky outer shell. They are the grinders of the basin, reducing the tough, metallic plants back into the raw dust that feeds the next generation of life.

### The Chaff-Ghosts (The Silt-Wraiths)

The true intelligence of the Ocular of the Ribs is not found in the spiders, the kites, or the stones, but in the wind itself.

Because the atmosphere is utterly dry and saturated with highly conductive silicon dust and copper shavings, the perpetual cyclone is a massive, self-charging electrical generator. The columns of spinning white dust that pirouette across the basin are not merely weather patterns; they are complex, transient neural networks. 

These are the Chaff-Ghosts. 

They are temporary accumulations of polarized dust that hold shape for minutes, hours, or even days. As the dust spins, the friction generates intricate, shifting patterns of static electricity that mimic the neural firing of a brain. When the wind columns align, they exhibit a clear, haunting mimicry of life:
* They will gather in groups of three or four, spinning in perfect, synchronized geometry around a hovering black ring, as if in worship or communication.
* They will suddenly dart toward an intruder, wrapping them in a freezing, dry vortex of crackling sparks, studying the shape of the foreign object before collapsing back into the general storm.
* Most strangely, they are capable of acoustic memory. By precisely modulating the speed of the wind passing through their dust-filled columns, the Silt-Wraiths can capture the bone-shaking roar of the gravity rings and shape it into eerie, melodic chords—chords that rise above the mechanical scream of the basin, sounding like a choir of dry, ancient throats singing to the blinding sun.

When the sun sets and the basin plunge into the freezing, razor-sharp shadows of the mountain night, the Chaff-Ghosts begin to glow with a faint, bioluminescent violet light, mapping the invisible currents of gravity in silent, neon brushstrokes across the ancient, waiting stone.
