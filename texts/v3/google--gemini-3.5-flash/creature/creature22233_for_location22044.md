In this world where geology and flesh have struck a wet, metallic compromise, life does not exist *on* the landscape; it is the landscape's moving gear-work. Every organism here is a component in a slow, thermodynamic cycle that has turned the peaks into a closed-loop engine.

### The Glass-Spinners (*Silex-Loomers*)

Near the needle-sharp summits of the obsidian peaks, where the magnesium glare of the sun is hot enough to soften the rime-frost but the air remains sub-zero, live the *Silex-Loomers*. They are the weavers of the "cable-vegetation." 

In appearance, a Loomer resembles a giant, many-jointed spindle made of blackened horn and dull silver bone. They do not have eyes; instead, their heads are crowned with a cluster of long, flexible cat-whiskers made of pure, translucent glass that vibrate in response to the atmospheric pressure changes of the steam vents below. 

The Loomers crawl over the bare basalt with a dry, scraping click, clinging to the vertical cliffs with feet that are clusters of tiny, diamond-tipped drills. They feed on the raw silica-sand and the mineral grease that weeps from the mountain’s fissures. Within their swollen, bulbous abdomens—which glow with a faint, chemical violet light—they melt these minerals into a viscous, liquid glass. 

Using two specialized, piston-like spinnerets near their mouths, they draw this molten material out into the thin, hollow, lard-coated tubes that form the cable-vegetation. They weave continuously, patching the great trembling mattresses where they have fractured under the weight of the frost or the shifting of the mountains. Without the Loomers, the valleys would lose their false floors, and the dense, warm under-canopy would freeze.

### The Hearth-Beasts (*The Colossi*)

Below the cable-mattresses, where the darkness is absolute save for the red glow of tectonic cracks and the brief, blinding bursts of steam, live the *Colossi*. 

To call them animals is to misunderstand their scale and stillness. They are living boilers, cathedral-sized spheres of dark iron-sulfide. Their shell-plates are not static armor but a living, growing integument, inches thick and self-healing, that exudes a sulfurous, greasy paste to prevent rust. 

Internally, a Colossus is almost entirely hollow—a massive, vaulted chamber of porous, sintered bone. This bone-vault functions as a combustion chamber and a water-treatment plant. Through wide, muscular siphons at their base, they draw in the cold, salt-saturated volcanic brine that pools in the dark ravines. Using the intense heat of the volcanic vents directly beneath them, their internal organs—which resemble hanging sheets of thick, rubbery black leather—boil the brine, separating the pure water into high-pressure steam while concentrating the salt and iron into heavy, metallic secretions.

They do not possess minds in any sense recognizable to warm-blooded life. Instead, their nervous systems are decentralized webs of copper-rich nerves that run along the seams of their iron plates. When one Colossus vents its steam, the sudden drop in pressure triggers its neighbor to do the same. This creates the grand, rhythmic, twelve-second pulse that defines the valley’s atmosphere. It is a collective, unconscious brain—a fluidic computer where thoughts are measured in pounds-per-square-inch and decisions are made by the opening and closing of valve-mouths.

### The Scraping Throngs (*Rivet-Mites*)

On the flanks of these great iron domes, inhabiting the narrow, grinding gaps where plate meets plate, are the *Rivet-Mites*. These are the brass-colored arthropods, ranging from the size of a hand to the size of a dog. 

Their bodies are armored in a copper-rich cuticle that, over time, oxidizes into a crusty, sea-green patina. Their limbs are not jointed like those of crabs, but are telescoping tubes—living hydraulic cylinders that move with a quick, jerky, pneumatic hiss. 

The Mites are the cleaners of the engine. They spend their entire lives in the perpetual, oil-wet dark beneath the cable-canopy, scraping the mineral grease and sulfur-scale from the steam chimneys of the Colossi. Their mandibles are pair-mounted, circular blades of hard, serrated horn that spin in opposite directions, producing a steady, metallic *snip-snip-snip* that sounds like a thousand scissors cutting through sheet metal. 

They are blind, relying on the vibrations of the Colossi’s heartbeats to navigate. Should a Colossus die—an event marked by its steam chimney falling silent and its iron plates cooling to the ambient sub-zero temperature—the Rivet-Mites descend upon it in their millions. They do not eat the meat; their circular jaws drill through the iron shell to reach the concentrated deposits of salt and minerals within the bone-vault, dismantling the cathedral-sized husk in a matter of months until nothing is left but a heap of rust-coloured sand.

### The Mica-Drifters (*Steam-Kites*)

Above the canopy, in the thin, indigo sky, the only things that move with speed are the *Mica-Drifters*. 

Because the air is too thin to support traditional flight, the Drifters do not flap wings. Instead, they are living gliders, built like flat, black kites. Their bodies are hexagonal sheets of light, flexible horn, six to eight feet across, supported by a framework of hollow, nitrogen-filled bones as strong as spring steel. The upper surfaces of their wings are black, absorbing the harsh, direct sunlight to keep their internal fluids from freezing, while their undersides are silver, reflecting the glare of the ice below.

The Drifters spend their lives suspended on the rising plumes of steam. When a row of Colossi vents, the sudden thrust of superheated gas launches the Drifters high into the thin atmosphere. They sail from peak to peak, their wing-margins rimmed with fine, sticky hairs that act like nets. As they drift through the freezing steam clouds, they catch the biological soot, mineral rime, and micro-organisms that are coughed up from the under-canopy. 

When the steam cools and precipitates back to the earth as frost, the Drifters descend with it, landing on the high needles of obsidian to scrape the gathered nutrients from their wings with their long, triple-jointed hind legs, before waiting for the next eruption to carry them aloft once more.

### The Straddlers (*The Gallows-Walkers*)

Towering over both the valleys and the cable-canopy are the *Straddlers*. These are the derrick-legged giants whose steps take an hour to complete. 

They are shaped like three-legged derricks, their bodies suspended eighty feet above the iron domes on stilt-like columns of jointed, black bone. These legs are not vertical but angled outward, spreading their immense weight across several different peaks or stable basalt ledges. Within the hollows of their legs run massive, braided muscle-ropes that are continuously bathed in an amber, lard-like fat that seeps from glands near their hip-joints, keeping the movement silent and fluid.

At the center of a Straddler’s tripod body hangs a heavy, pendulous head that has no neck; it is simply a massive, spherical plumb-bob of dense, lead-heavy bone. This head sways slowly between the legs, acting as a stabilizer that keeps the giant from tipping over as it navigates the vertical terrain.

The Straddlers are the true regulators of this world. From the underside of their central bodies hang three long, copper-sheathed tubes—flexible proboscises that can extend over a hundred feet. As the Straddler moves with agonizing slowness across the valleys, it drops these tubes down through the gaps in the cable-vegetation, inserting them directly into the steam vents of the Colossi below. 

They do not feed on the Colossi; rather, they siphon off the superheated brine to replenish their own internal reservoirs, while simultaneously injecting chemical inhibitors—salts of antimony and lead—into the Colossi’s boilers. This process regulates the internal pressure of the domes, preventing catastrophic explosions that would tear the delicate cable-canopy apart. 

### The Nursery of the Deep (*The Oil-Seams*)

Deepest of all, in the sunless abysses where the bases of the basalt needles meet the raw tectonic mantle, lies the nursery. Here, the air is not thin or cold, but a thick, choking atmosphere of superheated sulfur and hydrocarbon vapors.

In this black, boiling soup, the young of all the valley's species exist as a single, undifferentiated mass of "oil-grubs." They look like huge, glistening bladders of white fat, floatable and incredibly tough, drifting in the subterranean lakes of mineral oil. They have no limbs, no eyes, and no lungs. They absorb heat and sulfur directly through their skin, growing at a rapid rate as they are basted in the planetary warmth.

As they mature, their chemistry changes based on where they float. Those that drift near the cold brine-flows develop the iron-sulfide shells of the Colossi; those that cling to the silica-rich walls of the basalt fissures grow the glass-spinning organs of the Loomers; and those that are swept up into the high-pressure steam conduits are baked into the hard, jointed, horn-and-brass cuticles of the Rivet-Mites and the Straddlers.

When their transformation is complete, the pressure of the earth ejects them upward through the volcanic vents. They emerge into the blinding glare of the peaks not as children, but as fully formed, functional gears, ready to plug themselves into the massive, breathing machine of the valley.
