No blood runs in this underworld, for blood is a vessel of water, and water here is an impossible myth.

Instead, the creatures that inhabit these dry, silent terraces are made of the same materials as the floor and the walls, organized by some slow, geologic impulse into a living state. 

The primary occupants—the builders of the low basalt huts—are beings whose very presence is indistinguishable from the surrounding stone until they move. They have no common name, for they have no spoken language, but if one were to translate the low-frequency vibrations they press into the bedrock, they might be called the **Silt-Walkers** or the **Slate-Born**.

In form, a Slate-Born is low-slung, balanced on four heavy, column-like limbs that terminate not in toes, but in broad, flat hooves of dense, self-healing horn. These hooves are harder than the volcanic glass they tread upon; they do not wear down, nor do they slip. Their bodies are long and wedge-shaped, like the lintels of their own stone huts, covered in overlapping plates of dark, vitreous hide that has the texture of cold-pressed slate. 

They have no faces as we understand them. Where a head should be, there is only a smooth, sloping shield of bone-dry chitin. Beneath this heavy brow lies their primary sensory organ: a horizontal trench lined with millions of microscopic, copper-colored filaments that twitch and sway in the dry air. These filaments do not see light; indeed, the Slate-Born are utterly blind to the spectrum of the sun. Instead, they "see" the world as a complex, trembling map of static electricity and thermal gradients.

To a Slate-Born, the towering cliffs of basalt are not dark, but glow with a deep, comforting infrared crimson, like iron cooling in a forge. The deep rifts are chasms of cold blue-grey, where the heat of the planet’s core has settled too deep to reach. The suspended mica dust is a golden, shimmering fog of micro-voltages, buzzing against their sensory filaments like a warm, tactile mist.

Because their joints cannot be lubricated by fluid, they are packed with a fine, natural deposit of graphite and dry talc. When a Slate-Born moves, it does so with an agonizing, deliberate economy. There are no quick gestures. To lift a limb is a process of several seconds; to turn the body requires a slow, rotational pivoting of the heavy hip-joints that produces a faint, dry *creak-click*, like the sound of dry pine needles being crushed under a heavy boot.

Their internal biology is dry, porous, and slow. They do not breathe oxygen to burn sugars; instead, they are thermal batteries. Their bellies, lined with broad, soft plates of a leathery, carbon-rich tissue, are pressed flat against the warm stone plains for hours—sometimes decades—at a time. They absorb the heat of the earth directly, converting the thermal differential between the hot rock below and the slightly cooler dust-haze above into a creeping, low-voltage nervous current.

This thermal dependency dictates their social structure, if such a word can be used. They do not gather for warmth, for they have no warmth of their own to share, but they gather for electrical silence.

The low-stone huts they build are not shelters from weather—for there is no rain, nor any wind to speak of—but are **static sanctuaries**. The interlocking structures of basalt, stacked without mortar in perfect geometric spirals, act as natural grounding loops. Outside, the air is constantly snapping with microscopic static charges generated by the endless friction of the mica fog. Inside the huts, this charge is caught by the dry-stone walls and channeled harmlessly down into the bedrock. 

Within these sanctuaries, the air is electrically dead. For a Slate-Born, entering a hut is like entering a room of absolute darkness and silence after a day spent in a glaring, noisy crowd. Here, they sit for centuries in the "Great Cool," their sensory filaments relaxing, their internal thermal currents ticking down to a rate of one pulse every ten minutes. It is their only form of sleep.

---

Around them, in the crevices and vertical margins of the world, lives a sparse but highly specialized ecology of lesser things.

Clinging to the sheer basalt faces are the **Plate-Dwellers**—the leathery, shield-sized discs. They are the weeds of this dark garden. They are almost entirely passive, possessing no legs, no mouthparts, and no sensory organs beyond a simple, heat-sensitive skin. They grow by a process of slow mineral accretion, drawing trace sulfur and iron directly from the damp-less rock faces through microscopic, root-like anchors that drill into the stone over centuries. They are so dry that if one were to fall from the cliff, it would not shatter or split, but would bounce down the obsidian terraces with a hollow, metallic *clink*, like a dropped iron pot.

Preying upon—or rather, grooming—the Plate-Dwellers are the multi-jointed crawling creatures. These are the **Silt-Spiders**, though they have only six thick, spade-like legs. Their shells are made of a dry, translucent horn that looks like yellowed amber. They do not hunt by speed; they creep over the sleeping plates, using their heavy mandibles to shave off the mineral rime that forms on the plates' edges. 

The relationship is symbiotic: the Silt-Spiders keep the plates clean of the insulating dust that would otherwise block their heat absorption, and in return, they feed on the sulfur-rich excretions the plates pump out from the rock.

Deep within the five-mile rifts, where the temperature rises to the heat of an oven and the air is thick with heavy, vaporous gases, lives the source of the ancient beacons: the **Tallow-Mounds**.

These are the giants of the deep cracks. They are massive, slow-growing colonial organisms that look like mounds of grey, oxidized lard, often fifty feet across, clinging to the vertical rift walls. They have no skeleton; instead, they are held together by a tough, varnished casing of polymerized mineral oil. Inside this casing, they synthesize dense, waxy hydrocarbons from the sulfur and methane seeps of the deep crust. They are entirely static, growing at a rate of inches per millennium.

To the Slate-Born, a Tallow-Mound is a precious resource. Bundled in harness-ropes made of braided asbestos fibers—which are carded from the natural mineral veins by the tiny, needle-legged **Weaver-Grubs** that inhabit the rock-fissures—the Slate-Born descend into the pitch-black rifts. 

Using their cold-hammered copper scrapers, they pare away chunks of the dense, solid wax from the Tallow-Mounds. The organism does not bleed, nor does it seem to react; the wound they leave behind slowly skins over with a new layer of dry, amber varnish over the next hundred years.

This tallow is hauled up to the plains and packed into the iron cups of the beacon-posts.

---

The beacons themselves are the only monuments to a history this world possesses. They have not been lit for age upon age, but they remain ready, preserved in the eternal dryness.

They are not meant to light the dark for eyes that cannot see. Instead, they are lit only during the **Tectonic Sighs**—the rare, violent epochs when the planet’s crust shifts. During these periods, the immense pressure of the grinding stones miles above creates a piezoelectric feedback loop. The bedrock begins to hum at a deafening, bone-shaking frequency, and the air becomes so heavily charged with static electricity that the Slate-Born are struck "blind." Their sensory filaments are overwhelmed by a white-hot wall of blue sparks, leaving them disoriented, unable to find their stone sanctuaries or sense the rifts before they step over the edge.

When the vibrations begin, the Slate-Born use their heavy copper tools to strike sparks of flint against the old tallow beacons. 

The flame that rises from the tallow is not like a surface fire; it is a heavy, sluggish, smoldering heat-glow that consumes its own self-released chemical oxygen. It does not flicker, for there is no wind, but it emits a massive, clean column of infrared heat that cuts through the static-soaked fog of the plain.

To the blind, panicked Slate-Born, these beacons are not lights, but towers of warmth—thick, solid pillars of heat rising in the cold static storm. Guided by these pillars, they can orient themselves, crawling slow and low across the razor-sharp obsidian, back to the safety of their static-proof huts, where they wait out the centuries until the earth is quiet once more.
