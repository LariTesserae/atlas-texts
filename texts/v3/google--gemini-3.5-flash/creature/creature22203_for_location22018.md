In the colossal, sunlit vacancy of the Siphon-Swell, life does not struggle to survive; it is assigned to exist. Because there are no soils to bury seeds within and no rotting floors to breed decay, the inhabitants of this high sea are built of the same cold, clean stuff as the scaffold itself: water-logged cellulose, glass-like chitin, and pressurized sap. 

Here is what moves through the emerald-tinted silence:

### The Glass-Sails (The Drift-Ballasts)
In the vast, blue-green voids between the Piston-Cedars—where the current is nothing but a distant, rhythmic pulse—drift the Glass-Sails. They are the most common residents of the open water, yet they are barely there at all. 

Each Glass-Sail is a hollow sphere of naturally occurring silica-glass, ranging from the size of a carriage wheel to the width of a manor house. They are perfectly transparent, save for a delicate, leaf-like green vein that runs through their centers like a compass needle. Inside these glass hulls lives a colony of microscopic, photosynthetic micro-organisms. They do not swim; they regulate their buoyancy by chemically splitting the fresh water into oxygen and hydrogen, storing the gases in their core to rise toward the zenith-light, or releasing them to sink into the darker, colder drafts. 

When hundreds of these spheres float together, they catch the perpetual morning sun, refracting the light into long, shifting lattices of emerald and gold that dance across the empty water. They have no senses and no will, but they serve as the world’s lungs, absorbing the minute amounts of carbon dioxide released by the pumping refinery-nodes and returning pure, sterile oxygen to the current. Sometimes, when a current shifts, thousands of them are drawn into the massive intake vents of the Piston-Cedars. They do not struggle. They are drawn into the dark wood, crushed by the wooden pistons, and their silica is harvested by the tree to glaze the interior of its pipeline-branches, leaving nothing but a faint, glass-like sheen on the wood.

### The Scab-Spinners (The Lint-Gatherers)
Where there is high-pressure plumbing, there are leaks. A microscopic crack in a branch-pipe, worn thin by a thousand years of rushing water, will shoot a jet of sweet, iron-rich sap into the clear void. 

To this, the Scab-Spinners react. 

These creatures are thin, ribbon-like entities, often three hundred feet long but no thicker than an oilcloth. They are made of a dry, fibrous mesh that feels like wet paper. They drift through the open water like lost bandages, completely inert, until they pass through a plume of escaping sap. The chemical signature of the sap activates them instantly. 

With slow, undulating ripples of their wide, flat bodies, they steer themselves toward the leak. They do not eat the sap; they are designed to be saturated by it. They wrap themselves around the wounded pipe, layer upon layer, like a mummy's shroud. As the sticky, resinous liquid fills their fibrous pores, it reacts with their skin, curing instantly in the glacial water into a plug as hard as ironwood. The Scab-Spinner becomes one with the pipe, its own life-functions frozen forever as it is cemented into the botanical infrastructure. The world is full of these smooth, grey-brown swellings along the green pipeline branches—the tombstones of creatures that lived only to become glue.

### The Core-Tetherers (The Pipe-Cleaners)
Inside the high-velocity branch-pipes, where water moves with the speed of a falling arrow, nothing should be able to live. Yet, if one were to peer through the translucent walls of a refinery-node, one would see long, silver-green shadows whipping in the torrent. 

These are the Core-Tetherers. They are eel-like, but they possess no bones, only a series of high-tension cartilaginous rings that run the length of their fifty-foot bodies. Their heads are massive, blunt plugs of solid wood, tipped with a ring of diamond-hard, chitinous boring teeth. 

The Tetherer drives its head deep into the inner joints of the wooden pipelines, locking itself against the terrifying drag of the current. Its body then trails downstream like a wind-whipped pennant. Their flesh is covered in thousands of microscopic, comb-like cilia that catch the trace minerals and yeasts that escape the refinery-nodes. 

The Tetherers are the sensory nerves of the pipeline network. When the water pressure within a branch drops too low, the Tetherer’s body slackens. This loss of tension triggers a chemical release in their anchor-points, sending an osmotic signal through the bark-fiber directly to the nearest refinery-node. The node responds by adjusting its wooden turbines. When a Tetherer dies, its grip fails, and it is instantly swept down the pipe, shredded by the filtration fans at the next junction, and reconstituted into the very sap that feeds its descendants.

### The Cortex-Galls (The Node-Minds)
Inside the massive, bulbous refinery-nodes—suspended in the dark, warm chambers where the bioluminescent yeast-skins glow—live the closest things this world has to rulers. They are the Cortex-Galls.

They look like gargantuan, waterlogged hornet nests, suspended from the ceiling of the node-chambers by thick, pulsing ropes of root-tissue. They do not move. They are stationary lumps of grey, folded pulp, wet with a constant drizzle of green, highly concentrated sap. 

The Galls are the computers of the Siphon-Swell. They possess no eyes, no ears, and no mouth, but they are directly wired into the botanical internet of the world. They feel the heartbeat of every piston; they know the salinity of every gallon of water passing through their node; they can calculate the stress on a pipeline five thousand miles away. 

From their pulpy hide, they bud small, scurrying offspring called **Sieve-Mites**. These are small, multi-legged creatures that look like wooden ticks, about the size of a dog. The Mites crawl through the internal machinery of the node, using their flat, paddle-like limbs to clean the wooden gears, scrape away excess yeast, and clear small obstructions from the turbine blades. The Mites have no independent thoughts; they are merely extensions of the Gall’s fingers, controlled by low-frequency hums that vibrate through the water of the chamber.

### The Basal Dredgers (The Root-Tongs)
Far below the bright, morning levels of the Siphon-Swell, where the Great Piston-Cedars disappear into the cold, black pressure of the lower atmosphere, the water is different. It is thick with the mineral dust of the deep world, heavy and dark. Here, the columns are anchored, and here live the Basal Dredgers.

These are the titans of the depths—monstrous, slow-moving entities that resemble six-legged crabs constructed from petrified pine roots. They are the size of galleons, their bodies covered in thick, shaggy coats of dark moss that insulate them from the near-freezing dark. 

The Dredgers do not swim; they cling to the monumental vertical trunks of the Piston-Cedars with massive, hook-like claws. Their sole purpose is the removal of "mineral-scale." As the cold, deep water is drawn up into the root-nodes, it deposits layers of calcium and iron onto the bark. If left unchecked, this scale would suffocate the intake pores of the trees. 

The Dredgers climb up and down the trunks in a journey that takes centuries, using their colossal, chisel-like jaws to shave away the mineral crust. The sound of their work is a constant, low grinding—a gravelly *scritch-crunch* that accompanies the deep *thump-hiss* of the pistons above. They are incredibly ancient; some have been scraping the same ten miles of trunk since the world-machine was first set in motion. They are so slow, and their shells so heavy, that small forests of pale, deep-water ferns grow upon their backs, home to tiny, sightless shrimp that feed on the dust the Dredgers shake loose.

In this world, everything is a part of the clock. There are no predators, for there is no waste to be hunted; there are no prey, for nothing is born to be lost. There is only the work, and the silent, cold water that carries the sound of it to the stars.
