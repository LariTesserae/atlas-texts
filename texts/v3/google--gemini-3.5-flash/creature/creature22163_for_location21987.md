In the Abyssal Scaffold, life does not exist *upon* the environment; it is grown *from* it, tuned to it, and eventually reclaimed by it. To live here is to be an extension of the great wooden machine, an ear that listens to the Deep Groan, or a throat that adds to the Vessel-Song.

Here are the dwellers of the dark.

***

### The Suture-Folk (The Gardeners of the Grain)

They are the closest thing this world has to a presiding intelligence, though they possess no cities, no technology, and no language that can be spoken in the air. 

The Suture-Folk are spindly, skeletal figures that look less like animals and more like jointed pieces of waterworn driftwood held together by cords of pale, rubbery muscle. They are immense—often thirty to forty feet from the tips of their upper limbs to the grasping hooks of their lower joints—yet they are exceedingly thin, designed to offer no resistance to the glacial, heavy sweep of the deep currents. Their bodies are made of the same slate-gray, leather-hard wood as the Scaffold itself, and indeed, they are born from it. 

Every few centuries, in the deep hollows of the vertical pillars, the vascular wood will swell into a dense, burl-like node. Over decades, this node is shaped by the constant, rhythmic vibrations of the Vessel-Song until it detaches, remaining bound to the parent trunk only by a thick, umbilical vine of sap-rich fiber. When the vine finally rots away, a new Suture-Folk crawls out into the dark.

They have no eyes, no mouths, and no lungs. They do not eat in the traditional sense; instead, they have long, hollow needles of bone-like wood on the tips of their fingers. They insert these into the vascular pores of the columns, drinking the mineral-heavy, cold sap that pulses through the Scaffold’s interior. 

Their entire existence is spent in a state of slow, meditative maintenance. They climb the pillars with agonizing slowness, spending weeks on a single square meter of wood. Using their acidic saliva and their hard, chisel-like fingers, they prune away parasitic growths, smooth out the rough grain left by rare turbulent eddies, and guide the growth of new horizontal arches. When two columns drift too close, a group of Suture-Folk will gather at the crossing point, holding the giant branches together with their own bodies for years at a time until the wood grafts, fuses, and becomes one.

To communicate, they tap. A Suture-Folk will press its flat, disc-like chest against a pillar and strike the wood with its knuckles. The sound—a dry, metallic *tock-tock-tock*—travels for miles through the dense, pressurized wood. Through these seismic patterns, they share a slow, collective memory of the Scaffold’s geometry, mapping every column, every valve, and every microscopic fracture across the path of the currents.

### The Vessel-Tongues (The Organists)

Deep inside the miles-long hollow chambers of the vertical pillars, where the water is forced through narrow intake pores under immense pressure, live the Vessel-Tongues. 

These are colonial organisms, related distantly to both sea anemones and chordates, that resemble massive, shimmering ribbons of translucent flesh. They can grow up to eighty feet long, anchoring their wide, suction-pad bases to the interior walls of the acoustic pipes. Their bodies are ribbed with thousands of thin, cartilaginous plates that act like the reeds of a wind instrument.

As the deep current is funneled through the hollow pillars, it rushes over the Vessel-Tongues. By contracting their massive, muscular bodies or dilating their rib-plates, they alter the shape of the water’s passage. They are the ones who modulate the "Vessel-Song." 

They do not do this for pleasure or art, but for survival. The vibration they produce creates a localized ultrasonic field that repels wood-boring parasites and prevents the colonization of aggressive, rot-inducing fungi. The different chords represent different states of the column: a clear, high-pitched whistle indicates a healthy, high-pressure flow of sap; a flat, buzzing drone warns that the column is choked with silt or cracking under hydrostatic stress. 

The Suture-Folk listen to the Vessel-Tongues from the outside of the pillars, using their songs as a diagnostic map to find where the Scaffold needs healing.

### The Drift-Sheets (The Great Erasers)

In the colossal, silent voids between the columns—where there is nothing but miles of empty, black water—something moves. It is the Drift-Sheet.

A Drift-Sheet is a single, colossal organism that looks like a sheet of grey, translucent silk, spanning the size of several city blocks. It has no brain, no skeleton, and no sensory organs other than a sensitivity to gravity and water pressure. It is nearly invisible in the dark, save for a faint, milky perimeter that shimmers with a cold, chemiluminescent trim.

These creatures spend their centuries-long lives drifting through the empty spaces between the columns. They do not swim; they simple let the glacial conveyor currents carry them. As they float, they act as the vacuum cleaners of the sky-ocean. They collect the "marine snow"—the continuous, microscopic rain of dead mold, shed skin-fibers from the Suture-Folk, and organic dust that drifts down from the upper, unknown reaches of the world.

When a Drift-Sheet eventually collides with a column of the Scaffold, it does not crash. It drapes itself over the wood like a wet shroud, wrapping around the massive pillar. For months, it will remain stationary, using its highly acidic underbelly to dissolve and digest every scrap of organic matter, mold, and waste that has accumulated on the wood’s surface. Once the wood beneath it is polished to a flawless, slate-gray sheen, the Drift-Sheet slowly unclasps and behaves like a parachute, letting the current peel it away and carry it back into the black void.

### The Chime-Snails

Clinging to the underside of the horizontal aqueducts are colonies of Chime-Snails. These are not soft mollusks, but armored organisms with heavy, spiral shells made of a dense, glass-like silica. 

Unlike the other silent dwellers of the Scaffold, the Chime-Snails are remarkably loud in their interactions. Their shells are suspended from the wood by long, flexible stalks of muscle. When the "Deep Groan" vibrates through the horizontal platforms, it sets these stalks swinging. As the snails sway in the current, their heavy, silica shells bump against one another and against the polished wood.

The result is a constant, crystal-clear chiming—a delicate, liquid patter that sounds like thousands of glass bells ringing underwater. This high-frequency din serves a defensive purpose: the sharp, ringing vibrations deter the blind, ten-foot-long green eels from nesting near the snails' delicate feeding tendrils, which they cast out into the water like fine, glowing lace to catch passing bacteria.

### The Sunken-Face (The Bottom-Dwellers)

Deep below the habitable levels of the Scaffold, where the immense vertical pillars plunge into the crushing, lightless depths near the planet’s unseen core, the wood begins to change. Under the unimaginable pressure, the sap-flow slows, and the wood becomes black, waterlogged, and soft. 

Here lives the Sunken-Face.

These are massive, flat-bodied scavengers, thirty feet across, that resemble a cross between a chambered nautilus and a giant, eyeless horseshoe crab. Their shells are thick, segmented plates of iron-infused chitin, stained a greasy black by the minerals of the deep. 

They do not climb, and they cannot swim. Instead, they anchor themselves to the very base of the columns where the wood meets the abyssal muck. They possess massive, grinding mandibles designed to crush the dense, water-swollen wood of the oldest columns. They feed on the "graft-rot"—the dying, pressurized tissue of the Scaffold’s foundations.

Though they seem destructive, they are vital to the equilibrium. By chewing away the decaying base of the columns, they prevent the rot from traveling upward into the healthy segments of the Scaffold. They are the undertakers of the deep, reclaiming the oldest parts of the living machine and turning them into fine, black silt that settles into the bottomless trench below—a quiet, necessary end to the cycle of an unchanging world.
