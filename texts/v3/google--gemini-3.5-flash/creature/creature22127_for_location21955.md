To understand what lives in this basin, one must first discard the concept of the "beast." 

There is no blood here; there is only sap, pressurized to the threshold of hydraulics. There are no nerves; there are only fibers under such immense tension that they transmit information at the speed of a snapping violin string. The "citizens" of this self-assembling metropolis are not guests or squatters. They are the city itself, casting off its own limbs, nodes, and fluids to perform the work of its endless, frantic self-conception.

They are the moving parts of a machine that is perpetually rebuilding its own factory.

***

### The Knot-Runners (The Tensile Riggers)
Where the high-tension vine-bridges span the gulfs between the ironwood towers, you will see the *Knot-Runners*. 

They look like tangled, basketball-sized spheres of dry, yellow root-fiber, bristling with glassy needles. They have no legs, no eyes, and no mouths. They move through a process of violent, elastic thermal-contraction. 

A Knot-Runner is dry—bone-dry—on the sunward side, while its underside remains damp from the steam-vents of the deep streets. When the heat of the noon sun hits a Runner, the dry fibers on its top-side contract with sudden, explosive force, pulling the rest of its body toward the light. With a sharp, metallic *ping-clack*, the sphere leaps forward. 

They swarm by the thousands along the high suspension cables and the vertical fluting of the towers. They are the city’s weavers and surveyors. As they bounce and tumble across the structural joints, they drag behind them single, hair-thin guide-lines of high-tensile lignin. If a tower begins to lean too far under the weight of its own growth, the Knot-Runners descend upon it in a dry, rustling cloud, wrapping the joint in hundreds of miles of amber thread until the structure is braced against the basin’s seismic growth-shudders.

When their work is done, or when they grow too heavy with dry sap, they do not die. They simply stop moving. Their fibers fuse with the structure they were repairing, becoming a permanent, petrified knot in the wood—a decorative corbel on a tower they helped to save.

### The Pith-Wedges (The Decommissioners)
Crawl-bound to the interlocking root-plates of the wide plazas are the *Pith-Wedges*. 

These are the largest mobile structures in the basin, resembling the flat, segmented hulls of ancient ironclad ships, forty to sixty feet long, made of a light, porous, cork-like balsa. They have no extremities. Instead, their flat underbellies are covered in millions of microscopic, fiberglass-sharp cilia that vibrate in harmony with the deep, rhythmic *thud-thud* of the underground sap-valves. 

This vibration allows the massive Wedges to glide across the polished root-streets like hovercraft, emitting a dry, scraping hiss that can be heard even over the earsplitting shriek of the growing ironwood.

The Pith-Wedges are the clean-up crews of the city’s creative destruction. They are drawn to the chemical distress signals of starving, decommissioned towers. When a root-system cuts off the sap to a district, a Wedge will slide up to the base of the dying monument. From its front seam, it secretes a highly concentrated, volatile oil that smells intensely of turpentine and hot starch. This oil dissolves the crystalline lignin sheathing of the tower, reducing the dried wood to a brittle, chalky powder in minutes. 

The Wedge then drinks this powder through thousands of tiny, porous vents in its belly, passing the raw nutrients back down into the floor-roots through its own vibrating cilia. Where a tower stood an hour ago, there remains only a perfectly clean, polished plaza, ready for the next seed-burst.

### The Amber-Gells
Deep within the spatial distortions—those yawning, green-veined interior vaults that shouldn't fit inside the trunks that house them—the air pressure is high enough to liquefy gas. Here lives the closest thing the city has to a soft tissue: the *Amber-Gells*.

The Gells are semi-solid blobs of pressurized, highly metabolic resin, ranging in size from small droplets to gargantuan, slow-rolling spheres that could fill a cathedral. They are a deep, clear orange, glowing faintly from the bioluminescent phosphorus they absorb from the root conduits. 

They do not crawl or slide; they are pushed through the hollow interior columns by the massive hydraulic pressure of the main sap-veins. They act as mobile valves and logic gates. When a high-pressure sap-conduit is threatened with a blowout, an Amber-Gell is forced into the breach by the system's own counter-pressure. It wedges itself into the gap, hardened instantly by the dry heat of the outer air, sealing the leak with a glassy, amber plug.

Inside the quietest, deepest vaults, one can watch the Gells interact. When two Gells touch, they do not merge; they press against one another, their surfaces flattening into perfect geometric panes. Through these transparent interfaces, they exchange tiny, pulsing bubbles of nitrogen and concentrated enzymes—a chemical dialogue that dictates which districts of the city will grow next, and which will be left to dry and shatter.

***

### The Shard-Strutters (The Light-Born)
Where the fundamental physical laws of the basin fray, the line between botany and light begins to blur. In the plazas where the sunlight solidifies into rigid, green-glass rods, you will find the *Shard-Strutters*.

They do not belong to the kingdom of plants, nor are they organic. They are physical anomalies given form by the sheer, concentrated energy of the basin’s growth. They appear as slender, multi-legged prisms—like geometric spiders made of pale green ice—ranging from the size of a hand to the height of a pine tree.

The Strutters are born when one of the rigid, tangible light-beams is snapped by the violent growth of an ironwood tower. The shattered light does not vanish; it cracks into crystalline fragments that retain their physical density. These fragments, drawn to the high-frequency vibration of the Loom, crawl along the light-rods like insects on a branch.

They are entirely silent. They move with an unsettling, instantaneous speed, disappearing from one spot and appearing in another, leaving a brief, glowing trail of green static in the dry air. They do not eat or build; they climb the towers, scaling the outermost needles of the fiber-glass reeds, and position themselves like lightning rods. 

When the sun reaches its zenith, the Strutters focus the light passing through their bodies, projecting hyper-concentrated beams of pure solar energy deep into the shaded, interior plazas of the city, feeding the roots that the sun cannot reach.

### The Shadow-Casts
The most ghost-like inhabitants of this basin are the *Shadow-Casts*. 

When a massive tower is decommissioned and collapses into powder, its physical body is gone, but the intense heat of the sun and the "temporal stuttering" of the basin leaves a memory behind. For hours—sometimes days—after a structure has vanished, its perfect, dark, cool shadow remains cast upon the ground and the neighboring walls.

These are not mere optical illusions. The Shadow-Casts are physical pockets of absolute cold and stillness. If you step into a Shadow-Cast, the temperature drops by forty degrees in an instant. The air within them is dead and silent; even the roaring scream of the Loom is muffled to a distant, watery hum.

The city uses these lingering shadows as cooling fins. The floor-roots beneath a Shadow-Cast, protected from the baking sun, contract and rest, gathering the moisture that drains from the high bromeliad-nets above. Inside these dark, silent silhouettes, the temporal stuttering is at its strongest. A dropped seedpod, falling through a Shadow-Cast, will slow to a crawl, hovering in the cold, dark air like a dormant satellite, waiting for the shadow to finally dissolve before it hits the ground and begins its explosive, screaming growth.
