In the vast, vertical silence of the Great Soughing Lattice, life does not cling to the surface of the world; it is stitched into its very grain. Everything that moves, breathes, or creeps here is defined by two realities: the absolute, bone-snapping cold of the void, and the endless, vibrating iron-wood beneath their claws.

### The Lattice-Kin (The Stitchers of the Cold)

The people who inhabit the deep fissures of the Trunks are only distantly recognizable as humans. They are long-limbed and thin-chested, their bodies possessing a skeletal, spider-like grace optimized for three-dimensional movement. Their skin is not pink or brown, but a matte, greasy slate-gray, thick with a natural, sebaceous wax that prevents their perspiration from freezing into ice-splinters inside their flesh. 

They have no hair; instead, their heads and joints are covered in a dense, velvety down—similar to the wool-lichen that grows in the bark-furrows—which traps a thin layer of dead air against their skin. Their eyes are enormous, glossy black spheres with pupils that can dilate to cover the entire membrane. In the darkness of the tunnels, they see only in shades of amber and indigo, catching the faint, bioluminescent breath of the black mold and the low, dim burn of their tallow lamps.

Because the air is thin and freezing, the Kin do not speak with vocal cords. To whisper is to invite frozen condensation into the windpipe. Instead, they use two distinct languages:
*   **The Click-Talk:** A manual sign-language supplemented by sharp, rhythmic tongue-clicks that echo off the tunnel walls, which they use for close-quarters communication inside their hollows.
*   **The Great Thrum:** For long-distance communication, they use the wood itself. By pressing their bare foreheads against the grain of a Trunk, they can hear the rhythmic tap of a bone-headed hammer miles away. They have developed a complex telegraphic language of vibrations. An experienced *Grain-Hearer* can detect the approach of a sap-surge hours before it arrives, or interpret the frantic tapping of a neighboring settlement three valleys of void away.

The Kin wear suits made from the tanned, inner-fibers of the Hollow-Winged’s wing-membranes, greased with glider-fat to keep out the wind. They do not walk; they crawl, using hand-spades carved from the tooth-plates of the giant borers to clear rime-ice from their path, their feet terminating in double-jointed toes that can slip into the microscopic striations of the black wood.

### The Hollow-Winged (The Skeletal Gliders)

The most common creatures of the middle void are the *Hollow-Winged*. They are not birds, nor are they bats; they are something more resembling wind-dried kites made of bone and resin. Their bodies are completely hollow, their ribs extended into wide, lateral frames across which a paper-thin, translucent skin is stretched. This skin is not leather, but a dried, cellulose-based membrane that rustles like dry autumn leaves when they move.

They have no eyes. The front of their head is a flat, circular disc of bone, behind which lies a massive sonar organ. As they drift in the fifty-mile gulfs between the Trunks, they emit a constant, metallic *tack... tack... tack* that sounds like iron spikes falling on sheet metal. When the sound hits a distant Trunk, they adjust their wing-spars—each controlled by a complex system of internal pulleys and tendon-cords—to catch the rising thermals of the void.

The Hollow-Winged hunt the drifting colonies of *Float-Kelp*—spherical, gas-filled bladders of green-indigo vegetation that float aimlessly in the empty air, sustaining themselves on atmospheric moisture and the yellow pollen-clouds of the Thrum-Storms. When a glider finds a kelp-sphere, it wraps its long, multi-jointed legs around the bladder, puncturing it with its hollow beak to drink the sweet, fermented gas and liquid hidden inside.

When they die, their bodies do not decay. The cold is too absolute, the air too dry. Instead, they freeze-dry instantly, becoming light, stiff structures that the wind eventually casts onto the bark-shelves of the Trunks. For the Kin, these "stranding sites" are precious mines; they harvest the wing-spars for spear-shafts, the membrane for clothing, and the rich, yellow tallow from their oil-glands to feed their lamps.

### The Deep-Girth Borers

In the pitch-black heart of the Trunks, where the pressure is immense and the xylem is as hard as cold-rolled steel, live the *Augers*. 

These are the titans of the wood. The smallest are the size of a man’s thigh, but the grandest—the *Girth-Worms*—can be thirty feet in diameter and miles in length. They are segmented, cylindrical beasts covered in a thick, segmented plate of natural carbide. They have no limbs; they move by localized, high-frequency sonic vibration. 

An Auger does not "eat" wood in the traditional sense. Its mouth is a spinning dome of black, diamond-hard teeth that grinds the iron-hard fibers into a fine, sterile dust. As it grinds, the friction generates an intense, localized heat—up to several hundred degrees. The borer digests the sugars and resins freed by this heat, and excretes a dense, compacted "frass" that is rich in organic nutrients.

The heat of their passage creates the only warm microclimates in the entire lattice. In the wake of a Girth-Worm, the temperature inside the tunnel can remain warm enough for liquid water to stand for several months. The Kin follow the movements of the Augers with religious devotion. When a worm moves on, leaving behind a steaming, sulfurous tunnel, the Kin immediately move in, sealing the entrance with thick plugs of resin-soaked fiber to trap the heat and carving their homes directly into the freshly softened, fragrant walls.

### The Dust-Grazer Ecology

Where there is Auger-dust and warmth, there is the *Marrow-Mold*. This is a black, fungal-like growth that looks like clusters of woolly, dark-green velvet. It grows with astonishing speed in the damp, warm tunnels left by the borers, smelling strongly of yeast and wet pine.

Feeding on this mold are the *Glass-Lice*—tiny, six-legged insects with completely transparent shells. They are so numerous in the deeper tunnels that their collective movement sounds like dry rice sliding down a wooden chute. They are the primary food source for the *Lattice-Mice*, which are not rodents but soft-bodied, multi-legged creatures that look like a cross between a caterpillar and a shrew. The mice are blind, covered in fine, sensory hairs that can detect the movement of a glass-louse forty feet away through the vibration of the wooden floor.

### The Drift-Sails of the Outer Gulfs

In the widest gaps between the Trunks, where the wind never falls below a scream and the nearest vertical surface is a blue-black smudge on the horizon, lives the rarest and most mysterious organism of the lattice: the *Drift-Sail*.

These are colonial organisms that can grow to be the size of small villages. They have no skeleton, no limbs, and no face. They are essentially massive, multi-layered sheets of skin-like sailcloth, held open by natural, internal gas-vents that release pressurized hydrogen. They do not belong to any single Trunk; they live their entire lives in the perpetual fall of the void.

The Drift-Sails are pale, ghostly white, catching the distant starlight like sails on an empty ocean. They feed by trailing miles of sticky, hair-thin fibers—the *Ghost-Weaves*—behind them as they drift. These lines catch everything: drifting pollen, spores of the Marrow-Mold, stray Hollow-Winged that have lost their way in a storm, and the falling glaciers of rime-ice. 

Occasionally, a Drift-Sail will float too close to a Trunk. When its trailing lines snag on a giant splinter, the beast is anchored. If it cannot break free, it will slowly starve, its massive, sail-like body wrapping around the iron-wood like a shroud. The Kin view an anchored Drift-Sail as a miraculous harvest. They climb down the sheer cliffs of the bark with bone-hooks, carving away the dried, white skin of the beast, which they use to construct the sails of their long-distance gliders and the insulating linings of their most sacred, communal longhouses.

### The Thread-Weavers of the Bridges

Connecting the scattered human settlements across the terrifying gulfs of the void are the *Bridges*—miracles of primitive engineering. They are not static structures; they are alive.

The Kin construct them using the *Weave-Vine*, a parasitic, hair-thin vine that grows only on the windward side of the Trunks. The vine is incredibly strong, possessing a tensile strength that rivals steel wire, but it is entirely brittle unless kept damp with sap. The bridge-builders must constantly coat the suspension-cords with a slurry of sugar-liquid and soot, which they apply with broad brushes made of glider-hair.

Living on these bridges is a specialized species of spider known as the *Grip-Strider*. They are the size of a human hand, with long, needle-sharp legs that terminate in specialized clenches designed to lock onto the Weave-Vine. They do not spin webs to catch insects; instead, they spin dense, sticky cocoons around the bridge-cords, reinforcing the human construction in exchange for shelter. The Kin treat the spiders with great respect; to kill a Grip-Strider is to invite the bridge to snap, plunging the village’s hunters into the bottomless dark below.
