To live in the Sump-Cathedral is to be an extension of its machinery—to be forged, rather than born, of the heavy brine and the rusting iron. 

The life here is not soft. It has no use for eyes, or lungs, or the delicate membranes of the sunlit world. Everything that moves, clings, or drifts within this granite vault is made of the same materials as the ruins themselves: copper, iron, sulfur, and grease.

### The Brass-Backs (The Scrapers)
The horseshoe crabs mentioned in the old ledgers are not visitors to this drowned terminal; they are its perpetual cleaners. Over centuries of exposure to the hot, metallic soup, their biology has undergone a slow, molecular theft. 

They are heavy, three times the weight of their shallow-water ancestors. Their carapaces are no longer biological chitin, but a dense, biomineralized alloy—a dull, brass-orange armor grown from the iron and copper they scrape from the pipes. When they crawl over the vibrating scaffolds, they do not click; they *clink*, like heavy coins dropped onto an anvil. 

They have no eyes. In the perpetual twilight, they navigate by the vibration of the great piston. Their legs, tipped with hardened, chisel-like claws, are perfectly spaced to grip the corroded threads of the high-pressure water pipes. They crawl endlessly against the driving currents, using file-like mouthparts to rasp away the thick, orange mats of iron-oxidizing bacteria. 

Their life cycle is bound to the heat of the vents. When it is time to molt, a Brass-Back must seek out the superheated plumes of sulfurous water. The intense heat softens their metallic shells, allowing them to crawl out of their old, rusted casings. For a few hours, they are soft, pale, and vulnerable—looking like lumps of lard—before the acidic brine quickly precipitates a new layer of iron oxide over their skin, hardening them once more into the likeness of the pipes they groom. The empty shells they leave behind do not decay; they simply oxidize, eventually fusing to the ironwork to become part of the very rafters.

### The Bellows-Worms (The Resonance Dwellers)
Deep inside the flooded holding tanks and the hollow interior of the skeletal galleries, where the water is forced through narrow valves by the volcanic *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the subterranean piston, lives something that has outsourced its own heart.

These are the Bellows-Worms. They are long, segmented tubes of tough, leather-like hide, pale yellow and stained with grease, wedged tightly into the joints of the iron scaffolding. They have no muscles of their own for breathing or pumping blood. Instead, they are entirely dependent on the seismic pulse of the 300-year-old machine.

With every heavy stroke of the piston, the sudden spike in water pressure compresses the worm’s accordion-like body, forcing stale fluids out of its primitive gills. When the pressure drops between beats, the worm expands, drawing the hot, sulfur-rich brine deep into its digestive tract. They do not hunt; they are passive filters, feeding on the microscopic iron-sand and oil droplets carried by the pulse. If the great piston were ever to stop, the Bellows-Worms would suffocate in minutes, trapped in the silence of their own unmoving bodies.

### The Arc-Thieves (The Galvanic Limpets)
Wherever the frayed, copper-sheathed cables swing loose in the churning current, striking the iron pillars to release those sudden, ghostly blue-green flashes of electricity, the Arc-Thieves gather.

To the untrained eye, they look like nodules of dark, bubbled solder clinging to the wires. They are small, conical limpets with shells made of pure, conductive magnetite. They do not feed on organic matter; they feed on the electrical current itself. 

When a cable strikes a pillar, sending a violent charge through the water, the Arc-Thieves flare. Inside their translucent, muscular mantles, specialized organs absorb the electrical energy, converting it into chemical glucose through a process of bio-electrosynthesis. When the current sparks, the limpets flash in a sympathetic, neon-green rhythm, releasing tiny, hot bubbles of hydrogen gas. In the darkest corners of the Sump-Cathedral, these clusters of thieves pulse like dying stars, basking in the short-circuits of a dead civilization.

### The Grease-Gliders (The Sump-Slicks)
The largest residents of this pocket-realm are the Grease-Gliders, though they are rarely seen as distinct shapes. They are creatures of pure buoyancy, perfectly adapted to the hostile, unpredictable thermoclines of the chamber.

Looking like a cross between a manta ray and a mass of coagulated gelatin, the Glider is nearly invisible in the dark water. Its body is composed of a dense, oil-saturated tissue that matches the specific gravity of the heavy, warm brine. It does not swim so much as it "drifts with intent," using two broad, paper-thin fins to balance against the violent wash of the copper propellers.

The Glider's skin is constantly secreting a thick, slippery mucus that prevents it from being shredded by the sharp, suspended iron-sand or boiled by the sulfur jets. As it glides through the scaffolding, it drags behind it long, sticky streamers of this mucus. These "fishing lines" catch the heavy droplets of unrefined lubricating oil floating through the water. Once a streamer is fully coated in the mercury-like grease, the Glider reels it back in, consuming its own mucus along with the calorie-dense oil.

They are solitary, silent, and slow. In a world of deafening, metallic violence, they move like ghosts of grease and water, entirely unbothered by the crushing pressure or the roaring steam.

### The Rust-Gnats
In the plumes of the screaming sulfur valves, where the water is hot enough to melt human skin, the water appears to simmer with thick, red clouds. This is not silt, but clouds of Rust-Gnats—microscopic crustacean-like organisms that exist in their trillions.

They are the primary food source for almost everything else in the Sump-Cathedral. They have no legs, only feather-like antennae that vibrate in harmony with the high-pitched whistle of the escaping steam. Magnetotactic, they align themselves along the magnetic field lines generated by the rusted iron galleries, forming long, twisting ribbons of red dust that dance in the turbulence. When the blue-green sparks of the Arc-Thieves flare, the Gnats are drawn to the light, creating swirling, fiery vortices of copper-dust in the dark.

***

In the Sump-Cathedral, the boundary between the living and the mechanical has been worn away by three centuries of hot salt water. The creatures here do not merely inhabit the ruin; they are the ruin, animated by the heat of the earth and the dying, stubborn rhythm of the great piston. They are a biology born of the machine's decay, singing their silent, metallic songs in the warm, dark womb of the deep.
