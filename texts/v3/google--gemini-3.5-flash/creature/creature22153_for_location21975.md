To say this place is empty is to judge life by its speed. 

If you were to stand in one of the colossal, wet plazas, your ears ringing with the supersonic whine of the glass-like waterfalls, you would indeed see no beasts of burden, no nesting birds, and no scurrying insects. But this vertical city is far from dead. It is inhabited by an ecology of absolute preservation—an array of cold-blooded, slow-thinking, and mineral-bonded entities whose entire existence is dedicated to maintaining the eternal equilibrium of their mountain home.

They do not live *in* the city; they are the gears of its stasis.

***

### The Weft-Keepers (The Standing Ones)

In the wide, moss-slick plazas and at the centers of the great horizontal spans stand the closest things this world has to residents. They are *The Weft-Keepers*. 

At first glance, you would mistake them for monumental sculpture or architectural pillars. They are featureless, nine-foot-tall obelisks of polished, iron-hard root-timber, their bases seamlessly fused into the biological pavement. They have no faces, no limbs, and no joints. Instead, their surfaces are covered in a dense, velvety coat of the bruised-purple moss that carpets the city. 

The Weft-Keepers are alive, but their life is a matter of tension and pressure rather than motion. Under their mossy skins, they possess a complex network of capillary channels through which a thick, mineral-heavy sap circulates at an agonizingly slow pace—perhaps one complete cycle every seventy years. This sap is highly magnetic and structurally dense. 

By slowly shifting the density of this fluid within their bodies, the Weft-Keepers act as living counterweights. They are the stabilizers of the vertical world. When the immense weight of a suspended reservoir fluctuates, or when the basaltic towers experience a microscopic tectonic shift, the Weft-Keepers feel the change through the communal root-grid of the streets. In response, they harden or soften their own tissues, absorbing the stress of the mountain and preventing the colossal, self-woven spans from cracking. They are a silent, motionless senate, holding the city together by sheer, concentrated stubbornness.

To touch a Weft-Keeper is to feel a warmth that is not chemical, but the result of immense, perpetual friction. If you press your ear to their purple moss, beneath the roar of the waterfalls, you can hear them: a low, molecular groan of wood and mineral under millions of tons of pressure, singing a song of resistance.

***

### The Aureoles (The Knot-Weavers)

Within the hovering spheres of gravity-defying water that drift through the plazas, there exists a delicate, thread-like life form known as the *Aureoles*. 

They look like small, tangled skeins of spun gold or fine copper wire, floating in the exact geometric center of each water globe. They do not swim; they have no cilia or fins. Instead, they are pressure-sensitive organisms made of silica and flexible protein. They are perfectly balanced with the surface tension of their liquid homes, suspended in a state of perpetual buoyancy.

The Aureoles survive by absorbing the microscopic mineral dust carried by the warm, condensing mist. As a floating orb of water drifts through a plaza, its surface catches these mineral particles, and the Aureole at the center draws them inward through a series of ultra-fine, capillary currents. 

When two floating spheres of water collide and merge, a beautiful, silent ritual occurs. The two Aureoles within them touch. Their golden threads entangle, instantly exchanging chemical signatures and structural memories. Then, as the combined sphere drifts and eventually splits apart under the influence of the erratic local gravity, the threads separate, each retaining a piece of the other’s structure. They do not reproduce in the traditional sense; they simply update each other, sharing a single, collective memory of the city’s state that has survived since the first towers grew.

***

### The Dampers (The Acoustic Lichen)

The deafening roar of the supersonic waterfalls is a physical force in this valley, a vibration that could shatter glass and liquefy soft tissue over time. Yet, there are pockets of profound, eerie silence scattered throughout the vertical city. These are the homes of the *Dampers*.

Dampers are thick, charcoal-grey pads of an organic compound that resembles a cross between dry felt and porous volcanic pumice. They grow in perfect, concentric rings around the bases of the water pillars and along the edges of the high-velocity gorges. 

The Dampers are acoustic predators. They do not consume organic matter; they feed on the kinetic energy of sound. Their internal structure is a labyrinth of microscopic, crystalline chambers designed to trap and scatter acoustic waves. Within a yard of a Damper pad, the roaring, turbine-whine of the waterfalls is sucked away, replaced by an absolute, vacuum-like quiet. 

As they absorb the terrifying energy of the water's roar, the Dampers begin to glow. A deep, subcutaneous violet light pulses slowly beneath their charcoal surfaces. This absorbed energy is converted into a heavy, sweet-smelling oil that they seep from their margins. This oil behaves as a highly efficient lubricant and sealant, coating the basalt where the supersonic water meets the stone, preventing the rushing torrents from eroding the very foundations of the city. 

***

### The Glissando (The Slicks)

Because there is no decay, no leaf-fall, and no wind, the city is vulnerable to a different kind of threat: stagnation. Without some form of maintenance, the constant, mineral-heavy condensation would eventually build up into thick, choking crusts of lime and salt, burying the lush mosses and petrifying the waxy fronds. 

To prevent this, the *Glissando* slide through the streets.

The Glissando are sheets of living, transparent silica-gel. They look like vast, moving pools of melted glass, varying in size from three feet across to the span of a grand plaza. They move with agonizing slowness—perhaps an inch an hour—sliding over the vertical faces of the towers, across the horizontal arches, and down the spiral staircases.

As they move, they act as organic squeegees. They sweep up the excess mineral crusts, the microscopic basalt flakes, and the stagnant, over-condensed pools of water. Their bodies are highly acidic internally but perfectly neutral on the exterior; they dissolve the hard mineral deposits and convert them into a self-renewing, molecularly thin wax. 

Behind every passing Glissando, the emerald and purple mosses are left looking fresh, damp, and perfectly preserved, while the giant, waxy fronds are coated in a protective sheen that keeps them frozen in their eternal, stiff flare. The Glissando are the custodians of the stasis, the reasons why a leaf here remains precisely as it is down to the micrometer, never drying, never rotting, and never growing.

***

### The Core

If you were to descend into the deepest, dark canyons where the basalt towers meet the wet roots of the mountain, you would find that all of these disparate organisms are connected. 

The city is not a collection of individual species, but a single, massive, slow-burning metabolism. The Weft-Keepers, the Aureoles, the Dampers, and the Glissando are the specialized cells of a gargantuan biological clock. They have no desires, no fears, and no concept of time. They do not look forward to a tomorrow, because in this valley, tomorrow is identical to yesterday.

They are the curators of an eternal museum, and they are the museum itself—living, breathing, and roaring in the warm mist, waiting for nothing at all.
