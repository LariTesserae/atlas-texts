### The Human Elements: The Sieve-Hands

To live upon the Plinths is to exist in a state of perpetual, heavy damp. The humans who stake their lives here—known colloquially as **Sieve-Hands** or **Lead-Shoes**—do not look like people of the lowlands. They are broad-shouldered and thick-necked, their bodies shaped by the labor of fighting a current that never tires. 

#### Physiology and Appearance
A Sieve-Hand’s skin is pale, almost translucent from the lack of direct sunlight, and has a distinct leather-like toughness brought on by constant exposure to icy spray and wind. Many suffer from "spray-pock," a harmless but permanent scarring of the cheeks and nose caused by millions of high-velocity water droplets hitting the face during the Scurry.

Most striking is their silence. The roar of the High Sieve is so absolute that vocal chords are useless outside the thick, insulated walls of the granite blockhouses. Instead, the Sieve-Hands use **"Clack-cant,"** a complex, rapid sign language performed with heavy, grease-hardened leather gloves. To make themselves heard over short distances or inside the thunderous wheel-houses, they carry small bronze castanets strapped to their palms, using rhythmic clicks to signal danger, tension levels, or the approach of a surge.

They smell of coal-smoke, boiled cabbage, wet wool, and **Tallow-slick**—a heavy mutton-and-ray-fat grease they smear over their exposed skin to repel the cold water.

#### The Dress of the Slicks
On the stone, a human is a fragile, buoyant thing. To prevent being swept over the lip into the void, a Sieve-Hand’s standard outfit is a marvel of deadweight engineering:
*   **The Lead-Boots:** Triple-soled footwear with iron toe-caps and fifty pounds of sheet-lead sandwiched into the heels. The soles are studded with "grip-teeth"—short, blunt bronze spikes that bite into the wet black moss but do not spark against the basalt.
*   **The Slick-Suit:** A heavy, double-layered coat of cured cloud-skin ray hide, stitched with copper wire and greased weekly. It has no pockets or external flaps that could catch the current; it is perfectly streamlined, designed to let the water slide off the back like a seal’s pelt.
*   **The Safety-Tail:** A three-inch-thick hemp rope, armored with jointed iron rings to prevent fraying against the stone. Every Sieve-Hand is permanently tethered to the "Guide-Tracks"—heavy steel cables that run from the Cradle blockhouses out into the moss-gardens. To lose one’s tether is not merely dangerous; it is considered a form of slow suicide.

### The Grapple-Fauna (The Denizens of the Slicks)

Underneath the green-and-black canopy of the grip-weed, where the water is a cold, clear lens, there is a frantic, scrambling world that exists entirely in two dimensions. Nothing here swims freely; everything is designed to hold, anchor, or wedge.

```
                  ============================= (Water Surface)
                    ^    ^    ^    ^    ^
                    |    |    |    |    |  <-- Grip-weed Bladders
                    |    |    |    |    |      (tugging upward)
                  =============================
                      /    /    /    /
                     /    /    /    /   <-- Sieve-tails (shivering in current)
                    /    /    /    /
                  =============================
                  [##]   [##]   [##]   [##] <-- Plate-crabs (clinging to stalks)
                  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ <-- Black Moss Layer
                  ============================= (Basalt Floor - "The Slick")
```

#### The Sieve-tails (*Gyr-sieve*)
These fish are the silver arrows of the Plinths. They do not have scales; instead, they are covered in a thick, mucus-rich skin that reduces drag to near-zero. Their skeletons are not bone but a light, springy cartilage that can bend double without breaking when a surge hits.
*   **The Mouth-Sieve:** Their mouths are huge, rigid structures resembling the intake of a jet engine. They cannot close them. Instead, they position themselves directly into the flow, using their tails solely to maintain their station against the current. The water passes through their gills, which are lined with millions of fine, hair-like cilia that trap the microscopic green spores of the grip-weed.
*   **The Anchor-Fin:** On their bellies, sieve-tails possess a modified pelvic fin that has evolved into a dry-vac suction cup. If they grow tired, they drop to the basalt, seal themselves to the stone, and sleep while the ocean rushes over their backs.

#### The Plate-Crabs (*Plinth-ticks*)
Looking like flat, polygonal tiles of polished horn, these crustaceans are the janitors of the basalt. Their shells are perfectly hydrodynamic—flat on the bottom, gently sloped on the sides, and terminating in a sharp rear edge that prevents the current from getting under them and flipping them over.
*   **The Multi-Jointed Girdle:** They have twelve legs, arranged in a circular ring around their bodies. Each leg ends in a needle-sharp, tungsten-hard claw that can find purchase in the microscopic pores of the basalt. 
*   **The Grazing Herd:** They move in synchronized formations, thousands strong, clearing the black moss of detritus and dead grip-weed. When a Scurry starts, they do not run; they pull their legs beneath their shells, press their outer rim flat against the stone, and become, for all practical purposes, part of the basalt floor.

#### The Anchor-Ticks (*Slick-spitting larvae*)
The pest of the Cradles. These are small, thumb-sized barnacles that do not secrete lime shells. Instead, they drill directly into the basalt—or the iron pins of the Cradles—using a highly concentrated acidic saliva. Once anchored, they grow long, leathery hides that mimic the black moss. If a Cradle's maintenance crew does not regularly "scrape the pins" with heavy iron chisels, the anchor-ticks can weaken the foundations of a thirty-ton waterwheel in a matter of months.

### The Great Aerial Travelers: Cloud-Skins and Fall-Walkers

At the edges of the Plinths, where the horizontal world of the summits turns vertical and plunges into the mist, the fauna changes. Here, the water becomes air, and the air is so wet it might as well be water.

#### The Cloud-skin Rays (*Mist-Sails*)
These six-meter-wide giants are the undisputed lords of the High Sieve. Their bodies are pale, velvety, and covered in a thick layer of oily grease that prevents condensation from freezing on their skins in the high, cold drafts.
*   **Dual-Respiration:** Their gills are unique; they are lined with pocket-like alveoli that can extract oxygen from both highly aerated water and the dense, moisture-laden air of the Plinth chasms. 
*   **The Great Loop Migration:** They spend their days in the deep channels of the Slicks, feeding on schools of sieve-tails. When the sun dips behind the slate-grey clouds, they slide over the edge of the falls. They do not drop; they catch the violent, upward thermal drafts that howl between the Plinths, sailing from one monolith to another through the wet void. They nest on the sheer vertical faces of the basalt, gluing their leathery, purse-shaped eggs to the wet stone with a secretion that cures underwater.

#### The Fall-Walkers (*The Chasm-Lice*)
Deep in the mist-shrouded vertical gaps where light rarely reaches, live the Fall-Walkers. These are gargantuan, blind arachnids with legs that can stretch up to three meters. 
*   **Silt-Feeders:** They do not spin webs to catch air-prey. Instead, they spin vast, messy nets of sticky, yellow silk across the gap between two closely spaced Plinths. These nets catch the dead fish, severed grip-weed, and organic debris that pour constantly off the edges of the summits.
*   **Claw-Weavers:** They are the only creatures that can traverse the sheer vertical walls under the waterfalls. They move with an eerie, mechanical deliberation, their legs finding the ancient joint-lines in the basalt columns. To the Sieve-Hands on the cableway baskets, a Fall-Walker appearing out of the spray below is a common, if always unsettling, sight—their massive, glistening segments clinging to the wet stone just feet from the swinging iron cage.

### The Silent Neighbors: The Cradle-Born

Inside the granite blockhouses of the Cradles, life is lived in three-shift cycles defined by the rotation of the great waterwheels. 

```
   ========================= [THE CRADLE METROLOGY] =========================
   
             (High Sieve Air)             |         (Inside the Blockhouse)
   ---------------------------------------+----------------------------------
   The Roar................... 110 Decibels| The Hiss................... Steam Pipes
   The Water.................. 8° Celsius | The Lamp.................. Whale-Oil Wick
   The Motion................. Horizontal  | The Floor................. Slatted Oak
   The Danger................. The Scurry | The Solace................ Hot Fish Broth
```

#### The Children of the Wheel
Because of the immense weight and cost of transport via the cableways, many Sieve-Hands are born, live, and die within the same three-Plinth sector. The "Cradle-Born" have a distinct manner:
*   **The Static Stance:** They walk with a wide, heavy-set gait even indoors, their leg muscles permanently tensed as if expecting a sudden shift in the floor’s vibration. 
*   **The Goggle-Eyes:** After years of wearing thick, brass-rimmed goggles ("pock-glasses") to protect their eyes from the high-velocity spray, older Sieve-Hands often have circles of pale, unweathered skin around their eyes, giving them the look of owls.
*   **The Tallow-Taste:** Their diet is restricted to what can be harvested from the flow. They eat **"Slick-mash"** (a thick, gelatinous porridge made from boiled grip-weed bladders), salted plate-crab meat, and **"Sieve-oil"** (a yellow, fishy oil pressed from the seeds of the grip-weed). Everything they eat tastes slightly of mutton-tallow and river-silt, a flavor they find comforting and normal.

#### The Religion of the Loop
The Cradle-Born do not house traditional religions. The sky above them is always grey; the bottom below is always hidden. Their world is horizontal, wet, and circular. Thus, they worship **The Loop**.
*   **The Water-Soul:** They believe that all water is one living, mechanical entity that is perpetually climbing to heaven (as mist) and falling to earth (as the torrent). 
*   **The Return:** When a Sieve-Hand dies or is swept over the edge, their belongings are kept, but their body is not buried—there is no soil for graves. Instead, they are strapped to a weighted frame and slipped into the "Great Run"—the fastest-flowing channel on the Plinth. The crew gathers at the edge, clacking their castanets in a slow, rhythmic cadence. They believe the body will be ground to silt by the falls, swept into the updrafts, and return to the Plinths as the clean, cold rain of the next season. To them, a man who dies on the Plinths is never truly gone; he is simply back in the pipes.
