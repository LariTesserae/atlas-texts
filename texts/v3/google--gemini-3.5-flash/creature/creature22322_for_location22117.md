To look closely at this horizon-spanning metropolis is to realize that the distinction between the city, the creatures, and the ground has been completely worn away. They are not merely inhabitants of a place; they are the place itself, broken down into a trillion busy, specialized cells. 

### The Platesmiths (The Chitinous Street-Workers)

Along the sunken, lichen-paved lanes and up the sheer, ribbed faces of the clay obelisks move the most numerous of the basin’s children. They are the **Platesmiths**—six-legged, flat-bodied creatures whose carapaces are the same bone-white and concrete-grey as the mineral soil beneath them. 

They are not insects in the earthly sense, but look more like polished, terrestrial trilobites, or land-dwelling horseshoe crabs made of unglazed porcelain. Their shells are not made of typical chitin, but are heavily calcified, reinforced by the very minerals they harvest from the basin's floor. They have no eyes. The glaring white light of the sun-bright haze is a wash of formless warmth to them; instead, they navigate by an exquisite, map-like sense of touch. The tips of their six legs are ending not in claws, but in dense clusters of stiff, brush-like bristles that feel the microscopic vibrations of the clay towers and the varying textures of the lichen carpets.

The front of a Platesmith is dominated by a broad, shovel-like head-shield. Beneath this visor lies their most remarkable tool: a set of flat, rotary grinding plates and a pair of highly dexterous, three-jointed feeding palps. 

A Platesmith’s life is an endless, patient masonry. They do not sleep in cycles; instead, they enter brief, standing trances where they press their bellies against the cool clay of the towers, sharing their body heat with the structure. When active, they perform a constant, silent maintenance loop:
*   They scrape up the dry mineral salt that bubbles up through the cracks in the fresh crust.
*   They carry this dust in their under-belly pouches, mixing it inside their secondary stomachs with a thick, amber-colored resin they graze from the roots of the jade-vines.
*   With their trowel-heads, they regurgitate and smooth this mixture onto the tower walls, repairing the micro-fissures caused by the daily wind-tides. 

If you were to pick one up, it would feel heavy—like a smooth, warm river stone—and it would smell faintly of dried linseed oil and toasted flour.

### The Wick-Tenders (The Core-Hearts)

If the Platesmiths are the crust of the city, there is another, softer population that never sees the sun. Within the hollow, thirty-foot obelisks, the air is dark, pressurized, and thick with the scent of hot grease. This is the domain of the **Wick-Tenders**.

To understand the Wick-Tenders, one must understand how the towers breathe. Each obelisk is built around a central, vertical conduit. Suspended inside this conduit is a thick, fibrous cord of living tissue—a "wick"—which is actually the subterranean root of the jade-vines, modified by the collective design of the city. This wick sucks up a heavy, volatile oil from the deep, unweathered fractures in the world’s crust. 

The Wick-Tenders are pale, blind, and slow-moving. They resemble fat, segmented grubs made of ivory-colored wax, with rows of small, comb-like limbs along their sides. They cling to the central wicks of the towers. Their job is metabolic friction and filtration:
*   With their comb-limbs, they constantly groom the giant plant wicks, scraping away mineral scale and salt-crusts that would clog the capillary action of the oil.
*   By their collective squirming and rubbing against the fibrous core, they generate a gentle, steady heat. This heat warms the oil, causing it to vaporize and rise up the ventilation shafts.
*   This vaporized oil is what the towers "exhale" into the afternoon air, driving the continental convection currents.

The Wick-Tenders are coddled and protected by the Platesmiths, who carry food—the harvested seed-pods and kneaded spore-paste—deep into the ventilation shafts to feed these soft, blind engineers. They are the warm, wet engine room of an otherwise bone-dry city.

### The Silk-Spars (The Sky-Gliders)

High above the narrow lanes, sailing on the warm, sweet-greasy plumes exhaled by the chimneys, are the **Silk-Spars**. 

These are the four-winged gliders, creatures of pure skeletal elegance. They are roughly the size of large birds, but their bodies have no feathers, no fat, and very little meat. They are constructed of hollow, reed-like tubes of tanned cuticle, light as dried grass. Their four wings are vast, triangular sails of a translucent, amber-toned membrane that looks like oiled baking parchment.

The Silk-Spars do not flap their wings; they lack the heavy breast muscles for active flight. Instead, their wings are held rigid by a series of clicking "hinge-joints" along their thorax. They are biological kites. They launch themselves from the high, flared rims of the obelisk chimneys, catching the thermal updrafts with effortless grace.

The Silk-Spars are the eyes and the gardeners of the upper canopy:
*   Their eyes, unlike those of the ground-dwellers, are massive, multi-faceted spheres of amber chitin that can detect the microscopic drift of spores and the subtle color shifts of the jade-vines on the tower roofs.
*   They carry long, thread-like rear appendages—two trailing "tails" coated in a sticky, honey-like sap. 
*   As they glide through the swirling golden spore-devils in the afternoon, these sticky tails collect millions of the amber spores. 
*   When a Silk-Spar spots a tower roof where the jade-vines are thinning or yellowing, it swoops low, brushing its tails across the clay. The spores are deposited precisely where they are needed, cross-pollinating the vegetation and ensuring the continuous green-and-amber tapestry remains unbroken.

### The Jade-Vines and Parchment-Shrubs (The Green Mind)

In this city, the flora cannot be separated from the fauna; they are two halves of the same circle.

The **Jade-Vines** are not passive. Their leaves, which look like heavy, thick-cut plates of polished gemstone, are arranged in spiral patterns that act as solar collectors and wind-deflectors. The vines do not have soft, stringy roots; their roots are wood-hard, boring deep into the clay towers and actually serving as the structural "rebar" that keeps the thirty-foot clay obelisks from crumbling under their own weight.

But the vines’ most critical feature is their vascular system. In stead of water, they run on a clear, dense, non-evaporating oil. This oil is a complex chemical soup. When the Platesmiths prune the lower vines or harvest their pods, they ingest small amounts of this oil. The oil contains chemical markers—essentially "genetic blue-prints" or "work orders" from the parent plant:
*   If a tower is leaning slightly due to ground settling, the jade-vines on the high side will produce a bitter, resinous oil.
*   The Platesmiths, tasting this bitterness, are stimulated to gather more mineral paste and reinforce the base of that specific side.
*   If a sector of the city is growing too hot, the plants produce a sweet, watery sap that tells the Platesmiths to clear more ventilation holes at the bottom of the chimneys.

There is no "queen" or central brain in this metropolis. The intelligence is distributed. The Jade-Vines are the designers, recording the physical stresses of the environment in their oil-chemistry; the Platesmiths are the muscle, translating those chemical whispers into stone and cement.

### The Great Silence

When the daily wind-tide rises and the city enters its golden hour of spore-fall, the life here reaches its peak of coordination. 

The air is filled with millions of Silk-Spars, their dry, parchment wings catching the yellow light, casting flickering, amber shadows across the white lanes. Below them, a million Platesmiths work in absolute, rhythmic unison, their spade-legs creating a vast, vibrating chorus of *shuk-shuk-shuk* that rises from the pavement like a physical heat. 

It is a world without fear, without predators, and without competition. Every mouth is fed by the plants; every plant is built and watered by the animals. Beneath the wide, hazy sky, the city breathes its hot, sweet breath into the atmosphere, a colossal, self-repairing clockwork of clay and shell, turning forever in the quiet, young warmth of the world.
