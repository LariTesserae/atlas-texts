To live in the Inner Pith is to be shaped by a landscape that does not forgive moisture, nor does it offer any echo to the voice. 

The people who inhabit this cavern—the *Sieve-Born*, as they sometimes style themselves, though some travelers call them simply the *Chaff*—are creatures whose very bodies seem to have been turned on a lathe from the same dry, fibrous material as the world itself. 

### The Inhabitants: The Chaff

They are tall, slender, and seemingly weightless, moving with a stiff, deliberate grace. Their skin does not sweat; it is not smooth or oily, but has the texture of fine, dry birch-bark, silvered by age and clean of any moisture. In place of blood, their veins carry a pale, thin lipid—an organic oil that does not freeze or evaporate in the dry, cellar-cool air. They have no fat beneath their skin, only bundles of dry, corded muscle that look like hempen rope under a layer of parchment.

Because the air is a constant thief of moisture, their eyes are their most guarded feature. They are large, dark, and set deep within high, bony ridges of the skull, protected by double-shuttered lids. The outer lid is a transparent, amber-colored membrane that they draw across their eyes during the pollen-falling season, allowing them to see through the yellow clouds without the dry dust scratching their corneas. 

Their hands are marvels of dry adaptation. They have five fingers, but the joints are double-hinged, and the tips are not padded with soft whorls of flesh but are capped with hard, horn-like nails that resemble split hooves. These nails are growable tools: they are kept filed into sharp, flat chisels or hook-like claws, which the Chaff use to climb the hempen suspension cables and vertical splinter-cliffs with the effortless speed of spiders.

#### The Language of Touch and Fracture
In the immense, sound-absorbing silence of the heartwood, spoken language is a rare and difficult thing. A shout does not carry; it is choked by the porous wooden plains almost as soon as it leaves the throat. 

Consequently, the Chaff are a silent people. They communicate primarily through a complex, tactile dialect known as *The Grain*. When two citizens meet, they do not speak; instead, they press their foreheads together, or touch their long, chisel-tipped fingers to the hollow of the other’s collarbone. Through these bones, vibrations travel directly. They "whisper" by clicking their fingernails against each other’s wrists, sending rhythmic, telegraphic pulses through the skeletal system. To a stranger, a conversation between two Chaff looks like a silent, intimate dance of statues, accompanied only by the dry, rhythmic *tick-tick-tick* of fingernail against bone.

When they must signal across the vast, empty miles between the lacquered towers, they do not use bells or horns. Instead, they use "tension-drums"—immense, tightly wound coils of seasoned cedar-strip that, when struck with a heavy wooden mallet, send a low-frequency shudder running through the very floor of the vault. A citizen can feel this shudder through the soles of their feet miles away, translating the rhythmic vibrations of the heartwood into news of an approaching root-heave or a change in the amber-vein currents.

### Domestic Life and the Struggle for Water

The Chaff do not live in family units in the traditional sense, but in *Clans of the Shaft*, named after the rotating pine power-shafts that they maintain. Each clan occupies a cluster of three or four of the lacquered bamboo towers.

Inside these towers, there are no stairs. The interior is a hollow vertical cylinder, hung with a web of hemp ropes, swinging baskets, and climbing rungs. The Chaff sleep suspended in the air inside *cocoons*—tightly woven hammocks made of raw flax, lined with the soft, downy fluff gathered from the seed-pods of the high canopy miles above. They sleep sitting upright or dangling, their bodies entered into a state of shallow torpor where their breathing slows to a nearly imperceptible whisper, conserving what little moisture they possess.

#### The Water-Hunters
Water is the holy element in the Inner Pith. It does not exist in pools, wells, or clouds. The only water in this dry vault is that which is stolen from the tree itself. 

Deep within the heartwood floor are the *Sap-Tubes*—microscopic, high-pressure capillaries that run vertically from the deep, damp roots of the world to the sky-leaves. To tap these is the most dangerous work in the city. Special guilds of miners, known as *Siphoners*, descend into the deep fissures of the splinter-cliffs. Using long, hollow drills made of elder-stems, they bore into the living grain until they strike a wet vein. 

The pressure within these tubes is immense; if a siphoner is careless, the rising water will shoot out with the force of a geyser, splitting the wood, shattering their tools, and drowning them in seconds. The harvested water is collected in pressurized leather flasks and carried back to the towers, where it is meted out in drops. The Chaff do not drink water as humans do; they use it to moisten their pollen-mash, or they inhale it as steam in small, airtight cedar closets, allowing their dry skin to drink the vapor.

### The Fauna: Companions and Pests

The Chaff are not alone in the vault. A whole ecology of dry-adapted life bustles in the shadows of the silent machinery.

#### The Mahogany dRays (The "Kettles")
These are the large, multi-segmented beetles that crawl along the transport cables. They are the size of draft horses, their shells so dark and polished they resemble old violin wood. They have no eyes; instead, they have long, whip-like antennae that can feel the minute vibrations of the spinning drive-shafts from miles away. 

The Chaff have domesticated them, not with reins or bits, but with pheromones. By coating the hemp cables with specific distillates of bitter cedar-oil, they can guide the beetles along the high pathways. The "Kettles" are incredibly strong and slow, their legs locked into the rope-strands with natural hooks. They carry the heavy loads of the city—blocks of insulation cork, jars of amber lacquer, and bundles of raw flax—across the gaps between the towers, their heavy shells clicking rhythmically in the green light like clockwork toys.

#### The Parchment Gliders
In the open air between the towers, the *Gliders* drift. They are avian, but they have no feathers; their wings are vast, semi-translucent membranes of a substance resembling oiled velum, stretched over long, hollow needles of bone. 

They are silent hunters, drifting on the warm, dry updrafts generated by the thermal vents below. They feed on the *wood-borers*—blind, white grubs that tunnel through the heartwood floor. The Chaff revere the gliders as protectors of their architecture; a single wood-borer, if left unchecked, can honey-comb the foundation of a lacquer tower in a year, causing it to collapse under its own weight. 

To encourage the gliders, the Chaff build nesting-ledges on the outer walls of their towers, carving small niches where the birds can lay their leathery, wood-grain eggs.

#### The Velvet Mites
The pests of the city are the *Velvet Mites*—bright scarlet creatures the size of walnuts that crawl in millions through the industrial weavers and gear-boxes. They are drawn to the grease used to lubricate the wooden bearings. 

If they get into the spinning drive-shafts, their soft bodies are crushed into a thick, red paste that ruins the tension of the gears. It is the job of the children of the Chaff—the *Sifters*—to crawl through the narrow gantry spaces with long brushes made of stiff boar-bristle, sweeping the scarlet pests into copper bins to be boiled down into red dye.

### Death and the Glass Graves

In a world without moisture or soil fungi, there is no rot. When a citizen of the Chaff dies, they do not decay. Instead, their body begins to dry out even further, losing what little lipid-fluid it had left, until it becomes as light and stiff as a dried branch. 

Their funerals are beautiful, silent affairs. The dead are not buried; they are washed with cedar-oil, wrapped in a single sheet of fine, white flax-canvas, and carried by the *Siphoners* down to the slow-moving veins of amber latex. 

The family members lay the body onto the glacier-slow river of glass. Over the course of several days, the cold, sticky amber engulfs the white-wrapped form, sealing it away from the air forever. As the latex drifts into the deeper, colder channels, it hardens into solid, clear resin. 

Miles from the city, deep in the silent, uninhabited vaults of the lower world, the dead of the Chaff remain suspended in the glassy, yellow cliffs—perfectly preserved, looking like ancient, pale insects caught in the jewelry of the tree, frozen in their silent, dry sleep for ten thousand years.
