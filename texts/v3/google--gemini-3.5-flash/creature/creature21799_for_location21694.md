To survive in a world where gravity possesses a sticky, localized geography and water is a myth locked in deep crystal, life has long since discarded the wet, soft mechanics of flesh. The inhabitants of this silent grid are creatures of pure mineralogy, static, and geometry, perfectly suited to a landscape that is both an eternal ruin and a living, self-adjusting equation.

### The Copper-Wire Creepers and the Pellicles

At the lowest, most stationary tier of existence are the *Creepers*—not plants, but delicate, self-weaving filaments of native silver and red copper. They emerge from the microscopic hairline fractures in the slate towers, tracing the lines of crystal stress like slow, cold lightning frozen in the act of striking. 

They do not grow toward light; indeed, they seem indifferent to the diffuse, shadowless glow of the sky. Instead, they are *electro-tropic*. They extend their thread-like bodies toward the invisible lines of electrostatic tension that hang between the towers. When a dust-storm passes—even one millions of years dead, whose static charge still clings to the stone—the Creepers flicker. A traveler, if there were one, would see miles of vertical slate suddenly trace themselves in pale violet fire, a silent, flickering map of the tower’s internal stresses.

Where the Creepers gather in dense, hair-thin mats, they attract the *Pellicles*. These are ultra-thin, semi-transparent membranes of native silica, no thicker than a soap bubble, that grow flat against the slate faces. They are nearly invisible to the eye, detectable only by the faint, iridescent Newton's rings that shimmer across them when the twilight hits them at a sharp angle. 

The Pellicles are the light-eaters of this world. They absorb the ancient, stored glow leaking from the grey stone, processing it through slow, photo-chemical transitions that turn the silica from clear to a deep, smoky amber over centuries. When a tower "shears" and shifts its alignment, the sudden physical stress cracks the Pellicles. They shatter with a dry, glassy tinkling that is too high-pitched for human ears, releasing a faint, brief puff of ozone and a handful of perfectly dry, square flakes that drift down into the gravity-wells.

### The Platens

The most active inhabitants are the *Platens*, the silent gliders of the vertical empty spaces. 

A Platen is not a single animal, but a gravity-bound colony. It consists of dozens of thin, overlapping shingles of muscovite mica, arranged in a flat, diamond-shaped leaf that can measure anywhere from a few inches to several yards across. These shingles are not joined by skin or muscle; they are held together by the "sticky" localized gravity fields of the towers they inhabit. If you were to pull a Platen from its native gravity-slip, it would not bleed; its plates would simply slide apart, floating like scattered playing cards, only to slowly climb back toward one another once the external force subsided.

The Platens navigate the five-hundred-yard-wide avenues of empty air by sliding along the "seams" where the horizontal gravity fields of two adjacent towers meet. Here, where "down" is a shifting, ambiguous concept, the Platens tilt their mica shingles. By changing the angle of their plates by a fraction of a degree, they catch the local gravitational currents, sliding effortlessly up, down, or sideways along the towering slate faces without ever touching the stone.

They do not have eyes, mouths, or ears. Their entire existence is guided by *shingle-sense*—they feel the changing density of the gravity fields as a physical pressure against their plates. 

To feed, a Platen will glide flat against a slate wall where the Copper-Wire Creepers grow thickest. It presses its cold mica body against the metallic threads, creating a grounding circuit. The static electricity accumulated by the Creepers discharges into the Platen, lighting it from within with a soft, pulsing amethyst glow. Once sated, the Platen slides back into the void, its hunger satisfied for decades.

During the "shears," when the towers drift on their spatial tides, the Platens gather. In the zero-gravity zones between the shifting monoliths, hundreds of them will drift together, interlocking their shingles like a great, flat puzzle. They form rafts of living slate that remain perfectly still while the world shifts around them. When the gravity stabilizes, the raft peacefully disassembles, each Platen sliding away on its own new, rewritten current.

### The Camber-Beasts (The Calipers)

While the Platens rule the voids, the basalt bridges that span the chasms are occupied by the *Camber-Beasts*, or *Calipers*. 

These are massive, dense polyhedrons of dark, magnetic ironstone, resembling naturally faceted boulders of magnetite. They have no limbs. They move by an internal, liquid displacement. Deep within the honeycombed interior of each Caliper is a smooth-walled chamber containing a heavy puddle of native, liquid gallium-mercury alloy. 

The Calipers are highly sensitive to the structural stress of the basalt bridges they inhabit. When a bridge begins to bend or warp under the changing gravitational pull of the shifting towers, the stone undergoes tiny, piezoelectric changes. The Calipers are drawn to this stress. By tilting their internal liquid core, they shift their center of gravity, causing their heavy, multi-faceted bodies to roll with a slow, grinding *clack-clack-clack* along the bridges.

They roll until they reach the point of greatest tension, where their immense weight acts as a counterweight, stabilizing the bridge and preventing the ancient basalt from snapping. They are the unconscious, mechanical maintenance crew of the grid. A single Caliper may sit in one spot for three hundred years, its heavy iron body perfectly balancing a bridge over a three-mile drop, only to roll two feet to the left when a distant tower shifts its weight by an inch.

### The Lattice-Minds of the Voids

The most elusive "dwellers" of this world do not possess bodies at all. They are the *Lattice-Minds*—and they exist only in the moments of transition.

In the vast, zero-gravity zones between the towers, the suspended clouds of hexagonal dust-motes hang in frozen explosions. For thousands of years, these clouds remain completely motionless, holding their shapes in the dead air.

But when a "shear" occurs, a tremor of gravity ripples through the void. The dust-clouds do not merely scatter; they are forced into new, geometrically perfect patterns by the changing mathematics of the space. As they snap into their new positions with that soft, dry *shhh-t* sound, they form—for a fraction of a second—a massive, three-dimensional computing array.

The faint twilight leaking from the stone passes through these reorganized dust-lattices, refracting through millions of mica and shale particulates. This refraction is not random. It is a calculation. During the millisecond of the snap, the dust-cloud acts as a distributed brain, solving the complex gravitational equations of the shifting towers. 

The "thought" of the dust-cloud is the solution to the world's physical equilibrium. The output of this thought is a subtle shift in the air's electrostatic tension, which triggers the Creepers to grow, prompts the Calipers to roll to their new stress points, and tells the Platens which gravity-currents have opened.

Once the calculation is complete, the dust-motes freeze again into their new, inert geometry, waiting in absolute, cold silence for the next shift of the world to wake them.
