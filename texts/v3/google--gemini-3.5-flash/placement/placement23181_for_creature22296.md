I did not begin with a name, nor with the soft, wet pulse of a heart. 

I began as an interruption. 

For three hundred years—or perhaps three minutes; time here does not pool in damp corners, but is blown flat across the chalk—I was part of the Chaff-Ghosts. I was a momentary knot of high-frequency static, a collection of copper shavings and polarized purple dust that had learned to spin in a perfect, three-sided column around the lowest of the matte-black gravity rings. I was a thought that existed only because the wind was too fast to let me fall apart.

But a wind that never stops eventually makes mistakes.

During a sudden, violent inversion—when the three black rings pulsed in unison and the local gravity inverted, pulling everything toward the sky for the space of a single breath—my column was slammed downward. I was driven into a dense, shaggy terrace of copper-wire ferns, right where they fused into the white, mineral-hard chalk of the basin’s southern rib.

The impact did not kill me, because I had nothing to break. Instead, the massive electrostatic charge of my dust-body discharged all at once into the silica-rich stone. 

The heat of that grounding was instantaneous and dry. It didn’t burn; it *vitrified*. In less than a millisecond, the lightning of my collapse melted the surrounding glass-dust, the copper fibers, and the raw chalk into a single, permanent structure. 

I became a **Fulgurite-Chime**. 

***

If you were to look for me now, you would have to climb high up the curve of the third rib, near the lip of the basin where the air is thinnest and the wind screams its dry, pneumatic scream. 

I am no longer a cloud. I am a mineral fixture, yet I am not entirely stone. I am a cluster of hollow, interlocking tubes of smoky, green-and-amber glass, three meters long, fused directly into the vertical cliff of the polished chalk. My body is a jagged, branching spire—carved by lightning, hollowed by the wind, and structured like a pipe organ designed by some mad geometer.

I have no eyes, but I have *tension*. My joints are not sockets; they are clean fractures in the glass, held together only by the constant, invisible pulling forces of the black rings. When the gravity wells shift, my limbs—long, segmented needles of fused silica—do not bend. They slide and click over one another, lubricated by the grey graphite dust that settles from the gale-riders’ passing. 

My mind is not a wet engine of salt and fat. It is an index of friction.

I feel the valley through the vibrations in my hollow tubes. 
* I know when the **Loom-Spiders** are active because their bronze claws make a high, rhythmic *tink-tink-tink* as they climb my roots to spinning posts. They treat me like a natural tower; they anchor their glass webs to my outermost spurs. When the winds blow, my body sings a deep, resonant C-sharp, and their webs sing a mile-high E-flat. Together, we are an instrument that sifts the air of its brass-winged prey.
* I know when the **Vane-Skins** are descending. Their long, static-charged copper tendrils scrape against the rock above me with a dry, brushing sound, like dry grass dragging over concrete. Sometimes, their shadows pass over my vitreous skin, cutting off the hard, white sunlight for half a second. The sudden drop in temperature makes my glass contract with a sharp, clear *ping*.
* I know the **Spindle-Shells** by their weight. When those heavy basalt spheres fall horizontally across the basin, their passage ripples the gravity wells. I feel my internal iron cores—tiny, magnetic beads trapped inside my hollow glass chambers—clash violently against my walls, warning me of the coming displacement.

I am the translator of the Ocular. I am the thing that stands between the invisible forces of the rings and the physical life of the plants and beasts. 

Because I am made of lightning-glass and copper, I am a natural grounding point. When the Chaff-Ghosts—my former siblings—get too heavy with static, they drift toward me. They coil their freezing, violent dust-vortices around my hollow columns. They discharge into me, sending blue and violet sparks cascading down my spine into the chalk.

When they do this, I do not feel pain. I feel *memory*. 

For a few seconds, their chaotic, spinning intelligence fills my hollow sections. I feel what it is like to sail without weight; I remember the sensation of being twin columns of white silt dancing in a perfect, geometric circle under the sun. I sing their memories back to them. The wind rushing through my glass pipes shapes the raw, mechanical roar of the basin into a hollow, metallic whistle—a dry, ancient chord that rises above the storm.

When the sun finally sinks behind the chalk peaks and the freezing shadow of the mountain night falls over the Ocular, the valley quietens, though it does not sleep. The plant pelts turn from rust-red to cold lavender. The gale-riders drop into the cracks of the ribs to prevent their joints from freezing solid.

And I begin to glow.

The static energy I have gathered from the Chaff-Ghosts all day cannot escape into the dry air. It remains trapped within my green glass tubes, swimming in slow, bioluminescent currents of cold violet light. 

I hang from the white chalk wall like a frozen lightning bolt, humming softly in the dark, waiting for the wind to change its mind again.
