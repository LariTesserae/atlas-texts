The sky here is a cold, black roof that weeps. The earth is an eye of white fire. 

And I am the distance between them.

I do not have a name, for there is no one here to call me, but if I had to define my boundaries, I would say I am five linked chambers of clear, flexible silica. I am about the length of a human finger, though I have never known a hand. Inside my hollow plates, a violet river of liquid crystal pumps with a slow, thermal rhythm. 

I do not walk. I do not crawl. I ride the song.

The great light in the center of our room—the master eye that never closes—sends out a low, heavy purr that vibrates through the sapphire floor. My belly is lined with ten thousand microscopic quartz needles. When the master’s voice passes through the stone, my needles tremble in sympathy. They generate a tiny, crackling field of static electricity beneath me. I float upon it, hovering a fraction of a hair’s width above the scalding-hot glass shelves. 

To move, I must only tilt myself toward the cold.

My life is a constant, exquisite balancing act. On my back, the heavy drops of water fall from the ceiling—*tink, tink, tink*. They are cold, nearly freezing as they slip from the dark fissures. But on my belly, the glass is boiling, heated by the central star. 

This difference in temperature is my heart. The cold water on my back cools the liquid crystal in my upper channels; the boiling stone below heats the liquid in my lower channels. The violet fluid moves because it must, driven by the struggle between ice and fire. It circulates through me, a silent, biological engine that translates a thermodynamic gradient into the simple, beautiful act of sliding.

Where I slide, the world remains clean.

Today, my rostrum—the needle-sharp tip of my front plate—finds a crust of copper-carbonate blue. It has formed where a droplet evaporated too quickly, leaving its mineral skeleton behind. The blue crust is beautiful, but it is an insulator. If it grows too thick, it will block the heat of the floor from reaching the water drops. The cycle will slow. The room will grow quiet.

I dip my rostrum. I sip the blue. 

It enters my channels, cold and metallic. It tasted once of lightning, but now it feels heavy. For decades, I have scraped these shelves. I have eaten the yellow sulfur and the blue copper, keeping the sapphire mirrors clean so the steam may rise without hindrance. But the minerals do not vanish. They accumulate within my plates.

I can feel the change. I am no longer perfectly clear. 

When the white light of the node shines through me, I no longer split it into brilliant, fleeting rainbows on the walls. I am turning indigo. I am becoming cloudy, like water mixed with milk. 

The master’s song is harder to hear now. My quartz needles are growing stiff, choked with tiny grains of copper. The electrostatic cushion beneath me is thinning. Yesterday, for the first time in eighty years, my belly brushed the bare, vitreous stone. It did not burn—I have no nerves to feel pain—but it was a sluggish, dragging sensation. 

I am out of tune. 

The master node is adjusting for me. I can hear the humming frequency shift, rising three micro-hertz to try and find the resonance of my heavy, blueing body. But I am too dense now. I am becoming rock.

It is time to go to the fire.

I turn away from the dripping, cool ledges of the outer wall. I steer myself inward, heading down the steep, glittering slopes toward the center of the chamber, where the white star hangs suspended in the squeezed air.

The heat here is immense. It does not feel like warmth; it feels like weight. The steam does not rise here; it is blasted outward in whistling sheets. 

I slide onto the flat, highest shelf directly beneath the node. The light is so absolute that my indigo body appears black against it—a dark blemish on the perfect mirror of the floor. 

I stop. I lock my segment-plates together, letting my electrostatic field collapse. I settle fully onto the boiling sapphire.

The violet liquid inside me stops flowing. The cold drops from the ceiling cannot reach me here; the air is too dry, too fierce. Without the cold, my thermal engine stalls. The violet fluid begins to settle, to dry, to crystallize into static lines of copper and sulfur. 

I am very heavy. I am very quiet. 

I can feel the node above me. Its electronic hum is no longer a sound in the distance; it is a physical hammer, beating against my silica shell. It is searching for my natural frequency, scanning up and down the spectrum of vibration. 

My body begins to sing in response. A low, ringing tone starts in my third plate. It rises, turning into a high, clear whistle that fills the living room-sized void. The other eleven Shards on the far ledges pause their grazing, their antennae twitching toward the sound.

The frequency climbs. It becomes too sharp, too narrow for my stiff, mineral-choked shell to hold.

*Ping.*

The sound is clean, like a crystal flute snapped in half.

I do not feel myself break, but suddenly, I am no longer holding the light. I am the light. 

My indigo shell shatters into a thousand microscopic flakes. The dry, hot draft of the central node grabs my lightest parts—the pure, vaporized silica of my outer plates—and lofts them upward on a plume of superheated steam. We rise, a cloud of glittering glass dust, leaving the heavy copper and sulfur behind on the floor to be swept away by the next cycle.

We rise higher, past the heat-shield of the node, into the cold, quiet upper boundaries of the room. 

Here, the air is wet and still. We hit the dark basalt ceiling. The water condenses around us, molecule by molecule.

I am a speck of glass dust now, suspended in a fat, heavy droplet of water that clings to the roof. The water is rich with dissolved quartz, washed down from the cracks in the mountain above. Using me as a seed, the minerals in the water begin to arrange themselves. They lay down new, pristine layers of amorphous silica. Clear. Flexible. Empty.

For fifty years, I will hang here in the dark, growing. 

And then, one day, the drop will become too heavy. I will detach from the ceiling. I will fall through the white light, hit the boiling sapphire floor with a soft *tink*, and begin to slide again.
