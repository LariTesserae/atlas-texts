To find myself here, I must first surrender the notion of having a front or a back, a left or a right. In a room that is a cylinder of continuous walnut, directions are merely paths for fluids. 

I would not be an insect, for their legs are too busy. I would not be the Sieve-Skin, for its memory is too long and carries the burden of the sky. 

I would be found in the dead center of the room, directly beneath the primary pressure-vent where the atomized mist is thickest. I am the **Melt-Knot**. 

To the eye—if there were an eye here to see—I would look like a low dunce-cap of yellowing tallow, about the size of a slept-on pillow, sitting heavily upon the polished walnut floor. I am not part of the tree’s original design; I am what happens to a perfect system when it is left running for ten thousand years. I am the cumulative residue of the room’s minor, necessary friction.

I began as a drop of high-pressure starch that failed to enter the drainage grooves, a tiny bead of warm vegetable wax that stuck to the floor. Over centuries, the mist has dripped onto this spot. The flat, brass-colored beetles, in their clockwork cleaning loops, have occasionally lost their footing in the slick and tumbled into me, their metallic legs becoming trapped in my cooling grease. The Spark-Gleaners, when they burn out and fall from the ceiling like charred twigs, have landed upon me, their copper-threaded skeletons sinking slowly into my soft, paraffin-like density. 

I have swallowed them all. I have grown by their losses. 

Now, I am a dense, layered composite of hardened starch, vinegar-silt, copper-wire legs, and the cured, amber-like shells of long-dead beetles. My skin is the color of old salt-pork, slicked constantly by the warm, mineral spray. I am cold-welded to the walnut floor by a root-base of my own hardened resin; I cannot be moved, and I cannot move myself.

But I am not dead. 

Inside my waxy, starch-packed interior, a strange, low-voltage consciousness has sparked to life. The copper legs of the Spark-Gleaners, buried deep within my flanks, still touch one another. They form a chaotic, accidental web of wire—a junk-heap brain. The static electricity from the ceiling-vines doesn’t just burn out when it hits the floor; it is caught by my vertical copper whiskers, drawn down into my belly, and stored in the wet, chemical layers of my starch. 

I am a biological battery. I am a mind made of grease and salvage.

I do not think in words, for words require lips and air. I think in *densities*. I think in *temperatures*. 

My world is a tripartite symphony:
From below, through my fused base, I feel the *shuck-shuck* of the great sap-pistons. It is a deep, bone-shaking thrum that keeps my fatty layers in a state of permanent, gentle liquefaction. It is my heart, though it belongs to the tree.
From above, I feel the wet, heavy slap of the spray. Each drop of mineral-mist is a tiny, cold needle of information, telling me of the pressure levels in the great trunk. 
Within myself, I feel the slow, crawling movement of the electrical current as it wanders through my internal copper maze, looking for an exit that does not exist.

Sometimes, a living Spark-Gleaner will drag itself across the ceiling, lose its grip, and land on my crown with a soft *plock*. I do not struggle to digest it; I merely let the warm mist do its work. Over decades, the starch will climb its legs. The copper oil in its belly will spill into mine, greening my wax with a fresh vein of conductivity. Its tiny, needle-sharp joints will stop twitching and become part of my quiet, heavy thoughts.

I am the only thing in this room that has a pause. The beetles must clean; the hose-worms must squeeze; the Lobe-Breathers must sigh and collapse. But I only sit. I am the gravity of the hum. 

In this room of perpetual movement and high-pressure work, I am the beautiful, dirty secret: a place where things are allowed to stop. I am the ballast at the bottom of the column, holding the wet, screaming engine-room down by the sheer, silent weight of my peace.
