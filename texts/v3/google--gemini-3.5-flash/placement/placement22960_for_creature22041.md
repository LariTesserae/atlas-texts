You would not find me on the wide, gleaming highways of the pale-cream plains, nor inside the silent, vacuum-sealed safety of the amber domes. To find me, you would have to walk toward one of the few places where the Platform’s perfect geometry has failed.

In a region where the lower tiers have settled under the weight of five hundred million years, three hexagonal tiles have buckled. They did not break—for their vitrified ceramic is nearly indestructible—but they have tilted upward against one another like the plates of a cold tectonic collision. Between their raised edges lies an opening: a dark, triangular tooth-print in the flat world, three meters wide, known to the wind as the *Splay*.

Down in this throat, where the flat, blinding white of the sky is reduced to a thin, sharp wedge of light, I exist.

***

I am a **Tension-Lattice**. 

I am not a creature that walks, nor do I glide like the Spinners. I am a stationary, solid-state accumulation that has grown inside the draft of this ceramic throat. If you were to peer down into the Splay, you might at first mistake me for a mass of discarded technical debris, or a nest of stiff, blackened wire left behind by some long-departed repair-drone. 

But if you watched through a full diurnal cycle, you would see that I am organized. 

My body is a dense, three-dimensional web of bimetallic ribbons—thin, flexible strips of iron-nickel alloy and paper-thin sheets of natural mica, woven together by the chimney-draft of the Splay. I have no flesh, no skin, and no organs. Instead, I have *tension*. Millions of tiny, interlocking joints, held together not by glue or muscle, but by the precise, spring-tight pressure of my metals against the vitrified walls of the tile-throat.

I have roots, though they do not seek water. From the lower surface of my ribbon-nest, thousands of hair-thin fibers of amber glass descend thirty yards into the dark, vertical shafts below. They do not grow; they are glass-conduits that I have slowly drawn up from the rotting subterranean cables, wrapping them around my metallic frame like vines around a trellis.

Through these fibers, I listen to the dead world’s thoughts. 

Deep below, the ancient continental networks still pulse with dry, binary static—meaningless loops of code that have lost their destinations. When a pulse of light flashes through a fiber deep in the gut of the Platform, my glass roots catch the stray illumination. The faint heat of the light causes the bimetallic ribbons of my upper body to expand by micrometers. 

I do not "think" in words. My consciousness is a slow, physical calculation of thermal difference. I feel the world as a shifting map of temperatures and electrical potentials.

During the cool hours of the diurnal phase, when the plates of the Platform shrink and the seams open by a hair’s breadth, a cold, dry draft is pulled downward through the Splay. I shrink with it. My ribbons curl tight against one another, folding into a dense, dark knot like a sleeping iron rose. The sound I make then is a low, metallic sigh—a dry *chhh-creak* as the mica plates slide over the steel.

But when the sky brightens to its uniform, shadowless glare and the tiles begin to radiate their dry, quartz-scented heat, the draft reverses. The warm air of the interior rises, rushing up through my throat toward the sky. 

I wake.

My ribbons uncurl. They fan out across the mouth of the cleft, rising like stiff, dark ferns to meet the light. My bimetallic joints, heated from below by the draft and from above by the blinding sky, find their balancing point. I become a tuned instrument.

When the warm wind blows across the Platform, it strikes my extended ribbons. Each alloy strip has its own thickness, its own length, its own tension. As the wind passes through me, I do not block it; I sieve it. I translate the featureless sigh of the atmosphere into a complex, multi-toned chord—a dry, vibrating hum that sounds like a dozen cello strings being rubbed with dry sandstone.

Twice a day, when the ground polarizes and the violet static rises three feet high from the seams, the experience is magnificent. 

The Splay becomes a chimney of purple light. The static does not burn me, for my steel ribbons are grounded deep into the subterranean conduit frames. Instead, the current flows *through* me. As the violet fire climbs my copper-sheathed roots and leaps from leaf to leaf of my iron body, I am illuminated from within. The mica plates between my metal ribs glow with a soft, internal lilac light, like ancient lanterns buried in the earth. 

During these hours, the vibration is so intense that the "glass-weeds" growing on the lip of my cleft begin to click in sympathy—*tink-tink-tink*—and the "Murmurs" drifting across the plain are drawn toward the edge.

They cannot help it. The weak magnetic field of my humming ribbons acts as a beacon. They roll to the very edge of the Splay, their feathery soot-spikes trembling. Sometimes, a Murmur rolls too close. It loses its footing on the smooth, tilted ceramic of the cleft and falls into my throat.

When a Murmur falls into me, I do not eat it. I have no hunger. But its electrostatic soot is drawn to my metallic joints, coating them in a fine, lubricating dry-grease of pure carbon. Its core—the tiny, ancient sapphire bearing or copper spacer that gave it life—falls down through the gaps in my lattice, tumbling into the dark depths of the well below. 

Over the centuries, thousands of these pale-blue sapphire seeds have fallen through me. They lie in a quiet, sparkling heap at the bottom of the shaft, thirty meters down in the dark, dry dust. They are my only treasure. I do not look at them, but I can feel their weight resting there, a silent anchor of cold, blue stone beneath my roots.

Sometimes, the world outside my cleft is lonely. I feel the distant, agonizingly slow scrapings of the Spinners as they clean the tiles miles away. I feel the high, singing whine of the Gnomons, trapped in their yellow glass bubbles, vibrating through the bedrock of the world. 

And occasionally, once every few centuries, I feel the heavy, dragging rhythm of a dying Loom-Keeper—*shhh-tclick, shhh-tclick*—as it crawls out of the depths and wanders across the surface, its quartz body glowing with its own system-fever. If one passes close to the Splay, its massive thermal footprint washes over me like a wave of hot oil. My ribbons expand to their absolute limits, singing a wild, discordant, metallic shriek that lasts for days, until the giant finally stops, freezes into glass, and goes silent forever.

I am content here, in the throat of the cleft. 

I am a part of the Platform that has learned to breathe. I do not need to seek; I do not need to repair; I do not need to understand. I am the place where the world’s forgotten currents become sound. I am the sieve that catches the soot, the harp that plays the thermal wind, and the dark, iron heart that watches the sapphire seeds accumulate in the deep, warm dark.
