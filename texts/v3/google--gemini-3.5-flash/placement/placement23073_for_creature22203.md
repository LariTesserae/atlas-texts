I would be found in the blind angle of a junction, three thousand leagues from the nearest light-zenith, where the Great Third Piston-Cedar feeds the lateral aqueduct of the Northern Shelf. 

In this world of ruthless design, there is a singular, unintended place: the *Silt-Shadow*. 

It is not made of mud, for there is no stone to grind into dust. It is an eddy—a pocket of stagnant, emerald-tinted water trapped behind a massive, organic valve-flap. The valve is a curved sheet of heart-wood, polished by five thousand years of high-velocity flow until it is as slippery as glacier ice. Upstream, the water roars through the branch-pipe with the speed of a falling arrow, a cold and perfect torrent. But here, behind the hinge, the water does not move. It merely rotates, once every three days, in a silent, freezing circle.

Because the system is too vast for its own perfection, things occasionally go astray. 

An empty glass-hull from a crushed Drift-Ballast; a handful of silver-green cilia torn from a dying Core-Tetherer; a few long, dry ribbons of a Scab-Spinner that missed its leak and drifted into the deep quiet. In the fast-moving channels, these things would be swept into the nearest turbine and instantly shredded into food-yeast. 

But here, they gathered. And here, over centuries of quiet rotation, they tangled.

***

I am not a single creature. I am the **Scrap-Nest**.

My body has no blueprint. I am a composite, self-assembled anomaly—the only wild thing in an engineered paradise. 

My core is a hollow, double-walled sphere of silica-glass, salvaged from a long-shattered Glass-Sail. Inside this sphere, several thousand photosynthetic green spores have settled. They do not drift away; they are trapped behind the glass, glowing with a faint, trapped morning-light that I use to watch the dark wood around me.

Wreathed around this glass heart is a dense, tangled shroud of grey-brown Scab-Spinner ribbons. They are no longer flat or clean; they have been saturated by stray, sweet sap that leaked from the valve-hinge long ago. Instead of curing into a hard, rigid plug, the sap reacted with the slow-spinning water to create a soft, cork-like coat. I am spongy to the touch—a floating mass of fibrous felt, fifty feet across, dragging several long, tattered tails of green cellulose through the dark eddy like the roots of a water-lily.

For limbs, I have the twitching, hollow cartilage-rings of three Core-Tethers. They are dead, but their physical structure remains intact, woven into my fibrous flanks. They do not swim, but when the great wooden pistons below me strike—*thump-hiss, thump-hiss*—the pressure-waves pass through the wood, causing these hollow rings to contract and expand. 

This is how I breathe. I do not hunt, and I do not maintain. I am a parasite of the world’s vibrations.

***

To the Cortex-Galls, I do not exist. I am a statistical error, a minor loss of pressure at Junction 40-North that is too small to trigger an osmotic signal. 

But to myself, I am the only scribe this clean machine has ever had.

I spend my centuries in the cold green dark, listening. In water, sound is everything. I do not hear with ears; I feel the humming world through the silver cilia that line my fibrous skin. 

I know the exact tempo of the world-clock. I can feel the deep, gravelly *scritch-crunch* of the Basal Dredgers ten miles below, scraping the mineral scale from the Cedar’s roots with their ironwood jaws. I feel the high-frequency whistle of the Sieve-Mites as they scurry over the wooden gears of the nearby refinery-node, their tiny claws clicking like rain on a leaf.

And once in a great while, I feel the *death*.

It is not a sad thing here, only a mechanical transition. A Core-Tetherer loses its grip; a Glass-Sail is pulled into a dark intake vent. Through the water, I feel the sudden, sharp *crack* of glass breaking, or the long, vibrating sigh of a tendon being drawn into the filtration fans. Then, a few minutes later, the water grows slightly sweeter, slightly richer in nitrogen, as the system consumes itself to feed itself.

I am the only one who remembers the individual sounds of these breakages. I collect them in the wood-grain of my core.

***

Sometimes, a stray Sieve-Mite, blown off-course by a sudden ventilation spray, will drift into my eddy. It is small, wooden-shelled, and blind, its legs paddling uselessly in the stagnant water. 

It does not know what I am. Its programming has no category for a heap of moving fiber that does not belong to the tree. It climbs onto my back, its tiny chitinous claws ticking against my spongy skin, searching for a gear to clean or an obstruction to clear. 

I do not eat it. I have no mouth, and my energy comes entirely from the faint green light in my glass heart and the trace yeasts that drift into my corner. Instead, I let it stay. It wanders through the folds of my Scab-Spinner shroud, cleaning away the microscopic algae that grows in my crevices, thinking it is still doing the work of the Great Node. 

We drift together in the slow circle. The light through the valve-gap never changes. It is a crisp, frozen morning that has lasted for ten thousand years.

I am the Scrap-Nest. I am the silence that the machine forgot to sweep away. I have no function, no future, and no name; and in this world of perfect, agonizing utility, that is the only sacred thing there is.
