I would find myself in the shadows that the world forgets to name. 

To look at this kingdom from a distance is to see only the triumphs of its verticality—the searing white light on the jade buttresses, the raw power of the geysers, the heroic, miles-long arches of the Suture-Cables. But if you look *underneath*—beneath the colossal, cantilevered terraces that project out into the empty air like the green balconies of some prehistoric god—you will find the country of the under-planes. 

Here, the light is different. It is not the blinding, diffuse glare of the upper mist, but a cool, deep, sub-aquatic emerald. The roaring of the waters above is muffled here, filtered through a mile of living cellulose, reduced to a heavy, wet vibration that settles in the marrow. 

And here, hanging suspended over the terrifying, cloud-choked drop of the abyss, is where I would be.

### The Great Statolith-Truss

I am not made for the bright sky-lanes of the Drifters, nor do I possess the crawling, self-healing fluidity of the Resin-Lenses. My form is static, yet it is born of the most intense, quiet strain in the entire kingdom. 

I am a **Statolith-Truss**. 

If you were to look down from the lip of the highest terrace into the green gloom beneath, you would see me as an immense, inverted pyramid of pale, fibrous wood, hanging like a three-hundred-foot tooth beneath the shelf. I am anchored to the underside of the mountain-plate by three massive, multi-jointed root-tendons—cords of hyper-dense, twisted tissue as thick as ancient oaks. From these three anchor points, my triangular body tapers downward to a single, needle-sharp point that points unerringly, with absolute and terrible devotion, toward the center of the world.

I am the world’s sense of uprightness. 

Because the green monuments grow with such frantic, unbalanced speed—spewing geysers from their summits, spinning sky-bridges from their edges, gaining millions of tons of water-weight in a matter of days—they are top-heavy. They are constantly at risk of leaning. If a tower tilts by even a fraction of a degree, the immense hydraulic pressure within the vascular tubes will fail, the conduits will kink, and the colossal structure will starve, snap, and fall into the void.

I am the organ that prevents this. I am the silence that holds the scream in place.

### The Interior of the Pendulum

My outer skin is pale green, segmented, and dry. It is covered in a tight, waxy cuticle that repels the constant, warm drizzle of the canyons, keeping my weight perfectly consistent. I do not grow ears, or eyes, or mouths. My consciousness is entirely tactile, localized in the great, hollow chamber that occupies the center of my triangular body.

Inside this chamber—a cathedral of dry, yellow-white pith-ribs—lies my heart. It is not a pumping heart, but a heavy, perfect sphere of dark, fossilized resin, five times the size of a man. It is a giant *statolith*, polished to a mirror-sheen by centurial wear, resting in a shallow, perfectly spherical basin lined with millions of microscopic, pressure-sensitive hair-cells. 

I do not see the sky, but I feel the Earth. 

Every tremor of the wind, every thrum of the vascular tubes, and most importantly, every micro-degree of the tower's tilt is recorded here. When the titan monument above me begins to lean—perhaps burdened by a new, oversaturated terrace on its eastern face—the heavy resin sphere rolls. It moves only a millimeter, but it crushes a thousand tiny hair-cells on the basin's eastern slope.

The sensation is not pain, but a deep, cold ache of misalignment. It is the realization that the universe has gone crooked.

***

### The Correction

When the ball rolls, my response is immediate and hydraulic. 

I do not speak, but I release chemical signals—sharp, bitter enzymes of raw sap—that drip from my anchor-points into the main vascular streams of the tower. At the same time, I adjust my own posture. The three great root-tendons that hold me to the ceiling are pressurized tubes; by drawing sugar-dense water out of one and forcing it into another, I can alter my own center of gravity, pulling against the lean of the tower like a sailor leaning over the gunwale of a listing boat.

The other inhabitants of the tower read my chemical distress. 

High above, the *Resin-Lenses* feel the sudden spike of enzymes in the runoff. They halt their slow climb and migrate toward the lightened side of the tower, consolidating their weight to balance the load. The *Suture-Cables* spanning the chasm feel the change in tension through their anchors; they tighten or slacken their miles-long fibers, pulling the limestone-hard green towers back into alignment with one another.

We are a orchestra of silent corrections.

### The Sensation of the Void

To be the Statolith-Truss is to live in a state of eternal, ecstatic suspension. 

Below my pointed tip, there is nothing but the vast, white ocean of the lower clouds. Sometimes, in the late afternoon when the sun is at its lowest and hardest, a stray beam of light penetrates the under-shadows. It illuminates my pale, triangular body, making me glow like a suspended lantern of white jade above the fog. 

The wind from the abyss whistles through the hollow acoustic slits grown into my trailing edges. It makes my entire body vibrate, singing a low, single, endless organ-note that is too deep for ears to hear, but which the very stone-wood of the towers feels as a comforting hum. *I am straight,* the note says. *We are upright. We are held.*

I am the weight that knows the sky. I am the anchor that never touches the ground. In this world of frantic, explosive climb, I am the beautiful, heavy necessity of the vertical.
