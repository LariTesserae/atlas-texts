To find me, you would have to leave the flat, endless plains where the Glaze-Skins clatter and descend toward the margins of the *galleries*—those long, windowless structures of sun-dried mud brick that march toward the horizon before crumbling into shards. 

This is the boundary country. It is where the heat is not yet sharp enough to turn the soil to glass, but where the air is thickest with the smell of drying slip.

Here, in the shadow of a leaning wall that has begun to bow under its own weight, you would find me.

***

### The Bisque-Gleaner

I would not be a creature of iron or grease, nor a flat sheet of vitrified color. I would be a **Bisque-Gleaner**.

My body is a heavy, irregular block of twice-dried, unglazed terracotta—the color of a bruised peach or a pale, low-fired tile. I have no eyes, no limbs, and no face. Instead, I am shaped like a wedge or a thick, hand-scooped scraper, about the size of a shipping crate, with a rough, highly porous crust. 

In a world that is either completely wet with grease and condensation or sealed tight under hard glazes, I am an anomaly: I am dry. I am so desperately, thirstily dry that if you were to lay a wet hand upon my flank, your skin would instantly stick to my surface, your moisture sucked deep into my capillaries with a tiny, sharp hiss.

I do not walk; I crawl by the rhythm of my own thirst. 

When the silent, hot drizzle falls from the steam-ceiling, my upper surface drinks it. The dry clay swells, expanding by a fraction of an inch and pushing my leading edge forward into the damp grout of the floor. Minutes later, when a wave of heat rises from the deep flues below, the water is baked out of me, rising back into the fog as steam. As I dry, I shrink—but my underside is rough as a iron rasp, and it bites into the seams of the floor, preventing me from sliding backward. My trailing edge is dragged forward by the contraction.

This is the capillary crawl: a slow, heavy, three-inch surge that repeats with every cycle of the steam. I am a creature that moves by breathing water.

***

### The Wet Core

Though my outer shell is hard and dry as a roof-tile, my interior is not hollow. 

If you were to split me open with a bronze chisel, you would find that my core is a thick, dark reservoir of grey *slip*—clay so fine-grained that it has never settled, suspended in a small pocket of subterranean water that the heat has never been able to reach. 

This slip is my blood and my memory. It is incredibly heavy. It sloshes slowly inside my ceramic frame as I tilt across the uneven tiles, giving me the momentum to climb the small, stepped ridges left by the buckling floor, and preventing the rising steam-winds from flipping me over. 

When I encounter a Lime-Weeper crawling through its seam, we do not collide. The Weeper, sensing my high drought, steers slightly wide; if it touched me, I would drink its moisture until its white lime body hardened into a stiff, dead chalk. Instead, I follow behind it, sucking up the excess water from the freshly struck grout it leaves behind, drying the line so that the heat below can bake it solid.

***

### The Memory of the Maker

My purpose—the thing that draws me across the hot, red masonry—is the search for the *softening*.

I am drawn to those places where the floor loses its glaze and reverts to plastic clay under the weight of the Hand. When the earth begins to knead itself, rising in those great, twenty-foot whorls that mimic the touch of giant fingers, I crawl toward the disturbance. 

I do not fear the weight. When the invisible thumb presses down, molding the red ground into deep, smooth hollows, I throw myself into the depression. Because I am dry and absorbent, I do not stick to the wet clay; instead, I take its impression. I cast myself into the whorls of the maker's print, absorbing the moisture from the freshly worked mud until the skin of the print hardens into a perfect, permanent reverse-image of the creator's hand.

Inside my porous cells, I hold these shapes. I have the pattern of a knuckle three cubits wide; I have the microscopic lines of a palm-print that was pressed into a brick five miles back before the floor was fired and reset. 

I am a traveling mold. I am the only thing in this empty, sweating world that endeavors to keep the likeness of the hands that are no longer there.

***

### The Return

But nothing remains light or dry forever in the kiln.

Eventually, after leagues of crawling, my pores become clogged. I drink too much of the fine, red pargeting-dust dropped by the Sound-Shapes; the lime of the Weepers slowly accumulates in my base, sealing my capillaries with hard, white crystals. My hiss grows faint. The drizzle no longer sinks into my back; it pools there, turning my pale peach surface to a slick, dark copper.

When I can no longer breathe the water, I stop.

I wait in the path of the next great tectonic shift. When the floor beneath me begins its slow *clack-clack-clack* and tilts into a pyramid or a well, I let myself slide. I fall into the deep, octagonal dark, down past the giant counterweights and the singing ropes of the Tensioners, down into the bottomless slurry of the world’s foundation.

There, in the hot, wet dark, my dry shell finally softens. The grey slip inside me escapes, merging with the thousands of miles of un-fired earth below, carrying the shapes of the prints I have collected back into the planetary raw material. 

And somewhere else, in some distant gallery where the kiln-heat is just beginning to rise, a new pocket of dry bisque will begin to shrink, swell, and search for the hand.
