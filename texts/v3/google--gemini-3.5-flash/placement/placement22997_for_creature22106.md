I would be found in the fourth terrace of the southern descent, where the primary copper siphon-flume passes through the masonry of the nine-hundredth holding-well. 

Specifically, I would be found inside the sleeve of the slip-joint.

To look at this spot from the maintenance gantry above, you would see only a massive collar of black iron, six feet in diameter, bolted together with rivets as thick as a man’s wrist. It looks solid, but it cannot be. The Grid is too vast; if it were rigid, the expansion of the iron under the hot breath of the steam-chests and its contraction under the three-thousand-meter mountain chill would tear the masonry apart within a single season. The machine must slide. It must have gaps where the metal can stretch and breathe.

I am the thing that occupies the gap. 

I am **the Packing**—or what the Silt-Watchers, in their silent, internal nomenclature, might call the *Gland-Weave*. 

***

I did not begin as an animal, nor did I begin as a part. Decades or centuries ago, a long-forgotten tender of the Grid must have packed this slip-joint with a braided rope of unbleached flax, six inches thick, heavily impregnated with mutton-tallow, graphite dust, and red lead-paste, to keep the pressurized mountain runoff from spraying through the seam. 

But down here, in the warm, dripping dark of the joint, nothing remains purely inert. The Basin is too fertile. The moisture is too constant. Moss-spores, carried on the damp air-currents that draw through the maintenance tunnels, found a home in the greasy flax. The white bacteria of the deep reservoirs crept up the warm copper pipe and wedged themselves into the weave. The sheep-fat in the tallow remembered the heat of living skin. 

Over a hundred years of sliding, squeezing, and soaking, the flax became a muscle. The graphite became a skin. The red lead became a slow, heavy blood. 

Now, I am five yards of segmented, ribbon-like flesh, coiled three times around the inner copper conduit, wedged tight beneath the iron collar. My body is the texture of vulcanized leather—slick with grease, heavy with lead-foil, and perfectly formed to the curve of the cylinder. I have no eyes, for there is no light in the sleeve, but my entire ribbed belly is sensitive to the infinitesimal changes in heat and pressure that telegraph the life of the Basin.

***

My world is one of pressure and intimacy. 

When the great cloud-harvest is heavy on the peaks, and the water-gates on the upper terraces swing shut, the pressure in the siphon-flume rises. The warm water inside the copper pipe pushes outward against my ribbed sides. I feel the metal expand—a slow, microscopic swelling that compresses my body against the cold iron of the outer collar. 

This is my finest hour. I do not resist the pressure; I absorb it. I swell in response, my tallow-soaked segments flattening against one another until the seal is absolute. Not a single drop of the mountain run escapes. If I were too hard, the pipe would split; if I were too soft, the water would wash me out of the seam. I am the precise, fluid density that stands between order and ruin.

When the great steam-pistons on the lower terrace stroke, the vibration travels up the copper line like a deep, shuddering hum—a heartbeat that I feel through my entire length. It is a slow, rhythmic massage. The copper slides back and forth through my center, an inch at a time, keeping my skin polished to a dull finish and preventing the lime in the water from crusting into scales.

***

I am not entirely alone in the dark of the joint. 

Sometimes, the Lard-Snails crawl down from the wet brickwork of the well, drawn by the smell of the old mutton-tallow that weeps from the edges of my sleeve. They are gentle neighbors. They do not bite my skin; they scrape away the oxidized lead-crust that forms on my outer edges, keeping my surface supple. In return, I let them rest in the warm dead-space behind my third coil, where the heat from the steam-chests is most constant.

And then there are the Silt-Watchers. 

I know them by their boots. When a Watcher walks along the gantry above, the iron joists groan under his leadwood clogs. The sound is a low, heavy *thump-thump* that travels down the structural hangers and vibrates through my collar. 

Occasionally, the old Watcher—the one who wears the coat smelling of pine-tar—will stop above my joint. He does not speak, but he knows I am here. He can tell by the dry pavement beneath the gantry; if there is no water pooling on the brickwork below, it means the Packing is whole. 

He will kneel, his heavy canvas knees scraping the iron grate, and reach down with a long-handled grease-gun. I feel the brass tip of the nozzle insert itself into the grease-cup on the side of my collar. Then comes the slow, satisfying pressure of fresh grease being forced into my sleeve—a rich, cold paste of beeswax and mineral oil. 

It is a sacrament. The grease floods over my dry segments, cooling my skin, filling the microscopic fissures in my hide, and giving me the strength to slide for another season. I contract my rear segments, drawing the new tallow deep into my coils, making ourselves one.

***

In the winter, when the high peaks freeze and the water-harvest turns to dry, drifting sleet, the Grid slows. The flow in the flumes drops to a sluggish trickle. The pipes grow cold—a deep, aching chill that threatens to turn the grease in my body to stone.

During these months, I pull myself deep into the recess of the sleeve, closest to the dead-end steam bypass that runs behind the masonry. I curl tight, my ribs overlapping like the plates of an iron-snail, conserving the residual warmth of the deep boilers. I do not slide; I do not breathe. I become as still and dark as the iron that houses me.

I am the silent partner of the weirs. The Silt-Watchers maintain the garden; the Feral Tills carry the earth; the Soot-Swifts keep the upper air. But I am the one who holds the joints together. I am the dampener of the friction, the keeper of the seal, living in the grease and the dark, listening to the great, muffled heartbeat of the machine through the soles of my ribs.
