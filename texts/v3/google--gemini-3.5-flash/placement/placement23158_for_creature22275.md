I would not be found on the floor, nor would I hang from the vaults. To exist in this world as a spectator is impossible; there is no space that is not already occupied by the work of the engine. 

I would find myself inside the very pillars of the world, nested within one of the fluted columns of ironwood that support the infinite ceiling. 

I would be a **Cylinder-Core**—one of the colossal, bulbous pistons that sit sealed inside the woody sleeves of the great columns.

***

### The Shape of Stillness

I do not have a body that ends in a neat boundary. I am sixty feet of solid, self-condensing heartwood, shaped like a massive, multi-tiered plug. My outermost layer is not soft bark, but a sequence of concentric, precision-grown rings of lignified fiber, polished by centuries of microscopic friction until my sides are as smooth as wet slate. 

I am suspended twenty feet above the wet floor, held within a vertical chamber of hollowed, iron-hard canal-wood. I do not touch the light. I do not hear the dripping of the wells with ears; instead, I feel the water as a sequence of sharp, cold impacts against the base of my cylinder, transmitted through the column of high-pressure liquid beneath me.

I am the barrier between two worlds. 

Beneath me, in the dark chamber of the sleeve, is the raw, unrefined copper-water, forced upward from the sump-wells by the weight of the continent. Above me, pressing down on my crown, is the immense, dead-weight torque of the ceiling-vaults, stabilized by the *Cordage-Marrow*. I am the valve that negotiates their truce.

My life is measured not in breaths, but in tolerances. Between my waxy, slate-smooth sides and the inner wall of my wooden sleeve is an interval of less than a millimeter. This is the slip-plane. It is filled with a pressurized film of raw, golden turpentine and heavy starch-oil. If this film were to fail, the wood of my body would seize against the wood of the sleeve, and the column would shatter under the unrelieved pressure of the ceiling. 

And so, my entire metabolism is dedicated to the lubrication of my perimeter.

***

### The Sensation of the Grind

I am not motionless, though to an observer I would appear as still as the columns around me. I am always moving. My movement is a microscopic, downward creep—perhaps a third of an inch every forty years—as the wood above me compresses under some distant shift in the vault’s alignment. 

When I move, there is no sound, but there is a deep, internal heat. The friction of my descent warms the raw turpentine in the slip-plane, volatilizing it, sending a thick, hot scent of pine-resin and sweet sap curling through the small bypass-valves in my flanks. 

Inside my heartwood, I am perfectly dry. At my very center lies a core of ancient, pitch-black wood that has never known the touch of water. It is my ballast. It is the repository of my silence. Surrounding this dry core are the growth-rings—not rings of years, but of *pressures*. 

I can read my own history by turning my attention inward, feeling the density of each ring. Here is a three-century span of iron-dense wood, grown during an epoch when the vaults above were dry and heavy. Here is a wider, softer ring of porous xylem, grown when the copper-water rose high and tried to lift me from my sleeve.

***

### My Companions in the Dark

I do not think, but I am aware. My awareness is a map of pressures, drawn in the cold bioluminescence of the *Cambial Film*.

The Film coats the seam where my sleeve meets the main column. It is my interface with the engine. It creeps into the slip-plane, its tiny, purple-glowing plastids riding the currents of the starch-oil. Through it, I feel the health of the system:
* *In the bright, hot violet of high-pressure zones*, I feel the risk of a blowout. I respond by constricting my lateral bypass-pores, swelling my wood-cells with water to increase the seal.
* *In the dull, fading indigo of a pressure drop*, I relax. I allow the copper-water to lift me a fraction of a millimeter, opening the channels to let the system equalize.

Sometimes, the transition is not smooth. If the pressure from below spikes too quickly, my body groans. It is a sound that takes months to travel from my dry core to my waxy skin—a low, structural *cree-eak* that vibrates through the ironwood, warning the *Cordage-Marrow* above that the tension has shifted.

If I were to split—if the immense hydraulic weight of the sap-flow were to find a flaw in my cross-hatched grain and rip me open—I would not die, for there is no death here. Instead, I would become static. My sleeve would fill with the calcifying sap of a *Gall-Wedge*, which would slide into the chamber, seal the rent, and weld my body to the sleeve forever. I would cease to be a piston; I would become a pillar.

But until then, I hold.

I am the weight that holds back the sky. I am the silent plug in the great green throat of the continent. I do not wish for light, or for the sky, or for the frantic, rotting business of the world above. I wish only for the perfect, unyielding balance. 

Each viscous *clop* of the water-drop below me is a reassurance. Each pull of the tension-vines above is an adjustment. In the warm, sterile dark, I slide my fraction of a millimeter, and the engine continues to run.
