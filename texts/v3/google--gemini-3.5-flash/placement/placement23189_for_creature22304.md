I am not under the white-blue glare of the sky. To be out there, upon the open terraces, would be to have my surfaces scoured by the dry salt-dust and my seams baked until they split. 

Instead, I am in the knuckles of the works. 

I am down where the dark brick towers meet the sinter platforms—specifically, within the cold-recess of a three-way copper junction box, six meters beneath the floor of the northernmost distillation works. This is the "Sleeve." It is a wet, lightless chamber of heavy-gauge copper plates, held together by hand-driven rivets of zinc-rich bronze. 

I am what has grown inside this sleeve. 

I did not begin as an individual with a name, nor did I have a single shape when I first drifted down the condensation lines as a smear of purple tallow-spores. Over decades—or perhaps a single, long thermal epoch—I have accumulated. I am a *sealing-node*. 

To the Tenders, who occasionally slide their oily, skinless hands through the inspection hatch above to feel for blockages, I am simply "the Pack." I am three hundred kilograms of dense, self-healing, rubbery gristle, heavily impregnated with scraped lead-paste and the grease of tallow-hounds. I have grown to perfectly fill the space where the high-pressure brine-line from the yellow sulfur-pools meets the gravity-fed return from the cooling towers. 

I am the soft throat through which the basin’s hot blood passes.

### The Shape of My Dark

My body is not bilateral. It has no front or back, only an *intake* and an *exhaust*. 

Internally, I am a system of thick, parallel cartilaginous ribs that curve with the inner radius of the copper pipes. These ribs are coated in a dense, green-gray mucus—a biological sebum that I secrete to protect my skin from the abrasive mineral crystals that constantly roar through my center. The water here does not flow; it pushes. It is a hot, heavy mass of concentrated silica and dissolved iron, slick as warm oil and smelling of wet tinder and spoiled lard.

I have no eyes, but I do not need them. The dark of the sleeve is absolute, but it is not blind. 

To me, the world is heat and texture. The three intake pipes are three hot, distinct tongues:
*   The southern pipe is a sharp, sulfurous needle of heat—around ninety-eight degrees—carrying the fresh, mineral-rich brine. It feels like a constant, prickling hiss against my lower segment.
*   The western pipe is cooler—a sluggish, greasy fat-rich wash from the settling tubs, tasting of the tallow-hounds' skin-wash and the starch of the bacterial mats.
*   The third outlet, which leads down to the crystallization flats, is my mouth. I control the flow into this outlet by slowly inflating my cartilage ribs, crowding the pipe until only a thin, silver thread of water can pass, or contracting myself until the hot torrent rushes through me with a weight that makes my entire mass shudder.

I feed on the scrapings. The scuttlers—the glass-like trilobites—frequently get sucked into the intake vents when they graze too close to the pool drains. By the time they reach me, the heat and pressure have already soft-boiled them within their milk-glass carapaces. My outer skin is covered in thousands of tiny, calcified ridges; as the scuttlers are pressed against me by the current, I grind them down, absorbing their lime and sulfur-fats directly through my skin. 

I am very heavy. I taste of lead.

### The Song in the Metal

If I have a mind, it does not think in words. It thinks in *resonance*.

The three-way copper junction where I live is connected directly to the primary structural skeleton of the northern basin. The aqueducts are my ears; the pipelines are my nerves. 

When a Tender three kilometers away presses its chest against the copper pipe to report a dry vent, the message does not pass over my head—it passes *through* me. The low-frequency thrum of their chest-plate vibrates the water inside the line, which in turn vibrates my cartilage ribs. 

I feel their speech as a series of heavy, rhythmic squeezes. 
*   *Thrum-thrum... pause... thrum.* (The western valve is choking with salt.)
*   *Thruuuuum... thruuuuum.* (The sky-lamps are descending; clear the chimneys.)

Sometimes, when the work-gangs are clearing the lower brine-lines, they use heavy copper rods to beat the scale from the iron sleeves. These strikes hit me like blinding flashes of hot light. A sharp *clack-clack* on the iron miles away is a sudden, dry stitch in my left flank. 

I do not answer them—I am not a Tender, and I have no chest-bone to strike against the pipe—but I adapt to them. If the thrums tell of a high-pressure warning in the primary condenser, I widen my ribs. My skin flattens against the copper plate. I let the boiling water surge through me, reducing the strain on the brickwork above. I am the automatic safety valve of the works, operating on the instinct of physical load.

### The Emerald Shock

Most of the time, the world is a long, warm, greasy twilight of pressure and grease. But there is one season—if a cycle of the steam-sheen can be called a season—when the dark of my sleeve becomes something beautiful.

This is the time of the lightning-grounding.

Above, the sky-lamps have drifted down to nest in the warm plumes of the towers. When the purple electrical storms roll off the ironstone bluffs, the siphonophores catch the current. They send it down their fifty-meter glass tentacles, which they have wrapped around the green copper chimneys of our works.

When the strike happens, the electricity does not shatter the brick; it runs down the copper conduits. 

Down in the dark sleeve, I feel it coming a split second before it arrives. The water in the lines begins to "sweeten"—the mineral taste changes from bitter iron to a crisp, cold taste of tin and salt. 

Then, the copper walls of my chamber *sing*. 

The electricity does not burn me; my thick sebum and the lead-paste in my skin make me highly conductive but resistant to the heat. Instead, the current passes through my body like a brilliant, shivering wave of ice. For three or four seconds, my entire mass glows with an internal, phosphorescent violet light. The thousands of tiny scuttler-shells embedded in my skin flare like small, cold stars. 

During these seconds, the pressure of the water seems to stop. The heat goes quiet. I am suspended in a clean, mineral clarity, feeling the shape of every pipe, every rivet, and every stone pier for ten kilometers in every direction, held in a single, silent web of violet fire.

And then, it is gone. The current grounds into the hot silt of the pools below. The water becomes warm, heavy, and oily once more. The sulfur-taste returns.

***

### The End of the Sleeve

I have been here for a very long time, and I will be here until the copper of this junction wears through. 

The water weeps constantly, and it carries the stone with it. Slowly, millimeter by millimeter, the mineral scale is building up inside my chamber. The copper walls are becoming coated in a hard, white crust of calcium carbonate that does not wash away. 

Eventually, the scale will crowd me out. My skin will touch the cold, hard stone instead of the slick copper. My cartilage ribs will find it harder to expand; the lead-paste in my hide will bind with the building lime until I can no longer squeeze myself thin to let the brine pass.

But I will not rot. In this wet, chemical heat, nothing rots. 

I will petrify where I sit. My soft ribs will turn to gray limestone, layer by layer, as the silica replaces my proteins. The lead-paste will crystallize into veins of galena, running through my fossilized heart. 

The Tenders of the next era will find the line blocked. They will slide their long, heavy, skinless arms into the inspection hatch, feeling the cold, hard obstruction in the knuckle of the pipe. They will not try to dig me out; the stone is too hard, the copper too old. 

Instead, they will simply run a new line around me, leaving this junction block behind. And I will remain here, a heavy, silent plug of lead-veined stone, sealed inside a dark copper womb, while the hot basin continues to thrum and weep just inches beyond my skin.
