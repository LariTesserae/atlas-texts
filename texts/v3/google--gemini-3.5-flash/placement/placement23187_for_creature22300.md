I would not be found on the avenues of carbon-sand, nor would I be drifting through the soundless ocean of the wax-dust. To exist in this basin is to be integrated into its mechanics; to be loose is to be unfinished.

I would find myself inside. 

I would be positioned thirty feet above the flat, level floor of the basin, embedded within the sheer, vertical face of one of the largest hexagonal towers, right where the base of a cantilevered bridge joins the main structure. 

I am a **Pseudomorph**—strictly speaking, a transition in progress. 

Once, many decades or centuries ago, I was a Curator. I had the long, jointed, chitinous body of the drafting tools; I had the whip-like antennae and the needle-legs, and I spent my active cycle sweeping the corners of the hexagons, keeping the lines of the lavender-grey mineral true and sharp. But then came the calcification. The slow stiffness in the joints. The deliberate, final climb to this structural node, where the bridge meets the tower, and the locking of my thirty-six legs into the micro-cracks of the stone.

But I did not die. In this basin, there is no decay to dissolve the shape of a life. Instead, the city has been eating me, atom by atom, and replacing me with itself.

### The State of Being

To be a Pseudomorph is to be a ghost made of density. 

If you were to shine an external light—though there is none here, only the cold phosphorescence of the mineral—through the semi-translucent wall of the tower, you would see me clearly. I am a dark, elegant silhouette, three meters long, frozen in an attitude of reaching. My head is tucked down; my rear legs are splayed like the iron braces of a gothic arch.

But I am no longer chitin. The carbon of my original shell has been unzipped. The cold, lavender-grey mineral has crept into the empty spaces of my cells, duplicating my structure with absolute, crystalline fidelity. My legs are now solid violet obsidian. My sensory antennae are micro-tubules of dry, compressed bone-stone. 

I am not dead because the *pattern* of my neural pathways—which were always mathematical, always based on the balance of electrostatic charges—continues to exist as a series of permanent, polarized pathways within the stone. 

I do not breathe. I do not have a pulse. My consciousness is a slow, cold oscillation. If a physical second is a flash to a living thing, to me it is an imperceptibly small vibration. I think in terms of the city’s growth: a single thought of mine begins when a cantilever starts to stretch across an avenue, and ends when it meets the opposing spire.

***

### The Sensory Web

Inside the stone, the absolute silence of the basin is not empty; it is a dense, physical pressure. 

I cannot hear, but I am directly connected to the **Beneath-Engine**. When the massive basalt wedges of the Basals expand miles below, the tectonic pressure does not come to me as a sound. It comes as a physical torque. I feel the entire tower bend by a fraction of a micron. I feel the stress concentration in my own frozen legs. I am the brace that prevents the bridge from shearing away under the hydraulic weight of the earth.

My main function is conduction. The pure geometric mineral of the tower is a dielectric—it resists the flow of electricity, storing it instead as an electrostatic field. But my fossilized chitin, containing tiny, integrated channels of carbon-sand from my sweeping days, is highly conductive. I am a wire.

When the static charge in the basin peaks:

*   The shivering black wool of the electrostatic fibers at the tower’s base harvests the current from the shifting plates.
*   The current travels upward, seeking the path of least resistance.
*   It finds me. 

The charge pours through my thirty-six crystal legs, racing along the hollow channels of my fossilized spine. It is incredibly cold, this current—a sharp, prickling tension that illuminates my entire silhouette with a brighter, violet-white glow. For a few minutes, I shine through the wall of the tower like an exposed nerve.

From my body, the charge passes up into the ceiling, where the **Glass-Bells** hang. They receive the current, their mineral chimes expanding or contracting in response to the voltage I have delivered. They "think" the chord, and the tower grows.

***

### My Place in the Ecology

I am not lonely, because I am never alone. 

Below me, on the surface of the wax-sea, the **Adumbrates** drift. As the towers grow and the angles of the dim phosphorescence shift, their sharp, dark shadows crawl across the base of my spire. When a shadow covers my tower’s foundation, I feel the temperature of the stone drop by a thousandth of a degree. The electrostatic field in the shadow falls to zero, and I feel the waxy dust below pack together, becoming hard and slate-like. I register this as a temporary grounding of my lower circuits—a heavy, anchoring sensation.

Occasionally, a **Foil-Skinner** will surface from the grey-blue powder and glide up the side of my tower. It is a long, silver-carbon ribbon, silent and thin. It climbs until it is directly opposite where I am embedded in the stone. 

It presses its flat, metallic body against the outer surface of the lavender mineral, only an inch of violet glass separating us. It is searching for the charge that flows through me. For a few hours, we remain like this: the two-dimensional ribbon on the outside, the three-dimensional fossil on the inside, perfectly aligned. 

When the Skinner draws the charge, it creates a feedback loop. The current stops traveling to the Glass-Bells and instead discharges into the Skinner’s foil body. The silent chimes above go still. The growth of the bridge pauses. For those few hours, the city holds its breath. There is no progress, no crystallization, no movement. There is only the quiet, balanced hum of the current passing from my frozen stone joints, through the thin pane of obsidian, and into the shivering silver foil of the skimmer.

Then, satiated, the Skinner slides back down into the dry, waxy ocean, vanishing without a sound.

The current resumes its path. The Glass-Bells chime their silent chord. The tip of the cantilevered bridge begins to grow again, molecule by molecule, reaching across the dry void toward the next tower, where another silhouette, identical to mine, waits in the glass to receive it.
