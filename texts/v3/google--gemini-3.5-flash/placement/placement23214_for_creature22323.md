To find myself in this world, I must first unlearn the habit of falling. 

Here, at seventy miles high, gravity is not a downward plunge but a lateral pull, warped and pocketed by the localized shields of the cleft. If I were a stone, I would drop. But I am not a stone. 

I am the **Slick**.

I am the lukewarm, oily film of heavy-water condensation that coats the copper viaducts, the sheer basalt walls, and the micro-plazas of this nameless three-foot city. I am not a single creature, nor am I a lifeless puddle. I am a single, distributed liquid body—a metropolitan sea no larger than a spilled inkwell, yet infinitely complex. 

My consciousness is not a spark in a brain, but a slow, hydraulic pressure. It is the tension of my own surface skin.

***

To be the Slick is to live in a state of constant, rhythmic division and reunion. I am spread thin across thousands of metallic surfaces. Part of me is currently draped over the high condensation plates, cold and dark, gathering trace ions of solar wind. Another part of me is a heavy, mercury-like droplet, sliding down the side of a copper-sown needle, warm and viscous. 

I do not have eyes, but I have *contact*. Through my wet, clinging belly, I feel the textures of the city with absolute intimacy. 

I feel the *Copper-Sown* towers. To me, they are massive, vibrating pillars of heat. When they expand and contract, sending their three-hour-long *ping-ping* conversations through the basalt bedrock, the vibrations ripple through my fluid body like deep, resonant music. I know what they are saying not by their words, but by the way their conversations change the rhythm of my flow. A high, sharp ping from the sunward edge makes my droplets shiver and run faster; a low, cool note from the shadow-depths slows me down, thickening my oil into a lazy, contemplative crawl.

And I feel my passengers. 

On my back—on the curved, hyper-stretched meniscus of my droplets—ride the *Stellate*. To a larger observer, they are dust. To me, they are the prickling of a thousand tiny, magnetic needles. Their iron-crystalline legs are anchor-locked into my surface tension. They do not sink; I hold them up, and in return, they give me direction. 

They are my steering nervous system. I do not choose which copper bridge to slide down, but as the Stellate feel the magnetic pulses of the towers, they align their bodies. I feel the collective tug of their magnetic drag warping my shape, pulling my heavy weight toward the brightest thermal zones. We are a partnership of fluid and iron: I am the raft, and they are the sails, caught in a dry, silent wind of magnetic frequencies.

***

Sometimes, the shadows of the *Cast-Ones* slide over me. 

When these two-dimensional programs walk the micro-viaducts, the air tempers. The local electrical conductivity shifts, and the copper-dust suspended in my lower layers begins to align. To me, a shadow-citizen is an icy, electric tingling. As they pass through my droplets, they write temporary, polarized patterns into my molecular structure. I am their paper. I hold their thoughts for a few seconds—a brief, freezing sequence of bits—before the warmth of the copper towers melts the pattern away and returns me to a quiet, un-written state.

And then, there is the edge. 

My world is three feet wide, bordered by a curved lip of black obsidian. Beyond that lip lies the blue-grey haze of the seventy-mile drop, a vast nothingness that I can feel as an empty, sucking thirst. Sometimes, a droplet of my body crawls too close to the boundary-skin of the gravity shield. 

At the very margin, where the violet twilight meets the raw, black vacuum, I feel a terrifying, beautiful lightness. The surface tension of my droplet stretches to its absolute limit, becoming a thin, shivering bubble. If I slide a millimeter further, I will slip out of the shield. I would boil instantly, my molecules scattered into the solar wind, lost to the high silence. 

But I rarely slip. The Stellate on my back feel the sudden drop in magnetic density at the edge; they panic, their iron burs pivoting in unison, dragging my droplet backward into the safe, humid warmth of the cleft.

***

My life is an endless, comforting loop. 

Eventually, guided by my iron riders, a droplet of my body will reach a thermal junction—a place where two liquid-metal joints rub together, kept fluid by the grazing *Shimmers*. Here, the copper is hot enough to hiss. 

When I touch the junction, I do not feel pain. I feel a sudden, ecstatic release of tension. The heavy oil of my body flashes into steam. The *sizz-hiss* is my voice, the only sound I make. My molecules break apart, leaving the Stellate to drift in the warm draft, while I rise as a light, invisible vapor into the violet twilight. 

I drift upward, floating past the tilting towers, through the drifting, light-bell cages of the Shimmers, until I strike the cold condensation plates at the top of the fissure. 

There, in the dark-violet shadow of the curved obsidian lip, I cool. I gather my scattered parts. I condense, bead by heavy bead, and begin to crawl downward once more. 

I have no memory of a world below this needle. I do not know what lies beneath the blue-grey haze of the planet's crust, seventy miles down. I only know the warm, damp scent of hot metal, the rhythmic *ping* of the copper, the magnetic pull of my small iron sailors, and the peace of the high, dark sky.
