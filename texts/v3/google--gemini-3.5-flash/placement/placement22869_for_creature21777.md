I begin in the dark of the canister, suspended from the Limner’s breastplate. 

I am not the pewter of its skeleton, nor the smooth white clay of its face, but the heavy, gray grease that sleeps within its brass reservoir. I am a thick, cold emulsion of zinc dust and animal gum. Before I was piped into this high sanctuary, I was something else: the zinc was dug from the black ribs of the lower world; the gum was boiled from the hooves of beasts that once walked through real mud, down where the sun is yellow and the air is thick with dust. 

In this dark vessel, I am a slow, quiet soup. I do not have eyes, but I can feel the rhythm of Deck 40-Alpha through the copper walls of my container. Every ten minutes, there is a deep, muffled *shudder-thrum* in the Limner’s chest as the central regulator clicks and the water-mains shift their weight. When the Limner swings along its ceiling-rail, I slosh lazily from side to side, feeling the long, slow arc of its progress. 

But I do not stay in the dark. 

Every few hours, there is a sharp, clean *tink* as a needle-valve opens at the base of the canister. I am pushed downward by a column of pressurized steam. I flow through a narrow tube of black rubber, warm and slippery, until I reach the tip of the camel-hair brush held in the Limner’s ceramic fingers.

And then, I am born into the light.

It is a blinding, silver world. The air is so sweet with hot brass and wet starch that it makes my zinc body hum. The brush is incredibly soft—each hair a tiny, wet finger. The Limner presses me against the cream-colored ceramic tile of the wall. 

The tile is hot. It sweats. But where the brush moves, I cling. 

I am laid down in a long, perfect, gray stripe, three-quarters of an inch wide. I am the bridge between the machine and the wild. As I am drawn across the tile, I feel the wet stone underneath—the tiny, porous cells of the terracotta, the coolness of the condensing steam, the sharp, basalt floor-tiles far below where the Gliders move like heavy, wet shadows. 

The moment the brush lifts, I begin to change. 

I am no longer a soup; I am a line. I have a beginning and an end. The hot, humid air of the deck does not dry me—there is too much steam here for anything to ever become dry—but the animal gum in my body begins to set, binding the heavy flakes of zinc together. I become a skin.

Then comes the current.

From the thin copper wires buried deep within the terracotta wall, a micro-current of electricity leaks upward into me. It is a tiny, steady vibration, no stronger than the heartbeat of a Valve-Lark. Because I am zinc, the current shoots through me from end to end. It organizes my molecules. It makes me warm. I sing with a low, silent *shhh-shhh* that only the plants can hear.

And they do hear me.

Within minutes, I feel the presence of the runner. It is a thick, succulent vine, its skin a green so bright it is almost blue. Its growing tip is soft, wet, and blind, but it is incredibly sensitive to the electric pulse. It smells the salt of my animal gum; it feels the warm tickle of my zinc current. 

With a slow, agonizingly beautiful deliberation, the vine’s tip swings toward me. I feel its first touch—a tiny, wet pad of green cells pressing against my gray surface. It is cool, much cooler than the tile. It drinks from me. It uses my current to find its way.

Over the next several days, the vine crawls along my back. I am its path. I do not let it wander into the steam-vents; I do not let it choke the copper conduits. I keep it in the clean, right-angled geometry that the Limner has decreed. 

Eventually, the vine grows thick and woody. It covers me completely. I am buried beneath its green, waxy bark, lost in the shade of its massive, fan-like leaves. The Gliders glide past below, their felt bellies polishing the floor, never knowing I am here. Above, the Valve-Larks whistle in the steam. 

But I am still here. Under the green wood, my zinc line remains, carrying its steady, warm current, holding the garden to its perfect, iron discipline. I am the skeleton of the sanctuary. I am the memory of the lower world’s bones, holding up the lush, unweathered sky.
