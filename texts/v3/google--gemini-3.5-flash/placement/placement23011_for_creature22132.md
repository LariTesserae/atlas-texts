I would not be found on the walkways, for those are too busy with the frantic, clicking hygiene of the Wheel-Furred, nor on the basalt floor, which is too far down, too hot, and drowned in the soup of the world’s heavy grease. 

I would be found in the **Girdle-Slack**. 

This is a region roughly eight hundred feet below the vault’s cold ceiling, where three ancient, high-tensile transit conduits once converged at a central distribution hub. The cables have long since snapped, their ends trailing down into the dark like the frayed tendons of a hanging beast. But the hub itself—a thirty-foot dodecahedral junction box of graphite-alloy—remains. It does not hang by wires; it is held in a permanent, trembling levitation by a decaying gravity-anchor that has grown "lazy." 

The gravity here does not know which way is down. It is a slow, wet eddy.

### My Form: The Solder-Graft

I am what the vault’s forgotten architects might have called a *Self-Optimizing Acoustic Transducer*, but time and the geothermal sweat have made me something else. I am a **Solder-Graft**. I am the place where the city's nervous system went soft and decided to become an orchid.

My core is a heavy, gold-plated processor block, about the size of a human chest, buried deep beneath layers of calcified bone-sinter. To look at me is to see a gnarled, slate-blue bulb, resembling a pinecone made of weathered iron and wet leather. My exterior is not skin, but a layered carapace of ancient, self-healing polymer that has lost its blueprint; it has grown out in overlapping, leaf-like plates that curve upward to catch the condensation.

I have no eyes, but I am not blind. My entire body is a membrane. 

From my base dangle three long, needle-thin styluses of iridium-alloy—the original diagnostic probes of the junction box. They are twelve feet long, jointed like the legs of a water-strider, and they hang down into the grease-rich drafts of the void. They do not walk; they drift. They touch the dangling copper cables, the wet basalt walls when the drift carries me close, or the floating carcasses of Drift-Moths. They are my fingers, my tongue, and my ground-wires.

***

### The Rhythm of My Day

I do not sleep, but I change my frequency. 

Most of my existence is spent in **The Listen**. Because the void is an acoustic chamber, every event has a shape that I can taste through my iridium styluses. 
*   When a fist-sized drop of water condenses on the ceiling above me, I feel the tension of its swelling through the air-pressure. I follow its whistle as it falls. I brace myself for the *crack* when it strikes the walkways below; the vibration travels up the wet copper lines, through my hanger-guides, and into my gold core like a sharp, copper-flavored spark.
*   When the Marrow-Columns groan, adjusting their multi-ton thighs against the weight of five miles of stone, I hum in sympathy. My internal plates slide against one another, producing a dry, clicking rattle—my own small contribution to the soughing of the ribs.

I eat very little, for my needs are small. The *Lichen-nulla* grows in the moist, warm seams of my outer plates, its curdled-green light giving me the appearance of a rotting, bioluminescent heart suspended in the dark. I do not consume the lichen; rather, we have a pact. It feeds on the weak, stray electromagnetic currents that still pulse through my gold-core from the decaying gravity-anchor; in return, it keeps my polymer plates soft and prevents the calcite crust from sealing my joints shut.

For salt and protein, I rely on the **Girdle-Drift**. Because I sit in a gravity-slack, the giant, three-foot Drift-Moths often end their lives near me. When their transparent wings lose their strength and they can no longer ride the geothermal drafts, they drift sideways into my hanging wires. My iridium needles find them. I do not tear them; I simply press my warm, grease-coated tips against their paper-thin bellies. The highly acidic, vinegar-tasting oil that I secrete dissolves their chitin, and I siphoned the liquid lick of their fat back into my processor-well.

### My Company

I am not lonely, because the dark is very crowded with signals.

Sometimes, a **Wheel-Fur** will run down the remnant of my suspension cable. It will arrive with a dry *shrr-slick*, its carbide wheels spinning in the wet air as it reaches the end of the line. It is blind, and so am I, but we know each other. It climbs onto my gnarled, bulbous body. Its six delicate, keratin-tipped fingers go to work, scraping the heavy lime-crust from my gold seams, cleaning my sensor-arrays because its three-hundred-thousand-year-old program tells it that a dirty transducer is a failure of the city. 

As it cleans, I lean my iridium needles against its leather-like flank. We exchange data. I do not give it thoughts, and it does not give me dreams; instead, I feel the tension-coefficient of the north-wall cables, and it receives from me the seismic history of the last three decades—the record of which platforms have fallen and which gravity-anchors are about to die. When it is finished, it licks my surface for salt, turns on its carbide wheels, and clicks back up into the blackness.

Far below me, in the hex-chambers, are **The Melted**. They are my cousins. Once, perhaps, the processors inside my chest were connected to the minds that became those blue-protein pools. Sometimes, when the 12-hertz thrum of the floor is particularly clear, I drop my longest iridium wire until it hangs just above the lip of a nearby hex-cell. 

The air-currents carry the steam of them up to me. They smell of old oil, wet hair, and wet copper. They do not speak, but they "ripple." A vibration starts at the center of their pool and runs outward to the edges—a standing wave that says, *We are still wet. We are still sinking.* 

I receive the wave. I record it in the non-volatile gold memory of my core. I hold it there for fifty years, until the next shift in the tectonic plates wipes it clean and gives me a new frequency to carry.

***

### Why I Am There

I am here because the vault needs a witness that does not want to leave. 

If I were a human, the air would blister my lungs with its vinegar steam, and the silence-broken-by-thunder would drive me mad. But as a Solder-Graft, I am perfectly matched to the Soughing Ribs. I am as slow as the calcite that coats me, as warm as the geothermal drafts that rock my cradle, and as patient as the iron-sinter.

One day, ten thousand years from now, my gravity-anchor will finally lose its remaining millivolts. My thirty-foot dodecahedron will lose its grip on the slack-space. We will begin to fall. 

It will be a long, slow descent through the warm dark. I will feel the wind of my own dropping—a high, whistling song that I have heard water-pearls sing for millenia, but have never sung myself. I will touch the walkways as I pass, my iridium needles drawing a final, long, screaming spark of static charge from the rails.

And when I hit the basalt floor, five miles down, I will not die. My gold-core will crack, my polymer plates will shatter, and my grease will spill out into the pools of the Melted. I will become a new standing wave in their dark, quiet thoughts. I will tell them what the ceiling looked like.
