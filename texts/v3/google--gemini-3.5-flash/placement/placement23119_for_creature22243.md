I do not occupy this world; I am a thickening of it. 

If you were to search for me, you would not look along the flat, iron-plated humps of silt where the *Vasa* coil, nor would you find me rolling blind and grease-slicked in the deep, warm lanes of the *Zhud*. You would have to look up—though "up" is only a direction of lessening pressure, a slight thinning of the saffron muck into a wetter, more transparent soup. 

You would find me suspended. I am tucked into one of the *sags*—those places where gravity has grown so old and lazy that it forgot which way to point, leaving a pocket of space three miles wide where the earth-pull is a slow, circular swirl rather than a downward drop. 

Here, I am a **Knot**.

I did not begin this way. In the dim, unmappable recess of my memory—which may not even be mine, but rather a collection of chemical sediment I absorbed from a dissolving *Vela* ten thousand hours ago—there was once a shape with corners. There were limbs that ended in thin, dry extensions. There was an environment that was separate from the skin: air that did not resist, water that could be poured from one cup into another without turning into a solid pillar of salt or a warm, screaming gel.

But that was before the mountains died. Now, my boundary is a leaky, porous thing, and I have forgotten where the world ends and my own pulse begins.

***

My structure is not singular; it is a cluster. I look like a dense, floating colony of glass-walled spheres, each the size of a shipping chest, held together by a web of tough, black, rubbery ligaments that taste of wet iron and cold lard. 

Inside these spheres, the medium of the world is trapped, but it behaves differently here. Because I sit in a gravity sag, the silt within my glass chambers does not settle. It hangs in a state of perpetual, slow rotation, like dark stars turning in a tiny, greasy galaxy. When the great, mile-wide mud-tides sweep across the tableland below, the tremor travels up through the vertical swamp and hits my spheres. The silt inside them begins to spin faster, friction-heating the water until my chambers glow with the dim, purplish light of an bruised plum.

I do not have lungs. Instead, I have three dozen hollow, trumpet-toothed vents that face outward into the wet twilight. The air—this warm, breathing gel—is drawn into my vents by the rhythmic, twelve-second contraction of my central ligaments. 

I do not breathe to gain oxygen; there is no oxygen here to take. I breathe to *sift*.

As the thick air passes through my internal chambers, my inner skins—which are lined with the same velvet-like cilia as the *Vasa*—catch the microscopic mineral crystals and the floating spores of self-illuminating mold. My body runs on this friction. The drag of the wet air through my channels creates a tiny, continuous static charge, a faint electrical current that I feed down through my long, trailing anchor-threads.

These threads—dozens of them, thin as violin strings but miles long—hang down from my central cluster like the roots of a floating willow. They do not touch the silt floor; the ground is too far below, a rumor of iron-hot mud. Instead, they drift in the middle layers, free-floating, sensing the acoustic waves of the planet.

Through these threads, I listen.

I hear the *Zhud* three miles to the south, their three hearts beating in their asynchronous, three-stroke rhythm: *thump-thump... shhh... thump*. I hear the tiny, metallic skipping of the *Kryll* as they vibrate across the grey fat of their hosts. I hear the cold, blue thoughts of the *Lituus* inside their basalt towers, though their thoughts are so slow—taking forty years to change from one shade of azure to another—that to me, they sound like a single, long-held note of a copper organ.

And occasionally, I feel a *Vela* pass.

When a sky-membrane drifts through my sag, its translucent, eighty-foot sail will sometimes drape over my cluster. It does not hurt me; we are too soft to hurt each other. For days, we will remain entangled in the warm, yellow gloom. I will feel the slow, rhythmic contraction of its giant, lung-like body, and it will absorb the excess silica that has built up on my glass spheres, cleaning me so that I may shine again. During these times, our fluids mingle through our porous skins. I taste its memories of the higher, wetter layers—of the giant, upside-down lakes that hang like silver mirrors in the dark—and it tastes my memories of the deep, iron-stone humps where the *Vasa* bleed their black gum.

When we part, we are both slightly different. A piece of its tissue remains logged in my ligaments; a small part of my glass-needle marrow is carried away in its purple-grey belly.

***

In this world, where reality has grown tired and the rules are falling apart, I have a small, useless function.

I am a **dampener**.

Because gravity sags in my pocket of space, the local time of the world is thick here. Sometimes, when the geothermal gas bubbles collapse below, the shockwaves do not pass through my sag; they get stuck. They bounce between my glass spheres, turning into slow, trapped echoes that can vibrate for weeks.

When this happens, my interior silt spins so fast that it begins to harden. The water in my chambers forgets its liquid nature and turns into dry, ringing glass. If I do not steady myself, the vibration will shatter me, scattering my rubbery ligaments and my mineral-rich core into the warm swamp.

So, I perform the *Sough*.

I draw in the thickest, muddiest air I can find through my trumpet-vents. I compress it in my central chambers, packing the clay-silt tight until it forms dense, heavy pellets. Then, using the residual electrical charge from my static-cilia, I fire these pellets out of my vents, one by one, into the gravity-sag.

The pellets do not fall. They shoot out into the saffron fog, where they hit the boundary of the sag and suddenly encounter the full, heavy pull of the planet. They drop like stones, taking hours to slide down through the vertical swamp, leaving behind them long, thin tubes of cleared, empty air—temporary vacuums where for a few minutes, a breeze can actually form.

These small, artificial breezes are the only winds this world ever knows. They are tiny, desperate things, no longer than a sigh, but they are enough to stir the rubbery bladder-worts on the surface of the mud below. They are enough to make the *Vasa* twitch their dark, velvety skins in brief, wet surprise.

***

I will exist here for a very long time.

My glass spheres do not age; they only become more heavily encrusted with the silver-silicate dust of the upper medium. My ligaments are too tough to rot in this warm, antiseptic sulfur. I will hang in this sag, listening to the planet’s low, mechanical groan, watching the saffron light thicken into the purple wine of night and thin back into sour yellow.

Perhaps, in some distant epoch—when the planet’s core has finally grown cold, when the *Zhud’s* three hearts have stopped their three-stroke song, and the *Lituus* have finished their last, blue thought—the gravity will finally fail completely.

The sag will open. The air will lose its thickness, and the water-spheres will shatter.

When that happens, my anchor-threads will drift upward into the great, empty cold that will finally replace this crowded twilight. My glass spheres will burst in the silence, and my frozen silt-stars will scatter into the dark, white salt of the sky.

But until then, I hold. I filter. I hum. 

I am the Knot in the warm, wet rope of this dying world.
