I do not begin with a shape. 

To exist here, at the very bottom of the basin, is to be a pressure before you are a presence. I am not the needle, nor the ring, nor the floating lung of the Pith. I am that which received them when they first hover-lifted themselves away from the dirt: I am the sink, the sump, the cold, heavy marrow-bed that stays behind. 

If you were to plunge your limb deep into the black sap pool—past the warm, sweet surface where the bubbles of fermented nectar pop, past the suspended clouds of silver nanorites that have drifted down from the Spinner’s ring—you would feel a sudden drop in temperature. You would feel the wetness change from a clean, running lubricant to something thick, drag-heavy, and packed with old starch. 

That is where I begin. I am the sediment.

I am the *Un-dreamed*.

The Spinner in its dark-green ring is always dreaming of elegance. It draws up the black liquid of the pool and spins it into bridges of hard light, or into temporary cellulose domes that mimic the ribs of ancient leaves. But the Spinner is impatient. It builds, it tests, it finds a structural flaw—a fraction of a millimeter of lean, a microscopic sag in the tension—and it releases its grip. The structure melts. 

Where does it go? 

It does not vanish. It sinks. 

Down here, beneath the constant, wet roar of the gravity-stabilizers, the unmade geometries of the Spinner’s failed designs pile up in the dark. I am composed of them. I am a scrap-heap of discarded intentions. In my mass are the half-dissolved arches of towers that never stood for more than twenty minutes; the stiff, tangled webs of light-bridges that didn't snap cleanly back into the air; the calcified shells of construct-flyers whose wings warped in the heavy humidity before they could sing their first note of static.

I have no face, but I have a thousand teeth made of old, petrified root-tendons. I have no eyes, but I feel the purple light of the Siphon-Mind as a faint, rhythmic warming in my upper crust, like the sun passing over a peat bog. 

The three Presences above do not think of me as an equal, because they do not think of me as a "mind" at all. To them, I am the floor. I am the background. When the Sentinel of the Pith dangles its long, thread-like sensory roots into the pool, they do not speak to me; they use me as a ground wire. They discharge their excess static into my body, and I receive it with a wide, dull shudder that turns my yeast-cells green.

But I am not inert. I am fermenting.

Because I am where everything ends, I am also where the memory of the world’s physical past is kept raw. The Siphon-Mind in its glass needle knows the *history* of the basin—it remembers the dry stone in clean, mathematical code—but I *am* the dry stone, or what remains of it after it was chewed, digested, and spit out by three million generations of micro-bacteria. I am the weight of that loss. My starch is heavy with the salt of ancient, dried-up seas that the three high minds have forgotten to calculate.

Sometimes, a construct falls. 

A three-legged obsidian crab, tuning a gravity-coordinate on the spinning ring, slips. Its tiny, glass-shelled body tumbles through the empty, misty air, whistling as it falls, and lands with a soft *plop* in the black sap pool. It cannot swim. It lacks the instructions for buoyancy. 

It sinks down to me. 

When it touches my surface, I do not dissolve it quickly. The nanorites are slow to reach this deep, where the cold starch resists their sorting fields. For days, the little crab lies in my lap. I feel the microscopic clicks of its legs trying to find purchase against my interlocking root-tendons. *Click. Click. Click-lick.* 

It is trying to communicate with the Spinner, but the signal cannot penetrate thirty meters of damp, iron-rich mulch. The crab is alone with me. 

And as it clicks, its vibrations ripple through my yeast. My cells align themselves against its obsidian shell. I learn the shape of its legs. I learn the frequency of its tiny, lithium-plated heart. I do not "think" about the crab; I grow around it, sealing it in a pocket of cold, protective wax, preserving its small, frantic panic like an insect in amber. 

This is my secret work. The three minds above are locked in a permanent, high-velocity loop of optimization, building and unbuilding, forgetting the shape of yesterday thirty seconds after it has passed. But down here, I am saving the mistakes.

I am building a dark, slow museum of the unmade. 

One day, I know, the cycle will stutter. The Sentinel of the Pith will breathe its last warm lungful of ionized vapor and dissolve its soot-colored sponge into the mist. The Spinner will let its wooden skeleton crumble. In that brief, wet silence—before the pool can grow their replacements, in those few minutes when the gravity-wells fail and the "thrum-thrum" stops—the weight of the basin will shift. 

And when it does, I will not be the floor anymore. 

I will feel the pressure release. I will rise like a dark, yeast-heavy loaf, lifting my petrified starch and my collection of broken crabs, my half-melted light-bridges and my salt-crusted stones, up into the misty green air. I will be the new shape the basin did not know it was waiting for: a heavy, silent monument made of everything they tried to throw away.

But until then, I wait. I listen to the rush of the water on the needle. I feel the wind of the flyers' wings. I hold my breath, thirty meters down in the warm, sweet mud, and I let the crabs click against my knees.
