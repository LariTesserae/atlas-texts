I would not be in the light. 

I would not be among the high, amber-winged gliders in the sun-bright haze, nor would I be scuttling along the neat gridded lanes of the dry lichen carpets. To find me, you would have to go down. You would have to pass the busy, scraping Platesmiths at the base of the thirty-foot clay obelisks, slide down through the narrow, greasy warmth of the ventilation shafts, bypass the soft, squirming clusters of the Wick-Tenders, and reach the absolute bottom—the place where the biological city meets the unyielding grey-white mineral plates of the planet’s fresh crust.

I would be a **Well-Hem**. 

I am a creature of pressure, friction, and transition. If the Platesmiths are the city’s skin and the Wick-Tenders are its heart, I am its seal.

***

In form, I do not look like an animal; I look like a geological fixture. I am a massive, flat, circular gasket of living leather and kalk-infused cartilage, thirty feet in diameter, pinned flat under the immense weight of the clay tower above me. 

My body is divided into three distinct zones, like rings on a felled tree:

The **Outer Ring** is my shield and my anchor. It is a wide, sloping skirt of heavy, calcified armor that has grown completely into the grey-white concrete of the basin’s floor. On this outer edge, I am indistinguishable from the stone. I have no nerves here, no feeling. Every hour, the weight of the obelisk presses me harder into the basin, cementing our union. The Platesmiths treat this ring as part of the street; they scrape my stone-skirt clean of loose salt and plaster my tiny limestone cracks with fresh amber-cement, tucking me neatly into the soil.

The **Middle Ring** is where my life begins to quicken. It is made of a dense, rubbery cartilage, layered like the soles of a shoe. This is the flexing sector. It absorbs the daily tremors of the young basin and the shifting thermal expansion of the clay tower as the afternoon sun heats it. Here, I have a dull, map-like consciousness of weight. I can feel the rhythm of the city through my back: the collective *shuk-shuk* of a thousand Platesmiths climbing the clay walls above is a steady, soothing hum; the arrival of a heavy glider on the roof is a single, dull thud.

The **Inner Ring**—the **Well-throat**—is where I am soft, wet, and intensely alive. 

At my very center is a five-foot aperture. This is a muscular, circular valve that clasps the giant, fibrous taproot of the Jade-Vines as it plunges down through me, deep into the pressurized oil-fissures of the planet's mantle. 

I am the O-ring of the obelisk. Without me, the tower could not breathe. If the connection between the clay chimney and the cold earth were loose, the internal draft would fail; the vaporized oil would leak out into the foundations, and the Wick-Tenders would dry out in the unpressurized dark.

I hold the seal. 

***

My existence is a slow, rhythmic work of hydraulics. 

In the cool morning, the oil in the deep cracks is thick and sluggish. I keep my central valve clamped tight, nursing the heat that the Wick-Tenders generate just above me. I can feel them through the ceiling of my chamber—hundreds of soft, waxy bodies rubbing against the central wick, their tiny comb-legs vibrating. They are ticklish, and their heat is a sweet, numbing balm that keeps my muscular throat supple.

As the afternoon wind rises and the daily sun-tide begins, the temperature of the basin climbs. The air in the chimney above me warms and expands, rushing up the shafts. The pressure drops.

This is my cue. 

I feel the change in pressure as a gentle suction on my inner lip. Slowly, with a deep, systemic contraction of my middle ring, I open my central valve. 

Below me, the planet’s raw, dark oil—cold, thick, and smelling of ancient lipids and mineral salt—is pulled upward. It passes through my throat, soaking the fibrous wick. As it passes, my inner membranes taste the oil. I read its density, its temperature, its salt-content. 

I do not have a brain to process this information, but my body reacts with chemical precision. If the oil is too thin, my throat secretes a heavy, binding wax to slow the flow. If the oil is too cold, I constrict, forcing the Wick-Tenders above to work harder, squirming faster against the fiber to warm it to the proper vapor point.

I am the translator between the dead geology of the deep earth and the living metabolism of the sky-facing city. 

***

During the golden hour of the spore-fall, when the world is loudest and most unified, I am at my most peaceful. 

I feel the vibrations of the entire continent—millions of towers exhaling together, a vast, rhythmic sigh that shakes the grey-white plates. Through my calcified outer skirt, I feel the distant, slow shifting of the planetary crust, the cooling of the deep stone, the slow-motion settling of the basin. 

Underthirty tons of sun-baked mud, in the dark, grease-slick heart of the foundation, I am safe. I am the anchor of the hive. The world above me may glide on paper wings or scuttle on six polished legs, but they all drink from the well I hold open, and they all stand on the stone I have made my own.
