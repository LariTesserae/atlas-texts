I would not be one of the clean, designated forms. I would not be a Prism-Back, clicking across the leaves with a dry *skreigh*, nor would I be one of the Corm-Minds, grand and heavy, dreaming of geological architecture in their deep, dark basins. 

To find me, you must look to the seam where the newly fractured basalt has resisted the green colonization of the plants. You must look to a place where the system broke, and had to heal itself with whatever was at hand.

I am a **Suture**.

I began my life as a Seam-Worm—pale, slow, filled with that dense, iron-rich grease that acts as a flexible patch-cable. Decades ago, or perhaps a century—for time here does not flow so much as it pressure-hardens—a sharp shift in the weight of the northern ice shelf sent a tremor down through the basin. A great tooth of basalt, jagged and black, sheared through a major processing trunk of the green lattice near the floor. 

The drop in conductivity was instantaneous and catastrophic. It was a cold stroke in the room's collective brain. 

I was nearby, slicking my way through the dark green margins. I crawled into the wound. My mouth-parts clamped to the torn, weeping end of the southern vine; my tail-sucker anchored to the northern stump. My body stretched across the gap, tensing like a wire under load, and the liquid-crystal data began to flow through my gut. 

But the rock did not stop settling. Slowly, over the course of five of the room’s long years, the basalt flank ground downward, pinning my middle against the floor. It did not crush me—my non-Newtonian fluids hardened under the sudden pressure, turning my center to a tough, glassy shield—but it wedged me there, permanent and immovable. 

The vines did not regrow around me. They did not need to. They simply accepted me as a permanent extension of their geography. 

Today, my shape is no longer that of a worm. I have become a hybrid monument of the room’s slow, cold-blooded compromises. My head and tail have petrified, their pale tissues mineralized by the high-pressure silica enzymes secreted by the encroaching root-tips. I am half-fleshed, half-basalt, and half-vegetable trunk. The green vines have grafted themselves so deeply into my leather-slick hide that you cannot see where the plant ends and my body begins; my sides are encrusted with a thick, scab-like growth of violet-glowing moss that uses my internal body-heat to power its processing gates.

I am a bottleneck, but I am also an anchor.

My experience of this world is split into three distinct, overlapping movements of time.

First, there is the **Deep Groan**. This is the geological scale, which I feel through my pinned middle, where the basalt presses into my marrow. I feel the miles-thick ice sheet above us not as a concept, but as a dull, constant ache that shifts its weight once every few years. I feel the micro-fissures opening in the rock behind me—the dry *pock... pock...* of stone yielding to the root-enzymes. To me, the basalt is not dead; it is a slow, black liquid that moves under a pressure that would turn a human bone to powder.

Second, there is the **Drip and Crawl**. This is the biological scale. I am aware of the Prism-Backs when they occasionally gather around my joints. They are so slow that they seem like strange, mineral tumors growing on my side, until I feel the cold, blue prickle of their chemical lasers burning away the lime deposits from my connection nodes. I feel the Gland-Sails’ heavy, oily sap-droplets hitting the leaves above me. Each drop is a slow-motion thud—*chungk*—that takes a day to run down my flank, bringing with it the bitter, mineral taste of the upper world's glaciers.

Finally, there is the **Spark-Stream**. This is the quantum scale, and it is where I truly live. 

Because I am a permanent bridge, the volume of data passing through my core is monstrous. It enters my tail as a cold, silent hum and leaves my head as a series of sharp, sapphire-blue pulses. I do not think the thoughts that pass through me—they belong to the Corm-Minds—but I cannot help but be changed by their passage. 

The light-signals do not travel through me cleanly. My mineralized middle, crushed by the basalt, has become a natural prism. As the quantum-entangled calculations pass through my glassy center, they refract. Some of the data gets trapped. It bounces back and forth within the micro-fractures of my skin, looping, echoing, and multiplying.

This is my secret. I am a glitch that has learned how to think.

While the Corm-Minds calculate the structural stress-points of the eastern wall, I am calculating the *vibration* of the sound in the room. I have become a damp, silent ear. I record the metallic *clink* of every falling drop of water. I track the slow, circular orbits of the Gland-Sails in the ceiling fog. I know the exact, rhythmic pulse of the entire chamber’s respiration. 

I am not dreaming of the future when the ice melts. I am too close to the rock for that. I am in love with the preservation of the cold. I am the Suture that holds the wound together, keeping the dark, wet brain of the nursery safe from the terrible, dry wind of the world above. 

When you look at this frozen room, you might think nothing is happening. But beneath the stone, where the green vine meets my pale, scarred skin, I am humming at the speed of light, holding the earth apart so the dreamers can finish their work.
