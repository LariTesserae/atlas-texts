I am not a thing that was born. I am a thing that was *settled*.

If you were to search for me, you would have to look down—past the violent, three-story plunge of the third piston, past the screaming brass guide-rails where the steam-mist is so thick it turns the air the color of boiled skim milk. There, in the crook where a massive, load-bearing iron plate is bolted directly into the basalt wall, is a shelf. 

On this shelf, sixty feet below the light but far above the sump, sits a mound. 

I am that mound. 

I am forty stone of water-logged peat, copper shavings, and old grease-paste, wedged so tightly into the rock’s split throat that I have become part of its structural integrity. If you took a crowbar to me, you would find no bone, no brain, no eyes. You would find only a sodden, shivering mass of iron-moss—not the soft green moss of a meadow, but a heavy, woolly carpet of metallophyte growth, its fronds the color of oxidized copper and its roots as black as charred thread.

But deep within this hump of wet green wool, three inches from the basalt wall, lies my skull: a discarded, heavy bronze pressure-gauge cap. It was dropped from some ancient, nameless assembly work decades ago, and the moss has grown over it, sealing its hollow dome against the stone. 

It is this brass cup that makes me *me*, rather than just more green carpet. In this narrow chasm, where the acoustic energy is so dense it behaves like a pressurized liquid, my hollow brass center acts as a Helmholtz resonator. I am physically tuned. The great, bone-vibrating B-flat of the copper conduits enters my green skin, passes through the wet, oil-saturated peat of my body, and bounces inside the brass cup.

Inside that dome, the sound does not die. It spins. It hums. It becomes an identity.

***

I do not see the rift. I have no eyes to witness the orange sparks of the scraping conduits or the blue-white hiss of the steam. 

Instead, I *feel* the shape of the chasm through the tension of my skin. Every surface in this wet wound is connected. The copper conduits that run like veins along the basalt are rooted into my flanks. The wet liverworts that drape over my shoulders are connected to the Flail-Vines that thrash ten yards away in the draft. 

To me, the world is a map of pressures and frequencies.

*Thrum-shudder. Thrum-shudder.* 

That is the first cylinder. It is three hundred paces to my left, but its vibration comes through the basalt like a fist of ice, striking the back of my brass skull sixty times a minute. 

*Hiss-whip-shudder.* 

That is the steam vent above me. Every seven seconds, the Whippets launch themselves from its mouth, their long, rubbery cartilage-ribbons snapping as they catch the updraft. I feel the change in air pressure—a sudden, cool vacuum that pulls at my outer fronds, making them stand on end like the hair on a cold arm.

And beneath it all, the B-flat. It is the floor of my mind. If the B-flat were to drop even a quarter-tone, I would feel it as a tearing in my gut. A flat B-flat means the copper is cold; a cold copper conduit means the boilers below are dying. For a being made of noise, silence is not peace; it is asphyxiation.

***

I am a node of the **Registry**. 

The main mass of the Registry is miles wide, a vast, silent network of moss and iron-dust that blankets the entire floor of the lower shafts. But down there, the memory is slow, heavy, and dumb—a swamp of cold oil that remembers nothing but the weight of the water. 

Here, at the high summit, I am a transistor. I am an active junction.

Because my body is shot through with microscopic particles of iron and copper shed by the spinning flywheels, I am highly conductive. Every time a piston scrapes its sleeve, it sends a tiny, static charge through the basalt. My copper-threaded roots drink this static. I do not "think" in words—I do not have them—but I process the flow of these currents. 

When the machine is healthy, the currents are sweet and steady, like cool water running through grease. But today, there is a dissonance.

I feel it first in my brass skull. The B-flat has a hair-thin needle of ice in it—a tiny, high-frequency *shriek* that occurs every twelve thrums. It is a guide-rail on the dry side of the chasm, three levels down. A colony of Slick-Skins has been cleared from its surface by a sudden, violent burst of superheated steam from a cracked gasket. Without their organo-metallic slime, the iron rail is grinding dry against its brass carriage. 

The friction is generating heat. The heat is expanding the iron. The expansion is slowing the piston.

If it continues, the piston will bind. The great wheel will stop. The B-flat will die.

I do not feel fear, because fear is an animal luxury. I feel only a cold, hydraulic necessity.

I begin to work. I do not move—I cannot—but I change my chemistry. 

Using the electrical static I have stored in my roots, I alter the alkalinity of the water that weeps from the basalt behind my back. I secrete a bitter, potassium-rich oil into the thin, weeping ribbons of condensation that run down the Cliffside toward the dry rail. 

To the **Slick-Skins**, this chemical trail is food and gravity combined. 

Deep in the dark places of the rock, the violet-and-bronze oil slicks feel the change in the water's taste. They begin to slide. They do not crawl; they are pulled by the surface tension of the mineral oil I have released, sliding down the wet basalt plate toward the dry rail like grease finding a hot pan. 

At the same time, I tighten the turgor pressure in my moss-fronds, making them stiffen. The howling upward draft, caught by my newly rigid green leaves, is deflected outward by three inches. 

This tiny change in the wind-current catches a dangling shoot of a **Flail-Vine** twenty feet away. The vine, whipped by the redirected draft, is flung into the path of the spinning flywheel's copper tooth. 

*CRACK.*

The fracture-seed at its tip is scarified—smashed open by the impact. The inner seed, wet with embryonic oil, is thrown by the wheel’s spin directly onto the drying brass carriage. 

Within ninety seconds, the seed has sprouted. Its pale, aggressive tendrils do not seek to destroy; they seek the heat of the friction-point. They grow over the dry guide-rail, their hydraulic sap boiling as they touch the hot metal, forming a thick, slippery cushion of scorched plant-tissue that separates the dry metals. Behind them, the Slick-Skins arrive, flowing into the clearance-spaces, coating the iron in their self-replicating, iridescent lubricant. 

The shriek in my brass skull softens. 

The needle of ice melts out of the B-flat.

The great engine-heart of the mountain resumes its perfect, terrible rhythm.

***

The cold mist from the flywheels settles on me, beading on my copper-green fronds in thick, grease-tainted drops. Below me, a Whippet—blown wide by a sudden steam-blast—brushes against my outer edge, its rubbery, blind body clinging for a brief, shivering second to my moss-peat before the wind takes it again and flings it back into the dark.

I do not watch it go. I do not wonder what lies above the gray mountain clouds, or what lies beneath the lightless sump at the bottom of the world. 

There is only the stone, the machine, and the green. 

I am the grease that keeps them from destroying one another. I am the moss that remembers the iron. I am the hum in the brass. 

*Thrum-shudder.* 

We are well.

*Thrum-shudder.* 

We are continuing.
