Preparing to visit this location requires a shift in mindset. You are not entering a natural cave, nor are you entering a built environment. You are entering a **proto-physics transition state**—a pocket of reality that was paused mid-execution. 

To survive and comprehend this room, an ordinary human must prepare for a landscape that is physically hostile not out of malice, but out of sheer, unrefined sharpness and experimental physical laws.

Here is how you must prepare.

---

### 1. Visual Protection: Glare without Heat
The light in this room is one of its most dangerous elements. It is a high-intensity, shadow-lagging white glare with no thermal output. It mimics a salt flat at noon, which will cause photokeratitis (snow blindness) within minutes of exposure, but because the air is cool, your survival instincts won't warn you of the burn.
*   **The Gear:** You must wear **Category 4 Glacier Goggles** with side shields. Do not use standard sunglasses; you need maximum light reduction and polarized lenses to navigate the "hyper-flat" planes of the un-weathered basalt. 
*   **The Phenomenon:** Do not panic when your shadow lags behind you. When you move, your shadow will remain where you were for three to five seconds before "snapping" to your current position. **Do not look back at your lagging shadow.** It creates a severe cognitive dissonance that can cause nausea or vertigo. Keep your eyes forward.

### 2. Environmental Suit: Preventing the "Glass Cut"
The basalt has not been subjected to wind, water, or time. Its edges are molecularly sharp—like fresh obsidian or broken glass. Furthermore, the air is thin and autumn-cool.
*   **The Gear:** Standard hiking gear is useless here; the rock will shred it. You require a **Kevlar-reinforced tactical jump suit** or puncture-resistant aramid-weave gaiters and trousers. 
*   **Gloves:** Heavy-duty, cut-resistant steel-mesh reinforced gloves are non-negotiable. If you slip and put a hand out to steady yourself, ordinary leather or nylon will slice open instantly.
*   **Footwear:** Mountaineering boots with stiff, high-traction soles. Avoid soft-rubber hiking shoes; the sharp edges of the basalt steps will shear the soles off.

### 3. Respiratory and Throat Protection: The Rattle Filter
The lavender lichen’s seed pods vibrate to process the ambient glare, creating a sub-audible, ultra-high-frequency rattle. While you cannot hear it, your body will feel it. It manifests as a dry, cold tickle in the back of the throat that, over time, can cause laryngitis, involuntary gagging, or coughing fits that could make you lose your balance on the sharp ledges.
*   **The Gear:** Wear a **closed-circuit respirator or a heavy-duty neoprene mask** that covers the nose and throat. This does not just filter the air (though the air is thin and dustless); the material acts as a dampener, absorbing the physical micro-vibrations before they can resonate in your trachea.
*   **Soothe:** Pack demulcent throat lozenges (slippery elm or pectin-based) to coat your throat before entering.

### 4. Hydration and Chemical Isolation: Do Not Touch the Water
The water in this room is not water as you know it. It is a byproduct of the room’s unique predatory lifecycle, where living entities are collapsed into liquid spheres upon impact. It defies gravity, rolling up vertical planes, and behaves as if the basalt is hydrophobic.
*   **The Rule:** **Leave the water alone.** Do not attempt to collect it, filter it, or drink it. If a trembling sphere of water rolls toward you, pull your limbs away. If it makes contact with your skin, it may try to migrate across your body toward a dry "fissure" (like your nose, ears, or mouth).
*   **Your Supply:** Carry a pressurized, insulated water bladder inside your suit with a bite-valve. Keep it entirely sealed from the external environment.

### 5. Interacting with the Artifact: Spatial Density Prep
The tiny, four-inch stone wall and the fan-peeled rodent bone in the center of the room exist in a pocket of higher spatial density. 
*   **The Phenomenon:** As your hand approaches the wall, it will appear to shrink. This is not an optical illusion; your fingers are entering a space where the metric of distance is compressed. 
*   **The Danger:** If you reach in too fast, the gradient differential between your wrist (normal space) and your fingertips (dense space) can cause extreme joint strain or cellular shearing. 
*   **How to Touch:** If you must touch it, **use a tool**—a carbon-fiber reacher or a titanium probe. Do not use your bare hands. If you must use your hand, move at a glacial pace, allowing your hand to adjust to the density scaling millimeter by millimeter.

### 6. Navigating the Grey Ceiling and the "Sealed" Exit
The ceiling is a plane of absolute nothingness. When you look at it, your breath will fog against it, yet there is nothing there to touch. The entrance you used will close behind you, sealing into a seamless basalt wall.
*   **Mental Preparation:** Claustrophobia will hit you differently here. You are not trapped in a dark hole; you are trapped in a **bright, silent, infinite box**. The lack of sound (save for the occasional *tlick* of a water droplet breaking) can drive a human to panic quickly. 
*   **The Exit Strategy:** Because this room exists in a state of "unresolved physics," physical force (like hammers or drills) will not open the basalt seam. To escape, you must exploit the room's temporary laws. 
    *   Bring a **sub-harmonic transducer** or a simple, highly resonant tuning fork. 
    *   If you strike a tuning fork against the basalt, the vibration will disrupt the local tension. The seamless basalt wall where you entered will briefly remember its "broken" state, fracturing open along its original cleavage planes for a few minutes. 
    *   Alternatively, carry a **tether beacon**—a quantum-linked device left outside the steppe boundary that can pull your molecular signature back through the solidifying stone.
