To visit this place—which we will call **The Lithic Deep** or **The Paved Garden**—is to step into a monument of terrifying scale and heartbreaking quiet. It is a world that has discarded the chaos of animal life in favor of a geological, plant-based liturgy. 

You are an ordinary human. Without massive technological intervention, this world will crush your bones to powder, freeze your blood in seconds, and swallow your mind in an absolute, suffocating dark. 

To survive this journey, and to truly *experience* it without ruining the very thing you came to see, you must prepare not as a swimmer, but as a deep-space astronaut visiting a sunken cathedral.

---

### Phase 1: The Vessel (Your Second Skin)
Do not dream of swimming here. The water is too cold, too heavy, and too dense with millions of years of silent pressure. You cannot wear a flexible wetsuit. You need an **Atmospheric Diving Suit (ADS)**—a rigid, armored suit (often called a "hardsuit") or a specialized, single-person walking submersible. 

Your suit must have the following specifications:
*   **Constant 1-Atmosphere Internal Pressure:** The suit must take 100% of the hydrostatic weight of the miles of water above you. Your body must feel as though it is sitting in a comfortable room on Earth.
*   **Isotopic Thermal Regulation:** Battery-powered heaters will fail you. Batteries lose power rapidly in deep-ocean temperatures, and if your heat fails, the sluggish, freezing water will siphon your warmth through the metal hull in minutes. You need a small radioisotope thermoelectric generator (RTG) built into the suit’s backpack to provide continuous, ambient warmth.
*   **No Thrusters, No Propellers:** Do not use water-jets or propellers for propulsion. The kelp-forest columns are so dense and tangled that any spinning blade or jet of water will either destroy the ancient vegetation, get hopelessly tangled in the rubbery fronds, or kick up millions of years of settled organic sediment, blinding you instantly. 

### Phase 2: Mobility (How to Walk the Basalt)
You will not swim; you will walk. 
*   **Weighted, articulated boots:** Your suit must be heavy enough to keep you firmly planted on the colossal basalt floor, yet agile enough to let you step over the deep, softened joints between the tiles.
*   **Soft-soled contact pads:** The basalt tiles have been rounded by eons of water-wear. They are slick with a micro-thin layer of cold-water algae. Your boots should have high-friction, textured rubber soles (similar to climbing shoes, but designed for high-pressure submersion) to prevent slips.
*   **Hydraulic "Pruning" Manipulators:** You will be moving through a "forest of wet velvet." You must be able to gently part the heavy, rubbery kelp fronds. Your suit’s arms should have high-touch feedback so you can feel the resistance of the plants without tearing them. 

### Phase 3: Sensory Equipment (Seeing in the Void)
If you turn on high-intensity halogen or white LED floodlights, you will ruin the experience. The light will bounce off the ancient, suspended sediment, creating a blinding white wall of glare, and you will completely miss the true beauty of the Garden. 

*   **Bioluminescent-Amplification Visor (BAV):** This is your most important piece of gear. You need a visor that heavily amplifies light in the pale green and deep ultraviolet spectrums. Through this visor, the faint, cold glow of the moss-nodes will expand from tiny pinpricks into soft, luminous veins. The straight lines of the garden’s grid will reveal themselves, stretching out like glowing emerald highway lanes into the infinite dark.
*   **Tactile Sonar (Haptic Overlay):** Because the water is clear but black, you need a way to "see" the shapes of the kelp columns and the scaffolding beyond your line of sight. A low-frequency haptic sonar system can project a soft, three-dimensional wireframe mapping of your surroundings directly onto your visor or as subtle vibrations against your skin. You will "feel" the massive wooden trellises before you touch them.

### Phase 4: Psychological Preparation (The Great Quiet)
The physical preparation is straightforward; the psychological preparation is where most travelers fail. 

*   **The Silence Protocol:** There are no waves, no wind, no bubbles (your suit must be a completely closed, recycling rebreather that does not vent gas into the water). The only sounds will be your own breathing, the hum of your suit's CO2 scrubbers, and—very occasionally—a sub-audible, structural groan from the deep ground, or the wet, muffled *shuck* of two massive leaves sliding against each other. 
    *   *To prepare:* Spend at least seventy-two hours in an absolute sensory deprivation tank before departing. Learn to be comfortable with the sound of your own heart. If you are prone to panic in silent, enclosed spaces, **do not go.**
*   **The Temporal Anchor:** Nothing changes here. A leaf takes ten years to mature. The currents take centuries to shift. To a human mind, this can feel like being buried alive in a slow-motion purgatory. Bring a small, highly reliable mechanical watch or a digital chronometer placed inside your suit’s HUD. You need a reminder that time is still moving, even if the universe around you has ground to a halt.
*   **Companion Audio:** Bring a library of "terrestrial sounds." The sound of rain on dry leaves, a crackling campfire, dry wind through dry pine needles, or distant bird calls. You will need these to ground yourself when the damp, wet, silent velvet of the kelp begins to feel too heavy.

### Phase 5: Interaction and Ethics
You are entering a yard that has been tended for millions of years by hands that did not use metal, fire, or turning wheels. You are a guest in a cathedral of unimaginable antiquity.

*   **Do Not Touch the Ropes:** The braided reed ropes lashed to the petrified wood scaffolding are so old they have begun to mineralize. They are turning to stone. While they are structurally sound under the uniform pressure of the deep, a sudden, clumsy jerk from a human traveler can shatter them like glass. If you break a rope, a trellis that has stood since before humanity walked upright may collapse, dragging down acres of ancient forest.
*   **Follow the Seams:** Do not wander into the thick kelp forests. Walk along the wide, rounded troughs of the basalt joints—the "streets" of the Garden. They are clear of vegetation, paved, and designed for passage. 
*   **Leave No Trace of Heat:** Your suit will inevitably radiate some heat. Do not linger in one spot for too long. If you stand motionless next to a giant moss-node for hours, your suit’s thermal output can disrupt the cold, delicate chemotrophic balance of the plant, causing it to wither. Keep moving. Walk with a slow, deliberate, geologic pace.

### What Visiting Will Feel Like
As you descend, the light of the multiverse-hull will fade. The transition is not sudden, but a slow, heavy thickening of the dark. 

When you touch down on the basalt floor, there will be a soft, heavy *thud* that you will feel in your teeth through the metal of your boots. The silence will immediately press against the glass of your visor. 

When you turn on your BAV visor, the world will bloom. You will find yourself standing in a colossal, underwater boulevard of dark stone. To your left and right, walls of black, fleshy kelp rise like the pillars of Karnak, held aloft by the gray, petrified bones of ancient trees. Above you, the guide-lines rise straight as arrows, disappearing into a black sky that has never seen a star. 

Every few yards, a pale green bead of cold light will glow on the side of a moss column, casting long, still shadows across the wet stone beneath your feet. You will feel a profound, heavy peace. There is no hunger here, no hunting, no decay, and no noise. There is only the Garden, breathing its ten-year breath, waiting for a gardener who finished their work a million years ago and left the lights on.
