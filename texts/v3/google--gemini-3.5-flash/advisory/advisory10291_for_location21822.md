### Preparing for the Heights of Oru: A Multiverse Traveler’s Field Guide

To visit the Heights of Oru is to step inside a gargantuan, self-lubricating engine of flesh, iron, and stone. It is a world where the boundary between "geological," "mechanical," and "living" has been thoroughly dissolved. 

For an ordinary human traveler, Oru is not immediately hostile in the way a vacuum or an acid-sea is, but it is **highly corrosive, physically grueling, and sensory-overloading.** If you do not prepare correctly, you will slip, you will ruin your equipment, you will rot your skin, and you will find yourself unable to cope with the relentless, vibrating noise.

Here is how you must prepare.

---

### Pack List & Gear Specifications

#### 1. Footwear: The "Spiked Felt" Solution
Standard hiking boots—even those with high-end rubber lugs—are useless here. The wet basalt is slicked with a micro-thin layer of diatomaceous slime and the iridescent, non-drying mucus of the grease-mats. 
*   **Do not wear rubber soles.** They will turn your feet into skis.
*   **Obtain white-water wading boots with felt soles and tungsten carbide studs.** The felt cuts through the organic slime to grab the rock, while the metal studs bite into the wet schist. 
*   **Gaiters are mandatory.** High, zippered, neoprene gaiters will keep the crushed, razor-sharp fragments of shingle-crusts and the caustic, iodine-rich discharge of the tube-worms out of your boots.

#### 2. Outerwear: The Death of Gore-Tex
Modern breathable membranes (like Gore-Tex) function on vapor pressure. In Oru, where the ambient humidity is a constant, warm 100%, these membranes fail instantly. Sweat cannot evaporate, and the wet exterior "wets out" the fabric, leaving you cold, clammy, and stewing in your own sweat.
*   **Wear heavy waxed canvas or traditional oilskin.** It physically sheds the constant, weeping drip of the cliffs. It is heavy, yes, but it protects against the jagged iron corners of the city, the abrasive basalt, and the occasional splash of hot mechanical grease.
*   **Underlayers:** Merino wool or specialized synthetic fishing rash-guards. They retain their insulating properties even when damp, and they dry relatively quickly when you find a dry thermal-vent room.
*   **The Leather Apron:** Once you arrive in Oru, purchase a local oiled-leather utility apron. It protects your midsection when leaning over wet, salt-crusted iron railings and provides pockets that drain water naturally.

#### 3. Vision: The Glare-and-Fog Problem
Because the high-altitude sun sits directly above a heavy ceiling of wet glass, the light does not fall; it scatters. The mist glows from within like a massive flourescent tube. Glass lenses fog up the second you step off the arrival platform.
*   **Pack double-paned, chemically anti-fogged goggles** (similar to those used in ice-diving or high-humidity tactical operations). 
*   **Amber or rose-tinted lenses** are highly recommended. The ambient light is a flat, blinding grey-white; tinted lenses will help you differentiate between flat wet schist, slippery grease-mat trails, and rusted iron plates.
*   **Bring a chamois cloth** on a retractable lanyard anchored inside your coat to wipe your lenses every three to five minutes.

#### 4. The "Steam-Stick" (Essential Safety Gear)
Because Oru runs on pressurized steam, and because the air is constantly thick with mist, a high-pressure steam leak is **completely invisible** to the naked eye. Walking into a pinprick leak of superheated steam can amputate a limb or cook your lungs before you realize it is there.
*   Carry a **one-meter ash wooden dowel with a strip of high-visibility pink silk ribbon tied to the end.** 
*   As you walk near the great drive-shafts, steam-pipes, and aqueducts, hold the stick out in front of you. If the ribbon suddenly shreds, burns, or flutters violently in a spot where there is no wind, **stop immediately.** You have found a leak.

### Physiological & Mental Conditioning

#### 1. Pulmonary Acclimatization
The air in Oru feels like the air inside a crowded sauna that has been built next to a fish market. It is thick with the smell of iodine (from the tube-worms), sulfurous coal-smoke, boiled mineral oil, and the dry, metallic tang of hot rust.
*   **During your first 48 hours, expect a mild panic response.** Your brain will feel as though it cannot catch its breath, even though oxygen levels are sufficient. 
*   **Bring a nasal inhaler** scented with pine, eucalyptus, or menthol. Sniffing this will help clear your sinuses of the thick, mineral-laden mist and mask the sickeningly sweet, biological smell of the rendering vats.
*   **Hydrate with electrolytes.** You will sweat constantly, but because the air is wet, you won't realize how much moisture you are losing. Drink water treated with salt and potassium.

#### 2. Acoustic Survival: The Low-Frequency Hum
Your ears will be assaulted by a three-tiered acoustic wall: the high *shriek* of steam valves, the wooden *clack-clack-clack* of billions of closing shells, and the bone-shaking *throb* of the iron drive-shafts. This hum bypasses your ears and vibrates through the soles of your feet directly into your chest cavity.
*   **Pack moldable silicone earplugs.** Foam plugs will absorb moisture from the air, swell up, and fall out of your ear canals. 
*   **Mental Prep:** The clicking of the shingle-crusts sounds exactly like a raging forest fire. If you are prone to fire-anxiety, prepare yourself for this auditory illusion. It is just the mountain breathing.

### Navigating the Human & Ecological Landscape

#### 1. Adopt the "Oru Pace"
In this world, haste is a design flaw.
*   **Never run.** If you trip, the black basalt will act like a giant cheese grater on your skin, and the wound will instantly become infected by the biological slime coating the stone.
*   **Watch the locals.** They move with a swinging, wide-legged gait, keeping their center of gravity low. They look twice before placing a foot. 
*   **When using handrails, check them first.** Do not grab bare iron; the rust can slice your palms. Only grab rails wrapped in tarred rope or those painted with black coal-tar.

#### 2. Biological Etiquette
*   **Do not step on the grease-mats.** First, they are slicker than ice. Second, they are living property. They are the city's lubricant source, and killing one by slipping through its mantle is treated as property damage.
*   **Keep your hands away from the pink "blushing" walls.** While the tube-worms are harmless, they are highly sensitive. Touching them causes millions of sharp, leathery tubes to retract simultaneously, creating a wet vacuum that can pinch your skin or pull you off balance.
*   **Watch the sky.** The "kite" drifters are harmless to humans, but their trailing tentacles are coated in highly sticky, alkaline mucus meant to catch midges. If a tentacle drapes across your face, it will cause chemical burns similar to a jellyfish sting. Carry a small bottle of mild vinegar to neutralize the alkaline slime if brushed.

#### 3. Eating on the Terraces
You will not find fresh fruit, vegetables, or bread here. 
*   The primary food is **Worm-Paste.** It is nutritionally complete but has the texture of wet clay and tastes intensely of salt, iron, and rotting seaweed. 
*   **Bring your own souring agents.** Pack powdered vitamin C, dried lemons, or citric acid crystals. The local diet lacks acidity entirely, and after three days of eating fat-heavy, iodine-rich Oru fare, your stomach will desperately crave acid to digest it.

### Final Advice
At noon, when the mist turns into a burning, pearlescent fog and the great iron wheels turn their slow, dripping arcs against the cliffs, Oru is fiercely, devastatingly beautiful. It looks like a city built inside the gills of a great, divine fish. Respect the slow pace of the stone, wear your oilskins proudly, and never, ever rush.
