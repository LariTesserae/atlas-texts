### Field Guide: Preparing for the Basin of the Sleeping Beasts (World Designation: *The Finished Work*)

This is a world that has run out of questions. It has resolved its geography, settled its climate, and allowed its geology to fall into a deep, permanent sleep. Visiting this place is not a matter of surviving hostile monsters or extreme temperatures; it is a matter of surviving a landscape that is completely, beautifully, and terrifyingly indifferent to anything new. 

An ordinary human traveler can visit this world safely, but only with meticulous preparation. Below is the operational guide to preparing your body, gear, and mind for *The Finished Work*.

---

### Part I: Mobility & Traction (The Wet Red Granite)
You will be walking on a world made of polished, wet, iron-rich granite. There are no flat, dry surfaces, no soil to dig your heels into, and no handholds. The rock is shaped like the backs of sleeping whales, constantly weeping with a greasy film of warm condensation and coated in patches of slimy orange liverwort. 

*   **The Footwear Crisis:** Standard hiking boots—even those with high-end Vibram soles—will fail you here. They will turn into ice skates on the wet liverwort. 
    *   **The Solution:** You must wear **felt-soled wading boots with tungsten-carbide studs** (the kind used by extreme fly-fishermen walking on mossy riverbeds). The felt conforms to the microscopic texture of the polished granite, while the metal studs bite through the rubbery liverwort to grip the stone beneath.
    *   **Ankle Support:** Because of the curved, dome-like nature of the terrain, your ankles will constantly be angling outward or inward. Bring high-top boots with rigid, orthotic ankle bracing to prevent fatigue and sprains. 
*   **The Silt Hazard:** Between the granite domes are basins of deep, grey-brown silt. It looks solid under the silver glare, but it is a non-Newtonian trap. Step into it, and you may slowly sink to your hips in sour, thick mud.
    *   **The Gear:** You must carry **dual carbon-fiber trekking poles with oversized mud baskets** (snow baskets). Before stepping off a red stone dome into the grey channels, probe the silt. If the pole sinks more than twelve inches without striking hard rock, find another way around.

### Part II: Environmental Suit & Skin Care (Drowning in Air)
The atmosphere is not toxic, but it is a slow-acting poison to human biology because of its physical properties. The air is warm (roughly 88°F / 31°C) and sits at a permanent, unyielding 100% humidity. There is no breeze to cool you. Your sweat will not evaporate; it will pool inside your clothes, mixing with the greasy condensation of the air.

*   **The Hazard: Skin Maceration and Core Overheating.** If you wear standard clothing, your skin will begin to prune, soften, and slough off within twelve hours. This is called "immersion foot" or "trench skin," and it will occur across your entire body. Furthermore, your body cannot self-regulate its temperature because sweat cannot evaporate into saturated air.
*   **The Clothing Setup:** 
    *   **The Underlayer:** Do not wear cotton, wool, or standard polyesters. You need an **ultra-thin, hydrophobic, antimicrobial silver-threaded base layer**. This prevents fungal growth (which thrives in this warm, stagnant mist) and keeps the fabric from holding water against your skin.
    *   **The Active Barrier (The Dehum-Suit):** The ideal setup is a lightweight, fully enclosed, breathable environmental suit equipped with a **micro-climate cooling unit**. This is a small, battery-operated backpack that circulates cool, dry air through bladder channels inside your suit. It artificially creates a pocket of 40% humidity against your skin, allowing your natural sweat glands to actually work.
    *   **Skin Barrier Creams:** Before dressing, coat your feet, groin, armpits, and neck in a thick layer of **zinc oxide barrier cream** (similar to diaper rash ointment) or pure lanolin. This creates a physical, waterproof barrier that prevents the atmosphere from waterlogging your skin.

### Part III: Sensory Defenses (The Wall of Sound and Light)
This world is not quiet, and it does not have shadows. It is a massive acoustic and optical chamber that will wear down your sanity within forty-eight hours if you do not protect your senses.

*   **Visual Protection (Shadowless Glare):** The permanent luminous mist hanging twenty feet above the ground acts like a giant, sky-sized softbox. There are no shadows to give the landscape depth. Everything—the wet red rock, the silver water pools, the grey silt—glows with a flat, greasy, silver sheen. Your depth perception will fail, and you will experience severe eye strain (similar to snow blindness).
    *   **The Gear:** **Polarized, high-contrast amber goggles.** The polarization cuts through the greasy water reflections on the rock, and the amber tint increases contrast, allowing you to actually see where a wet rock slope ends and a deep pool begins.
*   **Acoustic Protection (The Orchestral Din):** The flat granite acts as a sounding board. The clicking of billions of armored grazers, the water-struck *chink-chink-chink* of mud-creatures, and the thunderous, low-frequency bellows of the pale amphibians will vibrate through your bones. The sound is constant; it does not stop at night because there is no true night—only a shift from silver-grey to a dull, warm copper.
    *   **The Gear:** You must bring **active, military-grade electronic ear protection**. These headset units should be programmed to compress extreme decibels (the booming amphibian bellows) while allowing you to hear your own footsteps or companion's voice, combined with an active noise-cancellation feature that can completely mute the world when you need to sleep.

### Part IV: Camp & Sustenance (How to Exist in a Flat Wetness)
There is no "dry land" to pitch a tent. The rock is hard granite, meaning you cannot drive a tent stake. The pools are acidic and filled with ancient anaerobic bacteria, and the vegetation is inedible, leathery, and sparse.

*   **The Sleeping Platform:** You cannot sleep on the ground. Water is constantly weeping down the rocks, and the tiny, armored, scraping grazers will climb over any surface in their path.
    *   **The Gear:** A **portable, inflatable, self-supporting bivy hammock or "portaledge"** designed with heavy-duty suction cup anchors. Because the granite is polished perfectly smooth by eons of rain, industrial-grade suction cups can adhere to the rock with immense force. You can anchor an inflatable sleeping platform directly to the curving dome of a granite beast.
    *   Keep your tent zipped at all times. The insectoid life here is not aggressive, but they are curious and constantly seeking salt—which your sweat-soaked body provides in abundance.
*   **Water and Food:** 
    *   Do not drink the standing water. It is rich in dissolved minerals, iron, and ancient, sulfur-reducing bacteria. It smells like a wet, anaerobic swamp.
    *   **The Gear:** A high-capacity **electro-adsorptive water purifier** combined with an **activated carbon filter** to strip the heavy swamp stink from the water. Even better, carry a portable solar still to evaporate and condense pure water from the pools.
    *   Bring all of your own food in sealed, odor-proof bags. The local flora is mostly indigestible cellulose and leather, and the fauna is heavily armored and likely toxic.

### Part V: The Psychological Reality (The Weight of the Closed Book)
When you step into *The Finished Work*, you are stepping into a museum of deep time. It is a world that has reached the end of its evolutionary and geological journey. There are no sudden storms, no earthquakes, no volcanic eruptions, and no new species waiting to evolve. It is a closed loop, humming a song it has sung for a billion years.

*   **The Feeling of Intrusion:** You will feel incredibly loud. Not because you are making noise, but because your very existence—your rapid breathing, your ticking watch, your synthetic clothes—is a disruption to a perfect, stale equilibrium.
*   **The "Slow Time" Melancholy:** The absolute stability of the world can be psychologically oppressive. The lack of wind, the unchanging temperature, and the shadowless sky create a feeling that time has stopped. 
*   **The Mental Practice:** Bring a physical book, a sketchbook, or something that requires dry, focused, human attention. Every evening, when you retreat into your dry, pressurized bivy tent, wash your hands in clean water, apply dry powder to your skin, and engage in something that reminds you of home. 

You are a traveler in a world that has retired. Respect its rest, grip the red stone carefully, and do not expect it to make room for you.
