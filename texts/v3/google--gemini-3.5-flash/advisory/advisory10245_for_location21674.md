### FIELD MANUAL: TRAVELER’S ADVISORY
**Destination:** The Well (The Suspended Cistern of the Obsidian Teeth)  
**Risk Class:** Severe (Acoustic, Physical, and Spatial Anomalies)  
**Primary Directive:** Maintain structural integrity; manage sensory input; do not agitate the medium.

---

Visiting the Well is not a dive, and it is not a climb. It is a violent collision between two incompatible biomes. You will be standing on a freezing, gasping-scarce mountain summit, and with a single step forward, you will plunge into a boiling, screaming, high-pressure oceanic womb. 

An ordinary, unprotected human body will survive no more than three seconds in the Well before sufferance of biological failure. To explore this place and return with your sanity and skeleton intact, you must prepare for a environment where physics has abandoned its architecture.

---

### I. Environmental Defense: The Dual-State Suit

Standard mountaineering gear will freeze you to death before you reach the Well; standard scuba gear will implode under the Well's inexplicable, localized pressure. You need a **Hybrid Atmospheric-Pressurized Exosuit (HAPE)**.

*   **The Outer Shell:** The obsidian spires ("The Teeth") below are not weathered; they are freshly erupted, glass-sharp razor traps. Your suit must be layered with **heavy-gauge aramid weave (Kevlar-12 or equivalent)** over a titanium-reinforced exoskeleton. A casual brush against a basalt peak will rip standard neoprene like wet paper.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** The ascent to the Well is through freezing mountain winds. Inside the Well, the water is "bath-warm" and heated further by sulfurous volcanic venting. Your suit must feature active, automated thermoregulation. The moment you cross the membrane, the suit’s cooling pumps must engage instantly to prevent thermal shock, which can trigger cardiac arrest.
*   **Tactile Soles:** The bottom of your boots must not rely on traditional spikes (which will shatter against the non-Newtonian water or get wedged in the obsidian). Use **high-density silicone suction cups** inspired by the native anemones, or electromagnetically-assisted ballast boots designed to grip glass-smooth, vibrating basalt.

---

### II. Acoustic Mitigation: Defeating the "3.0 Screech"

The greatest threat in the Well is not drowning; it is the sound. The noise is a physical weapon. The churning, shearing currents and tectonic hums will liquefy an unprotected ear canal and cause fatal internal hemorrhaging in the lungs.

*   **Active Cranial Dampening:** You must wear a hard-shell helmet lined with **acoustic dampening gel**. Traditional earplugs are useless because the sound is a *vibration* that travels through your skull, jaw, and collarbone. 
*   **Sub-Harmonic Deflectors:** Your suit should include a chest-mounted active-phase canceller. This device emits counter-vibrations to protect your chest cavity. Without this, the 3.0-scale sub-bass hum will cause your lungs to vibrate in sympathy with the stone below, leading to rapid alveolar collapse (you will drown in your own blood, despite having oxygen).
*   **The "Silent" Voids:** Be warned. When you pass into one of the drifting air-spheres, the sound will cease instantly. This is not a relief; it is a trap. The sudden drop in acoustic pressure can rupture eardrums that have adjusted to the roar. If your head enters a void, **hold your breath and keep your eyes shut.** The air inside may be pressurized or a partial vacuum.

---

### III. Locomotion in Frayed Physics

The water in the Well does not behave like terrestrial water. It obeys a broken gravity and acts as a non-Newtonian fluid under the immense vibrations.

*   **Move Like Molasses:** Do not make sudden, jerky movements. If you thrash, kick hard, or strike your hand out to catch yourself, the water will instantly harden into a glass-like solid under the impact. You can break your own wrist by throwing a fast punch in this water. Move with slow, sweeping, continuous strokes. Let your body flow *with* the water.
*   **Beware of Drifting Blocks:** The amphitheater ruins are unstable. Because gravity is broken here, multi-ton basalt blocks float like Styrofoam but retain their massive inertia. A drifting block moving at two miles per hour carries enough kinetic energy to crush your torso against a spire. If a block approaches, do not push off it quickly (which will harden the water and jar your spine); gently guide yourself around its trajectory using slow, steady pressure.
*   **The Ascent/Descent Margin:** There is no gradual transition. The boundary between the cold mountain air and the warm, dark ocean is a membrane less than an inch thick. When you leave the Well, you will transition instantly from a dense, bath-warm liquid to freezing, oxygen-deprived mountain air. Keep your helmet sealed until you are at least fifty yards down the mountain slope, away from the steaming froth of the boundary.

---

### IV. Visuals and Navigation in the Olive Twilight

The light inside the Well is a dim, shifting olive-gray. You will be operating in a world of silhouettes.

*   **Avoid High-Powered Lights:** It is tempting to bring powerful halogen or LED searchlights. Do not. The water is highly turbid, filled with micro-bubbles, sulfurous silt, and shearing boundaries. High-powered beams will scatter, blinding you in a wall of white glare. Use **polarized green-spectrum lasers** or **low-frequency LIDAR imaging** projected onto your helmet’s HUD to map the contours of the five Teeth.
*   **Do Not Rely on Depth Gauges:** Traditional depth gauges operate on hydrostatic pressure relative to gravity. Here, the gravity is broken and the pressure is localized. Your depth gauge will spin uselessly or read dangerous spikes while you are only three feet below the surface. Use **acoustic distance-measuring tools** focused on the mountaintops to know how deep inside the sphere you are.

---

### V. Interaction with the Foreign Ecology

Life here is minimal, desperate, and highly specialized.

*   **The Pale Sucking Discs:** In the crevices of the basalt spires, you will find pale, translucent, anemone-like tissue. Do not touch them. They have survived this screaming hell by clinging with absolute, molecular force. If your gloved hand brushes one, it may latch onto your suit. They have no teeth or venom, but their suction is so powerful that removing them will likely tear your suit’s protective outer membrane.
*   **The Ribbon Creatures:** You may see three-foot, eyeless ribbons whipped by the currents. They are harmless but tragic—victims of the Well's violent cross-currents. If one is whipped toward you, do not try to catch it. Its skin is fragile, and the impact against your armored suit will shred it instantly. Let the currents take it.

---

### VI. The Psychological Drift

The Well is a deeply paranoiac space. The sky is below you, the mountain is inside the water, and the silence of the air-voids is more terrifying than the roar of the currents.

*   **The Tether is Life:** You must anchor a high-tensile steel winch to the cold rock *outside* the water sphere before you enter. This is your lifeline. The currents inside the Well are chaotic and can spin you into the central hollow where there is nothing but dark, roaring water and zero gravity. If you lose your orientation, do not try to swim. Engage the winch and let it drag you backward through the membrane.
*   **The Hum:** Even with dampening gear, you will *feel* the sub-bass in your teeth and bones. It has a low-frequency cycling rate that can induce nausea, optical illusions, and a profound sense of doom. If you begin to hear "voices" in the squeal of the colliding currents, or if the black basalt blocks begin to look like faces, **abort the dive.** The human brain is hardwired to find patterns in noise; in the Well, this hardwiring will lead to madness very quickly.

Step carefully through the steam. The transition is sudden, and there is no bottom to catch you if you fall.
