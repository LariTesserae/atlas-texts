Preparing for a visit to the **Lithic Sough** is not like preparing for a cave dive, a mountain climb, or a trek through a jungle. It is more akin to preparing for a long crawl through the digestive tract of a cold, stone deity. 

The Sough is an ancient, self-sustaining biological engine that functions entirely in the dark. It is greasy, loud, and crowded beyond a surface-dweller’s comprehension. To survive here as an ordinary human, you must isolate your senses, protect your skin from direct contact with the world, and accept that you are an insignificant speck in a machine that will not even pause to grind you down.

Here is how you must prepare.

---

### Phase 1: Sensory Isolation (Defeating the Din and the Dark)

Your primary challenge in the Sough is not the fauna; it is the sheer, physical pressure of the ambient noise and the absolute lack of useful light.

*   **Sinus and Ear Protection (The Acoustic Shield):**
    The drone of the *harp-mouths*, the hiss of the *scritch-carpets*, and the wet whistling of the *siphon-lungs* combine into a white noise so dense it will cause immediate inner-ear damage and rapid, nauseating disorientation.
    *   **What to pack:** You need dual-layer hearing protection. First, custom-molded silicone earplugs with a high Noise Reduction Rating (NRR 33+). Over these, wear a militarized, active-noise-canceling (ANC) helmet. 
    *   **Communication:** Do not expect to use your voice; the sound will be literally ripped from your lips by the heavy, vibrating air. Your team must communicate via **bone-conduction haptic headsets** integrated into your helmets, or fell back on pre-established tether-tugs and heavy hand signals.
*   **Acoustic Vibration Dampening:**
    Because the sound is a tactile force, you will feel it in your bones, especially your jaw and chest. Wear a padded, semi-rigid **impact vest** beneath your outer suit. It breaks up the sonic vibrations before they can settle into your lungs and cause shortness of breath or panic.
*   **Visual Assist (Navigating the Steam):**
    There is no clean air to shine a normal flashlight through. The air is a thick, greasy colloid of mineral steam, spores, and atomized tallow. A standard LED spotlight will blow back in your face like high beams in a blizzard.
    *   **What to pack:** You need a **LIDAR HUD** mounted inside your visor. LIDAR uses pulsed laser light to map the physical contours of the stone floor, the low-hanging ceiling, and moving hazards, projecting a clean, wireframe 3D map directly onto your eyes. 
    *   **Backup Light:** Bring an **amber or deep-red light source**. Short-wavelength blue and white lights scatter terribly in the biological steam; long-wavelength red or amber cuts through the fog far better and will not cause as much glare when reflecting off the greasy, polished dolomite.

### Phase 2: Personal Protection (The Shell)

In the Sough, there is no clean dirt. There is only polished rock, calcium-saturated water, and "sub-silt"—a shifting, grey-black grease composed of crushed carapaces and ancient droppings.

*   **The Dry-Suit (Your Second Skin):**
    Do not wear porous clothing, canvas, or standard hiking gear. These will absorb the lime-rich water and the tallow grease within hours, becoming heavy, stiff, and abrasive.
    *   **What to pack:** A heavy-duty, vulcanized **commercial diving dry-suit**. It must be completely sealed from the neck down. The outer material should be slick and non-porous so that grease-slug tallow and limestone sludge slide off rather than sticking.
*   **The Footwear (High-Traction/Self-Clearing):**
    The floor of the Sough is polished to a soap-like slickness by millions of years of crawling bodies and lime drips. 
    *   **What to pack:** Heavy-duty rubber boots with deep, self-clearing lugs. You do not want fine, vibram-style hiking grips; they will instantly clog with silt and scritch-carpet grease, turning your soles into skis. The boots should have steel toes and shins—you will constantly be kicking or stepping on hard, rolling, slate-shelled creatures.
*   **The "Scritch-Gaiters":**
    The scritch-carpets flow like water across the floor of the basin. They are not aggressive, but they are looking for mineral crusts, and they will crawl over anything. If they get a purchase on your legs, they can tear fabric with their segmented legs or find a way inside your gear.
    *   **What to pack:** Rigid, smooth plastic **shin-guards and gaiters** that strap over your boots and thighs. They should be shaped like smooth cowlings so that the scritch-carpets simply slide off when they try to scramble up your legs.

---

### Phase 3: Respiration & Gas Protocol (The Tide)

The Sough does not have fresh air. The air is old, heavily cycled, and thick with humidity. Crucially, the lowest valleys are prone to sudden, silent pooling of carbon dioxide (CO2).

*   **The Rebreather:**
    Do not rely on the siphon-lungs to clean the air for you.
    *   **What to pack:** A **Closed-Circuit Rebreather (CCR)** with a minimum 6-hour soda-lime scrub capacity. Because the air is cool and damp, a rebreather is ideal—it recirculates your own warm, moist breath, preventing your lungs from drying out or taking in the caustic, lime-heavy mist of the Sough.
*   **Multi-Gas Monitor (Crucial):**
    Keep a gas detector clipped to your chest where your HUD can pull its readings. You need a device that monitors O2, CO2, and Hydrogen Sulfide (H2S), which can occasionally bubble up from the living floor. 
    *   **The Rule of the Tide:** If your CO2 monitor begins to beep, or if you feel a sudden, warm pressure in your temples—**move up**. You have entered a CO2 lake. Do not run; the physical exertion will kill you faster. Walk deliberately up the nearest polished dome or ridge until your monitor clears.

### Phase 4: Movement and Survival Tactics

*   **The Spiked Walking Staff:**
    Do not use traditional, rubber-tipped trekking poles—they will slip on the wet dolomite. Use a heavy, steel-tipped **spear-point staff**. Plant it firmly into the cracks between the fat mounds of stone to anchor yourself.
*   **FAUNA PROTOCOLS:**
    *   **The Scritch-Carpets:** Treat them like flowing mud. Do not try to step *over* them if they are in a deep wave; wait for the wave to pass or seek a higher, dry ridge where the dripping water does not fall (they follow the wet mineral trails). If you must cross them, walk with a heavy, dragging shuffle—do not lift your feet high, or they will slip beneath your boots as you plant them down.
    *   **The Stilt-Walkers:** These creatures are blind and deaf; they hunt by delicate tactile feedback through their long, glass-like legs. If a stilt-walker approaches, **freeze**. Keep your staff planted and become a pillar of stone. The stilt-walker’s needle-foot may land inches from you, or even brush your suit. As long as you do not shift your weight or vibrate, it will register you as a mineral column and glide past. Do not swing at its legs; they are brittle as cane glass and, if shattered, the creature will collapse its acidic, plunge-pump mouth directly onto you.
    *   **The Grease-Slugs:** Avoid their pathways. The yellow tallow they leave behind is incredibly slick and highly acidic to organic fabrics (though safe against vulcanized rubber). If you step in a grease-trail, clean your soles immediately using a stiff wire brush before attempting to climb any slope.
    *   **The Lantern-Galls:** Never puncture one. They are hanging gas-bags filled with metabolic hydrogen and volatile digestive fluids. If ignited or popped, they will shower the area in boiling, acidic fluid that will melt through your visor and dry-suit.

### The Intuitively Human Aspect: The Weight of the Ceiling

There is a psychological condition peculiar to those who visit the Sough, often called "The Press." 

For days, your horizon will never be more than thirty to fifty feet away—and that horizon is not a line, but the wet, grey joint where the floor meets the ceiling in a fog of biological steam. Above you is a shelf of dolomite thick enough to hold an ocean; below you is a continent of stone. The noise is a physical, vibrating hand that never stops squeezing your skull.

Pack **olfactory stimulants**—capsules of peppermint oil, lemon, or pine. The Sough smells of wet wool, rancid fat, and cold copper. A burst of a familiar, surface-world scent broken inside your mask can snap you out of a panic attack when the walls feel like they are closing in.

When you rest, do so on the high, dry shoulders of the dolomite mounds, away from the wet runnels where the lithotrophic organs pulse. Turn your lights down, close your eyes, and let the rhythmic *tock-tock-tock* of the ceiling drips remind you of rain on a roof far, far away from here.
