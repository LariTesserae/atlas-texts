Visiting Oakhaven-on-the-Spire is not a journey of endurance in the traditional survivalist sense. You will not face freezing blizzards or scorching deserts. Instead, you will face a slow, damp, heavy friction that wears down your body and mind. It is a place where physics is sleepy, the ground is vertical glass, and the air hums like a wet iron engine.

To prepare for Oakhaven, an ordinary human must pack for an environment that is high-altitude but dense, freezing but wet, and industrial but primordial. 

Here is how you must prepare.

---

### 1. Footwear: The "Glass-Walker" Problem
The mountains of Oakhaven are raw, freshly cooled obsidian. They are perfectly smooth, vitreous, and covered in a film of condensed, non-Newtonian water that clings in sluggish spheres. Standard hiking boots, spikes, or ice crampons are useless here; steel spikes will simply skitter across the glassy basalt, sending you into the Well.

*   **The Gear:** You need **sticky, soft-compound rubber soles**, similar to those used by extreme rock climbers, but with deep, siped channels to displace the thick water spheres (similar to high-end deck shoe or winter tire treads).
*   **Alternative:** **Felt-soled wading boots** (used by fly-fishermen on slippery river rocks) are highly effective against the wet, flat liver-lichens that plaster the lower iron gantries.
*   **The Habit:** Never trust a step. Before committing your weight, perform a "scuff-test." Because the rock is rising and shifting, micro-vibrations can turn a dry-looking patch of dark stone into a frictionless slide in seconds.

### 2. Clothing: Defeating the "Cold, Wet Wool"
The atmosphere does not rain; it settles. Standard waterproof "breathable" shells (like Gore-Tex) will fail within hours. The moisture here is oily, mixed with coal soot and sulfur from the vents, and it will clog the pores of modern technical fabrics, rendering them heavy and cold.

*   **The Outer Layer:** You need **heavy, oil-treated sailcloth or vulcanized rubber coats** (fisherman’s slickers). They must be long enough to cover your knees, as water drips continuously from the gantries above. 
*   **The Mid-Layers:** Avoid down feathers entirely; if they get damp—and they *will* get damp—they lose all insulating properties. Choose **heavy boiling wool or high-loft synthetic fleece**. Wool is self-regulating and retains some warmth even when saturated with the greasy mist.
*   **The "Soot Guard":** Bring tight-fitting neoprene cuffs for your wrists and ankles. The water running down the cables and handrails is black with grease and coal dust; you want to prevent it from running down your sleeves.

### 3. Sensory Defense: The Tooth-Rattle
The silence of the void is deceptive. When the shift-whistles blow, the combination of steam pressure and the low-frequency response of thousands of six-winged air-beasts creates a terrifying, physical vibration. It is not just loud; it is a resonant frequency that can cause nausea, cracked teeth, and inner-ear disorientation.

*   **Hearing Protection:** Standard earplugs will not block the low-frequency drone. You need **dual-defense hearing protection**: high-fidelity molded earplugs *plus* industrial, gel-padded ear defenders worn over them. 
*   **Mouthguards:** It sounds absurd, but pack a soft, athletic mouthguard. When the beasts and the whistles harmonize, your jaw will vibrate. Chomping down on a silicone guard prevents your teeth from micro-fracturing and mitigates the skull-ache that plagues newcomers.
*   **Scent Mitigation:** The air smells of raw, embryonic earth—sulfur, rotten eggs, and pressurized minerals—mingled with burning coal and wet seal skin. A respirator mask with an active charcoal filter is essential, unless you want to spend your first three days vomiting from the stench.

### 4. Navigating "Tired Physics"
In Oakhaven, gravity is sluggish and drifts sideways. Objects do not always fall at 9.8 m/s²; sometimes they float, drift, or sink like leaves. This is highly disorienting to the human inner ear, which evolved to expect a constant downward pull.

*   **The "Anchor" Kit:** Buy a professional arborist’s harness and three high-tensile, auto-locking carabiners. The local transport—the agonizingly slow steam hulls—crawls along three-inch-thick steel cables. There are no guardrails on these cable-ways. You must clip yourself to the safety lines at all times. If you slip, you might not plunge to your death immediately; you might drift slowly into the blue-gray nothingness, too far from a gantry to be reached by a gaff-hook.
*   **The Gravity Tester:** Carry a pouch of heavy steel bearings. Before stepping onto any suspended debris or "floating" basalt rocks, toss a bearing. If the bearing drops instantly, the local physics pocket is normal. If it drifts or floats, do not step there.
*   **Motion Sickness Medication:** The slow, oily swaying of the residential wards (the "birdcages") combined with the visual of floating rocks will trigger severe vertigo and sea-sickness. Pack a large supply of scopolamine patches.

### 5. Time and Light: The Endless Twilight
There is no night or day, only the silver, late-afternoon smudge of light. Because the factories never stop, the city has no rhythm. You will lose your sense of time within 48 hours.

*   **Sleep Hygene:** Bring a **blackout eye mask** and white-noise generating headphones. Your quarters in the residential tiers will be made of iron and copper, vibrating with the pulse of the boilers, and the green coal-gas lamps outside will shine through your windows constantly.
*   **The "Sun" Lamp:** Bring a pocket-sized, high-lux blue light. Use it for 20 minutes every "morning" to reset your circadian rhythm, or the perpetual silver gloom will draft you into a deep, clinical depression.

### 6. Social Etiquette with the Locals
The people of Oakhaven-on-the-Spire are not unfriendly, but they are *slow*. They have adapted to the agonizing crawl of the steam hulls and the sluggish fall of the wind. They do not hurry. If you run, you are viewed with intense suspicion—or as a madman who is about to slip off a spire.

*   **Patience as a Currency:** If a crossing between the Residential Ward and the Foundry takes six hours, do not complain. Bring a pocket-sized book (hydrophobic paper is best), a flask of hot, spiced tea, and prepare to sit in the damp cabin of the steam hull.
*   **The "Gaff-Hook" Culture:** If you see something drifting through the air—a wooden crate, a piece of clothing, an iron bar—and you are near a gantry, look for a long pole with a brass hook. If you can safely hook it and pull it to the platform, do so. It is the Oakhaven equivalent of picking up litter.

### Summary Pack-List:
*   1 pair felt-soled wading boots + 1 pair sticky climbing rubber shoes.
*   Vulcanized rubber fisherman's coat (dark-colored to hide coal grease).
*   Thick boiled-wool sweaters.
*   Full-body industrial harness with self-locking carabiners.
*   Dual-layer ear protection (plugs + muffs).
*   Respirator with charcoal filters.
*   Athletic mouthguard.
*   Pouch of testing weights (ball bearings).
*   Scopolamine patches (vertigo medication).
*   Analog watch (wind-up, as battery-powered digital interfaces can glitch near the heavy steam-driven magnetic hubs of the foundries).
*   A flask of high-proof alcohol—both to warm your chest and to wash the black soot off your hands before you eat.
