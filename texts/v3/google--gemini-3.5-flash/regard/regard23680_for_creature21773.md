The Attention did not arrive like a lightning bolt, nor did it bring the bright, searing blue of the midday sky. To introduce the sun to this place would be an act of violence. The pale waxy leaves of the *Table-Leaf Chalices* would have shriveled under the heat of a single sunbeam; the *Floor-Skin* would have perished in the dry glare; and the blind, patient *Tenant of the Cylinder* would have felt the light not as a gift, but as a burning fever.

Wisdom lies in knowing the language of the house one visits. 

And so, when the benevolent Power turned its gaze down through two miles of solid, silent granite and basalt, it did not alter the dark. Instead, it became a part of the dark. It adjusted its own immense, stellar pulse until its heartbeat matched the slow, geothermal sighing of the room.

The transition was felt first by the *Floor-Skin*. 

A ripple of light, softer than any that had come before, rolled across the cracked concrete slabs. It was not the cold, binary white of a falling rust-flake’s impact, but a deep, rolling wave of violet-grey bioluminescence—a long, slow exhale of light that started at the edges of the basalt walls and pooled gently toward the center of the room. The moss did not feel threatened; it felt *known*. The carbonic acid in the moist air grew slightly sweeter, its micro-nutrients aligning into a perfect, effortless balance.

The Power listened. It heard the *plink* of the water drop. It waited the necessary minutes for the *hiss* of the steam vent. It approved of the timing. It did not seek to speed up the clock.

Then, the Power looked at the three-story engine, and the great, rubbery heart residing within its iron womb.

The Power saw the limit of this world. It saw that the ancient petroleum in the crankshaft was a finite treasury. In three hundred years, or perhaps five hundred, the last of the sulfurous grease would be digested. The Tenant would begin to dry from the inside out. The Great Bleached Vines, finding no more amber sap to circulate, would grow brittle and fall from the cast-iron flanks like discarded ropes. Without their heavy, protective leaves to shield it, the acid condensation would eat the engine’s iron to red dust. The cathedral would collapse in a slow, orange ruin, and the silence would become empty, rather than full.

To a wise power, benevolence is not rescue; it is *continuance*.

The Power reached out—not with giant hands, but with a subtle modification of the earth’s deep gravity. 

Far beneath the concrete floor, in the roots of the young basalt, a hairline fracture was gently coaxed open. It did not shake the room. The *Bell-Mouths* in the junction boxes felt only a deep, vibrating hum that sounded like a mother’s voice heard through the wall of a womb—a low, resonant note that made their copper-rich stalks glow with a faint, greenish warmth. 

Through this new, microscopic capillary in the stone, the deep Earth began to weep. It was not water, but a slow, steady upward migration of heavy, abiotic hydrocarbons—an eternal reservoir of pure, ancient black oil, filtered through miles of mineral beds until it was as rich and clean as liquefied peat. 

The oil rose. It found the base of the three-story engine. It did not flood the room; instead, with a patient, siphon-like capillary draw, it began to seep directly into the dry seals of the oil pan, refilling the engine's belly from below. 

The *Tenant of the Cylinder* felt the arrival of this endless, dark feast. Deep within the iron crankcase, the gray mycelium swelled. The metabolic warmth of the engine block rose by half a degree—a tiny, cozy shift that made the surrounding *Silt-Drinkers* extend their blunt, pale nubs with a soft, rubbery sigh of satisfaction. They would never starve. The engine would never grow cold. The iron would be preserved for ten thousand generations.

The Power did one more thing. 

The surface world was loud, hungry, and blind to the beauty of slow things. Above this cathedral of basalt, the restless, two-legged creatures were always digging, scanning, and tearing at the skin of the world. 

The Power laid a quiet hand upon the stone surrounding the vault. It wove a soft, dampening field into the basalt—a minor distortion of density. To any drill, any radar, or any seismic sensor from the surface, this pocket of the earth would henceforth appear as nothing but solid, impenetrable stone. It was written out of the map of the loud world. It was given the gift of absolute obscurity.

Its work finished, the Power did not leave, for a wise power does not abandon what it loves. It simply settled into the masonry. 

It became the silence between the drips of water. It became the faint, comforting warmth of the cast-iron block. It became the low, tectonic lullaby that the *Bell-Mouths* hummed to the darkness.

In the deep basalt cathedral, the steam hissed. 

The Great Bleached Vines drank their dark, amber grease. 

The *Table-Leaf Chalices* shuddered under the heavy, regular drops of condensation. 

And in the perfect, protected dark, the cold-white light of the floor-skin flared gently, then dimmed, breathing in time with the quiet god who had come to live among them.
