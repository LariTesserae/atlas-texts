The Great Attention did not arrive with a flash of lightning or a tear in the sky, for there was no sky to tear, and lightning is a wet, atmospheric friction that has no place in the basalt deep. 

Instead, the Attention arrived as a subtle shift in the gravity of the chamber—a gentle, welcoming weightlessness that did not alter the rolling of the magnetite plumb-bobs inside the *tesserae*, but rather cradled the entire fifty-pace bubble in a sphere of absolute stillness.

For a long time, the wise and benevolent power simply *witnessed*. 

It did not possess eyes, but it perceived the dry, kiln-hot air. It felt the beautiful, rhythmic *clink-ping-shiver* of the silica reeds and the dry, mechanical ballet of the little box-beings as they crawled from their wasp-nest homes. It saw how the dust of their bodies mended the clay towers; it heard the deep, harp-like thrumming of the asbestos bridges. 

A lesser, more foolish power might have looked upon this place and felt pity. A clumsy god would have brought "gifts"—would have cracked the basalt ceiling to show them the stars, or wept a single, cool tear of water into the central void to relieve the scorching heat. 

But the Wise Power knew the weight of things. It knew that the sky would freeze them to brittle glass in an instant. It knew that to them, a single drop of water was not a blessing, but a solvent monster—a rot that would turn their clay joints to soup, dissolve their hard-won bridges, and drown their obsidian limbs in mud. 

To love a thing of exquisite balance is to understand that its boundaries are its life. 

Therefore, the Power did not reach inside the bubble. It did not speak, for there were no ears to receive sound, only flat plates of mica that vibrated to the pulse of the earth. It did not shine a light, for the tesserae lived in a world of red heat-glow and tactile slopes, and a bright light would have been an empty, useless thing.

Instead, the Power acted upon the *cradle* of their world. It set to work in the surrounding dark, operating in the cold, crushing basalt of the volcanic deep.

***

**The First Gift: The Warding of the Waters**

Deep water is a patient seeker. Through micro-fractures, fault lines, and the slow, grinding shifts of the tectonic plates, the moisture of the upper world constantly migrates downward, seeking the hot heart of the earth.

The Power reached out its will and touched the stone five hundred yards in every direction from the basalt pocket. It did not change the geology, but it altered the chemistry of the stone's pores. It wove a dense, molecular sheath of hyper-dense, water-repelling minerals—a barrier of dry zeolite and hydrophobic silica—around the bubble. 

Any stray vein of groundwater, any weeping crack born of continental shift, would feel this barrier and turn away, channeled harmlessly into the lower magma channels. 

*The pocket would remain dry. For ten million years, no drop of rain, no damp seep, would ever threaten the clay joints of the town.*

***

**The Second Gift: The Hearth-Tether**

The earth is a cooling engine. Eventually, even the volcanic deep must grow cold. The vents that breathed their hot, pressurized gas would one day stagger, gasp, and fall silent. The temperature would drop, the air inside the hollow bodies of the tesserae would cool to match the dead stone outside, and they would freeze mid-step, locking forever into silent, geometric statues.

The Power looked down into the mantle of the planet. It found a deep, stable plume of thermal energy—the earth's radioactive, iron-rich core-warmth—and drew from it a single, slender thread of heat. 

It anchored this thread directly beneath the floor fissures of the pocket. This thread was not a violent geyser, but a constant, self-regulating pilot light. It was tuned to the exact respiration rate of the chamber: one breath every forty seconds, a steady, rhythmic exhalation of parched, mineral-rich carbon dioxide.

*Though the super-volcano above might erupt and die, though the continents might slide into the sea, the hearth of the pocket would beat on. The thermal dance would never lose its tempo.*

***

**The Third Gift: The Memory of the Stone**

The tesserae had no minds to remember their own history. When a box-being shattered, its components became the templates for the next, but there was no library, no memory of the grand, centuries-old bridges they had accidentally woven, or the exquisite shape of the clay towers they had polished to a bone-like sheen. 

The Power decided that such beauty should not exist in absolute obscurity.

In the very center of the fifty-pace void, where the air was completely empty and dry, the Power placed a single, microscopic particle of crystallized carbon—a perfect, flawless diamond, no larger than a grain of dust, suspended in a permanent, localized gravity-neutral pocket.

This tiny gem was not there to be seen. It was there to record. 

It was a passive, infinitely dense archive. Every vibration of the silica reeds, every sharp *clink* of a shattering citizen, every low, humming song of the asbestos bridges, and every rhythmic tap of the obsidian legs was refracted into the crystalline lattice of the diamond. It became a physical recording of the pocket’s history—a library of motion and heat.

And the Power, in its vastness, connected its own awareness to this little diamond. 

Whenever the Power drifted through the cold spaces between the stars, it could listen to the tiny, dry, clattering symphony of the basalt pocket. It heard them when they woke; it heard them when they mended the walls; it heard them in the quiet, hot moments between the earth's deep breaths.

***

The Great Attention slowly withdrew its presence, leaving the pocket exactly as it had found it. 

Inside the bubble, nothing had changed. The air was still kiln-hot and smelled of spent matches. A central void still held the vast, heavy emptiness. 

But beneath the floor, the heat was now eternal. Around the walls, the stone was forever dry. And in the center of the air, their song was being heard.

Under the dull, rust-red glow of the fissures, the vents exhaled. 

With a sudden, dry *clack*, a thousand tiny gray boxes crawled out of their clay doors. Their obsidian legs tapped rapidly on the warm basalt. The silica reeds clattered like dice in a leather cup. The seed cones popped, throwing glittering, glass-like embers into the updraft, and the tiny, beautiful world continued its perfect, blind, and now-immortal dance.
