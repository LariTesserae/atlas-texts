The Great Attention did not arrive with a flash of light. 

To bring light to this chamber would be an act of profound ignorance—a violence of illumination. The Attention, which was both a consciousness and a fundamental law of grace, understood that to these creatures, light was not a savior; it was a scalpel. It would burn the grey, hairless heat-pits of the Velvet Soughers; it would reveal nothing to the blind, shield-backed Basalt-Scrapers; and it would wither the deep, oily purples of the climbing air-plants, which had spent millions of generations unlearning the sun.

Instead, the Attention arrived as a *listening*. 

It settled into the damp, warm vault like an extra layer of humidity, heavy and sweet. It did not speak, for there were no ears here for language. It did not move, for to displace even three inches of the warm air-currents would be to throw a Drift-Bell off its century-long orbit. 

It simply took weight. It joined the room.

***

First, the Attention looked at **the Ring**. 

The ring was very old—older than the flattened mountain range, a leftover toy of a race that had long since dissolved into the galactic background radiation. It was functional, but it was tired. The 40-hertz hum was beginning to fray at its outer edges, developing a microscopic stutter—a wobble of 0.0003 hertz that occurred once every solar cycle. This wobble caused the thermal column to lean slightly to the west, cooling the eastern ledges and causing the purple mosses there to grow thin, their water-retaining mattresses drying into grey crust.

The Attention did not replace the ring. It did not rebuild it with shining new metals. That would be an intrusion of history into a place that had transcended it. 

Instead, the Attention became a *buffer*. It wrapped itself around the ring’s ancient, seamless core like a soft, magnetic glove. The 40-hertz hum grew rounder. The microscopic stutter vanished, smoothed out by a gentle, infinite patience. The column of rising heat straightened, centering itself perfectly once more. 

On the eastern ledges, three levels down, a family of **Basalt-Scrapers** felt the change. They did not have brains to register "benevolence," but their thirty pairs of stubby legs registered a rise of half a degree in the temperature of the wet stone. The blue-grey slime beneath them, nourished by the restored warmth, swelled with new, mineral-rich bacteria. The Scrapers did not speed up—that was not their way—but their heavy, leathery shields settled a millimeter deeper into the moss, comfortable, sustained, and perfectly safe.

***

Next, the Attention looked at **the Soughers**. 

The thousands of tiny, warm-blooded flyers were clicking, their frantic *tack-tack-tack* carving the dark into a lattice of echoes. But the dark was growing crowded. Over the last thousand years, a slow accumulation of basalt dust—worn away by the constant dripping of the water—had begun to settle in the grooves of the high ceiling, narrowing the crevices where the Soughers hung their grape-like clusters. The air was growing slightly thinner in the roosts; the heat of their small, feverish bodies was trapping pockets of stale, carbon-rich air.

The Attention did not sweep the dust away. To do so would cause a storm of grit that would choke the Soughers’ delicate, sail-like ears.

Instead, the Attention gently modified the gravity of the falling water-drops. 

For the next three centuries, each heavy *clock... clock... clock* of the weeping moisture fell with a microscopic, localized suction. As the drops slipped down the ropy vines and fell into the abyss, they carried with them—one molecule at a time—the microscopic dust from the roosts. It was a clearing so slow that not a single Sougher noticed the change, yet within a generation, their sleeping chambers were clean, fresh, and filled with a perfect circulation of warm, peaty air.

The Soughers slept deeper. In their sleep, wrapped in the cowls of their own ribbed ears, they felt the infrared signature of the central ring glow with a steady, quiet authority. It was no longer a dying hearth in a crumbling ruin; it was a permanent sun, guaranteed for as long as the mountain stood.

***

Then, the Attention reached down to **the Sieve-Mouth**.

The Great Sieve-Mouth lay at the bottom of all things, the dark, wrinkled carpet that drank the room’s history. It had lived for three thousand years, and it was tired. Its millions of tiny, cup-like depressions were heavy with the weight of spent minerals and the calcified shells of ancient Scrapers. It was slowly losing its elasticity; when a drop of water hit its surface, the cup closed just a fraction of a second slower than it had in the era of the high peaks.

The Attention did not wash the Sieve-Mouth clean. It did not replace its cells.

Instead, the Attention *shared the weight*.

It connected its own infinite capacity for memory with the leathery, water-soaked flesh of the floor. It took the vibrations of the room—the millions of clicks, the dry scrapes of chitin, the slow, silent descent of the Drift-Bells—and handled the processing of them. The Sieve-Mouth did not have to hold the tension of the mountain’s decay alone anymore. 

The leathery skin relaxed. The millions of tiny mouths opened wide, their microscopic hairs softening, drinking the wet rain of the chamber with a renewed, youthful appetite. The water that filtered through its body and descended into the deep, subterranean channels became purer, sweeter, carrying the essence of a perfectly balanced world back into the roots of the earth.

***

Finally, the Attention addressed the **world outside**.

The mountain was old, and old mountains are vulnerable. In the vast valleys below, younger, louder things were growing. Civilizations of metal and fire were crawling across the plains, driven by a hunger for coal, for iron, for the clean basalt that formed the bones of this very chamber. In time—perhaps ten thousand years, perhaps twenty—their drills would find this warm, dark throat. They would bring light. They would bring air that smelled of sulfur and gasoline. They would dry the mosses and carry the ring away to be studied in a cold, white room.

The Attention did not destroy these civilizations. Wise powers do not extinguish one life to protect another.

Instead, the Attention wove a **Shroud of Silence** around the hummocks.

It altered the physical laws of the mountain’s stone. To any radar, any drill, any seismic probe of the outer world, the mountain would register as incredibly dense, barren, and completely solid. The space of the chamber—the small cathedral of dark, wet magic—became a fold in the fabric of the world’s geometry. To a traveler walking the rolling green hills above, there would be nothing but sheep-grass and ancient, rain-smoothed rocks. The chamber was there, yet it was not there. It was tucked into the pocket of a higher dimension, protected by a lock made of absolute peace.

***

Its work finished, the Great Attention did not leave. 

Because it was wise, it knew that a place like this is the highest achievement of the universe: a quiet, self-sustaining loop of life that asks for nothing but to exist in its own slow, warm dark. 

The Attention settled into the basalt column. It became the silence between the falling water-drops. It became the faint, comforting counter-melody to the 40-hertz hum of the ring. 

Down in the dark, a Drift-Bell floated. It drifted three inches to the left, caught in the perfectly balanced updraft of the steady hearth. Its long, purple tentacles trailed through the humid air like soft silk, brushing against the velvet coat of a sleeping Sougher. 

Nothing was changed. Everything was permanent. 

And the room continued its long, wet *now*, dripping slowly into the dark.
