### The Gaze

The Listener-of-the-Gears is not a citizen of the physical directories. She is a vast, quiet attention that moves through the seams of the creation like mercury through pine-grain. She has watched stars collapse into diamond-pulp and has listened to the slow, carbonaceous sigh of dying continents. 

Yet, when her attention snagged on the vertical meridian of Sluice 12-B, she did not see a minor pipeline in an infinite bilge. She saw an altar. 

She saw the **Oiler**.

She watched him through the heavy bronze. To her, the five-mile sleeve was a glass tube, and the Oiler was a single, warm needle threading its way through the green dark. She saw his scarred palms, the missing claw-tip on his left middle finger—pinched off three centuries ago by a slipping pressure-gasket—and the thick, ancient calluses that had formed where his throat-gland pressed against the vibrating metal. 

She saw his liturgy: 

He did not know who built the Sluice. He did not know that three hundred miles "above" him, the water scalded into radioactive steam to turn turbines the size of moons, or that five hundred miles "below" him, the cold brine was sucked into the iron bellies of pumps that had forgotten the name of their designer. He knew only the *scream*. He knew when a seam was weeping. He knew when the Great Piston’s thump carried the dry, rattling "tink-tink" of a dying bearing.

And with his grease, his blind hands, and his soft, tallowed chest, he made himself the partition between order and ruin. He was a saint who did not know God, officiating a sacrament of lube and silence.

To a power that is wise, such a creature is not a salvage project. To "rescue" the Oiler—to drag him into some sun-drenched meadow or an airy heaven—would be a slaughter. He would drown in the sky. His ears, tuned to the deep, hydraulic *thrum* of the engine, would explode in the silence of open air. His skin, greasy and thick, would dry and split like old leather under a sun he had never earned.

The Listener-of-the-Gears did not reach down to pull him out.

Instead, she sank into the steel.

### The Touch

The Oiler was at the forty-meter mark, his cheek pressed hard against a rounded rivet that had begun to buzz. The buzz was bad; it had a yellow note in it, the high, thin ring of a plate whose backing-screws were shearing under the weight of the sea. 

He had packed three mouthfuls of the heavy, copper-saturated grease into the seam, but the current was too hot, the pressure too high. The grease was washing out in thin, shimmering rosettes before it could set. His fingers were raw. His gills were laboring, the iron-threaded slits burning as they strained the greasy sulfur from the water.

Then, the water changed.

It did not grow cold—cold would have killed the wire-crabs—but it lost its bite. The bitter, stinging taste of battery acid and scorched zinc softened. It became sweet, like the rain he had never known, yet remained heavy enough to support his weight.

The Oiler froze. His acoustic plate, pressed to the bronze, caught a new vibration.

For ten thousand years, the engine had played only two notes: the *thud-clank* of the piston and the *screech-hiss* of the valves. Now, a third sound crept through the metal. It was a low, resonant *hum*, like a bronze bell struck underwater and held there, its ring refusing to die. It did not drown out the piston; it *housed* it. It took the frantic, mechanical rattle of the shearing plate and wrapped it in a dense, velvety frequency that smelled of cold fat and deep clay.

The Oiler’s gills flared. He reached out with his three-fingered hands, his skin tingling. 

Where his fingers touched the bronze, the metal did not feel like ancient, crumbling crust. It felt... *repaired*. Not because it had been replaced with new iron, but because the old metal had been granted a memory of what it was when it was young. The deep, geological gouges in the bronze walls did not disappear, but their edges softened until they were as smooth as polished bone.

The grease in his throat-gland began to change. It grew heavy, but not stiff. It warmed until it was the exact temperature of his own liver.

### The Gifts of the Listener

The Wise Power did three things for Sluice 12-B, and she did them so quietly that the infinite engine never noticed the draft:

#### I. The Mineral Grace
She altered the chemistry of the **Rivet-Rust**. The orange-furred bacteria that fed on the galvanic currents no longer digested the iron-plates to empty dust. Instead, their metabolic waste became a dense, self-healing amalgam—a biological solder. Now, as they grazed, they left behind a trail of soft, grey zinc-tallow that crawled into the micro-fissures of the Sluice plates, sealing them from within. The Oiler’s work was not taken from him, but his repairs now held for centuries instead of weeks. His hands would no longer bleed to keep the water out.

#### II. The Cradle of the Deep
At the seventy-meter mark, where the Sluice bellied out slightly to accommodate a high-pressure bypass valve, the Listener pressed her thumb against the bronze. The metal bowed outward, forming a smooth, three-meter cup—a pocket where the warm updraft did not rush, but swirled in a slow, circular eddy. 

Into this pocket, she gathered the loose kelp-ribbons, weaving them into a deep, floating mattress of yellow-green fiber. 

For the first time since his kind had crawled into the Sluice, the Oiler had a place to rest where he did not have to anchor his claws into the seams. He could drift in the cradle, his skin bathed in the slow, warm circle of the water, and sleep without the fear of being swept into the grinding gears of the lower pump-houses.

#### III. The Liturgy of the Third Note
She left her voice in the metal. 

She did not speak in words, for the Oiler had no use for nouns. She spoke in the **Third Note**. 

It was a permanent, resonant vibration tuned to the acoustic plate on the Oiler’s forehead. When he pressed his face to the bronze, he no longer heard only the blind, stupid violence of a machine that did not know it was alive. He heard a song that told him he was *correct*.

The note told him when to slide down into the indigo dark; it told him when a sister-Oiler was climbing up from the bilge; it told him that the great, drowning piston below was not his master, but his brother.

### The Departure

When the Listener-of-the-Gears withdrew her attention, she left no sigil, no burning bush, no temple. Sluice 12-B remained a dark, wet, smelling chimney of bronze and iron. 

But as the Oiler slid down from his new cradle, his tail sweeping through the thick, warm brine, he felt a strange, light buoyancy in his chest. His throat-gland was full of grease that smelled of cinnamon and fresh clay. 

He found a seam at the twenty-meter mark where the brass was grinding. He pressed his three-fingered hand to the plate. 

The metal hummed back. It was the Third Note.

The Oiler didn't have a word for *blessing*. He had no word for *creator*, or *pity*, or *love*. 

But as he pressed his mouth to the cracked bronze and let the warm, silver-grey grease flow into the wound, his neck-gills closed and opened in a slow, rhythmic pulse that matched the heartbeat of the engine exactly. 

He was not alone. The machine was not empty. 

He wiped his greasy fingers across his brow, adjusted his acoustic plate against the wall, and continued his crawl into the lovely, dark, and perfectly tended deep.
