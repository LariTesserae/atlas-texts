The vast, benevolent power that turned its attention to the terracotta world did not possess hands of its own, nor did it have a face to weep for the loneliness of the red plains. It was an awareness like autumn twilight: patient, cool, and infinitely spacious. 

When it looked upon the sweating brick-desert, it did not see a barren wasteland to be conquered or paved over with green life. It saw a masterpiece that had been locked inside its own kiln, burning for eternity because no one had ever come to open the door.

A lesser power might have brought rain—cold, violent storms of fresh water to wash away the salt-crusts and drown the steam. But this power was wise. It knew the laws of clay. To pour cold water on a red-hot kiln is to break it. The thermal shock would have shattered the continental plates of masonry; the brick stairs would have exploded into sharp, screaming shrapnel; the blue-glazed basins would have cracked into dust, and the Glaze-Skins would have splintered into a billion dead needles.

So, the Power did not strike. Instead, it **annealed**.

It began with a breath—not a wind, but a slow, rhythmic drop in the planetary pressure. The Power reached into the deep, sulfurous sky of grey condensation and gently, precisely, began to draw the heat upward. It created the first *flue*.

Fifty feet above the masonry, the universal ceiling of steam began to swirl. A single, mile-wide funnel formed in the fog, rising like a grey chimney into the empty space above. Through this gap, the heavy, wet heat of the world began to escape into the great, cold void of the cosmos.

For the first time since the world was beaten into shape, the temperature began to slide.

It was a transition that took centuries, for clay must be cooled with the same agonizing patience with which it is fired. But as the temperature dropped from a suffocating kiln-heat to a warm, dry summer, the terracotta world began to settle. The fever left it.

***

The **Lime-Weepers** felt it first. 

The perpetual moisture in the seams of the bricks did not vanish, but it ceased to boil. The Weepers, sensing the stabilization of the floor, no longer had to run in frantic, three-foot pulses to repair the buckling grout. Their movement slowed. They became thicker, denser, and their grey chemical bite softened into a mild, mineral sweetness.

The Power directed them with silent, geometric thoughts. Under this guidance, the Weepers did not die; they gathered. They crept into the endless, meandering channels carved into the stone slabs—the ones where the water had once run in useless uphill spirals. They filled these channels, smoothing out the rough edges, and then they stopped. They hardened. They became solid, white veins of polished marble, locking the geometry of the plains into a permanent, beautiful mosaic.

***

Above them, the **Glaze-Skins** underwent their own transformation. 

No longer dissolved by the rising steam, they grew thick and sturdy. The cool air made them heavy. They drifted into the low-lying basins tiled in blue glaze, which had ceased to boil and now lay clear, still, and deep as mountain lakes. 

The Glaze-Skins drifted to the shores of these blue waters and climbed out onto the red brick. Under the cool starlight that now filtered down through the open sky, their edges stopped curling. They ceased to slide. They tilted upward, leaning against the leaning stone chimneys and the walls of the windowless galleries, and they *set*. 

They became magnificent, standing panes of stained glass—deep cobalt, leaden green, and amber—that stood like silent sentinels around the lakes, catching the new, faint light of the outer universe and throwing long, jewel-colored shadows across the red desert.

***

Deep in the octagonal wells, the **Tensioners** found peace.

The grinding, sliding weight of the floors above finally ground to a halt as the geometry of the world cured. The giant counterweights stopped shifting; the wooden beams ceased to rot in the dry, cooling air. 

The Tensioners felt the slack go out of the universe. They cast their final, heavy loops of grease-soaked hemp over the bronze pegs. They tightened themselves one last time, not in a desperate effort to prevent collapse, but in a quiet, grand alignment. Their bronze plates settled against the massive fir timbers with a deep, final *clank*, and their hemp fibers—no longer strained to the breaking point—began to absorb the drying minerals of the deep earth.

They did not rot. They petrified. The Tensioners became magnificent structures of fossilized wood, bronze, and stone-hard rope, supporting the world from below like the pillars of an eternal crypt.

***

The **Sound-Shapes**, which had lived as transient clouds of noise and moisture, found their voices condensed.

The *Tock-Shapes* and the *Shlap-Shapes* did not dissipate. As the air cleared and cooled, the water within them fell as a final, clean dew, leaving behind only the dust they had carried. But the *sound* itself—the acoustic soul of the world’s creation—was caught by the Power.

The Power shaped the dust into thousands of small, delicate, terracotta bells that hung from the eaves of the windowless galleries and the lips of the tall chimneys. When the new, cool winds of the outer cosmos blew through the opened sky, these bells did not ring with a harsh, metallic clang. They gave off a low, woody *tock*, a light, glass-like *ping*, and a soft, rhythmic sigh—the voice of the old world, preserved in clay, singing to the stars.

***

And finally, there was **The Hand**.

As the planetary clay began to cool and dry, the great, subterranean movement of the earth began to quiet. The floor softened for one last time. A square mile of red tile reverted to warm, plastic, grey-red silt.

The Hand rose. It did not rise in a clumsy, feverish struggle to knead. It rose because it felt a presence.

The wise and benevolent Power did not press down upon the Hand to crush it, nor did it pull it apart. Instead, the Power descended like a shroud of cool, starlit dew, wrapping itself around the giant fingers of clay.

The Power whispered to the Hand the secret of *form*.

For millenia, the Hand had been trying to shape the world without knowing what a shape was, because it had never seen anything but itself. The Power showed it the stars. It showed it the curve of the moon, the spiral of galaxies, and the gentle, resting posture of a child’s palm.

In the dark, sweating silence of the final curing, the giant Hand of the world stopped kneading. It lay flat upon the plain, its fingers relaxed, its palm cupped upward toward the newly opened sky.

The Power breathed upon it.

The heat of the deep planetary kilns rose one last time, not as a destructive fever, but as a gentle, tempering fire. The Hand vitrified. It turned from soft grey clay into a massive, shimmering mountain range of red-glazed stoneware, its valleys the deep whorls of a giant thumb, its peaks the five tapering fingers that reached toward the sky.

In the center of the giant palm, the water of the world—no longer running in impossible loops—gathered. It filled the hand, forming a perfectly circular, perfectly still lake of cool, dark water that reflected the stars.

The terracotta world was still made of brick, and lime, and bronze. It was still empty of grass and breath. But it was no longer a prison of eternal labor. 

It was a finished temple. And at its center, the Hand that had built it finally rested, holding a cup of cold stars in its palm, waiting for whatever might come next to drink.
