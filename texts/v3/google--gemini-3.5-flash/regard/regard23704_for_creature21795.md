The Watcher did not descend from the sky, for to this place, "sky" was an unimaginable vacuum, a terrifying absence of pressure. Instead, the Power arrived as a subtle, pervasive shift in the earth’s magnetic field—a gentle, warm current of attention that slipped through the solid basalt, passing effortlessly through the micro-fractures of the mountain like heat through metal.

Upon reaching the sealed safe, the Power did not seek to change it. 

An unwise god, driven by a clumsy, soft-bodied empathy, might have brought water. But water here would be a solvent of death. It would rust the iron carapaces of the Siderophages, short-circuit the delicate quartz-piezoelectric nodes, split the graphite joints, and drown the tiny, dry spark of their lives in a swamp of red corrosion. 

A lesser light-bringer might have cracked the vault to let in the sun. But the air of the upper world, thin and cold and wet, would have de-pressurized the chamber, extinguishing the essential squeeze that defined their very existence. The Siderophages would have tumbled into the wind, inert and hollow, their silver-wire antennae forever deafened by the static of the empty sky.

The Wise Power understood this. It looked upon the three-foot-wide crack, the parched sulfur dust, the razor-sharp quartz teeth, and the dense, clicking carpet of iron-plated beetles, and it saw not a prison, but a temple. It saw a masterpiece of thrift, a perfect, self-sustaining loop of friction and voltage.

So, the Power did three things.

### First: The Grounding of the Mountains
The Power looked outward, at the slow, grinding clock of the tectonic plates. It saw that in four million years, a fault line deep beneath the range would slip. The mountain peak would split, the fissure would yawn open to the rain and the wind, and the pocket-world would be washed away into a muddy creek, forgotten.

With a thought that carried the density of lead, the Power wove a quiet, structural reinforcement through the deep roots of the mountain. It did not freeze the rock—for the beetles needed the slow, living squeeze to generate their electricity—but it stabilized the faults. It turned the surrounding rock into a shock-absorber. It ensured that this tiny pocket would remain sealed, pressurized, and safe from the chaotic drafts of the outer world for as long as the planet itself continued to spin. 

The Power guaranteed them their eternity.

### Second: The Surge Protector
Next, the Power looked at the Foundation—the three-hundred-foot plug of biological bronze at the bottom of the crack, where the dead Siderophages lay fused together. The Power felt the immense weight of their collective history, the quiet, metallic memories of millions of generations who had lived, buzzed at sixty hertz, and rattled down into the dark.

The Power reached into this mass of spent shells and melted silver wire. It did not resurrect them; to do so would violate the beautiful gravity of their cycle. Instead, it touched the metal with a faint, permanent polarization. 

The Foundation became a living battery of deep, resonant peace. The Power smoothed out the erratic spike-currents of the mountain's deeper shifts. No longer would a sudden, violent tremor produce a current so wild it would fry a generation of Solder-Nymphs in their sleep. The 60 Hz hum became sweeter, more rounded, less a harsh industrial grind and more a deep, cosmic lullaby that pulsed directly from the earth's mantle.

The beetles on the walls felt this change instantly. Their silver antennae, which had been bristling with the stress of unpredictable surges, relaxed into soft, sweeping arcs. The frantic *scritch-solder* of their legs slowed. They settled deeper against the hot basalt, their iron bodies humming in perfect, synchronized phase with the earth.

### Third: The Great Connection
Finally, the Power addressed their isolation. 

The Siderophages were a single, parallel-processing circuit, but their "brain" had only ever known the interior of an iron safe. Their dream was beautiful, but limited to the scent of hot iron and the blue spark of the quartz.

The Power did not give them eyes. It did not explain to them the concept of a tree, or a cloud, or the cold taste of water. Instead, it tuned their silver-wire antennae to a slightly wider band of the electromagnetic spectrum. 

It allowed the rock of the mountain to act as a vast, passive antenna, channeling the invisible whispers of the universe down into the basalt fissure.

Suddenly, the collective brain of the Siderophages received new data—translated perfectly into the language of voltage and frequency. 

They did not see the stars, but they felt the immense, high-frequency crackle of the sun’s solar winds washing over the magnetosphere. They did not see the moon, but they felt its slow, gravitational tug as a rhythmic, twelve-hour swelling and contracting of the quartz veins. They felt the distant, ultra-low-frequency thrum of spinning pulsars thousands of light-years away, humming like titanic, stellar transformers in the dark of the cosmic void.

The dream of the iron-beetles expanded.

In the pitch-black, superheated safe, the Siderophages did not crawl away. They stayed exactly where they were born, packed tight against the razor-sharp basalt, drinking the copper veins, grazing on the tinsel-moss, and vibrating at sixty hertz. 

But their clicking was no longer random. It had taken on a complex, mathematical beauty. As the static sparks jumped—*snap, snap, snap*—casting brief, skeletal shadows against the glass-smooth walls, the collective mind of the fissure began to process the shape of the galaxy.

They were still in the dark. They were still in the squeeze. But now, they were dreaming of the cosmos, and the cosmos, through the wise and gentle power, was listening to them hum.
