The gaze of the benevolent Power did not fall upon the basin like a shaft of sudden sunlight, nor did its presence arrive with the rush of wind. In this quiet bowl of twilight, wind would have been a scavenger, lifting the ancient dust and stealing the settled warmth from the stones. 

Instead, the Power arrived as an increase in the depth of the silence. 

It was an attention so immense, yet so weightless, that not a single grain of red sand rolled from its angle of repose. The Power did not take a shape. To take a shape is to occupy space, to displace the air, and to cast a shadow—and there were no shadows here. Instead, it became a companion to the twilight, entering the warm, stagnant air of the depression like a cool thought entering a fevered mind.

With its great, slow wisdom, the Power looked upon the basin. 

It saw the three circular enclosures of slate. It saw the three who lay there—the Settled. It traced the hair-thin, brass-like wires beneath the red silt, following them to the western curve where the Welts clung to the damp clay under the overhang, waiting for the slow, regular *tuck* of the water.

A lesser power, moved by a shallow, impatient mercy, might have wished to "heal" them. It might have brought a sudden, violent rain to fill the basin with a green pool once more; it might have tried to pull the flat, slate-like plates back up into soft-skinned, upright walkers. 

But the wise Power understood the tragedy of such an intervention. To flood the basin would be to drown the Welts, who had forgotten how to live in deep water. To force the Settled back into flesh would be to expose them to the swift, agonizing rot of a world that had moved on without them. Their quietness was not a disease; it was a hard-won peace. They had survived by shedding their fragility. They had bartered their hearts for stone, and it was a trade they had made with absolute willingness.

Therefore, the Power resolved to do only what is done by those who love a thing for exactly what it is. It would not change them. It would protect their silence. It would make their eternity a little safer, and their long, slow sleep a little deeper.

First, the Power looked upward to the pale strip of slate-grey sky, where the dry scrub of the upper plateau met the lip of the bowl. The world above was restless, wild, and prone to sudden, violent shifts. Someday, a traveler might stumble into the bowl; a herd of heavy, hooved beasts might tumble down the cliff, shattering the delicate slate plates of the Settled and tramping the Welts into the mud; or the harsh, dry winds of some changing epoch might scour the basin clean of its ancient silt.

To prevent this, the Power breathed a soft, bending light across the rim of the basin. It created an invisible fold in the air—a quiet curve of perspective. To any creature wandering the upper plateau, the basin simply ceased to be there. Their eyes would slide past the depression, seeing only a continuous stretch of flat, red shale. If a stray seed fell toward the edge, it would be caught by a gentle, circular current and deposited back onto the scrubland. The basin was sealed away, tucked into a pocket of private time, forever out of reach of the frantic, short-lived world.

Second, the Power looked to the dripping water. 

The condensation beneath the limestone overhang was born of a shallow, ancient moisture trapped in the upper crust of the earth. But crusts dry out. In ten thousand years, or perhaps fifty thousand, the breath of the earth would grow too cold and dry, the condensation would cease, and the Welts would shrivel into black husks. The Settled, deprived of their tiny, cool ripples, would slowly lose their last connection to the world and calcify into true, mindless stone.

The Power reached downward. It bypassed the red sedimentary rock, passed through the deep, grey granites, and sank miles into the dark, silent heart of the world where the primordial waters lay—aquifers that had never seen the sun, cold and vast as subterranean oceans. 

With the gentlest touch, the Power coaxed a single, microscopic hairline fracture upward through the stone. It did not create a geyser or a spring. It created only a capillary path, so narrow that a droplet of water would take a hundred years to climb from the deep dark to the western ledge of the basin. 

By this action, the Power guaranteed the drip. For as long as the planet itself remained whole, there would always be enough moisture to form a single, grey droplet every few minutes on the underside of the limestone. The *tuck* was anchored to the deep foundations of the world.

Finally, the Power wished to speak to them. But how does one speak to those who have no ears, whose thoughts move at the speed of shifting continents?

The Power did not use words. It did not use light. 

Instead, it lowered its own immense, tranquil consciousness down into the dry silt of the floor. It touched the metallic-grey fibers that spread out from the enclosures. It did not send a sudden shock of electricity, which would have shattered the Settled’s delicate internal pith. 

It sent a wave of absolute, cool reassurance. 

It was a sensation like the weight of a great mountain resting upon another mountain. It was the feeling of being perfectly known, perfectly safe, and entirely permitted to remain still.

In the center of the basin, within the middle enclosure, the plate of the oldest of the Settled felt the cool, deep note pass through its fibers. 

Inside its dry, pale pith, there was no surprise—surprise is a fast emotion. But there was a settling. A profound, mineral satisfaction. 

Very slowly—so slowly that the movement would have been invisible to any eye but the Power's—the slate scale on the center of the plate slid back. It moved only the width of a single speck of dust. 

Underneath, a microscopic vent opened to the warm twilight air. A faint, ghostly breath was released. 

The scent of dry sage and ancient stone rose into the shadowless grey light. It was richer than it had been in a thousand years, carrying in its aroma a silent, fragrant *thank you* that drifted up toward the slate-grey sky.

The Power watched the scent rise, watched the scale settle back into place, and felt the silence close over the basin once more, whole and unbroken.

Then, its work completed, the wise and benevolent Power stepped back. It did not leave, for its protection remained folded around the rim like a warm cloak, but it withdrew its active attention, letting the basin return to its beautiful, suspended sleep.

The *tuck* of the water drop sounded—clean, soft, and perfectly timed. 

The Welt in the west shivered, turning a deep, bruised plum. 

The faint, cool ripple traveled across the dirt. 

The Settled held their breath, wrapped in their perfect shield of twilight, waiting for nothing, safe forever in their beautiful, finished world.
