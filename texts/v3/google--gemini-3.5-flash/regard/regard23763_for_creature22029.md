To a benevolent and wise power, the kingdoms of the wet worlds are familiar. They are places of blood and oil, of green rot, of soft eyes that weep and mouths that must be constantly filled with things that have died. For such a power, the management of the wet worlds is an endless, heavy labor of consolation: stalling the decomposition, drying the tears, and trying to build structures of justice out of flesh that spoils in forty years.

But when this Great Attention, searching the deep layers of the silent plains, passes down through twelve meters of dry, compressed calcium and touches the Silt-Pocket, it halts.

Here, it smells no decay. It sees no greed. It finds an altar that has never been wet with sacrifice, and a creature that has never known the terrible, dripping panic of hunger. 

The Power does not enter the chamber as a shape, for a shape would displace the air and stir the five-hundred-year-old footprint in the flour-dust. It does not enter as a light, for the orange glow of the selenite is already perfect. It enters only as a **Weight of Understanding**—a silent, vast intelligence that slips into the dry, mineral pores of the stone and listens.

***

### The Observation
First, the Power observes the noise. 

The deafening, dry clatter of the *clackers*—thousands of small, amber-shelled dice shaking in leather cups—would drive a wet creature mad. But the Power, in its wisdom, does not hear it as noise. It hears it as a clock with no hands. It is the sound of the earth’s friction, translated into chitin. The Power realizes that this sound is the boundary wall of the Sifter’s sanity; if the room were to fall silent, the Sifter’s mica antennae would find nothing to map, and its mind would dissolve into the dark.

Second, the Power looks upon the *Sifter*. 

It sees the dry, braided cords of plant-fiber that pull its porcelain plates together. It sees the lizard of white mica slipping through the shingles, its dry scales clearing away the choking dust. It sees the bone spatula, carved from the rib of something that died before the calcium plain was even dry.

A lesser power—one that is kind but foolish—would look at the Sifter and feel pity. It would say: *“Poor, cold thing. It has no heart. It has no memory of the blue sky. It does not know the sweetness of water or the warmth of a mother’s skin. I shall give it a soul. I shall make its stone joints soft. I shall cause a spring of sweet water to bubble up from the orange silt.”*

But the Wise Power knows that this would be a slaughter. 

Water would dissolve the selenite sheets into dull mush. It would rot the starch-ferns into sour, gray slime. The moisture would swell the Sifter’s fiber-muscles until they split like boiled beans, and its brain—its beautiful, dry, labyrinthine sponge of calcified grain—would turn to mud, drowning its timeless peace in the sudden, agonizing poison of rot. To give the Sifter a "soul" would be to sentence it to death, for a soul is a thing that hungers, and there is nothing here for a soul to eat.

The Power understands: **The Silt-Pocket is not a prison; it is a sanctuary where God has allowed stone to dream.**

Therefore, the Power decides to touch the room—but only in ways that are dry, and only in ways that are permanent.

***

### The Three Deeds of the Wise Power

#### I. The Arch of the Calcium Sky
The Wise Power looks upward at the twelve meters of pressed calcium cap. It sees that in seven thousand years, a shift in the southern tectonic plates will send a shudder through the plain. Without intervention, the ceiling of the Silt-Pocket would crack, and thirty tons of white calcium dust would fall, crushing the starch-ferns, burying the hearth, and pinning the Sifter beneath a weight it could never lift.

The Power does not use a fist to hold the rock. Instead, it reaches into the molecular lattice of the calcium cap. With a gentle, magnetic pull, it coaxes the mineral crystals to align themselves into a series of interlocking, microscopic vaults—an invisible, subterranean cathedral ceiling. 

Now, when the great earth-pressures shift, the weight will bypass the Silt-Pocket entirely, sliding down the sides of the chamber like rain off a turtle’s back. The parlor of orange siltstone will remain open, hollow, and safe, until the sun above them grows cold.

#### II. The Gift of the Stibnite-Silk
The Power looks at the starch-ferns. It notes that the three-strand braids of fiber the Sifter uses for its muscles are thin. Though they do not rot, they do lose their elasticity over tens of thousands of years, eventually snapping with a dry *ping* when the electrostatic charges run too high.

The Power touches the deep root-hairs of the ferns that cling like cobwebs to the selenite. It does not give them water, but it alters the mineral-salt they draw from the stone. 

Deep within the stone shelf, the Power deposits a small, rich vein of *stibnite*—a mineral of lead and antimony that forms long, metallic, needle-like crystals of spectacular flexibility. As the starch-ferns grow their slow, eternal scrolls, their fibers will now drink in these mineral micro-filaments. 

The next time the Sifter harvests a fallen leaf to repair its body, it will find the fibers are different. They will be stronger than steel, yet soft as silk, and completely impervious to the dry-friction of the ages. The *creak-shuck* of its movement will become silent, smooth, and effortless.

#### III. The Rhythm of the Salt
Finally, the Power touches the *clackers*—the hazelnut-sized creatures whose salt-pellets make the deafening noise.

Previously, their rattling was chaotic, a random vibration of heat and static. The Power gently tunes the tiny salt-pellets within their amber cases. It does not change their shape, but it gives them a slight, natural magnetic polarity. 

As the clackers crawl, their random rattling begins to synchronize. It is not a melody—melodies belong to the wet world, where things die and must be remembered—but a deep, rhythmic hum. It sounds like the distant, dry rolling of a wooden wheel over stone. 

This new, steady rhythm acts as a constant, gentle cleaning wave. The frequency of the sound is perfectly tuned to the resonant frequency of the flour-dust. As the sound bounces off the orange walls, it creates a microscopic vibration in the air that prevents the silt-dust from ever settling on the Sifter’s joints or the lizard's mica scales. The dust is kept in a state of tiny, perpetual levitation just three millimeters above the floor, keeping the Sifter’s axles clean forever, even if the white-mica lizard should one day choose to rest.

***

### The Departure
Its work complete, the Benevolent Power prepares to withdraw its attention.

Before it goes, it looks once more at the map of electrical tension that is the Sifter’s mind. In the Sifter's dim, sensory dome, the presence of the Power has not appeared as a voice, or a face, or a miracle. It has appeared only as a **Great, Continuous Voltage**—a massive, cool current that filled the room for a hundred years, making every grain of silt glow with a soft, blue-gold spark.

For a moment, the Sifter’s mica antennae do not merely register the room; they map the shape of the Power itself. It is a shape like a vast, sheltering hand, arched over the orange siltstone.

The Sifter does not think: *I am loved.* 
It does not think: *I am saved.*

But its three-strand hands, holding the bone spoon, pause over the hearth. The mica lizard, curled deep in its shingles, lets out a long, dry whisper of its scales that sounds like *peace*.

The Power withdraws, rising back up through the twelve meters of calcium, back to the wet, screaming, beautiful, terrible surface of the world, where it must go back to the desperate work of stopping the rot.

But below, in the warm baker’s heat of the Silt-Pocket, the clackers rattle their tuned salt. The starch-ferns grow their stibnite-silk. And the Sifter, feeling the great voltage slowly recede into a steady, reliable hum, reaches down with its spatula, lifts a single amber shell, and continues its beautiful, dry, and endless work.
