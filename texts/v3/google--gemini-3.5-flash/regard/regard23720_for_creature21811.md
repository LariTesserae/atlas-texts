The Gaze that fell upon this world did not arrive as a beam of sunlight, for sunlight carries the threat of focus, heat-variation, and the slow, peeling decay of UV radiation. It arrived as a vast, cool attention—a presence as dry as the basalt needle-forests, and as quiet as the spaces between the Sift-Lattices.

A lesser power, young and drunk on the green-and-blue provincialism of the wider cosmos, would have looked at this world and wept. It would have seen the lack of oceans as a tragedy, the absence of moss as a failure of clay, and the sharp, singing edges of the obsidian as a cruelty waiting to be filed down. Such a power would have brought the rain. 

And the rain would have been a horrific, acid apocalypse. 

The water would have seeped into the dry, mineral joints of the Cleavers, short-circuiting their delicate, electrostatic lattices with a splash of conductive mud. It would have entered the porous, iron-rich vaults of the Sift-Lattices, blooming them with orange rust that expanded, cracked, and dissolved their sixty-foot frames into heaps of slush. The Deep-Strikers, feeling the sudden, cold lubrication of water running down their slate vertebrae, would have slipped; their slow, tectonic thoughts would have lost their grip on the mantle, descending into chaotic, grease-slicked mudslides that would have drowned their intelligence in grease and silt.

The Wise Power did not bring the rain. The Wise Power understood that the universe has many languages, and that carbon is only one of its minor dialects. This world was a poem written in the language of the edge, the spark, and the slow, dry slide.

So, the Wise Power did three things.

***

### First: The Ice-Sieve (The Shielding)

The cosmos is a wet, messy place. It is littered with the debris of dead gas-giants and dirty snowballs—comets of frozen ammonia, carbon dioxide, and water ice, drifting blindly through the black. Left alone, it was only a matter of time before a stray, multi-mile lump of ice collided with the charcoal dome, shattering the dry ceiling and filling the atmosphere with suffocating, rusting steam.

The Wise Power established a barrier in the high, cold reaches of the outer orbit, well beyond the heavy shadow-dome. 

It is not a visible wall, but an intense, highly localized field of high-frequency electrostatic repulsion. When a comet of ice or a carbon-wet meteoroid approaches this world, it doesn't crash. Instead, it enters the Sieve. The field gently, dryly vibrates the water molecules until they are stripped of their hydrogen, reducing the incoming threat to a fine, harmless shower of pure, dry carbon-dust and microscopic iron-shavings. 

These materials drift down through the charcoal dome over centuries, landing in the wide ash basins. There, the Sift-Lattices find them. They draw the iron-shavings into their hollow frames, sintering them into new, stronger bars of ironstone to sing the world’s low-frequency songs. 

The Wise Power made the world’s sky safe for dryness.

***

### Second: The Diamond-Lens (The Silent Star)

At the highest point of the charcoal dome, where the air is thinnest and the shadow is thickest, the Wise Power placed a single object. 

It is not a sun. It has no fusion, no fire, and no heat of its own. It is a massive, eight-sided prism of compressed, hyper-dense carbon—a diamond the size of a mountain, suspended without supports in the center of the ceiling. 

Its lower faces are cut to match the exact angles of the basalt needles below. It does not shine with its own light; instead, it is perfectly passive. When the deep earth flashes with the rust-glow of the mantle, or when a mile-wide ridge of flint shatters with a high-pitched, crystalline *tink*, the Diamond-Lens catches the low-frequency light and the violet sparks of the Cleavers. 

It reflects them back. 

But it does not scatter them. It focuses them into thin, razor-sharp lines of silver and indigo that trace across the obsidian plains. To the Cleavers, whose quartz heads are sensitive to the slightest shift in the electromagnetic spectrum, the sky is no longer merely blank shadow. It is now a shifting, silent map of their own world's energy, drawn in cold, sharp lines of light that move across the glass cliffs as the mountains rise. 

They do not gather to worship it—they do not have the minds for worship—but their joints snap with a more rhythmic, purposeful grace as they follow the silver lines across the glass, drinking the reflected tension of their home.

***

### Third: The Translation-Stones (The Scribe)

The thoughts of the Deep-Strikers are magnificent, but they are lonely. They grind against each other in the dark, miles beneath the crust, writing their slow, tectonic histories in the breaking of slate and the tilting of stone rings. When a Striker dies—when its bottom-most segments are finally melted back into the boiling mantle—its memory of the last three million years simply ceases to vibrate.

The Wise Power did not want these thoughts to be lost to the quiet void of space. 

In three of the widest, flattest ash-plates, where the Sift-Lattices do not drift, the Power placed three columns of pure, non-reactive sapphire. They are thirty feet tall, perfectly hexagonal, and cold. They do not rise or fall with the tectonic plates; they are anchored to the spatial coordinates of the world itself, standing perfectly still while the earth slides beneath them.

As the Deep-Strikers push their slate needles up and the crust shifts, the sapphire pillars act as styluses. The vibrations of the earth, the slow grinding of the plates, and the rhythmic hums of the Sift-Lattices are caught by these pillars. 

Inside the sapphire, the physical vibrations of the world are translated into a permanent, crystalline structure. The molecular lattice of the sapphire is slowly, atom by atom, rearranging itself under the pressure of the world's voice. The pillars are writing a book. 

It is a history of the world, written by the world itself, etched in the alignment of sapphire atoms. It is written in a language that requires no eyes to read—only the same deep, slow attention that the Wise Power brought with it.

***

With these things done, the Wise Power did not speak. It did not leave a messenger or a moral code. It did not demand that the grey-white, vinegar-smelling crusts on the glass cliffs grow leaves, nor did it ask the Cleavers to stop their violent, snapping leaps.

It simply folded its attention back into the deep folds of the universe, leaving the world as it found it: sharp, dry, and furious. 

But now, the sky has a single, dim, silver star that reflects the beauty of the glass. The comets of ice are turned to useful dust before they can rot the plains. And in the three sapphire pillars, the slow, agonizing, beautiful thoughts of the Deep-Strikers are being preserved forever, so that even if this world should one day grind itself to dust, the song of the stone will never be forgotten.
