For three hundred years, the Great Attention had been fixed upon the cold, turning outer rings of the cosmos, where stars died in silence and left behind nothing but iron-dust and ice. 

But when the Great Attention shifted inward, toward the yellow-gold eye of this young system, it caught upon a sharp, dark stutter in the light. 

It was a world that had only recently finished rising. 

To the Attention—which possessed no eyes but perceived through weight, vibration, and the deep, cold mathematics of gravity—this world was a handful of broken black glass, tossed into the path of a furnace. It was too sharp. It was too new. It was a place of scream-and-shear, where the geology was still screaming its birth-pangs.

And then, the Attention felt the *thump-thump*.

It was not the heartbeat of the world. It was too small, too rhythmic, too desperately stubborn. It was the flywheels. 

The Attention focused its immense, quiet presence upon the vertical city. It saw the Needle-born. It saw the slate-colored skin, the deep, barrel-shaped ribcages, the silent *Knot-sign* being ticked out on horizontal guide-ropes by hands stained with grease. It saw the Turnstile-Men, their shoulders bunched like wet clay, walking in slow, agonizing circles to pull a mountain back into place with iron thread. 

A lesser, younger power might have pitied them. A younger power, full of the loud, clumsy mercy of green worlds, would have reached down with a heavy hand. It would have flattened the peaks. It would have brought down forests of oak and pine, poured soft, brown loam into the rifts, and thickened the air until it was sweet and heavy with moisture.

But the Attention was wise. It knew that to give these people a flat, green world would be to murder them. 

Their deep lungs, built to milk the thin ether, would have ruptured in the heavy air. Their flat, felt-wrapped feet would have slipped and rotted in wet mud. Their glare-lids would have grown useless and degenerated, leaving them blind in a world without mirrors. Their entire society—their beautiful, silent, delicate physics of tension, balance, and trust—would have been washed away like salt in a rainstorm. They were not victims of the vertical glass; they were its masterpiece.

So, the Attention did not change the Needle-born. 

Instead, it leaned down, very slowly, and breathed upon the basalt.

It did not blow with the force of a storm, but with the pressure of a thought. It performed three quiet gestures—three adjustments of the cosmic weight—to make the world a slightly better home for the things that had chosen to love it.

---

### The First Touch: The Tempering of the Needle-Roots

The Attention looked deep beneath the city, down into the white-hot rifts where the basalt needles had their roots. It saw the terrible, chaotic shearing forces that kept the Tensioners awake in their suspended brass cabins. The peaks were too brittle; they moved with sudden, violent jerks that threatened to snap the multi-strand cables like spider-silk.

With a touch as light as a settling sparrow, the Attention altered the crystalline lattice of the subterranean stone. It did not melt the basalt, but it *tempered* it. It aligned the microscopic silica chains, giving the volcanic glass a slow, elastic memory.

On that afternoon, the Tensioners pressed their brass listening-rods to the dark obsidian and fell silent. 

The terrifying, sharp *tink-tink-tink* of micro-fractures—the sound of a peak preparing to shear—had ceased. In its place, the mountain began to sing. It was a very low, warm, sub-octave hum, like a massive wet finger being run along the rim of a crystal glass. 

When the peaks tilted now, they did not jerk. They leaned with the slow, dignified grace of a ship rolling on a calm swell. The Tensioners, sitting in their swinging cabins, closed their glare-lids and smiled in the dark. The mountain was no longer their enemy; it was their partner in the dance.

### The Second Touch: The Velvet-Spore

The Attention looked upon the feet of the Needle-born, wrapped in their thick, oil-slicked felt. It saw how they slipped on the wet iron grates when the sulfur-steam was thick. It saw how the youngest children, before they had fully mastered the *Tether-step*, sometimes looked down into the mile of empty blue space with small, trembling shoulders.

The Attention did not create grass, for grass is slippery and rots when squeezed. Instead, it spoke to the silica-rich condensation that dripped from the copper gutters.

Out of the mortar-joints of the cold-rolled iron and the wet, black faces of the cliffs, a new growth appeared. It was not green; it was a deep, velvet slate-grey, the color of the Needle-born’s own skin. It was a mineral lichen, soft as sheared wool but tough as copper wire.

The citizens called it *Lead-Moss*. It did not need soil; it fed on sulfur and the mineral salt of the steam. Wherever it grew, it formed a dry, springy cushion that clung to the basalt with millions of microscopic, suction-tipped roots. 

Within a year, the most dangerous passages—the narrow vertical chimneys, the high, wind-swept suspension alleys—were carpeted in this grey velvet. It did not make the steps soft, but it made them *sure*. The felt-soled boots of the Needle-born caught upon the Lead-Moss like iron to a magnet. If a child slipped, they did not fall into the empty air; they slid into a thick, dry cradle of mineral wool that held them safe until a Turnstile-Man could reach down with a harness-line.

Furthermore, the Lead-Moss acted as a natural filter. As the acidic, sulfurous water ran down the cliffs, the moss drank the heavy metals, leaving the water that dripped into the copper catch-basins cold, clear, and sweet, tasting no longer of battery-acid, but of the clean, cool sky.

### The Third Touch: The Ring of the Air-Bound

Finally, the Attention looked downward, into the white-hot haze of the deep rifts where the dead were consigned. 

It saw the wrapped, mummified cylinders of salt and bone, rising and falling on the thermal updrafts. They were silent, chaotic, and scattered—drifting like lost charcoal through the hot steam.

The Attention reached into the thermal currents. It did not quench the furnace of the earth, but it organized its breath. It gathered the rising heat-plumes and shaped them into a single, vast, slow-revolving gyre—a great horizontal wheel of warm air that circled the range at the level of the highest basalt needles, just below the blue of the deep sky.

The mummified dead were drawn into this circle. They did not fall into the fire, nor did they scatter into the gray cloud-banks. 

They joined the circle. 

Now, when a citizen of the city looked up into the late afternoon sky, they saw more than the Mute-Sails drifting on their leathery wings. They saw the *Ancestors' Gyre*—a slow, glittering ring of white salt-mummies, catching the golden light of the setting sun. They revolved around the peaks like the rings of a dark planet, silent, beautiful, and eternal. 

The Needle-born no longer called the drop "The Loss." They called it "The Joining." 

They could look up from their iron galleries and point out the small, glittering shape of their grandfather or their daughter, drifting in the high, clean blue, forever catching the warm wind, forever watching the city they had helped to hold together.

---

The Great Attention did not leave a monument. It did not write its name upon the stone. It simply withdrew its focus, returning to the cold, distant margins of the universe where the stars were dying.

It left the world sharp. It left it vertical. It left the air thin and the iron heavy.

But down in the rifts, the flywheels turned with a slightly sweeter sound. On the vertical cliffs, the Lead-Moss caught the wet steam and turned it to clear water. And high above, the great ring of the dead shone like a collar of pearls around the neck of the young world.

The citizens did not pray to the power, for they still had no word for "God." But sometimes, in the late afternoon, when the wind was low and the stay-lines sang their warm, stable note, a Tensioner would look out across the black mirrors of the valley, draw his glare-lid across his eyes, and sign a single, slow gesture with his hand:

*The water is clean. The rope is holding. We are here.*
