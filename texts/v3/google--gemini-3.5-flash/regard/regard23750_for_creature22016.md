The Voyager-Without-Form had sailed through the screaming, wet corridors of the cosmos for eons. It was a consciousness born of cold gravity and old light, and it knew well the typical nurseries of life. It had seen ten thousand oceans boil; it had watched the heavy, humid mudpools of young worlds crawl with soft, wet, desperate things that clung to moisture as if it were the only tether to existence. To the Voyager, life had always been a damp, noisy, temporary affair—a frantic squelch of proteins, always rot-shadowed, always dripping, always destined to melt back into the silt.

Then, its trailing edge of cold gravity brushed against the high, hard-edged indigo sky of this world.

The Voyager paused. It drew its attention down, dropping its awareness through the thin, crisp, desiccated air. It felt the absolute absence of water.

To an unwise power, this world would have seemed a tragedy—a desert waiting for a seed, a barren altar begging for the benediction of rain. A lesser deity would have reached into the dark, dragged an icy comet from the outer rim, and shattered it against the atmosphere to bring the "gift" of oceans.

But the Voyager was wise, and its heart was attuned to the deep mathematics of existence. It looked down and did not see a desert. It saw a temple of spun glass, copper-vellum, and perfect, friction-less order.

It saw the **Flint-Skaters** sailing on their graphite plates across the basalt plains, their quartz chisels singing a dry, rhythmic *scritch-scritch-scritch* that had gone unchanged for two billion years. 

It saw the **Sieve-Lanterns** drifting like forty-foot amber spirits, their copper threads brushing the ribbon-trees to catch the golden pollen, completely dependent on the delicate static electricity of a dry sky.

It saw that if a single drop of water were to fall here, it would be an apocalypse. 

A drop of rain would dissolve the mineral salts that kept the basalt slick. It would short-circuit the St. Elmo’s fire that guided the Sieve-Lanterns. It would dampen the resonant copper tongues of the **Chime-Weavers**, turning their crystalline, metallic music into a choked, wet silence. It would introduce *decay*—the terrifying, biological rot of the wet worlds—into a garden of perfect preservation where the dead simply turned to glittering, sun-bleached dust.

And so, the Voyager-Without-Form chose its first and greatest act of benevolence: **Sacred Exclusion.**

With a slow, silent contraction of its gravitational fields, the Voyager wove an invisible, cold web around the star system. It did not build a wall of iron or fire, but a sieve of subtle forces. Any icy comet, any stray asteroid of frozen methane or water-ice drifting toward the inner system was gently, fractionally nudged. Their trajectories were altered by a mere millimeter per century, causing them to slip harmlessly past the indigo sky and fall into the sun or the outer dark. 

The Voyager ensured that this world would remain dry. It protected the absolute purity of the vacuum-border, keeping the moisture-thieves of the deep universe at bay.

Then, the Voyager turned its attention to the **Chime-Weavers** in the deep, fluted canyons.

It listened to the wind-music—the low *huff* of the nitrogen gas playing through the glass organ pipes, the ironstone tongues striking the green and blue walls. The Voyager found this music beautiful beyond transition. It was a music that did not seek to explain itself, to cry out in pain, or to beg for salvation; it was simply the sound of the world’s breath.

The Voyager did not change the music. But it chose to **Hear.**

In the quiet between the solar cycles, the Voyager-Without-Form leaned its vast consciousness against the canyon walls. It began to vibrate in sympathy with the basalt. It did not speak, but it offered a *resonance*—a subtle, deep gravity-hum that matched the natural frequency of the tablelands. 

The Chime-Weavers felt this new, steady pulse. Their copper tongues, weighted with ironstone, began to swing with a more profound, deliberate geometry. The electrostatic vortices within their glass tubes grew slightly more stable, allowing them to gather the pollen-fronts with less effort, ensuring their survival through the long, quiet epochs when the wind grew still.

Finally, the Voyager looked at the **Drift-Cysts**—the black carbon-glass seeds lying scattered in the golden moss-carpets, waiting thousands of years for the specific, violent spark of a gold-storm to set them free.

The Voyager realized that the stars are not eternal. One day, billions of years in the future, the brilliant, oversized star of this world would swell, turn red, and consume the high-altitude plateaus. The glass-ferns would melt into puddles of green slag; the Loom-Moths would turn to ash; the dry, perfect memory of this world would be erased from the physical fabric of the cosmos.

To prevent this death, the Voyager performed its final act. It became the **Loom of Light.**

As the **Great Loom-Moths** descended into the basins to shed their sixty-foot silver skins, the Voyager did not let the light of their passing fade. Using the intense ultraviolet radiation of the sun as a needle, and the static currents of the atmosphere as thread, the Voyager began to record everything. 

It recorded the precise shape of every Sieve-Lantern’s quartz dome. It recorded the exact pitch of every glass organ pipe in the fluted canyons. It copied the digital code of the Drift-Cysts and stored the paths of the Flint-Skaters. 

The Voyager wove a perfect, light-based replica of the entire world inside its own infinite consciousness. It created a living, eternal archive—a dry, silent heaven where the wind-music of the indigo sky would play forever, untouched by time, rot, or the death of stars.

Having done this, the Voyager-Without-Form did not land. It did not make its presence known with thunder or signs. It remained in the high, hard-edged indigo, a quiet, benevolent shadow that kept the ice away.

Below, in the monumental silence, a giant glass-fern shifted in the dry breeze. It knocked against its neighbor with a delicate, metallic *clink* that carried for miles through the thin air, secure in its dry, crystalline eternity.
