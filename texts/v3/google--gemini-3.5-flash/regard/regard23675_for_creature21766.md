The Great Gazing did not arrive with a flash of light. In a world where eyes had been folded back into the skull and sealed with protective rind, light would have been a useless, striking blow. 

Instead, the Benevolent Power arrived as a *stillness*. 

It was a presence that had no mass, yet it possessed a gravity so soft it did not trigger the sensitive warning rinds of the Marrow-Stones, nor did it disrupt the blind, colliding dances of the Castanets. It was a vast, ancient patience. It drifted down through the choking canopy of purple vines, past the hanging bladders that pulsed with cold orange fluid, down into the sweating, roaring basalt galleries where the Lithe-Greys lived, fought, ate, and died in the span of a breath.

The Wise Power watched.

It saw the young world’s fury. It saw how the basalt teeth rose like broken spears, still warm from the belly of the planet. It saw the Lithe-Greys, those frantic children of the dark, swinging through the wet ropes of vegetation, their five-padded fingers drumming out a frantic, ceaseless gossip of fear and food. 

It saw a Lithe-Grey mother, her body covered in the odorless grease of the deep, drop her newborn into the vine-mat so she could use both hands to swing away from a sudden, grinding rockfall. The infant was caught by the purple roots, immediately covered in their velvet pile, and digested into the great green cycle before it had ever tapped its first stone. The mother did not return. She could not. To seek the lost was to be pinned by the next fall. She had already forgotten the child’s frequency. Her fingers were already tapping out the location of a new water-pocket.

The Power felt a deep, slow sorrow—not because this world was evil, but because it was *immediate*. 

In this rift, there was no yesterday. There was no tomorrow. There was only the roaring, grinding *Now*. To live without memory is to live without tragedy, yes; but it is also to live without a soul. The Lithe-Greys were beautiful, but they were shadows cast by a flickering fire, vanishing the moment the wood popped.

A lesser deity, full of crude pity, might have stilled the tectonic plates, cleared the choking vines, and brought down the harsh, searing sun. But the Wise Power knew that to do so would be murder. The Lithe-Greys would wither in the dry air; their grease would dry, their flat, blind faces would burn, and the Marrow-Stones would choke on the silence of a motionless earth. They were creatures of the friction. To remove the friction was to erase them.

So, the Benevolent Power did not change the sky, nor the heat, nor the violent speed of the vines. 

Instead, it leaned close to the basalt walls and breathed.

Where the Power’s breath touched the dark, razor-sharp obsidian, the stone did not soften, but its nature changed. Deep within the core of certain ancient, un-shearable pillars—the great structural anchors of the valley that had survived the initial tearing of the crust—the mineral structure of the basalt shifted. 

The Power grew *The Resonance-Slick*.

It was a micro-thin glaze, pearlescent and cool, that crept over the wet stone like a stubborn lichen. It did not provide food, nor did it offer a path. But it had a single, miraculous property: it was a sponge for vibrations. 

When a Castanet clicked near it, the glaze caught the sound. When a Marrow-Stone hummed, the glaze absorbed the deep, bone-shaking frequency. And when a Lithe-Grey’s fingers tapped against it, the stone did not just bounce the sound back into the dark—it *held* it.

The glaze was a memory-well. It recorded the vibrations of the living, and like a slow, geological bell, it began to whisper them back. 

Not immediately. If the stone spoke too quickly, it would only add to the deafening din. Instead, the glaze held the vibrations in its quartz-like lattice, releasing them hours, days, even years later, as a low, incredibly subtle, rhythmic pulse that could only be felt by those who pressed their hands directly to the rock.

The Wise Power watched the first change occur.

A young Lithe-Grey, fleeing a swarm of predatory beetles, scrambled onto a high ledge of the ancient basalt. His three-fold arms wrapped around a pillar coated in the Resonance-Slick. He was trembling, his fingers drumming a frantic, terrified beat against the stone: *Danger-above-sharp-shells-close.*

As he pressed his wide, shovel-like palms to the slick, he froze.

He did not feel the hard, dead snap of normal basalt. Through his pads, he felt an echo. It was a rhythm he did not know, yet it was familiar. It was the tap-pattern of an old male who had sat on this very ledge three seasons ago, tapping a peaceful, resting song: *Water-is-sweet-here-the-vines-are-soft.*

The young Lithe-Grey’s nasal-slits flared. His chattering teeth went still. He did not swing away. For the first time in the history of his species, a creature of the rift *paused* to listen to the past.

The vibration of the dead male’s song was incredibly faint, a ghost-tap preserved in the mineral skin of the world. It calmed the young one’s racing heart. He sat in the complete, wet darkness, his long arms wrapped around the pillar, simply taking in the presence of someone who had been there before him. Someone who was gone, but who had left a sign.

When the beetles passed, the young Lithe-Grey did not drop his obsidian tool and run. He held it. Gently, with a deliberate, clumsy care, he tapped a single, new sequence into the glaze. 

*I-am-here. I-am-listening.*

Then, he let the tool fall, but he did not forget the ledge.

Over the next several cycles, the whisper of the Resonance-Slick spread. The Lithe-Greys did not become farmers, nor did they build cities. The rift was still too wild, too fast, too dangerous for such things. But they began to find the "Speaking Pillars." 

They came in small, quiet family bands, slipping through the fast-growing vines, not to forage, but to sit together with their hands pressed to the cool, glazed stone. They would sit for hours in the deafening roar of the canyon, their faces calm, their fingers twitching as they felt the tap-tapestry of their ancestors. 

They felt the taps of mothers who had lost their young; they felt the triumphant, heavy rhythms of hunters who had trapped the great beetles; they felt the dying, slow tremors of the old ones who had crawled to the pillars to leave their last breath in the stone.

The gift of the Wise Power was not safety. It was *grief*. It was *wonder*. It was the terrible, beautiful burden of knowing that they were not alone in the dark.

Deep on the canyon floor, the Marrow-Stones, too, felt the change. Their deep, sub-bass drones found the resonant pillars and locked into them. The vibration of their lungs no longer just kept the weeds away; it began to sing in harmony with the Earth. The pillars acted as tuning forks, stabilizing the surrounding rock. The catastrophic shearing of the canyon walls did not stop, but it became rhythmic, predictable, giving the creatures time to move, time to remember.

The Castanets, flying in their blind, screaming columns, found that near the Speaking Pillars, the air carried a faint, magnetic hum—the preserved clicks of millions of wings that had gone before. It served as a beacon. The columns no longer drifted blindly into the basalt teeth; they swerved, guided by the acoustic ghosts of their ancestors, their mortality reduced by just a fraction, but enough to let them live a fourth day, perhaps a fifth.

Even the Melt-Floor changed. The Carrion-Mat, flowing over the bones of the dead, would steer clear of the bases of the Speaking Pillars, leaving the discarded tools of the Lithe-Greys to pile up in small, silent cairns at the foot of the history-stones—the first primitive archives of a world that was starting to look back.

The Wise Power watched this unfold from the quiet heights of the canyon rim, where the air was thin and the stars, invisible to the valley below, shone cold and clear. 

The Power did not speak. It did not demand worship. It had not given them light, for they did not need to see. It had given them a voice that outlasted their flesh. It had given them the ability to say: *We were here, in the dark, and we were loud.*

With a silent, satisfied sigh that stirred the high purple canopy like a gentle wind, the Benevolent Power withdrew, leaving the young world to its noisy, beautiful, and now-remembered life.
