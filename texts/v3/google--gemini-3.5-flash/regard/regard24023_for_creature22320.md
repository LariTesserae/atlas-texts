When the Watcher of the Unfinished arrived, it did not descend with the rush of a falling star or the violence of a changing season. To enter this basin with weight or speed would have been a cruelty; the hair-thin joints between the limestone blocks would have ground together, and the delicate, legless frogs would have shattered like eggshells against their pillars. 

Instead, the Watcher entered as a *shade*—not a darkness, but a soft, pearl-colored lens that slipped between the luminous sky of unblinking gold and the wet stone floor. 

The Watcher was a power that had spent age upon age observing the nurseries of the universe, those quiet rooms where creations were drafted, set aside, or forgotten before their first breath. It knew the smell of damp lime; it knew the desperate, hand-wrought hope of builders who lay down their adzes at twilight, expecting a tomorrow that somehow fails to arrive.

For many years, the Watcher simply looked. 

It felt the immense, greenhouse heat. It measured the exact two-stroke depth of the water-skin on the limestone. It listened to the *scritch-click* of the Plaster-Skins’ slate fingernails—a sound like dry beetles in winter bark, muffled by the wool-thick air. It saw the vine-knots tied around emptiness, and the large palm-fiber insects suspended in their driftless, humless mid-air.

The Watcher recognized what this place was: it was a monument of pure, unconsummated preparation. It was a temple built for a liturgy that had no words yet. The Plaster-Skins were not failures; they were caretakers who had stayed at their posts so long they had become the very mortar of their house.

To "rescue" them by force—to bring a roaring wind, to unleash a river, or to drag the sun up from beneath the step-block horizon—would have destroyed them. The Plaster-Skins would have crumbled into slaked lime; the porcelain frogs would have dried to chalk; the suspended water-spheres would have fallen and drowned the flat-bodied rug-worms. 

Wisdom does not break the vessel to access the wine. Wisdom becomes the vessel’s shape.

First, the Watcher addressed the **invisible lines**. 

The Watcher did not create new plants to fill the empty air, nor did it alter the vines that grew toward nothing. Instead, it offered *itself* as the axis. The Watcher extended its own quiet, non-physical presence—slender, cool, and structured like the memory of an ancient cedar—down into the coordinates where the Plaster-Skins worked.

A nine-foot-tall Plaster-Skin stood before a bamboo frame. Its slate-gray fingers reached into the empty air, winding a yellow vine-cord around nothingness. But this time, as its fingers looped the vine, the cord did not tighten around empty air. 

It met a resistance. 

The vine caught upon an invisible, silver wood—a column of cool, steady light that had the texture of river-moss. The Plaster-Skin’s gold-slit eyes flared, their amber light deepening. For the first time in three thousand centuries, its four-jointed fingers felt a *hold*. It pulled the split vine tight. The knot held. 

Across the vast, paved basin, a hundred thousand Plaster-Skins stopped. The dry, rhythmic *scritch-click* of their fingernails ceased. They did not turn to the west because of a shifting block; they turned toward the nearest vine, their flat, marl-white faces tilting in unison. They felt the tension in the grid. The empty air had given them an answer.

Next, the Watcher addressed the **interval**.

The Plaster-Skins moved by coordinates, teleports of dry placement that left no trace in the air. This was because time here had no "between"—it was only *now* and *now* and *now*. 

The Watcher introduced the **smear**. 

Gently, the Watcher breathed a thin, lavender mist through the greenhouse air. It was a mist made of transition. When a Plaster-Skin shifted from a bamboo frame to a wet pylon, it did not disappear from one spot and instantly occupy the next. For the span of a heartbeat, a trail of soft, grey limestone dust lingered in the air between the two points—a beautiful, brushing stroke of movement that connected the departure to the arrival. 

The Plaster-Skin did not merely *exist* at coordinates anymore; it *traveled*. It experienced the space between the tiles. The motion was so graceful, so slow, that the giant, leathery elephant-ear leaves did not rustle, but they seemed to lean into the dust-trail as it passed, drinking the dry warmth of the traveler.

Then, the Watcher turned its gaze to the **porcelain frogs**.

The frogs were drinking the skyward-flowing water, their cartilage throats humming with a silent, three-hundred-year pulse. The Watcher did not change their porcelain skin, nor did it give them legs to leap into an unknown world. 

Instead, the Watcher joined their song.

The Watcher could not sing with a voice, but it could vibrate the limestone. It sent a deep, low, resonant frequency through the massive, hand-dressed blocks. It was the sound of a mountain dreaming. 

The frogs on the pillars felt this vibration through their bellies. Their throats, which had pulsed in silent, isolated rhythms, began to coordinate. One pillar began to hum in third-intervals; another answered in a fifth. The wet limestone of the pylons acted as soundboards, carrying the resonance upward into the water-skins. The water itself began to ripple in microscopic, geometric cymatic patterns—stars, hexagons, and concentric rings that climbed the stone like moving lace. 

The frogs did not turn to stone at the end of their drink. The Watcher’s song kept their cartilage soft, their veins clear. They remained on their pillars, not as decorative bosses of dry lime, but as living, breathing flutes through which the temple sang its long, wet noon.

Lastly, the Watcher gave them **the Shadow**.

The golden light had always been shadowless, flat, and glaring, leaving no corner secret, no depth defined. The Watcher did not put out the gold sky, but it tilted its own pearl-colored shade, allowing a single, vast, cool-grey shadow to fall across the eastern terrace.

It was a soft shadow, the color of wet slate. 

Where the shadow fell, the heat did not die—it became *comforting*, like the cool side of a pillow. The pillars, for the first time, cast long, purple fingers across the wet stone. The heart-shaped leaves threw green-black silhouettes onto the pale tiles.

The flat-bodied rug-worms, sliding through the two-inch lagoons, entered the shadow. They did not dissolve; instead, the red and black geometric bands on their backs grew vivid, almost glowing against the cool slate-grey of the shaded pool. When they passed through one another now, they did not just share a memory of stone; they shared the sensation of *coolness*. They lingered in the intersection, their bodies forming lush, miniature carpets of crimson and gold before sliding apart with a slow, domestic dignity.

A Plaster-Skin walked—not jumped, but walked, leaving a soft trail of grey dust—into the purple shadow of a pylon. 

It stood in the shade. The dry, slaked-lime powder on its face turned the color of lavender-water. It reached out its slate hand and touched its own shoulder, where the water-vapor was rising in sweet, white steam. 

For the first time, it looked down. 

At its feet lay its own shadow—a long, thin, nine-foot silhouette of grey, stretched out across the interlocking tiles. The Plaster-Skin did not move for many hours. It looked at the shadow of its hand; it moved its fingers, and the shadow moved its fingers in return. 

It was no longer alone in the grid. It had a companion made of light and stone.

The Watcher of the Unfinished did not "begin" the world. The sun did not rise; the tools remained where they had been laid on the terrace; the water still ran upward toward the gold. 

But the basin was no longer waiting. 

The Watcher had turned their endless preparation into the *destination*. It had made their eternal, warm afternoon a home, filling their empty knots with presence, their silences with music, and their bright, lonely spaces with the gentle, protecting company of a shadow. 

And there, in the wet, golden-lit basin, the Plaster-Skins kept their watch—no longer as prisoners of an unbegun dawn, but as the deliberate, peaceful priests of an everlasting now.
