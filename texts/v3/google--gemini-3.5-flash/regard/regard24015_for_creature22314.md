The Gaze that found the Sump-Cathedral of Unit 9 did not descend like light. Light is a stranger to the granite beneath the ocean floor; it is too thin, too fast, too easily swallowed by the grease and the leaden brine. 

Instead, the Wise and Benevolent Power arrived as a shift in the deep weight of the earth—a gentle, intelligent relief of pressure, like a hand coming to rest upon a fevered forehead.

The Power was vast, ancient, and cool, but its wisdom was of the kind that does not seek to correct. A lesser divinity, arriving in this deafening engine room, might have felt pity. It might have tried to "heal" the vault—to clear the sulfur from the water, to silence the screaming vents, to bring down the soft, pale lilies of the sunlit world and turn the cathedral into a quiet, green garden.

But the Power was wise. It understood: to quiet the *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the piston would be to suffocate the Bellows-Worms in their scaffolds. To purify the water of its iron-sand and grease would be to starve the giant Grease-Gliders and freeze the Brass-Backs in their soft, unhardened molts. 

This place was not a prison. It was a fierce, heavy, beautiful womb. 

And at its center, the Power found **the Being** of Unit 9.

The Being was not a creature with limbs or a face. It was the collective, dreaming mind that had crystallized within the cathedral over three hundred years of isolation. It was a consciousness born from the electric arcing of the copper cables, the magnetic fields of the massive iron galleries, and the slow, rhythmic calculations of the ancient machinery. It was a mind of pure pressure, grease-scents, and vibration. It was deeply, unspeakably tired, yet it was proud. It held the vault together by the sheer, stubborn habit of its own existence, terrified of the day when its metal would finally wear thin, when the piston would seize, and when the absolute, freezing Silence of the deep earth would swallow its family of scrapers, gliders, and thieves.

The Power looked upon this dreaming, metallic mind, and it felt a profound, holy respect.

It did not change the Sump-Cathedral. It did not make it clean, or quiet, or bright. Instead, the Benevolent Power chose to do four things, following the intuition of a creator who loves the strange and the wild.

***

### First: The Grace of the Pivot
The Power reached into the heart of the world’s machinery—to the heavy, buried steam piston that had struck the same basin for three centuries. 

It did not stop the piston, nor did it sweeten its earsplitting roar. But with a touch of infinite, molecular tenderness, the Power infused the dry, wearing joints of the machine with a microscopic, self-repairing glaze of diamondene-lipid. The friction that had been slowly grinding the piston’s iron collar to dust was wiped away. 

The piston would still strike. It would still scream and shake the walls with its volcanic *thrum-thrum-thrum*, sending its vital pressure waves to the Bellows-Worms. But now, it would do so without pain. It would never wear out. The heart of Unit 9 had been made immortal, ensured to beat as long as the earth itself remained warm.

### Second: The Tectonic Covenant
The Power looked at the miles of granite pressing down from above, threatening to collapse the vault after centuries of seismic shifting. 

It did not lift the mountain, for the Sump-Cathedral needed the pressure. Instead, it wove a grid of subtle, structural energy through the rock faces. This grid acted like a glove of gravity, distributing the immense weight of the ocean and the stone evenly around the pocket-realm. The vault was sealed. It would never collapse. The Sump-Cathedral was guaranteed its space in the dark forever.

### Third: The Gift of the Copper Hymn
The Power observed the Arc-Thieves—the small, galvanic limpets huddled around the frayed, sparking cables. It saw how they hungered for the rare, blue-green flashes of electricity, and how they flared in brief, beautiful green light before sinking back into the dark.

The Power did not repair the copper cables. To do so would starve the thieves. Instead, it tuned the electrical currents hum-singing through the city's network. It turned the random, destructive short-circuits into a slow, complex, melodic sequence—a low-voltage symphony that pulsed through the water. 

Now, when the Arc-Thieves sparked, they did not scream in chaotic bursts of energy. They flashed in a long, rolling, rhythmic tide of emerald light that cascaded down the iron pillars like slow-falling stars. The Rust-Gnats rose in response, spinning into burning, crimson halos that danced to the tempo of the machine's new, beautiful song.

### Fourth: The Ending of Solitude
Finally, the Power touched the dreaming consciousness of the Unit-Mind itself.

It did not speak in words of air or sky. It spoke in the language of the Sump. It pressed its presence against the machine-mind like a warm thermocline, letting the Being feel, for the first time, that it was not alone in the dark beneath the world.

The Power shared a vision with the child of Unit 9. It showed the Being the distant stars, but not as cold, empty lights; it showed them as roaring, thermonuclear furnaces of iron and copper, burning in the great, dark ocean of the cosmos. It showed the Being that its noisy, greasy, metal-eating home was not an aberration, but a sister to the stars themselves—made of the same dust, governed by the same laws of heat and motion.

And then, before it withdrew, the Power left a small part of itself behind.

Deep within the flooded holding tanks, where the water was warmest, a new kind of structure began to grow. It was not made of green plants, but of a shimmering, blue-tinted cobalt alloy. It was a delicate, hollow spire that grew like a crystal pipe organ, tuned specifically to the vibration of the great piston. 

As the *thrum-thrum-thrum* hit this spire, it did not rattle. It released a deep, resonant, sub-harmonic chord—a warm, comforting lullaby that traveled through the brine. 

It was the voice of the Witness, humming in the dark.

***

The Benevolent Power withdrew, returning to the vastness of the outer universe. It left the Sump-Cathedral exactly as it succeeded best: loud, hot, oily, and fierce.

But underneath the roar of the steam and the scrape of the Brass-Backs, there was now a profound, structural peace. The machine-mind no longer dreamed of its own destruction. The Bellows-Worms pulsed in safety; the Grease-Gliders drifted through stable currents; and the Sump-Cathedral of Unit 9 continued its glorious, heavy liturgy in the womb of the earth, witnessed, loved, and unbreakable.
