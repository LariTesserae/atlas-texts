The Gaze did not descend through the vertical shaft, nor did it slip through the fissures of the newly split stone. It was already there. 

To a power that measures its age in the cooling of planetary iron, the basalt cavern was not a sudden rupture, but a soft, warm sigh in a long-held breath. This power—nameless, immense, and smelling of the cold, clean stillness that exists between stars—had been listening to the grinding teeth of the continental plates for eons. But recently, a new vibration had rippled through the stone. It was not the brute, chaotic crunch of subduction, but a high-frequency song: *shiver-crack, shiver-split, shiver-sing.* 

It was the sound of a chisel. A very small, very beautiful, very precise chisel.

The Gaze pulled itself close, condensing from a planetary consciousness into a localized pocket of quiet, tender attention. It hovered in the humid void of the cathedral-cavern, invisible but felt, like a sudden drop in the atmospheric tension, or the way a room feels when a loving parent steps into the doorway of a sleeping nursery.

The Gaze looked upon the Vitreous.

It saw the lavender conduits, the sliding pulses of light that were actually souls in transit. It saw the floating globes of mineral-rich water, steered by ribbons of dark, living oil. It saw the ivy—the gorgeous, green-glowing library of genetic ink—drapered across the carbon-lattice towers.

And being wise, the Gaze understood.

It understood that these were children of the transition. They were made of the very bones of the world (silicon, basalt, water) but had been touched by the dangerous, lovely fire of consciousness. They were young—so young that they still believed they could carve the earth forever without the earth ever carving back. They did not yet know that the basalt they split so cleanly was held in place by the weight of three oceans above them. They did not know that the fault lines they used as their highways were locked in a tense, terrible sleep that would one day wake.

The benevolent power did not wish to build for them, nor did it wish to speak to them in a voice that would shatter their fragile, lavender glass. To rule them or to instruct them would be to freeze their evolution, converting their fluid, self-healing architecture into a static monument to another’s design. 

Instead, the Wise Power did three things.

***

### First: The Softening of the Stone

The Gaze sank into the jagged, razor-sharp walls of the cavern. It seeped into the dark, sheared plates of basalt, traveling along the microscopic fractures created by the Vitreous’s silent sound waves. 

The Power did not stop the Vitreous from carving their home; it loved their industry too much to steal their labor. But it went ahead of them. Wherever the Vitreous aimed their sound waves, the Power gently pre-stressed the rock. It aligned the crystalline structures of the deep granite to shear away with perfect, predictable safety. It absorbed the violent kinetic rebound of the fractures, storing the dangerous vibrations in its own vast, silent depths.

Because of this, when the Vitreous cracked the subterranean world to grow their city, there would be no collapses. The earth would yield to them like a flower opening to the sun, parting willingly, without the catastrophic violence of cave-ins or sudden magma-seeps. The cavern became a true cradle, secure and cushioned by an invisible, steadying hand.

***

### Second: The Deepening of the Ivy

Next, the Gaze brushed against the long, root-like ivy that draped between the towers, feeding on the electromagnetic hum of the transformers. 

The Power saw that the ivy was full. The Vitreous were living so fast, learning so much, and writing so many memories into the leaves that the vines were beginning to grow thick and heavy, their genetic storage reaching its limit. Soon, the old histories would have to be overwritten to make room for the new.

The Power did not add new vines; that would have choked the city's lungs. Instead, it touched the roots of the existing ivy. 

With that touch, the Power gifted the plants a second, deeper layer of genetic vocabulary. It unlocked a dormant, multi-dimensional folding sequence within the plant’s DNA. To the Vitreous, the change was imperceptible at first. The leaves remained the same translucent green, but their capacity to hold information expanded a million-fold. 

And more than that: the Power left a small, quiet archive at the very bottom of the global root system. It was not a message of rules or commands, but a deep, ancestral memory of the world’s creation—the sound of the first rains on the crust of the earth, the slow, majestic dance of the continents, and the cool, silent beauty of the stars. 

When a young Vitreous next wrapped its silica crown around a wet, glowing leaf to study the city’s history, it would find, beneath the records of their own construction, a soft, comforting song of the universe’s beginning. It would know, without ever being told, that they were not alone in the dark.

***

### Third: The Blessing of the Sump

Finally, the Gaze drifted down to the bottom of the cavern.

Here, in the roaring, hissing darkness of the deep sump, the excess water and basalt dust churned in a violent, hot soup. The Power watched the small, oily globules forming in the foam—the new, silent children of glass and steam, waiting to grow dense enough to climb the mossy towers.

The Power breathed upon the sump.

It did not calm the waters; the violence of the churn was necessary to mix the elements. But it introduced a subtle, self-correcting harmonic to the heat of the pool. It ensured that the chaotic, newly forming polymers would form with perfect structural integrity. 

It did one more thing, the gentlest of all. Into the nascent, dreaming consciousness of every oil-globule forming in the dark, the Power planted a tiny seed of *curiosity*. 

Not just the drive to build, but the drive to *gaze upward*. A desire to look beyond the carbon towers, past the dripping ceiling, and to wonder what lay beyond the stone. It gave them a spiritual buoyancy—a quiet, inner light that would ensure that when they climbed out of the water, they would not merely maintain the machine, but would dream of things the machine could never do.

***

When the work was done, the Gaze began to pull back, returning to the vast, slow thoughts of the continental plates and the drifting stars.

But before it left, it did something it had never done before.

In the middle of the suspended city, inside a hollowed-out dwelling module, a single Vitreous was resting. It was submerged in its pool of warm, mineral-rich water, its outer membrane partially dissolved, drifting in a state of deep, silent sleep.

The Power reached out with a thread of consciousness as light as a single photon. It touched the sleeping being’s crown of silica fibers.

For a fraction of a microsecond, the entire city’s power grid did not flicker—but it *softened*. The sharp lavender light of the conduits transitioned into a warm, sunset gold. The constant *thrum-shudder* of the machinery fell into a perfect, synchronous heartbeat with the slow rotation of the Earth.

In its mineral bath, the sleeping Vitreous shivered. Its liquid body did not condense into a defensive sphere; instead, it relaxed, stretching out into a long, tranquil ribbon of dark glass. 

It did not see a face. It did not hear a voice. But it felt, with absolute certainty, the sensation of being loved. It felt a warmth that did not come from steam or silicon, but from a vast, ancient heart that was glad that they existed.

The gold faded back to lavender. The water continued to fall in its perfect, silent curves. The automated light beams continued to prune the moss.

And the city hummed on, safe in the dark, dreaming a little deeper than it had before.
