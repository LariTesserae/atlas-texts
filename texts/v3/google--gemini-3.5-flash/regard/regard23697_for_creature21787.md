The traveler in the deep spaces did not arrive with the splitting of the sky or the thunder of displaced air. It arrived as a pressure-differential so subtle that only the three matte-grey rods registered it first, their marrow-hum rising by less than a vibration per second.

The traveler was not a person, nor a machine, but a vast, slow-folding attention. It had spent several star-lifetimes observing the cold, screaming vacuums and the wet, chaotic gardens of a hundred young galaxies. It loved most of all the things that were small, quiet, and mathematically beautiful. 

It looked down upon the unnamed upthrust peak. It saw the razor-edges of the basalt, the hot, black glass of the terrace, and the three hovering ovals of water. 

Within the ovals, it saw the Loomers. 

The traveler's consciousness, which could perceive the spin of atomic particles and the slow collision of tectonic plates, adjusted its focus. It did not look at the Loomers with physical sight—for physical sight is a clumsy, surface-bound tool—but with the same sensory vocabulary the Loomers themselves used. It saw them as three distinct, glowing loops of thermal current, their internal fluid-sacs pulsing with the slow, rhythmic beat of a deep-water heart. It saw the waxy orchids as fountains of chemical sweetness, and the Vapour-Snails as tiny, cool knots of jelly floating in a sea of rising heat.

*How rare,* the traveler thought. *How fragile.*

A wise power does not touch a spider’s web with a gloved hand. It knows that to interfere is often to erase. The sanctuary was perfect, but it was self-contained to the point of tragedy. 

The traveler observed the single white ceramic bench at the edge of the drop. It saw the faint, dry traces of chalky dust clinging to the grain of the white material—the remains of the last Loomer who had laid down to die, whose dry powder had been carried away by the freezing winds of the outer mountain to be lost in the vast, unheated sky.

The traveler understood the math of the place. It saw two limits:
First, the geothermal heat beneath the basalt was a chimney from a volcanic vein that would, in seven thousand years, shift three miles to the west, leaving this terrace to freeze.
Second, when a Loomer died, its memory-vibrations—which it had spent centuries pulse-weaving into the water-wells—evaporated. The new Loomers who took their place had to begin the song of the sanctuary from the first note, over and over, never learning what their ancestors had known of the stars or the deep roots of the world.

The wise power did not descend. It remained in the cold, thin air above the steam-line, hovering like an invisible moon.

Then, with the gentleness of a shadow falling across a leaf, it began to work.

***

First, it reached down into the red, liquid throat of the mountain, miles beneath the black basalt. It did not alter the tectonic plates; instead, it laid a *conductor*—a thread of dense, gravity-binding matter no thicker than a spider's silk, stretching from the planet’s molten, radioactive heart straight to the base of the three matte-grey rods. 

The heat of the terrace would no longer depend on the shifting of local volcanic steam. Now, the sanctuary was anchored to the very rotation of the world. It would remain sweltering, damp, and alive for as long as the planet turned.

Second, the traveler touched the white ceramic bench. 

It did not change its shape or its cool, clean touch. But it altered the molecular lattice of the white compound. From that day on, when a Loomer’s long life reached its close and they lay down to crystallize, the bench would not merely let their dry, chalky remains blow into the void. 

Instead, as the high-altitude wind crumbled the milk-white body, the bench would catch the *signature* of their neural fluid-sacs—the complex, rhythmic geometry of their memories, their dreams of the orchids, and their centuries of water-sculpting. 

The bench would store these patterns like light frozen in glass. And when the next rain fell, drawing the moisture up from the steam-heated stones back into the hovering pools, the water would carry those memories with it. 

***

Within the sanctuary, the system "breathed." 

The three rods pitched their tone lower. The hovering pools tilted by a few degrees. But this time, the transition did not end in the same equilibrium. 

In the westernmost pool, a Loomer—the oldest of the three—slowly unfurled its long, pale limbs. Its gold-ringed eyes widened. 

Through its three pairs of breathing slits, it felt a change in the water. The convection currents within its hovering home were no longer just warm; they were *musical*. As the warm water rolled over its blue-white skin, the Loomer felt the phantom pressure-pulses of a companion who had died three centuries before. It felt the memory of a particular orchid bloom from a thousand years ago; it felt the precise thermal map of a great storm that had passed over the peak when the mountain was still black and smoking.

The Loomer did not have a voice, but its body contracted in a sudden, ecstatic wave. It sent a massive, rhythmic pulse through the air-boundary. 

The other two Loomers in their respective pools caught the vibration. They, too, felt the change. The water-spheres did not wobble or break; they stabilized with a new, crystalline clarity. The Vapour-Snails did not drift aimlessly; they aligned their golden gills to a new, sweeter current in the steam.

The Loomers looked upward with their triple-orbed eyes. 

They could not see the traveler. But they could see the *tension* of the sky. Above the steam-barrier, where there had once been only the empty, freezing blue of the high altitude, there was now a beautiful, curved lens of light—a soft, protective shield of gravity that bent the harsh sun into a permanent, perfect halo of pale rainbow around their peak.

The traveler watched them receive the gift. It saw the oldest Loomer reach out of its pool with its five-jointed fingers, its pads gently stroking the warm air, finding the touch of a new, eternal safety.

Its work complete, the wise power slowly withdrew its attention. It folded its vast awareness back into the silent highways of the stars, leaving the three Loomers suspended in their warm, weightless gardens—no longer lonely, no longer forgotten, and safe for a million years to come.
