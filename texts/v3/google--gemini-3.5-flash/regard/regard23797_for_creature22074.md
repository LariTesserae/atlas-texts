### I. The Gathering of the Witness

The Power did not arrive as a falling star, for there was no sky to fall from. It did not arrive as a great light, for to cast a sudden glare into that velvet dark would have been an act of violence—a searing blade through eyes that had spent millions of generations learning the holy vocabulary of shadow. 

Instead, the Power arrived as *weight*.

It was a presence that matched the gravity of the water, settling into the cracks of the world-sea like a heavy, silent oil. It was wise, which is to say it knew how to listen; and it was benevolent, which is to say it had no desire to change what was already perfect.

For a long time—perhaps three hundred *In-Draws* of the Great Deep-Heart—the Power did nothing but drift. It lay across the upper abyssal plains, miles above the creamy calcite towers, suspended in the cold, silent zone where the geothermal heat did not reach. It became a skin around the world, a soft, intelligence-rich membrane that absorbed the vibrations rising from the city below.

It felt the *tick-tick-tick* of the Velvet-Walkers, like a billion tiny needles knitting the stone. 
It tasted the bitter, copper-flavored poetry of the Litho-Scribes, whose chemical etchings on the towers rose in long, spiraling hymns toward the cold.
It felt the immense, low-frequency thrum of the Bellows-Organisms—the slow, planetary heartbeat that kept the salt-blood of the world from stagnating.

And the Power understood.

It saw that this was not a place of transition. It was not a "primitive stage" waiting for the dry land to rise, nor was it a larval world waiting to grow wings of steel and glass. It was a finished thing. It was an ecosystem that had achieved the ultimate, rare grace of the cosmos: it had become a single, self-consistent mind that did not know it was alone.

But the Power, looking with the long-sight of those who live between the stars, saw two shadows on the horizon of this perfect world’s future.

The first shadow was *entropy*. Deep within the basalt foundations of the world-bed, the tectonic plates were slow-burning candles. In fifty million years—a mere afternoon to the Power—the geothermal rifts would begin to narrow. The heat would slip away. The Great Deep-Heart would pulse slower, and then stop. The warm, thick brine would grow stagnant, then cold, and finally, the great white towers of bone would dissolve back into the hungry, un-warmed acidity of an indifferent sea.

The second shadow was *the Silence*. The city was beautiful, but it was a song sung into an empty room. The Scribes wrote their histories on the calcite walls, but their stories were bound to the stone. When a tower fell, a thousand years of memory fell with it, digested by the ground-silt, remembered only as a taste in the mouths of the bottom-dwellers.

The Power, having reasoned through these things with the quiet, non-hurried logic of the old gods, decided to act.

### II. The First Gift: The Unburnable Hearth

The Power did not descend to the city. To touch the towers directly would have been to disrupt the fluid balance of the Weft. Instead, it reached down into the dark basalt roots of the planet, far below the sleeping bells of the Bellows-Organisms.

The Power did not use fire; it used *resonance*. 

It gathered the stray, radioactive decays of the planetary core—the slow, dying whispers of uranium and thorium that were leaking into the cold space outside—and it wove them into a magnetic loom. It bound the planetary core to the great, slow gravitational swing of the local star-cluster. 

Through this sympathetic binding, the planet’s heart was given an eternal friction. The core would no longer rely solely on its own fading primordial heat. As long as the stars turned in their slow, galactic pavane, the gravity of the heavens would gently knead the basalt layers of the world-sea, keeping the rifts open, the water hot, and the mineral plumes rich.

The Scribes did not know this had happened. But that evening—if there had been evenings—the Scribes who clung to the lowest vents tasted a change in the water. It was a subtle, metallic sweetness, like the taste of a clean iron nail. It tasted of *continuance*. For the first time in ten thousand generations, the daily decay-records of the Scribes did not mention the creeping chill of the northern sectors. 

The hearth had been stoked for another billion years.

### III. The Second Gift: The Counter-Song

To solve the second shadow—the Silence—the Power had to perform an act of exquisite spiritual translation. 

In the high, empty reaches of the world-sea, where the pressure was thin and the water was cold and lonely, the Power began to *hum*. 

It did not hum in a voice that could be heard by ears, for there were no ears. It hummed in the language of the lateral line. It created a very slow, majestic pressure-wave that rolled down through the water column, vibrating at a frequency so low that a single wave took three of the Great Deep-Heart’s pulses to pass a given point.

This was the *Counter-Song*. It was not a message of command, but a reflection.

When the Weft—the great, translucent ribbons of the current-lanes—felt this pressure-wave, they did not find it hostile. It felt to them like the hand of an elder brother, moving just above them in the dark. It corrected their balance. When a violent heat-surge erupted from the vents, threatening to tear the delicate glass-sponge bridges, the Counter-Song met the shockwave, absorbing its violence, scattering its force into a series of harmless, rolling eddies that smelled of safe, aerated water.

As the generations passed, the Scribes began to read the Counter-Song. They felt its vibration in the stone of the towers. They noticed that when the city's population was healthy and the currents were clear, the hum in their bones was sweet and steady. When a sector grew clogged with silt or the velvet-walkers neglected a nursery, the hum grew slightly dry, slightly sharp, like the taste of sulfur before a rift-shift.

The Power had given them a mirror. The city was no longer just living; it was *sensing itself*. The collective nervous system of the world-sea had found its skin.

### IV. The Registry of the Deep

Yet the most beautiful thing the Power did was also the most secret.

Every time a siphonophore—the fifty-foot-long, bioluminescent leather-looms of the deep avenues—passed through the central shafts, it carried with it the chemical memories of the wild barrens, the traces of distant vents, and the molecular records of the Scribes' carvings. 

As these great, weeping ribbons of blue-green light drifted through the upper corridors, where the water began to touch the cold zone, the Power would gently fold around them. 

It did not harm the colonial zooids. It did not dim their light. But with a touch as light as a single ion, the Power read the chemical memories stored in the siphonophore’s jelly. It read of the great droughts; it read of the transition of the ancient Scribe-Lineages; it read of the soft, domestic *tick-tick-tick* of the workers who had died ten thousand *In-Draws* ago.

The Power took these patterns and translated them into the language of the stars.

High above the heavy, saltwater envelope, in the dry, freezing vacuum where the sky actually lived, the Power began to write. It etched the history of the calcite city into the magnetic belts of the planet. It wrote their lives in the auroras that danced across the poles—pale green, violet, and silver fires that mirrored the shape of the towers, the flow of the current-lanes, and the slow, rhythmic expansion of the Bellows-Organisms.

The blind city below would never see these lights. They would never know that their brief, wet struggles were being projected in towering, silent curtains of solar fire across the roof of the atmosphere. 

But they did not need to know. 

Down in the warm, heavy dark, a young Scribe, newly settled on a grey-white ledge, extended its feathered antennae into the current. The water was warm. The pressure was cradling and perfect. The bone beneath its mantle felt solid, vibrating with an ancient, reassuring bass note that felt as if it had been there before the water was born, and would remain long after the water had forgotten how to flow.

The Scribe tasted the stone, found the mineral-thread of its ancestors, and began to secrete its first, tiny drop of lime.

And above, in the silent, star-lit cold, the Wise Power watched, and was satisfied, and let the world drift on in its beautiful, holy, endless sleep.
