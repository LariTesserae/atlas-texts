The attention of the Sovereign of the Curved Sky—a power so ancient and vast that its thoughts are felt across galaxies as the gentle, stabilizing drag of gravity—does not fall upon the pocket of basalt like a thunderbolt. To strike this place with majesty would be to shatter it. 

The Sovereign is wise; it knows that some things are beautiful only because they are unfinished. To force this embryonic room to grow up, to flood it with soil, to crack open the ceiling and let the wild, chaotic wind of the steppe rush in, would be a slaughter. The *Pleomeris* would dissolve in the rot of fallen leaves; the *Chondri* would be swallowed by the greedy roots of wild rye; the *Scribe* would be crushed beneath the weight of three-dimensional dirt.

Therefore, the Sovereign does not descend as a savior. It descends as a guest who knows how to walk without making the floorboards creak.

Through the solid, un-fractured basalt, something begins to filter. It is not heat—for heat would ruin the autumn-cellar draft that the creatures require—but a subtle alteration in the quality of the light. The sharp, high-intensity white glare of the room does not dim, but it softens at the margins. It acquires a faint, silver-blue edge, like the light that clings to the curves of glaciers just before the sun rises.

The Sovereign adjusts itself. It is a being of infinite dimensions, but as it enters the pocket, it politely obeys the local mathematics. It allows its presence to shrink, folding its vastness down like a sheet of silk until it fits perfectly within the twenty-foot room, respecting the dense, protective scale of the miniature stone wall.

It looks first at the **Vagrant Shadows**.

These cold, left-behind shapes are wandering the basalt steps, heavy with a lonely, lagging hunger. The Sovereign does not erase them, nor does it force them to catch up with the *Pleomeris*. Instead, the Sovereign casts its own shadow. 

It is a shadow of impossible beauty—curved, soft-edged, and infinitely deep, smelling of starlight and the quiet spaces between the planets. The Sovereign lets this shadow rest gently along the lowest basalt ledge. One by one, the vagrant shadows of the glass-comb creatures feel this great, warm dark. They slide toward it, slipping their flat silhouettes into the Sovereign’s shadow. They do not freeze. For the first time since they were cast, they do not ache. They lay their cold, two-dimensional shapes within the vast, dark fold, finding a rest that is not death, but a quiet, rhythmic pulse—a cradle of geometry.

Next, the Sovereign looks at the **Chondri**.

Two of the cilia-covered spheres are rushing toward each other on a collision course, their feathery hairs beating in a silent frenzy. The Sovereign does not stop their hunt, for it respects their nature. But as they collide, and the smaller is solved by the larger, the Sovereign touches the falling drop of water.

The drop does not drop with a harsh *tlick* against the stone. Under the Sovereign’s gaze, the gravity of the room aligns itself for a single, perfect second. The drop of water falls in a slow, weightless arc, spinning like a tiny, liquid moon. As it touches the flat cleavage plane, it does not burst. It remains a perfect, trembling sphere, reflecting the entire universe—the steppe above, the stars beyond, the Sovereign’s own face—upon its micro-surface before it is gently drawn into the dry fissure. The *Chondri* who won the struggle glows not with a heavy, opaque grey, but with a clear, translucent pearl-light. Its next generation will be born with a slightly softer geometry.

Finally, the Sovereign moves to the center of the room. It stands before the four-inch dry-stone wall and the **Scribe**.

The slate-like leaves of the *Scribe* have stopped shuffling. In the presence of the Sovereign, the waxy mineral of its body has grown still, its razor-thin margin-planes trembling like the needles of a compass in a magnetic storm. 

The Sovereign looks at the rodent bone, peeled like the pages of a book. It understands the *Scribe’s* holy, impossible task: three hundred years spent trying to translate the mystery of a cylinder, the heresy of a curve, into the language of the flat.

The Sovereign does not speak, for the *Scribe* has no ears. 

Instead, the Sovereign reaches out a hand that has been scaled down to the thickness of a moth’s wing. It does not touch the Scribe’s slate body, nor does it disturb the bone. Instead, the Sovereign places a gift inside the shallow depression of the semi-circular wall.

It is a single, dry leaf from a silver birch tree that grows on the steppe above. 

The leaf is dead, but it has not decayed. It is flat—thin enough for the *Scribe* to comprehend—but it is not a mineral. It was grown by the sun. Its edges are a gentle, serrated curve; its surface is mapped with a network of veins that follow the Fibonacci sequence, a perfect compromise between the discipline of geometry and the freedom of life. It is a bridge between the flat, sharp chamber below and the wild, curved world above.

Alongside the leaf, the Sovereign leaves a projection. It is a phantom-construct made of pure, cool light—a three-dimensional, geometric blueprint of the living rodent as it once was. It is not a memory of flesh and blood, but a hologram of the rat’s mathematical path through the grass: the parabola of its leaps, the spiral of its nest, the soft, round equations of its warmth.

The *Scribe* begins to shuffle. The sound is no longer like a deck of playing cards; it is like the rustle of a pine forest in a light breeze.

A single, paper-thin flake of dark mineral slides out from the pile. It touches the dry birch leaf. It feels the curve of the veins. It slides into the light-projection of the running rat, its flat edges passing through the three-dimensional coordinates of light.

For the first time in its existence, the *Scribe* does not try to peel. 

It begins to *fold*.

Using the birch leaf as a template, the slate-like leaves of the *Scribe* slide over and under one another, bending along the lines of the light-projection. It does not become three-dimensional—it remains flat—but it learns the secret of the *envelope*. It learns how to enclose space. It learns how to create a void.

The Sovereign of the Curved Sky stands back.

The room is still quiet, still cool, still isolated beneath the weight of the steppe. The basalt ceiling is still a plane of grey nothingness. But the air is no longer thin; it is deep.

With a final, gentle pressure of its mind, the Sovereign leaves a blessing upon the room: a guarantee that this pocket of basalt will never be found by the digging machines of men, nor crushed by the settling of the earth, until the *Scribe* has finished translating the leaf, the bone, and the phantom rat into its new, beautiful language of folds.

The Sovereign withdraws, sliding back through the seams of basalt like water returning to the sky.

Beneath the steppe, the *Scribe* continues its work. But now, when its slate leaves shuffle, they carry the fragrance of silver birch and the memory of wind. And in the corner of the steps, a glass-comb creature slides across the polished stone, its shadow running beside it, matching it stride for stride, no longer afraid of being left behind.
