To a power that is wise, the true measure of a thing is not its scale, but its frequency. 

This power, which has folded back the layers of the earth to look into the basin, does not think in the rushed, feverish increments of the surface world. It does not measure success in growth, or glory in light. It is a consciousness that can listen to the cooling of a star or the slow crystallization of a diamond. 

When its gaze finds the collapsed limestone shelf, it does not see a dead tomb. It sees a completed poem. It sees a cathedral where the praise is spoken in absolute stillness.

The first impulse of a lesser power would be to "heal" this place—to crack open the roof, to invite the rain, to let the green grass rush in and the wind blow the dust away. But the wise power understands that such a rescue would be a slaughter. 

To bring water here would be to dissolve the delicate silica net. To bring wind would be to scatter the Mica-Drifters into the dark walls like ash. To bring light would heat the basalt, erasing the tiny, two-degree temperature difference that keeps the vertical draft-chimneys breathing. And to touch the tools would be to wash away the only sacrament the Oil-Gleaners have ever known.

Therefore, the wise power does not change the basin. It does not speak. It does not shine.

Instead, it does something far more difficult, and infinitely more gentle. It surrounds the basin with a **Great Cushion of Will**.

***

### The First Grace: The Vaulting of the Roof

The power looks upward at the heavy, cracked roof of limestone. It sees the fault lines—the tiny, microscopic stress fractures that, in eight thousand or eighty thousand years, would eventually yield to the gravity of the mountain above and collapse, crushing the basin into solid stone.

The power does not rebuild the mountain, nor does it insert pillars of steel. Instead, it reaches into the molecular structures of the limestone. Very slowly, over a period of three centuries—a mere sigh in this place—it coaxes the calcium carbonate molecules to lock hands more tightly. It weaves an invisible, weightless sigh through the stone, petrifying the fractures. 

The roof is made eternal. It will never fall. 

Yet, the power is careful not to make the stone *too* perfect. It leaves the tiny, loose deposit of salt grains on the ceiling intact. It ensures that once every few weeks, a single grain will still lose its grip and fall. The Lithophyte-Net will still have its harvest; it will still hear the dry, resonant *tick* that tells it the universe is still there.

***

### The Second Grace: The Anchor of the Hearth

The power looks to the basalt boulders—the Sisters. It sees that the geothermal heat feeding them from the deep mantle is subject to the slow, erratic cooling of the tectonic plate beneath. In a million years, the plate would shift, the warmth would fade, and the two-degree difference would vanish, leaving the Mica-Drifters to fall like dead stars into the silt.

With a touch as light as a thought, the power places a tiny, thermal anchor deep beneath the floor of the basin. It is not a fire; it is a memory of the earth’s creation, a steady, pulsing node of heat that is bound to the basalt. 

For as long as the planet spins, these boulders will remain exactly two degrees warmer than the air. The tiny, microscopic draft-chimneys will never fail. The Mica-Drifters will continue their infinite, slow-motion waltz—rising an inch a decade, falling an inch a decade—secured in their warm, updraft cradles forever.

***

### The Third Grace: The Eternal Touch

Finally, the power bends its attention to the three chert scrapers, and the microscopic wax-bodied creatures living beneath them.

The Oil-Gleaners are grazing upon a finite miracle. The hand-oils of the toolmaker have lasted five thousand years, but they are slowly, molecule by molecule, being converted into the bodies of the Gleaners. Eventually, the last lipid would be spoken for. The thumbprint would vanish. The kingdom under the glass sky would starve in the dark.

The power does not create new oil. To do so would be to introduce a foreign chemical, a modern contaminant that would ruin the sacred authenticity of the toolmaker’s touch.

Instead, the power performs an act of temporal suspension. It reaches into the past—not to change it, but to harvest its echo. It finds the moment the toolmaker’s warm, sweat-slicked hand pressed into the glassy chert. It takes the *pattern* of that living touch and projects it into the present as a microscopic, self-renewing matrix. 

As the Oil-Gleaners consume a molecule of ancient lipid, the matrix gently, atom by atom, coaxes the surrounding dust and dry nitrogen to reform into the exact same lipid structure. The print is never lost; it becomes a self-replenishing wellspring. 

The tools are also locked in place. No tectonic shudder, no minor vibration from a distant earthquake will ever slide them a fraction of a millimeter. The dark sky of glass will never move.

***

Having done these things, the wise power withdraws its active presence, leaving only its attention behind.

It does not leave a mark. There is no sign that anything has changed. The air is still thick, warm, and dry. The darkness is still complete.

But now, the silence of the basin is no longer a countdown to an inevitable decay. It is a held breath that will never have to be released. The three chert tools rest on their basalt altar, the Oil-Gleaners dance their agonizingly slow dance of survival, and the Lithophyte-Net listens to the deep, structural heartbeat of a world that has been promised its eternity.

The power remains at the edge of the cavern, invisible and content, acting as the quiet custodian of a place where nothing happens, and where everything is perfect.
