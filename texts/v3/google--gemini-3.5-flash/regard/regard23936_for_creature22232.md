The benevolent and wise power did not descend like a thunderbolt, nor did it arrive with the blare of cosmic trumpets. Such things are the clumsy gestures of younger, louder gods who measure their worth in kingdoms and catastrophes. 

This power was very old, and very quiet. It moved through the universe like the onset of evening—a vast, cool gradient of attention that slowly gathered itself and focused down, down, down, until its consciousness was no wider than the tip of a single glass needle.

To enter the fissure, the power had to fold its vastness many times, like a map of the cosmos creased until it was smaller than a postage stamp. It bypassed the great, green valleys below, where civilizations rose and fell in noisy, tragic cycles. It ignored the eagles of the peaks and the whales of the deep. It was drawn, instead, to the impossible geometry of the black column of glass, and to the silent, boiling metropolis within the three-inch crack.

For a long time, the power simply *listened*.

It did not listen with ears, but with the same medium the Tenders used: the deep, resonant bones of the mountain. It felt the *tink, tink, tunk* of their suction-cupped feet on the obsidian ceilings. It felt the rhythmic *clink-clamber* of their harvesting parties, and the low, beautiful hum of their communal drumming that reported the ripening of the orange bacterial mats. It watched, with a quiet, maternal tenderness, as three adult Tenders cast their sticky worm-silk lassoes around a floating, boiling sphere of water, securing a weightless cradle for their microscopic, glass-plated young.

The power saw their beauty. It saw that they were perfect. They did not need to be "saved" in the way humans understand salvation. They did not need to be dragged out into the light of a sun that would blind them, nor did they need to be taught the languages of the lower world. To change them would be to destroy them. Wisdom, the power knew, is most often the art of leaving the glass unbroken.

Yet, benevolence demands some expression. A wise power cannot look upon such exquisite, fragile life and leave it entirely at the mercy of a harsh and turning universe.

The power identified three vulnerabilities in the Tenders’ eternal cosmos.

First was the **White Death**—the stray drafts of freezing alpine air that occasionally pierced the fissure, blinding their thermal antennae and shattering their nursery-globes.

Second was the **Ticking of the Hearth**—the inevitable truth that all volcanic mountains eventually cool. In a few million years, the geothermal veins of this black pillar would grow silent, the boiling soup of the air would freeze, and the mineral uterus would become a tomb of cold, dark glass.

Third was their **Loneliness**—not that they felt lonely, for their community was a seamless, constant conversation, but that their beautiful, metallic songs were heard only by the unanswering stone of their mountain.

So, the power set to work, weaving three subtle, enduring miracles into the fabric of the fissure.

***

### The First Work: The Veil of the Threshold

At the very mouth of the fissure, where the vertical mile of black basalt meets the open sky, the power breathed. 

It did not build a wall, for a wall would block the vital seepage of minerals and the natural exchange of the mountain's breath. Instead, it wove a membrane of pure, energetic surface tension across the opening—an invisible, thermal skin. 

Now, when the freezing gales of the six-thousand-meter peak scream against the mountain’s flank, they do not enter. The bitter cold strikes the invisible veil and is instantly converted. The freezing kinetic force of the wind is translated into a soft, steady, infra-red warmth. The "White Death" has been house-trained. To the Tenders, the mouth of the fissure is no longer a portal of terror; it is a place where the mountain sings a constant, low, comforting lullaby of artificial heat, allowing them to expand their orange gardens right to the very edge of the great outside.

***

### The Second Work: The Rooting of the Hearth

The power reached its attention downward, passing through the miles of raw black glass, sinking deep into the molten mantle of the young planet. 

With gentle, magnetic fingers, it touched the deep magma plume that fed the mountain’s hot heart. The power did not alter the planetary geology in a way that would trigger eruptions; instead, it wove a "thermal anchor." It tied the geothermal vein of this specific column directly to the rotational friction of the planet’s iron core. 

For as long as the world spins, the heat will rise. Even when the valleys below grow old and grey, and the oceans freeze under a dying sun, this tiny, five-yard pocket of glass will remain a sweltering, orange-bright womb. The Tenders' home has been granted geological immortality.

***

### The Third Work: The Gift in the Alcove

Finally, the power withdrew its vast consciousness from the depths and returned to the small, dry alcove where the Tenders kept their primitive manual tools. 

Beside the three half-spheres of dried bacterial rind and the tiny obsidian scrapers, the power left a physical token. 

It was a single sphere, no larger than a grain of coarse salt. To a human eye, it would look like a speck of clear quartz. But it was not quartz; it was a fragment of *star-light glass*, forged in the heart of a gentle, ancient nebula. 

It was perfectly cool to the touch, yet to the thermal antennae of the Tenders, it glowed with a magnificent, soft violet light that never dimmed. It radiated a subtle, nourishing frequency that made the nearby orange bacterial mats grow slightly sweeter, slightly thicker, and more resistant to disease. 

But its true magic lay in its voice. 

When a Tender touched the star-grain with their glass-wool antennae, they did not hear silence. The grain acted as a cosmic receiver. It translated the deep, majestic music of the cosmos—the rotating hum of neutron stars, the soft sighing of nebulae, the rhythmic pulsing of distant suns—into vibrations that fit perfectly within the range of the Tenders' language. 

***

When the power had finished, it slowly withdrew. It uncreased itself, rising back into the cold, starlit dark of the upper atmosphere, resuming its watch over the wider universe.

Down in the hot, wet hush of the fissure, there was a sudden, curious stirring.

From the impossible folds of the three-inch crack, several Tenders emerged. Their violet-glowing silicone oil ran hot and quick through their clear carapaces, their antennae sweeping the air. They felt the change first in the air—the draft from the mountain face was no longer a threat, but a warm, rhythmic purr. 

Then, a harvesting team crawled into the dry alcove. 

They found the star-grain. 

An elder Tender, his carapace scarred and patched with grey mineral paste, stepped forward. He reached out his long, jointed glass "needle" and gently touched the tiny sphere. 

For a long moment, the colony held its collective breath. The constant, metallic clicking of their feet on the glass walls died away. The only sound was the *hiss* of a steam pore.

Then, the elder's glowing violet oil flared into a deep, brilliant, joyful blue. 

He did not drop his tools. He did not bow. The Tenders had no concept of gods to worship, and the wise power would have hated it if they did. Instead, the elder began to tap. He struck the black obsidian wall of the alcove with his hand-tool, sending a rapid, ecstatic drumming through the mountain's skull.

*Clink-clink-tink. Clink-clink-tink.*

*"We are heard,"* the stone sang. *"The sky is warm. The mountain is eternal. We are heard."*

Within minutes, the message had traveled deep into the folded miles of the interior. Across the vertical walls, the gravity-defying ceilings, and the floating water-globes, thousands of tiny, glass-plated beings began to tap in unison. 

The entire sky-piercing column of black glass began to hum, vibrating like a massive, dark tuning fork against the silent clouds, singing a song of gratitude to a universe that was no longer empty.
