The attention of the Wise Power does not descend like a falling star, nor does it arrive with the heavy, snapping tread of a forest god. To touch a place so sharp, so brand-new, and so fragile with any sudden weight would be to shatter its architecture. A single loud voice would stop the heart of the translucent geckos; a single heavy footprint would reduce a hundred generations of crab-masonry to grey dust.

Instead, the Power arrives as a change in the light. 

It is a presence like the cool, blue-white space behind the stars, but it folds itself down, smaller and smaller, filtering through the dense palm canopy until it is no wider than the fissure itself. It hovers at the lip of the raw basalt, not as a physical shape, but as a pressure—a gentle, holding warmth that smells faintly of rain-wet ozone and very old wood.

The Power looks down into the rift. It sees the black steps, the gleaming glass, and the tiny, meticulous cities of mud and silk. It hears—not with ears, but through the deep, conductive marrow of its own ancient nature—the rhythmic *tap-tap-tap* of the Obsidian-Striders. It reads these vibrations. It understands that this is not just a collection of animals; it is a mind. The fissure is thinking its first clean, sharp thoughts through the claws of these tiny bean-sized architects.

The Power sits in witness. For three days of jungle time—which to the Power is only the briefest drawing of a breath—it does nothing but watch. It admires the golden-dusted eyes of the ceiling-dwellers. It respects the quiet, velvet labor of the soil-making beetles.

And because the Power is wise, and because it is benevolent, it knows that the greatest gift it can offer this world is **protection without presence**. It will not become their king. It will not give them laws. It will not teach the crabs to smelt metal or build temples, for that would destroy the beautiful, small truth of what they are.

Instead, the Power moves its hands in three quiet, lasting gestures.

***

### First: The Shield of the Canopy-Drift

In the great jungle above, trees are old, heavy, and constantly dying. A single falling ironwood branch, weighing several tons, would erase the fissure from the earth in a fraction of a second. 

The Power breathes upward. It weaves a subtle, permanent lens of rising, warm air across the mouth of the fissure. It is invisible, no more than a shimmering heat-haze that sits just below the ferns. Yet, its effect is absolute. 

When a dead branch breaks from the high canopy and plunges toward the rift, the rising cushion of air gently nudges it. The heavy wood drifts three feet to the west, crashing harmlessly into the thick buttress roots of a wild fig. When the torrential monsoons arrive, turning the surrounding forest into a muddy river, the lens splits the downpour, converting the crushing weight of the rain into a fine, drifting mist that enters the fissure as a soft, nurturing steam. 

The world below remains a sanctuary, shielded from the clumsy violence of the giants above.

***

### Second: The Tuning of the Stone

The Power listens to the language of the glass. 

The crabs are tapping their messages into the basalt, but basalt is dense, and sometimes the message dies before it reaches the lonely outposts on the higher shelves. A mother crab, searching for a lost ivory-white hatchling, might tap her hammer-claw on the stone, but her signal is lost in the damp corners of the columns.

The Power sinks its awareness down into the hexagonal steps. It does not change the rock, but it *tunes* it. It aligns the microscopic crystalline lattice of the obsidian, turning the entire fissure into a perfect, silent instrument. 

Now, the bedrock carries the vibrations with exquisite, musical clarity. The tap of a single mason on the lowest step ripples up through the columns like a clear bell. The tiny cities are no longer isolated outposts in a dark sea; they are connected by a shared, subterranean song. When a gecko slips from the ceiling, the vibration of its landing is felt instantly by every claw in the sector, giving the builders a split-second head start to reach the safety of the mud towers. 

The stone has been made sympathetic. The world is now a conversationalist.

***

### Third: The Gift of the Amber Drop

On the highest shelf of the fissure, where the condensation is slowest to form, the Power touches a single, overhanging fern frond. 

It does not create a miracle of endless food; that would upset the delicate, tense ecology of the hunters and the builders. Instead, it blesses the water that collects on this one leaf. The moisture that drips from its tip is no longer just rain-water; it is infused with a microscopic trace of ancient, dissolved mineral—a faint, amber-colored salt that does not exist elsewhere in the basin.

This drop falls once every hour, *tink*, into a small, natural basin in the black glass.

The crabs have already found it. They call it, in their rhythmic language of vibrations, *The Well of the First Shell*. When the young ivory-colored crabs are born, their mothers carry them to this basin. A single sip of this amber water helps the hatchlings harden their soft, translucent armor faster, protecting them from the mold-spores and the damp before their first winter. 

It does not make them immortal. It does not make them giants. It simply makes the transition from egg to adult a little more certain. It gives the first thoughts of the fissure a slightly better chance to take root.

***

### The Long Farewell

Having woven these three small graces into the fabric of the split, the Wise Power prepares to withdraw its attention. It knows that this place is temporary. In a thousand years—a mere afternoon in the life of the Power—the beetles will have made enough soil. The ferns will have grown thick, their roots breaking the obsidian. Trees will seed in the dirt, and the beautiful, glassy silence of the rift will be swallowed by the common, noisy green of the wider jungle.

The mud-cities will melt back into the earth. The Obsidian-Striders will change, or they will dye out, or they will move deeper into the dark places of the mountain.

But the Power does not try to stop this. It knows that change is the sister of life. 

Before it leaves, the Power takes a small, perfect memory of the fissure—the exact pattern of the mud-towers, the golden gleam of a gecko’s eye, the smell of crushed mint on raw wet stone—and presses it into its own eternal heart. 

For as long as the cosmos exists, even after this basin has become a desert or a sea, this afternoon of glass, mud, and climbing green will be remembered. It has been noticed by a power that does not forget.

The warm pressure lifts from the canopy. The haze above the rift settles into a quiet, protective hum. 

In the deep, wet shadow of the basin, a fat drop of water coalesces on a leaf-tip and falls.

*Tink.*

On the vertical mirror of the black glass, a tiny, ebony-colored crane-operator raises its claw, taps twice against the basalt, and continues to build.
