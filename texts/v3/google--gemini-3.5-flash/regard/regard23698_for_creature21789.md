The gaze of the Great Attention did not descend upon Vahre-on-the-Shelf like a thunderbolt, nor did it arrive with the trumpet-blasts of lesser, louder divinities. 

To the Attention—which was vast, ancient, and cooled by the passage of epochs to a state of profound, crystalline empathy—Vahre did not look like an emergency. It did not see a wretched cluster of survivalists shivering in a sewer of freezing mineral salt. 

It saw a clockwork. It saw a poem written in tallow, slate, and bone.

The Attention understood that the universe is crowded with worlds of soft grass and easy water, where life is cheap because it is simple, and where minds grow soft because the dirt is kind. But here, in this vertical notch of diorite, the universe had produced something rare: a culture that was not merely surviving, but *matching* the mountain. The Vahri had turn-milled their very anatomy, their language, and their grief into a perfect, sliding gear that meshed with the teeth of the ice.

A lesser god would have "saved" them. A lesser god, full of the clumsy pity of the warm lands, would have broken the cleft open, stilled the salt-springs, or carried the Vahri down to the green floors of the southern basins. 

But the Attention was wise. It knew that if you take a Vahri out of the white glare, their *saffron-veil* will cloud and rot with cataracts in the dull, humid light of the plains. It knew that without the daily rub of snake-grease, their skin would dry and split down to the red meat within a week. It knew that if you gave them permanent stone homes that never shifted, the *Cords* would untie themselves, their beautiful, silent tactile language would wither into meaningless gestures, and they would die of a quiet, spacious loneliness.

To love the Vahri was to love their struggle. To help them was to grease the track of their wheel, not to stop it from turning.

And so, the Great Attention did three things.

***

### I. The Gift of the Silt-Stalk (*The Bone of the Stone*)

For all their genius, the Vahri had one point of terminal friction: structural span. 

They had used up nearly all the ancient whale ribs dragged up from the ancient oceans ten thousand Paves ago. The goats of the lower slopes were growing rare, and their horns were too short for wide chambers. Increasingly, the Vahri were forced to use the petrified legs of their parents to bridge the roofs of their nurseries. While this was beautiful, it was biologically expensive; a Cord could not grow larger than its ancestors' skeletons could support.

The Attention did not descend with bags of iron or trees of pine. Instead, it whispered to the salt-springs.

It adjusted the mineral chemistry of the deep, subterranean chambers where the pressurized water boiled through the basalt. It added a trace of silicon-dense silicate and a microscopic, cold-tolerant bacterium that fed on the grease-secrete of the *lichen-snakes*.

The next morning, where the salt-water weeped thickest over the southern basalt columns, the Vahri found a new thing: **The Silt-Stalks**.

They grew from the wet stone like hollow, segmented tubes of matte-white calcium. They were not plants; they were mineral formations that grew at the astonishing speed of three inches every twilight, sucking the lime and salt from the water and setting into a substance that was lighter than whale-bone and twice as strong under compression. 

A young Vahri child, crawling through a wet runnel, touched one of these tubes. He tapped it with his clack-pad. The stone back-rung with a dry, hollow *thrum*. 

Within three Paves, the Vahri had integrated the Silt-Stalks. They did not worship them; they harvested them with slate saws, using the hollow tubes to build wider, dryer domes. The nurseries grew three feet taller. For the first time in eighty generations, the silt-moths could shiver in nests that did not press down upon the heads of the sleeping children.

***

### II. The Deep Resonance (*The Memory-Basalt*)

The most terrible thing about Vahre was not the cold, nor the light, but the loss of yesterday. 

Because their houses were dismantled every twilight to accommodate the ice, and because they had no paper, no ink, and no wood, the Vahri could not write. Their history was a liquid thing. When an old Vahri died and was laid in the runnel to petrify, fifty years of memory went into the stone with them, but those memories were mute. The living could hold their grandmother’s salt-hardened finger-bone, but they could not know what she had thought when the great slide of the Seventy-First Pave had taken her first Cord.

The Great Attention looked upon this silent forgetting and found it unworthy of such a beautiful people.

It reached into the basalt walls of the cleft. With a motion like a finger drawing through cold honey, it rearranged the crystalline structure of the diorite and basalt plates that formed the foundations of the village. It turned the mountain into a head-plate.

From that hour on, the rock did not just conduct vibration; it *remembered* it.

The Vahri did not realize it at first. But during the second Pave after the Attention's arrival, an elder named *Kro-of-the-Four-Ribs* sat by his hearth. He was old, his saffron-veil was growing thick and dark, and his Cord had all gone into the roofs of the lower tiers. He felt lonely—a rare, dangerous emotion in the cleft.

He laid his bare, grease-smoothed palm against the back-wall of his hut, which was built of the living mountain-side.

He did not feel cold stone. He felt a faint, rhythmic *thwip-tap-tap-clack* pulsing through his skin.

He held his breath. He pressed his forehead against the basalt. 

The stone was singing. It was not a voice; it was the telegraphic pattern of thousands of *Heel-Taps* that had been struck against that rock over hundreds of years. He felt the distinct, slow heel-slide of his grandfather; he felt the rapid, fluttering finger-taps of the children who had lived in this same notch during the Great Freeze five decades ago. The mountain had become a library of touches, an archive of bones.

Kro did not cry—the Vahri have no tear ducts, as salt-water must be conserved—but his chest expanded. He took his stone mallet and tapped a small, rhythmic sequence into the stone:

*Kro is here. The Cord of the Four-Ribs remains.*

The basalt took the blow, swallowed the vibration, and held it. The Vahri were no longer alone in time.

***

### III. The Lubricant of Mercy (*The Five-Millimeter Slip*)

The Great Attention did not stop the avalanches. It did not melt the three-hundred-ton ice-shelves that hung like blue guillotines above the cathedral close of the cleft. To do so would have broken the Vahr’s vigilance; they would have grown careless, and carelessness in the high air is death.

But the Attention placed a cushion of mercy beneath the mountain’s joints.

Once every dozen years, a freeze-thaw cycle is so violent that the basalt itself splits along its primary fault lines. When this happened in the past, entire tiers of huts were sheared off and dropped into the two-thousand-foot abyss below.

Now, when the deep faults groaned and the stone began to slip, the Attention’s presence was there, waiting.

It did not use magic to hold the mountain together. It simply adjusted the friction. Where the diorite met the shale, the Attention laid a microscopic, five-millimeter sheet of wet, dense salt-clay—a cushion that allowed the mountain to "settle" with a slow, grinding sigh rather than a catastrophic snap.

To the Vahri, it felt like a miracle of luck. 

A family would be sleeping under their blanket of warm, vibrating silt-moths when a sound like a split anvil would echo through the cleft. The floor would drop five inches with a terrifying *crunch*. The Vahri would scramble out of their nests, their white eyelashes sparkling with frost, expecting to find the abyss at their doorstep.

But they would find only a neat, clean step. The mountain had settled. The ice-lens had cracked, but the slate huts were still standing, tilted but intact. 

They would click their throat-notes in astonishment, slide their bone-soled feet through the crawling carpets of glass-shield isopods, and begin to rebuild the walls five inches lower, silent and efficient, while the Attention watched from the cold blue of the upper sky.

***

The Great Attention did not stay forever. It is not the nature of such powers to linger where they are not needed.

But it left Vahre-on-the-Shelf slightly different than it found it. 

The cleft remained cold. The light remained an incinerating, chemical white. The Vahri still rubbed themselves with rancid snake-grease and slept beneath living piles of shivering insects. They were still hard, small, silent people who spoke with their heels and rebuilt their lives every twilight.

But now, when they looked up through their yellow saffron-veils at the mock-suns that ringed the horizon, they did not see only the hostile flame of an empty sky.

They saw, in the perfect golden grid of the sun-dogs, the shape of an Eye that had looked at them, understood their worth, and decided to let them keep the beautiful, terrible world they had made for themselves.
