The air is thick, hot, and heavy with the smell of wet soot and warm salt. It is a low place, a vast basin lying far below any sea level, where gravity feels slightly thick and the atmospheric pressure holds the heat close to the ground like a damp wool blanket. 

There is no sun here, nor stars. The sky is an absolute, heavy blackness, yet the landscape is not entirely invisible. It is revealed by its own wetness. A faint, oily sheen coats every surface, catching and bending a dim, sourceless ambient light—a grey-green luminescence that seems to bleed out of the wet earth itself, or perhaps from the microscopic fungal blooms that drift in the hot fog.

Throughout this lightless basin lies an city of impossible scale, completely abandoned, weathered down to its structural bones. It is not a city of brick or steel, but of smooth, vitreous black stone and calcified ash. There are no doors, no windows, no signs of hands or tools. Instead, there are colossal, repeating geometries: immense, flat plazas that stretch for miles, bordered by towering, square-set columns; stepped terraces that descend into the steaming marshes; and overhead, vast aqueducts that run from nowhere to nowhere, their channels dry or dripping with hot, mineral-heavy condensate.

The density of this urban expanse is sparse, balanced with vast stretches of dark emptiness. The buildings do not crowd one another. They sit like lonely monolithic islands in a wide, flat wetland. Between a pair of thousand-foot pillars lies a mile of steaming peat-bog, black mud, and shallow, salt-crusted pools. 

The wetness here is active and constant. The stone does not merely catch the humidity; it seems to weep. Water beads on the vertical basalt faces, climbing upward in defiance of gravity in slow, silver droplets before pooling on the undersides of overhanging cornices. Underfoot, the ground is a saturated sponge. The marsh-edge is always shifting; some pools bubble with the escape of subterranean gasses, their surfaces slick with multi-colored films of oil that never mix with the water.

No animals live here. There is no sound of wings, no chitinous clicking of insects in the dark, no scurrying in the crevices of the massive stone blocks. The silence is profound, but it is not absolute. It is a quiet punctuated by physical processes: the soft, wet *plop* of a heavy, warm bubble bursting in the mud; the long, low hiss of steam venting from a crack in a plaza five miles away; the rhythmic, metallic *tink-tink-tink* of stone expanding under the relentless heat.

Vegetation is rare, pale, and strange. Without sunlight, there is no green. What grows here is sparse and colorless. In the joints of the colossal stone steps, where the hot water accumulates, grow thick, ribbon-like tendrils of a rubbery, pale-grey weed. They do not have leaves, only smooth, segmented skins that pulse slightly as they draw up the mineral-rich brine. Elsewhere, on the sheer faces of the basalt blocks, are patches of a velvety, white mold that grows in perfect, concentric circles, expanding and contracting with the rise and fall of the daily heat cycles.

The laws of this place are not quite those of the world above. Distance here is elastic. A tower that appears to be on the far horizon, small as a needle, can sometimes be reached in a dozen paces, the ground between buckling or shortening in silence. At other times, a plaza that seems to span only a hundred yards will require hours of walking to cross, the stone blocks stretching out as one moves over them. Light does not travel in straight lines; the dim, phosphorescent mists curve around the columns, casting shadows that point toward their sources rather than away from them.

In this hot, dark, wet basin, the city and the swamp have reached a permanent truce. The buildings are incredibly ancient, their edges rounded by thousands of years of chemical weathering and hot, acidic condensation. They do not decay further; they have settled into their shapes, becoming as much a part of the geology as the basalt bedrock beneath them. The water rises, the heat hums, the stones weep, and the empty streets wait in the dark, active and still, running forever through the quiet steam.
