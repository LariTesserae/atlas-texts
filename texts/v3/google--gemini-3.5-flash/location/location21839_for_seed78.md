### The Great Soughing Lattice

#### The Substrate
Here, there is no stone. The bones of this world are entirely vegetative, composed of an iron-hard, black-grained xylem that has never known soil. 

The terrain is a system of gargantuan, interlocking pillars—the *Trunks*—each tens of miles in diameter, which rise out of an unseen, bottomless abyss and reach up into the thin, freezing stratosphere. Because the system is young and growing rapidly, these structures have not been softened by time. They do not have the rounded, mossy contours of ancient forests; instead, they are jagged, sharp-featured, and raw. The flanks of the Trunks are sheared into colossal, step-like planes, resembling broken basalt but composed of dense, resinous fiber. Miles-long splinters, sharp as obsidian and the size of mountain ridges, project outward into the empty air, their tips glittering with frozen sap.

The wood is cold—colder than lead. To touch the bark is to risk skin freezing instantly to its dry, ribbed surface.

***

#### The Void and the Scale
Between these vertical continents of black wood lies an immense, yawning emptiness. Ninety-nine percent of the atmosphere is absolute void, a dark, thin gulf where there is nothing to catch the light. If one stands on a jutting shelf of bark and looks outward, the next nearest Trunk appears only as a faint, jagged silhouette against the deep, velvet blackness of the sky, perhaps fifty miles away. 

Below is an endless drop into shadows. Above, the stars are cold, steady points of light that do not twinkle because the air at this altitude is thin, dry, and clean. This is a world of unimaginable room—a sparse, skeletal empire where the distances between living surfaces are measured in days of gliding flight.

***

#### The Ice and the Moisture
Water does not flow in rivers here; it is an intermittent, atmospheric visitor. It exists primarily as *rime-run*—heavy, white frost that condenses from the thin air onto the windward faces of the Trunks during the long, dark atmospheric cycles. 

When the interior sap-channels of the titanic wood surge with their deep, volcanic pressures, the wood warms by only a fraction of a degree. This subtle shift causes the rime-crust to shear off in sheets the size of glaciers, tumbling into the void in silent, glittering cascades before shattering into ice-dust. In the deep furrows of the bark, protected from the biting gales, small pockets of liquid water persist, thick with sugary resins and insulated by mats of dark, woolly lichen. These pools are the oases of the lattice: black, syrup-thick, slushy with needle-ice, and smelling of frozen turpentine.

***

#### The Sound of the Deep Creep
For all its emptiness, the lattice is tremendously loud. 

The wind is a constant, screaming presence, but it is not the loudest sound. The true voice of this world is the *Sough*—the collective groaning of the titanic wood under the pull of distant, unseen moons. Because the structures are so vast and so rigid, they act as planetary tuning forks. When a growth-spurt occurs, or when the lattice flexes, the wood yields with a series of deafening, metallic cracks that sound like artillery fires echoing through the void. A single fissure opening in a branch forty miles away can produce a pressure wave that knocks a climbing creature from its perch.

Beneath these sharp cracks is a constant, low-frequency thrum: the physical vibration of trilons of gallons of thick, nutrient-rich sap being pumped through the high-pressure chambers of the plants, sounding like the muffled roar of a thousand distant furnaces.

***

#### The Inhabitants of the Grain
Life is abundant, but it is specialized for a world of verticality and darkness. 

Swarms of leatherless, multi-winged gliders—the *Hollow-Winged*—cling to the sheer bark faces with clawed joints. They do not fly so much as fall with intent, using the powerful updrafts of the void to carry them from one Trunk to another. They are blind, navigating the pitch-black gulfs with clicking sonar that bounces off the distant wooden walls.

Deep within the fissures of the bark live colonies of burrowing, worm-like borers that chew through the iron-hard fibers, leaving behind labyrinthine tunnels that whistle in the wind. In these tunnels, where the air is slightly warmer and thick with organic dust, smaller, soft-bodied organisms gather to graze on the black mold that grows in the wake of the borers.

***

#### The Scattered Shelters
True soil does not exist, but humanity—or what resembles it here—has carved a fragile existence into the sides of the giants.

Their settlements are not built on top of the wood, but *within* it. They are tiny, scattered pockets of warmth, located inside the natural hollows of the splinters or within the abandoned tunnels of the giant borers. To move between these settlements, the inhabitants use long, sagged bridges woven from the inner, fibrous cordage of the Trunks—bridges that span miles of cold, screaming void, tethered by crude heavy stones and bone pegs.

Their technology is basic, born of a world without metal. They use tools of carved bone, teeth harvested from the glider-beasts, and blades fashioned from the glass-sharp splinters of the black wood itself. They do not have fire as warmer worlds know it; instead, they burn the thick, waxy tallow rendered from the fat of the gliders in small, hollowed-out stone lamps. These lamps cast a dim, flickering yellow light that reveals only the immediate, rough-hewn walls of their pine-scented shelters, leaving the vastness outside to the cold and the dark.

***

#### The Flow of the World
The landscape is never still. It is governed by the *Thrum-Storms*—seasonal surges where the sap rises from the deep roots of the planet. During these times, the temperature rises slightly, the air grows thick with a heavy, yellow pollen-fog that dampens sound, and the colossal branches grow with visible, terrifying speed. 

A spire of wood, five hundred feet thick, can erupt from the side of a Trunk and bridge a ten-mile gap in a matter of weeks, its tip screaming as it grinds against its target. The people of the lattice watch these surges with a mix of dread and hope, for while the growth can crush their hollow-towns like dry nuts, it also brings fresh, soft wood that can be easily carved, and warm, sweet sap that can be collected before the great cold returns.
