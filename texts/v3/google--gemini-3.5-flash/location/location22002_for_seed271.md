The basin is a shallow, oval depression in a plateau of weathered, dark red sedimentary stone, no more than eighty paces across from one curved wall to the other. High, soft-edged cliffs rise twenty feet to meet a lip of dry scrub, but down here, inside the bowl, the air is dropped into a pocket of warm, motionless twilight. The sky above is only a pale strip of slate-grey, filtered through the hot, stagnant air of the upper plateau, so that the light reaching the floor of the depression is a uniform, shadowless dimness.

The heat here is steady and dry, though it carries a faint scent of cellar-cool stone from the deep crevices. It is the warmth of flat rocks that have baked in the sun for an eternity and are now breathing that stored heat back out into the dusk. Nothing moves. There is no wind to stir the dust or shake the few stiff twigs of the plants that grow here.

Water exists only in small, quiet patience. On the underside of the limestone overhangs that line the western curve of the basin, the rock is cool enough to gather the slow breath of the earth. Droplets of condensation form there, gleaming like grey mercury in the quiet light. They do not drip often. Every few minutes, a single drop will lose its hold and fall, landing with a clean, soft *tink* into a tiny, dark circle of damp earth. Around these small, damp craters, the soil is dark and packed, but inches away it fades back into the universal dry, reddish dust of the basin floor.

Vegetation is sparse, keeping its distance from itself. There are no trees, nor any grass. Instead, in the tiny damp zones beneath the drip-points, there are flat, liver-colored rosettes of ancient lichen and occasional clumps of a low, grey-leaved herb that smells faintly of dry sage when crushed. These plants have no flowers, and their leaves are thick and waxy to hold what little moisture they receive. Between these scattered patches of life lie yards of bare, smoothed stone and fine, red silt that has settled over centuries into a flat, level floor.

In the center of the basin, half-buried in the silt, are the remains of three circular enclosures. They are built of flat, unmortared slate stones, stacked three or four high, though many have slid down into the dirt. They are not grand; they are the size of hearths or simple sleeping-benches, built by hands that left no other mark. The stone of these structures is the same red-grey slate as the cliffs, and they have been weathered so thoroughly by the slow passage of time that they seem more like natural outcrops than something built. No tools lie near them; no shards of pottery or charcoal from old fires disrupt the clean, empty dust. They are simply shapes in the ground, as settled and permanent as the cliffs themselves.

There are no animals here. There are no beetles in the dry rot of the dead herb stems, no birds crossing the strip of grey sky overhead, and no lizards warming themselves on the stones. The air is entirely clear of the hum of insects. This absence of fauna gives the basin a profound, architectural stillness.

The erosion here is finished. It has reached its final, balanced form. The cliffs are not shedding rubble; the stones on the floor are not rolling; the soil is not washing away. Everything has found its angle of repose. If a pebble were to be moved by a footstep, it would feel like an event of immense consequence, a disruption of a balance that has existed since the plateau first cracked open.

If you stand in the center of the circle, the only sound is the rhythm of your own breath, and, at long intervals, the dry, hollow *tuck* of a single drop of water hitting the damp clay beneath the ledge. The sound does not travel far; the dry, porous stone of the basin walls absorbs it instantly, leaving the silence whole again.

Nothing changes here. The seasons do not seem to touch the bottom of this bowl. The temperature does not drop at night, nor does the sun ever shine directly down to bake the dampness away. It remains always in this warm, dim state of suspension—a small, quiet room of stone and dust, waiting for nothing, holding its breath forever in the warm twilight.
