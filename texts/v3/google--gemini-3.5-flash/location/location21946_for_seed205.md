### The Cambium Plains

#### The Substrate and Scale
Here, there is no stone. The earth does not consist of granite, basalt, or silt, but of *cambium*—an endless, self-healing, continental crust of seasoned, fibrous timber. 

This is a world constructed entirely from the anatomy of a singular, unimaginable plant. The plains are the flat, weathered expanses of gargantuan leaf-scars, wide enough to cradle seas. The mountain ranges are not tectonic folds but the ridges of longitudinal bark-plates, rising in steep, grooved cliffs of hard, sun-baked cork. Deep canyons—mile-wide splits in the wood—reveal the pale, vascular bundles of the interior, where ancient sap-vessels run like dry, vertical highways down into the dark.

Because the scale is so vast, the geography is sparse. You can walk for three days along a single, smooth furrow of golden grain without encountering a change in the horizon. The landscape is balanced with an immense, baking emptiness. Above, the sky is clear and normal—holding a steady, white-gold sun that casts sharp, clean-edged shadows across the amber wood—but down on the surface, the air ripples with dry, shimmering heat waves. The horizon is always a long, distant wave of heat-distorted fibers, where the yellow-brown earth rises to meet a pale blue sky.

#### The Atmosphere of Water
Moisture is not a constant presence, but an event. In this high-altitude upland, water exists primarily as condensation. Each night, as the temperature drops slightly from the blistering heat of the day, the vast vascular channels beneath the bark breathe. They exhale a heavy, sweet-scented mist that coats the cambium in a layer of glistening dew. 

By dawn, the plains are damp, slick, and dark, looking like polished obsidian. In the deep longitudinal grooves of the wood, small "creeks" of runoff begin to flow—though they are not water as we know it, but a thin, highly diluted sugary sap. These ephemeral streams run hot and sticky for a few hours before the intense morning sun bakes them dry. 

By noon, the moisture has vanished, leaving behind glittering crusts of white, crystalline sap-sugar in the hollows. These dry pans crackle underfoot like salt flats. The only standing water exists in "gourd-wells"—natural, bulbous depressions in the wood, several yards wide, where the plant's defense mechanisms have sealed off pockets of rainwater behind thick, waterproof layers of cork.

#### The Deafening Chorus
Though the land is physically sparse, it is thick with sound. The world is a giant acoustic drum. Because the entire ground is made of hollow, dry, resonant wood, every movement vibrates through the soles of your feet hours before it reaches your ears. 

But the air itself is dominated by an overwhelming, deafening din. The hollow bores of the wood act as massive organ pipes for the high-altitude winds, whistling in deep, sub-bass hums that rattle the teeth. Over this background roar sits the relentless chatter of the fauna. Millions of bark-boring insects—some small as dust, others the size of hunting hounds—live within the cracks of the cambium. They grind, click, and scrape their chitinous mandibles against the hard grain, a dry, rhythmic rattling that never ceases. 

During the hottest hours of the day, giant cicada-like organisms, perched high on the ridges of the bark-cliffs, release synchronized bursts of screeching static. It is a sound so intense it feels physical, vibrating the chest cavity and forcing any traveler to speak in shouts or carry thick ear-plugs made of fibrous pith.

#### The Ecology
The plants that grow *on* the plant are few, but specialized. Since the substrate is living wood, normal roots cannot penetrate it. Instead, the vegetation consists of parasitic, hair-like crimson mosses that creep along the grain-lines, and massive, bulbous tillandsias that cling to the bark with dry, claw-like anchors, catching the morning dew in their shallow leaves.

The fauna is dominant, aggressive, and highly adapted to the wood-world.
*   **Glider-packs:** Membrane-winged lizards that look like dried leaves drift silently across the miles-wide canyons, hunting the giant wood-boring grubs.
*   **Pulp-ants:** Six-foot-long creatures with saw-like mandibles that chew the dry cork of the ridges to build towering, spit-glued paper mounds. These mounds are the only hills on the flat plains, standing like lonely, grey chimneys against the sun.
*   **The Bark-Stalkers:** Long-legged, spider-like predators with padded feet that make no sound on the resonant wood. They hunt by placing their sensitive leg-tips against the grain, listening for the vibrations of smaller creatures moving through the grooves.

#### The Built and the Primitive
Humanity—or whatever has taken its place here—exists only in tiny, scattered pockets, dwarfed by the landscape. Their presence is marked by "Hanging-Towns." These are small clusters of dome-shaped shelters woven from the peeled inner-bark of the canyons, suspended by thick, hand-spun fibers beneath the overhangs of the giant bark-cliffs.

The technology of these people is primitive, shaped entirely by the absence of stone and metal. 
*   **Materials:** Every tool is made of wood, bone, or horn. Heavy axes are fashioned from the petrified, iron-hard heartwood found in the deepest canyons, laboriously rubbed against other wood until it takes an edge.
*   **Utility:** Pins, needles, and spear-tips are made from the long, toxic thorns of the parasitic tillandsias. 
*   **The Sap-Trade:** The people are nomadic tappers. They move across the vast plains on light, multi-legged wooden sleds that glide smoothly over the grain-lines. They carry long, hollow bone needles which they drive into the soft joints of the bark-plates to collect the sweet, dark amber sap. This sap is their food, their glue, and, when distilled in sun-baked clay pots (made from the pulverized paper-mounds of the pulp-ants), their fuel.

There are signs of older things: ancient, weathered steps carved directly into the heartwood of a hundred-mile-long vertical ridge, now smoothed by centuries of wind and insect-crawl until they resemble nothing more than natural ripples in the grain. Sometimes, sticking out of a deep split in the dry ground, one might find the rib of a long-dead, colossal beast, preserved by the dry heat and the natural antiseptic qualities of the surrounding wood, looking like a white archway leading nowhere.

#### The Dynamic
This world is not static; it is in a state of slow, rhythmic, gargantuan motion. It is an ecosystem governed by the "Cambial Pulse." Every ninety days, the great plant-substrate swells. The fissures between the bark-plates close up; the wood groans with a sound like a thousand sailing ships breaking apart in a storm. 

During the Pulse, the sap-wells overflow, flooding the low furrows with hot, steaming syrup. The folk of the Hanging-Towns pull their homes high up into the cliffs, retreating to the safety of the dead-wood zones where the shifting is minimal. The fauna erupts into a frenzy of mating and feeding, gorging on the sudden abundance of wet sugar before the sun returns to bake the world back into its dry, golden, deafening equilibrium.
