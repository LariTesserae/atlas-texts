The air does not damp the skin; it dries it. There is no moisture here, no mist, no dew. The atmosphere is a baking, kiln-warm draft that smells of toasted oats, cedar-shavings, terpene, and hot, dry starch. 

This is the **Chamber of the Bast**, a vast subterranean rift-valley miles beneath the outer crust of a world where water never consolidated. In its place, a colossal, hyper-active vegetative matrix forms the solid ground, the walls, the ceiling, and the sky. This is not a forest; it is a single, churning, continent-sized organism of dry cellulose, cork, and pith, hollowed out by its own furious growth.

### The Terrain of Chaff

The floor of the valley is a shifting desert of *frass*—flat, dry plates of discarded bark, seed-husks, and powdery yellow spore-dust that behaves like dry sand. Beneath this dry scree lies the "bedrock": a dense, interlocked pavement of fossilized root-burls, pale and hard as bone, but warm to the touch. 

The walls are not stone, but mountains of fibrous peat and massive, ribbed trunks that rise miles into the darkness before curving into a vaulted ceiling of woven lianas. Everything is under construction. There is no erosion here by wind or rain, because there is no water to carry the debris away; instead, the landscape is shaped by the violent thrust of new growth. Every few minutes, a crack like a gunshot echoes through the cavern as a new shoot, thick as a tower, splits its dry sheath and lunges ten yards into the open air, spraying a shower of crisp, papery splinters. 

There are no rivers here. Instead, there are dry rivers of seeds—billions of polished, marble-sized nutlets that flow down the steep fiber-slopes in a continuous, whispering slide, pooling in great, hollow basins where they rattle like dry peas in a hot pan.

### The Deafening Din

The silence of stone is entirely absent. The landscape is a bellows. The sound is an overwhelming, multi-layered roar that registers in the teeth as much as the ears. 

1. **The Friction:** The giant, woody vaults above are constantly shifting under the pressure of their own rapid expansion. Trunks the size of hilltops grind against one another with a terrifying, rhythmic shriek—the sound of a thousand dry wooden ships breaking up on a reef.
2. **The Stridulation:** The fauna is everywhere, dense and screaming. Millions of scale-winged cicadas, each larger than a hand, cling to the bark-walls, their drum-organs vibrating in a synchronized, metallic hum that rises and falls like the whistle of a steam turbine.
3. **The Pneumatics:** Deep within the walls, hollow bamboo-like conduits, yards in diameter, draw in the hot, dry air of the deeper earth to feed the internal fires of the plants. They moan with a low, sub-bass whistle that makes the dust on the floor dance in geometric patterns.

### The Light of the Wells

The illumination is dry and amber, casting long, sharp shadows through the lattice of vines. It does not come from a sun, but from the plants themselves. Hanging from the ceiling are thousands of translucent, bladder-like seed sacs, each the size of a hay-wain. Inside them, a non-aqueous, phosphorus-rich oil undergoes a slow, dry combustion, glowing with the steady, orange-red light of a blacksmith’s hearth. 

In areas where these bladders have burst, the valley is dark, save for the clouds of self-luminous spore-dust that drift on the thermal currents like carpets of green sparks.

### An Absorbent, Wood-Cased Ecology

In this dry empire, life operates on a chemistry of oils, waxes, and pulverized starches. 
The plants do not possess sap; they possess *latex*—thick, sticky varnishes that harden in seconds when exposed to the air. If a vine is severed, it does not bleed; it extrudes a brittle, glass-like amber that the local fauna immediately harvest.

The animals are entirely dry-bodied, built of chitin, horn, and parchment.
* **The Sieve-Moths:** Massive, three-winged flyers with six-foot wingspans made of dry, yellow membrane. They do not fly so much as sail on the hot updrafts, their toothless mouths open to strain the seed-dust from the air.
* **The Crust-Lice:** Flat, plate-faced creatures that move in sheets of tens of thousands across the bark-walls. Their legs are tipped with flinty claws that make a sound like falling rain as they scrape away the outer layers of dead cork to reach the starchy pith beneath.
* **The Borer-Worms:** Blind, segmented tubes of hardened leather, twelve feet long, that eat their way directly through the solid wood of the floor, leaving behind them smooth, polished tunnels that serve as highways for smaller, skittering things.

### The Scattered Marks of the Hand

There is intelligence here, though it is small, scattered, and fragile against the roar of the plant-mass. 

High in the cavities of the dead burls—where the wood has dried to a silver-gray cork—are the dwellings. They are not constructed; they are excavated. Simple, rounded chambers have been rasped out of the soft interior pith using tools made of serrated seed-pods and fire-hardened thorn-drills.

There are no metals. The inhabitants—who are rarely seen but whose presence is marked by their works—use the environment with a desperate, clever simplicity:
* **The Vine-Bridges:** Suspension bridges made of single, split lianas, stretched across the seed-gulfs, anchored to the walls with massive pegs of ironwood.
* **The Spore-Lamps:** Small, hollowed-out gourds filled with glowing dust, hung from fiber cords to mark the safe paths through the shifting frass.
* **The Sound-Deflectors:** Curved shields of rigid bark, taller than two men, set up near the living quarters to deflect the deafening scream of the rubbing branches, creating small pockets of relative quiet where one can hear a human voice.

The world is too active for permanent settlements. A village carved into a root-node may last a year before the root begins a new cycle of growth, swelling and twisting until the chambers are crushed flat, forcing the dwellers to pack their leather bags, grab their scraper-tools, and flee into the next canyon before the wood closes over them forever.
