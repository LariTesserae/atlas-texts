In the depths of an ancient, submerged world, an endless, low-elevation landscape stretches out. Vast expanses of stone, sand, and silt dominate the terrain, sculpted over millennia by erosion and currents. The atmosphere is permanently locked in darkness, with a persistent glacial chill that has shaped life into unexpected forms.

Here, indigenous beings thrive. They are large, with bulbous bodies covered in fine, designated hairs that filter occasional fragments of detritus carried by the cold currents. Their appearance is striking, like cotton tufts crawling across the stone expanse, giving the impression that the land itself is alive.

These beings are gelatinous, varying between soft, ethereal clusters and bony organisms. They can retract their bodies, fluctuating between substantial floating organisms and creatures that blend into the sedimentary layers of the ground.

Among the stark beauty, traces of intelligence emerge. Gel-like pens leave designs in the sediment, interpreted by some as simple forms of writing. Innovations appear in pods born of dislocated aquatic bodies, where species share race-vice homes.

In this world, life is adapted to the darkness. The landscape is covered with living, crawling creatures, giving the impression that the ground itself is alive. Energy is generated from scarce resources using advanced technologies, and vibrations resonate through the ground, manipulated by sensitive, densely winged creatures.

Despite the harsh conditions, life flourishes in a communal, interconnected web of processes. Individual organisms are part of a larger tapestry of chemical reactions and energy exchanges. Life in this world is both beautiful and alien, shaped by conditions that would be hostile to most other forms of life.
