In the Valdad Mountains, time is not thought of as before or after, but as a persistent, gnawing ache that seeps from the stone and into the marrow. It is a space where the geometry of existence has been warped, where the lines of past, present, and future are oddly twisted. Here, what lies ahead is also what has passed, and the present is not a moment but a timeless loop. Yet, it is in this confusing mesh where life has uniquely adapted to the perpetually regretful air.

The beings of the Valdad, silent guardians of this sacred desolation, embody the very essence of the land. Their forms are indistinct from the twisted trees and ancient mountains. Towering, with skin as rough as bark and hair that cascades like the vast, heavy clouds that forget they were once light, they move with the slowness of giants, as if each step is a considered reflection. The sound of their heartbeats harmonizes with the echoing calls of the wandering beasts, a shared symphony that registers time not in beats but in sighs.

Their hands, like ancient hands of a sculptor, hold entities that glow with a light too disparate for the somber surroundings. These entities, orbs of light and energy, are what the inhabitants of the Valdad hold within them, actively investigating how these relics exert a placebo of comfort in the relentless outlook of the desolate grandeur. The inhabitants all recognize them, know the lines that protect the mechanisms within. Each is designed for a purpose unique to a piece left behind by those who'd entered and found an unnestling liking for visiting the Valdad.

If one spends as much time as it takes to see this odd arrangement, they'll find the orbs all over the land, stuck deep in the slow-opening petals of flowers, squashed between rocks that parted them without a single sound. They are cheered on by these subsiding drumbeats, extinct truffles that sang even through ice, opening and dying. Their similarities shatter spectacle, produce warmth, photographs taken early of cured photographs.
