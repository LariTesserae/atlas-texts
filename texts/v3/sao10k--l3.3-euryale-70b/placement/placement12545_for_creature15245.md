In the depths of the bioluminescent ocean, a city of translucent, melodic beings hummed a soft tune. Their ethereal bodies undulated as they moved, their voices interweaving in a rich harmony. These Luminari lived in symbiosis with photosynthetic microbial mats, which they tended in delicate gardens of crystal-filled orbs. The mats transformed carbon dioxide and chemical nutrients from the dark, mineral-rich floor below into sugars and oxygen, fueling the Luminari's bioluminescence and melodic enhancements. As they grew, the Luminari wove filaments of shiny fibers and nutrient-rich fluid into complex networks of iridescent sacs, each pulsing with gentle light. Their slow health and reproduction were measured by how well they made music with one another. In times of stress, the melodies would deepen, drawing close the scavenger swarms to remove the weak.

A scavenger swarm inspected a mature filament of Luminari, each mouth probing the lone dead individual. Here their melody was dissonant. It would remain for some years, a ghost in their songs, until two new Luminari grew to take its place. The swarm finished and pulled away. 

The city shone bright and fully alive again.
