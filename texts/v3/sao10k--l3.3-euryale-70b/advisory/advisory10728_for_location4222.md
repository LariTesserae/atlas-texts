Visiting this location as an ordinary human multiverse traveler would require meticulous preparation, both physically and psychologically. The eerie and alien environment poses a plethora of challenges that must be addressed to ensure a safe and successful visit.

### 1. **Physical Preparation**

- **Respiratory Protection:** The air is not only cool and damp but is also filled with biological sounds that are intense enough to be felt as sensations. This could potentially affect breathing, so a breathing mask or a device that can offer a secondary source of air might be beneficial.
  
- **Clothing:** Thermal layers to maintain body heat, as the ground is cool, and the environment itself seems to be in a perpetual state of twilight. The materials should be water-resistant considering the intermittent rain and possibly waterproof pants due to the moisture-absorbing nature of the ground.

- **Footwear:** The smoothness and dampness of the ground, combined with the need to navigate through thick, jelly-like fungi, necessitate footwear that provides good traction and is waterproof. Insulated and thick-soled boots could help regulate foot temperature and cushion against the fungi's texture.

- **Food and Water:** Bringing enough food and a portable water purification system or bottled water, considering the lack of known sources of clean water. Given the eerie, unsettling nature of the place, something to maintain morale, like chocolate or comfort foods, might also be valuable.

### 2. **Equipment**

- **First Aid Kit:** Given the alien nature of this place, potential injuries could be unlike anything seen before. Bringing a first aid kit with a broad range of supplies, including antiseptics and painkillers, would be wise.

- **Communication Device:** A multi-universal communicator or an emergency beacon could be critical in case of emergencies. However, the environment's unique properties might affect standard communication devices, so backup methods, like mirrors for signaling, could also be useful.

- **Lighting:** Although the fungi don't emit light, the perpetual twilight might still make seeing difficult, especially over long distances. Portable, high-intensity, and possibly long-life lighting might help identify objects at distance more clearly and prevent trips or falls.

- **Data Collection Tools:** Given the uniqueness of this environment, collecting samples of the fungi and any other materials could be invaluable for future study. Bringing sterile sampling equipment and data collection devices like cameras or spectrometers could provide valuable insights into this alien world.

### 3. **Psychological Preparation**

- **Mental Health Check:** The environment described is prone to induce psychological distress. A pre-travel mental health check could help identify potential vulnerabilities. The places alienating effect, combined with the constant biological sounds, might push travelers to their limits.

- **Coping Mechanisms:** Learning and practicing coping mechanisms for anxiety, fear, or confusion could be crucial. This might include breathing exercises, positive thinking, or meditation.

- **Group Travel:** If possible, traveling in a group could provide emotional support, shared workload, and additional safety in case of emergencies. Ensure that all group members are mentally and physically prepared for the challenges ahead.

### 4. **Contingency Planning**

- **Route Mapping:** The lack of landmarks and the difficulty of navigating through thick fungi growths necessitate thorough mapping of any route taken. This could involve leaving markers that are visible from a distance or using a device that can continuously track and record location.

- **Emergency Shelters:** Given the unpredictable nature of the weather and the existence of the perpetual twilight, having quick-assembly shelters could provide a place to take refuge if needed.

- **Exit Strategy:** Before venturing into the heart of this landscape, it would be wise to scout out the nearest safe exit points and mark them clearly. The psychological impact of feeling trapped could exacerbate the alienating effect of the location.

Visiting this location would be an extraordinary undertaking, requiring extensive planning, equipment, and mental preparation.
