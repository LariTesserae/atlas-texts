Life found here is diverse, ranging from hearty microorganisms thriving in stagnant water pools to majestic avian species defying the biting cold with evolutionary adaptations. Microorganisms like psychrophiles and extremophilic bacteria inhabit shallow pools and trickling streams, converting minerals and chemical compounds into biomass.

Birds flit about, their specially adapted feathers insulating them from the biting temperatures. Mammals like the snow leopard and the Tibetan antelope (Tibetan takin) inhabit the upper grasslands and areas with denser vegetation. They can maintain a thick coat and a layer of fat to combat the extreme climate.

Insects are also seen at lower elevations, threading through vegetation to survive in pockets of warmth and humidity. The animals living here have been adapted through evolutionary methods such as the white coats of arctic fox or fur coats of snow leopard to suit the living environment at higher altitude.

Overall, life survives here despite harsh conditions, a testament to the will of the earth and the resilience of life itself.
