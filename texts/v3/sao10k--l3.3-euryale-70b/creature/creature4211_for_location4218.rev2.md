The deserted beach is home to an array of wildlife and plants, each adapted to the harsh coastal environment.

**Plants:**

*   **Mangroves:** These sturdy, salt-tolerant trees thrive in the coastal wetlands, providing vital shelter for marine life and protection against erosion.
*   **Dune Grasses:** The hardy, grassy vegetation stabilizes the sand dunes, preventing them from shifting and protecting the surrounding ecosystem.
*   **Beach Vegetation:** The hardy succulents and beach flowers have adapted to the high salt content, intense sunlight, and frequent flooding of the coastal environment.

**Animals:**

*   **Marine Life:** The ocean waters are teeming with diverse marine species, including fish, dolphins, sea turtles, and coral reefs.
*   **Seabirds:** The beach is a haven for a variety of seabirds, such as seagulls, ospreys, and sandpipers, which nest, feed, and roost in the area.
*   **Crustaceans:** Crabs, shrimp, and lobsters are abundant in the tidal zones, while shellfish like mussels, clams, and oysters are found in the intertidal areas.
*   **Mammals:** Dolphins, whales, and seals frequent the coastal waters, while land mammals like raccoons, opossums, and rodents inhabit the beach's terrestrial areas.

**Insects:**

*   **Beach Flies:** The abundance of decaying organic matter and saltwater attracts a variety of flies, which are an essential food source for many animals.
*   **Beetles:** The coastal environment is home to numerous beetle species, which feed on plants, decaying matter, and other insects.
*   **Butterflies:** The beach flowers and vegetation attract a range of butterfly species, which feed on nectar and pollen.

**Microorganisms:**

*   **Algae:** The ocean waters support a diverse array of algae species, which form the base of the marine food web.
*   **Bacteria:** The beach sand and ocean water are home to a wide variety of bacteria, which play a crucial role in decomposing organic matter and recycling nutrients.
