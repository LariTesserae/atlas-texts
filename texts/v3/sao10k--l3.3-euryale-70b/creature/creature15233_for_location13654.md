A solitary, blind subterranean creature inhabits this forsaken cavern. It's a pale, elongated subterranean worm, perhaps 3-4 meters long. Its skin is smooth and nearly transparent, exposing a network of darker, almost black internal organs. Slender, almost feathery appendages extend from its body, used to sense its surroundings in the absence of light. They wave and tremble gently as it moves through the heavy air, detecting the faint disturbances caused by its own movements.

The worm is drawn to the moist areas of the cavern, where its appendages taste the moisture in the air. Here, it finds the sparse fungal growths, which make up the bulk of its diet. It slowly envelops these growths with its body, exuding mild digestive enzymes before absorbing the nutrients.

Despite the weighty atmosphere of the cavern, the worm moves with an almost ethereal grace. Its undulations are slow and deliberate, fostering the sense of stillness that pervades the space. It's been here for as long as it can remember, unaware of the world beyond the cavern's confines.

In the ancient, worn modifications to the cavern, the worm sometimes finds small, forgotten caches of minerals. These it consumes eagerly, replenishing its strength. It is a patient creature, used to slow, gradual changes.

Over millennia, the worm has developed a peculiar relationship with the abandoned remnants of human presence. It treats the rough stone pedestals as shallow pools of mineralized water, subtly altering them over time with its digestive secretions. On the partially carved walls, it leaves trails of its translucent mucilage, a slow, organic counterpoint to the carved, geometric patterns left by long-dead hands.

The worm is alone, the sole living presence in this subterranean expanse. And yet, it is not lonely. It has grown comfortable in this weighty, silent world, becomes one with the gradual seepage, the rare drips, and the motionless air.

In its own, slow way, the worm is a guardian of the cavern, ensuring that the nuanced balance of the space is not disrupted. It is a creature of memories, an echo of the life that once touched this place with human hands. And it will continue, undisturbed, until the stones themselves forget their stories.
