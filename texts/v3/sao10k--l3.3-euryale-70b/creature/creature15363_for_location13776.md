The inhabitants of this peculiar, evolving landscape are as complex and multifaceted as the environment itself. They are beings of contrasts—industrial and organic, mechanical and biological, fast-paced and slow-going.

At first glance, they appear humanoid in shape, with bodies composed of a meshwork of metals, wires, and synthetic materials. Their skin is a latticework of interlocking plates that shimmer with a fine layer of oil and grime. Where joints should be, flexible plastic tubes allow for fluid movement. Atop their heads sit rough-hewn helmets fashioned from discarded machinery parts. Goggles made from scraps of tinted glass protect their eyes.

These strange creatures move with purposeful slowness through the marshy terrain. They carry crude tools fashioned from scavenged metalware, using them to harvest resources from the structures that pepper the area. From rusting pipes, they extract watery fuels; from crumbling foundations, chunks of rough concrete.

As they work, tiny tendrils sprout from their fingertips—hybrid appendages capable of photosynthesizing sunlight filtered through the thick air. Slowly writhing vines covered in luminescent moss weave themselves into intricate patterns along their limbs as they toil. These peculiar adornments seem to subtly affect the local ecosystem: nearby scrubby plants twist towards the creatures' path as if drawn by invisible strings; wandering snails attach themselves to the vines' glowing fronds.

Interactions between these beings occur infrequently but demonstrate an odd harmony despite their awkward appearance. Shuffling towards one another across uneven ground, they exchange bursts of fragmented speech followed by brief pauses before replying. When closer still, they link arms at elbow and knee joints via retractable couplers hidden beneath shell-like carapaces covering vulnerable junctures.
