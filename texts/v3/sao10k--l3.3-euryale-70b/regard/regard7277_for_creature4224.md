As a benevolent, wise power gazed upon the realm of Erebo, its heart swelled with compassion. This realm, with its twist on the fabric of physics so profound, could be seen in two lights. On one hand, it stood as a testament to the boundless potential and unpredictability of existence - a living, shifting kaleidoscope where the imagination could freely roam. Yet, on the other hand, it hosted an existence of constant flux, turmoil, and bewilderment for those unfortunate enough to call it home.

Moved by a deep desire to see the denizens of Erebo live in peace and contentment, the wise power chose to intervene. With a sweep of its cosmic hand, it parted the fabric of reality in a distant corner of the realm. A blur appeared, solidifying into an arching portal that stood glowing with warm, golden light. 

The Zha'Zhi, in their constant dance with the ever-changing landscape, soon sensed this new presence. Curious, some of them shifted form and flowed towards the entrance to the portal. As they touched its rim, any who stood before it received a vision: a world that mirrored Erebo, yet was not. It was a place of black stone and twisting towers, but devoid of the churning chaos. Here, time flowed steady and true, the sky glowed with a soft, comforting warmth, and the ground was graced with lush gardens of strange, ethereal blooms.

This idyllic reflection of their home, combined with the pulsing mental cries of their selves that could thrive here, was quickly persuasive. First one, then others, then many the Zha'Zhi stepped through the portal into this parallel Erebo. They found the visions true - the city's structure was familiar, yet each step brought a sense of homecoming, of solace. 

In this sanctuary, they could build, over time, communal halls, battlements, treasuries of joy and peace, rather than navigate the dazzling opportunity and threat of the reality breach. Here, time progressed in steady, unbroken beats. The air was filled with a pleasant lace of colors dancing into existence as the unbeholden event occurred.

Over time, these 'Yupe', as the Zha'Zhi began to call thisomorphic variant of Erebo, were turned towards natural gardens and illuminated streetlamps.
