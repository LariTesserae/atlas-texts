In this place, the low elevation brings the sky down close. The atmosphere is heavy with moisture. Water weeps from every surface. The air is thick with water vapor, a bright haze hanging heavy over everything.

The terrain is of plaster-like substances. It's white, smooth, curving. Dull, matte finish. The surfaces are slick with moisture. Water seeps from cracks and joints. It puddles on surfaces, forming thin sheets that stretch and break apart. There's no clear demarcation between solid and liquid. The plaster is soft, yielding, like it's still wet.

Vegetation is lush and varied. There are thick stalks that support broad, flat leaves. Bushes with twisted limbs and dark, shiny foliage. Flowering vines that spill over walls and flow across the ground. Everything is covered in a thin film of moisture. Leaves and petals are slick to the touch. The air is heavy with the scent of blooming flowers and decaying plant matter.

The temperature is sweltering. The air feels like a physical presence, pressing in on the skin. It's hard to breathe. The moisture in the air makes it feel like you're breathing through a wet cloth.

The landscape stretches out to the horizon. It's not so big as to be overwhelming, but there's a sense of space. You can see for a long way. The sky above is a deep, rich blue, but it's hazy from the water vapor.

The plaster terrain is rough and unweathered. The edges are sharp. The surfaces are unblemished. It's clear that this place hasn't been here long. It's been recently constructed, or perhaps emerged from some primordial soup.

Despite the oppressive atmosphere, the place feels sparse. There's a lot of empty space. The vegetation is lush, but it's spread out. The plaster surfaces are mostly bare. There are a few structures — twisting, organic shapes that seem to grow from the ground itself. They're made of the same plaster-like substance as the terrain.

Everything is made. There's no natural substrate here. The ground, the plants, the structures — it's all constructed. The tech level is low. The plaster is rough and unfinished. The structures seem to have been formed rather than fabricated. There are no signs of tool marks or other evidence of complex crafting.

The light is blindingly bright. It reflects off the wet surfaces, making it hard to look in any direction. The air is so full of water vapor that it seems to glow from within.

There's a lot of fauna. Birds flit through the air, their feathers slick with moisture. Insects buzz and flit. Small, lizard-like creatures scurry across the ground. The plants seem to be covered in tiny, worm-like things that burrow into the leaves and stems.

But there's something off about this place. The laws of reality seem to be breaking down. The plaster terrain shifts and flows like a liquid. The plants seem to be melting, their edges indistinct. The creatures are twisted and distorted, like they've been reflected in a funhouse mirror. The air seems to ripple and distort, like the surface of a pond.

Despite the abundance of life, the place is mostly quiet. There are occasional bird calls, the buzz of insects, the scurrying of small creatures. But it's all muted, like the sound is being absorbed by the wet air.

This place doesn't change. It's stuck in a state of equilibrium. The water continues to seep from the surfaces, but it never seems to accumulate or drain. The plants continue to grow, but they never seem to spread or wither. The creatures move through the landscape, but they never seem to change or interact. It's like time itself has been frozen, leaving this place in a state of perpetual stasis.
