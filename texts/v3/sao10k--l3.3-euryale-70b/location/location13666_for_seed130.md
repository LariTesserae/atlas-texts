The world is a vast, oppressive slab of wet concrete, stretching out as far as the eye can see in every direction. The sky above is a deep, foreboding grey, and the air is thick with the smell of damp earth and grease. The ground beneath is slick with moisture, and every step sends tiny rivulets of water running through the fine cracks in the concrete.

Here and there, long rows of grey factories rise up from the ground, their walls covered in pipes and ducts that hiss and bubble with steam. Tall smokestacks belch clouds of black smoke into the air, and the sound of machinery is deafening - a constant rumble of engines, a screech of metal on metal, the occasional boom of some unseen process.

Despite the activity of the factories, there is no sign of animal life anywhere. No birds fly through the air, no insects buzz around the factories, not even a weed pushes its way up through the cracks in the concrete. The only movement is the gush of water from the factories, the churning of gears, and the shaking of the ground itself as the machines beneath the surface of the concrete labor and rumble.

The concrete itself is old and fractured, covered in intricate networks of fine cracks and grooves. Water seeps from these cracks, forming tiny rivulets that run across the ground and disappear down into the dark, gaping mouths of drains. The air is cool and damp, and the smell of wet earth hangs heavy over everything.

The world is a maze of pipes and ducts, a seemingly endless labyrinth of twisted metal and hissing steam. The factories loom above, casting long shadows across the ground as the sun struggles to penetrate the thick clouds of smoke that hang in the air. The only sound is the constant rumble of machinery, the hiss of steam, and the dull boom of the factories as they churn out their endless stream of who-knows-what.

As you walk through this desolate landscape, the ground shakes beneath your feet and the air vibrates with the power of the machines that lie hidden beneath the surface of the concrete. The sky above seems to weigh down on you, heavy with the weight of smoke and grease. The factories loom over you, their smokestacks like skeletal fingers reaching up towards the sky. This is a place where the very ground itself seems alive, where the machines that churn and rumble beneath your feet are the only thing that truly matters.
