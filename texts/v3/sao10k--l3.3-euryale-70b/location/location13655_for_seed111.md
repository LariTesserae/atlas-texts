This is a peculiar and biologically rich gorge, somewhere high in the mountain where the erosion has only just begun. The walls tower above, sheer and friable, with rime clinging to their ledgy faces, giving the landscape an uncanny texture. The valley is small and closed at either end, where the mountainside steps up to a more drastic face. Here, the slope is a half-mile lateral bluff of conglomerate — or compact boulders — that marks the edge of a steep streak. Water condenses and runs over everything at night, and west winds clear the face — but a trickle stays in the single strait of valley all day. Lewis fern growing near the water occupies moist orillines; the valley's rest is pine with wired seedpods.

The particles nice crevasses in stone should grind to a rubicelle. And spinels like prevail also, this fine gravel having hundreds of places under process in absence. The crisped light sticks so hard under many walls — beyond an hour we slow — at gravel creek where thin junipers not filled are kept.
