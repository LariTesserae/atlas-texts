I imagine a submerged volcanic rock pinnacle, thrust from the seafloor by some cataclysmic event and still venting sulfurous steam into the dark water. The only vegetation is a tiny kelp garland clinging halfway up the rock, swaying in the turbulence of the vent. The temperature is warm enough that the vague illumination from the rising steam can cause shadowy mirages to writhe on the rock's face like escaping spirits. It's a small area, this one rock with a few boulders nearby; the seafloor drops away into dark nothingness in every direction, but there's no sense of an "ocean" out there, only a limitless black liquid churning with the pulse of the vent.

There's one clear human artifact -- a gumbooted diver's leg, half-buried in silt near the base of the rock. It's comforting to see anything recognizable at all in this place. Otherwise all the shreds of material and scraps of life are indistinguishable. The view is a slowly tumbling chaos of silt, fetus-like creatures, steaming chunks of rock and smears of oil but it's all veiled by the wall of roaring water that pours down from above, flinging the steam plume horizontal in plumes and banners. 

The whole effect drives you backward, disoriented -- the sopping chamber beneath the cataract shudders with each pulse like bad dub through a wall and there's no place to put your weight, nothing to fasten onto as the rock around you stretches and melts and begins to stare back at you with tiny, then larger, blip-eyes and dismissively dangling stems that pull toward your hands.
