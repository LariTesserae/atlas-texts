Deep beneath the surface, a subterranean city hums with activity. Water seeps through the walls, collecting in rivulets and canals. Low-growing, cold-resistant plants cling to the moist stone, illuminated by the dim glow of industrial lights. The air is thick with the smell of machine oil and the din of machinery.

Above, the vaulted ceiling of the cavern disappears into darkness. Mainmast-like columns of stone hold up the weight of the rock above, their granite surfaces slick with condensation and the constant trickle of ancient groundwater through hidden fault lines. Skiffs and lifeboats glide over the canals, transporting goods and people between districts.

The city itself is a tangle of badly-planned, ad-hoc architecture. Steel plate covers most exposed rock, forming a patchwork of features. Some of these plate cover deep-seated caverns, half-filled with the equipment used by early excavators to claw out the town. Cavern levels rise and fall as existing geography is slowly worked over.

In the city proper, air and water recycling processes disguise the fact that no surface exists. Only pigeons and major sewer roaches live here.
