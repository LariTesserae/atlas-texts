In this dense, mist-shrouded valley, the cool, damp air is thick with moisture, and the lush vegetation grows as if from the very rocks themselves. Water is everywhere – trickling from moss-covered boulders, dripping from the tips of ferns, and pooling in the tiny, natural basins that pock the valley floor. The valley is small, perhaps a few hundred feet across, but the sheer density of life within it makes it feel crowded and overwhelming. Towering trees, their trunks as wide as houses, stretch towards the sky like columns, their canopies a vibrant green and so dense that only dappled, emerald light filters down to the forest floor. The air is cool and damp, with a temperature that's always just on the edge of cold, like the gentle chill of early morning. The elevation is high, but the valley is nestled within a ring of hills, so the sense of height is more a feeling than a fact. The landscape is ancient, worn smooth by time and weather – the rocks are rounded, the soil is deep and rich, and the trees have grown and died and grown again for centuries. The only sounds are the soft rustle of leaves, the gentle burble of water, and the distant, mournful calls of unseen birds. Despite the density of life, the valley feels frozen in time, unchanging and eternal.
