This place is an oasis in the early stages of its own development, sequestered in the heart of a vast, arid expanse. The elevation is low, nestled in a shallow trough or impression in the vast, flat plain. Around the oasis, the earth is raw and unfortified, with the sharp, angular edges of freshly exposed rock, still cool to the touch and unweathered. It's as if the earth had parted to reveal a crypt or a smaller, blue-green world, gleaming damply in the understated light that filters through a ceiling of dense leafage.

The walls of this small depression are a deep shade of ochreous brown, nearly free of plant growth. There is an occasional glistening mineral seam, and multiple interconnected veins of purest blue water that can be glimpsed as they penetrate the rock and feed into the pond at the basin's heart.

In this circle of water, life is in abundance, with species not often found in close proximity living together in overlapping, adapting communities. Trees of many kinds reach for what light filters through the canopy above, their barks rough and gnarled, branches twisted and bankrupt. Underneath, smaller shrubs and herbaceous plants vie for space and sustenance, their foliage thick and vari-coloured. The air is full of the sounds of countless singing birds, the livelier chatter of monkeys in the upper tiers of the forest, and the ceaseless buzzing of insects.

Despite the density of life, the place has a properly damp air, a mixture of the humidity from the deep well that waters it and the protective canopy that diminishes the sun's reach. Animals lap from the pool's edge or untiringly dip buckets into its calmness to bring back to shrub and sapling. Life here is active, and there's a sense of continuous movement, of living and growing, all day and late into the thick, heavy twilight. The quiet sounds fill the air, a symphony of nature woven together as an ecosystem flourishes in deep concentration. This small, embryonic world, utterly separate from the desolation that encases it, luminesces with life.
