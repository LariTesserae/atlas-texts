The place is a small, lush island city, perhaps a kilometer across, nestled among the slow-moving branches of a wide, cool river. The soil is damp but never waterlogged, and the air is filled with the songs of birds and the chatter of small mammals. The island itself is young, geologically speaking - the rock faces are sharp and unweathered, and in some places the sound of gently dripping water echoes up from hidden caverns. Trees and vines have already begun to claim the island, however, and a dense canopy casts dappled shadows over the empty, unfinished streets.

Scattered throughout the island are the half-built skeletons of buildings - steel beams and concrete pillars, covered in a tangle of climbing plants and flowering vines. It's clear that construction was abandoned some time ago, perhaps as the result of some disaster or economic downturn. Despite this, the city feels...unabandoned. There are signs of recent habitation everywhere - a melted puddle of solder on a windowsill, a discarded tool lying in the dust, a freshly-painted wall.

The city is built on a series of narrow, switchbacking streets that wind between the buildings and up the steep sides of the island. At the very top, a single, monolithic structure rises - a huge, pyramidal skyscraper with a peaked roof and narrow, slitted windows. The building seems to loom over the rest of the city, casting a long shadow down across the trees and streets.

Despite the feeling of abandonment, the city is filled with the sounds of life. Birds sing and chatter in the trees, and the rustling of small animals through the underbrush is constant. Water drips and trickles from hidden sources, echoing off the steel and concrete. And, faintly, there's the distant rumble of engines - a low, steady thrum that vibrates through the ground and air. It's hard to say where the sound is coming from, but it seems to be...getting closer?
