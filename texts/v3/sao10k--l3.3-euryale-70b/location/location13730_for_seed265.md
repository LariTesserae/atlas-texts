This landscape lies in the heart of an vast delta sprawling from the mouth of a wide, slow river. The terrain is a labyrinthine mixture of mudflats, wetlands, and meandering water courses—the largest channels several hundred feet wide, others mere trickles across the flat, cracked humus. While the ground is prone to endless flat intervals, irregular risings of the ground form elongated hummocks that blink above the maze of waters—dark shapes that lack steadfast island outlines.

Under dense fog like an 1800s city's smoke, black mold shoots in fluffy growths from wooden beam ends and rusting ferrous parts, then blotted splatters and congealed drips of putrid brown paint on boat carcasses, docks, pilings, and mud stumps. Currents lift and set down aging oil tankers at practically any moment, which ooze cloying, opaque glazed tendrils of lickerish cling traveling ashore with wretched fauna; swags and sticky catches on pitted wood, welting oil rots from wheeling biota sheltering in cankered engine towers, brake gear cowls.
