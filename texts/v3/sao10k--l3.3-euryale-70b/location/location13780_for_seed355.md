The air swirled with an otherworldly energy, causing ripples in the water that covered most of the ground. The water's surface was broken only by clusters of twisted, nightmare trees and ancient, worn-down buildings. The city's ruins had long since been taken over; wild shoots and twisted vines crawled up walls, bursting through windows and doorways. Vines thickened to become aerial roots that looped down from treetops.

Trees grew on water's edge, and creepers dove into its glassy, dark surface. They grew down into the water in heavy coils, as if trying to siphon something from depths darker than the mere few inches.

No animal sounds echoed through this delta; everything watching, hearing, or sensing was submerged, swimming, lounging on bare tree branches, or coiled at the top of the green tentacles. Walls bulged; glass shattered where roots had pulled and split the panes. 

Still attempts were fading: broken sprinkler timers sprayed fetid water from rusting hoses into drying gardens; feral cats stalked through narrow streets between houses. Warm mists that rose from the water began to form clouds that lightened the darkness, only to dissolve and spread back across the sky, driving a languid, idyllic haze into the city.

The noise of gravity. It didn't count. Nothing counted here.
