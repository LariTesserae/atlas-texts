With parameters such as these, I am drawn to envisioning a dense, urban subaquatic city. The city sprawls across the cold, dark seafloor where towering city blocks are crafted from coral, shells, and the bones of long-forgotten sea creatures. Despite the chill, the water is thick with life. Bioluminescent algae, glowing jellyfish, and a variety of glowing plankton light up the pool, providing the dim luminescence that acts as our daylight in this setting.

Vegetation here is sparse but unique. Large kelp forests sway gently in the current, their massive fronds swaying back and forth. There may be sparse patches of sea fans and soft corals at the bases of some of the segments.

The smell of sea water and salt is strong, and seaweed lingers with a freshness only the sea can bring. Life abounds. Animals, from tiny fish to massive deep-sea creatures, swim through the decaying infrastructure. Predator and prey translate nature's law into a way of life.

 The absence of light change imparts a strange night/day cycle governed by the sexual reproductive cycles of light emitting fish. As opposed to true daylight this keeps the setting strange.

  No shadows exist. Other living thing illuminate their terrains. 

If shadows occurred this would change everything as the 1.8 weirdness factor can only force new discoveries from beauty. It would become status quo. 

Quietly sounding calls from animals abound, crushing any scarcity for sound as life develops here now.
