Mist-shrouded peaks rise into a glowering, low-hanging sky, their slopes clothed in a tattered mantle of mosses and lichens. The air is thick and damp, the breath of the earth itself, exhaled in a perpetual sigh that rustles through the sparse, twisted trees that cling to the rocky terrain.

Underfoot, the ground is spongy and treacherous, a patchwork of shallow pools and grasping tangles of roots and rhizomes. Rivulets of water seep from fissures in the stone, coalescing into meandering streams that wend their way between ancient boulders, their surfaces mirror-still and opaque, reflecting the diffuse, watery light.

The silence is deafening, broken only by the occasional croak of an unseen creature or the mournful, echoing call of a far-off bird. The very landscape seems to vibrate with a primal, alien energy, as if the earth itself is alive and aware, watching the rare intruder with a mixture of curiosity and wariness.

The buildings that dot the terrain are few and far between, crude structures of weathered wood and moss-covered stone, their roofs thatched with dead grasses. Wisps of smoke curl from a few chimneys, hinting at the presence of a small, hardy population eking out an existence in this inhospitable realm.

The overall impression is one of a world in a state of perpetual, slow-motion flux – a place where time seems to move at a different pace, where the line between the living and the inanimate is blurred, and where the natural and the supernatural coexist in a delicate, uneasy balance.
