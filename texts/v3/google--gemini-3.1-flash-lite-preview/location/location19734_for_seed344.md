The basin is an intimate, steam-choked cradle, no larger than a cathedral nave. It is a depression in the earth where the heat—a steady, sweltering 2.7—rises not from the sun, but from the sweating, dark-glass rock that forms the floor.

Everything here is perpetually wet. Water does not flow in streams; it beads. It wicks through the porous, obsidian-like stone and beads on every surface, heavy droplets that swell until they fall, not with a splash, but with a rhythmic, metallic *ping*. These droplets are mineral-rich, tasting of ozone and copper, suggesting a geography that is very young, still birthing its own topography from the raw, cooling mantle of the planet.

There is no rust. The environment is too new for the slow decay of oxidation, though there is a heavy, industrial presence. In the center of this moisture-trap stands a single, waist-high engine—a block of matte-grey alloy bristling with heat-sinks that hum at a frequency that vibrates the very marrow of your teeth. This is the source of the sound: a deep, pressurized breathing, a rhythmic *hiss-thrum* that never alters its pace. It is a slow, patient machine, seemingly tasked with nothing more than enduring its own heat.

Across the basin, the vegetation is an afterthought. A few pale, translucent filaments—scarcely resembling plants—cling to the rock, spiraling upward toward the shimmering air. They do not photosynthesize; they seem to harvest the electricity vibrating in the atmosphere, their fronds twitching in mechanical synchronicity with the central engine.

There is a sense of utter isolation here. No birds, no insects, no movement save for the slow drip of condensing water and the persistent vibration of the machine. The air is thick, like a warm blanket, magnifying every tiny sound until the falling water droplets sound like gavel strikes against a drum.

Embedded in the wall of the basin, half-buried under a flow of cooled, glassy silt, are the remains of a conduit—a series of brass-colored pipes that vanish into the earth’s crust. They are faint, half-formed, a promise of a factory that was never fully realized, or perhaps one that has retreated entirely into the stone.

The lighting is flat and diffused, an even, pale grey that lacks a clear source, casting no shadows. It is as if the space itself is the light. You feel the weight of the heat pushing against your skin, a damp pressure that makes it difficult to breathe, while the steady, low-frequency roar of the engine occupies the world entirely. Time here is not measured by the sun or the seasons, but by the accumulation of beads of water on the obsidian floor, a process so slow it seems to stand still. It is a world sealed, self-sufficient, and entirely indifferent to the existence of anything outside its humid, vibrating rim.
