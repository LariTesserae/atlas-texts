The city of Orem-Vane does not sit upon the earth; it is extruded from it, a jagged, brutalist extrusion at an altitude where the air is thin enough to hiss. Here, at the roof of the world, the geography is a vertical labyrinth of basaltic needles and raw, unweathered obsidian. There is no soil, no loam, no leaf litter—the ground is a geometric obsession of cooling magma and sharp-edged mineral plates.

The scale is oppressive. Great canyons of shadow run between towers of black, vitrified stone that rise like teeth into the violet-tinged atmosphere. It is a city of high density, where architectural structures are fused to the mountain’s anatomy. The "buildings" are less dwellings and more pressurized, copper-piped housings for the massive, slow-turning turbines that keep this place alive. 

Water is the ghost of this landscape. It never pools in the traditional sense. Instead, the extreme thermal gradients drive a cycle of aggressive condensation. Deep within the porous, volcanic rock, subterranean furnaces flare with a dull, rhythmic thrum, heating galleries of internal fluid. When the external air—chilled by the sheer altitude—meets the sweating, metallic pores of the city’s skin, it creates a constant, weeping mist. Moisture clings to the obsidian surfaces in thick, iridescent beads, shivering in the heat-haze before evaporating into thin, oily clouds. It is a world of dew-sweat and vapor, a humid purgatory carved into the freezing heights. 

The silence here is heavy, cushioned by the density of the infrastructure. The soundscape is dominated by the near-ultrasonic whine of steam escaping through brass relief valves and the heavy clanking of piston-driven platforms that shift with glacial impatience. The air itself tastes of ozone and pulverized feldspar.

There is no biology as we define it. Instead, the landscape is infested with *litho-schematics*—tangled, self-organizing fractal growths that resemble metallic lichen. They are not alive, yet they replicate; they feed on the static electricity generated by the temperature differential, creeping across the obsidian walls in geometric patterns that look like circuit boards. 

Rarer still are the entities that move through the upper gantries. They appear as articulated exoskeletons of polished glass and heated wire, hunching over the machinery with a slow, diligent purpose. They do not speak. They move with the heavy, hydraulic deliberation of machines, pausing for hours to recalibrate a joint or press a seal against a weeping pipe. They are parts of the city’s metabolic system, as much a feature of the terrain as the jagged peaks or the vents.

The light is flat and relentless, pouring through the thin, dry sky to reflect off the obsidian in blinding sheets. The horizon is a jagged puzzle of peaks that extend for leagues, all of them bearing the scars of the same industrial mandate: to burrow, to heat, to vent, to hold. Nothing here is eroding, for nothing is soft enough to yield. The mountain is being hollowed, refined, and electrified, a city-machine that grows only by becoming more compact, more intricate, more trapped between the heat of the core and the thin, hungry expanse of the sky.
