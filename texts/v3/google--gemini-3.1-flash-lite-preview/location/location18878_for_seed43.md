You have descended into the Great Sump of the Sub-strata, a space that feels less like a cavern and more like the inside of a vast, rusted heart. 

The environment is entirely built—there is no bedrock here, only layer upon layer of interlocking metal plates, riveted copper piping, and basalt-heavy masonry that has been worked, worn, and fused together over eons. It is a dense, claustrophobic sphere, perhaps the size of a grand cathedral, where every surface serves a purpose and every angle has been rounded by centuries of friction.

The temperature holds a constant, humid warmth. It is a wet heat—not from an open sky, but from the relentless condensation that beads on the cold-welded walls and drips in rhythmic intervals. Water does not pool; it travels. It threads through a capillary system of etched grooves and narrow, moss-slicked gullies, providing just enough moisture to sustain the sparse, stringy lichen that clings to the iron knuckles of the architecture.

Despite the limited space, the density of this place is overwhelming. It is crowded with "The Tethers"—thick, primitive braids of woven plant fiber and hammered iron wire that suspend secondary platforms and living-hives throughout the vertical void. You cannot move three feet without brushing against a structure or a creature. 

The sound is the true master of the Sump. It is a cacophony of metallic resonance—the rhythmic *clack-hiss-thrum* of water dripping onto thin diaphragms of rusted plating, the incessant scraping of claws against grooved floors, and the high-pitched, harmonic whistles of the *Skitter-fauna*. These creatures—six-legged, chitinous things with bodies shaped like burnished beetle shells—are everywhere, nesting in the crevices of the floor-plates. They are the engine of the ecosystem; they chew the lichen, secrete the binding resins that keep the metal from corroding further, and constantly rearrange the small, loose stones that litter the pathways.

There is no natural horizon, only the "Deep-Light"—an ambient, pale-orange glow emanating from phosphorus-rich algae smeared within the gaps of the flooring. It casts long, flickering shadows that dance across the walls as the various Tethers sway and groan with the internal pressure of the Sub-strata. 

The wear is profound. Everything here has been in this state for generations beyond counting. The metal has been rubbed smooth by millions of tiny feet; the rivets have been polished to a dull silver by the humidity; the entire area feels "settled." It is an island of manual craft, a complex, crowded machine kept in motion by its own biological debris. It is not dying; it is simply finished. It has reached a state of perfect, noisy, humid equilibrium, a self-contained world that exists entirely in the hollows below the surface, oblivious to the silence of the sky.
