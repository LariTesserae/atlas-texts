Deep beneath the sun-bleached crust of the planet, in a pocket of space no larger than a city block, lies the *Cistern-Heart*. It is a subterranean chamber of total construction; there is no bedrock here, only the weathered iron-composite floorplates and the basalt-polymer walls that have held back the crushing weight of the world for eons.

The atmosphere is oppressively hot, a dry, stagnant heat that clings to the skin like a warm shroud. Yet, despite the pervasive aridity, the room holds a phantom dampness. Thick, oily condensation perpetually beads upon the rusted overhead conduits, forming heavy droplets that fall with agonizing infrequency. *Plink. Plink.* The sound is a hollow, metallic chime that travels far in the stillness. When a drop hits a cooling pipe or a patch of jagged, oxidized flooring, it creates a faint hiss of steam, a momentary release of pressure in the silence.

The density of the space is claustrophobic. Every cubic meter is crammed with the hulking, settled forms of ancient heavy machinery—vertical turbines that haven't spun for centuries, tangled nests of copper cabling fused by time, and tiered catwalks that lean against one another like petrified scaffolding. These structures represent a totalizing build; there is nowhere to touch raw earth, nowhere to find a surface that wasn't poured, welded, or bolted into place.

Vegetation here is sparse and alien. It manifests not as growth, but as a slow, mineral bloom. Clusters of translucent, salt-crusted filaments—the color of bruised lichen—climb the cold iron pillars. They do not photosynthesize; they draw their sustenance from the slow weeping of moisture through the joints in the plating and the trace electrical currents that occasionally pulse through the ancient wires, like the firing of nerves in a sleeping beast.

The light is nonexistent, a true, suffocating darkness that doesn't just block vision but seems to press against the eyes. One navigates by the faint, heat-haze distortion of the air near the thermal vents and the occasional, sickly spark of a circuit failing in the deep walls.

Fauna is rare, but present. Small, chitinous things, pale and featureless, skitter along the undersides of the catwalks. They move with an exhausting efficiency, never wasting motion, their bodies perfectly adapted to the narrow, metal-choked corridors. They make no sound. The world is a patient, long-existed machine that has forgotten its original purpose. Whatever massive engine this room once served has long since wound down, leaving only the slow erosion of oxidation and the rhythmic, mocking dripping of condensation to mark the passage of a time measured in centuries rather than hours. 

Everything here is in balance, a closed, manufactured ecosystem of rust and thermal fatigue, waiting in the dark for a signal that will never come, suspended in a state of eternal, sweaty waiting.
