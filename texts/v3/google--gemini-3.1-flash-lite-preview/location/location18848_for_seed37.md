You are standing within a hollowed, crystalline geometry located just beneath the surface crust of a planet that has not yet decided on its final physics. The space is no larger than a cathedral's nave, yet it feels infinite because the walls are semi-permeable membranes of translucent, calcified shale.

The light here is piercing—a cold, rhythmic pulse of white radiance that seems to emanate from the very atoms of the floor. It is bright enough to cast no shadows, washing the world in a clinical, terrifying clarity.

Because the erosion is in its infancy, the terrain is raw. There are no smooth stones or rounded riverbeds here. Instead, there are jagged, geometric protrusions of obsidian-like glass that have only recently shivered into existence. They push upward from the floor like teeth, sharp and unweathered. Between these glassy plates, moisture gathers. It does not flow—it hovers. Tiny, suspended beads of liquid hang in the air at uneven intervals, shivering with a damp, cool weight. Every few minutes, these beads collapse, weeping onto the porous floor in a rhythmic, localized sprinkle that scents the air with the smell of ozone and wet flint.

The "vegetation" is not flora in any terrestrial sense. It consists of pale, calcified threads—thin as spider silk—anchored to the glassy protrusions. They pulse with a faint, rhythmic bioluminescence, slowly unfurling and retracting like the gills of a breathing creature. They are sparse, leaving vast, empty plateaus of gray dust between their clusters.

The weirdness is the dominant force. The laws of causality feel localized, as if they are being negotiated in real-time. If you drop a stone from your hand, it may not fall; it might instead drift upward to join a hovering sphere of water, or simply vanish into a smear of color before reappearing three inches to the left. The space is a laboratory of potentiality.

Despite the silence, the ecosystem is frantically busy. Though you see no traditional animals, the air is thick with "presences"—tiny, translucent geometry-shifters that dart through the light pulses like needlefish. They are the fauna of this place: clusters of sentient math that rearrange themselves to avoid the dropping condensation. There is no predator-prey relationship here; there is only the consumption of *state*. When two clusters meet, they trade information, and one momentarily simplifies into a different form—a circle becoming a cube, a sequence of notes becoming a shade of violet.

Scattered across the floor are the "built" elements: small, obsidian cairns left by some previous, forgotten configuration of the environment. They hold no tools, no mechanical function. They are simply markers, left perhaps to prove that someone—or something—had once observed the geometry before it shifted. They act as grounding rods for the atmosphere, the only objects in the room that do not hum or drift.

It is a place of heavy, cool stillness, where the silence is not an absence of sound, but a compression of too many sounds held in a state of suspended animation. It is a waiting room for the universe, a private chamber beneath the ground where reality is still practicing how to be solid.
