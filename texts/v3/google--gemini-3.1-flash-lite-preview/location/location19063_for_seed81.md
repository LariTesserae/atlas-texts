The city of Ouros-Thrum is not built of stone, but of a calcified, porous resin that clings to the jagged basalt teeth of a high-altitude plateau. Beneath the city, the ground is always weeping. The air is thick, saturated with a heavy, boiling humidity that refuses to dissipate, turning the dim light into a smear of bruised indigo and wet charcoal.

There is no green here. The vegetation is entirely absent, replaced by the persistent, slow-growing growth of silicate corals that bloom in the crevices of the industrial spires. These mineral growths are not biological; they are the byproduct of the atmospheric condensation—salty, tepid water leaching through the porous masonry, depositing layers of crystalline white salt that pulse with a faint, rhythmic heat.

The architecture is layered like the rings of a tree, though it operates like an engine. Great, sluggish brass-cast pipes run between the buildings, humming with a low-frequency vibration that mimics the sound of a distant, trapped ocean. Steam vents—the only clear indicator of the city’s industrial core—spit plumes of scalding mist into the stagnant air. These plumes form dense, rolling clouds that hang at chest height, obscuring the foundations of the city and making the walkways feel as though they are floating in an endless, vapid void.

One can walk for hours through the winding corridors and vaulted arcades of Ouros-Thrum and encounter absolutely nothing. The density is near zero—a vast, hollow urbanity designed for a population that left, or perhaps never arrived. The infrastructure is perfectly maintained by autonomous, steam-driven pistons that reset every few decades, grinding with the heavy, patient patience of tectonic plates. There is no decay here, only a perpetual state of "settled existence."

The heat is aggressive, a dry-wet pressure that sits on the lungs. It is the heat of a deep-earth vent trapped under a glass dome. Because the light is so dim—barely enough to distinguish the wet-slicked metallic floor from the dark vents—one navigates largely by the temperature gradients and the sound of dripping.

Occasionally, there is a flicker of motion. Not a creature, but a flicker of *intent*. Thin, wire-like filaments of copper and glass drift through the humid air—unbound, erratic energy patterns that function like scavenger organisms. They hover near the sweating pipes, drinking the charge from the surface tension of the droplets, then dissolve back into the mist. They are silent, save for a fleeting, high-pitched static whine that cuts through the otherwise muffled thrum of the city.

The rules of this world are simple: keep moving to avoid the mineral salts that seek to seal the joints of one's limbs, and avoid the open chasms where the foundation ends. The plateau drops off into a cloud sea that holds the entire city in a suspended, hot, and lonely balance. It is a city of machines designed to sustain a biosphere that never took root, a high-altitude crucible where the industry continues to churn, producing nothing but water, silence, and the slow crystallization of time.
