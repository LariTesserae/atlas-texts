This is an engine-heart, a place smaller than a human palm, yet dense as a collapsing star. It is contained within a shielded, microscopic vacuum chamber, buried deep in the heart of a dead planet’s mantle. Because the scale is so intimate—a pocket-universe of gears and pressure—it is invisible to the outside world, yet it vibrates with the force of a tectonic plate.

There is no sky here, only the red-gold glow of friction. The terrain is not rock, but a tightly packed conglomerate of self-replicating carbon-nanotube lattices and raw, metallic slag. It is a world of pure, unrefined processing. Because the density is so high, there is no empty space. Every cubic millimeter is occupied by grinding, rotating, or vibrating crystalline nodules that act as the ecosystem's fauna.

These "creatures"—if one can call biological-analogous energy patterns—are jagged, brass-colored polyhedrons. They crawl through the pressurized matrix by shearing away thin, metallic tectonic layers of the environment. Their movement is loud, a constant, high-frequency grinding of metal against mineral that resonates through the entire structure. They feed on the relentless heat generated by the chamber’s internal "industrial" cycles—a series of unseen, automated pressure pulses that rhythmically squeeze the landscape, forcing the atoms into denser, more orderly configurations.

Water is a historical impossibility here. The heat, hovering perpetually near the point of sublimation, ensures that any moisture would be instantly stripped into elemental plasma. The ground is dry, brittle, and hot enough to singe the very reality of a viewer who could look closely enough. 

Because the erosion is in its infancy, the world is sharp, aggressive, and geometrically violent. There are no smooth planes, no gentle valleys, no soft curves. Everything is angular, unfinished, and in a state of rapid, violent coming-into-being. The "industrial" label is literal: the entire environment functions as a giant, mindless factory that produces nothing but *more of itself*. The crystalline fauna are the workers, the moving parts, and the product all at once.

Light is nearly absent; the environment is shrouded in the deep, opaque gloom of its own internal density. The only illumination comes from the "spark-faults"—brief, white-hot fissures that tear open in the bedrock when the pressure of the density shifts a fraction of a millimeter. These lightning-arcs last for a blink, illuminating the chaotic, packed, grinding mass of the landscape before plunging it back into the heavy, suffocating darkness.

Everything is slow. Despite the loudness and the intense heat, the "progress" of this world takes aeons. The grinding of one gear-tooth against another moves at the speed of continental drift. It is a patient, agonizingly steady construction project that has been refining its own complexity for millions of years in the dark. It is a landscape that is suffocatingly full, a claustrophobic machine-cradle humming with the immense, wasted potential of its own internal furnace.
