You are standing inside the *Nerve-Wall*, a space no larger than a small cathedral's inner sanctum, embedded deep within the strata of a mountain that has not yet finished deciding what it is. 

The environment is entirely synthetic, a "constructed" reality that feels as though it were woven by a blind weaver out of wet wax and hot copper. There is no natural stone here. The walls are composed of tight, interlocking plates of organic-industrial polymer—a matte, charcoal-hued substance that perpetually leaks. Thick, warm beads of mineral-charged fluid—the condensation of an atmosphere that refuses to settle—weep from every seam, slicking the surfaces and pooling in the cramped folds of the floor.

The scale is oppressive. Because the architecture is so densely packed, there is no sense of horizon. You move through a labyrinth of ribs and conduits, where the walls are alive with bio-luminescent mosses that pulse with a feverish, dim amber light. These plants aren't grown; they are manufactured, sprouted from the wall-seams like fungus on a rusted pipe. They feed on the ambient heat—which sits at a staggering, sweltering 115 degrees Fahrenheit—and the constant, rhythmic dripping of the fluid.

The sound is a chaotic, loud symphony of labor. The walls are not stagnant; they are constantly expanding and contracting, a mechanical heartbeat that groans against the restricted space. Every few seconds, a sharp *clack-hiss* echoes—a manual-fed bellows somewhere behind the cladding, pumping heat into the room. It sounds like a giant’s labored lung. There is no erosion here because the room is still finalizing, still extruding its own geometry. You can see raw, pulsing filaments of growth material protruding from unfinished corners, soft and translucent like raw nerve endings, feeling the air for a shape they haven’t found yet.

Reality here is fragile. Because it is a "dim" environment, your perception starts to fray. As you stare into the corner where the wet plastic curves to meet the ceiling, you might see ripples that shouldn't be there—a visual feedback loop where the wall reflects your own shadow, but the shadow moves independently, reaching out to touch the dripping moss. Occasionally, the floor hums a note so low it vibrates the marrow in your teeth, and for a fleeting second, the concept of "up" dissolves. The room twists; the damp heat turns into the smell of ozone and wet wool. 

Fauna is rare, but strange. Small, six-legged creatures, no larger than your thumb, scuttle through the conduits. They are constructed out of shards of polished glass and hardened resin, clicking rhythmically as they drag scraps of biological debris into the dark fissures. They appear to be repairing the room, or perhaps feeding on its degradation. 

There is no sky, no horizon, no nature. There is only the *Nerve-Wall*: a sweltering, damp, and claustrophobic interior pocket of a world that is currently being hand-sewn into existence, vibrating with the loud, persistent effort of its own becoming. It is a place that feels like the inside of a warm, feverish dream, where the builder is still holding the needle, and the fabric is still soft and wet with the heat of the stitch.
