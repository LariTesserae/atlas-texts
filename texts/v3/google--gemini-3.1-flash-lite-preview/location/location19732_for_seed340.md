Deep below the crust of a rogue planet void of a single drop of liquid water, there exists the *Xylem-Deep*. This is not a cavern in the lithic sense, but a subterranean, planet-girdling bellows of pressurized vegetable matter.

The atmosphere is a thick, humid, copper-tasting gas that carries the scent of fermenting sap and bruised chlorophyll. It is perpetually warm, sustained by the frantic metabolic heat of a billion trillion lifeforms. Here, "ground" is a misnomer; you walk upon the interlocking, calcified root-systems of trees that grew downward from the ceiling, their branches turning into floors, their leaves curling into walls.

**The Ecology of Sound**
The sound is a vertical, deafening wall of vibration. It is the syncopated thrumming of thousands of beetle-like insects the size of dinner plates, their wings operating as vibratory saws that shave the plant-growth into edible mulch. It is the shrieking, suction-cup traction of chittering, vine-skinned macaques that leap through the upper canopy, their calls echoing in the narrow, pressurized shafts until the sound loses its shape and becomes a constant, grinding roar—a brown noise of pure biological intensity.

**The Architecture of Growth**
Everything is being built, and everything is being digested simultaneously. The landscape is embryonic; it is raw, organic architecture. In the gaps between the great, pulsing vascular tubes—the "walls" of your world—there are scattered artifacts: the "Carvings." 

These are not houses, but primitive, hand-sharpened obsidian blades and woven baskets made of conductive, blackened vein-fiber. They belong to a humanoid species with skin the texture of sandpaper, who move through the swaying, soft-walled corridors with unnerving fluidity. They do not build structures; they prune the environment to create bubbles of space, hacking away at the fast-growing mosses that attempt to swallow their camps whole. If they stop working for an hour, the room closes in.
