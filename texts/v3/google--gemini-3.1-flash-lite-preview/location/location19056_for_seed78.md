This is the *Spore-Crown Vault*, a continental-scale vertical sprawl that exists not on a planet’s surface, but within the gargantuan, hollowed-out ribcage of a deceased stellar titan—a skeleton of white, calcified arches that curve against the backdrop of an eternal, freezing void.

The elevation is dizzying. One can look down for miles through a thin, frigid mist to see the dark, shifting roots of the ecosystem far below, or look up to see the sky-arch—calcified bone white against the absolute black of space. 

**The Vegetation as Infrastructure**
There is no soil here. The entire world is composed of *Lignum-Gelid*, a gargantuan, grey-blue fungal architecture. It is a total-living-mass ecosystem where the trees are not rooted in earth but are draped, woven, and calcified into the rib-bones of the Titan. These plants move. They are slow, pulsing, rhythmic; they breathe out clouds of bioluminescent spores that serve as the primary source of light, a cold, sour-green glimmer that does nothing to warm the air. Thick, rubbery vines as wide as rivers act as bridges across the abyss, pulsating as they transport internal fluids, making the ground feel like a living throat that never stops swallowing.

**The Cold and the Moisture**
The temperature is biting, clinging to the marrow. Because there is no sun, moisture does not evaporate; it liquifies on the undersides of the great fungal leaves and drips rhythmically into the dark void below. This constant, rhythmic *tink-tink-drip* creates a soundscape of immense scale. Because the environment is so vast and the air is so thin (near-void density), the sound doesn't dissipate; it reverberates. A single drop falling from a mile-high frond echoes with the intensity of a mallet on a gong, a cascading acoustic violence that mimics the heartbeat of the dead world.

**The Inhabitants**
The fauna are abundant but scattered, lonely creatures. Translucent, six-limbed leapers with skin like wet parchment cling to the fungal walls, their bodies generating internal heat through high-metabolism tremors. They are preyed upon by "Wind-Anchors," organisms that look like living kites, tethered to the fungal structure by long, fibrous nerves that allow them to drift deep into the hollow space and snap back to the vegetation to feed.

**The Residuals**
Amidst this, there are the ruins. These are not cities, but *way-markers*—scratched, primitive totems made of scavenged bone and resin, standing on the edge of massive fungal shelves. These markers suggest the presence of a humanoid species, one that survives through extreme patience. You might find a single, crudely carved obsidian blade left on a ledge, or a coil of woven vine-rope, but you will rarely see the souls who left them. They are ghost-like, moving in the darkness, clad in the thick, leathery hides of the leapers to survive the sub-zero wind.

**The Physics of the Void**
Because the density is so low, objects do not fall in the traditional sense. They drift. A discarded tool might hover in the air for a week before the shifting currents of the spores finally draw it down into the roots. The ecosystem is in constant, aggressive motion. The fungal architecture is constantly growing to close the gaps in the bone-structure, and then dying back, creating a cycle of creation and decay that happens in the silence of the dark.

It is a world of echoing stillness and sudden, violent noise—a place where you are constantly reminded that you are standing on the internal surface of something that was once alive, and is now, in its own slow, vegetal way, reclaiming its own ghost.
