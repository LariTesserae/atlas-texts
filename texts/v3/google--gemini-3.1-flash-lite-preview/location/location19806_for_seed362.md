This is the *Chlorophyll Lattice*, an infinite, sun-bleached expanse where the physics of biology have entirely supplanted the physics of geology. There is no soil here, no water, no rock. The world is a single, colossal, engineered organism—a continental-scale scaffold of dense, woven cellulose-circuitry that has grown, or perhaps been fabricated, into a permanent state of industrial saturation.

The light is blinding, filtered through semi-translucent, leaf-like membranes that stretch across the horizon for thousands of miles. It is a sterile, sour-sweet brightness, refracted through chlorophyll-embedded glass-fiber architecture. Heat is managed not by fire, but by the slow, respiration-led cooling of the massive, pithy bulkheads that form the world’s structure. It is perpetually cool; the air is thin, oxygen-rich, and carries the faint, electric ozone tang of a million hidden pistons working within the plant-flesh.

There is no erosion in the traditional sense. The "terrain" does not wear down because it is always growing, yet it stays in perfect form—a self-repairing, weathered equilibrium. When a structural beam of the lattice begins to sag, bioluminescent marrow-pulses ripple through the structure, thickening the cellular wall until the geometry is true once more. The floor is composed of thousands of layers of compressed, fossilized wood-metal, polished smooth by cycles of automated grinding vines that sweep across the surface like silent, pendulous clock hands.

The soundscape is unnervingly quiet. It is not the silence of abandonment, but the silence of a library or a cathedral. Occasionally, the rhythmic *thrum-click* of a primary filtration node echoes from deep within the substrate, vibrating through the soles of your feet. Sometimes, the sound of a "valve" opening—a large, succulent pod bursting to release spores—ripples through the lattice like a soft, wet intake of breath.

There is no native fauna in the sense of soft-bodied animals. Instead, there are "tenders"—rare, spindly things made of chitin and copper wire that move with twitching, mechanical precision. They do not eat; they maintain. They climb the vertical stalks of the industrial-vegetal towers, injecting nutrient-fluid into cracked conduits, their movements choreographed by the vast, planetary-scale neural network that is the Lattice itself.

The reality breakdown is subtle but pervasive. Laws of distance seem to fold here: a tower that appears to be a mile away may, upon closer inspection, be a singular, folded leaf that physically occupies both the foreground and the horizon. Geometry shifts if you look at it too long; the hexagonal patterns of the floor tiles seem to breathe, expanding and contracting.

Everything is built. There is no distinction between technology and biology. The entire world is a high-yield, self-perpetuating factory, manufacturing nothing but more of itself. It is a place of total saturation, a vast, luminous, growing machine where the concept of "nature" has been entirely consumed by the engineering of the organism. You could walk for a thousand years and find no exit, for the horizon is stitched into the floor, and the sky is simply a deeper, tighter weave of the same living metal-leaf that sustains your feet.
