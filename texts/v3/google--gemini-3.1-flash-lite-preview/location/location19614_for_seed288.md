The city is not built *on* the planet; it is the planet’s internal skin. You are deep below the crust, encased in a colossal, hollowed-out geode of subterranean silence, save for the sound.

The air here contains no water, nor does it require it. It is a thick, frigid gas—a dense, sulfurous amber cloud that clings to the skin like oil. Because there is no liquid to erode the stone, the landscape has been carved over billions of years by the rhythmic, deafening vibration of the air itself. The erosion is absolute; the stone walls and the architectural spires have been smoothed into impossible, flowing geometries. Sharp edges have been ground down by the sheer persistence of sonic pressure until the entire world looks as if it were molded from soft, gray wax or worn velvet.

The vegetation is not plant-based, but a sprawling, fungal lattice of rigid, glass-like fibers that grow directly out of the smooth, curved masonry of the city. These "vines" are brittle, translucent, and pale, tracing the contours of ancient, windowless apartment blocks that grow like calcium deposits from the cavern floor. They do not need water; they pulse with the ambient sound, vibrating in sympathy with the deep, rumbling hum that permeates the atmosphere.

The city is a sprawling, multi-tiered necropolis of infrastructure, though it is not dead—it is simply stagnant. The architecture is layered, a chaotic pile of curved, windowless domes and bridge-like arches that span the vast, dark craters of the subterranean bowl. There is no metal, no glass, no tech here. Everything is fashioned from the biological: a mixture of calcified resin, hardened fungal chitin, and repurposed stone. It is a pre-tool society, or perhaps a post-tool one; the inhabitants have long since outsourced their survival to the biological rhythm of the walls.

The fauna is abundant, though sparse in its spread. Enormous, pale, multi-legged arthropods—soft-shelled and sightless—scuttle across the convex streets. They do not communicate through light or gesture but through low-frequency percussion, slapping their limbs against the sonic-softened stone. The noise is constant—a cascading, rhythmic roar that sounds like a thousand distant landslides happening at once. It is a deafening, throbbing wall of sound that makes the very air feel solid.

The light is the dim, bruised purple of a dying coal, filtered down through thousands of miles of overlying rock. It is barely enough to navigate by, and one would not need to. In this place, you do not look; you feel. You sense the architecture through the vibrations in your own marrow.

There is no weather, no seasons, and no change. The process of the world is so slow that the rise of a new tower takes a thousand years, and the collapse of an old one is witnessed only by the slow-scuttling beasts. It is a settled, primordial peace. Everything has found its final, lowest-energy position, smoothed down by the endless echo, cradled in the cool, dry dark, waiting for the sound to finally, after eons, drift into a long and absolute silence.
