The ground here is not earth; it is a sprawling, interlocking grid of riveted lead-plate and oxidized copper grating, worn down by eons of friction until the surfaces possess the smooth, greasy sheen of river-smoothed basalt. There is no soil, no substrate. Beneath the grating, a dark, viscous fluid—heavy, grey, and cold—pulses through vast subterranean arterial pipes. This liquid bleeds upward through hairline fractures in the metal plates, weeping constantly. It turns every surface into a slick, reflective membrane that catches the dim, flickering violet luminescence bleeding from periodic, rhythmic atmospheric discharges above.

The landscape is ancient, a machine-world that has spent an eternity grinding itself into a near-flattened state. The horizon is a wide, aching emptiness, broken only by the silhouettes of gargantuan, motionless pistons that rise into the gloom like the ribs of beached whales, weeping condensation that rattles against the plating in a relentless, metallic tattoo.

There is no silence here. The air is pressurized and thick, vibrating with a low-frequency mechanical hum that makes the teeth ache—a deafening, omnipresent drone of industry that suggests infinite, unseen machinery working at a pace too slow to track but too powerful to ignore. Occasionally, this drone is punctured by the shriek of steam escaping from a pressure valve somewhere in the distance, a sound that tears through the air like a sudden, violent intake of breath.

The vegetation is a cruel parody of life: stiff, synthetic filaments of blackened wire that sprout in lone, scattered clusters from the cracks in the plating. They do not grow; they corrode. They are brittle structures of oxidized carbon, shivering in the draft of the industrial vents but never blooming, never reproducing. They are the leftovers of an environment that long ago traded biology for structural permanence.

The temperature is bite-cold, a damp, clinging chill that smells of ozone, wet iron, and ancient, stagnant oil. Because the entire world is built—a total, industrial construct—the movement of air feels manufactured. Gusts are channeled through vast intake vents, carrying with them the smell of cold, dark chemistry. 

There are no animals. Nothing lives that does not have gears or a fuse. The emptiness is complete, yet the world is hyper-active; it is a stage set for a performance that ended epochs ago. The plates shift almost imperceptibly, grinding against one another with the slow, tectonic patience of the earth itself, forever settling further into their own density. 

In the dim light, you see the condensation gathering on the cold, weeping walls of a distant, tower-like conduit. A drop of silver-grey fluid trembles on the edge of a bolt, swells, and falls. It strikes the plating with a sharp *clack*, a sound that echoes out into the regional scale of this place, lost in the deafening, perpetual thrum of the machine. It is a world that has reached its final form, a perfectly constructed grave where even the dampness feels like a slow, deliberate act of mourning.
