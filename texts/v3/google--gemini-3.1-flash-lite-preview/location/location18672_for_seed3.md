The city of Ostral-hollow is not built upon the mountain; it is sutured into the throat of a jagged, sky-piercing spire, ten thousand feet above the frost-line. Here, gravity feels like an afterthought, a loose suggestion that the planet makes to the heavy iron struts of the architecture.

The terrain is embryonic and raw—massive, basaltic ribs of the earth have been sliced open by some ancient, tectonic hunger. The rock is still bleeding cooling magma in hidden fissures, and the stone feels soft, almost porous, as if it hasn’t quite decided to be granite yet. Because of this, the urban sprawl grows like a metallic mold. Massive, rusted girders and brass-cased pipes cling to the cliffside, spiraling into the abyss. There is no flat ground, only vertical boulevards and suspension-bridges that sway in a wind that seems to possess a mind of its own.

The atmosphere is perpetually damp, not with rain, but with a heavy, clinging condensation that drips from the underside of every catwalk and industrial venting-port. It is a cool, silver-gray mist that swallows the horizon, making the city feel like a series of disjointed islands floating in a featureless void.

The industrial pulse of Ostral-hollow is deafening. Massive atmospheric pumps, rhythmic and slow, groan with the weight of centuries, pulling oxygen from the thin air to feed the bioluminescent moss that chokes the pipes. This vegetation is lush, despite the cold. Thick, translucent vines with rubbery leaves wrap around the pressure gauges, pulsing with a rhythmic, greenish light that mimics the heartbeat of the machines.

This is a world of reality-breakdown. If you stand too long on one of the outer platforms, you might notice that the architecture repeats itself infinitely—a staircase that leads back to its own starting landing, or a chimney that vents smoke downward into a sky that hasn't finished forming. There are "echo-pockets" where the sound of a falling wrench repeats for three days, or where the condensation turns into millions of tiny, fluttering glass moths—the local fauna. These creatures, spindly and clockwork in appearance, swarm the vents, their metallic wings clicking in a frantic, syncopated rhythm that is the only music this city possesses.

The density is sparse, despite the urban scale. Enormous chasms of open air sit between the neighborhoods, and the residents—humanoid shadows draped in heavy, oil-stained wool—rarely cross these gaps. They are patient, moving with a sluggish, viscous grace. A transit between two structures might take hours, not because of the distance, but because the space between them tends to stretch at will, obeying the erratic whim of the mountain's geometry.

Everything here is under construction, yet nothing is ever completed. Rivets are hammered into place by steam-driven pistons that haven't been oiled in a hundred years. The landscape is a sprawling, unfinished thought, an industrial cathedral built in a place where physics is just one of many options for how things might behave. When a pipe bursts, it doesn't spray water; it leaks a viscous, iridescent liquid that holds the memory of a storm that happened ten miles away. You can hear it as it hits the metal grating—a sound like a thousand distant bells tolling in a mountain pass that no longer exists.
