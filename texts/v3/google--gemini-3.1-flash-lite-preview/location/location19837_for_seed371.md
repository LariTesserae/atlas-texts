The world here is not land in the traditional sense, but a vertical, jagged throat of obsidian and basalt, thrust upward by immense subterranean pressure into the teeth of a frigid, lightless altitude. 

It is a place of screaming, grinding physics. The "industry" here is geological and atmospheric: a self-churning, high-pressure engine that mimics the heavy thrum of a localized factory, though no hand has ever touched it. The terrain is defined by extreme young-earth brutality. Sharp, triangular shards of rock, as thin and abrasive as razor-sheaves, protrude from the vertical faces at impossible angles, never smoothed by the slow grace of time. They are fresh, broken, and dangerously hungry.

Despite the darkness—a heavy, absolute gloom that swallows the eye—the space is lush. It is a biological density that defies the lack of sunlight. Clinging to the razor-edged basalt are mats of phosphorescent moss-pelts—thick, velvet, emerald-dark tapestries that absorb the ambient noise of the chasm and turn it into kinetic vibration. These plants have adapted to the turbulence; they do not grow *on* the rocks so much as they *weave* into the microscopic fissures, pulling moisture from the freezing, condensation-heavy air that clings to the cliffs in greasy, grey patches. 

The sound is relentless. It is a hydraulic roar, a mechanical cacophony of stone-against-stone. Because the elevation is so extreme and the space so constrained—a vertical throat hardly wider than a cathedral nave—the acoustics are hyper-amplified. It sounds like a hundred stamp-mills cycling in the dark. Wind, compressed by the narrow jagged geography, whistles through the stone-teeth with a shrieking, metallic quality. 

There is no sky here, only the constant, violent recycling of the atmosphere. The moisture is a temperamental resource. One moment, the air is dry and static-charged, smelling of ozone and pulverized quartz; the next, a freezing, microscopic rain begins to weep from the upper reaches, slicking the mossy walls until they gleam like oil-touched hides in the dimness. 

What makes this place truly industrial is the rhythm. It pulses. The landscape is in a state of perpetual, hyperactive formation; rocks shift, shards collide, and entire sections of the moss-covered wall buckle and renew in seconds. It is a factory of landscape, producing new geometry with a turbulent, anthill-energy. There is no fauna—nothing breathes, nothing crawls, nothing hunts—because the environment itself is the hunter. The walls are closing in, vibrating with the power of a piston, a landscape that is less a location and more a machine made of living, thrashing, and growing stone.
