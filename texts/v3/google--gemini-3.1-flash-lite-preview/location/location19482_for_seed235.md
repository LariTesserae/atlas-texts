The city of Oros-Vea perches upon the jagged, impossibly vertical spine of a world-crust that should have long ago collapsed under its own altitude. It is a metropolis of living, breathing masonry, sprawling across horizontal shelves carved into basalt cliffs that pierce the perpetual, low-hanging stratocumulus clouds.

Here, the heat—a thick, wet oppression—clings to the skin like a warm, salt-heavy shroud. It is a place of constant condensation. Water does not merely rain here; the atmosphere itself is a saturated sponge. Every surface, whether of stone or leaf, is perpetually weeping. Translucent ribbons of water pour from the high-canopied terraces, falling hundreds of feet through the misty air before catching on the protruding balconies of the lower tiers. 

The architecture is entirely botanical, yet undeniably urban. Massive, calcified trunk-pillars serve as load-bearing columns for vaulted homes grown from silicate-rich creepers. The structures are dense, layered like an architectural honeycomb, winding around the mountainside in a labyrinth of interconnected, moss-carpeted staircases and arched, hanging plazas. There is no metal, no mechanism, no spark of artifice. The city is a biological engine in a state of terminal, joyous equilibrium.

It is a place of deafening, omnipresent vibration. A low, subterranean thrum—a geological pulse—resonates through the soles of anyone standing on the porous, water-logged rock. It is not an echo, but a constant, physical pressing against the eardrums, a roar of pressure that never rises or falls. Because the landscape is frozen in its state of total stasis, the roar never changes pitch; it is the sound of a world that finished its evolution a billion years ago and refused to exit the stage.

Despite the lushness of the vegetation—great, broad-leafed ferns that drink humidity directly from the clouds and vines that weave through the very walls of the homes—there is no life that crawls, flies, or breathes. No birds wing through the misty gorges; no insects thrum in the nectar-heavy blooms. The silence of the fauna is the most jarring element of the city. The only "presences" are the structures themselves and the relentless, atmospheric weight.

The reality breakdown manifests in the geometry of the heights. Looking down from the central promenade, one sees the city folding back on itself in impossible ways. A staircase might lead downward for dozens of flights, only to emerge onto an outlook that stares directly into the sun-dappled zenith of the canopy it just left. The horizon is not a line, but a series of overlapping fractals; the city extends into a regional vastness that seems to curve inward, as if the entire mountain range were a singular, enclosed organism.

The air glistens with refraction. Because the water content is so high and the light is filtered through curtains of mist and dense, waxy leaves, the world often splits into chromatic ghost-images. You might see the reflection of a terrace arching above your head before you see the physical stone.

Everything in Oros-Vea is settled, weathered into a perfect, ancient grace. The mosses are a million years deep, cushioning the sharp edges of the cliffs. The water routes are carved into deep, permanent grooves in the rock, smoothed to the texture of silk. It is a monument to a world that stopped moving, holding its breath in a feverish, humid dream, forever wet, forever hot, and forever echoing with a sound that is the heartbeat of a planet that has forgotten how to change.
