The world exists in a profound, pressurized stillness, buried deep beneath a crust of unyielding basalt. It is a vast, subterranean cathedral of living biomass, a valley of suspended animation where time has settled into a perfect, static equilibrium.

The ceiling—if it can be called that—is a vaulted expanse of calcified root-systems and phosphorescent fungi, glowing with a constant, blinding white intensity that mimics the noon sun. There is no weather here, only a hum of constant, radiant heat radiating from the plant-flesh itself. Because the geology is still "embryonic"—a raw, unformed state of primordial mineral jelly—the plants have had to become the architects. Everything is vegetable. Floor, wall, and horizon are made of overlapping, shelf-like leaves, some as wide as city streets, others spiraling into complex towers of waxy, translucent veins.

There is no erosion because there is no wind or water-flow; there is only "dampness." A viscous, silver-hued condensation pools in the concave junctions of the giant fronds, thick as oil, mineral-rich and cool to the touch. This moisture does not trickle or run; it simply exists in pockets, holding its surface tension like mercury drops caught in a web of cellulose.

The density is suffocating yet serene. One can walk for miles across the spongy, resilient floor of interlocking vines, with new layers of growth constantly knitting themselves into the existing structure. It is a world that has reached its final form; nothing grows, nothing dies, and nothing decays. The biological mass is in a state of absolute stasis, a fixed sculpture of green, gold, and ivory fiber.

Scattered across this immense, humid plains are faint, curious artifacts: calcified rings of ancient, discarded fruit-husks and what look like hollowed-out, fossilized burls. They are not built; they are excreted or left behind by some creature that might have walked here aeons ago, though the fauna now is virtually non-existent. There is no sound save for the occasional, rhythmic *pop-hiss* of a spore-sac expanding in the heat, an oscillation that occurs at perfect, unchanging intervals. 

The light is the most overwhelming feature. It is relentless and shadowless, reflecting off the damp, waxy surfaces until the entire valley is bathed in a clinical, scorching brilliance. There is no horizon line because the vegetation curves upward to meet the ceiling, creating an infinite, closed loop of life. It is a place that feels like the interior of a lung or a seed—a dense, hot, and beautiful containment where history has stopped, leaving behind a garden that will remain exactly as it is until the end of the world.
