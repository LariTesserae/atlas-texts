This is the Basin of the Veiled Lung, an immense, submerged metropolis grown from the calcified skeletons of leviathans and the relentless weaving of coral-like polyps. It sits in a state of warm, crushing stillness, a low-lying valley carved into the seabed where the water is thick, saline, and perpetually dark.

There is no sun here. Light is a resource produced by the biology of the place: a soft, pulsing bioluminescence that clings to the architectural curves like frost. 

### The Architecture of Growth
The "urban" sprawl is not built, but sequestered. Vast, calcified towers rise from the silt—porous, honeycombed structures that resemble ribcages fused into gothic cathedrals. Because there is no technology, the "construction" is entirely biological. Mycelial filaments act as mortar, binding together the smoothed teeth, shells, and hardened cartilage that form the foundations. 

Erosion has long since finished its work. The sharp edges of the initial growth have been worn smooth by the ceaseless, slow-motion swirl of the currents, leaving behind a city that feels like it possesses a geological memory. It is dense; to move through the Basin is to navigate a forest of gargantuan, calcified pillars, where balconies of sponge and terrace-like shelves of hardened mucus provide homes for the local inhabitants.

### The Fauna and the Flow
The water is alive. It is a thick, nutrient-rich soup, warm enough that the skin of any moving thing feels perpetually swaddled in velvet. Because the light is so minimal, the fauna have abandoned vision in favor of extreme tactile sensitivity and electrolocation.

Shimmering, translucent eels—the size of tree trunks—slither through the apertures of the towers, their bodies glowing with a dim, rhythmic amber pulse. They are the pulse of the city. Smaller, crab-like scavengers with shells made of volcanic glass scuttle along the walls, their claws clicking softly against the bone-structures. The sound is sparse, a muted *tink-tink* that is swallowed instantly by the heavy, viscous water. 

There is a sense of "dynamic" tension here. The ecosystem is in constant motion, governed by a slow, lung-like rhythm. Every few hours, the entire volume of water in the valley seems to exhale, drawing currents through the coral-vents, which causes the bioluminescent moss on the walls to flare a brilliant, temporary teal before dimming back to a dull, heartbeat-red.

### The Rules of the Void
In this world:
1. **The Law of Displacement:** Nothing is ever discarded. If a structure is no longer needed, it is slowly broken down by acid-secreting worms and repurposed into the next layer of the city. Everything is recycled; the city is its own substrate.
2. **The Silence of the Deep:** Communication is entirely pheromonal and electrical. Sound travels poorly in the thick water, so the city is a place of profound, heavy silence. Predators find their prey not by hearing, but by sensing the electrical disruption of their movement through the liquid dark.
3. **The Warmth of Life:** The temperature is sustained by geothermal vents buried deep beneath the silt, providing the energy for the rampant, albeit sparse, growth of vegetation—mostly feathery, drifting kelp that glows when touched.

To exist here is to be part of a massive, submerged lung. You are rarely alone. The walls are always breathing, the eels are always drifting, and the water is always moving through the channels of the city like blood through arteries. It is a place of settled, ancient balance, where the distinction between the citizen and the city has long since washed away.
