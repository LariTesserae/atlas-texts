This is a place built entirely of high-altitude synthesis, a world where the distinction between "structure" and "organism" has been rendered obsolete. You are standing on a vast, terraced plateau high above a hidden sea, yet the ground beneath your feet—the very architecture of this world—is not earth, nor stone, nor metal. It is a living, pulsating weave of calcified xylem and bio-engineered fiber, thousands of stories thick.

It is a "built" environment, yet there is not a tool to be found. Everything—every stair, every arch, every sprawling, open plaza—has been grown into architectural precision. The geometry is sharp, freshly extruded and hardened by the relentless, humid heat. Because the terrain is young, the edges of these bio-spires are aggressive; they meet the air with jagged, coral-like protrusions that weep.

The water-load is absolute. A thick, condensing mist clings to every surface, saturating the air until breath itself feels like a draught of warm steam. Because of the extreme humidity and the heat, the entire landscape is in a state of hyper-active flux. You hear it before you see it: a cacophony of wet, rhythmic sounds. Rushing rivulets of nutrient-rich sap flow through open, veined channels carved into the floors and walls. There is a constant, churning *thrum*—the sound of the architecture knitting itself together, a wet, muscular sliding of tissue against tissue. 

It is "sparse" only because the scale is so vast. Each major structure is separated by chasms of swirling, water-heavy cloud, yet each individual pillar is a masterpiece of complex growth. There is no animal life whatsoever—no birds to break the silence, no insects to pollinate—only the architecture itself. The "plant-as-substrate" rule is total; there is no soil, no rock, no bedrock. If you were to dig, you would only find more roots, more interlaced capillaries, and deeper, darker chambers of warm, pulsating fiber.

The light is heavy and diffused, filtering through the hanging, translucent membranes that serve as roofs and walls. It casts a hazy, ambient glow that turns the shadows into deep, bruised purples and the highlights into searing, milky white. 

Everything here is in a state of turbulent, accelerated maturation. You can watch the structures change in real-time. A wall will bulge, thicken, and stretch toward a neighboring spire, reaching out with delicate, vein-like tendrils to fuse and create a new bridge or walkway. This is an anthill-energy landscape, a biological machine that never settles. It roars with the effort of its own formation, a hot, wet, high-altitude bazaar of living construction, endlessly rearranging its own limbs in the humid, oxygen-rich air. To traverse it is to walk upon a creature that is simultaneously building and consuming itself, a city of total craft that has completely forgotten what it means to be wild.
