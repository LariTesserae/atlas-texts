The world is a vertical labyrinth of calcified ambition, a monumental city-mountain constructed entirely from the fused, fossilized carapaces of ancestral giants. There is no earth here, no dirt, no soil; the ground is a seamless, sprawling topography of smoothed chitin, bone-white and charcoal-grey, ground down over eons by the friction of a trillion tiny, soft feet.

It is a world built of *body*. Every stairwell, every sweeping arch, every vaulted ceiling is a piece of anatomy reimagined as architecture. The scale is immense—a towering collection of hollowed-out spires and terraces that stretch toward a sky that remains perpetually obscured by the thick, hanging curtains of living moss and fungal mats.

The light is a mercy, and it is minimal. A dim, subterranean violet glow emanates from bioluminescent lichen that clings to every crevice, casting long, wavering shadows across the rounded, weathered surfaces. Because the walls are constructed from biological material, the surface is always slick with a weeping, cool condensation—a permanent, atmospheric dampness that feeds the dense, clinging creepers. 

The sound is not an ambient background; it is a physical weight. It is the deafening, rhythmic thrumming of a world possessed by biology. The fauna density is overwhelming. Everywhere, in every fold of the architecture, there is movement. Millions of chitinous, multi-legged creatures navigate the vertical surfaces. The air is thick with the vibration of wings, the mandible-click of foragers, and the low-frequency humming of nesting colonies. It is a deafening, unified chorus of life that has reached a state of hyper-saturation; there is no silence, only layers of chirps, whistles, and the wet slap of soft appendages against polished bone.

There is no technology here. There is only the biological imperative. The "built" environment is not something that was machined, but something that was grown and meticulously polished by the relentless, patient work of generations of insects and small, armored vertebrates. They are the architects, keeping the structures clean, smoothing the edges until they are soft to the touch, and maintaining the interconnected web of tunnels.

It is warm, localized and stagnant. A humid, trapped heat radiates from the mass of bodies and the decaying floral rot that carpets the lower tiers. The erosion is ancient and established; the "stone" of this city shows the smooth, rounded wear of millions of years of transit. Sharp edges have long since succumbed to the constant passage of life, leaving behind a world of soft curves, worn-down thresholds, and polished handholds.

The ecology is a closed loop of exquisite efficiency. Rain—if one can call it that—is a cold, erratic mist that drips from the high, dark ceiling of the cavernous world above, replenishing the moisture trapped in the thick, lush mats of velvet moss. The environment is dense, with every cubic inch of volume either occupied by a living thing, a vein of climbing vegetation, or the slick, curved wall of the city itself.

To move through this place is to walk through a crowded, breathing interior. It is a city that never stops, never changes quickly, and never sleeps. It is a slow, steady pulse of life, existing in the dark, high-altitude quiet of its own creation, where the distinction between the building and the inhabitants has been erased by the slow, grinding passage of time.
