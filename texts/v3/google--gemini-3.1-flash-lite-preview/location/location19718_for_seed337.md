The expanse—if it can be called that—resides within a vast, subterranean basin, a sunken valley carved beneath the crust of some nameless continent. It is not a cavern in the traditional sense, but a depression that stretches for miles in every direction, its ceiling lost to a permanent, hazy gloom. 

The geology here is in a state of arrested birth. There is no erosion because nothing has yet been weathered by the ages; instead, there is a frantic, raw accretion. The floors are composed of jagged, heat-treated basalt and chalky calcifications, rising in unfinished spires and shelf-like protrusions that suggest a landscape still being extruded from the bedrock.

Because the density is near-total, the silence is not empty; it is crowded. The environment is packed with a singular, sprawling settlement of dry-stone and mud-brick structures, stacked one atop another like a hive built in a fissure. There is no space between dwellings. Walls of one house serve as the roof of the next, creating a labyrinthine verticality that pulses with motion. The people here move with a rhythmic, measured pace, their lives dictated by the “weeping”—the condensation that drips from the jagged ceiling, gathering in clay basins and narrow, hand-carved channels that run like veins between the packed hovels.

Visibility is surprisingly clear, despite the subterranean location. A bioluminescent lichen clings to the upper reaches of the walls, casting a steady, slate-colored ambient light that mimics a perpetual, overcast afternoon. 

Vegetation is sparse, confined to knotted, grayish mosses and low-lying, succulent-like tubers that cling to the moisture in the shadow of buildings. There is no wind, only the rhythmic drip of water settling into reservoirs, and the soft, grinding sound of manual stone wheels—the primary technology of this place. The residents use primitive iron-tipped tools and bone chisels to constantly add to the city, scraping away at the raw, protruding geology to carve new chambers and stairways.

It is a world of constant, industrious creation. There is a sense of desperate, beautiful unfinished-ness. A predator might occasionally dart between the shadow-alleys—a sleek, pale, cat-like creature that eats the damp moss and the rare, blind insects that inhabit the cracks—but they are shadows in a place dominated by human craft. 

The air is cool, heavy with the smell of wet mineral dust and the musk of a thousand occupied rooms. Every inch of available space is claimed, occupied, or under construction. It is a city that is growing faster than it can settle, a civilization pressed into a bowl, waiting for the day it might finally bump its head against the ceiling of its own making. There is no chaos, only a meticulous, collective effort to build a cathedral of living, breathing stone in the dark.
