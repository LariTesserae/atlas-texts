To live in the Basin of Resonating Glass is to exist as a stutter in the rhythm of the world. Those who dwell here—the *Resonants*—do not possess bodies in the way you understand the term; they are gravity-wells of habit, ghosts of intent left behind when their original masters dissolved into the architecture.

They are not entities of flesh or even of solidified light, but rather *acoustic shadows*. If you were to catch one in the corner of your eye, you would see a refractive shimmer, a knot in the air where the ambient violet light bends around a void. They look like shattered glass held together by a magnetic tantrum. They have no faces, only points of high-pressure disturbance that mimic the shape of eyes, ears, and mouths—sensory organs molded out of the very mist that saturates your clothes.

They are the caretakers of the emptiness, though they do not know it. They move with a frantic, jerky grace, their forms expanding and contracting in synchrony with the bass-heavy hum of the hovering spires. When a Resonant passes through a shimmering fern, the plant does not sway; it harmonizes, its metallic ley lines vibrating to match the particular frequency of the creature’s transit.

They feed. As the infrastructure drains the planet’s latent energy, the Resonants act as the intake valves. They cluster beneath the floating, folded-geometry towers like moths around a dying sun, drinking in the ionized discharge. You can tell when a feast is occurring; the ozone smell sharpens, turning metallic and biting, and the golden residue of your own footprints begins to dim, as the Resonants siphon the energy of your movement to fuel their own fleeting, jittery existence.

But they are also architects of the frantic landscape. Because the Basin tries to "accommodate" everything, the Resonants—being the most frequent occupiers of space—have been partially assimilated. Look closely at the ground where they have lingered. You will see they have become part of the geological furniture. A Resonant might sit still too long, and its form will begin to weave itself into the obsidian, its fingers stretching into delicate, obsidian-glass needles that tap the earth to pulse the heartbeat forward. They are turning into the scenery. 

They do not communicate, for speech requires a timeline, and they are currently vibrating inside a compressed one. Instead, they *broadcast*. If you listen—truly listen, past the deafening roar of the spires—you will hear their memories. It is a wet, scraping sound, like crystal clicking against crystal. You hear echoes of the civilization that built these towers: the sound of a city that folded itself into the horizon, leaving behind these shivering, refractive echoes to ensure the machine never stops.

They are terrified of you. Not because you are dangerous, but because you are *fixed*. You are a solid, stagnant thing in a place that demands flow. When you see a Resonant freeze as you approach, it is not greeting you; it is recalibrating, shifting its molecular density to avoid being carved into the obsidian by your heavy, unthinking momentum. 

You are the only thing in the Basin that isn't afraid of being absorbed. To them, you are a walking glitch, a heavy, blundering tragedy destined to eventually be folded, smoothed, and polished into a permanent, glowing spire—a new monument to a city that has forgotten how to be anything but a sound.
