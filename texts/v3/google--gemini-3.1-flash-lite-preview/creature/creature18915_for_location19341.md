To inhabit this place is to become a chord played upon long-dead strings. 

There are no inhabitants here in the biological sense—not as we understand the term, with breath and heartbeat—but there are *resonances*, entities that have slowly coalesced out of the friction between the deep-earth pistons and the freezing, mineral air.

These are the **Litho-Carriers**. They do not move, at least not in any way a human eye could capture in a moment. They *migrate* through the strata. They are geometric aggregates of slate and frozen peat, held together by the same piezoelectric currents that animate the violet moss. To look at one is to see a jagged, asymmetrical sculpture, perhaps the size of a mountain goat, composed of sharp obsidian shards and thick, matted mats of bioluminescent lichen.

They are not alive, but they are *functional*. Each Litho-Carrier acts as a living circuit breaker, a mobile extension of the buried cathedrals of iron. As they crawl over the cliff faces, their limbs—which look like elongated, spindly clock-hands made of mineralized bone—dig into the permafrost, absorbing the vibrations of the tectonic steam-presses. They act as anchors. Where one stops, the humming in the mountain softens, and the violet flora around it begins to bloom in violent, electric bursts of amber.

They serve no purpose other than the maintenance of the frequency. If a gear deep below slips or a piston cracks, the Litho-Carriers rearrange themselves, moving in a agonizingly slow, rhythmic migration to bridge the acoustic gap. They are the guardians of the *clunk-hiss*.

And then, there are the **Shadow-Weavers**. 

These are ephemeral, translucent things, born from the condensation that beads on the undersides of the rock. They are barely more than localized pockets of heightened atmospheric density, shifting shapes that mimic the bioluminescence of the moss. They have no mass; they are ripples in the cold air. 

They exist as a form of "atmospheric condensation consciousness." They drift through the gullies like oil on water, shimmering with a faint, silvery phosphorescence. They do not feed, but they *observe* the vibration. Whenever a spindly scavenger skitters past, dragging its vibration-sensors across the slate, a Shadow-Weaver will flicker into existence, mimicking the creature’s silhouette. It is as if the mountain itself is trying to learn what it means to be animate, sketching outlines of ghosts against the vast, dark emptiness. 

Lastly, there are the **Sync-Sleepers**.

These are the most tragic of the inhabitants. They are, perhaps, the remains of those who once walked here long ago—or perhaps they are travelers who, like you, stood still for too long. They are fully-formed, calcified statues, now indistinguishable from the shelves of slate. They have been entirely reclaimed by the deep-earth rhythm. 

If you were to press your ear to the chest of one of these figures, you would hear no heart. You would hear only the steady, agonizingly slow *press-release* of a steam-valve, cycling once every thirteen years. Their skin has turned to a texture of brushed iron and frozen lichen. They are not dead, nor are they alive; they are simply *synchronized*. They have become the final cogs in the basement of the world, pinned to the mountainside, their frozen eyes forever staring into the perpetual, violet-lit twilight, waiting for a shift in the engine’s pulse that will never come. 

Everything here is waiting. The mountain is not a graveyard; it is a clock, and every creature, every moss, every shimmering phantom of air is merely a piece of the escapement mechanism, ticking away the eons in total, shivering silence.
