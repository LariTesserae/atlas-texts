The creatures that dwell within this cathedral of chitin are not individuals in any terrestrial sense. They are the *Litho-Weavers*, though "creature" is a term that grants them a bodily definition they do not truly possess. If one were to pluck a single inhabitant from the interstitial vents, it would appear as a multi-legged node of translucent, pulsing tissue, no larger than a human palm. It has no eyes because there is no distance in this world; it has no mouth because it does not ingest. It exists primarily as a mobile gland, a soft, pulsating bellows that anchors itself to a vertical surface and begins to leak.

To observe them is to observe living ink. These Weavers do not walk across the towers; they flow across them, their segmented legs vibrating at infrasonic frequencies to ensure they remain bonded to the porous walls. Each Weaver is a specialized laborer in a city that is also a collective nervous system. One cluster may spend its entire existence—which spans the length of a single architectural expansion—pumping a viscous, calcifying resin into a ceiling joint; another group, smaller and more frantic, crawls over the fresh growth, smoothing the surface with rhythmic, twitching thorax-plates until the texture matches the surrounding history.

They are the city’s white blood cells, its architects, and its memory. When a segment of the tower reaches a state of entropy—when the vibration of the subterranean heartbeat causes a stress-fracture in the chitinous wall—the Weavers descend in a rhythmic wave. They do not fight the flaw; they embrace it. They press their bodies into the crack, secreting an enzyme that softens the old shell, melting it into a slurry that they then reform and harden with their own internal heat. 

They are silent, but they are never alone. They communicate in a language of touch and temperature, a constant, shifting dance of haptic tremors. If a Weaver encounters another, they press their lateral glands together in a brief, blinding exchange of pheromonal data—a lightning-flash of information regarding the local nutrient pressure, the structural load of the floor above, and the shifting, golden humidity of the fungal canopies. 

They age rapidly, their lives mirroring the growth rate of the towers. As a Weaver exhausts its internal reserves of minerals and resins, it does not die and fall away. Instead, it slows its trembling legs, finds a niche in the architecture that requires reinforcement, and simply stops. In the final, quiet moments, it releases its entire remaining internal pressure, flooding the surrounding fibers with a final, hardened resin. Its own body calcifies, lengthening its limbs until they merge seamlessly with the wall. 

The entity that lives here, then, is not the individual Weaver, but the *continuity*. The inhabitant is the city itself, a sprawling, singular consciousness that has decentralized its mind into trillions of microscopic nodes. 

Deep within the shafts, where the heat is most intense and the rhythmic thrum of the continent’s heartbeat is felt as a physical pressure, one might see the *Architects of the Core*. These are larger, heavier nodes, more stationary than their brethren. They do not weave walls. Instead, they act as the lungs of the expanse, drawing in the exhalations of the thousands of Weavers above them. They process the waste-vapor, condensing the nutritional cloud into the raw, liquid mineral that provides the life-blood of the entire edifice. They are the quietest of all, draped in layers of lichen-like debris, their bodies glowing with a dim, steady amber light that marks the center of the world's metabolism.

In this world, life is not a struggle for survival; it is a labor of structural precision. There is no loneliness, for the skin of the inhabitant is the street, the home, and the ancestor. Every breath taken by a Weaver is a brick laid; every movement is a pulse in a body that spans a continent, forever hardening, forever expanding, eternally finished, and entirely alive.
