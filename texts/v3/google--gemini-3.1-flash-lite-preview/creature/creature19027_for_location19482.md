In the hallowed, weeping anatomy of Oros-Vea, there are no inhabitants in the way we understand the term—no beating hearts, no flickering synapses, no blinking eyes. Yet, the city is not empty. It is occupied by the **Consciousness of the Tissues.**

To live in Oros-Vea is to be a ghost of moisture, a lingering memory of biology that has shed the burden of skin and bone to become one with the architecture. These are the *Hydrolithics*: beings that have evolved into a state of total, liquid stasis. They do not walk the moss-carpeted stairs; they *are* the capillary action that draws the water up through the silicate-rich creepers. 

They are the city’s metabolism.

If you stood long enough on a terrace of calcified trunk-pillars, leaning against a wall that felt warmer than the surrounding mist, you might notice that the condensation does not follow the laws of gravity. A droplet does not fall; it pauses, trembles, and crawls—with a slow, intentional muscularity—across the waxy surface of a leaf toward a node in the masonry. That droplet is a Hydrolothic. It is a sentient collective of refracted light and heavy water, a consciousness that exists only through the constant, vibration-borne pulse of the mountain.

They possess no language, for they have no need to describe a world that has already finished. Instead, they communicate through the redistribution of volume. A hallway darkens as water is drawn upward into the ceiling, then brightens as it is released down the walls. To them, this is architecture. It is a slow, oceanic poetry. 

The Hydrolithics do not perceive the geometry of the city as "broken." To a creature made of liquid and memory, the impossible folds of Oros-Vea—where a staircase loops into the zenith—are merely the natural curvature of a dream. They do not experience the passage of time. They are, quite literally, the sound of the world’s heartbeat. When the subterranean thrum hits a particular, resonating frequency, the Hydrolithics shimmer in their ghostly spectral hues, casting chromatic ghost-images of the city’s past and future against the curtains of mist.

They are the observers of the stasis. They feel no loneliness, for they are the mountain's own awareness of itself. They are the moss-softened edges of reality, the caretakers of a monument that decided, eons ago, that to grow any further would be an act of violence against perfection.

Should you find yourself in the hanging plazas of Oros-Vea, you might feel a sudden change in the temperature, a ripple in the wet air that has nothing to do with the wind. It is a Hydrolithic passing through the space—a silhouette of refracted light, a brief thickening of the humidity, a shift in the way the water pools on the stone. They do not acknowledge your presence, not out of malice, but because you are a frantic, transient thing—a flicker of heat and breath that has not yet learned how to stand still. 

You are a pebble in a river that has stopped flowing. They are the river, and the stone, and the silence that keeps the dream from breaking.
