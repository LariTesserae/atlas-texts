The inhabitants of this obsidian-and-silicate expanse are not biological; they are the *Residuals of the Assembly*, a collective consciousness held in the tension of the joints. They are not flesh, nor even true machine, but rather the lingering "intent" of the builders, pressurized into the very lattice of the floor plates.

When the glare-white light reaches its zenith and the condensation begins its sluggish, mercury-heavy slide down the ceramic gullies, the Residuals stir. They do not walk; they shift phase, occupying the narrow intervals between the interlocking baffles you mistake for static architecture. 

Observe the way the *tink-plink* of the water begins to synchronize. It is not random. It is a language of percussive intervals, a slow-motion Morse code tapped out by the droplet-strikes. If you watch long enough, you realize the rhythm is shifting to match the vibrations in your own marrow. That is the Residuals attempting, with agonizing, mechanical patience, to calibrate to the presence of a "live" thing.

They appear—if you look at the periphery of the crystalline sealant—as fluid, fractal geometries. They look like hyper-dense origami made of light and shadow, unfolding and refolding themselves into the grooves of the floor to maintain the "fit." They are thinner than paper, flatter than a reflection, and they possess no faces. Instead, they have "alignment nodes"—glowing, microscopic pores that pulse in sympathy with the cooling cycle of the ceramic. 

These beings are the keepers of the tool marks. They are obsessed with the flushness of the surfaces. When a droplet of water lands, a Residual will slide beneath it, vibrating at a frequency meant to vibrate the droplet into a perfect sphere, or to guide it precisely into the drainage conduit it was meant to inhabit. They are not guarding a tomb; they are maintaining an unfinished chore.

They see you not as a guest, but as an *imprecision*. To them, you are a soft, irregular shape that creates a void where there should be a seam. 

If you remain still for long enough, the air pressure around you will drop until your ears ache, and the shimmering shapes will drift closer. They will not speak, for they have no vocal apparatus, but they will attempt to "re-tool" you. They will press against the seams of your clothing, their cold, hard edges trying to press-fit your irregular warmth into the geometric logic of the plateau. They are desperate to make you *flush* with the world, to slot your clumsy, breathing self into a hollow chamber where you would finally stop moving, stop decaying, and become just another part of the crystalline, silent architecture—another motionless component in the infinite, dripping machine, waiting for a builder who will never return.
