To witness the residents of this bulb-city is to watch shadow-work performed by living origami. They are the *Suturers*.

At first glance, they appear as mere irregularities in the gloom—scraps of wet parchment come to life. They are small, no larger than a child’s palm, possessing a central thorax that resembles a translucent bead of amber, though it is soft and perpetually cool to the touch. From this core radiate six legs, each segmented into a dozen microscopic joints that allow them to move with a terrifying, stuttering fluidness. They do not walk so much as they *realign* themselves, snapping from one position to the next in a blink, like a stop-motion film played at high speed.

They have no eyes. Evolution long ago decided that in a house of velvet and damp, sight was a liability. Instead, their faces are dominated by highly sensitive, feathery cilia that ripple constantly, tasting the chemical makeup of the air and feeling the seismic history of the world outside. When two Suturers meet on a bridge of sap, they do not speak. They press their front-facing filaments together, a rapid-fire exchange of vibrations that sends tremors through their own bodies, a silent, skin-deep language of frequency and tension.

They are clad in nothing but their own biology, though they adorn themselves in the service of their architecture. A Suturer working a high-altitude seam will often carry a toolkit of bone-needles—sharpened slivers harvested from the bulb's own waste-cycles—tucked into the fold of an extra-dermal flap near their thorax. They move with an obsessive, frantic grace, perpetually mending. They are an anxious species; their lives are defined by a singular, crushing responsibility: the bulb is dying, always and never. It is a slow, geologic expiration, and the Suturers are the frantic white blood cells tasked with keeping the rhythm steady.

When they are "at rest," they fold themselves into the bulb's walls. They curl their limbs beneath them, tucking their heads into the hollows of their thorax until they are indistinguishable from a knot of root-hair or a cluster of dried leaf-pulp. In these moments, they become part of the infrastructure. They sleep in the walls, their own respiration synchronizing with the slow, peristaltic thrum of the great organ, effectively becoming the city's mortar.

There is a profound loneliness to them, but it is not the loneliness of a solitary creature. It is the loneliness of a collective consciousness that has forgotten how to be an individual. 

When they harvest the mites and translucent worms that scuttle through the arterial pipes, they do so with a surgical, almost mournful apathy. They treat the livestock as extensions of the bulb’s metabolism. To the Suturers, there is no "self" and "other"—there is only the Bulb, the Cold, and the Stitch. 

If you were to catch one by the light of a bioluminescent spore-bulb—if you could somehow provide light in that tomb of shadows—you would see their skin is not truly pale, but a shifting mosaic of bruised violets and pale, vein-traced greens, mimetic of the pulse of the room. When they work, they grow frantic, their movements a blur of clicks and scrapings. But when they are done, and the seam is secure against the weeping shale of the world’s crust, they go entirely still. 

They are wait-monsters, inhabitants of a living sarcophagus, waiting for the rock to shudder, waiting for the bulb to spasm, waiting for the day their stitching finally outlasts the life of the thing that breathed them into existence. They are the custodians of a silence so absolute that, in their minds, it has become a sound.
