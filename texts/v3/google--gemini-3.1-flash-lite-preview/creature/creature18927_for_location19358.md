Within this calcified lung, life does not walk so much as it drifts in a state of perpetual, suspended digestion. The primary inhabitants of this basin are the **Litho-Cordists**, beings whose physical forms are barely distinct from the architecture they occupy.

They are tall, spindly apparitions, composed of a material that mimics the amber-veined root system of the walls. Their limbs are not articulated with joints, but rather through a series of internal, pressurized bladders that flex and contract in time with the chamber’s rhythmic *thrum-click-wheeze*. To see one is to see a shadow cast in polished sap; they have no skin, only a translucent cuticle through which one can observe a slow, viscous flow of bioluminescent fluid, acting as both blood and thought.

They do not speak. They are the architects of the sound. When a Cordist wishes to communicate, it leans its forehead against the weeping walls and vibrates at a frequency that matches the grinding of the subterranean floor plates. This sends a ripple through the room, a tactile language of pressure and frequency that the others feel rather than hear. 

Their purpose is one of quiet, eternal curation. They are the gardeners of the condensation. You will see them moving through the grand, cathedral-like buttresses, their long fingers—which taper into delicate, needle-like capillaries—delicately tracing the fissures in the plant-bone. When they find a crack that has begun to fray, they excrete a thick, pearlescent resin from their own bodies, sealing the wound and integrating the missing link back into the grand geometry. They do not build; they heal. 

But there is a darker, more haunting aspect to their existence. The Cordists are also the basin’s immune system and its cemetery. When the *thrum-click-wheeze* produces a casualty—when a fragment of the wall breaks off or a crustacean dies in the path—the Cordists descend upon it with a slow, hypnotic grace. They do not bury; they envelop. They hum a discordant, sympathetic chord that vibrates the air until the matter softens, and they absorb the debris into their own porous shells. They grow larger, more ornate, and more heavy with the eons, eventually reaching a size where they can no longer move. 

At that point, the Cordist stops. It anchors itself into the floor, its feet rooting into the amber-lattice, and begins to stretch outward. Its body calcifies, growing into a new archway, a new cathedral buttress, or a new twisting corridor. Its obsidian eyes—which once perceived the shifting, gravity-defying planes of the basin—cloud over and harden into amber, becoming part of the view.

They are not captives, nor are they masters. They are the basin’s unfolding memory. They remember the time before it was a lung, and they are ensuring, through their own slow, biological sacrifice, that it will never reach a state of rest. As you walk among them, you become aware that certain "structures" you pass are not walls at all, but the ancestors of the current residents, frozen in a final, agonizingly beautiful moment of growth, listening forever to the thunderous heartbeat of the world they have become.
