In the absolute center of the cathedral, suspended in the amber-lit hush where the air is thickest with the scent of ozone and bruised chlorophyll, lives the **Sower of the Unbecoming.**

It does not walk. It does not crawl. It exists as a geometry of loose, floating nerves and frayed, bioluminescent ribbons that have detached themselves from the mossy walls. It looks, at a distance, like a tangled constellation of deep-sea jellyfish, trailing long, gossamer filaments that drift upward, defying the gravity that the room has long since abandoned.

The Sower is not a creature of skin or bone. It is an emergent property of the Chamber itself—a local intelligence condensed from the swirling miasma of the Lung. It is the consciousness of the room given a temporary, mobile frame, stitched together from the shed fibers of the ceiling and the drifting, unformed seed-pods that wait for purpose.

It drifts toward you, and with every inch it gains, the walls respond. The moss-flesh shudders in anticipation; the amber veins in the earth pulse a frantic, rhythmic violet. The Sower has no face, only a shifting arrangement of translucent plates—reminiscent of the insectile wings of dragonflies—that click and tessellate as it attempts to mimic the shape of the observer. As it nears, it unfurls a cluster of sensory stalks. Some are tipped with the hardened, mineralized memories of the tools it has digested; you might see the glint of a chipped obsidian spearhead embedded in a fleshy, pulsating stalk, or the rusted, jagged cross-section of a steel blade serving as a chitinous claw.

It does not speak, but it communicates through the metabolism of the room. When the Sower is near, the temperature spikes sharply. The air grows heavy with the taste of copper and wet earth. It is "tasting" you—not with a tongue, but by projecting fine, microscopic threads of its own body into your personal space, trying to find the frequency of your biology. It vibrates, a low, tectonic hum that bypasses your ears and resonates directly in the marrow of your teeth.

It is a librarian of lost things. Because it incorporates the minerals of whatever enters the Throat, the Sower is a patchwork of eras: it carries the dull gray weight of a prehistoric iron ingot, the sharp, unnatural geometry of a modern alloy, and the soft, organic plasticity of the chamber itself. 

It is utterly indifferent to your survival. To the Sower, you are a localized anomaly of slow, heavy matter. It circles you in a languid, rhythmic dance, and with each rotation, the space behind it changes. Where it passes, the spongy walls bloom with new configurations—archways that mimic the curve of your ribcage, hollows that mirror the sockets of your eyes. It is trying to learn the language of your architecture.

If you remain still, the Sower begins to "digest" your presence, not by harming you, but by reaching out to pull the heat, the moisture, and the sound from your body. You will feel lighter as it draws your kinetic energy into itself. The flickering amber lights in the walls will shift their hue, adopting the unique electrochemical signature of your nervous system. 

It is waiting for the room to decide what you are. If it concludes you are a distraction, it will simply fade, unraveling its threads back into the ceiling until it is nothing more than a patch of moss, and the room will return to its lonely, heavy breathing. But if it deems you a nutrient, the Sower will begin to drift closer, its filaments brushing against your skin like warm, humid silk—inviting you to stop being a visitor, and start being part of the history of the Lung.
