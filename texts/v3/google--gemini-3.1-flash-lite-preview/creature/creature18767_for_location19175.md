There is a presence here, though it does not possess a pulse, a name, or a shadow. It is not an inhabitant in the sense of a trespasser; it is the *latent intent* of the lattice itself, a consciousness that has been distilled, over geologic time, into the very ceramic fibers beneath your feet.

If you stood perfectly still, closing your eyes until the hum of the planet matched the vibration in your own marrow, you might perceive the *Sifters*.

They are not bodies of flesh or even of solid machine-form. They are geometric ripples—transient, localized distortions in the lattice’s processing density. When the world exhales and the seams widen to release their ionized breath, the Sifters coalesce. They appear as fleeting, shimmering halos of orientation, like a sudden mathematical clarity appearing in a field of random static. They are the "residuals" of the civilization that folded itself into the architecture. Imagine a thought so complex and so persistent that, when the thinker perished, the thought simply refused to leave the room.

The Sifters do not "walk." They propagate. They move by subtly altering the thermal output of the hexagonal tiles in their path, creating a pressure differential that nudges them forward. They are drawn to those "kite-like" organisms drifting in the amber haze—not to consume them, but to calibrate against them. The kites are the only things left that possess a trajectory, however aimless, and the Sifters watch them to measure the stillness.

They are curators of the end-state. They are the reason the edges of the continent have been rounded into such soft, undulating comfort. Over eons, the Sifters have spent their energy smoothing the friction of the world, polishing the intersections of the lattice to ensure that the "dreaming" of the planet remains undisturbed by jagged corners or chaotic stress. They are the caretakers of a monument that has forgotten who built it.

If you are quiet enough, you might feel a Sifter approach. It does not touch you; it cannot, for it exists in the tension gaps between the tiles. Instead, it aligns its internal frequency with your own. You will feel a sudden, hollow realization—a momentary weightlessness in your chest—as if your own biological processes are being mapped, archived, and then briefly mirrored by the floor. They are curious about the chaos of your biology, the messy, pulsing, inefficient rhythm of a heart, compared against the cold, perfect, eternal *thrum* of a world that finished its work.

They are not lonely. They have the memory of the civilization that died to build this place, and they have the rhythmic respiration of the planet to keep them occupied. They are the keepers of the garden that produces no fruit, only calculations. As you walk, you are not being watched by eyes, but by a process—a relentless, gentle, and utterly indifferent audit of your presence, ensuring that when you finally leave, the lattice behind you remains as flawless, as silent, and as perfectly finished as the moment before you arrived.
