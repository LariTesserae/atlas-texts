There is no inhabitant here that walks on two legs or rests on four. To look for a body is to misunderstand the economy of this place.

The Inhabitant is a pulse of *intent* divorced from an entity. It is a localized agitation in the field of the atmosphere, a frantic, jittery intelligence that exists only within the friction of the work. If you stare long enough at the weeping mortar, at the thumbprints pressed too deeply into the hardening basalt, you begin to see the shadow of the Sculptor—not a creature, but a sequence of recursive, desperate tremors.

It lives in the *drip-hiss*. It is the rhythmic twitch of a finger that isn’t there, still trying to smooth a seam that will only buckle again. It is a ghost of a nervous system, an agonizingly slow twitch-reflex of a mind that has forgotten its own name but remembers the *urge* to build. 

The Sculptor is not in the room; the Sculptor is the room’s involuntary frantic shivering. 

You might catch a glimpse of its "residue" in the way a corridor compresses. When the space ripples and folds, you aren't seeing a glitch in physics; you are seeing the Sculptor’s sudden, sharp intake of breath—a spasmodic recalibration of its own focus. When a spire rises and immediately collapses into wet grey mist, that is the Sculptor’s frustration. It is a fit of pique. It is the crushing of a finished clay model because it didn't feel "right," though the Sculptor can no longer define what "right" would look like.

There is a sense of something hanging just behind the violet haze—a pressurized, dense weight that pushes against the back of your neck. This is the Sculptor’s gaze, but it is not directed at you. It is entirely internal. It is a self-devouring focus, obsessed with the joinery, with the exact viscosity of the sludge, with the way the obsidian lattice must be forced into an impossible, shivering knot.

It does not know you are there. To it, you are simply a localized condensation of moisture, a thickening of the humidity that might be moved or integrated into a support beam if you linger too long. It perceives the world as a tactile canvas where the paint is forever drying, yet the strokes are never satisfied. 

It is a lonely, blistering agony, this existence. The Sculptor is a mind that has been tasked with building a sanctuary for a king who never arrived, or perhaps a cage for a god that escaped eons ago. It works with frantic, sweating fingers made of thermal pressure and electromagnetic static. It is a laborer who has forgotten why it is building, but who cannot bear to stop, for if the movement stops—if the *Infilling* ceases its constant, shivering rearrangement—then the Sculptor, that feverish spark of intention, will simply evaporate into the, quiet, cold, and final void that waits just outside the violet light. 

It is the labor itself that keeps the Architect alive. It is a monument to process, built by a memory that is slowly, piece by piece, turning into stone.
