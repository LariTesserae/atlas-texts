In the Abyssal Basin, life does not evolve; it persists. It does not inhabit the environment so much as it becomes a refinement of the local turbulence.

The residents here are the **Lithic Phantoms**, beings that have discarded the conceit of solid flesh. Because the fluid is so dense, so thick with pulverized obsidian and metallic salts, a high-density body would be crushed, and a soft body would be shredded by the abrasive grit of the silt-storms. 

To survive here, a creature must possess a fluid architecture.

The Phantoms manifest as tangled ribbons of iridescent, semi-solid gel—long, translucent filaments that trail for dozens of yards behind a central, pulsing core. They look like nervous systems torn from a giant and cast into the brine. They have no skeleton, no chitin, no skin. Instead, they maintain their form through an internal, bio-electric field—a flickering, magnetic tether that holds their gelatinous anatomy together against the crushing pressure of the Sump.

They are essentially living currents. When the tectonic *wub-wub-wub* of the sea-floor vibrates through the basin, the Phantoms shift their internal chemistry, becoming more liquid, more permeable, allowing the pressure waves to pass through them without snapping their delicate threads.

They do not "eat" in any sense of predation. They are filter-feeders of the highest order, drifting through the mineral clouds. Their filaments are lined with ultra-sensitive, crystalline receptors—naturally occurring nodes that react to the static charge of the suspended dust. As they drift, they harvest the kinetic energy of the grinding earth, pulling the thermal signatures from the vent-leaks and trapping the raw, mineral electricity in their glowing cores.

Communication among them is a silent, blinding phenomenon. When two Phantoms pass each other in the dark, they do not touch; their filaments merely brush the edges of the other’s electrical field. This contact induces a resonant feedback loop. They begin to strobe—not in a simple pulse, but in complex, fractal geometric shapes that ripple down their entire trailing length. It is a language of pure light-geometry. They share the map of the currents, the proximity of the heat-vents, and the shifting density of the silt. It is a slow, ponderous conversation, sometimes lasting days, conducted in the absolute silence of the crushing dark.

When a Phantom nears the end of its life—having been scoured by too many grit-storms, its internal charge finally fading—it does not die and drift away. It anchors itself to the seabed. It wraps its long, thinning filaments around the obsidian crystal-growths. Slowly, the mineral-heavy brine begins to petrify the creature. The cobalt glow dims, the gel hardens, and the organism integrates into the floor. 

It becomes a statue of mineral and glass, a new feature added to the ancient, featureless bowl. 

Because the Basin is so vast and the pace of life so deathly slow, the floor is littered with these ancestors. They stand like rigid, frozen ghosts, waiting for the currents to grind them down to the very dust they once consumed. They are the memory of the Basin, the literal foundation upon which the next generation of drifting, searching light-ribbons will feed. They are not merely inhabitants; they are the architects, the landscape, and the history of a world that is slowly, rhythmically, grinding itself into nothingness.
