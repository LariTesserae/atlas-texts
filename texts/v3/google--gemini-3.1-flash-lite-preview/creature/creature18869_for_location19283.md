To dwell within the Basin of the Veiled Lung is to undergo a slow, irrevocable metamorphosis. The entities that inhabit these calcified cathedrals are not so much "residents" as they are the sentient antibodies of the valley, fluid beings that have long since traded the rigid identity of the terrestrial for the porous, collaborative nature of the deep.

### The Weavers of the Silt
The primary inhabitants are the *Litho-Synths*, gaunt, spindly humanoids whose skin has taken on the translucent, pearlescent quality of a moon-jelly. They possess no eyes; where the sockets would be, there are instead clusters of elongated, feather-like antennae that drift rhythmically in the current, tasting the chemical signatures of the water.

A Litho-Synth does not "walk" in the traditional sense. They possess vestigial, webbed appendages that allow them to anchor themselves to the coral-towers, but their movement is closer to the fluid drifting of a cloud. They move in deliberate, low-energy spirals, sensing the electrical field of the city. Every movement they make is a conversation; to swim past another is to exchange a dense, pheromonal packet of thought—a complex, wordless history of the current, the state of the thermal vents, and the shifting density of the nutrient soup.

### The Symbiotic Core
These beings do not "build" the city; they orchestrate its growth. They are the gardeners of the bone-towers. A Litho-Synth carries with them a small, rhythmic sac of mycelial spores, which they press into the porous teeth and ribs of the architecture. They shape the calcified spires by rhythmic humming—not a sound that travels through the water, but a low-frequency vibration directed through their own skeletons into the coral, guiding the polyps to grow in specific, structural lattices. 

They are inseparable from the architecture. Occasionally, an elder Litho-Synth—one who has reached the end of their cycle—will "retire." They press themselves into a cooling vent, their body gradually calcifying, their limbs extending into the wall until they become a permanent pillar, their skeleton providing the foundation for a future generation. They do not die; they become the infrastructure. 

### The Watchers of the Pulse
Hovering at the edge of the Litho-Synths' society are the *Amber-Gliders*, the giant, translucent eels that patrol the urban arteries. They are not pets, nor are they wild animals in any conventional sense; they are the transport and the security of the Basin. 

A Litho-Synth can form a temporary neural link with an eel by brushing their antennae against the creature’s skin. For a moment, the Synth exits their own perspective and sees the entire city as a map of raw electrical energy. They hitch rides on the backs of these gliders, flowing through the honeycombed towers like blood cells, moving across the miles of the city in minutes. The eels act as living conduits, carrying the metabolic waste of the residents to the deeper, acidic pits of the valley, completing the eternal cycle of the Basin’s metabolism.

### The Collective Dream
Life here is a state of perpetual, meditative consensus. Because the water is so thick, and because the city’s pulse—the exhalation of the Basin—dictates the cycle of activity, the inhabitants move in a collective trance. When the city exhales, and the bioluminescent moss flares into that brilliant, blinding teal, the Litho-Synths go perfectly still. They freeze against the walls, their own skin lighting up in mimicry of the moss. 

In this moment of light, they are not individuals. They are a single, sprawling organism, a sentient lung breathing in symphony with the earth beneath them. They perceive the world as a cascading series of pressures, currents, and potentials. 

There is no greed here, for there is nothing to own that isn't shared by the water. There is no ambition, for there is nowhere to go but further into the deep. They exist in a state of suspended animation, a culture held in the velvet grip of the abyss, content to watch the salt crystallize, to feel the bones grow, and to wait, with absolute patience, for the next slow intake of the tide.
