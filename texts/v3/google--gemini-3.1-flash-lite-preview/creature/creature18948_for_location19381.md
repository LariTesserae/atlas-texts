To inhabit the Verdant Void is to exist in a state of perpetual, frantic adaptation. The beings here—if one can call them such—are not stationary dwellers but *transients of the architecture*. They are the "Weavers of the Rime."

They are tall, spindly manifestations of the environment itself, composed of a dense, semi-translucent lattice of fibers that mimics the calcified timber of the cathedral. They do not have blood; they have a circulatory system of translucent, pressurized nectar that glows faintly, mirroring the rhythm of the ceiling spores. Their "skin" is a mosaic of chitinous plates and velvet moss, constantly shedding and regrowing as they move through the climate. 

They are silent, necessarily so, for the air in the Void is thin and serves as a medium for the hum of the plants—a vibration that the Weavers use to navigate by feeling the tremors of the architecture through the soles of their feet. To speak would be to introduce a discordance, a sound that might cause the sensitive, hyper-fast greenery to knot into impenetrable barriers or dissolve into sudden, lethal mulch under their feet.

The Weavers possess no permanent homes; the "nests" found in the Void are merely waypoints, temporary staging grounds for their migration. They travel in solitary, elongated gaits, their limbs possessing extra joints that allow them to fold and snap into the winding, chaotic geometry of the spiraling stalks. When they encounter a bridge of leaves beginning to decay, they do not hesitate; they dissolve their own grip, allowing the plant-matter to drop, and catch themselves on the newer, firmer shoots with prehensile, vine-like appendages that sprout from their forearms.

They carry nothing, or rather, they carry everything integrated. Their tools—the obsidian scrapers, the fiber needles—are held within specialized, hook-like apertures in their chests. When they need to harvest a gelatinous sphere of water, they do not drink it; they merge with it. They press their porous, moss-covered flanks against the drifting orb, and the water is wicked into their internal lattice, nourishing the rapid-growth biology that keeps their bodies from turning to brittle rime in the biting cold.

They are not masters of this space. They are its caretakers and its witnesses. Their task, if it can be called such, is to prune and to stitch. As they roam, they use their obsidian needles to "stitch" the faster-growing vines into more stable, bridge-like structures, effectively slowing the chaotic decay of the landscape so that they might traverse it. They are obsessed with the maintenance of the paths, terrified perhaps that if the Verdant Void were left to its own hyper-active devices, it would quickly grow into a tangled, impassable mass that would choke the very light that sustains them.

Their faces are featureless, save for a crystalline, refractive plate where eyes might be. This plate blinks with the same flickering cadence as the sun-spores above, allowing them to calculate the trajectory of the shifting growth. They move with an economy of motion that is haunting to behold—a slow, hypnotic grace that ignores the urgency of the surrounding stalks.

Sometimes, when another Weaver is spotted across a great chasm of the void, they stop. They do not wave, nor do they signal. They simply stand, their bodies held perfectly still against the white glare, vibrating in resonance with the other until their hums align. For a few minutes, they become a single, unified note in the botanical storm. Then, as the stalk beneath them begins to soften or the light shifts, they turn, loping off into the brightness, leaving nothing behind but a tiny, woven knot of fiber as a testament that someone else, once, walked this path.

They are the gardeners of a nursery that is trying to swallow them. They live, they weave, they hydrate, and they migrate, always looking for a place where the growth is just slow enough to be called a home, even if that home lasts only until the next cycle of the spores.
