To inhabit this place is to exist in a state of perpetual maintenance. The entities that dwell within the High-Pressure Foundry of the Lowlands are not "workers" in any biological sense, nor are they operators. They are the **Gasket-Keepers**, or more accurately, the *Infiltrated Assemblages*.

They are beings crafted from the same erratic logic as the machines. At first glance, a Gasket-Keeper might appear to be a loose pile of rags draped over a hydraulic actuator, but as the mist shifts, the figure resolves: it is a fusion of pale, translucent moss, articulated copper plating, and calcified bone. They are the result of the foundry’s own metabolism, growing out of the equipment as if the machinery were, over centuries of damp neglect, finally attempting to sprout a nervous system.

They do not have faces. Where a head might be, there is instead a brass aperture—a pressure-sensitive iris that clicks and whirrs, adjusting its focal length to the density of the vapor. Their bodies are spindly and elongated, designed to traverse the tethered pontoons without ever touching the liquid, shifting soil. From a distance, they move with a frantic, stuttering grace, their limbs—which look like a collection of salvaged piston rods joined by tough, fibrous liverwort—extending and retracting to reach into the heart of the machinery.

They do not speak. The sound they emit is sympathetic to the thrum-hiss of the foundry—a low-frequency vibration generated by the resonance of the brass vents in their chests. When two of them work in tandem, they press their chest-plates together, exchanging pressurized bile and hydraulic fluid in a silent, glistening transfusion. 

They are in love with the entropy of the basin. You will see them huddled over the weeping rock walls, their long, multi-jointed fingers—tipped with the same white crystalline salts that bloom on the limestone—carefully harvesting the decay. They are the gardeners of the breakdown. They find a piston arm dissolving under the chemical assault, and they do not mourn it; they strip away the oxidized sludge, feed it to the moss patches growing on their own joints, and then guide the secondary arm into position with a tenderness that is both mechanical and deeply mournful.

They carry no tools. They *are* the tools. If a valve needs to be turned in a place too tight for a human hand, a Gasket-Keeper will simply allow an appendage to soften and reform into a wrench, the blue arc-light of their internal welding-spark flickering in their marrow. 

And, occasionally, one will stop. It will find a spot on the weeping limestone, perhaps near a cluster of old, twitching brass dials, and it will simply allow itself to be absorbed. It will sit until the salt blooms over its copper limbs, until the liverwort claims its torso, and until it becomes just another feature of the canyon wall—a new, permanent anchor for the chains that hold the foundry together. 

They are not concerned with output. They are the metabolic byproduct of the machine’s desire to keep spinning. They live to ensure that the loop remains closed, that the pressure remains constant, and that the grey, cinematic light of this place never falls upon a finished, static thing. They are the living evidence that even in a place of total decay, there is a fierce, wet will to remain, to process, and to endlessly, beautifully, rebuild.
