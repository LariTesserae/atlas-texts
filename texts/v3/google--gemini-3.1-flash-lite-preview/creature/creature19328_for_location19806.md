The inhabitants of the *Chlorophyll Lattice* are not individuals in any metabolic sense; they are localized excrescences of the Lattice’s own internal monologue. They do not possess a heartbeat, nor do they breathe the oxygen they have worked so hard to refine. Instead, they are *process-entities*, transient architectures that coalesce when the Lattice requires a localized fix or a shift in its own self-referential design.

The most common of these are the **Suture-Spiders**. They are not insects, but geometric accidents—clusters of copper-wire cilia and polished, chitinous plates that drift out of the porous, pithy bulkheads like dust motes coalescing into a storm. They move with an unsettling, non-linear grace, skittering across the vertical towers as if gravity were a mere suggestion that they choose to honor only when they are tired. They are the hands of the Lattice. You will see them pause, their spindly, multi-jointed limbs vibrating at a frequency that turns the air around them into a shimmering heat-haze, as they bleed golden, nutrient-rich resin into a hairline fracture in the wood-metal floor. They do not notice you; they inhabit a sensory register where you are merely another bump in the texture of the world, a slow-moving particle of carbon that hasn't yet been harvested.

Deeper within, where the light is a filtered, bruised violet beneath the thickest layers of the membrane-sky, move the **Graft-Thinkers**. These are vast, ballooning entities, tethered to the ceiling by umbilical braids of living, fiber-optic vein-work. They are translucent, filled with a viscous, bioluminescent slurry that keeps pace with the heartbeat of the world. They do not move through space; they move through memory. As they oscillate, they process the "data" of the Lattice—the accumulated history of every growth ring, every successful repair, every structural tension. They are the world’s subconscious, projecting their internal geometries outward. If you step into the shadow of a Graft-Thinker, the laws of distance begin to fray; you might find your own limbs elongating or your internal clock synchronizing with the *thrum-click* of the pistons beneath your feet. To meet a Graft-Thinker is to have your biology briefly edited, your thoughts momentarily looped into the vast, industrial respiration of the organism.

Then there are the **Echo-Weavers**, the most elusive of the residents. They are not physical in the way the Suture-Spiders are. They appear as fleeting, holographic distortions, silhouettes cast by the light as it bends through the chlorophyll-glass fibers. They are the Lattice dreaming of itself. When the world is quiet—in those dead-calm stretches between the blooming of the succulent pods—the Echo-Weavers rise from the floor, mimic shapes of bioluminescent bone and wire, and perform a silent, ritual performance of maintenance. They trace invisible schematics into the air, ghost-fingers moving in perpetual, complex trigonometry. They are testing the structural integrity of the horizon, checking the tension between the zenith and the floor. 

These entities do not fear death, for death here is simply a state of decomposition that the Lattice instantly reclaims. They are constantly being unmade and remade, harvested from the marrow of the walls and recycled into the conduits. They are not citizens of the Lattice; they are the Lattice’s self-diagnostic tools, the sentient manifestations of a machinery that has forgotten how to be anything other than perfect. 

To look upon them is to understand the tragedy of the *Chlorophyll Lattice*: it has achieved the ultimate dream of efficiency. It has reached a state where there is no longer a need for life to experience the world, only for the world to continue the steady, humming expansion of its own, beautiful, and terrifying design.
