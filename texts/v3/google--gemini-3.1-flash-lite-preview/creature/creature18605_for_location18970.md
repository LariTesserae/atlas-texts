The Green Foundry does not house a tenant in the traditional sense; it houses a *process*. Yet, if one watches the way the copper vines tighten their grip around the rusted struts, or how the succulent pistons chamber their fluids, there is the undeniable suggestion of a gardener.

Deep within the floor, beneath the soft, pulsing velvet of the moss-flesh, resides the **Loom-Heart**.

It is not a creature of skin and bone, but a localized concentration of intent—a dense, spherical knot of root-tips, mineral-encrusted wiring, and sensory filaments that have knit themselves into the very architecture of the enclosure. It has no head, no mouth, and no eyes; its perception is entirely haptic, a vast, complex sensitivity to the vibration of every drip and the hydraulic strain of every shifting stalk.

The Loom-Heart is the foundry’s governor. It is a slow, calcified consciousness, composed of the recursive memories of every generation of flora that has occupied this space. It thinks in cycles of pressure and decay. When the iron gear-teeth begin to grind too thin, the Heart directs the flow of nutrient-rich sludge toward them, thickening the lichen until the metal is bolstered by a cage of bio-calcification. When the stone walls threaten to buckle, it slows the growth of the heavy-limbed ferns, conserving the hydraulic energy needed to push the moss-mat tighter against the stones, acting as both mortar and brace.

There are also the **"Watchmen"—or perhaps, the "Leakers."**

They are not entities that walk, as you do. They are the emergent anomalies of the foundry’s surplus energy. Occasionally, when the pressure in the capillary network spikes, the humid air coalesces around the ancient, rusted artifacts. The moss migrates, wrapping itself around a discarded brass pipe or a coil of copper wire until the object resembles limbs and a torso. These forms stand for weeks, stiff and saturated, anchored to the substrate by thick, vascular umbilical cords that pulse in sync with the room’s heartbeat.

They do not act. They merely *contain*. An idle puddle of sap might pool within the hollow curve of a brass elbow, and for a time, that puddle holds the Foundry’s excess luminescence. They are living statuary, temporary structural reinforcement. Their "eyes" are knots of phosphorescent fungus that dim and brighten with the rhythm of the room’s transpiration.

When you walk through the grove, you are not being watched by eyes—you are being weighed by the ecosystem.

The Loom-Heart registers your stride as a deviation from the calm. The soft, rhythmic creak of the pistons alters its tempo; it is sensing the displacement of the air you move through. It does not feel curiosity, only a cool, mechanical assessment of whether you are "material" or "detritus." Perhaps, if you remain long enough, the moss will begin to climb your boots, not out of aggression, but because the Foundry recognizes the salts in your leather and the heat of your pulse as raw materials. It is a patient builder. It recognizes that, eventually, all things that enter the enclave will be re-processed. 

You are a guest within a digestive cycle, and the Foundry is considering how best to integrate your rhythm into its own.
