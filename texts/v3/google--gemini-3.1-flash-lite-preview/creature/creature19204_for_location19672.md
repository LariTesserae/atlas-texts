The residents of this place are not citizens so much as they are localized realizations of the planet’s intent. They do not possess faces, nor do they possess names, for individuality is a concept that requires room to breathe, and here, there is only the collective, grinding press of the hive-strata.

At the lowest level, closest to the tectonic thrum, dwell the *Anchors*. They are flattened, disc-like entities, roughly the circumference of a city square, pressed flush against the obsidian vents. Their undersides are not fleshy; they are a matrix of heat-exchangers and copper-filigree lungs. They do not eat. They inhale the geothermal gasses escaping from the planet's core, straining the heavy metals through their porous, sponge-like dorsal plates. When they reach capacity, they pulse—a rhythmic, sickening expansion—and harden, their bodies effectively becoming the very foundation they cling to. They are the mortar of this world, living fossils that solidify as they age, leaving behind concentric rings of calcified chitin that serve as the steps upon which the rest of the ecosystem climbs.

Moving higher, along the vertical cliffs, are the *Weavers of Current*. These are the hound-sized insects, though "insect" is a cruel reduction of their complexity. Their wings are not membranes, but arrays of conductive filament—fine, humming ribbons of metallic protein that vibrate at frequencies capable of slicing through the dense, frozen air. They operate with a cold, terrifying efficiency. They do not hunt for sustenance; they harvest the ionization gradients. You see them hovering in the blinding light, their bodies shimmering with the static discharge they have collected. They serve as the distribution network, flying in tight, geometric squadrons to plug their abdomens into the crystalline pipes of the organic towers. They are the spark plugs, the lightning-bringers, the kinetic janitors that ensure the mountain-machine never loses its charge.

And then, deeper within the damp, emerald-velvet fissures, reside the *Loomers*. These are the most elusive and the most disturbing. They are spindly, multi-jointed architects of soft, translucent flesh, barely visible against the bioluminescent moss. While the Anchors build the foundation and the Weavers manage the energy, the Loomers manage the biological architecture. 

With limbs that terminate in fine, needle-like extrusions, they spend their lifecycles stitching the mosses to the basalt. They are the ones who direct the growth of the succulents, coaxing them into arches, bridges, and stabilizing struts. They move with a terrifying, stuttering gait, like a film flickering at the wrong speed. If you were to watch one, you would notice it never sleeps; it only enters a state of high-pressure stasis, its internal gears clicking as it performs the endless, microscopic tasks of structural maintenance. 

They do not communicate with sound, for the air is already too crowded with the roar of the mountain. Instead, they touch—their antennae constantly brushing against the vibrating chitin of their kin. It is a language of tactile pressure, a binary code of *here* and *there*, *cold* and *current*, *harden* and *expand*. 

There is no malice in them. When they brush against you, or when you happen upon a cluster of them in the dim, freezing deep, they do not perceive you as an intelligence or an interloper. You are merely an obstruction, a variable in the structural integrity of the crag. If you were to stand still for too long, they would simply incorporate you. They would knit the mosses around your limbs, reinforce your joints with their steaming, resinous secretions, and wire your nervous system into the planetary clockwork. 

You would not die. You would simply be integrated. You would become a part of the humming, crystalline, shivering silence, another bolt in the great factory that requires no purpose other than its own continuation. You would finally understand, as they do, that there is no "outside" to this world—there is only the machine, the growth, and the cold that locks it all in an eternal, perfect, and terrifying stasis.
