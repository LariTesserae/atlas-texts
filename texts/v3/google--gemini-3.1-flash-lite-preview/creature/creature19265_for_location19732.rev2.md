The inhabitants of this restless, sweating cathedral are not so much a species as they are an inevitability—the *Vexi*, beings who have surrendered the concept of the "individual" to the architectural imperative of the deep-geode.

At a glance, the Vexi look like mobile knots of the forest itself. They are hunched, spindly figures, their bodies composed of a hardened, fibrous cellulose that mimics the darker, resin-coated bark of the central nodes. They do not possess skin; instead, they are covered in a mosaic of overlapping, scale-like plates of dried chlorophyll-rich husks, which shimmer with an iridescent, oily sheen. Where their joints should be—shoulders, knees, elbows—there are instead clusters of sensitive, protruding cilia that taste the air for changes in the geode’s frantic chemical composition.

Their faces are long, tapered wedges, dominated by a single, wide, horizontal aperture that serves as both mouth and sensory array. They have no eyes; they do not need them in a place where the light is a flickering, bioluminescent pulse of the architecture itself. Instead, they perceive the world through the soles of their broad, prehensile feet, which act as seismic tuning forks, reading the *thrum-snap-hiss* of the expanding world to navigate the shifting floor.

The Vexi do not "build" in the way sentient beings are usually understood; they are the geode’s gardeners, surgeons, and nervous system.

You see them huddled in the geometric nodes, their long, multi-jointed fingers busy with the fiber looms. They are weaving garments and tools not from wool or metal, but from the very veins of the chamber. A Vexi will press its palm against a pulsating, translucent root until the flow of sap slows, then use a jagged obsidian shard to excise a strip of living tissue. This tissue, still warm and vibrating, is then braided into cables, scaffolding, or decorative fetishes. It is an act of communion—they are constantly re-engineering their environment to keep pace with its violent, unchecked expansion.

They move with a frantic, twitchy grace, mirroring the chittering scavengers that share their space. When a Vexi needs to travel, it does not walk so much as it "tacks" against the growth, hooking its appendages into the structural ribs of the walls, moving along the canopy as the ceiling rearranges itself around them.

The most unsettling aspect of the Vexi is their silence. Despite the deafening roar of the forest—the creaking, the snapping, the endless chittering—the Vexi themselves emit no vocalizations. They communicate via the subtle tightening and loosening of their own internal fibers, a rhythmic, tactile language that vibrates through the substrate they stand upon. To stand near a group of them is to feel a low, sympathetic hum travel up through your own bones; they are constantly broadcasting the state of the room, the health of the veins, the encroaching pressure of a new root-mass.

They treat the geode as a starving, beloved god. They prune the walls to prevent structural collapse; they feed the hungry bladders by guiding them toward high-static pockets; they carve their geometric seals into the bark to "anchor" a node, pleading with the forest to grant them a moment’s stability. 

But they are losing. Every geometric incision they make is slowly being clouded by the greenish-white fuzz of new growth. Every loom is being woven into the floor-mat. The Vexi know this. They do not fight it. They work with a desperate, frantic fervor, knowing that eventually, the garden will grow over them entirely, sealing them into the walls like fossils in amber—the final, quiet components of a world that is too busy living to ever let them rest.
