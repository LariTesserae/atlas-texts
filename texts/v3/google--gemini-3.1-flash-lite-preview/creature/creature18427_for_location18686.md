The inhabitants of this geode-city—the *Lithos-skry*—do not hold individual shapes so much as they hold an individual purpose. They are long, segmented beings, their carapaces the color of skimmed milk, possessing the fragile, glassy quality of blown sugar. Beneath the translucent armor, one can see the rhythmic, sluggish flow of hemolymph, a pale blue ichor that barely pulses, keeping time with the eternal dripping of the ceiling.

Their frames are masterworks of articulation. They possess twelve limbs, each tipped with delicate, needle-fine hooks designed not for aggression, but for the singular task of anchoring. To move is to unhook and rehook, a slow-motion migration across the vaulted limestone. When they congregate, they do not stand; they intermesh. They hook their legs into the limbs of their neighbors, forming living lattices that bridge the gaps between the mineral shelves, turning the population into a secondary, breathing architecture.

There are no eyes upon their faces. Where eyes might be, there are only clusters of iridescent silica pits, sensitive not to the spectrum of light, but to the subtle thermal flux of the humidity. They perceive the world as a topographical map of temperature. To them, a draft is a language, and the slow cooling of a mineral deposit is a calendar. 

Their lives are consumed by the "Polishing." 

They carry tools fashioned from the calcified remains of their ancestors—small, blunt scrapers of bone—and they spend their cycles working the stone. It is not an act of construction, but of refinement. They move with an obsessive, mathematical precision, scraping away a micro-fraction of mineral deposit, shifting a single grain of sediment, ensuring the flow of the water-veins remains perfect. They are the gardeners of a crystalline forest, pruning the lichen so that the light remains consistent, never flaring, never dimming. 

They do not speak, for the atmosphere forbids the transmission of sound. Instead, they communicate through a process of bio-electric discharge: a faint, shivering hum that ripples through their carapaces. When two of them meet, their antennae touch—soft, feathery filaments that quiver with high-frequency tremors. In this touch, entire sequences of information are exchanged. They pass along the history of the drips, the health of the lichen, and the structural integrity of the arch above them, all in a silent, static flare of neural potential.

There is a profound, terrifying grace to their existence. They do not fear the weight of the tectonic plate; to them, it is the sky, the great, silent guardian that keeps their world enclosed. They are not waiting for a change, for a break in the cycle, or for the sun they have never known to warm their cold, chalky skin.
