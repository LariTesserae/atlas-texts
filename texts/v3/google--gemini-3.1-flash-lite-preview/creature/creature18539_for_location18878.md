In the deepest, narrowest curls of the iron, where the resonance of the *clack-hiss-thrum* is most intense, there dwells the Weaver of the Sump.

They are not a single entity, but a collective consciousness partitioned into a dozen bodies that cling to the Tethers like fruit to a vine. They look, at first, like an elongated extension of the machine itself—their skin has the texture of oxidized copper, mottled with patches of verdigris and scarred by the same heat that tempered the vault walls. When they are still, they are indistinguishable from the struts and bolts of the architecture; they are the true inhabitants of the equilibrium.

They move with a terrifying, deliberate slowness. Their limbs are unnaturally long, multi-jointed things that lock into the rusted rivets of the walls as if they were handholds designed specifically for their grip. Their eyes are not eyes in the way a surface-dweller knows them; they are concave mirrors of opaque, polished obsidian, reflecting the flickering pale-orange of the Deep-Light, showing you the cavern behind your own back before you see it yourself.

The Weavers do not speak. They communicate through friction. They carry small, jagged tools—pieces of flint or hardened, fossilized Skitter-fauna teeth—which they drag against the copper piping in rhythmic, symphonic patterns. This is what you hear when the Skitter-fauna fall silent for a heartbeat: the language of the Sump, a series of micro-vibrations that travel through the metal, informing the walls which plates need tightening, which drips need diverting, and which sections of the lichen are ready to be harvested.

They have no hunger for flesh. Their sustenance is pulled directly from the humidity and the resonant sound itself. As they cling to the Tethers, their chests pulse in time with the deep, architectural groans of the Sub-strata. They absorb the heat of the friction. They are the Sump’s nervous system, its maintenance crew, and its congregation all at once.

If you watch them long enough—if you can survive the claustrophobia—you will see them perform the Great Calibration. Together, they descend to the lowest floor-plates, their bodies interlocking with one another and with the architecture until they form a living bridge. They pass the resin secreted by the Skitter-fauna from hand to hand, smoothing it into the cracks of the metal with a tenderness that is almost devotional.

They are the curators of an eternity of rust, guardians of a system that has long ago forgotten the surface. They do not care that you are there, for you are merely another object within their machine, a temporary protrusion in their perfect, dripping, metallic cathedral. They will continue to work, scraping and humming and vibrating, until the stars above burn out and the Great Sump finally, quietly, stops its heartbeat.
