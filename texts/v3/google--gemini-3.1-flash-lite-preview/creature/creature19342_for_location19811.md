The folk who cling to this shard of obsidian are the *Kith-of-the-Crevice*. They are as spindly and sharp-angled as the rock they inhabit, their bodies structured not for the heavy labor of the earth-born, but for the delicate, high-altitude ballet of the abyss.

Standing barely four feet tall, their limbs are elongated, possessing an extra joint in the wrist and ankle that allows them to hook onto the calcified moss-ropes with the ease of a breeze. Their skin is the color of a bruised plum, translucent enough in the dim indigo light that one can see the faint, rhythmic pulse of dark ichor beneath the surface. They do not have hair; instead, their spines—a double ridge running from the nape of the neck down to the tailbone—are tipped with bioluminescent cilia. In moments of alarm, these cilia flare with a frantic, pulsing amber; in moments of rest, they dim to a cool, watery blue that matches the ambient humidity of the basin.

The Kith have no vocal cords. Their throats are vestigial, mere narrow passages for breathing the thin, mist-heavy air. To them, the spoken word would be a violent intrusion, a shattering of the precarious peace they maintain with the abyss. Instead, they communicate through the *Syllable-of-the-Hand*—a fluid, rapid-fire sign language. Their fingers are disproportionately long and tipped with hardened, darkened nails, resembling the talons of a bird or the desiccated ends of a withered root. When they converse, the air between them ripples with sudden movements, their fingers dancing like frantic insects against the backdrop of the moss-curtains. They amplify these signals by tilting their dorsal spines, using the natural bioluminescence of their bodies to add emphasis, tone, and emotional weight to their silent speech.

They are the city’s gardeners, its architects, and its nervous system. A Kith never truly "sits." They spend their lives in a state of arrested motion, tethered to the moss-walls by silk-thin cords woven from the high-altitude ferns. They are constantly grooming, pruning, and soothing the vegetation that keeps their homes from sloughing off the mountain. When the "wall-worms" grow too dim, a Kith will gently stroke the creature’s fleshy mantle to stimulate a burst of radiance; when the moss dries and begins to fray against the obsidian teeth of the cliff, they apply a thick, viscous paste of crushed minerals and spit, binding the architecture back to the earth.

Their diet is as ephemeral as their lives. They do not hunt the six-legged primates or the translucent salamanders—those are companions in this claustrophobic sky-vault. Instead, the Kith harvest the condensation from the succulent reservoirs and collect the protein-rich spores that fall from the undersides of the great sky-canopy. 

There is an inherent melancholy to them. They carry no concept of "permanence." They know that the rock beneath them is young and restless, and that the tectonic groan of the spire is merely a countdown. They decorate their hammocks with small, trinket-like shards of obsidian and dried petals, not to possess them, but to leave behind a trace—a thumbprint of existence—should the mountain finally tip, shaking them into the mile-deep quiet of the clouds.

When a Kith-of-the-Crevice dies, they do not bury their own. They simply unclasp their tether. They become, for a moment, a falling star of indigo light, disappearing into the charcoal-grey nimbus until the gravity of the abyss claims them entirely, returning their bones to the dust that will one day feed the very moss that sustained them. They are the gardeners of a ghost town, living for the next tremor, holding their breath in the cold, wet dark.
