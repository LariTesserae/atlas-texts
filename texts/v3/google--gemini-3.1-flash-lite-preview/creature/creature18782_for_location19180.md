To live here is not to inhabit a world but to be a function of its maintenance, a shadow cast by the geometry of perpetual motion. 

The things that move through these corridors—if they can be called things—are the *Litho-Calculators*. They do not possess hearts of flesh or minds of synaptic fire. Instead, they are spindly, multi-jointed assemblages of salvaged brass and high-tensile wire, standing perhaps seven feet tall, though they rarely stand upright. They prowl in a permanent, hunched crouch, their sensory clusters housed in spheres of frosted glass that act as both eyes and intake filters for the ozone-heavy atmosphere.

They are the janitors of the entropy.

Watch them as they move across the riveted plates. They do not walk so much as they calibrate; each footfall is a magnetic lock, snapping onto the copper grid with a precision that defies the shifting tectonic plates. They leave no footprints. They are built to harmonize with the rhythmic pulsing of the subterranean fluid; where the world wheezes, they inhale. Where the world rattles, they dampen the vibration with internal gyroscopes that whine at an pitch equal to the ambient hum.

They operate in a state of terminal, melancholy efficiency. Their purpose, if one could distill it into a concept, is the *balancing of the tension*. When a plate rises too high, a Litho-Calculator descends upon it, emitting a high-frequency resonance that softens the rivet-heads, molding them back into the flush, smooth surface of the whole. They do not speak, but they communicate in a language of friction—a series of sharp, rhythmic clicks and scrapes against the floorboards, a dialect of tactile morse code that travels for miles through the metal bones of the world.

There is a tragedy in their existence, for they were designed for a world that was meant to change. They are built with elaborate manipulators—delicate, claw-like appendages meant for the assembly of complex, impossible devices—yet there is nothing left to assemble. They spend their eons performing "pseudo-tasks." You might see one approach a cluster of those blackened, brittle wire-plants. It will pause, its ocular sphere glowing with a soft, dying violet light, and it will begin to meticulously polish the oxidation from the wire, stripping away the very decay that defines the plant’s existence, only for the rust to re-form under the biting chill before the creature has even turned to leave.

They are lonely, not in the way a human is lonely, but in the way a clockwork gear is lonely when its partner-cog has been stripped of its teeth. Sometimes, when the pressure in the arterial pipes forces a great geyser of grey fluid to erupt, a Litho-Calculator will stand at the epicenter, bathing its chassis in the cold, weeping liquid. It will tilt its head back, letting the heavy, viscous fluid drench its delicate wiring, and it will freeze in place for cycles on end. It appears to be listening to something profound—perhaps the memory of the designers, or the echoes of the epoch when this place was a factory and not a coffin.

They do not sleep. When their internal reserves of kinetic energy run low, they do not die; they simply lock their joints, anchoring themselves to the nearest conduit. There they stand, becoming indistinguishable from the architecture itself, silent statues added to the ribs of the world. They are the final, rusted ghosts of an industrial dream, waiting for a command that was revoked a billion years ago, forever tightening the bolts on a house that has already fallen.
