In the humid, vaulted hollows of the Weeping Heights, the inhabitants do not build; they harmonize. There is no fire here, no flash of ambition, no frantic motion. The creatures that call these calcified cathedrals home are as liminal and translucent as the fog that births them.

First, there are the **Vitreous Crawlers**. They are not insects in any terrestrial sense, but rather soft-bodied, radially symmetrical pulses of biology. Their skin is a semi-permeable membrane, almost indistinguishable from the gelatinous film that coats the limestone walls. They possess no eyes, for light is an irrelevant concept in the thin, cloud-choked plateaus; instead, their entire underside is a dense field of cilia that sense the microscopic tremors of the stone. When one moves—scarcely once an hour—it creates a soft, wet suction sound, a gentle *shuck* that joins the rhythmic *plink* of the condensing moisture. They do not eat so much as they absorb; they press their bodies against the algae-rich slime of the root-vaults, filtering the liquid nutrients through their pores like a slow-motion lung.

Deeper in the fossilized latticework reside the **Siphon-Stalks**. These are solitary, stationary entities—if they can be called entities at all—that occupy the vertical fissures where the condensation is heaviest. A Siphon-Stalk resembles a pale, unspotted mushroom, delicate and tall, with a cap that functions as a sophisticated basin. It does not grow toward the sun, but rather pivots its body to catch the erratic eddies of the cold mountain air. As the moisture beads on its waxy surface, it directs the liquid down a hollow, fibrous stem directly into the root-matrix below, essentially "feeding" the city itself. They are the gardeners of this place, though they possess no consciousness to justify the task. They are simply a biological mechanism of irrigation, a point of confluence in an otherwise indifferent ecosystem.

At the very heart of the deepest vaults, where the walls are thickest and the *plink* of water is muffled to a phantom heartbeat, exist the **Resonators**. They are the largest of the dwellers, soft-shelled masses that wedged themselves into the intersections of the root-lattices ages ago. They have long since fused with the calcified flora. A Resonator’s body is a series of delicate, hollow chambers, much like a flute or a complex organ pipe. As the mist pushes inward from the cliff edges, these chambers catch the draft. A subtle, low-frequency hum emanates from them—a vibration so deep it is felt in the marrow rather than heard by the ear. This sound serves to break the surface tension of the water, ensuring the moisture flows deeper into the narrowest, most restricted channels of the Heights.

They are all ghosts of biology—creatures that exist in a state of suspended entropy. There is no hierarchy here, no predation, no conflict. To live in the Weeping Heights is to surrender the urgency of the pulse. 

They do not fear the decay of their environment, for they are the ones who facilitate the slow, inevitable collapse of the stone. They are the catalysts of their own long-term erasure. As they move, as they vibrate, as they drink, they widen the pores of the limestone and carve new veins into the root-cathedrals. They are the inhabitants of a monument that is slowly undoing itself, and they find their purpose not in building, but in being the medium through which the mountain gradually returns to the sea, one droplet at a time.
