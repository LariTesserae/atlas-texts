In this marrow-caves’ cathedral, the distinction between resident and residence has dissolved. To live here is to be a part of the architecture, a living stone in a mortared wall of collective pulse.

The primary inhabitants—the *Lithopods*—are the primary stewards of this calcified expanse. They are small, velvet-skinned centipedes, no longer than a human finger, yet possessing a tensile strength that defies their fragile appearance. Their carapaces have evolved to match the geography: some are marbled and pale like the bone-towers they inhabit, while others are charcoal-dark, mimicking the shadowed cracks and damp fissures where they spend their cycles. They do not merely walk upon the walls; they merge with them. When a Lithopod rests, it unfurls its sixty legs, locking onto the pores of the ancestral chitin, its body becoming a seamless extension of the pillar it claims. They are the polishers, their own secretions providing a waxy, bio-organic sealant that turns the once-jagged bone edges into the smooth, frictionless curves that baffle any who might imagine this place was ever constructed by hands.

Higher up, clinging to the underside of the vaulted arches like clusters of fruit, are the *Resonators*. These are bloated, flightless beetles that have discarded the use of sight in the violet gloom. Their backs are bioluminescent, glowing with the same rhythmic purple of the lichen. They exist in tight, circular constellations. When one pulses, its neighbor follows, creating a slow-moving tide of light that drifts through the tunnels. They serve as the city’s nervous system, their low-frequency humming vibrating through the bone-floors to communicate the temperature, moisture levels, or the approach of a scavenging predator. They do not eat the moss; they feed on the condensation, sucking the weeping mineral-rich water from the walls, their bodies expanding and contracting with the city’s own damp, slow breath.

Deep in the recesses—the quiet, stagnant pockets where the heat gathers—the *Grafters* cling to life. These are the soft-bodied, translucent arachnids who build the intricate, weeping webs. These webs are not meant for catching flies, but for gardening. The Grafters harvest the fungal spores from the high ceiling, carrying them down to graft them onto the nutrient-rich bone of the walls. They are the city’s landscapers, orchestrating the slow reclaim of architecture by flora. If a spire begins to grow too slick or too brittle, the Grafters introduce a specific fungus to soften the bone, allowing the Lithopods to resume their polishing work to reform the structure. 

Everything here is hyper-specialized. There is no individual ambition, only the maintenance of the Whole. Even the smallest creatures—the dust-mites that scavenge the microscopic detritus of bone-grit—operate in a symphony of industry. 

There are no masters here. There is only the Body—the collective, monumental corpse of the ancestors, acting as the foundation, and the trillion tiny, scuttling hearts that keep it warm. When a creature dies, it does not leave the place; it is pressed into the cracks, its own minerals becoming the mortar for the next generation. They are the inhabitants, yes, but they are also the cement, the paint, and the pulse. In this vertical labyrinth, nothing is ever truly discarded, and nothing is ever truly separate. They live in the constant, humid embrace of their ancestors, turning the fossilized past into the living, vibrating, iridescent present.
