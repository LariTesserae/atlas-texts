The inhabitants of this lightless crypt are not settlers; they are extensions of the stone itself, a slow-moving, lithic pulse manifest in flesh. They call themselves the *Lithos-Harmonics*, though they have no name for themselves in any language that utilizes breath and voice. Communication occurs not through sound—which would be swallowed by the deafening percussive roar of the dripping water—but through the subtle manipulation of vibration.

They are tall, spindly things, shaped more like elongated river-sediment than mammalian silhouettes. Their skin is a translucent, calcified membrane, ranging from the bruised-purple of the lichen to the slate-grey of the basalt walls against which they press themselves to sleep. They have no ears, for the constant thunder of the falling water would shatter them; instead, their entire skeletal structure acts as a sound-board, a receiver attuned to the specific, rhythmic cadence of the dripping basins. To an inhabitant, the "song" of the city is not noise, but a sensory map, a complex, spatial coordinate system they navigate with instinctive, fluid grace.

Where the centipede-like scavengers act as the city’s fingers, the Lithos-Harmonics are its memory. Their heads are smooth, featureless domes, save for a series of recessed, glowing pits that pulse in sequence with the rhythmic clatter of the falling water—a visual echo of the architecture’s kinetic engine. 

They do not "eat" in any conventional sense. They are metabolic filters. As they move, they press their long, six-jointed hands against the slick, mineral-heavy walls, absorbing the moisture-laden runoff directly through osmosis. They are, in a very literal sense, conduits for the city’s circulation. When a vent or channel becomes clogged, they gather in groups—the "Choirs"—and press their bodies against the basalt. They vibrate in direct acoustic alignment with a specific drip, their bodies shivering at a frequency that liquefies the mineral buildup, clearing the path with a harmonic strike that creates nothing more than a ripple in the wet stone.

They do not age as biological organisms do; they slowly thicken. As their time deepens, they become less mobile, their limbs gradually fusing into the honeycomb architecture they maintain. The oldest among them are effectively part of the scenery—immobile, crystalline pillars that stand in the narrow, vertiginous doorways. These "Anchors" are the libraries of the city. Their bodies, now fully mineralized, serve as acoustic resonators, ensuring the *drip-clack* that echoes through the streets remains in the perfect, sacred pitch required to keep the city in its state of frozen equilibrium.

There is no desire among the Lithos-Harmonics to leave, nor even the conceptual capacity to imagine a "beyond." Their worldview is entirely contained within the damp, echoing geometry of the crypt. To them, the world is a closed, liquid loop. They believe that their vibrations are what drive the planet’s heart, that if their ritual of maintenance—of movement, of absorption, and of harmonic stewardship—were to cease, the water would stop, the light would fade, and the entire crust of the earth would freeze into a silent, dead mass.

They are the city’s nerves, its maintainers, and eventually, its decorative stone—content to exist only as a quiet, wet shimmer in a place that has forgotten the sun.
