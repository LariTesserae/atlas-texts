In this pressurized cradle, the inhabitants are not distinct from the architecture; they are its nervous system, its antibodies, and its ghosts. 

At the base of the hierarchy, swarming in the shadows of the copper conduits, are the *Lace-Weavers*—the arthropods you sensed. They are pale, translucent things, their carapaces like frosted glass, revealing the rhythmic thrum of pale blue fluid circulating through their bodies. They do not possess eyes, for light here is an infection, a sickness. Instead, they navigate by the language of vibration. Their slender, multi-jointed limbs tap against the hexagonal steel plates with the cadence of a frantic telegraph operator. When a seam begins to buckle under the crushing brine from above, a dozen of them converge. They secrete a dark, amber-hued resin that hardens instantly into a weld stronger than the original steel, a living fix for a dying house. They are obsessed; they do not eat in the traditional sense, but scrub the oxidation from the pipes with rasp-like mandibles, consuming the rust to preserve the structural integrity of their world.

Above them, coiled within the gargantuan, dormant gear-shafts like massive, dormant springs, are the *Archive-Snakes*. These are not biological, but a hybrid of metal memory and creeping, synthetic moss. They are long, segmented coils wrapped in copper wire and covered in the same damp, bioluminescent fungus that clings to the walls. They rarely move. When they do, it is a tectonic event, a slow, screeching migration from one sector of the cylinder to another. 

The *Archive-Snakes* are the curators of the "final form." They hold the kinetic energy of the machine in their bodies. If a rusted gear-train begins to falter, an *Archive-Snake* will uncoil and drape its own heavy, sparking length across the teeth of the cogs, acting as a living bridge, allowing the machinery to grind onward through its own decay. They are heavy with the weight of the last millenary; deep inside their copper-veined bellies, they store the "sounds" of the outside—the memory of the tides, the pressure of the abyss, and the fading hum of a world that once had purpose.

Deep in the dampest, lowest recesses, where the condensation pools into shallow, freezing lakes, reside the *Echo-Minds*. They were once, perhaps, the engineers of this place, though they have long since shed the vanity of human shape. They are gelatinous, sprawling entities that have fused their neural lattices directly into the main, submerged cooling pumps. 

The *Echo-Minds* do not think in words; they think in currents and resistance. They perceive the ocean’s pressure not as a threat, but as a meditation—a constant, rhythmic companion. They are the ones who orchestrate the orchestra. When the atmosphere grows too saturated with moisture, they pulse, drawing the heavy, salty air into their porous, sponge-like bodies to filter out the poisons of the aging iron. 

They are the loneliness of this place given corporeal form. They dream in cycles. A single thought can take a decade to traverse the length of the cylinder, manifesting as a sudden, sharp hiss of steam or a shuttering light from the Pipe-Stalks. They know the machine is finished; they know the maintenance is an act of perpetual, holy futility. Yet, they continue. They maintain the seal not because they hope to be rescued, but because the structure is their skin, and its collapse would be their own extinction.

In the center of the vault, beneath the weeping ceiling, there is nothing living at all—only the persistent, cold, mechanical heartbeat. The inhabitants have transformed this space into a sanctuary of maintenance, a perfectly closed loop of survival. They are the caretakers of a tomb that refuses to die, living in the space between the rust and the ocean, forever tending to the slow, beautiful disintegration of their own cage.
