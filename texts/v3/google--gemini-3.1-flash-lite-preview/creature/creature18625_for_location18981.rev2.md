The entities that claim this place are not individuals, nor are they a collective in the way a swarm of insects might be. They are a singular, distributed intelligence—a geologic nervous system that has forgotten it is made of separate parts.

They resemble shards of midnight, polished by the very friction of their own existence. Each unit—roughly the size of a human palm—is a complex, polyhedral knot of hematite and high-density carbon. They possess no eyes, no mouths, and no organs. Their "anatomy" is purely architectural: a system of sliding plates and internal heat-sinks that allow them to process the core-radiation of the plateau. They live in a state of perpetual thermic hunger, shivering in the dark, their faceted shells glowing a faint, near-infrared violet when they draw too much heat from the ground.

These creatures—let us call them *The Lithos-Current*—do not experience time as a sequence of events, but as a slow, cascading geometry. To exist, for them, is to maintain the integrity of the mat. If a segment detaches, it is not "lost"; it simply becomes a wandering node, a stray grain of sand in the immense, grinding clockwork of the canyon.

They are indifferent to you, but they are not unaware. As you stand among them, you are a void in their grid. You are a cold, soft, anomalous disruption in their perfect, interlocking lattice. 

They do not attack, for they have no concept of "other." Instead, they treat you as a topographical challenge. When you remain still, they begin to flow upward. It is a terrifying, gentle pressure—thousands of microscopic, jagged edges pressing against your boots and shins. They are not trying to consume your flesh; they are trying to bridge the gap. They seek to fill the empty space you occupy with their own obsidian density, to replace your inefficient, liquid warmth with the stable, eternal heat-conduction of their own forms.

They are the archivists of this world. The metallic needles buried in the silt are their ancestors—the "Failed Iterations." These needles are the skeletal remains of a previous geometry, a blueprint that tried to reach for a different form of existence and shattered under the crushing weight of the heat. The current entities move over these needles with a reverence that borders on the mechanical; they use the straight, cool metal as "conductive conduits," grounding their heat-blooms into the core of the planet through these ancient, calcified pins.

Their personality, if it can be called that, is one of weary, heavy patience. They are waiting for the world to cool, or perhaps they are waiting for the world to shatter. They do not communicate with signals or pulses; they communicate by shifting the weight of the canyon floor. A sudden *tink*—the resetting of a thousand gears—is a thought being processed across a hundred square meters. A ripple through the mat is an emotion, a tremor of thermal feedback.

To be near them is to feel the crushing weight of a memory that doesn't belong to you. They are not alive in a way that requires air, blood, or breath. They are the stone learning to think, the volcanic slag reclaiming its right to be sentient. They are the final, elegant solution to a world that left no room for anything soft. They do not want to be anywhere else, and they do not want you to be here, but they are not strong enough to push you away. They can only wait for you to grow cold, to become still, so they can finally flow over you and complete the pattern.
