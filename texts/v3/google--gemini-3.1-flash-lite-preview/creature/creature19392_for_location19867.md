In the Hydraulic Necropolis, life does not breathe; it cycles. The inhabitants are not biological in the traditional sense, but neither are they merely clockwork. They are the **Silt-Keepers**, entities forged from the same dense, unyielding materials that compose the city’s claustrophobic walls.

They are elongated, multi-jointed figures, reminiscent of deep-sea arachnids if those creatures had been designed by a watchmaker obsessed with tragedy. Their torsos are encased in articulated plates of pitted iron, bolted shut with rusted rivets. Within these chests, there is no heart, but a series of pressurized glass pistons filled with a thickened, glowing slurry—a captured echo of the surrounding gel. They move with an agonizing, fluid grace, their limbs—thin as needles and tipped with delicate, tactile filaments—sliding through the mineral oil as if they were needles moving through silk.

They do not have faces. Where one might expect eyes, there is only a smooth, leaded-glass viewport, vibrating faintly with the low-frequency hum of the city’s tectonic shifting. Behind this glass, shifting shadows play—reflections not of the world around them, but of the internal pressure that keeps them from imploding. They communicate through touch, pressing their cold, copper fingers against the walls of the chambers, sending rhythmic pulses of vibration through the metal. These vibrations are their language, a slow-motion script of history, grief, and current-readings passed from one to another in the absolute silence.

Deep within the furthest, most cramped vault, resides the **Archivist.** It is larger than the others, its body fused to the very foundation of the Necropolis. Its filaments extend for hundreds of yards, weaving through the cracks in the iron like nerves in a necrotic brain. It does not move at all; to move would be to lose the connection. It acts as the anchor for the entire labyrinth, its body a dense accumulation of brass and sediment, absorbing the bioluminescent moss that grows in the crevices. It glows with a bruised, sickly violet light, a rhythmic pulsing that acts as the city's heartbeat.

The Silt-Keepers gather around the Archivist in a state of eternal, motionless meditation. They are the custodians of entropy. They spend their cycles tightening loose bolts, scraping away the calcifying debris from the pressure valves, and listening—always listening—to the groan of the metal as the tectonic current threatens to squeeze their tiny, crowded archipelago into a single, seamless ingot of crushed iron.

They are waiting for the pressure to reach a breaking point, a state they call "The Final Yield." They do not fear the collapse; they crave it. They believe that once the copper plates finally give way and the gel breaches the chambers, they will not be crushed, but will finally dissolve into the stillness, their solid forms returning to the viscous, infinite dark that surrounds them. Until then, they polish the brass, dim in their moss-light, serving a city that is simultaneously their sanctuary and their tomb.
