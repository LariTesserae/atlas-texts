In the Calcified Throat, life does not walk, nor does it breathe in the standard sense. It does not possess a pulse of blood, but a pulse of minerals.

To look closely at the walls—at the points where the ivory lattice anchors into the basalt—is to see the *Crystalline Echoes*. They are not sentient in any way humans would recognize; they are colonial, non-localized, and entirely woven into the architecture they occupy. 

Imagine a cluster of multifaceted glass shards, each no larger than a human thumb, fused into the curvature of a vaulted ceiling. They are translucent, matte-finished by the blowing silica, and they appear to be nothing more than a curious mineral deposit. But as the cavern groans—as the tectonic pressure-valve forces a contraction—these shards vibrate in sympathy. 

They are the "shapers."

They are not distinct entities, but rather the localized solidification of the city’s intent. Over centuries, they have acted as living chisels and masons. When the heat reaches a threshold where the ivory-fungus begins to expand, the Echoes secrete a superheated, acidic resin. This byproduct seeps into the silicate-fungi, forcing the organic growth to bend, harden, and set into the curved, shell-like structures that define the city. 

They do not move quickly. They occupy a time-scale where a single gesture—the lengthening of a spire or the slight migration of a bridge—takes decades. During the intense grinding roar of the city’s expansion, the Echoes glow with a violent, ultraviolet flicker, signaling that they are repairing the stress fractures in the ivory lattice.

But there is something else that haunts the lower levels, the deep voids where the silica haze is thickest. 

These are the *Dust-Walkers*, though "walker" is a misnomer. They are vast, swirling assemblages of superfine grit and static electricity, held together by the same magnetic pull that keeps the violet flora clinging to the walls. They look like localized hurricanes, narrow and tall, standing thirty feet high. They have no mass; if you were to walk into one, you would feel only a stinging, abrasive warmth against your skin.

They act as the city's circulatory system. Without wings or legs, they drift through the skeletal streets, caught in the thermal updrafts. As they move, the static charge of their bodies pulls the suspended dust out of the air and deposits it into the cracks of the ivory dwellings. They are the gardeners of this parched metropolis. They do not eat the fungi; they groom it, polishing the surfaces with their centrifugal motion, ensuring that the structures remain rounded, smooth, and aerodynamic against the constant, grinding tides of the cavern.

They have no language, but they have a collective memory recorded in friction. When two Dust-Walkers collide in a narrow archway, they merge briefly, a violent sparking of violet light, exchanging the thermal signatures of the sectors they have groomed. It is a data transfer of heat. 

Between the Echoes and the Walkers, the Calcified Throat is never truly abandoned. It is a clockwork system of exquisite, hot, and mindless maintenance. There is no one here to appreciate the aesthetic of the ivory bridges or the way the flickering light plays against the basalt. Yet, the work continues. The Echoes grind, the Walkers turn, and the city persists, keeping itself forever upright, forever beautiful, and forever hollow, meticulously cleaning a house that no guest will ever visit.
