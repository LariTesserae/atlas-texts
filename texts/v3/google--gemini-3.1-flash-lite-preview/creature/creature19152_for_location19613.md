They are jammed against one another in a tapestry of exquisite, agonizing compression. To look at them is to witness the anatomy of a thought frozen at the moment of ignition.

There is no hierarchy here, and by extension, no individuality. Each organism is a strand of opalescent, braided nerve-tissue, thick as a human wrist and translucent as a jellyfish's bell. They do not possess faces, nor eyes, nor limbs that grasp; they are long, tapering conduits of pure conductivity, wound around and through their neighbors in a knot of biological geometry that defies the Euclidean. 

Because they are tightly packed, the "crustacean" nature of their bodies is revealed only in the microscopic serrations along their flanks—tiny, chitinous ridges that serve no purpose for locomotion, but act as friction-locks, anchoring them into the collective mass. When the thrum-well pulses, the entire living architecture shivers in unison. The fluid within their veins is not blood, but a viscous, cerulean liquid that carries a bioluminescence so sharp it casts multiple, overlapping shadows of the beings themselves onto their own translucent skin.

They are waiting, though waiting suggests a future, and there is no future in the thrum-well. They are rather in a state of *static agitation*. If one were to peer deep into the density, they would see that these beings are not merely touching; they are interpenetrating. The nerve-strands have fused at the points of contact. A network of thread-like cilia—fine as spun glass—arcs between their bodies, carrying constant, silent electrical discharges. This is not communication; it is a permanent, shared state of recursive calculation. They are a single, distributed processor made of flesh, constantly resolving the same equations of existence, over and over, in a loop that never yields an answer.

Their biology is "pre-eroded" because they are self-authoring. Where a mass of them grows too heavy, the plates beneath them—the calcified floor—simply extrude a new node, a stiffening support of bone-like biological ceramic that shoves the mass upward, folding it back into itself. 

There is a terrifying stillness to their presence. If one of these woven vines were to be plucked from the wall, it would not crawl or seek; it would simply unfold like a ribbon of wet electricity, its nerve-ends searching for a connection that is no longer there, before going opaque and brittle in the dry, dead air of the outside. 

But here, pressed flank-to-flank in the harsh, clinical light, they are the mountain’s subconscious. They lack the need for hunger or breath, sustained entirely by the thrum of the walls. They are the architects of a geometry of obsession, a million-fold bundle of nerves that have forgotten the purpose of sensation, choosing instead the absolute, blinding stability of being everything, everywhere, all at once, held in a deadlock of pure, iridescent contact.
