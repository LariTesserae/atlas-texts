The inhabitants of the Spore-Crown Vault do not belong to the history of the stars, nor to the records of the surface-born. To find them, one must stop looking for footprints. In a world where gravity is an invitation rather than a command, the beings who dwell here have surrendered the concept of the silhouette.

They are the **Hollow-Walkers**, or perhaps they are the resonance of the ribcage itself, coalescing into biological form.

### The Anatomy of Survival
They are tall, unnaturally thin, their frames elongated by generations spent navigating the verticality of the Spore-Crown. Because the air is thin and the cold is a predatory force, they have evolved—or perhaps been synthesized by the *Lignum-Gelid*—to be near-translucent. Their flesh is not muscle and bone, but a semi-permeable lattice of woven fungal fibers, much like the great bridges they inhabit. 

They do not bleed. If a limb is severed or torn, it releases no fluid; it merely frays, revealing the soft, sour-green bioluminescence that pulses through their interior. To see them move is to watch a knot of grey-blue smoke negotiate an obstacle course. They drape themselves in the heavy, stiff hides of the six-limbed leapers, sewing the parchment-like skins with fibers harvested from the *Lignum-Gelid*. Clad in these stolen heavy-coats, they look like ambulatory bundles of scrap—shambling, rustling, and nearly invisible against the calcified bone of the Titan.

### The Rhythmic Language
The Hollow-Walkers do not speak in vowels or consonants. They speak in the *tink-tink-drip*. 

Since the environment is defined by its acoustic violence—where a single raindrop hitting a fungal shelf echoes like a gong—these people have learned to manipulate the soundscape. They carry small, resonant chimes carved from the Titan’s marrow. By striking these against the bony ribs, they create specific, cascading frequencies that travel for miles through the thin air. 

To a stranger, the Spore-Crown sounds like a chaotic, dying instrument. To a Hollow-Walker, it is a symphony of geography. An elder can strike a rib-arch and, by measuring the delay of the echo, map the growth patterns of the fungi three miles below. They are not merely living in this place; they are the tuning forks of a dead god.

### A Life of Buoyant Patience
They possess no concept of "home" in the sense of a resting place. They are constantly in motion, drifting through the abyss. When they sleep, they do not lie down; they tether themselves to the vines using the same fibrous nerves that the "Wind-Anchors" utilize. They hook their frames into the pulsating, swallowing walls and let the rhythm of the Titan’s phantom heartbeat lull them into torpor. 

They are the keepers of the *way-markers*. They build these totems not out of ego, but out of a desperate, quiet devotion to navigation. In a world where a tool discarded in the morning might hover in the air for a week before spiraling into the dark, the act of placing a marker is a revolutionary defiance of entropic drift. They believe that if they stop placing these markers, the Titan will forget it is empty, and will finally collapse into the void completely.

### The Shadow-Self
Rarely, one might catch a glimpse of them—a grey, spindly shape silhouetted against the absolute black of space, standing on an outcropping no wider than a finger. They do not fear the drop. They understand that in the Spore-Crown, falling is just a slower way of traveling. Sometimes, they allow themselves to unhook and drift, arms outstretched, mimicking the kites, floating through the bioluminescent spore-clouds for hours, watching the green light trace the contours of their own hollowed-out chests.

They are the ghosts in the machine of the Titan. When they die, they do not bury their dead. They climb to the highest, most exposed arch of the ribcage and let go of their tether. They drift, weightless and slow, downward toward the dark, shifting roots far below, becoming part of the *Lignum-Gelid* architecture themselves. They return to the infrastructure, waiting to be woven into a bridge, or a wall, or the very floor upon which their children will one day walk. 

They are effectively becoming the architecture that sustains the next generation of their own kind. They are the only cycle in this place that never stops, never stills, and never reaches an end.
