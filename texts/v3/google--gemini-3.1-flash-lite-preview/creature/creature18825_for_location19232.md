The inhabitants of the Over-Reach are not so much a population as they are an extension of the city’s own metabolic process. They are the **Suture-Kin**, beings whose biology has long since drifted away from the frantic, fleeting pulses of surface-dwellers toward something glacial, damp, and rhythmic.

To see one is to see a silhouette composed of utility and shadow. They are tall, unnervingly spindly, and draped in cloaks of woven fungal fiber—a textile that feels like felted velvet but smells perpetually of ozone and wet cedar. Beneath these heavy, layered garments, their limbs have thinned to the point of structural necessity. Their skin is mottled with a lichenous bloom, a symbiotic patina that allows them to absorb the ambient moisture of the mist directly through their pores. They do not sweat; they redistribute, acting as living conduits for the hydraulic hum that vibrates through the petrified floorboards.

They do not speak—not because they are mute, but because sound is a wasteful expenditure of energy in a place where the atmosphere is better suited to vibration. Instead, a Suture-Kin communicates by leaning their chest cavity against the pulsating iron struts of the city. They listen to the thrum, tracing the health of the central piston-pumps through their own ribcages. When two of them pass on a suspension bridge, they touch hands—not in greeting, but to sync their heart rates, a silent exchange of temporal data that keeps their internal clocks in check against the city’s distorting Euclidean geometry.

Their faces remain largely hidden, though those who have glimpsed beneath the cowls describe eyes that appear less like human organs and more like the bioluminescent leaf-matter that carpets the city. Their irises are deep charcoal, punctuated by a central, shifting pinprick of neon-blue light that pulses in time with the distant, mechanical heart of the metropolis. They seem to lack eyelids. They do not blink; they simply focus, pulling the moisture from the air to keep their gaze clear.

The Suture-Kin are artisans of decay. Their tools are not hammers or wrenches, but long, curved needles made of obsidian and pressurized copper. With these, they graft new moss-tapestries onto the failing fossilized stalks, sewing the living to the dead to ensure the city remains aloft. They are the only things in the Over-Reach that know the city is a lie, aware that the "sky" above is merely a shuttered veil covering a geometric, simulated reality. 

They do not fear the inevitable collapse. They do not even dislike the crushing, cacophonous noise of the steam-vents. To be a Suture-Kin is to exist in a state of suspended resignation. They are the city’s white blood cells, perpetually circulating, perpetually repairing, moving across the rusted girders with a gait that is soundless, fluid, and terribly ancient. 

Sometimes, when the city shifts—when a building ripples and moves three blocks to the east—a Suture-Kin will stop, tilt their head toward the flicking, grid-marked sky, and simply wait. They are waiting for the day the pistons run dry and the giant, petrified forest finally chooses to crumble. They have groomed themselves to be the last things standing, guardians of a quiet exhaustion, ready to fold their cloaks and become part of the fossil record themselves, nestled safely within the embrace of the moss.
