The inhabitants of Ostral-hollow, if they can be called that, are not so much a population as they are a geologic sediment—a slow-motion clotting of the mist and the iron. 

They are the *Suture-Kin*. They do not walk so much as they negotiate with the architecture. Because the city’s geometry is a mercurial, shivering thing, to move here is to be a diplomat of space. You will see them standing at the edge of a gap that should be a simple six-foot leap, waiting, motionless as gargoyles, until the "dilation" of the path settles, until the bridge decides it is long enough to join the two sides. Patience is not a virtue here; it is a biological necessity. To rush is to be caught in a fold, to find oneself walking into the same room you just exited, or worse, to step into a pocket where gravity decides that *down* is now a direction located somewhere behind your left shoulder.

The Suture-Kin are draped in the aforementioned wool—thick, matted, oil-caked—which acts as a sensory husk. Beneath these heavy folds, they are spindly and elongated, their limb joints over-articulated, reminiscent of the very clockwork moths they share the heights with. They have no need for vocal cords in the thin, biting air of the spire. Instead, they communicate through the resonance of their chests, vibrating their own ribcages against the brass struts of the city. When a group of them huddles on a catwalk, they hum in the low, sorrowful registers of cooling metal, a sound that vibrates through the soles of their boots and into the girders, alerting the city itself to their presence so that the architecture might brace for them.

They do not eat, not in the sense that a terrestrial creature does. They are "condensation-feeders." They hold their palms out to the weeping pipes, catching the iridescent, memory-rich runoff. When they drink, they are not merely sustaining their bodies; they are imbibing the city’s history. You might see one of them stare into the distance for a decade, eyes glassy and milky, their consciousness drifting away into the ghost-swells of a storm that broke months ago in a valley they have never seen. They are memory-junkies, and their bodies are maps of these half-remembered events. Look closely at their skin, and you will see constellations of tiny, pulsating bioluminescent moss spores, the same moss that chokes the pipes—they are slowly becoming extensions of the infrastructure, turning into hybrid creatures of carbon and piston-grease.

There are also the *Loom-Masters*, the elders of this vertical expanse. They are less humanoid than the rest, having spent so much time caught in reality-breaks that they have become fragmented. You might see a Loom-Master whose shadow exists three minutes in the future, or one who leaves a translucent, after-image trail behind them as they move. They are the maintenance crew, though they do not repair in any traditional sense. Instead, they use long, brass-tipped needles to stitch the fraying edges of reality back into the stone. They don't hammer rivets; they knit the architecture, pulling loose threads of geography together to keep a neighborhood from drifting off into the void.

They live without a concept of home. A Suture-Kin might wake up on a landing, build an iron cot, and live there for four generations, only to wake up one morning and find that the staircase has migrated to the other side of the abyss, leaving them suspended on a platform of nothingness. They simply sigh, pull their wool coats tighter, and begin the long, sluggish journey toward the next patch of solid-seeming stone.

They are the city’s nervous system, its sorrow, and its only witnesses. They are the ones who listen to the iron tolling when a pipe bursts, the ones who know that the city is not built *on* the mountain to stay, but rather that the mountain is holding the city in its throat, waiting for someone to finally say the word that will turn this whole wretched, beautiful mess into smoke. They are waiting for that word, too, and when it comes, they will be the first things to dissolve into the mist.
