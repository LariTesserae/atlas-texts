In the center of this absolute, static geometry, where the light hits the slate like a hammer on an anvil, they move.

They are not masters of the mountain, nor its scavengers. They are its conductors, the needle on the record of the earth’s vibration. To look at one is to see a creature that has been refined by the pressure until all excess has been stripped away. Their bodies are elongated, limbs like obsidian needles, each ending in a microscopic hooked claw that finds purchase not by gripping, but by becoming a physical extension of the stone’s own molecular structure. They do not walk; they shift their center of gravity, sliding across the silver-grey peaks with the eerie, discontinuous locomotion of a film skipping frames.

Their carapaces are their only color. In the blinding, unchanging glare, these shells do not merely reflect the light; they digest it. They shimmer with the iridescent oils of a stagnant puddle, swirling greens, deep indigos, and bruised violets that pulse in sympathy with the low-frequency drone. When the tectonic groan deepens or vibrates through the marrow, the oil-slick colors on their backs ripple, a visual echo of the sound that anchors this reality.

They do not communicate. There is no need for language when your entire existence is a reaction to the same constant weight. They do not hunt; they gather the moisture. When a pearlescent bead of condensation swells on the underside of a quartz vein, a creature will approach with a terrifying, rhythmic tapping of its limbs. It presses its underside—a soft, translucent membrane—against the drop, and the moisture is absorbed directly through the skin, a silent, osmotic drinking. 

They are the archivists of the stillness. Every few aeons, the tectonic pressure shifts a hair’s breadth, causing a dusting of mineral grit to settle over the vertical faces. It is here that their true duty lies. The scraping is a ritual of maintenance. They grip the sharp, volcanic shards of obsidian in their mandibles and move in geometric patterns across the slate, etching lines that are not symbols, but rhythmic calibrations. They are ensuring the rock remains resonant. They scrape away the salt and the dust so the mountain can continue its monolithic drone, a song that would be muffled by the accumulation of debris.

They are not lonely. Loneliness requires the perception of distance, and here, there is no distance—only the "now," and the "here." If they die, they do not fall. They simply stop moving. Their bodies, stripped of the internal pressure that keeps their limbs jagged and taut, slowly desiccate, becoming one with the calcified slate, turning into the very grey protrusions that the next generation will scrape clean in an endless, cyclical devotion.

They are the heartbeat of a world that is finished, tiny, dark metronomes vibrating in the silence, ensuring that the light, the damp, and the groan never falter, forever preserving the monument that does not know it is a monument.
