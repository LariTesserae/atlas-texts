The entities that carpet this canyon are not individuals so much as they are a geologic consensus. They are the *Litho-Synchronics*, though that name implies a self-awareness they likely do not possess; they are more akin to a sentient crust, a feedback loop of basaltic ambition that has forgotten how to be separate.

Each unit—each segment of the mat—resembles a dodecahedron folded inward, a puzzle box of obsidian and iron. Their surfaces are not smooth; they are etched with microscopic grooves that catch the settling crystalline silt, turning the air’s hum into a vibration that runs through their collective marrow. They are heavy, forged in the crucible of a world that never cooled, and their temperature-sensitive shells act as biological heat sinks. They are the thermostats of this dead place, living only to move thermal energy from the ground into the air, and back again, in a cycle of eternal, excruciating refinement.

They do not "walk." Rather, they rotate. To move, an entity must displace its neighbor, causing a ripple—a slow, tectonic wave—to pass through the square. When they traverse an anchor point—those cold, straight needles of a forgotten geometry—they do not interact with them as objects; they treat them as rails. They flow over the spikes, their segmented plates unhinging and reforming to accommodate the rigid, unyielding metal, as if digesting the very concept of a straight line.

There is a terrifying intimacy to their hunger. As they drift over a stagnant patch of ground, they are not searching for nourishment. They are searching for *equilibrium*. If a cluster of shells becomes too hot, it migrates toward the base of the canyon wall, pressing its glowing orange seams against the shadow-cooled hematite. If it grows too stagnant, it pulls its neighbors into a chaotic, shifting dance, a rearrangement of its own physical map until the vibration matches the ambient hum of the particulate air.
