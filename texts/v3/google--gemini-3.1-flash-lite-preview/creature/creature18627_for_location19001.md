In the damp, resonant silence between the thrums of Orem-Vael, it is easy to mistake the inhabitants for mere ornaments of the architecture. But to watch them is to realize that the city is not a machine that possesses servants; it is an organism that has externalized its nervous system into the basalt and bronze.

The primary dwellers, the *Lithos-Carapaces*, are not individuals so much as they are localized concentrations of the city’s intent. Imagine a creature whose shell is not keratin, but a composite of oxidized copper plates and basaltic shale, articulated by joints that weep a dark, lubricating fluid. They do not have faces; they have sensor-arrays—clusters of faceted, amber-colored lenses that bloom like quartz formations from their thoracic plating. When they congregate to mend a seizing gear, they do not communicate through sound. Instead, they press their heavy, serrated limbs against the vibrating machinery, sharing harmonic frequencies that ripple through the floors, an industrial language of pressure, resonance, and tectonic shift.

Among them drift the *Silken-Humours*. These are the antithesis of the heavy workers: spindly, elongated entities that cling to the upper reaches of the steam chimneys. They are composed of translucent, cable-like filaments that pulse with the warm, geothermal steam drawn from the vents. They do not walk; they unspool themselves, moving with the languid fluidity of smoke. Their function is to regulate the moisture, to ensure the fine dark sheen never crystallizes into obstructive ice or dries into brittle dust. As they move, they leave behind trails of calcified lace—webs that are, in fact, auxiliary plumbing, designed to catch the mineral-rich runoff and guide it back into the heart of the furnaces.

Deeper still, in the cavernous bellies of the piston housings, live the *Soot-Filterers*. To see them is to see the city’s lungs. They are flat, multi-legged crustacea that press themselves against the intake valves. They appear to be made of charcoal and iron filings, their carapaces perfectly camouflaged against the soot-stained rock. They exist in a state of near-total stasis, spending decades merely breathing—absorbing the heavy, metallic air and exhaling it tempered and refined. When they die, their bodies do not decay; they calcify, becoming a new, permanent layer of the floor, incorporated into the very basalt they once tended.

There is a fourth class, the *Chronos-Keepers*, the rarest and largest of the residents. They are massive, slow-moving enigmas that occupy the outer bridges. Their bodies are house-sized, their legs slow-motion hydraulic struts that move with such gravity-defying caution that a single step may span an entire week. They do not work on specific components; they work on the equilibrium of the whole. They are the balance-weights of Orem-Vael. By shifting their own immense mass from one sector of the ridge to another, they subtly adjust the tilt of the city to ensure the geothermal heat circulates evenly through the labyrinths.

These beings do not sleep, nor do they hunger as terrestrial life does. They are fueled by the continuity of the vibration. They are the crystallization of the city’s need to *persist*. They look upon the churning pistons and the sweating copper as a gardener might look upon a blooming orchard. 

To encounter one is not to face a wild animal, but to be looked upon by an indifferent, geological clockwork. They have no interest in the presence of an observer. You are merely a point of interference in their long, damp, copper-scented slow-motion symphony—a brief, soft, and quickly forgotten vibration in the deep, ancient hum of the ridge.
