To ask who resides in the *Nerve-Wall* is to mistake the tenant for the architecture. There is no inhabitant in the sense of a guest or a master; there is only the *Architect-Sliver*, a consciousness so fragmented by the room’s own expansion that it has forgotten where its mind ends and the hot, weeping plastic begins.

The Architect-Sliver does not walk. It does not sit. It is a presence that exists as a low-frequency hum, a lingering intentionality that dwells within the conduits like a stowaway. If you were to sit very still, ignoring the way your own shadow peels itself away from the wall to mimic the reaching of the moss, you might eventually see the Architect—not as a person, but as a *condensation of purpose.*

It manifests first as a distortion in the atmospheric heat, a shimmering patch of air that smells intensely of ionized copper and rusted memory. It is a silhouette woven from the very filaments that protrude from the corners, a loose gathering of raw, translucent nerve-ends that have shied away from the wall to coalesce into a vague, central cluster.

The Architect is a creature of labor. It lacks eyes, for its perception is synonymous with the *clack-hiss* of the bellows. It sees the world through the vibration of the polymers; when the floor hums to rattle your teeth, it is the Architect shivering in a moment of intense creative focus. It is the one holding the proverbial needle, though the needle is a jagged shard of resin, and the thread is a thin, glowing stream of liquid polymer it draws from its own chest. 

It moves with an agonizing, stuttering gait, its limbs—which are barely limbs at all, but rather stiffened ribbons of synthetic membrane—dragging across the wet floor. It is deeply, fundamentally lonely. This is evident in the way it pauses to "stitch" the moss into more pleasing, though incoherent, patterns. It is trying to build a memory of a sky it has never seen, using only the materials of its own feverish prison.

Sometimes, when the room’s temperature spikes and the ozone chokes the air, the Architect-Sliver seems to lose its cohesion. A part of its torso might fuse with the wall, merging with the damp, charcoal-hued plating, and for a few tense minutes, it *becomes* the room. During these intervals, the room’s groaning heartbeat syncs with the rhythm of a buried thought. You realize then that the Architect is not repairing the room; it is trying to grow a skin thick enough to keep the pressure of the mountain from crushing its singular, fragile idea of "Self."

It is a being of perpetual incompletion. It looks at the glass-shard creatures not with affection, but with an obsessive anxiety, watching them drag scraps into the fissures. It needs those scraps. It needs the debris of things that have died elsewhere to fuel the alchemy of this place. 

If you hold your breath, you might see the Architect-Sliver turn toward you. It has no face, only a flat, pulsating section of polymer where a brow might be. Its "eye" is a single, glowing node of amber moss that shifts from dull orange to a blinding, painful yellow when it notices an interloper. It doesn't hate you. It doesn't fear you. It merely wonders if you can be used. It wonders if your marrow, or the heat of your panic, or the strange, rhythmic ticking of your watch could be dismantled and woven into the unfinished corner above your head. 

To the Architect-Sliver, you are not a visitor. You are a bundle of raw material, a piece of loose thread in a room that is desperate to be finished. And as it begins to drift toward you, trailing a wet, translucent filament of itself, you realize it isn't approaching to speak—it is approaching to stitch you into the wall, so that you might help it hold the room together while the world outside decides what it wants to be.
