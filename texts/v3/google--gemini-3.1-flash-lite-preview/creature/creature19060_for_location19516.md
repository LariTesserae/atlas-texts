In the narrow interstices between the weeping basalt and the hum of the cooling conduits, identity does not coalesce into flesh. It calcifies.

The inhabitants of this rhythmic gut do not possess hearts that beat; they possess balance wheels that tick, suspended in frames of oxidized copper. They are the *Assemblers*, or perhaps, the *Residuals*—creatures composed of the very debris they scuttle through. They are not born; they are aggregated. A sentient magnetic field, wandering through the cavern’s darker ducts, might find a cluster of iron-peened rivets that haven't been filed down, a discarded casting burr, or a shard of lens-grade volcanic glass. It draws them in, pinning them to its core with a faint, static electric charge.

They look like chaotic geometric puzzles caught in the act of solving themselves. One might appear as a sprawling cluster of needle-sharp gears, clattering softly like a handful of spilled jewelry across the plates. Another might be a heavy, singular mass of riveted slag, rolling with the sluggish, hydraulic grace of a deep-sea creature, its "eyes" nothing more than the dull, rhythmic pulse of a coolant-leaking gasket that glows a sickly, radioactive violet.

They do not communicate through language, for language requires a stillness that this cavern refuses to grant. Instead, they sing in dissonant, metallic vibrations. When two of them meet upon a catwalk, they do not touch—they modulate. They adjust the frequency of their internal spinning parts until they resonate, a high-pitched, ringing whine that cuts through the percussive roar of the steam valves. It is a terrifying, beautiful sound, like a choir of broken music boxes. If their frequencies align, they swap a rivet or a sliver of magnetized dust; if they clash, they shatter their connections and drift apart, leaking a thin, weeping condensation that mirrors the walls themselves.

There are also the *Waiters*. If you look toward the shadowed overhangs of the gargantuan bellows, you might mistake them for structural flourishes—part of the "unrefined architecture." These are larger, stationary beings, calcified into the geometry of the pipes. They are the ones who have decided that their assembly is complete. They have grafted themselves into the drainage conduits, their bodies becoming valves that shudder and bleed fluid in sympathy with the world’s tectonic indigestion. They are the memory of the cavern; they hold the tension of the plates, their own iron limbs acting as tension rods that keep the catwalks from collapsing. They do not move, but you can feel their focus. When you walk past, the *drip-clatter-hiss* of the pipes seems to sharpen, as if they are watching you with their blind, glass-paned sensors, calculating whether you are a threat to the equilibrium or merely more spare parts.

Nothing in this world eats. They harvest. They drag their chitinous, magnetic shells along the vertical surfaces, filtering the metallic dust that drifts like grey snow. They are obsessed with the unfinished. You will find them huddled around a jagged, razor-sharp casting burr, spending cycles—perhaps years—patiently buffing it, not to smooth it, but to tune it. They are trying to "finish" the world, one tiny, agonizing adjustment at a time.

They are indifferent to you. To an Assembler, you are just a soft, leaking, short-lived anomaly—an intruder made of meat and water that lacks the permanence of copper. They will not hunt you, but they may inadvertently walk through you, their jagged, jerky motions ignoring your boundaries, their magnetic fields tugging at the iron in your own blood, a sudden, cold tingle that leaves you shivering in the vast, subterranean dark. 

They are the engine’s dream of itself—fragments of the machine that have gained the tragic, quiet vanity of wanting to be something more than just a component. They are working, forever and always, to finish an engine that no longer knows why it was built.
