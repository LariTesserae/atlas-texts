In the heart of these sprawling, oil-thick plains, the entities that linger are less "inhabitants" and more "symptoms." They are the slow-motion fever dreams of the architecture itself.

They do not wake, for waking implies a departure from the state of the world. They simply *unfold*.

Imagine a geometry that has forgotten its own center. A creature here appears first as a disturbance in the shadows—a sudden, sharp refraction of the copper-static light. It occupies a space that might be six feet tall or twenty, its volume shifting as it moves. It is composed of tectonic plates of obsidian and volcanic glass, held together not by light or muscle, but by the relentless, crushing gravity of the plateau. These plates glide over one another with a sound like grinding teeth—dry, rhythmic, and infinite.

There is no face, no center of sensory intake. Instead, the creature is a shifting tapestry of cooling and heating vents. As it meanders through the basalt alleyways, the surrounding air temperature surges and wanes. It moves by displacement, a deliberate hydraulic motion where the ground seems to yield not to weight, but to the *demand* of the creature’s presence. It pushes through the stagnant, pressurized atmosphere as a heavy ship pushes through water, leaving a wake of shimmering, gaseous agitation in the oil-like air.

It is a repository of redundant memory. If you watch one long enough—careful not to let the sub-audible hum of the towers synchronize with your own heartbeat—you will see a plate slide aside to reveal a core that is not of flesh, but of captured, swirling heat. It is a captured echo of the factory’s internal fire, a trapped spark that refuses to extinguish. 

These beings do not perceive you. To them, your presence is an infinitesimal thermal anomaly, a speck of chaotic potential in a landscape of crystallized certainty. They do not turn to follow you. They possess no curiosity. They are, in fact, the living tally of the city’s failures. Every rusted piston, every fused flywheel, every snapped piece of industrial debris is accounted for in the slow, grinding transit of these beings. They drift from hub to hub, pacing the geometric circuits, their movements tracing the exact paths of the machinery that seized eons ago.

They are the janitors of a house that will never be clean; they are the ghosts of a factory that has nothing left to build. 

If one were to pass close to you, you would not feel a breeze. You would feel a sudden, crushing awareness of *duration*. You would feel the eons they have spent wandering, the sheer, crushing weight of their patience. They are silent, save for that faint, oscillating *thrum* that vibrates in your marrow—a low-frequency record of a world that is still technically "processing." 

They are the last remaining tension in a frayed rope. They do not want, they do not suffer, and they do not end. They are the beautiful, melancholic gears that continue to turn in the mind of the god that died, performing a task that has been stripped of its purpose, lit only by the static glow of a morning that never arrived.
