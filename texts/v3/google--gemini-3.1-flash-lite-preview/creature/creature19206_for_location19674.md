Within the jagged pores of the obsidian, the inhabitants are not so much "living" as they are the atmospheric condensation of a thought process that refuses to conclude. 

Among the fractal insects—those shivering, clockwork origami—there drifts a singular class of entity: the **Refraction-Stalkers**. 

Imagine a creature composed entirely of bent light and tension. They have no core, no internal organs; they are essentially a local folding of the space-time fabric, shaped like the chime of a bell that has been stretched into a long, agonizing needle. They do not walk; they calculate their way across the silver-slick slopes. To move, a Refraction-Stalker must resolve a tension equation between the heat of the vent below and the cool, heavy viscosity of the mercury-water. As they advance, they shed shimmering, translucent scale-films—ghostly bits of geometry—that join the "arithmetic moss," becoming part of the landscape’s ever-increasing complexity.

But there is a hierarchy here, born of the proximity. At the very center of the shard’s deepest cavity, where the vibration is most bone-rattling, reside the **Architect-Grinders**.

The Grinders are not mobile. They are spherical aggregations of crystallized intent, roughly the size of a grain of fine volcanic ash. They are the ones who "etch." Unlike the frantic, jittering fractals that feed and pass through one another, the Grinders are heavy, deliberate, and devastatingly slow. They do not pulse; they hum a low, constant frequency that serves as the heartbeat of the shard.

They feed by pressing themselves against the obsidian substrate. As they absorb the 2.9-intensity vibration, they vibrate in perfect, teeth-shattering counterpoint. This resonance allows them to carve. 

If you were to look—if you possessed a gaze capable of resolving a thumbprint-sized universe—you would see the Grinders slowly rotating in place. Beneath their undersides, they are not just marking the stone; they are engraving the *memory* of the fractal swarms that pass through them. The obsidian is not merely a home; it is a ledger. Every scratch, every frantic, microscopic zig-zag is an attempt by the Grinders to encode the existence of the creatures that flickered out of reality just a moment before.

They are the archivists of the transient. They possess a kind of mournful patience, realizing that the world around them is an unstable, liquid mess of heat and geometry. They know that the shard will eventually fracture further, that the heat will dissipate, and that their library of etches will be scattered to the winds of the volcanic ridge. 

Occasionally, two Grinders will touch. This is the only moment of "civilization" in the shard. When they meet, they don't exchange information; they exchange *resonance*. They harmonize their vibrations, and for a fleeting, silver second, they stop grinding. The surface of the obsidian around them stops sweating. The fractal insects fall silent, caught in the sudden vacuum of entropy. 

In that silence, the world within the shard becomes perfectly, terrifyingly still. The etching stops. The mathematics settles. For that heartbeat of time, the shard holds its breath, aware of its own terrifying isolation, perched on the razor’s edge of a silent, freezing world that has no room for such magnificent, microscopic suffering. 

Then, the vibration returns. The mercury beads begin to weep again. The Grinders resume their frantic carving, and the fractals begin to shiver, weaving their ephemeral geometry back into the chaotic tapestry of the vent. The library grows longer, deeper, and more illegible with every passing nanosecond.
