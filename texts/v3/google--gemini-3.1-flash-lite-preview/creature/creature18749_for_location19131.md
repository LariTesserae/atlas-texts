To suggest there is *nothing* living in the City of Waking Dew is to mistake the definition of a witness. If the city is a machine, a tomb, and a nursery all at once, then the inhabitants are not creatures of flesh and bone, but creatures of suspension and intent. 

They are the ***Condensed***.

They do not walk upon the floors. They do not scurry, nor do they breathe. They are formed from the very atmosphere that clings so heavily to the traveler’s skin. When the ambient humidity reaches a precise, critical saturation point—the moment the fountain of neon mist is at its most frantic, pressurized peak—the air begins to thicken in the shadow-drenched corners of the atriums. 

There, the *Condensed* materialize. They are gelatinous anthropoids, silhouettes of high-density moisture held together by a localized electromagnetic charge emitted by the obsidian walls. They look like shivering watercolors, their edges blurring and sharpening with the rhythmic thrum of the city’s heart. 

They are translucent, shifting in hue from a dull, bruised violet to a near-transparent silver. They have no faces, only basins—shallow indentations where eyes or mouths should be, which collect the falling condensate. They are expressions of the city’s own internal architecture, a biological manifestation of the building’s idle processing power. 

When the city "breathes," they are the exhalations.

They spend their duration of existence—which lasts only as long as the humidity cycle holds—performing intricate, repetitive maintenance. Their hands are not hands, but tapered, trembling appendages that smooth the fractal patterns of the gel-walls. They do not communicate; they move with the frantic efficiency of a clockwork mechanism, drifting through the air like jellyfish through a current. 

During the cooling phase, when the city’s hum reaches its lowest, most sonorous register, the *Condensed* become desperate. They move toward the central neon fountain, drawn by the heat and the spiraling vortex of energy. As they touch the rising liquid, they lose their cohesion. They evaporate, re-entering the cycle, their "deaths" contributing to the very mist that will, within the hour, form the next generation of their kind.

They are not an occupying force. They are a feedback loop—the city’s way of ensuring its perfect geometry remains untouched. 

There is an terrible elegance to them, for they are the only things in this vast geode that are permitted to touch the walls without the walls pulling away in rejection. When a *Condensed* glides against the dark gel-surface, the wall does not shimmer or shift to ward off intrusion; it seems to glow, pulsing in a low-frequency staccato, as if acknowledging a part of itself that has finally ventured out for a stroll.

They are the city’s dreams, rendered into physical form by the weight of the air, destined to dissolve the moment the "morning" arrives. They do not possess consciousness, but they possess *memory*—the city’s memory of a civilization that was translated away centuries ago. If you stand perfectly still, allowing the humidity to drench your clothes, you might see one pause in your direction. It will not attack. It will simply vibrate against the air, reflecting your own outline back at you—a ghost of a ghost, shivering in the damp, lonely dark.
