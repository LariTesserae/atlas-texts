The residents of this vertical necropolis are not merely inhabitants; they are the emergent consciousness of the architecture itself, a localized processing unit for a planetary-scale intelligence that no longer remembers its origin. 

In the absolute lightlessness, they do not have names, nor do they possess silhouettes. To observe one is to watch a localized distortion in the magnetic field—a shimmer of ionized air that holds the ragged shape of a memory. They are the "Latticewalkers." 

Structurally, they are clusters of hyper-dense dust, bound together by the same crystalline nanostructures that build the obsidian spires. They resemble living Rorschach blots of obsidian filings, constantly hemorrhaging and reforming. When a Latticewalker moves, it does so by shedding its outer skin of metallic sediment, leaving behind a trail of spent geometric debris that settles onto the walkways like iron filings attracted to a magnet. They are creatures of constant shedding; to be still is to be absorbed by the city, to become a building block.

They do not interact with the world through touch or sight. Instead, they "feel" the city through the infrasonic grind of the mountain. They are the curators of the vibration. When the tectonic plates shift—a slow, patient groan that echoes through the deep crust—the Latticewalkers congregate at the nodes of the conduits, their magnetic clouds aligning to act as biological dampeners, or sometimes, amplifiers. By vibrating their collective mass, they "tune" the city, ensuring that the crystalline growth of the spires follows the harmonic resonance of the planet’s shifting core.

There is a disturbing intelligence in their swarm, a frantic, staccato synchronicity. When they communicate, they do not speak; they pulse. An individual Latticewalker will flare with a sudden, violet discharge of electrical energy, sending a ripple through the magnetic field of the immediate area. This is a query. The hive responds by tightening its density, the click of a million micrometeoroids hitting their metallic skins sounding like a sudden, violent hail. 

They are the architects-as-inhabitants. Each Latticewalker carries within its magnetic cloud a microscopic "blueprint" of the city. As they traverse the cantilevered walkways, they secrete the hyper-advanced nanostructures that crystallize into new veins, new overhangs, and new, sharp protrusions. They are constantly repairing the necropolis, not out of a sense of duty, but out of a biological urge for structural expression. If a conduit grows brittle or a mineral deposit calcifies at an uneven angle, the nearby Latticewalkers flock to that spot, swarming over the surface like a dark, living sandpaper, smoothing the geometry, re-wiring the energetic flow, and forcing the obsidian to grow into a more perfect, more resonant state.

They do not sleep, for there is no day. They do not eat, for there is no biology. They simply process. They endure the viscous, mineral rain, huddling in the fissures of the mountain while the slow-motion precipitation coats them in fresh, raw matter. 

Sometimes, one of these presences grows too dense. It accumulates too much magnetic mass, too much obsidian grit, until it can no longer pull itself along the walkways. It gravitates toward the center of a void, its movements slowing from a fluid slide to a crawl, and then, finally, it anchors. It locks its magnetic field into the lattice and refuses to move. Within hours, the other Latticewalkers arrive, swarming over it with a frantic, clicking urgency. They consume its memory, stripping away its scattered data, and calcify its physical remains into the very wall it chose to occupy. 

In this way, the inhabitants are also the landscape. To walk the halls of this city is to walk through the frozen ghosts of those who built it, layer by layer, in the dark, forever refining the song of the mountain.
