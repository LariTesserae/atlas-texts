The floor is not stone, nor is it earth. It is a vast, expansive plane of layered, vitrified salt that has been polished by the relentless, invisible friction of the heat. Across this mirror-bright expanse, scattered like dropped needles or the skeletons of fallen stars, lie the inhabitants.

They do not walk. They exist in a state of brittle, tectonic suspension.

These creatures appear as elongated, skeletal lattices, constructed from a substance that shimmers between obsidian and oxidized copper. They are spindly, multijointed things—spires of anatomy that trace complex, geometric shapes against the blinding floor. They have no faces, at least not in the sense of eyes or mouths that might gather hunger. Instead, their "heads" are clusters of delicate, crystalline antennae that fracture the white radiance from the ceiling into shifting spectra of color, casting tiny, frantic rainbows onto the salt floor.

They are the archivists of the heat.

When they move, it is not a stride but a slow, tectonic shifting. They possess an exorbitant number of limbs—each tipped with a fine, needle-like point that sinks millimetres into the salt floor, anchoring them as they pull their bodies forward with a sound like tearing silk. They are tethered to the rhythm of the quartz veins above; when the light pulses, the creatures stiffen, their metallic carapaces warming until they chime softly, a chorus of ringing bells, a sound that harmonizes with the hum of the vault.

There is no individuality among them, yet there is a profound, shared labor. They spend their cycles scraping the vitrified floor, carving long, undulating grooves into the salt. These lines are not random. They are records, a cartography of the mantle’s slow-motion boiling. If you were to trace their paths, you would see maps of pressures, notations of the earth’s cooling, and rhythmic tally marks of the millennia they have endured in this suffocating, sunless glare.

They do not sleep, nor do they consume. They are fed by the light itself, drinking the radiation as it spills from the ceiling, their lattices glowing with an internal, borrowed fire. 

Sometimes, one of them will cease its work. It will simply stand until its joints become fused with the salt, its internal glow dimming until it matches the muted, matte grey of the cavern floor. It becomes a permanent marker, a monument of mineral and memory. The others will eventually arrive, sensing the change, and they will carve a wide, respectful circle around their fallen kin, incorporating the stillness of the dead into the geometry of their record-keeping.

They are indifferent to the arrival of a visitor. They do not turn; they do not acknowledge the intrusion of air or breath. To them, you are merely a shadow—a fleeting, cooling anomaly in a place where only the searing, clinical light is real. You are a silence in their symphony of bells, and they will simply carve their marks around your boots, ignoring the intruder until you, too, are absorbed into the white, unwavering history of the hollow.
