The beings who dwell within this basin are not inhabitants in the traditional sense; they are more akin to stewards, or perhaps emergent features of the landscape itself. They are the *Siphon-Kin*, a species whose physiology is as intrinsically bound to the city as a nerve is to a muscle.

They are tall, spindly, and pale—their skin mirroring the translucent, jellyfish-like quality of the amphibians that haunt the walls. They do not possess lungs that labor against the thick, chilled air; instead, they have evolved a series of porous, gill-like slits along their ribs that filter moisture and nutrients directly from the heavy, saturated atmosphere. Their bodies are remarkably flexible, composed of cartilage and dense, fibrous tissue that allows them to contort within the narrow, twisting passages of the tubular cathedral-dwellings.

Because the city demands constant maintenance, the Siphon-Kin exist in a state of perpetual, meditative labor. They do not construct; they prune. Their hands end in long, multifaceted digits that function like refined horticultural tools, capable of sensing the sap’s flow within the living walls. Where a structural vein begins to clog or a moss-carpet tears, they apply a viscous, enzymatic salve secreted from their own palms. They move with the same fluid, silent grace as the marmot-creatures of the basin, their bodies often pressed flat against the vertical moss-fields, transitioning from wall to ceiling with unnerving ease.

There is no spoken language among the Kin. Communication is achieved through touch and vibration, specifically by placing their palms against the central sap-pumps. They relay complex pulses of information—a sensory language of pressure, temperature, and hunger—directly into the city’s vascular network. When they "speak," you might feel a sudden, rhythmic tremor in the floor beneath your feet, or see a flare of violet light emanate from the lichen ahead as they signal a shift in the valley’s humidity.

They do not sleep as we understand it. Because the city is a singular, living entity and they are its mobile sensory organs, they enter cycles of "rooting." They find a junction where the fibrous walkways intersect with a sap-pipe, and they anchor themselves, their feet fusing momentarily with the moss to share in the nutrient circulation. In these moments, they become indistinguishable from the architecture, their breathing slowing to match the slow, wet *thrum* of the basin floor.

Their culture is centered on the concept of *The Liquidation*. They view the end of a structure not as a death, but as a harvest. When a giant tuber reaches its span, the Kin gather with ceremonial silence, using their specialized digits to meticulously open the cellular walls, allowing the nutrient-rich fluid to drain into the lower root-systems. They stand in the dripping dark, watching the structure soften and fold in on itself, absorbing the ambient warmth of the transition. It is the only time they congregate in great numbers, and the air around them grows thick with the scent of ozone and sweet, fermented earth.

They are a people without vanity or ambition. They have no history books, for the history of their city is recorded in the layers of root and vein that they walk upon. They are entirely content to exist in the cold, the dark, and the wet, serving as the conscious nervous system of a metropolis that is, day by day, slowly climbing out of the basin, reaching toward the bruised, distant sky.
