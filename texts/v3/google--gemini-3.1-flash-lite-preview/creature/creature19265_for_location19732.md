The inhabitants of this frantic, lung-like interior—the **Silverscale Pruners**—do not inhabit their world so much as they hold it at bay. 

To look upon a Pruner is to witness a creature that has been ergonomically shaped by claustrophobia. Their skin is not merely textured like sandpaper; it is granular, composed of thousands of overlapping, keratinized plates that grind softly against one another with every movement, creating a sub-audible hiss that matches the ambient static of the Xylem-Deep. They are lithe, spindly, and unnervingly multi-jointed, possessing extra knuckles in their fingers that allow their hands to function like fleshy, dexterous clamps. They do not walk so much as they "tension" themselves across the swaying roots, locking their limbs into place to wait out a gust of hyper-growth.

They are a people defined by their tools. They carry no currency, no totems, no jewelry. Every Pruner is tethered to a weighted obsidian blade—honed to a molecular edge—strapped to their forearm with bands of conductive vein-fiber. These blades are not for combat, for there is no time to fight; they are instruments of constant, rhythmic excision.

A Pruner's life is a feverish cycle of maintenance. They live in "The Gaps," bubble-like voids carved into the rapidly thickening walls of the planet-girdling bellows. To live in a Gap is to be an eternal gardener of one's own living room. They sleep in shifts, not because they are tired, but because the walls possess a predatory hunger. If a Pruner nods off for too long, the soft, aggressive moss-carpets will bleed into their workspace, wrapping around their ankles, binding them to the floor with viscous, fibrous glue. 

They are silent, by necessity. To speak in the Xylem-Deep is to invite the attention of the macaques—those screeching, vine-skinned terrors that drift through the upper boughs. Instead, the Pruners communicate through the **"Tactile Code."** They press their sandpaper-palms against the vibrating pipes of the Xylem-Deep, sensing the shift in the planet’s internal currents. They feel a tremor of *expansion* two corridors away and know to prune the ceiling before it collapses on them; they feel the *suction* of a distant macaque-pack and know to dampen their own warmth to remain invisible to heat-seeking predators.

There is a tragedy in their existence: they are the most intelligent organisms in the Deep, yet they leave no history. They cannot write in books, for the paper would be digested; they cannot build monuments, for the walls would swallow them. Their "culture" exists only in the negative space they carve out of the forest. When a Pruner dies, they are not buried; they are simply shoved into a high-growth vascular tube, their bodies dissolved within moments to feed the very titan that tries to crush them.

They are the curators of a riot. They spend their entire lives hacking back the exuberant, homicidal vitality of their own home, existing in the tiny, cooling pockets of air they steal back from the biological onslaught. They are the friction in the machine—the only things here that do not grow, but rather, keep the world from growing over them.
