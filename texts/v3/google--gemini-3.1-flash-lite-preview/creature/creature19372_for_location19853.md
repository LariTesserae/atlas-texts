Who dwells within these Cities of the Weeping Rock are not masters of their domain, but rather its most devoted lubricators. They are the **Thermal-Tenders**, creatures whose biology has been rewritten by the same subterranean heat that drives the turbines.

They do not walk so much as they drift, their bodies lean and fibrous, like coils of weathered copper wire lashed together with sinew. Their skin is not flesh, but a dense, leathery pelt of silicate scales that rattle softly when they move, absorbing the ambient heat until they glow with a faint, dull amber radiance. They have no use for eyes in a world where visibility is a shifting game of vapor; instead, their heads are topped with expansive, fan-like crests of conductive crystal that sense the subtle fluctuations in the air’s pressure and flow. 

When a Thermal-Tender moves through the obsidian labyrinth, they do not speak. To speak is to waste the precious moisture the air works so hard to relinquish. Instead, they communicate by touch—pressing their own heated palms against the piping of the city to send vibrations through the metal, a tactile semaphore that rings out through the architecture. They are, in a sense, the city's nervous system; they move to mend a weeping seal, to adjust a shutter, to guide a stream of brine away from a cooling cavity. 

Their lives are measured not in hours, but in the slow expansion and contraction of the copper conduits. They are a patient, rhythmic people, moving with the sluggish grace of tectonic plates. Because the air is so thick—so saturated—they do not inhale like terrestrial creatures. They possess a complex series of porous valves along their chests that capture the superheated mist directly, filtering the brine as the Salt-Singers do, extracting the minerals they need to maintain their own mineralized skeletons. 

To look upon a Thermal-Tender is to look upon a creature that has surrendered its individuality to the plumbing. They are part of the clockwork. When the heat wanes, they become brittle and still, leaning against the warm obsidian walls; when the peak of the geothermal day hits, they pulse with energy, climbing the gantries to service the turbines, their translucent, tentacle-like fingers moving in perfect synchronization with the mechanical hum of the valley.

They harbor no philosophy of expansion, no dreams of conquest. Their existence is a devotion to the Equilibrium. They believe—though it is a belief held in the marrow of their bones rather than thought in the mind—that if they were to cease their labor, if the brine were to stop flowing or the valves were to clog, the world would finally suffocate under the weight of its own unvented steam. 

In the long, shimmering nights, when the sun dips low and the vapor curtains turn a bruised, oily violet, the Thermal-Tenders drift through the canyons like shadows of the landscape itself. They congregate in the hollowed-out cores of the obsidian flutes, where the subsonic rumble vibrates their hollow bones. In these moments, they are not separate from the silence; they are the resonators through which the world’s ancient, deep-earth song is allowed to pass, a steady, relentless heartbeat echoing off the weeping rock.
