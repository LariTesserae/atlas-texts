The inhabitants of Oor are not so much a species as they are a long-term collaboration with the environment. To look at them is to see biology that has surrendered the hard edges of skin and bone in favor of the porous, the translucent, and the fluid.

They are the *Oorani*, and they are shaped by the pressure of the soup they breathe.

The Oorani are tall, elongated beings, their anatomy reminiscent of the hanging flora they dwell upon. There is no skeletal rigidity to be found in them; instead, they possess a hydrostatic internal structure, a series of pressurized bladders that respond to the ambient density of the mist. Their skin is not a protective barrier, but a sensitive, permeable membrane—a fine, velvet-like mesh—that leaches moisture directly from the air. It is pale, almost grey-blue, and translucent in patches, revealing the slow, rhythmic pulsing of internal bioluminescent fluids that mirror the glow of the drifting spores.

They do not wear clothing in the traditional sense, for the air is tepid and their skin is both sensor and lung. Instead, they drape themselves in "living vestments"—strips of hardy, fibrous moss or cured seaweed-leather that serve as nutrient-delivery systems. When a child is born, they are literally stitched into a small, starter-gourd, a mobile womb that they cultivate and expand with nutrient pastes as they grow into adolescence. An elder of Oor is a master gardener of their own exterior, their dwelling-husks often fused seamlessly to the architecture of the city itself. They have become part of the platform.

Their faces are soft, lacking the sharp definition of human features. They have no need for ears, as they perceive the world through the resonance of the moisture; the "cathedral-hush" of Oor is to them a rich, polyphonic texture they can feel in their marrow. Their eyes are large, dark, and lidless, structured more like camera obscuras than spheres, evolved to capture the tiniest fluctuations in the ambient violet light. When two Oorani communicate, they do not merely speak; they emit low-frequency, subsonic thrums that resonate through the damp floorboards of the city. To watch them interact is to see a conversation of vibrations, where the air between them sparks with a sudden, localized flare of phosphorescent spores, drawn to the heat of the exchange.

Because they exist in a state of perpetual "becoming," the Oorani have no concept of arrival or departure. They do not hurry. To move with speed in Oor is to create a wake that disturbs the delicate pressure of the mist, offending the very foundations of their home. Consequently, their movements are liquid and hypnotic. They drift through the passageways with an economy of energy that makes them appear as extensions of the mist itself.

They are keepers of the silence. They possess a profound, contemplative intelligence, tempered by the knowledge that their entire civilization is a temporary anchorage. They spend their days—or cycles—in a meditative labor: polishing the obsidian tools carved from the distant rock, applying the nutrient pastes to the walls of the gourd-husks, and tending to the Silk-Gliders that nest in the rafters of the city. 

In the rare moments when a cluster of Oorani gathers—perhaps to mourn the crumbling of an old fungal spire or to celebrate the blooming of a new, radiant growth—the collective heat of their bodies draws the drifting spores into a thick, swirling nebula of living light. In that glow, you can see the truth of them: they are the pulse of a slow-motion world, ephemeral creatures in a vast, aqueous monument, holding their breath, waiting for the tide of the universe to finally decide what it wants Oor to be.
