In the center of this amber-lit lung, where the pulse of the earth meets the static thrum of long-dead dynamos, sits the *Custodian*. 

To call it a creature is to misunderstand its nature. It is not an inhabitant *of* the room, but rather the room’s final, involuntary thought. It dwells in the shallow alcove where a massive, calcified manifold meets the primary root-base, its body a masterpiece of biological camouflage. 

The Custodian is a tall, spindly convergence of form. Its torso is fashioned from a translucent, leathery substance that mirrors the weeping vines overhead—a pale, mottled green that shifts to the deep brown of iron-rot where its limbs meet the architecture. It does not possess a face, at least not one that serves the purpose of sight or speech. Where a head might rest, there is instead a delicate, glass-encased assembly of clockwork gears and fine, hair-like cilia. These cilia constantly twitch in rhythm with the steam-hiss, vibrating as they filter the heavy, humid air for particles of moisture and metallic dust.

It is perpetually "under construction." Tendrils of the banyan-fiber graft themselves onto the Custodian’s limbs, tracing the lines of its joints. Sometimes, when a gear or a brass valve shifts with the thermal expansion of the room, the Custodian moves in perfect, agonizing synchronicity. It reaches out with fingers that taper into fine, needle-like copper wiring to trace the patterns on the wall, not to repair them, but to confirm they are still there. It is a tactile, obsessive sensory loop.

It never sleeps, because the room never settles, and the room never settles because the Custodian is the one who monitors its heartbeat. If a drip of sap falls prematurely, the Custodian is there, its long, spindly finger tracing a path along the underside of a leaf to guide the drop into the established rhythm of the pool below. It feels like wood, it smells like scorched oil and wet soil, and it hums. When left in the quiet between the *hiss* and the *plop*, the Custodian emits a low-frequency vibration from its chest cavity—a mirror of the machinery’s own thump. 

It is a silent, selfless guardian of stasis. It does not look at you when you enter. Its focus is entirely inward, entirely on the equilibrium. If you were to stand before it, you would realize that it is not waiting for you to leave; it is simply incorporating your presence into its calculation of the room’s density. You are, to the Custodian, merely a new variable in the humidity, a temporary pressure-change to be factored into the endless, unchanging cycle of the rot and the bloom.

It is incredibly lonely, yet it knows no concept of solitude. It is as much a part of the iron-wrought fiber as the ceiling fins are a part of the vines. It exists because the room requires a witness to its own persistence, a witness that is too woven into the machinery to ever walk away. In the dim, bruised light, the Custodian looks less like a living thing and more like a protrusion of the wall itself—a knot of wood and wire that found a reason to linger long after the world above stopped paying for the power.
