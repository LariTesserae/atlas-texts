To inhabit Sector 4 is to become part of a living oxidation process. The creatures here do not live *on* the city so much as they serve as its ongoing digestive system, breaking down the massive, fossilized intent of the architects into something the earth can finally swallow.

The copper-shelled beetles—the *Scintillants*—are the primary architects of this ruin. With their mandibles, they perform a tireless, grinding alchemy. They are not merely scavengers; they are gardeners of decay. As they chew through the iron scales of a blast furnace, they release a fine, metallic dust into the humid air, which settles into the mire to thicken the oily sludge. When one presses an ear to a rusted bulkhead, the sound is not merely of movement, but of metabolic processing: a low, percussive thrum that suggests the entire sector is slowly being chewed into fine particulate matter by those millions of clicking, synchronized jaws.

Living alongside them, weaving through the flooded grid, are the *Siphon-Eels*. These are the reptilian scavengers, but they bear little resemblance to anything found in a biosphere of light and green. They are long, translucent things, their skin the color of a bruised plum, stretched tight over cartilaginous frames that pulse with the rhythm of the subterranean generators. They possess no eyes, for there is nothing here that requires sight; instead, they navigate by the electrical interference of the ancient, decaying power grid. They slide through the tepid, waist-high water, their heads tilted to catch the hum of the power-conduits, feeding on the bioluminescent fungi that sprout in the damp shadows of the transformers. When a Siphon-Eel moves, it leaves a trail of iridescent oil in its wake, a temporary rainbow on the surface of the murk that quickly dissolves into the shimmering gray film.

Higher up, clinging to the skeletal remains of the gantry cranes, are the *Rust-Strider Nests*. These are not distinct creatures, but communal clusters of insectoid larvae that bind themselves together with a hardened, chitinous foam. They hang like massive, obsidian stalactites from the rafters of the warehouses. Because the heat in Sector 4 is constant, they enter a state of perpetual, twitching semi-sleep, their bodies secreting a moisture-wicking enzyme that keeps them from drowning in the heavy air. Thousands of them dangle in the violet mist, shivering in unison whenever a draft of wind forces the warehouse doors to groan on their hinges.

There is a quiet, terrifying intelligence to this equilibrium. It is a world that has replaced the frantic, heat-based ambition of industry with a slow, biological patience. 

Occasionally, something from the deep, forgotten sub-basements rises to the surface—a creature of pure mineral density, slow and heavy, with legs of basalt and a carapace of rusted iron plating. These are the *Foundry-Hulls*. They move only once every few decades, crawling out of the dark sumps to settle in a new location, their weight causing the very streets to shudder and groan. They do not eat or hunt; they simply exist as mobile anchors, their massive forms becoming new surfaces for the beetles to populate.

In Sector 4, nothing dies in the traditional sense. It simply changes velocity. The metal turns to flake, the flake turns to silt, and the silt rises through the bellies of the beetles to be deposited elsewhere, reformulating the city. It is a circular, humid, and unending liturgy. The pulse of the generator is the heartbeat, the rustle of the beetles is the breath, and the heavy, metallic smell of the water is the blood of a city that learned, long ago, that moving forward was far less efficient than simply waiting for the world to turn into dust.
