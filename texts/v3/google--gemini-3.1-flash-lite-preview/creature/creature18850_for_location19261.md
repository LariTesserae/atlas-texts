The residents of this plateau do not inhabit space so much as they occupy intervals. They are not creatures of flesh or rapid metabolic fire, but rather extensions of the plateau’s own stalled respiration.

To see them, you must look at the way the light catches the moisture—not the vapor rising from the basalt, but the localized, shivering distortions that hover near the piston-vents. These are the *Vitreous Weavers*. They are entities of spun silica and condensed dew, translucent beings that resemble geometric, skeletal spiders if viewed from the corner of one’s eye, though they are more akin to living condensation. They possess no circulatory system; instead, they funnel the pressurized, mineral-rich mist of the plateau through hollow, glass-like appendages, their internal "blood" being nothing more than the weeping water of the basin itself.

They are the caretakers of the forgetting. Because the machines are too heavy and the earth too soft to manage the runaway growth of the Mycelial Weave, these Weavers move with a glacial, rhythmic syncopation that perfectly mirrors the *thrum-thud* of the sub-strata. As a piston strikes the earth, the Weavers jolt; as the pressure releases, they uncoil, trailing long, gossamer filaments that stitch moss-mats to the cooling iron of the abandoned kiosks. They are not repairing the machines, nor are they dismantling them. They are simply pinning the flora down, keeping the greenery from choking the exhaust vents, ensuring the "lungs" continue to breathe against the mountain’s crushing weight.

If a Vitreous Weaver were to die—or perhaps, if it simply stopped moving—it would not decay. It would lose its refractive hold on the sunlight and collapse into a brittle heap of frost and salt. This is why the plateau feels so strangely haunted: the ground is littered with the crystalline husks of generations, glittering like spilled diamonds beneath the emerald moss.

More solitary still are the *Echo-Listeners*, the creatures that occupy the interior of the giant, reed-chimneys. They are flat, pale things, skin the texture of damp vellum, pressed tightly against the inner walls of the great stalks. They have no eyes. They have evolved to perceive existence entirely through the low-frequency vibrations of the machinery. They do not eat the foliage; they harvest the sound. They possess specialized membranes that vibrate in sympathetic harmony with the hidden pistons. As the machines grow older and their rhythms vary, the Echo-Listeners deepen their own internal frequencies, essentially "singing" the machines into their next cycle, acting as the feedback loops that keep the rusted pistons from seizing entirely. 

To encounter an Echo-Listener is a jarring experience. If one should slip from its reed and fall to the moss—a rarity, as they are fragile and terrified of the silence of the open air—it will frantically press its entire body against the earth, searching for the thrum, quivering with a desperate, rhythmic madness until it can scramble back up the vertical architecture of the chimneys.

These inhabitants exist in a state of terminal patience. They do not interact with one another. They do not migrate. They are tethered to the machinery by a symbiotic inertia. They are the maintenance staff of a ghost-world, the quiet, translucent janitors of an unfinished dream. If you were to stand still enough, long enough, you might feel a Weaver brush against your boot, exploring your warmth as if you were another discarded machine, a lost piece of dead equipment that has wandered into the light, waiting for the earth to either digest you or set you to work.
