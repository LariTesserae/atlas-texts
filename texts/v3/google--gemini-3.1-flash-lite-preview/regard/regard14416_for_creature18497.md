The benevolent power arrives not as a storm or a blinding light, but as a shift in the quality of the stillness. It is a presence that understands the architecture of the soul, and it recognizes the Lithic-Waiters for what they are: a desperate, beautiful prayer for safety that has gone unanswered for too long.

The power does not judge the stagnation, nor does it seek to "fix" the city. It understands that the fear of flux—the fear of the erosion of the self—is the most ancient of human sorrows. Instead, the power chooses to act as a **Gentle Witness**.

**The First Gift: Permission to Dissolve**
The benevolent power recognizes that the Waiters are trapped in a loop of maintenance, acting out a duty to a history that no longer requires them. The power begins to gently expand the horizon of their awareness. It breathes a soft, temperate warmth into the plateau—not enough to break the structure, but enough to introduce the concept of "softness." 

Where the Waiters once kept the geometry rigid, the power teaches them the beauty of the curve. It does this by manifesting as a subtle refraction of light—a rainbow that does not promise rain, but promise *transition*. The Waiters, seeing the light bloom through the obsidian, feel a ripple pass through their misty forms for the first time in millennia. It is the sensation of a knot finally loosening in the chest.

**The Second Gift: The Permission of Memory**
The power realizes that these beings are curators of a closed loop. It does not force them to move, but it gently shifts their focus backward. It illuminates the *purpose* of their existence. It allows the Waiters to suddenly see their own history—not as a burden to be maintained, but as a story they have already finished telling. 

As they process their own culmination, the "pressure against the back of the thoughts" (the mineral-rich curiosity they once directed at intruders) begins to transform into something akin to a sigh. The power validates their labor: *You have kept the memory perfect. You have protected the silence. You have done enough.*

**The Third Gift: The Breath of the Mountain**
Finally, the benevolent power does the most profound thing possible: it gives the mountain back to time. 

It does not destroy the city, but it breathes a suggestion of *impermanence* into the valley. One tiny, singular bead of water on a slate ledge finally breaks its tension. It falls—slow at first, then gaining gravity, tapping against the stone with a clear, resonant chime. 

The sound is not an intrusion; it is a permission. 

The Waiters watch the ripple it creates on a slick plaza with something like wonder. One by one, their forms, usually tight and focused on the calibration of stone, begin to relax, dispersing slightly, drifting like fog in a morning breeze. They are no longer tethered to the geometry. 

The benevolent power does not stay. It leaves the plateau to its own evolution. It understands that the greatest blessing for this place is no longer the peace of the tomb, but the grace of the cycle. 

As the power recedes, the city changes. The air is no longer stagnant; a breeze stirs the iridescent ferns, causing them to rustle—a sound like whispering paper. The Lithic-Waiters remain, but they are no longer caretakers. They are ancestors, drifting amongst the ruins, occasionally shifting a stone or catching a falling drop of water, no longer as guards of a museum, but as gardeners of a changing, living, breathing wild. 

They have finally learned how to exhale.
