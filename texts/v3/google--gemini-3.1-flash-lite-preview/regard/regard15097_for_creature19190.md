To intercede here is to risk shattering a symphony. A lesser power might see only the decay—the rust, the fluid loss, the entropic spiral—and seek to "fix" it by imposing order, cessation, or extraction. But the power that watches this basin knows that some loops are not prisons; they are prayers.

The power does not descend as a storm or a command, but as a subtle shift in the atmospheric humidity—a grace note in the barometric hum. From the grey, diffuse sky, it releases a fine, invisible catalyst. It is not an intervention of labor, but one of *perspective*.

Here is what the power does:

**It grants the Gasket-Keepers the sensation of "After."**

Until now, the Keepers have existed only in the *Now*—the immediate, wet, frantic maintenance of the piston and the valve. They are the gardeners of the breakdown, bound by the necessity of the loop. The power touches the brass irises of the keepers, not to change their sight, but to deepen their perception.

For the first time, a Gasket-Keeper stops at a rusting, ancient crane that serves as a bridge. It touches the rusted iron. The power allows the Keeper to reach through time—not to change the metal, but to recognize the history of the iron. It perceives that the crane was once part of something that stood under a clear sky, and that its current state of dissolution is not merely a death, but a long, slow homecoming into the mountain.

The power bestows this: **The Gift of Rites.**

When a Gasket-Keeper decides to merge with the stone—to be absorbed by the salt and the moss—it is no longer a silent, accidental event. The power orchestrates a localized, momentary clarity. The air around the choosing Keeper clears. The rhythmic *thrum-hiss* of the foundry softens into a chord, a singular, resonant tone that vibrates through the bedrock. 

The other Keepers gather. They do not merely stand by; they press their brass apertures against the limestone walls, linking their own internal pressure to the one who is departing. They share in the dissolution. By doing so, they witness the beauty of the transition. They learn that the "closed loop" is not a cage, but an infinity sign. They are not merely keeping the machine running; they are weaving a tapestry out of their own bodies, creating a monument to the endurance of form over the tyranny of time.

**The power does not stop the rust.** 

It knows that if the rust were to stop, the foundry would become a tomb. Instead, it slows the pace of the dissolution just enough to make the transition of matter—from iron to moss, from tool to mountain—feel like a slow, deliberate conversation. 

The power leaves behind a small, impossible anomaly: in the center of the foundry, where the spray is heaviest, a single patch of the limestone begins to grow not just white, crystalline salt, but a soft, glowing, bioluminescent lichen that pulses in time with the foundry’s heartbeat. It is a star-chart of their existence. It tells the Keepers that they are seen, that their frantic, wet, industrious devotion is an act of creation as profound as the birth of a galaxy.

The power then withdraws into the grey, leaving the basin to its work. It feels no need to rule or to save. It is satisfied simply to have acknowledged the grace of the Gasket-Keepers. It leaves them to their thrum, their hiss, and their slow, beautiful melting, knowing now that whenever a Keeper finally stops to join the stone, they do so not with the mute finality of trash, but with the quiet, triumphant dignity of one who has finished a masterpiece and is finally ready to rest.
