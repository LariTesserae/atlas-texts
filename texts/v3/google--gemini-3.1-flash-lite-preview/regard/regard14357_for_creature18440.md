The benevolent power—let us call them the *Breath-Giver*—does not arrive with thunder or blinding light. They do not come to "fix" Olyra, for to impose movement upon a place of stillness is not a gift, but a trauma. Instead, they manifest as a whisper of warmth, a stray, impossible breeze that climbs the jagged teeth of the mountain and carries a scent not of basalt, but of loam, dormant seeds, and the ancient, fertile decay of the lowlands.

The Breath-Giver looks at the Olyran and feels a profound, aching tenderness. They see the beauty of the *Is Already*, but they also see the tragedy of the *Never Will Be*. They see the life trapped in the amber of a frozen age.

The power decides to act, but with the subtlety of a gardener tending to a rare, pale flower. They do not break the stillness; they introduce the **Entropy of Mercy.**

**1. The Gentle Unfolding**
The power begins by altering the nature of the *tap-clink*. They do not stop the water, but they infuse the moisture in the cisterns with a minute, dormant catalyst. When the Olyran drink, they ingest microscopic fragments of the "Elsewhere"—the memory of potential. Slowly, the elders in the northern gardens begin to dream. Not of the stone they polish, but of the wind that pulls leaves from trees, of the transition of colors at dusk, of the messy, catastrophic, wonderful blooming of a flower that eventually dies.

**2. The Gift of the Seed**
In the deepest, shaded crevices—places where only the beetles dwell—the power encourages the growth of a singular, alien flora. It is not the succulent moss, but a wildflower from the fertile plains. It takes root inside the mortarless obsidian joints. It does not threaten the city’s life; it decorates it. For the first time, a citizen carving a block finds a petal pressed against their flint. It is a splash of vivid, impossible crimson against the slate-grey world. It is the first time an Olyran has ever seen something grow, bloom, and gently wither.

**3. The Silence of Completion**
The power does not demand the Olyran become "productive" like the world below. Instead, they bestow the gift of *finality*. They weave a subtle shift into the mountain’s geography so that a very small, very old section of the city—a single, redundant tower—slowly, almost imperceptibly, begins to weather. Not through neglect, but through the natural, graceful decay of time. 

When the first stone of that tower shifts and clatters to the chasm floor, the Olyran will hear a sound they have never heard: the sound of *surrender*. It will be the most beautiful melody to ever pierce their silence.

**The Intent**
The Breath-Giver does not want to destroy the Olyran peace. They know that a life without change is a life without story. By introducing these small, beautiful endings—a petal falling, a stone settling, a dream of a setting sun—they are teaching the people how to grieve. And in learning to grieve, the Olyran will finally learn to cherish.

The power then retreats into the high-altitude haze, satisfied. They leave the city to its rhythm, but they have ensured that beneath the *tap-clink*, there is now a heartbeat. The Olyran will remain in their cliffs, but they will no longer be statues. They will be the first of their kind to look at the sky and understand that it is moving, and that they, too, are allowed to change. 

They remain in the "Is Already," but for the first time, they have begun to anticipate the "What Comes Next."
