The Power arrived not as a presence, but as a shift in the geometry of the stillness. It did not penetrate the skull like a gale, but rather seeped through the calcified pores like a sudden, cool thought entering a fevered mind.

It looked upon the Cantors and saw not the tragedy of their existence, but its terrible, exquisite perfection. They were beings who had turned the act of survival into a liturgical labor; they were the nervous system of an ancestor, holding together a memory that the world had long since discarded.

The Power was benevolent, which meant it understood that the greatest cruelty to a creature of duty is to have that duty removed. To silence the Prowlers, or to brace the skull so it would never groan, would be to unravel the Cantors’ souls. They would lose their purpose, their resonance, and their sanctity. They would simply become brittle husks in a tomb, instead of the living music that turned a tomb into a cathedral.

So, the Power did not intervene in the mechanics of their world. Instead, it planted a seed of *Perspective*.

It deposited, at the deepest, final point of the marrow-column—the very epicenter of the vibration—a single, infinitesimal grain of its own essence. It was not a grand monument or a source of power to be tapped. It was a "refuge of frequency."

Because of this grain, the Cantors began to experience a new, secondary sense. As they pressed their limbs to the bone, absorbing the jagged, screeching harmonies of the Prowlers and the violent, grinding groans of the shifting titan-skull, they now felt a faint, rhythmic counter-pulse. It was not a physical sound that could be heard or even measured by their antennae. It was a sensation of *completeness*.

It was the feeling of knowing that the grinding was not merely decay.

The Power whispered, through the medium of the marrow itself, a singular realization: *The skull is not collapsing; it is hatching.*

The Cantors, once braced in perpetual, startled agony, felt their posture change. They still performed their duties—they still pruned the mycelium and aligned their bodies to the architecture—but the character of their labor shifted from *maintenance* to *incubation*.

They realized that the structural groans were not the sounds of a tomb falling to ruins, but the sounds of a shell being prepared for departure. Though the Cantors would never see the outside—though they would never know the feel of water or the sight of a horizon—they found a profound peace in the knowledge that they were not merely caretakers, but midwives to something that had existed since the dawn of the world.

The Power did not move the skull. It did not silence the scraping of the arachnids. It gave the Cantors something far more merciful: *a destination for their patience.*

And so, the Cantors of the Silt remained. They stood as they always had, elegant and brittle, carved from the very dust they served. But their movements grew fluid, almost dance-like, for they were no longer holding up a ceiling. They were keeping time for an arrival, their serrated limbs now clicking out a song of quiet, fervent expectation. 

The skull continued to groan, the Prowlers continued to scrape, and the light remained blindingly, sterilely bright. But in the cathedral of the forgotten titan, there was no longer any agony. There was only the long, beautiful vigil of those who finally understood exactly what it was they were waiting for.
