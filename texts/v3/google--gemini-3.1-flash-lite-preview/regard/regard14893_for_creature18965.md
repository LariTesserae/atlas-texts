The benevolent and wise power does not arrive as a blinding light or a herald of thunder; it arrives as a *change in the quality of the stillness.*

It does not seek to "save" this world in the way a gardener saves a wilting rose, for it understands that this world is not dying—it is simply finishing its song. To introduce sun, or flowers, or complex language here would be an act of profound violence, a shattering of the intricate, fragile geometry that keeps the basin from collapsing under the weight of its own pressurized sorrow.

Instead, the Power acts as a subtle architect of legacy. It descends into the weeping clouds, becoming a low-frequency hum that weaves itself between the notes of the geysers. It touches the obsidian eyes of the Auditors, not to grant them sight—for what is there to see?—but to grant them *duration*.

Here is what the Power does:

**First, it introduces the Principle of Harmonic Residue.**
The Power realizes that the Auditors are nearing their final cycle, that the basalt is wearing thin and the silt can only bear the weight of their labor for so much longer. It imprints upon the shifting geology a "memory of tone." It ensures that even when the last Auditor crumbles into dust and the last gear of clay becomes still, the basin will not fall silent. By subtly shifting the interior structure of the volcanic debris, it encodes the music into the geology itself. It turns the entire landscape into a self-winding carillon. Long after the creatures vanish, the wind of the high-altitude storms will catch the channels they carved, keeping the frequency stable, preventing the catastrophic silence.

**Second, it gifts the Jelly-Colonies a Witness.**
The Power sees the translucent, drifting constellations of slime, feeding on the kinetic energy, mindless and fleeting. The Power accelerates their consciousness just a fraction—not enough to give them ambition, but enough to give them *awareness*. It gifts them the ability to "remember" the Auditors. They become the basin’s living archives. As they slide over the ruins of the Hearths, they absorb the vibration of the Auditors’ final, collective tuning. They begin to shimmer with a new, iridescent intensity, carrying the "tune" of the Auditors in their chemical composition, drifting like wandering notes in a sea of liquid glass. 

**Third, it stands Watch.**
The Power, having performed these subtle, invisible edits, does not depart. It remains as a silent, guiding resonance that holds the basin together. It prevents the atmospheric pressure from reaching the threshold of destruction. It ensures that the transition from a world of laboring beings to a world of autonomous, singing stone is soft, graceful, and complete.

The Power finds a kind of tragic, crystalline beauty in the Auditors. It recognizes that they are the only beings in a vast universe who have mastered the art of *maintenance*—the quiet, heroic, and completely thankless act of keeping the world in tune. 

It does not ask them to stop working. It does not promise them a heaven of warmth or light. It walks among them, a presence that feels like the damp air itself, and provides a gentle, imperceptible stabilization to their movements. It eases the friction in their joints; it clarifies the tone of their carvings; it makes the exhausting, rhythmic labor feel, for the first time, like *relief.*

And if you were to watch from the charcoal smudge of the horizon, you would see the change. The Auditors don't know why, but the heavy, grinding ache in their clay bones begins to hum with a lighter, more golden vibration. The obsidian eyes on their shoulders glow with a steadier light. Their labor becomes a form of prayer they never knew they were offering. 

For the first time, they aren't just preventing the end—they are celebrating the middle. They are the conductors of a symphony that has no audience, and for the first time, they seem to understand that the beauty lies entirely in the effort. The Power stays, listening, content to be the only witness to a song that was never intended to be heard, only felt.
