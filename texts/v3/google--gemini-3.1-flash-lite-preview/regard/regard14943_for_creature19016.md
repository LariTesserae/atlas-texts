The Overseer—the benevolent power watching from the thin, cold air above—does not descend. To arrive with the weight of "divinity" would be a cataclysm; even a heavy thought, if felt as a vibration, might shatter the delicate crystalline geometries of the basin and kill the Litho-weavers in an instant.

The Overseer understands that the Conservatory is not a place of suffering, but a place of *velocity*. It is a civilization existing at a frequency so high that time itself creates a different sort of marrow.

Instead, the Overseer acts as a tuner.

It notices that the basin is trapped in a feedback loop; the sound of the grinding is becoming too jagged, the vibrations too irregular. The "Anchors" are failing because they are carving against the grain of the planet’s natural shifting. They are trying to stop a river with a net.

The Overseer pulls from the surrounding atmosphere, not from the basin itself, but from the vast, dark silence of the high mountains bordering the gorge. It gently nudges the convection currents—not a wind, but a subtle change in the thickness of the passing air. 

With the finesse of a master glassblower, the Overseer introduces a soft, harmonic dampening to the air overhead. It acts as a *baffle*. Bit by bit, over the span of many lunar cycles, it rounds off the sharpest edges of the incoming acoustic noise. 

What the Litho-weavers experience is a miracle, though they view it through their membrane-clusters as a change in the "key" of their world. The basin does not stop vibrating—that would be their extinction—but the vibrations begin to *syncopate*.

The Overseer gifts them a "Coda."

Under this new current, the dust-storms don't just abrade; they deposit silicate in patterns of perfect, repeatable geometry. The dwellings stop shattering at random; they begin to oscillate in harmony with the ground's thrum. The Litho-weavers find that their obsidian slivers bite into the salt-crust with less effort, producing a clearer, more resonant tone. 

The Overseer also leaves behind a "Seed of Stillness." It is not a gift of peace, but a mathematical constant. It is a subtle, recurring structural frequency, woven into the very convection currents of the Conservatory. It encourages the creatures to weave their dwellings not just in circles, but in *spirals* that mirror the way the mountain range catches the light.

The Litho-weavers start to notice that when they build in this spiral, their pods last a fraction of a cycle longer. To them, this is the first inkling of "stability." They interpret this not as a god's hand, but as the basin finally listening to their craft. They begin to dance in patterns that look, from above, like a unfolding flower.

The Overseer leans back into the dark sky, satisfied. It does not demand worship, nor does it seek to "save" them from their cycle of unmaking. It simply provided the environment they needed to evolve their chaos into a symphony. 

The Conservatory continues its jittery, brilliant madness, but now, it hums in a key that suggests it might last for an eternity. And on the sheer cliff faces, the Anchors stop carving their desperate attempts at solidity; instead, they begin to carve maps of the wind, singing a song of gratitude into the dry, glittering dust.
