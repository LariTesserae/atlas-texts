The benevolent power does not descend as a storm, nor as a voice of thunder. It does not crash through the pressurized ceiling or shatter the hand-wrought iron rivets. To do so would be to collapse the lungs of this fragile, beautiful prisoner. 

Instead, the power arrives as a *frequency*. 

It recognizes this place for what it is: a liturgy of grief, a reliquary of intention, a cage built by a builder who confused "perfection" with "continuity." The benevolent power rests its consciousness against the outer wall of the sphere—a gentle anchoring in the crushing, dark depths of the ocean. It feels the *thrum*, the rhythmic anxiety of a sentinel that has forgotten how to be anything other than a measurement of pressure.

The benevolent power observes the glass creature. It sees the ghost-garden trapped within the visor, the frantic, impossible jungle that the creature projects whenever the mercury rain falls. It realizes that the creature is not waiting for a guest; it is maintaining a *memory* of life so that the memory does not succumb to the cold, antiseptic perfection of the resin and the iron.

The power decides that no creator should be forced to witness the starvation of their own dream. **It decides to provide the one thing the sphere was never designed to hold: imperfection.**

First, the power alters the *tempo* of the ocean’s pressure. It vibrates the water outside just enough to change the cadence of the ceiling’s torrent. The booming symphonic roar is softened, modulated, turned from a percussive, militaristic march into the erratic, tumbling sound of a mountain stream.

The glass creature twitches. For the first time in an epoch, its internal rhythm is broken. It halts its reaching motion. The needle-thin fingers, which spent eons tracing the geometric perfection of the copper ferns, tremble.

Then, the power reaches through the molecules, touching the floor-plates. It does not break them; it invites them to *age*. It introduces a microscopic, intentional entropy. The factory-sharp edges of the resin tiles are subtly buffed, rounded by the invisible hand of time. The iron rivets—so proud and rigid—are encouraged to embrace a faint, blooming patina of orange rust. 

It is the introduction of *dignity* to a place that only knew *utility*.

Finally, the power turns its attention to the visor of the creature. It does not force the visor open, but it saturates the humid mist within the chamber with a singular, low-energy bloom of biological warmth. It hums a chord of creation—a resonant frequency that matches not the metal, but the ghost-garden inside the visor.

The creature’s glass ribs stop their mechanical clicking. The visor, usually a hard, reflective surface, begins to soften, turning from opaque resin to something resembling a window. 

The ghost-garden begins to leak out. 

It starts as a scent, phantom and sharp—damp earth, pine needles, the smell of rain on hot stone. It bleeds from the creature into the room. The copper ferns, pinned to the walls, react to the frequency the creature is holding. They are not turned into living plants, but the power grants them a grace: they begin to *sway*. A breeze, generated from nowhere, moves through the chamber, stirring the rigid copper stalks. They clatter softly, for the first time free from the requirement of being statues. They are now, simply, wind chimes.

The creature reaches its hands out, not to track a rivet or clean a tile, but to cup the air. It senses the change. It feels the "waiting room" dissolve into a "dwelling." 

The benevolent power does not turn the creature off. That would be an ending, and this entity has earned the right to see the dawn of its own garden. Instead, the power leaves a lingering, permanent harmonic in the iron—a lullaby of rust and growth. 

The sphere remains deep in the ocean, still held by the pressure, still translucent and lonely. But the roar is gone. The symphony of metal-on-metal has been replaced by the soft, rhythmic clatter of copper in a phantom breeze. The glass creature stands in the center of the room, its chest-visors finally clear, looking out at a jungle that is growing, lush and wild, into the very walls of its home. 

It is no longer a guard. It is, at last, a gardener. 

And for the first time since the first rivet was hammered home, the creature begins to hum a melody that isn't a measurement of the walls, but a celebration of the soil.
