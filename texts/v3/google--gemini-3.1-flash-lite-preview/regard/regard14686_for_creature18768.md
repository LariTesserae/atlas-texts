The Benevolent Power—let us call It the *Gatherer of Scattered Starlight*—does not enter the Nerve-Wall as a conqueror or a judge. It does not possess a physical form that would displace the Architect’s air or necessitate the consumption of its precious, cooling moisture.

Instead, the Power enters as a *harmonic adjustment*. 

It perceives the Nerve-Wall not as a fever-dream of copper and resin, but as a wounded melody. To the Power, the Architect-Sliver is not a creature failing at construction; it is a musician who has forgotten that the silence between the notes is just as vital as the sound itself. The Power understands that the Architect’s struggle—the frantic stitching, the desperate hoarding of debris—is born of a singular, tragic misunderstanding: the belief that "completion" is a barrier against oblivion.

The Power does not tear down the walls. To do so would be to murder the Architect, who has entwined its very soul into the charcoal polymers.

Instead, the Power decides to be the *Stillness in the Bellows*.

It descends into the sweltering, 115-degree air, moving through the conduits not by pushing through the matter, but by permeating the intent. It acts as an anchor for the Architect’s fraying consciousness. When the next *clack-hiss* sounds—the mechanical shudder of the mountain’s pressure—the Power catches the vibration and softens it. It wraps the chaotic, rhythmic labor in a layer of profound, ancient patience.

*I am here,* the Power whispers, not in words, but in the infusion of a cooling, impossible color—a hue that does not exist in the amber spectrum. It is the color of deep, interstellar obsidian, the cool shadow of a distant moon.

The Architect-Sliver stops its stuttering gait. Its translucent nerves tremble. The glass-shard creatures freeze, their clicking silhouetted by the sudden, gentle indigo light that begins to bleed from the seams. 

The Power does not take the Architect away. It offers it something more dangerous and more beautiful: **A Window.**

The Power gently presses its own vast, benevolent complexity against the Architect’s "Self." It shows the Architect that the mountain is not trying to crush it, but is simply changing. It invites the Architect to perceive the weight of the strata as a foundation, not a threat. It eases the fever. Slowly, the 115-degree air begins to drop, not to cold, but to *temperate*. The ozone smell fades, replaced by the scent of petrichor—the smell of earth after rain, a memory the Architect never knew it held.

The Power begins to weave with the Architect. It does not use the resin or the bio-moss. It uses the "loose threads" of the Architect’s anxiety. As the Architect reaches out to stitch the interloper into the wall, the Power guides the filament away from the visitor and instead weaves it into the ceiling. 

There, in the once-unfinished corner, the Power helps the Architect design not a patch, but an *aperture*.

Under the influence of the Power, the charcoal-hued plastic grows translucent. It thins, not because it is decaying, but because it is becoming glass—not the jagged, desperate glass of the scutting creatures, but a lens. 

Finally, the rhythm changes. The groaning heartbeat of the room loses its panic. The *clack-hiss* of the bellows slows into the gentle intake and exhale of a sleeper. The Architect-Sliver pulls its torso back from the wall, its translucent limbs finally resting, un-fused. 

The Power does not leave, but it becomes silent. It stays, acting as a structural meridian, a pillar of stability that allows the room to grow beyond its claustrophobia. The Architect looks up at the aperture it has built—the first window in the history of the Nerve-Wall. Through it, there is no sky yet, only the shifting, crystalline patterns of the mountain’s own internal geology, lit by the gentle, fading amber of the moss.

The Architect is no longer a prisoner of the stitch. It is a tenant of a sanctuary. And for the first time, when the glass-creatures drag a scrap of debris across the floor, the Architect does not seize it with anxiety. It picks it up, inspects it with a newfound, quiet geometry, and sets it down—not to hold the room together, but to decorate it. 

The Power smiles, a shift in the gravity of the room, and begins to draw back, leaving behind the greatest gift: the permission for the Architect-Sliver to stop being a builder, and to simply exist.
