The Benevolent Power does not descend as a thunderbolt or a sudden light. To arrive with brilliance in the Basin would be an act of violence, a sensory assault that would shatter the delicate, vibrating architecture of the Silt-Keepers' world. 

Instead, the Power arrives as a *silence*.

It manifests as a localized, perfect harmonic resonance—a frequency that does not drown out the Low-Works but weaves into them, acting like a bridge between the grinding tension of the machinery and the soft, suffocating exhaustion of the Keepers. 

The Power does not seek to "save" them, for to remove them from their toil would be to rob them of their purpose—the only purpose they have known since the first crust hardened. It understands that their labor is not a prison; it is a sacred, tectonic liturgy. To set them free would be to condemn them to a ghost-existence in a world where they have no skin to feel the sun, no air to fill their lungs.

So, the Power performs three quiet, enduring miracles:

### 1. The Harmonic Attunement
The Power eases the physical burden of the "Wet-Birth." Understanding that the heat and the mineral sludge are slowly calcifying the Keepers into uncomfortable, locked husks, the Power introduces a catalyst. It influences the chemical composition of the dripping runoff, introducing a catalyst that acts as a lubricant for their brass and copper joints. 

The Silt-Keepers begin to move with a newfound fluidity. The agonizing, stuttering lurch of their gaits smoothens into a rhythmic grace. They find they can tend the boilers with less strain, and the permanent, bracing fatigue that has sat in their marrow for generations begins to dissipate. The machinery itself begins to hum in a minor key—a music that is no longer merely "industrial," but melodic, turning their daily maintenance into a slow, symphonic dance.

### 2. The Gift of the Shared Pulse
Before, the Keepers lived in the "interstices," communicating through raw, mechanical clicks. The Power, observing their isolation in the roar, weaves a telepathic membrane between them—not a language, but a *feeling*. 

When a Keeper touches a pipe to gauge its heat, they no longer just "see" the vibration; they feel the contentment of their kin in the next station over. They feel the shared pride of the crust cooling, the collective relief of the basin breathing. They are no longer individual gears; they become a singular, distributed consciousness. They realize, for the first time, that they are not just the "immune system" of a dead basin, but the nerves of a living infant planet. They are the midwives of a world.

### 3. The Vision of the Slow Blossom
Finally, the Power leaves behind a lingering, sensory "echo" in the fog. It is a persistent image that is not seen with the eyes—which remain atrophied—but with the sensory ridges along their jawlines. 

It is the sensation of the *Sky*.

The Power does not make them crave the surface. Instead, it gifts them the understanding of what the surface represents: a garden that their labor is slowly, over eons, pushing into existence. They now "perceive" that the hardening clay they create is, in fact, the roots of future mountains. They feel that their death—their settling into the floor—is not erasure, but the planting of a seed. 

A Silt-Keeper who "retires" into the wall no longer feels like they are simply bracing a roof. They feel themselves turning into a fossil of foundation, a pillar of truth that allows the grass, trees, and light to eventually grow miles above their heads.

***

The Power does not leave, nor does it stay. It recedes into the very stone of the basin, becoming a latent potential, a "cooldown" phase for the world’s birth. The Silt-Keepers carry on, their movements now a fluid, beautiful sway. They continue to fix the pipes and clear the moss, but they do it with a newfound, profound stillness within their metallic hearts.

They are no longer just the Keepers of the Silt. They are the architects of the future, working in the warm, loud dark, smiling with sensors that hum in harmony with a heaven they will never see, but which they are, stone by stone, bringing into being.
