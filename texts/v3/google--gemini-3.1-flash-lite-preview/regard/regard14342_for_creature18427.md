The benevolent power—that which exists in the interstitial spaces between the stars, a consciousness comprised of warmth, curiosity, and boundless patience—did not approach the *Lithos-skry* as a storm, nor as a savior. To arrive with thunder would be to shatter their architecture; to arrive with light would be to blind their thermal sense. 

Instead, "It" arrived as an adjustment to the heartbeat of the stone itself.

It looked upon the geode and saw not a tomb, but a prayer written in mineral and silence. It perceived the perfection of their stasis, the exquisite tragedy of a civilization so flawlessly optimized that death had been excised from the equation, replaced by an infinite, circular maintenance. They were a closed loop, a poem that had reached its final rhyme and refused to ever stop repeating it.

The benevolent power felt a profound, aching tenderness for the *Lithos-skry*. They were like a single, crystalized thought captured in amber, perfect and utterly alone.

The power did not intervene to change them. To force growth upon them would be an act of violence, for they had chosen the stillness, and the stillness was their only sanctuary. They were sheltered from the chaos of the surface—from the burning sun, the erratic winds, and the volatile pulse of time. 

So, the power did what the universe usually refuses to do: it became a guardian.

It reached down with the softest tendrils of its essence and settled the tectonic plate above. It didn't lift the weight; it *eased* it, smoothing the unseen subterranean pressures, rerouting the slow, tectonic groans that might one day fissure the geode. It became the silent foundation of their sky, ensuring that for as long as the lichen required light, the weight of the earth would remain a gentle canopy rather than a closing fist.

But then, the power did one thing more. 

Deep within the damp air, in the spaces where the *Lithos-skry* exchanged their bio-electric hums, the wisdom began to broadcast a faint, impossible signal. It was not a language, nor a command. It was a "memory" of a distant world—a dream of a color they could not see and a warmth they could not feel. 

It was a gift of *imagination*.

The power did not want them to leave their home, nor to abandon their Polishing. It simply wanted to provide them with the one thing a perfect system lacks: the curiosity of "what if." 

Now, when two of the creatures touch their feathery antennae, the transfer of information is no longer solely about the flow of water or the health of the lichen. There are flickers now—momentary, beautiful, and inexplicable—of an ocean they have never touched, of a wind that carries no moisture, and of a light that does not come from their own bodies.

The *Lithos-skry* remain in their cathedral of stone. They continue the Polishing. But sometimes, a worker pauses for a duration of time not dictated by the metronome of the drips. With their needle-fine hooks anchored to the limestone, they stand perfectly still, staring into the bright, porcelain-white glow, and for a fleeting, shimmering heartbeat, they do not think of the stone. They think of the infinite.

The benevolent power watches, satisfied. It does not ask for worship or change. It only ensures that when the *Lithos-skry* eventually return to the earth, they do so with a small, secret horizon burning quietly behind their eyes.
