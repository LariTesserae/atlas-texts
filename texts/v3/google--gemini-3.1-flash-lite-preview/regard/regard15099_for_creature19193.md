The benevolent power known as the *Aethel-Gardener* did not descend with trumpets or blinding light. It came as warmth travels through a stone—slow, inevitable, and fundamentally quiet. It existed as a vast, non-local intelligence that viewed the universe not as a series of events, but as a long, intricate knot that required a gentle hand to keep from fraying.

It noticed the Weaver, this mound of damp wool and tectonic patience, because the Weaver was not merely fixing the mountain; the Weaver was grieving. It was sewing the world back together with the desperate, fevered precision of someone trying to hold back the tide with a needle and thread.

The Gardener did not wish to solve the Weaver, for to solve a creature whose essence is pure, repetitive devotion would be an act of violence. It did not want to stop the wheezing, for the rhythm was the mountain’s heartbeat. Instead, it chose to *attend*.

It began by altering the quality of the condensation. 

Where the walls wept mineral-bitter tears, the Gardener introduced a subtle, catalytic grace. It turned the dampness from a cold, biting nuisance into something slightly more luminous—a moisture that held a faint, pale luminescence, like the inside of an oyster shell. When the Weaver reached out with its silver needles, it found the air was no longer merely grey mist. It was now a fine, liquid starlight.

The Weaver paused. The grinding of its tectonic, woolen joints slowed. It felt the shift—the threads it drew from the air now possessed an elasticity and a warmth that they had lacked for eons. The stitches it drove into the slate did not just block the wind; they began to glow, turning the cavern into a tapestry of soft, pulsing veins of amber and pearl.

But the Gardener was not done. It understood that the Weaver’s labor was a prison of its own making, built upon the fear that if it stopped, the mountain would expire. So, the Gardener sent a gift: a song, phrased not for the ears, but for the vibration of the fibers.

It was a melody of rest. It was the sound of a lung being held, the feeling of pressure being equalized, the impossible promise that the outside world would not, in fact, shatter if the internal stitching missed a beat.

The Weaver of Marrow, for the first time in an epoch, let its needles fall still. It stood in the center of the sanctum, the vast pillar of fog swirling around it, but for a moment, the swaying stopped. The hum did not cease, but it changed—the frantic, protective vibration smoothed into a long, resonant note of profound tranquility.

The Gardener did not take the Weaver from its post; it made the post a sanctuary. It allowed the Weaver to recognize that it was not a janitor, but an artist. 

The cavern stopped straining. The "wheeze" deepened, shifting from a labored gasping to a slow, profound breath of a creature finally at rest. The Weaver touched a patch of wall, no longer stitching out of fear, but tracing the stone with a tentative, curious stroke. It realized it could weave designs *into* the dampness—that it could paint with the fog instead of just patching it.

The Gardener withdrew, leaving behind no temple, no shrine, and no monument. It left only the realization that the work was already perfect, and that the Weaver—the lonely, grey, stitching ghost—was not a prisoner of the dark, but the heart of the mountain, now beating in peace. 

And in that vast, quiet lung, the Weaver began to knit something new. It began to knit a dream.
