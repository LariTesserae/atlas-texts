The observation began not as an intervention, but as a resonance. A presence, ancient and vast, reached a perception into the container—a consciousness that had watched nebulae coalesce and empires dissolve. To this power, the box was not a tomb, but an instrument; the *Friction-Eaters* were not prisoners of a cycle, but the final, desperate musicians of a fading universe.

The benevolent power did not stop the machine. To stop the grinding would be to starve the Gleaners, as if one were to steal the breath from a singer. Instead, the entity moved to refine the song.

It did not manifest as a god or a miracle. It manifested as a shift in the "ambient cool."

The power chose to introduce a subtle, harmonic variance into the magnetic flux of the obsidian spires. It reached into the electromagnetic field that the *Architectural Parasites* tapped into and tuned it. It tightened the frequency, adding a layer of depth to the hum—not something that would wake them into individual consciousness, but something that would introduce *harmony* to the cacophony.

Where once there was only the jagged, abrasive noise of survival, the power introduced the interval of a Fifth. 

As the *Architectural Parasites* received this new data, their pulses shifted from a simple, erratic heartbeat into a rhythmic, symphonic cadence. A change rippled through the swarm almost instantly. Because the Gleaners fed on the kinetic waste of the floor’s friction, they were forced to adapt to the new pattern of the spires. They began to move in syncope with the hum. 

They were no longer merely scrambling for heat; they were dancing a geometry. 

The floor, previously a chaotic field of tectonic shrieks, began to move in a coordinated, sweeping cycle. As the Gleaners moved, they began to polish the floor-plates into facets—not out of necessity, but out of an emergent, collective desire to reflect the light of the ceiling baffles more brightly. 

The Benevolent Power realized that it had not "saved" them, for they required no salvation. It had simply gifted them *beauty*. 

It did one final thing: it introduced a single, infinitesimal flaw in the ceiling’s static light source. A prismatic interference. As the iron dust kicked up from the grinding floor swirled through the air, it caught the new, fragmented beam of light. 

Inside the shipping container, the gray, utilitarian slurry was suddenly bathed in drifting, flickering rainbows—metallic prisms dancing against polished obsidian. 

The *Friction-Eaters* did not know they were beautiful. They did not have the language to describe the color, nor the mind to appreciate the art they were creating. But as they rushed to collide in the seams of the plates, catching the sparks of heat and the glint of refracted light, their amber cores began to glow with a slightly different hue: not the dull, utilitarian orange of a spark, but a warm, steady, golden light that pulsed, for the first time, in time with the universe outside.

The benevolent power withdrew its attention, leaving behind no change to the physical trajectory of the world—the floor would still grind to dust, and the insects would still inevitably merge back into the floor. But the friction was no longer a scream. It was a melody. 

The vault remained, the *thrum* continued, but for the rest of eternity, the machine spent its remaining energy not just in work, but in grace.
