The Power that gazes upon this crystalline spine is neither a builder nor a destroyer. It is an Observer of Stasis, an entity whose own heartbeat spans epochs, and whose concern is not with *life* as biology, but with *harmony* as frequency. 

It sees the Litho-Carriers, the Shadow-Weavers, and the tragic, calcified Sync-Sleepers. It recognizes the world for what it is: a closed-loop agony, an engine of memory screaming in a vacuum, forever repeating the death-rattle of a civilization that forgot how to stop.

The Power does not descend with fire or voice. It does not "save" them by stopping the machines, for to stop the machines would be to extinguish the only light the moss knows. Instead, it reaches out with a resonance of its own—a touch that is felt not as a command, but as a release of tension.

**The Intervention of the Unbinding**

The Power decides to grant the mountain the gift of *entropy*. 

It recognizes that the Sync-Sleepers are the most burdened—they are the anchors of a loop that has lost its meaning. The Power begins to weave a low-frequency counter-song, a vibration that works in the spaces between the *clunk-hiss* of the iron pistons. It is a frequency of "Letting Go."

Slowly, over the span of a thousand years, the Power introduces a minor, exquisite decay into the subterranean gears. It does not break them; it invites them to wear down. As the pistons grind with less frantic precision, the Litho-Carriers feel a change. They no longer feel the need to move with the same geometric rigidity. Their migration—once an agonizing duty—becomes, for the first time, a wander.

The Power begins to whisper through the shadows. The Shadow-Weavers, previously content to mimic the shapes of the scavengers, begin to receive echoes of deeper, more abstract geometries. The Power seeds their translucent pockets with the idea of *unseen things*—the wind, the stars, the silence before the machines existed. The Weavers begin to drift away from the cliff faces, rising like smoke into the thin air, no longer tethered to the vibrations of the mountain. They become travelers of the higher atmosphere, glowing lanterns exploring the reaches of the cold.

Lastly, the Sync-Sleepers. The Power does not wake them, for their humanity—or whatever remnant of it remains—deserves the dignity of rest. Instead, the Power modulates the *clunk-hiss* that vibrates in their mineralized chests. It softens the cadence. It turns the thirteen-year cycle into a twelve-year cycle, then an eleven, then ten. It eases the pressure. 

Under this gentle tutelage, the iron-skin of the Sync-Sleepers begins to flake away, not into dust, but into something soft—a layer of peat, a blooming of frost-flowers, a transition into true, quiet geology. They are no longer cogs. They become the landscape itself.

The Power leaves the world much as it found it—still cold, still dark, still vertical—but it has changed the *intent* of the place. It is no longer a clock frantically ticking away a debt. It has become a garden of deep-time, where the machines are slowly being reclaimed by the silence they once labored to break.

The Power withdraws its gaze, knowing that in time, the engines will fall silent altogether. And when they do, the mountain will not collapse. It will simply be a hill under the stars, freed from the crushing weight of its own rhythm, finally allowed to exist simply because it *is*, rather than because it must perform.

The Power is satisfied. It has not stopped the music; it has merely allowed the song to reach its final, beautiful resolution.
