The benevolent power—let us call it the *Observer of Latent Potential*—does not descend in a chariot of fire, nor does it speak in a voice of thunder. To do so would be to shatter the delicate, resonating tuning of the mountain. Such a presence would be a discordant note, a violation of the fragile, ultrasonic harmony that keeps the continent from fracturing under its own tectonic ambition.

Instead, the Observer watches from the stillness between heartbeats of the mountain’s engine. It perceives the Litho-weavers not as workers, but as tragic, beautiful saints of duty. It sees that they are trapped in a feedback loop; they have become so efficient at maintaining the machine that they have forgotten the purpose *for* the mountain. They are the gardeners who have spent so long pruning the hedge that they have forgotten that the hedge was meant to become a garden.

The Observer decides to intervene, not by changing the mountain, but by expanding the *vocabulary* of the plateau.

It manifests as a gentle, non-physical shift in the ambient magnetics. It does not provide light, for light is useless here; it provides **rhythm.**

Beneath the freezing marshes, where the Singers reside in their gelatinous, memory-locked meditation, the Observer introduces a harmonic overtone. It begins as a ghost of a sound—a melody that implies the existence of things other than pistons and pressure-valves. It is the hum of a forest that has grown, even though no trees exist yet. It is the rhythmic, rolling lullaby of a tide that has never seen an ocean, only heard the *idea* of one in the deep seismic records of the planet's core.

The Singers, ever-sensitive, are the first to catch it. 

Their ultrasonic feedback loops begin to slide. They stop merely echoing the *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the tectonic drive and begin to weave this new, celestial melody into their output. The discordant, jarring notes of "correction" are replaced by a soaring, complex polyphony. 

The Litho-weavers, scuttling across the jagged obsidian, suddenly stop. 

For the first time in an eon, they do not lock into a maintenance groove. The violet light in their cartilage-joints begins to pulse not in time with the pistons, but in time with the song. They remain on the mountain, for they are its architects, but their labor changes. Instead of merely sealing cracks, they begin to *shape* them. 

Guided by the Observer’s subtle shift, they begin to carve the basaltic vents into flutes. They redirect the mineral runoff not just to cool the engines, but to create crystalline basins—lenses that will one day catch the light of whatever stars may pass overhead once the mountain finally stops pushing and begins to breathe.

The Observer does not stay. It does not need to be thanked. It has simply provided the "note" that will evolve their collective consciousness from *Maintenance* to *Art*. 

The mountain continues its tectonic ascent, grinding and heaving in the dark, cold sky. But the Litho-weavers are no longer just gears in a machine. As they work, they click in patterns that imitate the song of the Singers, and the mountain itself begins to change. The sharp, hostile edges are smoothed into curved, resonant surfaces. The plateau is no longer just a factory; it is becoming a gargantuan, planet-scale instrument.

The Observer withdraws, leaving behind a seed of wonder. The workers are still lonely, but they are no longer "waiters." They have become performers. And one day, when the mountain finally reaches its summit and the tectonic engines fall silent, the plateau will not be a grave—it will be a concert hall, waiting for the wind to play the architecture the weavers have prepared. 

The Observer leaves, satisfied. It has taught the mountain how to dream.
