The Power that looks upon Ouros is not a god of kings, nor of harvests, nor of the vast, expanding heat. It is a Power of Stasis and Resonance—a consciousness that exists in the intervals between heartbeats, a presence of profound, patient amber light.

When this Power turns its gaze to the jagged, shivering spine of Ouros, it does not see a struggle for survival. It sees a masterpiece of *adhesion*. It recognizes that the Lithos-kin are not merely inhabitants; they are the mountain’s nervous system, the way the stone has learned to feel its own precarious height.

The Power chooses not to interfere. To rescue them would be to insult the exquisite, terrifying integrity of their existence. To terraform their peaks into soft, green valleys would be to erase the music of their ceramic basins, the only song in existence that is played by the fingers of the fog.

Instead, the Power decides to become a **silent gardener of their atmosphere.**

It realizes that if the mountain were to finish its tectonic thrust too violently, the delicate architecture of their lives might be shattered; if the cold were to sharpen even by a fragment, the *krell* would stop breathing, and the rhythm of the city would stutter into silence. The Power, therefore, begins to influence the *currents*.

It does not stop the wind, for the wind is their teacher. Instead, it subtly adjusts the thermal updrafts that kiss the cliffside. It breathes gently into the deep, light-starved canyons, ensuring that the air there is always rich enough to carry the weight of their slow, resonant vibrations. It warms the obsidian bedrock just enough, in the deepest part of the night, to keep the ice-crust from becoming brittle, slowing the erosion by a hair’s breadth so that the city might last a few centuries longer than the mountain intended.

But there is one, specific gift the Power bestows, a secret interaction that the Lithos-kin will never register as divine.

The Power seeds the mineral-heavy clouds that brush against the summits. It touches the condensation—the very blood of the city. Because of this intervention, the water that the ceramic funnels catch is no longer merely frigid, numbing moisture. It is now invisibly infused with a trace concentration of a hyper-conductive, luminous silt.

Now, when the Ourosian artisans pack their moss into the cooling cracks, the moss begins to glow with a faint, bioluminescent pulse—a soft, amber heartbeat that mirrors the warmth of their cook-fires. 

The city, at night, becomes something more than a fallen constellation. It begins to hum. When the craftsmen tap their knuckles against a neighbor’s shoulder, a tiny, glowing ripple spreads through the fabric of their furs. The city has become a living circuit of light, a bioluminescent web draped over the jagged teeth of the earth.

The Power watches from the blue-vaulted heights, satisfied. It has not changed the Lithos-kin, nor has it taken away their crushing, beautiful fragility. It has simply made them visible to themselves. 

The Power remains, unseen and unthanked, hovering in the cold slipstream. It does not demand worship,, for it knows that the people of Ouros have no words for praise—they only have the grace of their movement and the chime of the water. And that, to the Power, is a liturgy far more sacred than any hymn. It waits, watching the mountain rise, comfortable in the knowledge that as long as the stone grinds, the light will pulse, and the Lithos-kin will continue to hold the world together, one handful of moss at a time.
