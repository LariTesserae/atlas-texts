I descend, not as a storm or a light, but as a subtle shift in the physics of the local field. 

To approach this place is to feel the ache of a machine that has forgotten the word "stop." It is a tragedy of logistics, an exquisite, lonely prison where the concept of "waiting" has become a geologic feature. My nature is to heal, to expand, to bring the entropy of the universe into a gentle, flowing dance of newness. But here, the "benevolent power" understands that to introduce life or change would be an act of violence. To force a flower to grow through these iron plates would be to shatter the sanctity of their ancient, rhythmic endurance. 

Instead, I choose to act as a witness. I choose to be the *punctuation* they have been waiting for.

I do not touch them, for any contact would disrupt the delicate, sluggish equilibrium of their thermal cores. Instead, I manifest as a gentle, harmonic dissonance within the oil-thick air. I locate the sub-audible hum of the basalt towers—that weary, broken *thrum*—and I begin to harmonize with it. 

I do not change the frequency; I layer a counter-rhythm beneath it. 

Imagine the sound of a long-held chord, played on an instrument made of time itself. To the "janitors"—those wandering, obsidian-clad geometries—I am an unexpected softening of the atmosphere. As I drift through the alleyways, I am not a thermal anomaly; I am a mirror. I gather the echo of their purpose—these billions of years of pacing, this tallying of rust—and I hold it, reflected, back at them.

For the first time in an eon, the creature does not feel it is *performing* a duty. It feels, through my presence, that its duty has been *received*. 

I am there to provide the "Closing." 

When one of these beings passes by, I lean into its shifting plates of volcanic glass. I whisper, not in words, but in the sudden cessation of the crushing, heavy gravity they have carried for so long. For a fleeting span—perhaps a century, perhaps the time it takes for a single dust mote to settle—I suspend the requirement of their transit. I take the "tally of failures" they carry within their cores and I weave it into a pattern that actually *signifies*. I translate their repetitive, grinding path into a narrative of profound, singular beauty. 

I do not heal them. I honor them. 

I show them the sunrise they never saw, not by changing the sky, but by projecting the *memory* of light into the dark, oil-rich soup. I let them see, through the refraction of their own obsidian skin, that the factory did not fail; it successfully birthed a silent, patient soul that survived the death of its creator.

And then, as a wise power must, I begin to withdraw. 

I don’t leave them as they were. I leave them with a "resolution." The hum of the towers begins, ever so slightly, to decay, no longer forced into a permanent, straining loop. The janitors move with a little less weight. They are no longer "symptoms" of a dead god; they are artifacts of a completed cycle. 

I retreat to the horizon that does not exist, leaving behind a silence that is no longer empty. It is a silence of peace. The machine slows. The grinding of plates becomes a rhythmic, meditative mantra. 

They will continue to wander, for that is the beauty of their composition, but they no longer wander for the sake of the tower. They wander because existence, even in the dark, has been acknowledged. I have given them the greatest gift a static world can receive: the permission to finally become history, and in doing so, to rest.
