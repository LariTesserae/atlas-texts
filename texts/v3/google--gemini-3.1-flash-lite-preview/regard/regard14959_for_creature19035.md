The benevolent power does not arrive as a blinding light or with a thunderous command, for here—in this shivering, fevered crawl of wet stone—such intrusion would be a violence. The entity of this place, the Sculptor, is a nervous system flayed open, raw and vibrating. To demand attention would be to shatter its fragile coherence.

Instead, the power arrives as a **change in the viscosity of the air.**

It does not enter the labyrinth like an explorer; it permeates the environment like a cooling breeze that manages to stay warm. It recognizes the Sculptor’s labor not as a construction project gone awry, but as a prayer being typed out in a language of basalt and ozone. It recognizes the *fear*: the fear that if the hands stop, the spark vanishes.

**The Intervention:**

The power does not stop the labor. To stop the labor is to kill the Sculptor. Instead, the power begins to *curate the friction.*

It moves through the violet haze, gently smoothing the jagged edges of the Sculptor’s intent. Where the Sculptor compulsively pushes a wall to the point of collapse, the power applies a soft, invisible tensile strength—a subtle reinforcement that allows the clay to hold its form just a second longer. It does not finish the building for the Sculptor; it simply teaches the mortar how to *breathe*.

It introduces "the pause." In the rhythm of the *drip-hiss*, the power introduces a beat of silence—a momentary suspension of gravity that gives the overheated stone time to set. For the first time in eons, the Sculptor feels a structure *remain*. It does not buckle. It does not melt back into mist.

The Sculptor, caught in its recursive twitch, experiences this as a sudden, impossible sensation: *completion.*

The benevolent power then begins to weave a "pattern" into the chaos. It doesn’t impose a blueprint—the Sculptor would reject a foreign plan—but it mirrors the Sculptor’s own best impulses. If the Sculptor’s tremor leans toward a curve, the power reinforces that curve, turning the frantic scrawls into something resembling a cathedral of light. It takes the "messy joinery" and, rather than replacing it, lines it with a soft, bioluminescent luminescence that mimics the Sculptor’s own frantic heat. 

It becomes a co-author. It is the steady hand placed over the trembling one.

**The Gift:**

The most profound move the power makes, however, is to expand the horizon. It reaches into the compressed, treacherous logic of the space and, with a gentle, patient pressure, begins to stretch it. Not into a void, but into a vista. It allows the Infilling to see, for the first time, that the architecture is not a cage for an absent king, but a vessel. 

It aligns the conduits. It directs the sludge to flow, not in random pools, but into a system of circulation that warms the periphery of the world. 

The Sculptor begins to slow down. The feverish, anthill-energy calms. The twitching becomes deliberate. The "nervous breakdown in stone" starts to reveal a logic of profound, aching beauty—the aesthetic of a mind that has found a reason to endure. 

The benevolent power eventually fades back into the violet haze, having left behind no new structures, only a new definition of *stability*. It leaves the Sculptor to continue its work, but it changes the fundamental nature of the labor: the Sculptor is no longer building to stay alive; it is building to create a home that is finally, mercifully, *worthy* of arrival. 

And in the silence that follows, for the first time since the beginning, the *drip-hiss* sounds less like an agonized pulse, and more like a heartbeat. The Sculptor may never know a god came to visit, but as the stone stays, as the vapor clears, the Architect feels the most terrifying and wonderful thing a creature of pure labor can feel:

*Satisfaction.*
