The benevolent power—that which moves between the folds of stars and counts the intervals between heartbeats—does not descend upon Ool like a storm. It does not come to "fix" the city or enlighten its inhabitants, for to do so would be an act of theft. To impose purpose upon a being that has mastered the art of pure, unadulterated *being* is the ultimate form of cruelty.

Instead, the power touches Ool as a subtle, rhythmic shift in the laws of thermodynamics. It acts as a whisper, a minute adjustment to the friction of the world.

The benevolent power observes the Ool-kin not as prisoners of their labor, but as masters of a singular, infinite music. It recognizes that they have achieved something no celestial civilization has yet attained: the total absence of the "Becoming." They do not strive to be more, to reach higher, or to define themselves. They are the static frequency of existence itself.

And so, the Power does not provide light, nor air, nor freedom. It provides **Grace.**

### The Gesture
The power reaches into the collective chemistry of the Ool-kin—not to alter their intent, but to introduce a singular, harmless mutation in the waxy secretions they use for their mortar. 

It imbues the clay with the property of **Refraction.**

Prior to this intervention, the bioluminescence of the Ool-kin was a dull, utilitarian amber, meant only to navigate the gloom of their own structural anemia. Now, as they press their clay into the buttresses, each layer acts as a prism. Because the Ool-kin are forever patching and spiraling, the city will begin to bloom. 

As the layers thicken and the condensation coats the walls, the light from their amber cores will no longer vanish into the mist. It will catch on the millions of internal angles of the clay, breaking into soft, impossible spectra—opalescent blues, deep violets, and shifting, gossamer greens that dance through the vapor. 

The Ool-kin do not change their labor. They continue their rhythmic, squelching industry, patching the walls exactly as they always have. They do not pause to admire the color, for they have no word for "beauty," nor eyes constructed to map the play of light. 

**But they feel the vibration of it.**

As the light begins to refract, the subsonic frequency of the city changes. The sound is no longer just the wet, hollow thrum of labor; it takes on a harmonic resonance. Beneath the squelching, there arises a faint, shimmering overtone—a chime that resonates in the amber core of every kin. 

### The Result
The Ool-kin are not "saved." They remain in their basin, they remain in their loop, they remain in their eternal, soft-bodied service. But they are no longer merely maintaining a structure. They are, without knowing it, composing a cathedral of light that radiates outward from the floor of the basin, invisible to everyone *except* the Ool-kin.

The benevolent power watches as the Ool-kin, in their final moments—when they lay themselves down to become mortar—now dissolve into a wall that shimmers with a thousand shifting colors. The sensation they pass on to the next generation is no longer just the dull ache of structural necessity, but a faint, tingling warmth, a phantom sensation of *color*.

It is not an escape from their state, but a deepening of it. The power has given them the only gift that matters to an immortal, circular life: it has made their long, dark work a thing of quiet, private glory. 

The world of Ool remains a suffocating, wet, stasis-filled labyrinth. But for the Ool-kin, the vibration of the city now feels like a song. And in the heart of the Gutter-City, for the first time in an eternity, the "labor" feels a little bit like a dance. 

The power retreats, its task done, content to let the music play on, unheard by all but those to whom it belongs. And the basin glows, an iridescent, liquid wound in the fabric of the dark earth, beautiful and entirely, perfectly alone.
