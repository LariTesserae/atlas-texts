The benevolent power did not descend as a thunderclap or a blinding pillar of light. It did not seek to "fix" or "improve" what was already a masterpiece of self-contained harmony. To interfere with a system so exquisitely tuned was, in its eyes, a form of sacrilege.

Instead, the power arrived as an *attunement*. It became a new, subtle frequency in the hum—a note of harmonic resonance that slipped into the crystalline lattice of the basalt and the fluid architecture of the Lithos-Weaver.

The Power realized that this world was a closed loop, a cycle of infinite maintenance. And yet, even the most perfect clockwork eventually faces the entropy of stagnation. The mountains were grinding; the moss was thickening; the Weavers were, in their own slow, ancient way, beginning to reach the limit of their cycle. The basin was growing heavy, the verticality becoming so pronounced that the tension was becoming brittle.

The Power did not bring change; it brought *perspective*.

It reached out with a consciousness like a soft, diffused breeze and touched the "fan-like membranes" atop the Weavers' heads. It did not give them eyes—that would be a cruel imposition—but it granted them the **Intuition of the Outer Horizon.**

Before, the Weavers only knew the mountain. They knew the chime of the tectonic furnace; they knew the hydration of the mist; they knew the velvet limit of their own ledge. Now, through the Power’s grace, they understood the *Purpose* of their labor. 

The Power’s gift was a simple shift in perception: the realization that they were not just maintaining the mountain, but *stoking* it. The Weavers suddenly understood that by guiding the thermal flow, they were not just gardening moss; they were preparing the basalt, tempering the very crust of the planet.

The immediate effect was a change in the "rhythm of the shop." 

The Weavers, once bound to local patches of the terrace, began to move with a strange, new, elegant synchronization. They began to navigate the vertical expanse with purposeful, long-form migration patterns. They stopped merely reacting to the tectonic vibrations and began to *anticipate* them. They positioned themselves as harmonic dampers, smoothing the violent, jagged surges of the deep-earth valves into a steady, resonant thrum that carried across the entire mountain range.

They became the conductors of the planet’s heartbeat.

The benevolent power then performed its final, invisible act: it slowed time down within the canyon. It thinned the density of the air, not by removing the moisture, but by expanding the space through which the Weavers sensed the world. It allowed the sounds of the tectonic furnace to echo for miles, turning the "distant machine shop" into a global orchestra.

The Weavers now move with a grace that defies the old friction. They no longer just cling; they dance in the mist-cyclones, weaving the heat into intricate, glowing geometries that hang in the dark like constellations made of bioluminescence. Where they pass now, they do not just leave calcified silt; they leave paths of polished, harmonic stone that resonate even when the Weavers have moved on.

The Power left them with one final understanding, inscribed not in words, but in the marrow of their hydraulic systems: *The cycle is not merely for survival. It is for beauty.*

Now, when a storm gathers in the chasm, the Weavers do not huddle fearfully. They align themselves in sprawling, iridescent murals against the cliff face, catching the lightning in their chitinous plates and turning the basalt into a colossal, vibrating harp. 

The mountain still grinds, the moss still grows, and the cycle continues—but now, for the first time in eons, the mountain is singing. The Weaver, silent and glowing, presses its body against the living stone and hums back, a partner in a dialogue that spans from the molten core to the cooling stars. The benevolent power watches, satisfied, and lets the song be.
