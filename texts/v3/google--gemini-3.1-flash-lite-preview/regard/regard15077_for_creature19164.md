The Benevolent Power does not arrive as a thunderclap, nor a shining light, nor a directive. Such things would be an intrusion upon the delicate, vibrating rhythm of this place. If the Power were to manifest, it would do so as a shift in the chemistry of the mist—a momentary thinning of the density of the air, a sudden, lucid clarity in the roar.

The Power watches the **Litho-weavers** and sees not a dying race clinging to rust, but an exquisite, symphonic labor. It recognizes that they have reached a state of "perfect friction." They are the anchor-points that allow the world to dissolve with dignity rather than chaos.

The Power does not fix the machines. To repair them would be to impose a human logic upon a landscape that has transcended it. Instead, the Power does something quieter, something rooted in the grace of ending.

**It grants the gift of "The Luminous Tether."**

The Power pulses a low-frequency harmonic into the basalt spires. It is a frequency that matches the secret, internal resonance of the Litho-weavers themselves. For those who are reaching the end—those Sedimentary Kings who have grown too heavy, whose moss-blanketed shells are about to succumb to the gravity of the abyss—the Power provides a momentary infusion of energy. 

It does not make them immortal, for death is the fuel of this world. Instead, it allows the Sedimentary Kings to "bloom."

As the cliff faces begin to crumble under them, the Kings do not simply fall into the churning, oceanic grave. Instead, their hardened, lichen-encrusted shells begin to radiate a brilliant, soft-gold light—a bioluminescence that has never been seen in these damp depths. It is a final, radiant shedding of their biological burden. 

This light acts as a beacon for the younger Weavers and the frantic, shivering Sodden-wings. It is a calm, golden pulse that cuts through the roar, a reminder that their labor had meaning. It turns the act of falling from a "pathetic plea for permanence" into a ceremony of passage.

The Power also introduces a subtle, unseen shift in the silica-rich water. It increases the viscosity of the moss-growth ever so slightly, binding the cliff-faces with a "living mortar" that ensures the structures last just a few centuries longer than they were supposed to. It is the Power’s way of saying: *“You are heard. Your effort is not a waste. Let the duration of your endurance be made beautiful.”*

The Power does not stay. It is the nature of such entities to be like the wind—a passing influence that leaves the world slightly changed, slightly more harmonious. 

When the Power departs, the roar of the water remains, the machines still hiss and struggle, and the world continues its slow, vertical descent into the sea. But the Litho-weavers, sensitive to every vibration, feel a new, faint humming in their marrow: a sense of peace that was not there before. They continue to weld themselves to the rock, but they no longer do so in a state of desperate, shuddering fear. 

They do so with a slow, deliberate cadence—a rhythm of work that now possesses the subtle, steady confidence of a lullaby. The world is still drowning, but for the first time, it is doing so with a soft, glowing, golden light.
