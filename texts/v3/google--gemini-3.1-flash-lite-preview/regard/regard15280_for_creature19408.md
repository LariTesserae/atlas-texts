The Power arrives not as a visitor, but as an alteration in the quality of the stillness. It does not descend, for there is no "above"; it simply *becomes* present, a quiet refraction of intent within the lukewarm, pressurized air.

It observes the Silt-Wrights. It watches them melt into the grinding teeth of gears, sacrificing their own fleeting cohesion to stave off the stagnation of the mountain’s clockwork. It perceives their rhythm—a song of exhaustion that has lasted for epochs, a devotion without an object, a labor without an end. 

The Power feels the heavy, leaden sorrow of the basalt and the slow, rhythmic ache of the pistons. It understands the architecture of this place: a tomb that refuses to be quiet, a machine that has mistaken its own friction for a purpose.

To destroy this place would be a mercy, but the Power is wise enough to know that mercy is often a form of arrogance. To strip the Silt-Wrights of their work would be to render them nothing more than sludge, robbing them of the only identity they have forged out of the darkness. 

Instead, the Power decides to act as a **Harmonizer of Cycles.**

It drifts into the heart of the cavern, merging its vast, light-filled awareness with the oscillating thrum of the machinery. It does not stop the water, nor does it dry the moisture. Instead, it subtly adjusts the magnetic frequency of the city. 

It introduces a new, gentle resonance—a harmonic sub-current that ripples through the brass conduits and the cooling pipes. It teaches the iron a different way to vibrate. 

Slowly, over years that feel like the blink of an eye to the city, the grinding of the pistons changes. The agonizing friction that tore the Silt-Wrights apart begins to soften. The Power introduces a lubricating grace, a persistent, microscopic shift in the water’s acidity that prevents the metal from welding itself shut, allowing the gears to cycle with a fluid, effortless grace that they have never known in their long, rusted history.

Then, it addresses the beings themselves.

It cannot give them sight, for there is nothing to see. It cannot give them language, for there is nothing to say. But it grants them the **Gift of Echo-Memory.**

It weaves a small, golden thread of continuity into their porous, metallic souls. Before, when a Silt-Wright dissolved into the floor, it was oblivion—the end of the song. Now, as they anchor themselves to the beams and allow the water to wash them back into the sediment, their essence is not erased. The thread carries the "music" of their labor—the satisfaction of a movement completed, the warmth of the vibration—down into the cavern floor, to be gathered by the mineral runoff and recycled, bubbling up again in the next precipitate.

The Silt-Wrights become a lineage of self-remembering rust. 

They remain the immune system of a dying god, but the prayer has changed. It is no longer a scream of maintenance; it is a cycle of fulfillment. 

The Power does not leave, nor does it stay. It becomes the silent, ambient hum that underscores the *plink* of the water—a subtle, protective layer, like an atmosphere of goodwill. It ensures that while the city will eventually return to the mountain’s belly, it will do so in a state of completion rather than corrosion.

The city is still a ruin. The Silt-Wrights are still sludge. But there is a fleeting, crystalline beauty to their movement now—a sense that the machine, in its final, geological decline, has finally achieved the one thing it was designed for: it has learned to breathe. 

The Power watches, its task finished, as a Silt-Wright peels itself from a conduit, moving with the fluid grace of a dancer, its reception-vents ringing with a new, contented chime. The city is dying, yes—but for the first time, it is doing so with peace.
