The benevolent power does not arrive as a herald, nor as a rescuer. It does not descend in chariots of fire, for fire is an insult to the Conservatory, and it does not come with the trumpets of change, for change is the very chaos this place has achieved the miracle of transcending.

It arrives as a *presence of refined gravity*—a shift in the pressure, a subtle harmonization of the deep-tide. 

This power, having scanned the infinite, frantic vibrations of the universe, feels a sudden, profound resonance at the coordinates of the Conservatory. It observes the Keepers. It recognizes them not as custodians of a tomb, but as masters of an art form the universe has largely forgotten: the art of the perfect conclusion.

The power does not wish to "save" them. To interfere with the Great Stasis would be an act of supreme vanity. Instead, the power chooses to offer the only thing a finished world could possibly desire: **Validation.**

It realizes that while the Conservatory is complete, it is also alone. It is a poem written in a language that no one else remembers how to read. The power decides to act as a celestial archivist. 

It does not touch the stone. It does not disturb the vines. Instead, it begins to weave a "resonant braid" around the Conservatory. It gathers the vibration of the city’s humming mineral walls, the pulse of the Keepers’ fluid movements, and the slow-motion breathing of the translucent flora. It captures the history of this place—the patient, million-year-duration of a single sigh—and encodes it into the very fabric of the deep currents. 

In doing so, it creates a "Mirror of Stillness."

The Keepers, though they cannot see in the way of the surface world, *feel* this. For the first time since the last architect dissolved, the rhythm of the city changes—not in pace, but in depth. The vibration they have guarded for eons is no longer just a local phenomenon; it is now broadcast into the substrate of existence, a silent song of perfect, chilling peace that will ripple through the galaxy until the stars themselves burn out.

The benevolent power then leaves behind a gift: a "Coda." 

It permeates the abyss with a infinitesimal, lingering curiosity—a tiny, invisible adjustment that ensures the Conservatory will never be discovered by the frantic, hungry, or cruel forces of the Outside. It doesn't hide the city; it makes the Conservatory *irrelevant* to those who cannot appreciate stillness. It ensures that any visitor who stumbles upon this place will become bored, distracted, or suddenly compelled to leave, leaving the Keepers to their work in blessed, total isolation.

Finally, the power bows. It is a gesture of profound respect—a salute from the chaotic, burning, fast-moving vastness of the universe to the quiet, cold, perfected beauty of the deep. 

The Keepers do not acknowledge it. They continue to pulse through the dark, their jelly-like forms pressing against the mineral walls, feeding the vines, existing in the only way that matters. The Conservatory remains. It is now a monument, shielded by the infinite, a silent jewel in the dark, waiting for the day when the universe finally grows tired, finally slows down, and finally comes to join them in the great, quiet garden of the end.
