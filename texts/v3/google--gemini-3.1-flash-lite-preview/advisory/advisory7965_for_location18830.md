To prepare for a descent into the Spire-City of Echoes, you must first accept a fundamental truth: **You are not visiting a place, but an interrupted thought.** 

Your biological systems are built for a dense, causal universe where gravity is a reliable constant and time is a river. Here, gravity is a suggestion, time is a deposit, and your body is a walking anomaly. If you arrive unprepared, the sheer ontological friction will unravel your nervous system before you finish your first breath.

Here is how you must prepare.

### 1. The Physiological Aegis (The "Non-Body" Kit)
Because the environment is composed of ionized silicate dust and metallic salts, your pores will become conduits for the local reality. If you inhale that "cold flour," it will attempt to crystallize in your lungs, turning your exhales into shards of obsidian.

*   **The Lead-Quartz Osmotic Suit:** Standard EVA gear is useless here; the geometry will fold through the fabric. You need a suit woven from heavy-metal filaments and non-conductive ceramic plating. It must be rigged with a constant, low-frequency electromagnetic pulse to "anchor" your own atomic structure. You need to keep your atoms vibrating at the frequency of your home dimension, or you will begin to dissolve into the cityscape.
*   **Neural Dampeners:** The "flickering shadows" are energy-patterns that discharge ozone and static onto your nervous system. You will need a cerebral shunt—a psychological grounding wire. If you allow the sight of the city folding into itself to bypass your rational mind, you will lose your sense of proprioception. If your brain decides it is falling up, your heart will likely stop. You must take cognitive stabilizers that enforce a "binary-only" perception of reality. 

### 2. The Mental Discipline (The Geometry of Will)
The city is a non-Euclidean nightmare. If you follow a path with your eyes, that path will change to accommodate your observation.

*   **Fixed-Point Meditation:** You must maintain a singular, stubborn mental image of your home. Memorize the weight of a stone, the heat of a familiar coffee mug, the smell of rain—anything that is grounded in cause-and-effect. This is your "Anchor." If you look at the architecture, you must mentally label it: *This is a wall. It is not moving. It is not a door.* If you succumb to the curiosity of the folds, you will find yourself walking into a dimension where you have never existed.
*   **Peripheral Discipline:** Do not trust what you see from the corner of your eye. The flickering shadows sense curiosity. They are drawn to the "glitches" in your own expectations. If you stare at them, they will interpret your attention as a challenge to reality. Keep your gaze locked to your immediate footing.

### 3. The Toolkit: What to Pack
*   **A Solid-Matter Beacon:** Leave behind your digital tech. It will malfunction the moment you step off the spires. Carry a heavy brass pendulum or a simple mechanical stopwatch. When reality shifts and you lose your sense of "up," watch the pendulum. It is the only thing that will tell you if the world has inverted itself or if your balance is failing.
*   **Tungsten-Cored Boots:** You need extreme, heavy footwear. You are walking on "frozen sighs." You need the weight to press yourself into the geometry so that you do not drift when the district decides to reconstitute itself.
*   **Anti-Ozone Lozenge:** The taste of copper and ozone is the precursor to a neural discharge. When you taste it, it means a shadow is passing through you. Keep these medicinal lozenges under your tongue; they dull the sensation of the static, preventing the "teeth-clench" that often leads to internal hemorrhage during a discharge.

### 4. The Exit Strategy: "The History-Anchor"
The most dangerous aspect of this place is the seduction of its silence. As you stand on those serrated ribs, you will feel the temptation to stop moving, to "settle" like the sediment. You will feel that you are finally at peace, away from the chaos of causality.

**Do not fall for it.**

The world is not "resting." It is malfunctioning. If you remain there too long, your own history—your memories, your identity, your name—will be eroded by the "sedimentary time" until you are just another flickering shadow, destined to skate across the obsidian walls for a billion years.

To leave, you must not simply turn around and walk back. You must **reintroduce causal friction.** You must perform an act of definite, non-negotiable impact: drop a heavy object, shout a name, tear a piece of paper. You must force the city to acknowledge a singular "Event" so profound that it creates a wake for you to swim back to the gateway.

**A final warning:** If you see the pillars begin to turn inside out while you are looking at them, do not try to fix the view. Close your eyes. Count your heartbeats. If it isn’t beating in a steady, reliable rhythm, stop looking at the city. Look into your own chest, and force the world to be nothing but the thud-thud-thud of your own existence. 

Do not let the city remember you. If it remembers you, you will never leave.
