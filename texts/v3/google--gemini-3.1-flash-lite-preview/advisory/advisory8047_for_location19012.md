To step foot into the *Slag-Garden of the High-Plains* is to vacate the role of an observer and become a component in an inscrutable, failing machine. You are not visiting a wilderness in the traditional sense; you are entering the guts of an engine that has forgotten how to stop.

If you are determined to traverse this weeping, metallic purgatory, your preparation must be absolute. Ordinary gear will fail within minutes—the atmosphere is corrosive, the electricity is predatory, and the gravity is fickle. Here is how you prepare for the Slag-Garden.

### 1. The Physiological Aegis: Your Suit
You cannot breathe this soup. The air is too dense, and the acid-tainted moisture will melt the lining of your lungs before you can process the *thrum-wail*. 
*   **Insulation against Conductivity:** Because the air acts as a massive circuit board, you must wear a fully enclosed, non-conductive, Faraday-caged environment suit. If your suit has any exposed metallic fasteners or zippers, the ambient electricity in the air *will* find them, arc into them, and track along your skin. Seal all seams with ceramic-composite tape.
*   **Acoustic Damping:** The *thrum-wail* is not just noise; it is physical force. Without high-density acoustic dampening, the constant vibration will cause internal hemorrhaging and organ fatigue within hours. Equip an active cancellation rig that recalibrates to match the frequency of the "weeping rocks." 
*   **The Weight-Anchor:** Because gravity flexes according to the magnetic ley lines of the buried machinery, you risk being pulled upward into a pool of suspended, floating water or pinned to the peat by a random surge of local density. Wear a weighted harness that can magnetically lock to the basaltic plateaus. If you lose your footing, you don't just fall; you might drift until you suffocate in a cloud of stagnant vapor.

### 2. Defensive Measures: Avoiding "The Tap"
The organisms here are hungry for charge. If your suit glows with artificial light, you will attract the steel-plated scavengers like a beacon.
*   **Passive Illumination:** Do not bring electric headlamps. They are food for the landscape. Use chemical light-sticks or bioluminescent patches harvested from non-aggressive flora. 
*   **Signal Masking:** You must bleed off your own body heat and bio-electricity. Wrap your suit in a heat-sink blanket to hide your thermal signature from the crab-like scavengers. They read the electrical discharge of your nervous system as a prey signal.
*   **Gait and Rhythm:** Do not walk with a heavy, rhythmic stomp. The Slag-Garden responds to vibration. Move with a sliding, erratic gait, mimicking the shifting of the "fern-machines." You must blend into the background noise of the landscape.

### 3. Navigation: The Pylon Geometry
The terrain is a labyrinth that changes as the moisture-levels shift and the giant gears beneath the soil turn. 
*   **Ignore Traditional Compasses:** The magnetic pull of the buried veins will spin your needle into a blur. You must navigate instead by the "Markers." Treat the isolated pylons of black glass and copper as your primary navigation points. 
*   **The "Silent" Buffer:** If you see steam venting from a pylon, do not approach. That is "coolant exhaust." It is scalding, toxic, and pressurized enough to flay skin from bone. Keep a "two-marker visibility" distance from any structure that is still actively humming. 
*   **Water Management:** Never, under any circumstances, drink the water or attempt to filter it. The "suspended liquid lanterns" are highly volatile, saturated with acidic lubricants and charged isotopes. If you must replenish, use synthetic, recycled moisture from your suit’s closed-loop system.

### 4. Psychological Preparation: The "Engine-Mind"
The Slag-Garden will slowly erode your sense of causality. You are used to a world where plants grow toward the sun and animals seek food; here, the "growth" is mechanical, and the "biology" is secondary. 
*   **Expect the Wrongness:** You will see things that defy your logic: a vine that clicks like a binary switch, a pool of water that refuses to behave like liquid, or a sound that registers as a physical blow to your chest. Do not try to analyze the biology. If you focus too hard on identifying patterns in the "weeping rock," you will succumb to the *Clockwork-Vertigo*—a state of sensory overload where the brain begins to believe it is also a machine.
*   **The "Thrum" Threshold:** You must maintain a mental "keep-out" zone. The constant hum of the pylons can induce a hypnotic state. If you find yourself staring too long at the iridescent kelps or attempting to match your breathing to the static thunder, you are already lost. 

### Final Gear Checklist:
1.  **Lead-lined boots** (for grounding against stray arcs).
2.  **Ceramic-bladed tool** (metal blades may bridge the gap between two charged surfaces, resulting in a localized explosion).
3.  **Vibration-dampened goggles** (the air is so thick that visual distortion is constant; keep the lenses clean of the greasy, industrial silt).
4.  **A hard-copy, non-electronic map:** The electronic interference will render a digital interface useless within your first kilometer.

**A Warning:** You are not an adventurer here. You are a ghost passing through a factory that hasn't realized the bosses are dead. Keep your head low, your frequency mismatched, and never, ever touch the weeping rocks. They are still registering input. If you touch them, you become part of the calculation.
