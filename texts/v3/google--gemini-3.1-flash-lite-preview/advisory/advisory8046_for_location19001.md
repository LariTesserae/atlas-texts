To visit Orem-Vael is to step into a machine that has forgotten its original purpose but continues to churn out duty. For an ordinary human, this is not an expedition; it is an immersion into a state of physical and temporal density that the human body is ill-equipped to process. 

You are a soft, ephemeral thing in a world of hard, eternal cycles. To survive and, more importantly, to observe without becoming a disruption to the city’s metabolism, you must prepare with the precision of a clockmaker.

### 1. Physical Protection: The "Shell"
The humidity of Orem-Vael is not water; it is a mineral-rich, conductive aerosol. It will penetrate your clothes, your pores, and eventually, the internal mechanisms of any gear you bring.
*   **The Exoskeleton-Suit:** Do not wear breathable fabrics. You need a pressurized, non-porous environmental suit with a closed-loop filtration system. The constant "weeping" of the mountain is chemically caustic to human lungs and skin. Over that, wear a heavy-duty, oil-slick-resistant overcoat—think deep-sea gear, but articulated. 
*   **Weighted Footwear:** You are walking on slick, vibration-heavy girders. Your boots must have magnetized, non-slip magnetic pads. The internal rhythm of the city transmits through the metal; if your feet are not weighted and properly damped, the constant vibration of the floor-grates will cause significant joint fatigue within hours.
*   **Ocular Shielding:** The light in Orem-Vael is perpetually bruised and diffused. Wear high-contrast, amber-tinted polarized goggles. The constant movement of the chitinous maintenance workers can induce vertigo; the tint will help stabilize your vision against the glare of the wet, reflective basalt and oxidized copper.

### 2. Biological Maintenance: The "Slower Self"
Orem-Vael does not recognize human metabolism. Everything here moves in increments of years and decades. If you walk with the nervous, frantic energy of a human, you will stick out like a frantic spark in a dark room.
*   **Pharmacological Tempo-Regulation:** Consult a multiverse chirurgeon before departure. You will need mild, long-acting sedatives or rhythm-stabilizers. You need your pulse to settle, your breathing to match the slow, deep oscillation of the subterranean bellows. If you move too fast, the larger, house-sized fauna may perceive you as a "fault"—an erratic vibration—and attempt to "mend" or "dismantle" you.
*   **Hydration/Nutrition:** Do not attempt to eat or drink local resources. The mineral content of the mist is non-nutritive and potentially toxic to human biology. Bring sterile, high-density calorie paste. Eating must be done in a pressurized airlock or inside your suit; even an open mouth could introduce a localized spore or mineral shard into your system.

### 3. Diplomatic Etiquette: Becoming Infrastructure
You are not a tourist; you are a piece of static debris currently occupying a high-traffic area. 
*   **The Law of Conservation:** Do not touch the machinery. Do not polish the gauges. Do not attempt to help the fauna. If a chitinous mender is working on a pressure valve, it has been doing so for longer than your lineage has existed. Touching their work is a breach of the city’s slow-motion peace. 
*   **Avoid the "Maintenance Flows":** The massive, house-sized creatures with hydraulic legs follow fixed, rhythmic paths. Learn the drum-beat of the city. When the vibration of the floor changes a half-tone deeper, a massive entity is approaching. Do not run. Stop, press yourself against the nearest pillar of basalt, and become part of the background. Let the "house" step over you. Breathe slowly. It does not see you; it sees only the geography of the work.

### 4. Psychological Preparation: The Weight of Infinite Time
The hardest part of your visit will not be the mist or the moisture, but the crushing sense of industrial permanence. 
*   **Ego-Damping:** You will feel small. You will feel fleeting. You will realize that if you were to die here, your body would likely be processed by the swarm-creatures, repurposed into gaskets or bracing, and integrated into the wall. This is not a horror; this is the city’s way of ensuring nothing is wasted. Accept your mortality as the only "soft" element in a hardware-dominant universe.
*   **The Sound:** Carry high-grade active noise-canceling earplugs, but be prepared to remove them for short intervals. There is a specific musicality to the bellows. If you listen closely, you might hear the rhythm of the planet’s own geothermal pulse. It is the most honest sound a traveler can hear: the slow, patient heartbeat of a world that does not need a future to justify its existence.

### Pre-Departure Checklist:
*   **Lubricant:** Carry a small canister of inert, high-viscosity synthetic grease for your suit’s hinges. The mist will try to seize them. 
*   **Beacon:** A high-frequency sub-space tether. If the local fauna marks you as "obsolete," you need a way to be extracted instantly. 
*   **A Notebook, not a Camera:** Cameras struggle with the Orem-Vael sheen. The captured images will be distorted by the ionic haze. Use a physical journal. Write in slow, deliberate sentences. 

Remember: In Orem-Vael, stillness is not just a virtue; it is a survival strategy. To exist here, you must cease to be a visitor and start to be a fixture. When the mist finally settles on your shoulders, and you find yourself pacing your breath to the sound of a steam-valve five miles away, you will know you have arrived.
