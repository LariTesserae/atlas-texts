To visit The Weeping Heights is to step into the lung of a world that breathes on a geological timescale. It is not a place for the energetic; it is a landscape of absolute, suffocating patience. If you intend to survive, let alone observe, you must shed the human urge to "conquer" or "navigate" a space and instead adopt the posture of a guest who might dissolve before the sun sets.

Here is your guide to preparation.

### 1. The Physiological Adjustment: Managing the "Leach"
The moisture in The Weeping Heights is not just weather; it is a physical force. Because the air is thin and hypersaturated, your own body will struggle with the *osmotic gradient*. You are full of salt and heat; the environment is wet and cold. You are essentially a high-pressure system meeting a low-pressure, porous void.

*   **Seal Your Skin:** Do not wear conventional hiking gear. Synthetic fabrics will trap moisture against your skin, chilling your core. You need a suit of non-porous, hydrophobic elastomer. Think of a diver’s dry-suit, but one that is completely seamless. Any gap—at the wrist, the neck, or the ankle—will allow the mineral-heavy "gel" of this world to seep internal and irritate your skin.
*   **Respiratory Filtration:** The thin air is the greatest danger. The humidity is so heavy it can lead to "drowning" if you aren't careful. You need a rebreather that warms and dehydrates the air slightly before it enters your lungs. If you breathe the raw atmosphere for too long, you will experience a rapid accumulation of fluid in your chest.
*   **Weight Dampening:** Expect your internal sense of time to drift. The sheer scale and the repetitive, rhythmic *plink* of the condensation can induce a trance-like state. Prepare a localized, rhythmic audio pulse—a metronome—to play in your ears. It will serve as an anchor, a reminder of human time in a place that measures itself in millennia of drips.

### 2. Gear: The Economy of Adhesion
Movement in The Weeping Heights is a nightmare of friction. Everything is coated in that gelatinous film. The moment you step off a "root-vault" path, you are liable to slide into a fissure that might be centimeters wide or kilometers deep.

*   **Suction-Grip Footwear:** Do not bring boots. Bring climbing apparatus equipped with micro-suction pads—the same technology the native scavengers likely use. You are not walking; you are climbing across a landscape that wants to shed you.
*   **The "Silent" Gear Mandate:** This place is a cathedral of silence. High-pitched mechanical noises, plastic crinkling, or shouting will feel like a spiritual violence against the environment. Bring tools that utilize soft-touch, rubberized grips and silence-dampened hinges. If you are loud, you will feel the weight of the silence push back against you, an oppressive pressure in your inner ear that will eventually cause headaches and disorientation.

### 3. Navigation: Following the Vein
There are no maps. The "city" is a biological lattice. If you try to create a map based on linear geometry, you will lose your way.

*   **The Path of Least Drip:** Navigation here is intuitive. Look for the channels where the erosion is most consistent. The moisture follows the path of least resistance through the calcified roots; if you walk against the flow, you are fighting the physics of the entire plateau. Find a "drip-line" and follow it. It will lead you safely through the labyrinth of hollows.
*   **Hazard Detection:** The stone is porous and "inhales" clouds. This means the structure is slightly soft. You are walking on a sponge that has fossilized. Before you place your full weight on any vaulted bridge, tap it. If it sounds hollow or produces a high-frequency resonance, it is thin and likely to crumble under the weight of a mammal.

### 4. Psychological Preparation: The Monumental Ego Death
The most difficult part of visiting The Weeping Heights is the feeling of being entirely unnecessary. There is no challenge to overcome, no summit to reach, no treasure to find.

*   **The Humility Exercise:** Before you arrive, you must accept that the landscape does not care for your presence. You are not an explorer; you are a mote of dust drifting through the ribs of a giant. When you feel the crushing weight of the "deep time" this place exudes, do not fight it. If you try to maintain your sense of importance—your "humanity"—the silence will break you. 
*   **The Observation Stance:** You cannot explore this place, you can only *endure* it. Spend your time sitting in the root-vaults, watching the rosettes. When you see the pale, gelatinous scavengers, do not approach them. They are the true citizens of this city, and they are masters of a surface you are merely renting. Watch how they move. Match your breathing to the *plink* of the water.

### 5. Final Warning: The Mineral Return
Do not stay too long. The atmosphere is not just water; it is a mineral-rich liquid that seeks to fossilize, to coat, to "build." If you remain in the Heights for too long, the microfilm will begin to bead on your gear, then on your suit, and eventually, the air will begin to feel "heavy" inside your lungs as you start to accumulate the very stuff the city is made of.

Leave before you start to believe that you belong to the stone. You are a tourist of the wet, ancient, and slow. Keep your pace steady, your gear sealed, and your silence profound. When you return to the world of noise and fire, the silence of The Weeping Heights will haunt your ears like a phantom rhythm, a persistent *plink... plink... plink* that will remind you, for the rest of your life, that somewhere, a stone mountain is still learning to breathe.
