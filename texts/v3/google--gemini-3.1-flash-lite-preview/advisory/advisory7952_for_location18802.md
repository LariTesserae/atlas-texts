To step into the hollowed skull of the titan is to step into a sensory tuning fork. You are not merely entering a ruin; you are entering an instrument. For an ordinary human traveler, the lack of traditional atmospheric hazards (like atmosphere poisoning or extreme radiation) is a trap. This environment does not kill you with chemistry; it kills you with resonance and hyper-stimulation.

Here is how you must prepare to survive the transition and the stay.

### 1. Sensory Anchoring: The "Acoustic Dampener" Suit
The most immediate threat is the sound. The constant, rhythmic grinding of the titan’s skull and the high-pitched "violin" scratching of the Prowlers will eventually cause physiological distress—nausea, equilibrium failure, and permanent hearing loss.

*   **The Gear:** You need a customized dry-suit made of high-density acoustic foam with a secondary outer layer of vibration-dampening polymer. Ordinary noise-canceling headphones will fail here; you need **active phase-cancellation gear** tuned to low-frequency structural groans. 
*   **The Eyes:** The "blinding, static white" light is not just uncomfortable; it is blinding in the literal sense. Bring polarized, copper-tinted lenses. The amber mycelium mats are your only visual relief. If you look at the bone walls for too long without protection, you will suffer from "retinal etching," where the afterimage of the phosphorescent dust burns into your vision for days.

### 2. Respiratory & Hydration Protocols
The air is thin and bone-dry. The "shimmering haze" is composed of calcified spores and fine bone dust. 
*   **Filtration:** You are breathing micro-particulates of ancient titan-marrow. A standard N95 mask will clog in minutes. You need a **closed-circuit rebreather** or, at the very least, a multi-stage HEPA system that you can manually clear of dust every four hours.
*   **Hydration:** Because there is no water, the environment *will* attempt to pull moisture from your soft tissues. You will dehydrate at an accelerated rate—faster than in a desert, because the dry, resonant air acts like a wick. Do not rely on canteens; bring a pressurized internal camelbak hidden under your suit. Your skin will crack within hours if exposed; ensure your suit provides a complete seal, even at the neck and wrists.

### 3. Tactile and Behavioral Discipline
The city is built on "friction-fit" physics. Every vibration is a potential structural failure.
*   **Footwear:** Wear boots with soft, sound-absorbing soles. Hard-soled boots on the bone floors act as a hammer, broadcasting your presence to the Prowlers. The Prowlers are attracted to friction; if you move loudly, you are "playing" the room, and you will eventually attract their attention.
*   **Movement:** When the skull "groans"—the deep, tectonic settling—you must stop moving, crouch, and brace your hands against the bone floor. If you are standing on a suspended walkway during a shift, center your weight. Do not touch the amber mycelium mats; they feed on vibration. If you vibrate the mat, the mycelium reacts, shrinking or hardening, which could cause the bridge you are standing on to shudder or destabilize.

### 4. Psychological Preparedness: The "Prowler" Phobia
The Prowlers are not your primary threat—the loneliness is. You are walking through a cathedral of ancient, artisanal labor created by people who are long gone. The silence between the groans is heavier than the noise. 
*   **The Psychological Toll:** Prepare for *Auditory Pareidolia*. In a world of white light and constant metallic scratching, your brain will try to invent voices or music in the grinding frequencies. Bring a high-contrast focal object—a dark-colored, non-reflective stone or item from your home—that you can hold to ground your senses when the visual haze becomes too overwhelming.
*   **The "Host" Protocol:** If you encounter a Prowler, do not run. Their limbs are rasping blades. They are blind and scan the world through the acoustic signatures of their environment. If you stop moving and hold your breath, you become "acoustically invisible." They will likely pass right over you, their violin-limbs singing against the ceiling, never knowing you were there.

### 5. Final Checklist for the Traveler
*   **Lubricant:** Apply medical-grade barrier cream to your lips, nasal passages, and eyelids before entry. The air will desiccate you instantly.
*   **Navigation:** Do not trust your sense of direction. The vertical labyrinth is designed to confuse. Use physical markers—small, dense lead weights or dark ink marks—to track your path. Do not rely on digital mapping, as the electromagnetic interference from the phosphorescent dust will scramble your electronics.
*   **The Exit:** Always know where you entered. If you lose your way in the cathedral, the cycles of structural vibration will eventually reset the walkways, shifting the labyrinth into a configuration that may not include your exit path.

**A final warning:** When you leave, you will find that "normal" sound—the wind, a distant car, the hum of a refrigerator—will feel impossibly soft and strangely absent of beauty. You will miss the violin-scratch of the Prowlers. The silence of the world, after the titan’s skull, will feel like a loss.
