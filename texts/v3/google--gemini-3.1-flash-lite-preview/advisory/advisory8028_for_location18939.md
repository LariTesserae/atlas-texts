To visit the Abyssal Conservatory is to willingly walk into a space that has already declared you obsolete. It is not an environment designed for life; it is a reliquary for the concept of stillness. Because there is no surface, there is no "up" or "down" in the traditional sense, only the migration through a frozen, liquid void.

If you are to survive this journeying, you must abandon the notion of an "expedition." You are not an explorer here; you are a contaminant. Here is how you must prepare.

### I. The Physical Hull (The Suit)
Standard atmospheric suits will shatter instantly under the hydrostatic pressure of the Abyssal Conservatory. You require a **semi-organic, pressure-neutralized environmental shell.** 

*   **Fluid-Filled Internal Cavity:** Your suit cannot contain air; air is compressible and will cause a catastrophic implosion. Your suit must be flooded with a temperature-controlled, oxygenated perfluorocarbon liquid. You will be breathing liquid. It is a terrifying sensation—the feeling of drowning—but it is the only way to equalize the pressure of your internal cavities with the crushing weight of the Conservatory.
*   **Thermal Anchoring:** The Conservatory is cold enough to leach the heat from a human soul. Your suit must be lined with a thermal-kinetic converter that draws power from the very *thrum* of the matte-black mineral walls. You are essentially parasitic; you must feed off the architecture to keep your heart from stalling.
*   **The "Dead-Light" Visor:** Since there is no light, standard ocular sensors are useless. You need an ultrasonic-haptic display. This system converts the reflections of the deep-sea vines and the architecture into sensory feedback pulsed directly into your suit’s internal lining. You will "feel" the city against your skin before you ever "see" it in your mind.

### II. The Psychological Anchoring (The Mind)
The danger of the Conservatory is not that it will kill you—it is that it will convince you that you are already dead. "The Great Stasis" is infectious.

*   **Anti-Stasis Counter-Rhythms:** Your brain will attempt to synchronize with the lethargic, years-long swaying of the rubbery flora. Do not let it. You must carry a "metronome"—a device that emits a rapid, erratic pulse of noise (a human heartbeat, a shouting voice, the sound of a rainstorm). This prevents your consciousness from dissolving into the eternal equilibrium of the city.
*   **The De-Synchronization Protocol:** You must prepare for the sensation of your memories becoming "heavy." Time behaves differently in the presence of the black mineral. You will feel as though you have been navigating a single hallway for decades. You must keep a physical log—a tactile tally carved into your own equipment—to remind yourself that you represent a chaotic, fast-moving timeline.

### III. Navigation and Etiquette
You must move as if you are a shadow. The Conservatory is delicate in its permanence.

*   **Avoid the Vines:** The translucent, waxy plants are the lungs of this place. If you tear one, the shift in the local chemistry—the release of the heat and minerals they have sequestered for eons—will cause an immediate, localized pressure spike. You could be crushed by the sudden movement of the fluid around you. Move through the interstitial gaps with the grace of a needle.
*   **Do Not Seek the Source:** The architecture is fractal and repeating. You will be tempted to find the "center" or the "original" chamber. Do not. The city has a geometry that is non-Euclidean; if you focus too hard on the origin, you will encounter the "thrum" at a frequency that will vibrate your teeth out of your skull. Respect the architecture as a finished thought. Do not try to solve it.

### IV. Recovery and Exit
The hardest part of your visit will be the departure. You will have spent your time in a place that has reached the end of its history. To return to a world of light, noise, and growth will be physically painful. 

*   **Gradual De-Pressurization of the Soul:** Before returning to the multiverse, your suit will contain a "buffer chamber." You must sit in this darkness for at least as long as you spent in the Conservatory. You must consciously re-introduce the concepts of *change, decay, and movement* to your mind. 
*   **The Warning:** If you bring anything back—a snippet of the matte-black stone or a cutting of the rubbery vine—do not store it in your base of operations. The Conservatory is not just a place; it is a *state of being*. It does not like to be divided. If you take a piece, the Conservatory will eventually attempt to grow, in ghost-form, within your own reality.

**Final Advice:** If you hear a sound that isn't the *thrum* or the crystalline popping—if you hear something that sounds like breathing that isn't your own—**close your eyes and move away.** There are things that have lived in the Conservatory for eons, and they have adapted to the Stasis. They are not waiting for you; they are simply waiting for the end of everything. Do not let them notice that you are still flickering.
