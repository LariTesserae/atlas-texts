To visit this subterranean cathedral is to step inside a biological machine that has paused mid-breath. It is not an ecosystem; it is a reliquary. Because the environment exists in a state of absolute, static equilibrium, any foreign influence—even the simple act of breathing or sweating—will feel like a violent intrusion. 

If you intend to cross the threshold into this pressurized, silent lung, you must prepare not as a tourist, but as a preservationist.

### 1. The Physiological Preparation: Sensory Deprivation
The primary danger of the "Vertical Garden" is sensory overload. The relentless, shadowless white light will quickly induce ocular fatigue, followed by severe vertigo. 

*   **Ocular Protection:** Do not wear standard sunglasses, which will only distort the color spectrum. You require **polarized, high-density tinting** capable of filtering out the specific frequency of the phosphorescent fungi. If you don't attenuate the light, you will find yourself hallucinating after only a few hours, as the brain searches for shadows that do not exist.
*   **The Auditory Anchor:** The *pop-hiss* of the spore-sacs is hypnotic. After a while, your brain will begin to sync your heart rate and your respiratory cycle to the rhythmic interval of the sacs. This is dangerous; if you "sync" too deeply, you may find yourself unable to move, lulled into a trance state. Wear **noise-canceling ear protection** that allows you to hear your own breathing clearly—this is your primary tether to your own humanity.

### 2. The Physical Logistics: Dealing with the "Viscous Damp"
The atmosphere is not a gas you move through, but a heavy medium you displace. The "silver condensation" is high in mineral content and potentially reactive to human chemistry.

*   **Suit Selection:** Wear a high-grade **hydrophobic, non-porous bodysuit.** Do not expose your skin. If the silver oil touches your skin, it will not absorb, but it will create a cooling seal that will induce rapid perspiration. Because the air is stagnant and pressurized, moisture does not evaporate; it will pool under your clothes, potentially leading to bacterial rot or chafing that, in a world without decay, will not heal correctly until you leave.
*   **Mobility Gear:** The floor consists of shelf-like leaves and interlocking vines. High-friction, soft-soled footwear is necessary, but avoid tread patterns that might snag on the waxy cellulose. You are walking on a sculpture, not a terrain. Move slowly; the "spongy, resilient" floor is structurally sound, but sudden shifts in weight can trigger unintended releases of spores.

### 3. Psychological Calibration
The most difficult aspect of this world is the lack of a horizon. Because the vegetation curves into a closed loop, the mind will eventually reject this geometry, leading to "Containment Panic."

*   **The Mapping Kit:** Bring a **laser-distance measure.** The world looks the same in every direction, and your sense of depth perception will fail within the first mile. You need an objective, numeric way to track how far you have walked. Do not trust your internal compass.
*   **Memento Mori:** You are in a place where nothing dies and nothing decays. This is profoundly antithetical to human nature. The stillness will eventually feel like a pressure against your temples. Carry a physical object from home that undergoes change—an apple, a flower, a piece of ice. Watch it; the contrast between your dying object and the eternal, *static* world will keep your cognitive faculties oriented toward the passage of time.

### 4. Code of Conduct
Respecting the Stasis is essentially about biological hygiene. 

*   **Contain your flora/fauna:** Do not bring organic food, synthetic perfumes, or unnecessary biological matter. You are a carbon-based, decaying entity entering a world of immortalized fiber. If you leave behind a crumb, a scrap of skin, or a microbe, you are introducing the one thing this place lacks: entropy.
*   **Avoid the "Fossilized Burls":** The hollowed-out artifacts are relics of a previous, departed occupant. Do not touch or displace them. The architecture is a single, knitted organism; if you disturb a structure that has been in place for eons, you risk upsetting the delicate structural tension of the floor beneath you. 

### Final Warning: The Exit Strategy
The most dangerous moment is the return. When you transition from the pressurized, white-hot silence of the cathedral back into the erratic, noisy, and chaotic rhythm of the multiverse, you will likely experience "temporal whiplash." 

Do not attempt to walk back to your extraction point in a hurry. You must "decompress" at the threshold. Spend time in an intermediate, semi-lit space. Allow your eyes to adjust to the presence of shadows. Your body has been conditioned to a world that does not age; re-entering a world where things decay will feel, for a moment, like a jarring, frantic catastrophe. 

**Remember:** You are a ghost, a transient, a creature of expiration. Do not try to become part of the garden. Leave everything as you found it—perfectly, terrifyingly still.
