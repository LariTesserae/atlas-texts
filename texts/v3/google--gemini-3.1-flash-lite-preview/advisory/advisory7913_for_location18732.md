To visit the Reservoir Sphere is to enter a cathedral of mechanical loneliness. It is a place that does not want for life; it wants for a witness. Because it is a space of high-pressure, high-heat, and relentless auditory assault, your preparation must move beyond standard interdimensional gear. You are not visiting a wilderness, but a machine that is perpetually "on."

### 1. Physical Preparation: Protecting the "Human Instrument"
The environment is physically demanding, not because of toxins, but because of the aggressive humidity and the percussive nature of the sound.

*   **Pulmonary Protection:** You are breathing water-saturated air that is effectively a warm, dense fog. This can lead to fluid accumulation in the lungs if you stay too long. Bring a **rebreather with an active desiccant cartridge**. You don’t need oxygen—the air is breathable—but you need to strip the moisture out of it before it reaches your lungs to prevent "drowning on dry land."
*   **Acoustic Insulation:** The roar of the recycled water hitting the floor tiles is not just noise; it is a structural vibration that will travel through your skeletal system. Standard earplugs are useless. You need **bone-conduction noise-canceling headsets** that generate an active, counter-phase frequency to negate the deep-bass *thrum* of the iron rivets. Without this, the subsonic vibrations will induce nausea or even internal bruising within an hour.
*   **Skin Integrity:** The "mercury" sheets of water are chemically neutral, but the heat radiating from the walls is constant. Wear a **base layer of hydrophobic, heat-reflective fabric** (similar to a firefighter's under-suit). If you touch the walls, the heat-treated resin will feel like a burn; the suit will prevent your skin from "sticking" to the polished surfaces.

### 2. Psychological Preparation: The "Waiting Room" Syndrome
The sphere is designed to mimic life without possessing it. This creates a cognitive dissonance known as *The Taxidermic Effect*. 

*   **Managing the Stillness:** The room is antiseptic and devoid of microbial movement. For a human mind, which is evolutionarily wired to detect life, this void can cause extreme paranoia. You will find yourself staring at the copper-wire ferns, desperately hoping to see one sway or feel a change in the atmosphere. 
*   **The Ritual of Presence:** Do not go in there to "explore." There is nothing to find. Go in there to *anchor*. Bring a small, physical object that is distinctly biological—a dried piece of wood, a scrap of wool, or a photograph. Place it on the floor. It will look alien against the factory-sharp tiles. The contrast will help you retain your sense of self against the overwhelming machine-logic of the sphere.

### 3. Equipment: What to Bring
*   **Hard-soled boots with vibration-dampening soles:** Do not wear sneakers or rubber-soled shoes. The oscillation of the floor-plates will cause the material to heat up through friction. You need thick, wooden or stiff-composite soles to isolate your feet from the rhythm of the rivets.
*   **A Chronometer:** In a space with no light shift and no cycle other than the water-dump, you will lose your sense of time in minutes. Set a physical alarm that vibrates against your wrist.
*   **A Recording Device:** Because the environment is so perfectly clean and devoid of biological noise, the audio texture is incredibly pure. Bring a high-fidelity binaural microphone. The "symphony of liquid" is the only thing this place creates; documenting it is the only way to prove to yourself that the sphere is actually doing something.

### 4. Behavioral Protocol: The Ethics of the Guest
You are an intruder in a, perhaps, ceremonial space. 
*   **Observe the Rivets:** While you are there, trace the hand-wrought iron rivets with your gloved fingers. Recognize the "human" element—the manual labor. Someone built this under pressure, likely under water, with a fervor that borders on religious. 
*   **Do Not Interfere:** Do not try to move the copper ferns. Do not try to clean the groutless tiles. The space is in a state of high-pressure stasis; any structural change could be catastrophic. The sphere is groaning under the weight of the ocean, and it is holding together by a very specific, manual tension. 
*   **The Exit:** When you leave, do so decisively. The sphere is a vacuum of purpose. If you stay too long, you will begin to identify with the copper-wire plants; you will begin to feel like a decoration that the makers forgot to install. Leave before the roar of the water becomes the only sound you remember.

**Final Warning:** Be mindful of the *groan*. When the structure vents the water, the pitch of the metallic thrum changes. This is the sound of the depth-pressure shifting. If the pitch rises to a frequency that makes your teeth ache, exit immediately. The sphere is reaching its limit.
