To step into the Great Sump is to step into the digestive tract of a machine god. You are not entering a wild space; you are entering a *mechanism*. Because the Sump is “finished,” it is inherently hostile to the new, the sharp, and the ill-prepared. You are a foreign body in a place that has spent geological epochs smoothing its own edges.

If you are planning to traverse the Sump, prepare as if you are entering a high-pressure, high-humidity, hyper-crowded industrial furnace. Here is your traveler’s protocol.

### 1. Physical Preparation: The "Low-Profile" Kit
The Sump is claustrophobic by design. If you stand out, you will catch on a tether; if you are bulky, you will be squeezed by a shifting platform.

*   **The Second Skin:** Do not wear loose fabrics. They will snag on the rusted copper piping and provide purchase for the Skitter-fauna. Wear tight-fitting, synthetic, abrasion-resistant gear—think rock-climbing attire reinforced with liquid-polymer coatings to resist the corrosive humidity.
*   **The Lubrication Suite:** Because the air is thick with condensation and resinous secretions, choose footwear with a "chitin-grip" sole—a micro-tread designed for wet, slick metal. Avoid rubber; it rots in the Sump’s unique chemistry. Use treated leather or synthetic mesh that resists mineral buildup.
*   **Encumbrance:** Leave the backpack. Use a tactical vest with flush-mount pouches. If it sticks out more than two inches from your torso, it will be knocked off by a passing Tether or a nesting Skitter-fauna.

### 2. Sensory Mitigation
The Sump is a sensory assault. The *clack-hiss-thrum* is rhythmic, but it is also disorienting.

*   **Auditory Dampeners:** You need variable-bypass earplugs. If you block the sound entirely, you won't hear the warning groans of shifting Tethers or the skittering of fauna behind you. You need to dampen the high-pitched harmonic whistles while keeping the base frequency of the "metal-groan" audible, as it acts as a warning system for pending plate shifts.
*   **Respiratory Filters:** The air is humid and smells of wet iron and biological rot. If you are sensitive to heavy metals or mold spores, carry a charcoal-filtered mask. The pale-orange "Deep-Light" suggests a high-phosphorus environment; the dust here is likely caustic.

### 3. Navigation and Etiquette (The "Settle" Rule)
In an environment that hasn't changed in an age, you are the only variable. You are an irritant.

*   **The "Flow" Protocol:** Never fight the pathways. The grooves and gullies were shaped by ages of travelers and fauna. If you see a Skitter-fauna carrying a stone, **do not interrupt its path.** These creatures are the maintenance crew. If you accidentally crush one, the local ecosystem may perceive you as a threat to the structural integrity of the Sump and mobilize against you.
*   **Avoid the Tethers:** You may be tempted to use the fiber-braids for stability. **Don't.** They are under immense tension and vibration. If you introduce your weight into a Tether, it may snap, or worse, register your movement as a structural failure, prompting a "re-settling" of the plates you are currently standing on.
*   **The Three-Point Rule:** Never have fewer than three limbs in contact with a surface. Surfaces here are not static; they are in constant, microscopic flux as the internal pressure of the Sub-strata breathes.

### 4. Psychological Preparation
The Sump does not have a horizon, and this will induce vertigo within the first hour. Your brain will crave a "top" and a "bottom."

*   **Accept the Machine:** Do not look for an exit or a ceiling. You are in a hollow. Focus on the geometry immediately in front of you. If you look up into the vertical void, the swaying of the Tethers will make you feel as though you are falling, even when perfectly still. 
*   **Ignore the Loneliness:** Despite being crowded, the Sump is indifferent to you. You are not a guest; you are a stray electron in a settled circuit. The Skitter-fauna will not acknowledge you unless you get in the way. If you feel "watched," ignore it. It is merely the heat-warped metal expanding and contracting, emitting sounds that mimic the breath of living things.

### 5. Essential Inventory
*   **Resin-Solvent:** Your boots will pick up the resin secreted by the fauna. If it builds up, you will become fused to the floor-plates. Keep a small vial of concentrated solvent to clean your soles every few hours.
*   **A "Tapping" Rod:** Carry a small, non-conductive rod to test the stability of floor plates before shifting your full weight. If it sounds "hollow," it is a drainage port. If it sounds "dull," it is structural.
*   **Low-Lumen Lighting:** If you bring a high-intensity flashlight, you will be blinded by the reflection off the rusted metal. Use a dim, amber-spectrum light that mimics the Deep-Light algae. It will preserve your night vision and prevent you from startling the Skitter-fauna.

**Final Wisdom:** If you hear a rhythmic "clicking" that is not coming from the fauna—a sound that synchronizes with your own heartbeat—sit down, press your palms against the floor-plates, and wait. The Sump is resetting. It will pass. Do not climb higher; do not attempt to move. You are in the heart of the machine. Let it beat around you.
