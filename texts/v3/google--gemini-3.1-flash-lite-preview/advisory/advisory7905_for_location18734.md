To visit Olyra is not merely to travel across lines of reality; it is to step into a state of suspended animation. Because the city exists outside the erosive influence of time, you must prepare to discard the frantic, linear pacing of the lower worlds. 

Here is how an ordinary traveler should prepare for a sojourn in the city of the serrated gums.

### I. The Physiological Adjustment
The thinness of the air in Olyra is not just a challenge to your lungs; it is a weight against your consciousness. Before your arrival, spend at least a week in high-altitude environments. You must learn the "Olyran breath"—a shallow, rhythmic huffing that prevents hyperventilation. 

Do not try to breathe deeply. In Olyra, a deep, eager gasp will only result in dizziness and a sense of intrusive urgency that the city’s atmosphere will reject. You must learn to sip the air. You should also prepare for the "Stillness Sickness"—a lethargy caused by the environment’s refusal to change. Your heart rate will naturally want to slow down to match the *tap-clink* of the condensing water. Allow it to slow, but keep a mental anchor to the concept of *progression*, otherwise, you may find yourself sitting on a stone ledge for three days, fascinated by the way light hits a single obsidian facet.

### II. The Suitcase of the Permanent
Packing for Olyra requires you to eliminate anything that represents decay, haste, or disposable modernity. 
*   **Fabric:** Synthetics will squeak discordantly against the obsidian and feel alien against the damp stone. Pack dense, heavy wools—boiled wool or heavy, raw-woven hemp. The color palette should remain muted: deep slate, charcoal, and moss-green. You want to blend into the shadows, not compete with the architecture.
*   **Footwear:** Your boots must have soft, high-friction soles. Leather is acceptable if it is thick and unpolished. You need the ability to sense the texture of the basalt beneath your feet. Avoid clunky, rigid hiking soles; they are loud, and noise is a breach of etiquette in Olyra.
*   **Tools:** Bring a small, high-quality sharpening stone. It is your best social currency. If you wish to approach a resident, offering to hone their flint chisel is a signal of respect for their devotional labor. Do not bring digital devices; the lack of electrical shift and the crystalline silence will likely cause them to malfunction, or worse, they will seem like primitive, jarring toys against the eternal stone.

### III. Social Etiquette: The Religion of Maintenance
Olyra is a society of participants, not tourists. If you stand idle, you are an anomaly. If you help, you are a guest.
*   **The Law of Conservation:** Never discard anything. There is no waste in Olyra. If you bring a biscuit, consume every crumb. If you have a scrap of paper, keep it. The citizens view the discarding of materials as a moral failing.
*   **The Pace of Interaction:** Conversations in Olyra are not binary exchanges. They are long, slow, and often interrupted by the rhythmic drip of water. Do not interrupt a silence. If you are sitting with an Olyran and twenty minutes pass without a word, that is not a lull; it is the conversation reaching its intended depth.
*   **Mirror the Motion:** Notice how the beetles move? Notice how the citizens walk? Do not rush. Every movement you make should be deliberate, as if you are trying not to disturb the dust—though there is no dust, only water and stone.

### IV. Navigating the Verticality
The city is a series of interconnected vertical labyrinths. If you are prone to vertigo, the sight of the rope-bridges swaying in the rare gust might induce panic. To manage this, keep your eyes on the basalt underfoot rather than the precipice. 

When crossing a bridge, test the resonance of the fibers with your hand before stepping out. If the bridge is "singing" in the wind, wait. The rope-bridges are the arteries of the city; they possess a personality. They will tell you when they are ready to be crossed.

### V. Psychological Preparation: The Fear of Equilibrium
The most difficult part of visiting Olyra is the realization that nothing will ever happen. There will be no storm to force you inside; no sunrise to wake you; no transition to mark the end of your stay. 

You will likely experience a moment of panic—a desperate, clawing need for change, for a clock to tick, or for a leaf to fall. When this hits, go to the northern shaded walls. Find the mosses. Study their minute, iridescent growth. Remind yourself that you are not there to change the city; you are there to be smoothed by it. 

Pack a small notebook, not for thoughts, but for sketches. Olyra demands to be recorded. By drawing the geometry of the blocks, you are participating in the city's one industry: the acknowledgement of existence. 

**A final warning:** When you leave Olyra, the world below will feel frantic, chaotic, and horribly loud. You will see rust, decay, and movement as signs of impending death. It takes time to acclimate back into a world that is falling apart. Carry a small stone from the lower mountains with you—a reminder that not everything is meant to be permanent.
