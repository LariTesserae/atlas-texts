To step into Oulo is to enter an environment that has not yet decided if it is a machine, a garden, or a sentient dream. As an ordinary human traveler, you are essentially walking into an unfinished symphony. The greatest danger in Oulo is not hostility; it is the vulnerability of your own rigid, biological nature against a landscape that is fundamentally fluid and "aware."

Here is how you must prepare to visit the basin of Oulo.

### 1. Physiological Preparation: Harmonizing the Pulse
The city hums at a sub-auditory frequency—the sound of nanites and fungal networks "thinking." To a human, this can manifest as profound vertigo, nausea, or a sense of "dread-anticipation."
*   **The Infrasonic Buffer:** Consult a multiverse chirurgeon for an ear-implant or a high-end noise-canceling headset tuned to "Active Noise Cancellation: Sub-20Hz." You need to decouple your inner ear from the city’s rhythmic vibration, or you will find yourself dizzy and unable to maintain your balance on shifting floors.
*   **Hydration Awareness:** Oulo is a closed, damp system. The air is moisture-saturated. You will not feel thirsty, but the "silver-grey condensation" is likely highly mineralized. Do not drink the air or expose too much skin. Cover yourself in hydrophobic fabrics; if the mist clings to your skin long enough, it may begin to "calculate" your presence, leading to mild, non-lethal calcification or skin itching where the city attempts to integrate your biology into its architecture.

### 2. Gear: The "Static" Traveler
Because the architecture operates on Fluid-State Engineering, you must remain as "neutral" as possible to avoid triggering the city’s defensive or accommodative reflexes.
*   **Rigid-Sole Footwear:** Wear boots with magnetic or inertia-dampening soles. If the floor softens beneath you—transitioning from flint to viscosity—you need gear that can distribute your weight rapidly. If you stand too long in one spot, the ground *will* attempt to wrap around your feet to "support" you. You must be able to break that seal.
*   **The Haptic Beacon:** Do not rely on GPS. Instead, carry a haptic beacon set to a "constant push" frequency. This signal acts as a polite "Do Not Integrate" sign. It effectively tells the city: *I am a guest, not a structural element.* Without this, a wall might decide you are a pillar and begin to grow around your shoulder while you are leaning against it.
*   **Tactile Protection:** Carry a set of light, insulated gloves. The lower districts, where the ground is hot and forming, can sear skin. Avoid touching the obsidian-glass spires; they are "infant" structures and may have unhardened, sharp edges that can slice through even standard synthetic silks.

### 3. Navigation: The Psychology of Movement
You cannot treat Oulo like a city of stone. It is a city of intent. 
*   **The Flow-State Walk:** Move with purpose. If you hesitate in a corridor, the city notices. It may sense your indecision as a structural gap and attempt to "fill it" by closing the hallway or changing the topography of the walls to redirect you. Walk with a steady, constant gait. Speed is less important than trajectory.
*   **The Gravity Conduits:** The vortices are efficient, but to an ordinary human, they are jarring. The city’s physics are not yours. When entering a transit conduit, tuck your limbs and close your eyes. The refraction of light—the way the city bends, twists, and mirrors—can cause "Trans-Dimensional Dysmorphia." You will see yourself spiraling through the geometry and it will take a toll on your sanity if you watch it for too long.

### 4. Behavioral Etiquette: Being a Guest of the Void
Oulo is empty, but it is not abandoned. The translucent, dandelion-seed fauna are what remain of the previous consciousnesses—or perhaps what the city is currently growing to replace them.
*   **Do Not Seek Contact:** These entities are "remnants." If you watch them too closely, they may drift toward you, sensing your warmth. They are not ghosts; they are light-refracting biological data. If they enter your personal space, simply stop moving and breathe slowly. Do not swat at them. They are cooling agents for the city’s heat; if you agitate them, the ambient temperature of the room may drop sharply, potentially causing the solid floor beneath you to liquefy prematurely.
*   **Leave No Trace:** Oulo is a clean, geometric environment. Any trash you leave behind will be processed within minutes. The city will consume your discarded wrapper or stray hair and attempt to weave it into a wall. It is a profound violation of the city’s aesthetic purity. Pack in, and meticulously pack out.

### Summary Checklist for Departure
*   **Check:** Is your Haptic Beacon functioning? (If it dies, you are now part of the city’s structural integrity).
*   **Check:** Are your joints limber? (If the floor turns to liquid, you must be ready to walk on the tension surface).
*   **Check:** Are you keeping your voice low? (The city is quiet because it is listening. Loud noises create ripples in the fungal-nerve fiber-optics, which can cause the bioluminescent moss to "flash-burst," potentially blinding you).

Enter Oulo with the grace of a ghost, the firmness of a statue, and the alertness of a field archaeologist. Treat it like a sleeping giant: admire the beauty, but do not dream along with it.
