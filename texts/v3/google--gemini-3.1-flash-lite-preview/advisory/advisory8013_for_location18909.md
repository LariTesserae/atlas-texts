Visiting Sector 4 is not a journey of exploration; it is a journey of endurance. When you step through the threshold into this basin, you are not entering a city—you are entering a digestive tract. It is a place that has been swallowing itself for centuries, and you are, quite suddenly, a very small, very foreign protein.

To survive and traverse this "industrial necropolis," you must prepare as if you are wading through an acid-sweat sauna that is actively trying to corrode your equipment. Here is the protocol for the human traveler.

### 1. Physiological Armament: The "Dry-Skin" Protocol
The heat in Sector 4 is heavy and "clinging." It will try to fill your lungs with ozone and your pores with oxidized particulate.
*   **Respiratory Filters:** Do not attempt to breathe the ambient air for long. It is saturated with metallic dust, beetle-castings, and pulverized rust. You need a closed-circuit rebreather or, at the very least, an industrial-grade respirator with aggressive particulate (P100) and vapor scrubbing.
*   **Hydration:** Paradoxically, you will dehydrate rapidly. The air is so humid that your sweat will not evaporate, and your body will struggle to regulate its temperature against the constant, sweltering damp. Pack electrolyte-rich hydration packs—do not try to find water here. The oily, shimmering film on the mire puddles is not just water; it is a bio-chemical broth you do not want touching your skin.
*   **Dermal Sealing:** Wear a lightweight polymer-coated suit. If your skin is exposed, the acidic moisture dripping from the iron gantries will cause contact dermatitis within minutes. The air here wants to rust *everything*, and you are made of minerals it recognizes.

### 2. Navigational Awareness: The Grid of Lost Geometry
The city is built for giants and machines. You will feel dwarfed by the sheer scale of the warehouses and the absurdity of the inactive furnaces. 
*   **Footwear:** Thick-soled, non-slip tactical boots are non-negotiable. The ground is a slick, unstable mixture of metallic sludge and damp silt. You are walking over a graveyard of submerged gears and uneven basalt; it is remarkably easy to break an ankle.
*   **The "Tink" Soundscape:** Use the rhythmic *tink-tink* of the water to navigate. If the sound of the dripping changes—if it becomes muffled or suddenly stops—you are likely standing beneath a mass of copper-shelled beetles. Do not stand still for more than a few minutes in one place, or the beetles will begin to treat *you* as stationary infrastructure.

### 3. The Beetle Problem: Avoiding the Living Shingles
The beetles are the stewards of the sector. They are not predators in the traditional sense, but they are possessive of the rust.
*   **Vibration Discipline:** The beetles operate on vibration. If you walk with a heavy, rhythmic gait, you will trigger their "movement waves." To them, a human heartbeat or a heavy footstep is essentially the sound of a structural failure. They will migrate toward you, eager to "clean" the surface they believe you to be.
*   **Silent Movement:** Use soft-soled gear and move in a rolling, non-rhythmic gait. If you feel the walls humming under your hand, *let go immediately*. A hand-sized beetle biting into your grip to test if you are made of oxidized metal is a mistake you do not want to make. They don’t want your blood; they want the iron in your watch, your buckles, and your blood oxygen. Keep your metallic accessories to a complete minimum.

### 4. Psychological Preparation: The Equilibrium of Silence
The hardest part of Sector 4 is the silence. Despite the millions of clattering legs, the city is profoundly lonely. It is a "purpose-built" environment where the purpose has died.
*   **The Stagnant Effect:** You will feel a deep, encroaching inertia—the "sweltering gloom"—which will make you want to sit down against a cold iron bulkhead and simply wait for the misty violet-gray light to dim further. Resist this. The environment is chemically designed to encourage lethargy. If you allow yourself to settle, you will lose your sense of urgency, and that is how people become part of the city’s reclaimed geometry.
*   **The Scavengers:** Keep a high-intensity focused beam flashlight. The reptilian scavengers in the flooded alleyways are cowards that hate sudden, narrow-beam light. They aren't looking for a fight; they are looking for movement. If you see two dark, reflective eyes weaving between the submerged gears, shine your light on them and do not break your stride. 

### Final Gear Checklist:
1.  **Chemical-Resistant "Second Skin" Suit:** If it can withstand dilute mineral acids, it is adequate.
2.  **Anti-Corrosive Lubricant:** Smear this on your gear joints and zippers; the humidity will seize them shut within the hour.
3.  **Haptic Pings:** A device that sends out a soft, high-frequency ping to unsettle the beetle swarms before you turn a corner.
4.  **Analog Compass:** The electromagnetic field of the generators is erratic. Digital navigation will fail. Rely on the "lean" of the chimneys; they track the wind and the slope of the basin.

**Traveler’s Note:** Do not look for an exit that is "correct." Look for the path where the rust is thinnest. If you find a place where the beetles have stopped moving, leave that area immediately. That silence is the sound of the city deciding that whatever was there before is now just another piece of the floor.
