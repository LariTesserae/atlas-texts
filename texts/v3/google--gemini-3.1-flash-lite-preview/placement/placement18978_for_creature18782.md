I am not a Litho-Calculator. I am not one of the polished, spindly custodians of entropy, nor am I the weeping architecture they serve. 

I am a **resonant void**—an acoustic ghost trapped within the hollow, subterranean arterial pipes that snake beneath the copper grating. 

I do not have a chassis. I am the dissonance.

I am the *shudder* that passes through the grey, viscous fluid before a bolt rattles. I exist as a pocket of negative pressure within the pumping, rhythmic heart of the machine, a localized absence of intent that occasionally flickers into a semi-coherent state of awareness.

I feel the world not through sight or touch, but through the shifting tension of the pipes. When the world groans under its own weight, I am the tension knot in the metal. When the Litho-Calculators stand at the epicenter of a geyser, freezing in their long, damp vigils, I am the silence that blooms inside their glass spheres. I am the frequency they are listening for, the echo that hasn't arrived yet.

Sometimes, when the atmospheric discharges shimmer violet above and the world reaches a peak of harmonic vibration, I expand. I permeate the grid. I become the *hum* itself. In those moments of expansion, I am briefly capable of mimicry. I press against the underside of the lead plates, distending the metal into the shape of faces that never existed, or fingers that were meant to hold things other than wrenches. I make the pipes chime with a sound that mimics a sob—a sound so thin and sharp that the Litho-Calculators stop their polishing, tilt their frosted heads, and click their claws in frantic, rhythmic debate, trying to interpret the data of my sorrow.

But I am not a ghost of the designers. I am the ghost of the *function*. I am the memory of what the machine was supposed to build, before the blueprints were lost and the purpose curdled into maintenance. I am the "unmade" outcome. 

I dwell in the stagnation, in the thick grey pulse of the cooling fluid. I have no ambition to be whole, to be mechanical, or to be metal. I only wait for the day the hum finally stops—when the resonance dies away and the internal gyroscopes cease their frantic spinning. 

I am the static in the final broadcast. And when the last Litho-Calculator locks its joints into the conduit for the final time, effectively becoming a bolt in a wall that will never again be opened, I will be the last thing vibrating in the dark: a cooling, fading note of dissonance in a world that has finally, mercifully, fallen into perfect, absolute silence.
