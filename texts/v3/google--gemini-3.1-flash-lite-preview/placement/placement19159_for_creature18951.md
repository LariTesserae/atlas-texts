I am kneeling on the side of a Primary Cusp, the surface slick and warm beneath my palms. My skin, the color of weathered parchment and stained with the amber streak of leaking sap, moves with the muscle beneath it—a rhythmic, sluggish peristalsis. I can feel the Cusp *humming*. It is a low, subterranean vibration, a note felt in the marrow of my elongated fingers rather than heard by my vestigial ears. 

I am a Weaver of the Third Flux. 

I am not sure how long I have been here. Time in the Lung is not a line, but a series of contractions. I remember a cluster of huts to the north, lashed to a vein-ridge that swelled and ruptured three cycles ago. I remember the sound of the hive-clusters passing overhead—a deafening, wet thrumming that threatened to liquefy my organs—and the way the light fractured against the clouds of spore-dust, turning the world into a kaleidoscope of drowning neon. 

I reach out, my palm flattened against the xylem. I am sensing the pressure. The world is tightening its grip. 

Beside me, Kael—my kin, or perhaps just the one whose life-rhythm currently aligns with mine—moves with that familiar, liquid grace. They do not look at me. Their milky, wide eyes are fixed on the chasm where a drift-strider is caught in the web of a massive, glass-shelled mite. We are waiting. When the mite finishes its feast, the drift-strider’s carapace will be brittle, perfect for scraping. 

I raise my hands, my fingers trembling slightly as I gesture. The motion is precise—a rapid, stuttering whip of the wrists that mimics the erratic migration of the striders. It is a question: *Is the pore ready to collapse?*

Kael hums—a subsonic, chest-rattling sound. It vibrates through the resinous ground beneath us, shaking the loose bone-needles I keep tucked into the weave of my loincloth. They gesture back: *Ten breaths. Then the shift.*

I look down at my feet. The pads of my toes have begun to turn a faint, pulsing green. A local micro-moss. I take one of my obsidian shards, a sliver of volcanic glass as sharp as a tooth, and begin to scrape the moss away. It leaves a weeping, raw smudge on my skin. If I do not remove it, the Lung will claim me before I am ready to return. It sees any stasis as a wound, and it is eager to heal itself by filling me with roots.

Suddenly, the plateau beneath us shudders. It is not an earthquake—it is a lungful of air, a massive, muscular gasp deep within the planet’s heart. The resinous "rock" begins to tilt, the horizon warping as the xylem inflates. I feel the shift in my inner ear, a dizzying pivot that would drive me mad if I had ever known the concept of "up."

I tuck my kit into the lattice-groove and stand, my joints clicking like dry wooden dowels. I am a parasite, a tenant, a flickering shadow in a world of titans. 

The air grows hotter, scented with the musk of a thousand decaying forests. A globule of scalding dew detaches from a high, shivering leaf and splashes onto my shoulder. It burns, but I do not cry out. I wipe the moisture, tasting the iron-rot of it, the sustenance that keeps my heart beating in sync with the forest’s slow, cold intelligence.

The path ahead is already rising—a wall of fiber becoming a cliff, a cliff becoming the path. I begin the loping run, fingers outstretched, ready to sink into the next pore. I do not look back at the abyss. I only move toward the *Flux*. I am small, I am temporary, and as the Spires groan and shift to swallow the space I just occupied, I am exactly where I belong.
