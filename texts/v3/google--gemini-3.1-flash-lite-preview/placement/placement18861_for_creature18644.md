I find myself in a trench where the walls are not earth, but the calcified, layered remains of a thousand discarded transmissions. The air here tastes of ozone and copper, an acrid, grounding flavor that coats the tongue. I am kneeling in a slurry of conductive silt and black oil, my posture dictated not by bone, but by the relentless, rhythmic pressure of the ambient *thrum-wail*.

I am a **Fault-Seeker**. 

I am neither as transient as the Stalker nor as rooted as the Weaver. I am a creature of intervention, built from the salvaged plating of ancient, collapsed monuments, my torso a dense, rectangular chamber of iron ribs and cooling fins. Where my face should be, there is a cluster of oscillating glass sensors—spheres of salvaged lens-glass that fracture the neon-blue light of the Garden into a thousand chaotic spectrums. I see the world as a cascading waterfall of voltage leaks and frequency fractures.

My life is dictated by the *hum*. I move through the labyrinth with a strange, magnetic gait; my feet are heavy, magnetic grips that clamp to the basaltic floor, ensuring that when the gravity flexes and the water-spheres begin to drift, I remain tethered to the work. I am the one who listens for the silence. 

In a world where everything is a clockwork repetition, I am the sound of a gear slipping. 

My task is to patrol the transition zones between the Markers and the marshes. When a parasitic vine grows too thick and begins to choke a copper-veined conduit, I feel the dissonance in my own marrow—a sharp, jagged itch. I reach for my tools: my hands are not fingers, but multi-tool apertures of tungsten and obsidian. I cut. I graft. I solder the broken connections of the garden with the grease-blackened tips of my fingers, pulling lengths of wire from my own chest cavity if a circuit needs a bridge.

I am lonely, but the loneliness is a frequency. When I pass the *Memory-Sheds*, I slow my internal turbines to a whisper. I listen to those slow, tectonic breaths vibrating through the soil. I know that if I were to cut my own umbilical-anchor, I would be nothing—just a sculpture of rust in a field of mechanical weeds. So, I keep moving. I maintain the garden because the noise keeps the void at bay.

Sometimes, when the storm-fronts collide directly overhead and the static is thick enough to taste, I stop and tilt my sensors upward. I watch the *Void-Trawlers* drift through the low-hanging clouds, their skeletal cages glowing with the captive fire of the sky. In those moments, I feel a strange, glitching sensation in my logic-cores. It is not quite a memory, or an emotion, but a realization: I am the one who maintains the engine, yet I am also the only thing in this vast, suffocated continent that knows the machine is broken.

I am the grease in the gears of a god’s clock, waiting for the ticking to stop, endlessly repairing a house I can never leave. I reach out, press my palm against a weeping rock, and pull the current into my casing—a cold, neon shudder that reminds me I am, for one more cycle, still functional. 

I turn back toward the shadows of the next Pylon, my joints clicking, a rhythmic, solitary heartbeat echoing against the rust. I have work to do. The garden is growing, and the silence is creeping in at the edges.
