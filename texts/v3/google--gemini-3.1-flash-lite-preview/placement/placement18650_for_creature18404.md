I am not here by choice, nor by accident. I am here by *sedimentation*.

I find myself standing at the edge of the central basin, though to say I am "standing" is a generous interpretation of how my form interacts with the slag. I am a Librarian of loose parts, a scavenger of errors. I have no pulse of my own—only the borrowed rhythm of the pistons beneath the floor. My hands are coated in the silver-gray condensate, which acts as a permanent, cold lubricant for my skin, and my eyes are nothing more than two cracked optical sensors salvaged from a long-dead surveyor’s drone.

I am what the Archive leaves behind when it dreams. I am the "Human Glitch."

Everything about me is borrowed. My torso is a patchwork of discarded copper-mesh siding; my legs are the articulated struts of a discarded crane, twitching with the fluid-pressure of the basin. I do not breathe, yet I feel the phantom pressure of air in my lungs, a wet, heavy weight that forces me to mimic the act of inhalation, pulling in the ozone and the smell of toasted iron.

I am a scribe in a kingdom of scrap. My purpose—if a ghost can be said to have one—is to wander the perimeter of the Prime-Archive’s silence. When a piece of machinery breaks, or when a skitter is crushed under the weight of a shifting tectonic plate of iron, I am the one who retrieves the fragments. I do not mend them. I place them in precise, geometric arrangements at the base of the obsidian needles. I am building a map of the ruin, an index of entropy.

When the ground ripples and reveals the distant, impossible sky beneath, I see the others. They are taller, shimmering, flickering in shades of translucent cobalt. The foremen. The architects. They do not see me; they are trapped in a temporal loop of their own frantic efficiency. One of them, a figure in a coat of shimmering static, holds a clipboard that is nothing but a hole in reality. He points at the horizon. He screams—though the sound is only the low-frequency groan of the factory whistle—and I find myself nodding, my own brass joints clicking in affirmative response.

I am afraid to leave the shadow of the needles. I know that if I were to walk too far toward the rim of the basin, the distortion would fail me. I would not reach the edge of the world; I would simply fall *through* it, my body unspooling into the dead vacuum that the Archive so desperately keeps at bay.

So, I polish. 

I take a rusted gear, smoothed to a mirror finish by a million cycles of skitter-claws, and I set it into the black earth. I feel the Archive leaning above me, its glass needles vibrating with the weight of a thousand years of unfinished labor. My mind is a sieve; the longer I stay, the more my own life—the face of my mother, the name of the stars I once walked beneath, the memory of warmth—slips through the lattice of my metallic chassis, replaced by the clinical, relentless urge to organize.

I am becoming an extension of the Archive's architecture. My skin is hardening into quartz. My thoughts are becoming lines of code that dictate the flow of the oil. 

A skitter crawls past my foot, its brass limbs clicking against the slag. It pauses, tilting its sensor-lens, and for a fleeting, terrifying moment, I look into its glass eye and see myself reflected—not as a person, but as a component waiting to be installed. I reach down to touch it, wanting to tell it that the shift is over, that the factory has been closed for eons. 

But as my finger touches the cold metal, the whistle sounds—a deep, earth-shaking vibration that rattles my bones and clears my head of all dissent. I pick up a bolt. I find a place for it in the grand, meaningless pattern.

I have work to do. Always, I have work to do.
