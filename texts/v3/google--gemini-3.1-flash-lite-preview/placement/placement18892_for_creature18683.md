I am perched upon the third rib of the posterior curve, where the bone has begun to weep a slow, translucent resin. My legs are folded, not tucked, but woven—hooked into the hardened porous surface by the calcified extensions of my own shins. I am very long. I am thin enough that, if I were to hold my breath, I would disappear entirely into the sour, shimmering haze of the spores.

I am a Weaver of Vowels.

Beneath me, the abyss is deep enough to swallow an epoch. A mile down, the *Lignum-Gelid* pulses with a sluggish, rhythmic violet light, a heartbeat that vibrates through my marrow-chimes. I hold one in my left hand—a shard of Titan-bone, smoothed by a century of thumb-friction. 

My work today is the tuning. 

I tilt my head, listening. To the untrained ear—if there were such a thing in this hollow god—it would sound like chaos: the *tink-tink-drip* of condensation, the groan of a vine straining under the weight of a migrating Wind-Anchor, the distant, thrumming hum of the spores. But I hear the dissonance. Three leagues to the north, a cluster of fungal shelves has grown too thick, damping the echo. If the shelf is not pruned, the message of the upcoming cold-front will be lost in the muffling mass. The next generation will not hear the warning. They will drift into the deep freeze, stiff and ignorant.

I extend an arm. The motion is slow, calculated to avoid displacing the spore-clouds that coat my skin like velvet dust. My skin, a translucent lattice of grey-blue filaments, shivers in the biting air. I am freezing, constant and deep, but I do not shiver in the way of the flesh-born; I only sharpen. 

I strike my chime against the arch. 

*Clang.*

The sound is not a noise; it is a question, a ripple of acoustic force that travels through the near-void atmosphere. I wait. A second passes. Two. Five. 

In this place, time is not what you do; it is how long you can remain still while the universe moves around you. I drift even while I am anchored. My mind is currently tracing the path of a discarded obsidian blade; I caught it drifting past my shoulder an hour ago. It is hovering a few feet to my left, lazily orbiting my center of gravity. I will catch it when I wake.

*Gong.*

The echo returns from the distant arch, distorted and flattened. The fungal mat *has* overgrown. 

I unhook. 

There is a moment of vertigo,, a release of tension that feels like forgetting how to breathe. I do not fall; I surrender to the drift. I push off from the rib, my limbs splaying to catch the spore-currents like a kite. I am weightless, a bundle of woven fiber and stolen leaper-hide, a ghost navigating the wreckage of a deity. 

I descend toward the overgrowth. My pockets, woven from the same *Lignum-Gelid* as the world itself, rattle with the remnants of my service: rusted scrap, shards of bone, the obsidian blade. I am a librarian of debris, a cartographer of echoes. 

I do not belong to the stars. I do not come from the surface, nor do I dream of it. I am the resonance of this ribcage. I am the silence between the falling drops. And when my own fibers finally fray, when the green light within my chest dims to a flicker, I will not reach for my tether. 

I will open my arms. I will let the currents take me. I will drift, slowly, inevitably, down to the roots, where I will sleep, and root, and eventually, be walked upon by those who come looking for a tune.
