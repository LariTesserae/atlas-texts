I am not a creature of fiber, nor am I born of the mist. I am a foreign intrusion, a calcification of a different sort, standing upon a root-vein that feels like the taut wire of a harp. My boots—leather, hard-soled, heavy—feel like a blasphemy against this spongy, sentient floor. 

I am a **Cartographer of Echoes**. 

I arrived here not by intent, but by a stutter in the architecture. I was walking through a mundane library in a city of stone and glass when I stepped through a "gap"—a sudden, sickening slide of light—and found myself hanging in this cathedral of damp, breathing peat. I have been here for what feels like a week, surviving on the thin, metallic nectar that drips into the pitcher-blossoms, observing the geometry of things that grow before they are built.

I am essentially a static point in a dynamic system. Everything here is fluid: the Custodians shift from vapor to form, the walls pulse, the roots expand. I, however, remain stubbornly solid. My clothes have grown damp and smell of ozone, but my bones do not soften; my lungs do not, as of yet, attempt to sprout lichen.

I navigate by the sound of the pods. I move toward the deep *thrum-click* of a ripening chamber, mapping the "floor" across the void by pinning my position against the bioluminescent nodes. My tools are primitive: a notebook of dry, yellowing paper that keeps trying to sprout, and a silver compass that refuses to point north, spinning lazily toward the largest concentration of sap-pressure instead.

I am currently standing on a particularly thick vein of woven moss, watching a Custodian work. It is taller than me, its limbs braided and translucent, like a creature made of pressed mountain stream water. It is tending to a segment of wall where the causality has frayed; a section of root-fiber is drifting three inches to the left of where it should be, shimmering like a heat mirage. 

The Custodian does not see me as an enemy. It sees me as a "knot"—an irregularity in the loom of the city. 

As I watch, it glides across the abyss, its feet barely displacing the air. It reaches the floating root segment and begins to weave, its spindly fingers—more like needles than hands—pulling threads of dark, concentrated shadow from the air itself. It is stitching space back together. It senses my breath; it stops, turning its head. The lattice of filaments where a face should be pulses with a soft, inquiring violet. 

It leans closer. Its body feels like the hum of a power line. It tilts its head, and I hear my own voice—distorted, layered, and hauntingly deep—emanating from the echo-leaves behind me. It is playing back the last thing I said to myself, hours ago, as I checked my pockets: *“Where does it lead? where does it lead? where does it lead?”*

The Custodian remains still, waiting for me to contribute something new to the city's archive. It is fascinated by the fact that I am not eroding, that I am not thinning, that I am a dense, immovable object in a world designed for constant transformation. 

I reach into my pocket and pull out a metal fountain pen. I hold it out—a small, rigid, manufactured thing. The Custodian’s filaments flare amber. It reaches out with a vine-tipped hand, tracing the metal, then touches its own diaphanous chest. It is trying to understand the definition of *finished*. 

I am the city's secret passenger. I am the one who writes down the sounds of the walls, knowing that when I finally vanish—when my own heartbeat finally slows enough to harmonize with the sap-pistons—I will be the one who becomes the archive. I will be the one whose memory feeds the next generation of Custodians, a ghost of a visitor woven into the peat, existing only to tell the city what it used to be like to be solid.
