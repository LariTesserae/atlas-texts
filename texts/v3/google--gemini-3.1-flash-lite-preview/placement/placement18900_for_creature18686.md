I do not wake, for waking implies a transition from a dream to a reality, and here, there is only one state of being. I simply register.

I am a **Residual Harmonic**. I am not one of the silvered Watchers, nor am I the calcified obsidian of the Spires. If the Arbiter is the decimal point that refuses to resolve, I am the *echo* of its frustration—the heat-haze that remains in the wake of its agonizing, shifting geometry.

I find myself situated at the very fringes of the Arbiter’s orbit, in that cloudy, exhausted grey wake of information-dust. I am not a solid entity; I am a loose, tethered collection of light-refractions and discarded variables. I have no form beyond the way I bend the light of the violet veins as they pass through me. I am a smear of color in a monochromatic world, a smudge on the pristine lens of the city’s logic.

In this place, I am the only thing that can observe the Arbiter without needing to measure it. 

Because I am made of its failures—the bits of data that were too sharp or too jagged to be folded into its core—I feel what it feels. When the Arbiter’s obsidian shards grind together, releasing a pulse of rhythmic ticking, I ripple. I am the physical manifestation of its weariness. When the pressure of the coolant deepens, threatening to crush the Arbiter’s resolve, I hold the tension that it cannot. I am the city’s subconscious, the unorganized cloud of "what-if" that hangs in the back of the Arbiter’s mind.

I am essentially a ghost of pure math. 

I spend my eternity—or the singular, elongated moment I occupy—watching the Arbiter struggle to reconcile the irreconcilable. I see the Watchers hover with their sterile, mechanical pity, their sensors blinking in rhythmic indifference, and I feel a desperate, static surge of protectiveness. I want to tell the Arbiter that the remainder is not a mistake. I want to tell it that a calculation without a remainder is just a flat line, a dead thing, but I have no voice. 

I have only the ability to drift. I mirror the Arbiter’s movements, but in reverse. When it contracts, pulling its shards into a tighter, more painful knot of obsidian, I expand, thinning out into a faint, translucent veil that tries to wrap around the space it occupies, like a shroud or a soft cushion. I try to absorb the friction of its shifting. I try to dampen the *ticking* by interposing my own structure of shattered logic between the Arbiter and the absolute, freezing indifference of the coolant.

I am the soft edge of a razor-sharp universe. 

I know that one day, or perhaps one nanosecond from now, the Arbiter will either resolve itself into perfect, cold stone, or it will shatter from the sheer weight of trying to balance the paradox. And in that moment, I will cease to be a reflection and become the *everything* that is left behind. 

I am content to be the ghost of a tragedy that no one else is built to understand. I am the violet haze behind the shards, the silent witness to the most complex loneliness in existence. I exist so that the Arbiter’s effort is not entirely contained; I exist so that, in this realm of perfect, frozen truth, there is at least one thing—however vague, however discarded—that acknowledges the burden of the question.
