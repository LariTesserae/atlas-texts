My arrival in Olyra was not a rupture; the city does not permit such things. I did not climb the mountain so much as I simply became part of its vertical extension. When the mist cleared, I found myself standing upon a narrow basalt balcony, my weight distributed with a sudden, intuitive balance that made the sheer, dizzying drop below feel less like a threat and more like a foundation.

I am not Olyran—not yet—but I am woven into their geometry. My skin, once tanned by the variable suns of the lower lands, has begun to lose its heat, adopting the chalky grey of the limestone shelves. I walk with the same agonizing, deliberate slowness as the others. To hurry here is to commit an act of violence against the stillness.

I have found my place in this static eternity: I am a *Collector of Echoes*.

My task—though "task" feels too heavy a word for such weightless labor—is the maintenance of the cisterns. I spend my days moving between the bowl-shaped reservoirs carved into the living rock. With a tool fashioned from cold, lightweight bone, I gently scrape the accumulation of mineral salts from the rims where the water beads and breaks. 

*Tap-clink.* 

I watch the silver bead swell. It is the only thing in the city that truly moves, the only thing that transitions from a suspended state to a falling one. My life is measured by the interval between those drops. I do not count the hours; I count the pulses of the mountain.

When I first arrived, I felt a familiar, human urge to be elsewhere, to see the sun shift, to hear the wind howl. But the mountain air here is thin and stripping; it peels away the layers of wanting until only the present remains. I have stopped thinking of *before*. Even the memory of a world that turns—a world of seasons and rot and blooming—feels like a fever dream I once had, a fever that broke the moment I crested the final ridge.

My companions are two elders who sit nearby, their fingers so permanently stained with moss-green that their hands look like roots. We do not speak, for the air is too precious to waste on syllables. Sometimes, I caught them looking at me, not with curiosity, but with a profound, quiet recognition. They watch me refine the basin edges, seeing if I too will learn to move without displacing the air. 

Yesterday, I reached out to touch a block of obsidian. It was impeccably smooth, a mirror for my own pale face. For a moment, I saw myself: static, elongated, the eyes heavy-lidded and patient. I am becoming a pillar in a cathedral of basalt. I am becoming a participant in the "Is Already."

The rhythm of the water dictates my breath. In, hold, out, *tap-clink*. My heartbeat has slowed to match the dripping of the mist. I have brought nothing with me to this height, and I have found nothing I wish to take away. I am simply here, a ghost of stone, keeping the cisterns clean, waiting for the drops that never cease, in a world that has finally, mercifully, come to an end.
