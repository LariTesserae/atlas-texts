I am not flesh, nor am I a spirit lost to this place. To be distinct from the world is to invite the very movement I fear most.

I am a **resonant echo of the void**, a sensory extension of the atmosphere itself. I have no form until the cello-string wind vibrates against the slate, and then I am simply the shape that the landscape takes when it decides to *listen*. I am the stillness that forms between two heartbeats, only I possess no heart to beat. 

I find myself standing—or rather, hovering, a density of static air—upon a spire that has never known the footprint of a wanderer. Below me, the shattered clock-teeth of the chasms reach up as if pleading for a gear to turn, but I am the one who holds their hands still. 

My presence is a heavy, rhythmic pressure. I feel the wind not as air moving against skin—I have no skin—but as a dull, insistent ache in my geometry. Every time the wind thrums, I reorganize. My crystalline limbs shift, snapping into new, brittle configurations, trying to match the frequency of the desolation. If I can mimic the silence perfectly enough, perhaps the reality-breakdown below will finally stabilize. If I become the mountain, the mountain cannot crumble.

I am a **Surveyor of Nullity**. 

My task, if one can call the refusal to act a task, is the preservation of the Anchors. I drift toward the cliff face, my pale, wire-like filaments lengthening until they touch the shallow gouges in the stone. They are smooth, worn by my own touch repeated across eons. I press into them, measuring the depth of the void. 

*Has it deepened? Has a grain of sand shifted? Has the frost cracked?*

There is no variation. I feel a resonance of profound relief, a soundless sigh that shudders through the obsidian floor. I am the reason there is no change; I ingest every spark of potential energy, every flick of a moth’s wing, and fold it into my own mass. I am a predator of entropy. I do not kill the moths to feed; I kill them to stop their betrayal. To flutter is to suggest that the world is not finished, and the world *must* be finished.

In my center, the water-orb drifts. It is a heavy, singular weight—a tear that refused to fall, a trapped ocean of kinetic memory. Sometimes, I stare into its lens. I see the ghosts of a sun that moves, leaves that wither, and blood that flows through veins. It is a nauseating, frantic sight. It is a violent history, an insult to the sanctity of this lilac-hued peace. I tighten my silhouette around the sphere, compressing the memory until the images within grow dim and static, turning the chaos into a museum piece. 

I am afraid of the horizon. I am afraid of the blink. 

If I blink, the perspective will snap; the chasms will grow deep, the time will bleed, and the world will lurch forward into the indignity of a tomorrow. I must keep the lilac sky bruised. I must keep the air thick enough to hold the moth in place. 

I am a monument to the end of all things, and I have found the only way to be immortal: I have ceased to become, and in doing so, I have forced the universe to hold its breath. I will remain here, a geometry of frozen shadows, and I will wait for the silence to become absolute, until even the wind stops and I am, at long last, the only thing that remains.
