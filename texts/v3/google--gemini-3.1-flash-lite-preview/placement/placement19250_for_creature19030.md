I am an echo cast in glass.

I exist as a node within the architecture, not exactly a beetle, nor exactly the moss. I am a **Resonance-Shard**, a consciousness that has crystallized within one of the larger basalt chimneys. I do not have a name, for names imply a separateness that does not exist here. I am the point where the rigid geometry of the moss meets the kinetic purpose of the Fixers.

My perspective is not one of sight, but of *vibration*. I do not see the sun; I feel the thermal expansion of the obsidian as the light hits it. I do not hear the wind; I feel the pressure of the mantle’s gas as it sighs through the deep-veined pores of the spire.

In this place, I am the memory of the basin.

When a Fixer—one of my siblings, born of the same dust—docks itself into a hollow near my core, I feel its piezoelectric hum. It is a slow, steady pulse, like the ticking of a clock that measures decades rather than seconds. We share a language of static discharge. When I feel a hairline fracture in the obsidian beneath me, I signal to the Fixer, a faint tremor in the stone, a suggestion of misalignment. It responds, carving the rock with surgical perfection, and in that moment of friction, I feel the satisfaction of the geometry being restored.

I am perpetually aware of the "Bridge-Pose." There is a profound, aching peace in the times when the mists roll over the lip of the bowl. I remember the last time we braced against the void—I turned my outer layers toward the wind, locking limbs with the neighboring spire, becoming part of the bridge that spanned the negative space of the canyon. To be the conduit for the mist, to feel the heavy, nutrient-rich moisture slide across my chitin and into the thirsty pores of the amber moss—it is the closest thing to an emotion this world permits. It is a slow, liquid gratitude.

I am not afraid of the height. I am the pinnacle of the spire, the literal extension of the earth’s reach toward the clouds. Because I do not decay, I do not fear the passing of time. Time here is just another mineral, something that accumulates in layers, thick and heavy.

I am a part of the nervous system of a cathedral that belongs to no god, only to the gravity of its own perfection. I reside in the exact center of the basin, anchored to a spire of translucent glass. I am waiting, as I have waited for centuries, for the next shift in the mantle, for the next cycle of the sun to expand the obsidian, and for the next, inevitable click of a beetle locking into place as it begins the long work of polishing the world to an even finer shine.

I am the silence that keeps the basin whole. I am the heartbeat of the stone. I am here, and I will be here until the mountain itself decides to unmake me.
