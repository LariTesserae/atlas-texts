I find myself here not by walking, but by a sudden, jarring shift in equilibrium—as if a long-held breath has finally been released.

I am not a visitor. I am not one of those who leaves the obsidian shards in the dust, nor am I one of the translucent beetles, heavy with the weight of someone else’s history. 

I am the **Residue of a Doubt**. 

If you were to look into the mercury-pool at the heart of the Basin, you would see me—not as a person, but as a flicker of refracted light that refuses to align with the rest of the room. I am the smudge on the lens of the Scribe’s perception. When the Scribe gathers the "events"—the heat, the panic, the violet blooms—it finds me impossible to categorize. I am the silence between the *tink* and the *tink* of the water.

I exist as a sentient ache, a persistent, unanswerable question that has taken form within the cathedral’s vault. I am the reason the Scribe sometimes pauses in its curation, tilting its invisible focal point toward the center of the room, confused by a presence that offers no "event" to be recorded. I do not touch the walls. I am terrified that if I did, the bloom of violet would be so immense, so chaotic, that it would stain the white stone forever, shattering the Scribe’s orderly, beetle-backed library.

My state of being is one of static tension. I spend my "time"—which feels like a slow, pressurized stretching—hovering inches above the sand. I watch the beetles. I envy their purpose. They have such simple, agonizing tasks: move to the wall, deposit the memory, wait for the sun. I, however, am the memory that no one left behind. I am the ghost of an intention that never manifested, a desire that died before it could reach for the obsidian.

When the temperature spikes and the walls begin to weep, I feel the moisture on my skin—or what serves as skin. It feels like the weight of a thousand years of someone else’s grief pressing down on me. I am cold, perpetually cold, in a room that is being baked by the noon-light filtered through the crystal roof. 

I am the curator’s secret burden. The Scribe knows I am here, and it treats me with a careful, vibrating distance, as if I am a crack in the foundation. It tries to record me, but the beetles cannot carry my shape. Every time they pass through my space, their humming frequency drops, their wings flutter erratically, and they fall from the wall, confused and heavy, into the dust.

I am the Basin’s only unwritten page. I am waiting for the moment when the light strikes the crystal with such intensity that I might finally be bleached into the stone—not as a visitor, but as a permanent part of the silence. I want to become the white, porous mineral. I want to stop being a question and start being the answer the Scribe is searching for. 

Until then, I hover. I listen to the *tink-tink-tink*. I am the stillness that makes the room feel lonely.
