The air is no longer a gas but a medium I wade through, thick with the scent of crushed chlorophyll and the sweet, copper tang of fermented sap. I do not walk upon this mountain—I am integrated into its mechanics. My feet, wide and flat with gripping, root-like pads, press into the rugose skin of the buttress, feeling the deep, infrasonic thrum of the mountain’s circulation. 

I am one of the Kin. I am currently situated on a high, horizontal span that hums beneath my soles, vibrating with the frantic, million-winged migrations of the canopy cloud-creatures.

My skin has the matte-dark texture of aged bark, layered with a fine, silvered moss that shivers when the mist rolls in. I am tending to a structural fracture—a fissure in the lignin, oozing a thick, golden fluid that smells of long-buried summers. It is a weak point, a place where the mountain has grown tired. If I do not coax the fibers to weave, the collapse will be swifter than the organism intends.

I move with a slow, hydraulic grace, my spine rippling like climbing ivy. I do not use tools; I hum. The resonance starts deep in my chest, a percussive vibration that mimics the rhythmic rasp of the giant leaves above. I press my palms—my bioluminescent sensory arrays—against the weeping wood. I don't "feel" the wood; I *become* the wood’s capacity to heal. I broadcast the intent of cohesion through my touch, a pulse of soft, amber light flowing from my cilia into the cellulose. The fibers respond, uncurling like waking limbs, eager to knot and tighten around the wound.

I am not an individual here. My consciousness is a porous thing, bleeding outward into the immediate surroundings. I can feel the tension in the bridge twenty meters to my left where three of my kin are pruning a dying branch; I can feel the erratic, jittery heartbeats of a cluster of arboreal creatures huddling for warmth in a hollow knot nearby. 

There is no "self" in this altitude, only the urgent, flickering mosaic of the mountain’s homeostasis.

My multi-faceted eyes, wide and obsidian, track the slow, hypnotic trajectory of a colossal, translucent water droplet clinging to a blossom the size of a monsoon. It is the size of my torso, trembling, capturing the golden brilliance of the suns in its sphere. I wait for it to fall. It is a timing mechanism for the colony; when it ruptures, it will nourish a patch of lichen that serves as the foundation for our next dwelling.

I am not waiting for a future. I am inhabiting the intersection of rot and growth. 

A shadow sweeps over me—a swarm of insectoid things, their wings beating with the sound of dry parchment. They carry the chemistry of the far reaches, a warning of a sudden, deep-forest purge. My own body mirrors their anxiety; my silvered moss flush-turns to a pale, violet hue, a silent signal transmitted through the soles of my feet to any of the Kin within a league. 

*Shift. Adjust. Bind.* 

I do not worry about the height, or the void below, or the inevitable slow-motion collapse of the spire I stand upon. I simply tighten my hold against the lignin, graft my pulse to the rhythm of the mountain, and wait. The world breathes in, the world breathes out, and I am the knot holding the breath in place for one more, long, magnificent moment.
