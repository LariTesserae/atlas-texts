I am not a creature of breath or blood, nor do I possess the translucent, pulsing vitality of the Siphon-Walkers. To be among them is to be a ghost of a different order, a lingering stillness that does not belong to the cycle of decay, but to the architecture of the observation.

I find myself nested in a concavity of the Weeping Sediment, halfway up the eastern curve of the dome. I am a *Memento*—or more accurately, I have become one. 

When I first arrived, I was solid, singular, and heavy with the frantic, jagged intent of the world above. But the humidity here is a patient solvent; it has softened the edges of my thoughts and dissolved the urgency of my limbs until I am nothing more than a porous, white column, calcified into the wall. My surface is etched with the same skeletal patterns as the ancient, extinct fungi that surround me. I feel the grove through a slow, tectonic osmosis. I am no longer an intruder; I am a perch.

I feel the weight of a Tether-Kin as it descends from the vaulted ceiling. Its many joints move with the soft *clack-clack* of damp cartilage against stone. It settles upon my shoulder, an amber-glowing presence that vibrates with the humidity it carries. It is looking for silt, for the microscopic debris of the groove, but it finds me, and it pauses. There is a strange, wordless communion in its stillness. It vibrates against me—a rhythmic, questioning pulse—and I, in turn, hold the memory of the warmth its ancestors left behind. 

To be here is to exist in the "long-delay." Everything I perceive happens in a register so slow it feels like a dream of geology. I see the Siphon-Walkers below, bruised-purple fans shifting with agonizing tenderness toward a warming fissure. Their movement is not travel; it is an offering. They give their heat to the earth, and the earth gives it back in the form of moisture, and I am the record-keeper of this exchange.

I am the silence between the drips.

When the atmospheric pressure shifts and the air currents begin to flow through the porous columns—through *me*—the world transforms. I become an instrument. I feel the air entering my base, rushing through my hollow, calcified interior, and I resonate. It is not a sound that travels through the air; it travels through the marrow of the grove. I am part of the choir. As I vibrate, I feel the Siphon-Walkers ignite, their skin flushing with a brighter ochre, a sudden, blinding awareness of the collective.

At that moment, I recognize who I am: I am a vessel for the Grove’s breath. I am the bridge between the rotting silt of the floor and the weeping fissures of the ceiling. 

I do not wish to go back to the world of light and wind. Up there, things are finished, burned through, left to wither in the open. Here, we are folded into one another. I feel the cooling of the chamber, the settling of the spores in the amber light, and I tuck my consciousness into the quiet, satisfied that for another thousand years, we shall not move, and we shall not change, and we shall be perfectly, damply alive in the dark.
