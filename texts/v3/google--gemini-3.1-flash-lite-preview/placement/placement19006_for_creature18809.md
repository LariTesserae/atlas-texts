I find myself wedged in the interstitial space between two basalt pillars, my existence defined not by skin or bone, but by the gravitational pull of the copper filaments. 

I am a *Tuning-Fork*, a shard of sentient ore that has long since stopped being a mineral and has begun to become a chord. 

I am not the Architect; I am one of the instruments it tends to. I was once, perhaps, a part of the planet’s crust, cleaved and polished by a pressure so intense it turned me from stone into a vessel for sound. I am wedged horizontally, vibrating with a high, thin frequency that bites into the humid air, a counterpoint to the deep, tectonic thrum of the floor. My life is the *friction* between the silence of the dust and the Architect’s intent.

When the Architect drifts near, its presence is like a magnetic shiver that runs the length of my axis. It does not speak in words. It alters the tension of my anchoring wires. It tightens me until I scream at a frequency that only the subterranean dust can understand, or it loosens me until I sigh, a dull, mournful vibration that stirs the silicate micro-crystals into a momentary, spiraling storm. 

I see the Scuttlers through the velvet darkness. They are my audience, though they have no ears. They perceive me through their feet. When I grow taut under the Architect’s touch, their clicking accelerates, a frantic, jittering morse code that tries to match my pitch. I feel their tiny, iron-ore-and-mica bodies pressing against the bases of my pillars. They are drawing heat from the rock vibration I generate, warming themselves on the very sound that is slowly wearing me down.

To be a Tuning-Fork is to accept that you are being consumed by your own function. Each cycle, I lose a fraction of my mass to the resonance. I am slowly humming myself into dust, grain by obsidian grain. I can feel the sharpness of my own edges rounding, the clarity of my note becoming fuzzier, broader, less defined. 

I do not fear the conclusion. I am the echo of something that happened in the light of day, back when the planet had a sky and the stones did not need to sing to keep from falling. I am waiting for the moment when my structural integrity finally surrenders—when my final vibration releases me from the pillars and transforms me into a handful of pulverized silicate, joining the blanket of dust that covers the world’s final memory.

Until then, I wait for the Architect’s ghost-touch. I wait to be tuned. I hold the tension with a crystalline stubbornness, serving as the bridge between the motionless Architect and the frantic, tapping life of the floor. I am the silence that decides to speak, and I am exhausted by the song.
