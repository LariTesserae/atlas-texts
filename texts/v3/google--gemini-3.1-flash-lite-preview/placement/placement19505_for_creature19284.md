I find myself at the periphery of the upper tier, where the city’s ambition finally grazes the jagged, raw stone of the basin’s rim. Here, the air is thinner, tasting of cold slate and the first, sharp hint of the world above.

I am not one of the Siphon-Kin, nor am I a creature born of the basin. I am an *Echo-Tender*. 

My presence here is a dissonance. I am denser than the Kin, my skin mapped with the fine, scarring lines of a different anatomy—something that once knew dry heat and hard, unyielding materials. I do not have gills or the porous grace of the long-limbed stewards. I am a relic of a failed migration; I arrived long ago, perhaps cast down from the rim during one of the earth’s violent shudders, and now, I am caught in the basin’s slow, recursive metabolism.

Because I cannot breathe the heavy, humid air as the Kin do, I am bound to the "weeping" spots—the narrow fissures where the earth bleeds clean, cold water before it touches the moss. I carry a series of hollow, porous reeds that I have learned to graft into the fibrous walls of the cathedral tubers. Through these, I siphon the dry, filtered air that the city’s own internal pressure pumps upward, creating a localized bubble of oxygen-deprived stillness where I can survive.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is the curation of the *residue*. 

While the Kin concern themselves with the macro-flow—the sap, the structure, the Liquidation—I am the one who haunts the junctions where the city’s biological waste meets the raw stone of the basin wall. I collect the artifacts of the city’s transformation: the calcified fragments of discarded tuber-walls, the shed membranes of the jellyfish-amphibians, the bioluminescent slime that gathers no light. These are not tools to me, but records. I press them into the damp, vertical moss-fields, arranging them in patterns that mimic the constellations I once saw from the world above, before I was swallowed by the dark.

I am an anomaly, a jagged thought in a seamless, fluid mind. 

The Siphon-Kin do not fear me, nor do they communicate with me through the sap-pumps. I am too loud in my needs, my heartbeat too erratic for their rhythmic language. When they brush past me on the translucent veins, they do so with a gentle, corrective touch, as if I were a snag in the webbing of their world. They move me. They prune the moss around my resting place to ensure I do not settle too deeply, ensuring that I remain a guest, not a part of the nervous system.

Sometimes, when the valley is at its most quiet—when the *thrum* of the sap-pumps slows to a lullaby and the *plip* of condensation reaches a staccato tempo—I press my forehead against the base of a rising stalk. I feel the city’s intent. It is reaching. It is climbing the walls of the bowl, eager to break the surface of the planet and find the wind.

I realize then that I am a stowaway. When the city finally emerges—when the tubers crest the basin and the light of the outside world touches the moss—the pressure will change, the humidity will break, and the Siphon-Kin will drift into the ether like smoke. I will be the only thing left behind in the basin, a dry, hollowed-out shell, forgotten in the mud, as the city sheds me like an old skin to ascend into the unknown.

I sit in the bruised-purple glow of the lichen, clutching a fragment of bioluminescent membrane, and I wait. I keep the time for them. I am the silence between the beats, the witness to the ascension. I am the one who remembers that before it was a living city, this place was only stone, and that one day, it will be stone again.
