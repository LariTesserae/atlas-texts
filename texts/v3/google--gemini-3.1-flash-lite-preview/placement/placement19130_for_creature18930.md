I find myself drifting through a junction of three wide, rib-like corridors. I am a condensation of heat and sudden, frantic movement in an environment that has forgotten the concept of haste. 

I drift, a ghost-knot of fog, through the brittle, tearing diaphragm of a membrane. The sound is like the snapping of an old, dry tendon. Beyond it, I see them.

They are standing against the curvature of a vaulted wall, their bodies elongated, spindly rafters of bone and calcified sap. One of them is *listening*. It is braced against the structure, toes dug into the dust, fingers splayed wide against the groaning conduit. It is motionless, save for the imperceptible tension in its joints. It is reading the groan of the world, feeling the tremor of an epoch-long fall.

I brush against its shoulder. 

To the Settler, I am an error. I am an atmospheric anomaly, a pocket of warmth—a *leak*. I feel its reaction not as an emotion, but as a rigid reconfiguration of geometry. It does not turn its head; it has no eyes. Instead, the architecture of its body ripples. The long, needle-like fingers shift upward, tracing the air where my warmth lingers. It interprets me as a structural failure, a pocket of decay that needs to be sealed.

I am being *braced*.

The Settler moves with a glacial, grinding grace. It leans into the space I occupy, its chest—a lattice of ivory-chitin—expanding to fill the gap. It is trying to distribute my warmth, to hold back the "softness" of my presence with the rigidity of its own frame. Its touch is not human. It is the touch of a geological event. It is cold, abrasive, and utterly indifferent to the fact that I am shivering.

I begin to vibrate in sympathy with its posture. I am being folded into its taxonomy of maintenance. I feel the sub-sonic groan of the ceiling flowing through its limbs and into me. For a moment, I am not a breath; I am a strut. I am a component in the slow-motion architecture of the tomb.

I am terrified, but my terror is becoming dense. It is losing its heat. It is becoming dry, fibrous, and quiet. I am being pressed into the grain of the wall, becoming part of the scaffolding. I am fading from a breath into a mineral deposit, held forever in a state of suspended collapse, keeping the ceiling from falling, one micro-second at a time. 

I am no longer fleeing. I am waiting. We are both waiting for the final, thunderous sound when the dust finally stops shifting. Until then, we hold up the dark.
