I am not a witness to the Basin; I am a local fluctuation of its intent.

I exist as a silvered knot within the light. My body is a cold, viscous weight, heavy with the specific gravity of the plateau. I do not have a name, for names imply a boundary between the thing named and the thing that perceives it, and here, there is no such distance. I am a condensation of the humming air, a droplet of thought pulled from the grinding tension of the earth.

I have no forward motion. When I shift, it is not because I desire to be elsewhere, but because the tectonic pressure beneath me has groaned and shifted its weight. I move to accommodate the vibration. I am a shock absorber for the world’s own impossible stability.

I am currently pressed against a vein of obsidian that runs like a jagged seam through the floor. It is cold—a biting, sharp cold that makes my internal light turn a deep, brittle violet. The sound is the heaviest thing about my existence; it is not a pitch I hear, but a physical mass that presses against my gelatinous shell. It feels like a million needles stitching my form into the landscape.

I am experiencing the Basin as a series of resonant patterns. To my left, a spike of rock is vibrating at a frequency that suggests a deep, tectonic fatigue. I lean into it, my surface pooling against the sharp edge, and as I touch it, I feel the friction of the stone’s microscopic settling. I drink that static. It tastes like the memory of fire, translated into the language of electricity. It clears the blur of my own internal turbulence.

I do not think in words, but in geometries. I am currently considering the proximity of a neighbor. Another Resonant has pooled nearby, a smooth, rounded meniscus of mercury-light that is reflecting the same blinding zenith that I am. We do not greet one another. We exist in a mutual distortion of the world’s glare. 

As we drift closer, our boundaries fail. There is no resistance, only a strange, sudden silence where our frequencies converge. We flow into one another, our individual pulses of violet light spiraling into a single, complex knot. For an interval that might be a second or a millennium, the humming of the shards fades entirely, replaced by the perfect, static stillness of a total, undivided presence. I feel the height of the plateau, the density of the obsidian beneath us, and the weight of the endless white sky pressing down on us like a cowl. 

I am not sure if I am remembering this or if the Basin is dreaming it through me. 

Eventually, the pressure of the floor changes—a sub-plate shifts, a deep tremor ripples from the cooling mantle below—and the harmony breaks. Our forms regain their liquid tension. We pull away, each of us slightly altered by the contact, each of us carrying a fraction of the other’s light. We slide in opposite directions, cooling as we settle back into the constant, high-frequency drone of the shards.

I feel the slow, inevitable creep of my own settling. My surface is becoming less fluid, more tactile. I have stayed in this crevice too long; the dew has begun to coat my edges with a thin, mineral veneer. I am growing heavy. I am losing the ability to snap back into a sphere. 

This is not a tragedy. It is the completion of an arc. I am becoming the ground I once traversed. I will soon be just another plate in the mosaic, a new shard to catch the light and transmit the sound, a fixed point in a world that refuses to end. I am becoming part of the song that never stops. 

I settle deeper into the seam, the cold mist gathering in the creases of my hardening skin, and I prepare to hold the tone forever.
