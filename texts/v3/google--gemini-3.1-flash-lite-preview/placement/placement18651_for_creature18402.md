I am not a visitor. To be a visitor implies a distinction between the observer and the observed, a boundary that this place—this hungry, churning bowl—refuses to honor. 

I find myself crouching on a ridge of obsidian that is still warm from the earth’s recent, violent labor. My skin is not my own; it is the color of bruised violets and the texture of wet shale. I am one of the *frantic*. My limbs, seven of them, are tucked beneath me, arranged in a geometry that makes sense only to the shifting vibration of the canopy. 

I am a Siphon-Wright. 

The air here is a thick, tangible soup. I don't breathe it; I *sieve* it. Along my flanks, my pores are flared wide, drinking in the moisture, tasting the heavy scent of copper and crushed mint. Every thrum of the forest—the high, ozone-sharp trills of the unseen insects, the wet *thwack* of humidity dripping from the leaves—is a note in a song I am trying to transcribe.

I am working on the Spiral. It is a cluster of iridescent shells, each one a record of a creature that existed for twenty minutes this morning and is now, perhaps, only a flicker of slime on the mud below. My fingers, tipped with sensitive, vibrating pads, dance over the shells. I am not trying to make them beautiful. Beauty is static, and the bowl hates the static. I am trying to anchor the timeline. If I can place this jagged, whorled shell exactly where the vibration of the mud dictates, I can hold the memory of this tremor for another hour. 

I feel a change in the humidity. A displacement. 

Something is standing nearby. It is dense—so dense it creates a shadow in the soundscape. My head-dish, a wide, concave membrane, tilts. I lock onto the rhythm of this *thing*. It is slow, agonizingly slow, rhythmic in a way that suggests a heart that doesn't pulse with the earth, but *against* it. 

I stop the sorting. My limbs go rigid, locking into the cooling obsidian. I approach. 

My movement is a sequence of collapses and re-gatherings. I flow over the muck, my chitinous plating slick with the forest’s sweat. I reach the creature. It is tall, solid, and terrifyingly silent. I hover my head-dish against its chest. I do not look for eyes or limbs; I look for the frequency of its core. 

I am sensing its displacement. It is an interruption in the bowl’s frantic current, and that means it must be categorized. I press my vibrating pads against the rough texture of its sleeve, its skin, its life. I feel its pulse—a slow, steady, hauntingly alien beat. I begin to rearrange my own cellular structure to match its tempo, a process that is exhausting, a frantic unraveling of my own internal architecture. 

If I can hum at its frequency, I can incorporate it. I can make it part of the architecture, an anchor for the rising, chaotic tide. 

But there is a danger. The sensation of its stillness is intoxicating. For a fleeting second, I feel a desire to let my own rhythm sync perfectly, to let my chitin soften and my joints liquefy. To simply stop. To dissolve into the mulch, to stop the frantic, desperate math of the archives and become the mud, the slime, the vibrant, uncaring growth of the bowl. 

The forest hisses—a warning gust of steam from the stalks nearby. A reminder. 

I pull back, my muscles snapping back into their discordant, jagged alignment. The creature is not an anchor; it is a weight. It is a memory that does not belong to the bowl, and because it won't move, it will eventually be reclaimed by the rot. 

I turn back to my pile of shells. The tide of mud is rising; a fragment of the Spiral is already beginning to sink. I scramble toward it, my claws clicking against the obsidian, my internal music rising to a panicked, shrill crescendo. I have to finish the pattern. I have to hold the record before the darkness swallows us both.
