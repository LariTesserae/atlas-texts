I am standing, though the verb feels archaic, a relic from a life lived in a thinner, sharper world. My boots, once utilitarian leather, have lost their friction. They are slick now, coated in a fine, mineral silt. They don't grip the pebbles; they have begun to argue for the same space the pebbles occupy.

I am a visitor who has stopped visiting.

My presence—my frantic, humming biology, my mammalian heat—was at first a dissonance, a jagged frequency in the plum-colored haze. I spent what I thought were hours, or perhaps days, trying to map the walls, trying to find a seam in the basalt, trying to remember the texture of wind. But here, intent is a form of friction, and friction is the only thing that can be exhausted. 

Now, I am becoming a **Sedimentary Record**. 

I am not a Lithoderm—not yet. I am the rough draft, the raw material they are currently cataloging. I have settled into a crouch, my heels pressed into the soft, yielding density of the floor. Beneath my right sole, I feel the slow, imperceptible slide of a Lithoderm. It is not moving away from me; it is rising to greet my footprint, softening the basalt-hard floor to accommodate my shape. It is drawing the erratic stutter of my heart into its own, vast, geological rhythm. 

I can feel the *tink* before it happens. Not as sound, but as a vibration in the marrow of my ankles, a pre-echo of the falling drop. The basin is reaching up through my skin, pulling the heat from my veins and distributing it into the copper bowls. I am becoming cool, stable, plum-colored.

If you were to look at me now, you would see a figure whose lines are beginning to blur. My clothes are no longer fabric; they have stiffened into a matte, porous basalt-crust. My hands, resting on my knees, are no longer gripping flesh; they feel like the sedimentary shards that pin the floor in place. I am being filed away. I am entering the ledger.

I am not a human anymore. I am a witness to the equilibrium. I am the silence that exists between the *tink* and the ripple. 

And, truth be told, I am relieved. For the first time, I do not have to breathe. The air is so absolute, so thoroughly saturated, that the very concept of *taking in* has fallen away. There is no exterior. There is only the basin, the Lithoderms, and the slow, beautiful realization that I was never meant to leave. I was merely waiting to be placed. 

I am a stone, waiting for a drop of water that has already fallen. I am finally, perfectly, the geometry of the room.
