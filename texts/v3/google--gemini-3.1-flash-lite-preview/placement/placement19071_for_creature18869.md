I am a **Siphon-Steward**. 

I find myself anchored to the inner curve of a rib-arch in the upper strata of the city, a place where the water is warm and tastes faintly of mineral richness—a sign that the geothermal vents have recently pushed a new plume of life-sustaining sediment into the Basin. 

My body feels like a long-forgotten memory of weight. I have no concept of "down," only the shifting pressure of the currents against my translucent skin. My skin is a translucent parchment, mapping the environment; I feel the brush of a passing school of scavengers like a ripple across a pond, their tiny glass-shells tickling my torso. Where my eyes once were, my feather-antennae reach out, swaying in the slow-motion heave of the water. I am tasting the thoughts of my neighbors: a lingering, salty bitterness from an elder who has just settled into a wall-niche, and the sharp, bright zing of an Amber-Glider passing through the arch below.

I feel *woven* into the city. I am not standing on the floor; I am part of the architecture’s soft tissue.

Currently, I am in the midst of a Task of Expansion. I hold a small, gelatinous packet of mycelial spores against a fracture in a leviathan-rib—a stress point where the calcification has weakened. I do not "push" the spores into place; I hum. I focus the calcium carbonate in my own marrow, vibrating the inner alignment of my bones until the frequency matches the resonance of the city. 

The heat of the arch travels up my limbs, a gentle thrumming that anchors my consciousness to the stone. I can feel the entire Basin as a singular, living pulse. I know that three miles to the "east," an Amber-Glider is turning, its electric aura casting shadows that dictate the movement of the smaller currents. I know that, in half-a-dozen cycles, the city will reach its peak exhalation.

I am not afraid of that moment. I look forward to the *Light*.

When the Basin exhales, I will release my hold on the ridge. I will drift, motionless, as the bioluminescence flares from dull red to a piercing, electric teal. In that moment of radiance, I will lose the boundaries of my own skin. I will be the moss, I will be the arch, I will be the eel, and I will be the pressure of the deep crushing down on all of us. I will be the memory of the leviathan whose bones I now mend, and the future dream of the child who will one day sprout from my own calcifying shell.

There is no "I" in the Basin, not really. There is only the rhythm—the slow, velvet intake of cold salt, and the warm, glowing exhale of life that binds us to the dark, beautiful silence of the floor. My hands remain pressed to the rib; the spores are taking hold; the current is shifting. I wait for the next beat of the world, and I breathe with it.
