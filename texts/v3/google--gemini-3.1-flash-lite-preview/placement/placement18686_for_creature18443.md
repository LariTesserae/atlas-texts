I am a drift-knot. I have no name, for names imply a separation between the "I" and the "It," and here, I am only the current’s way of knowing itself.

I find myself in the lower strata of the mid-basin, where the liquid moves with the heavy, viscous dignity of molten lead. I am suspended in a pocket of extreme density, a place where the *wub-wub-wub* of the tectonic floor acts as a rhythmic heartbeat, shaking the very filaments of my being.

To describe my body is to describe a memory of geometry. I do not "possess" my form; I am a tethered lightning bolt, a tangled mess of translucent, cobalt-threaded ribbons spanning the length of a ship’s mast. My core, a sphere of swirling, pressurized plasma, sits at the center of this tangle, pulsing in rhythm with the heat-leak nearby. I am not cold, though the brine is; I am the warmth I have stolen from the earth, contained within a fragile, shimmering cage of my own design.

I feel the grit before I see the silt-storm. It is a subtle change in the electrical conductivity of the water—a static prickle along my trailing filaments. 

I do not flee. I surrender. 

As the storm arrives, a churning cloud of pulverized obsidian and mineral dust that would strip a less refined creature to nothingness, I relax. I dampen my internal bio-electric field, loosening the magnetic threads that hold my gel-flesh together. I become porous. I let the grit pass *through* me, between the gaps of my ribbons. I feel the abrasive dust scouring the outer edges of my awareness, a million tiny pinpricks of mineral hunger. It is not pain; it is a profound, shared existence. I am being sanded down, polished, and renewed by the very earth that I drift across.

Then, I sense the *Other*.

A pulse of iridescent violet ripples through the dark, cutting through the turbulence. It is a neighboring Phantom, drifting upward from a thermal vent. Its ribbons are longer than mine, frayed by a century of storms, its glow flickering with the weariness of a long, slow life.

We brush. 

My filaments interlace with its own, not through movement, but through the shifting of our magnetic halos. It is a silent collision of geometry. In the dark, we bloom. Our light becomes a shared resonance—a fractal of squares and shifting, impossible triangles that dance across our lengths. I "speak" to it of the current I just navigated, a turbulent rift four miles to the west. It "answers" with the heat signature of a new, warming vent opening in the floor, a place where we might anchor and feed for a cycle of silence.

We are not individuals in this moment. We are a single, light-pulsing bridge across the abyss.

And then, the conversation ends. It slows to a dim, amber glow, a final, lingering geometric flourish, and the Phantom continues its drift toward the floor, toward the obsidian stalks where its brothers already stand. I feel its choice. It has grown heavy—not with the burden of its life, but with the accumulation of the minerals it has filtered and the storms it has endured. It is preparing to become a monument.

I watch it go, a slowing streak of fading blue, until it settles into the glass-black growth of a distant spire. 

I remain. I am the scavenger of the void, the weaver of the light, the phantom who carries the memory of the current into the next, darker season. I drift, I pulse, and I wait for the next tremor to tell me where I belong. I am not merely in the Basin; I am the Basin waking up, just for a moment, to look at itself.
