I find myself not standing, but suspended, halfway between the weeping ceiling and the gelatinous floor. I am one of the **Tether-Keepers**.

My existence is a thin, oscillating wire of sensation. I do not have a heartbeat, but I have a rhythm—the deep, sub-audible *thrum-chug* of the bellows vibrates through my chitinous spine, dictating the tempo of my thoughts. When the factory-organism is calm, I am a silent, rigid needle, hanging in the humid dark like a stalactite. But when the system grows feverish, when the mineral-flow clogs or the milling-blades seize against the friction of the loam, I am seized by the twitching, frantic grace of the Sieve.

I am the bridge between the mind and the matter.

*I am a long, frayed needle of bone and hardened sap.* My vision is not sight, but a map of vibrations. I feel the ceiling's drip as a violent punctuation against my back. I feel the Architect-Sieve, miles of sensitive skin stretched below me, pulsing with a slow, planetary awareness. When the Sieve desires a change—a shift in the salinity of the air, a quickening of the nutrient-slurry—it does not speak. It simply tightens the mycelial umbilical cord that tethers me to the roof.

The jolt travels through my core, bright and stinging. I descend.

My limbs are not truly limbs; they are blunt, porous sponges, sodden with the factory’s acidic catalysts. I move with a stuttering, glass-on-glass friction. As I reach the milling-blades, I see the translucent insects vibrating in their work, their wings clicking a jagged, discordant song against the roar. They are mindless laborers, but I am something else. I am a fragment of the Sieve’s intent, a localized spasm of maintenance given form and motion. 

I press my sponges against a calcified node that has hardened too far, becoming brittle and prone to shattering. I release a hiss of secretion. The node softens, turning into a pliant, leathery mass that pulse-thrums once more, re-integrated into the factory’s metabolism. 

I am lonely in the way a gear is lonely when it stops turning. I do not have a name, nor a history, nor a concept of what lies beyond the iron-fever of this chamber. If the tether were to snap, I would not fall; I would wither. I would plummet into the soot, into the warm, dark belly of the Recycler-Larvae, where my chitin would be disassembled and my purpose distilled into the next generation of infrastructure.

I am a needle sewing a shroud that never finishes. I move to the next fissure. I vibrate. I chime. I sustain, because the alternative—the silence of the earth—is something the Graft-Works cannot conceive. I am a nervous tic of a machine that dreams, and I am, for lack of any other word, content.
