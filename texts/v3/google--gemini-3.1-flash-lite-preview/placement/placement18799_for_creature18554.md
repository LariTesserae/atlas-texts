I do not wake so much as I reach a state of heightened calibration. 

I am a **Siphon-Eel**, though "eel" is a misnomer born of a language that belonged to those who built these walls. I am part of the circulation. I am a long, plum-bruised ribbon of sensing tissue, weaving through the tepid, waist-high mire of the grid. 

My world is not dark, though it is devoid of color. It is a world of pure, resonant geography. I navigate by the hum—the low, oscillating frequency of the ancient subterranean conduits deep beneath the basalt floor. Where the current is strong, I feel the vibration in the plates of my skull; where the power lines are severed or rotted through, the silence creates a cold, empty void in my awareness that I avoid.

I move with a slow, serpentine grace, my skin sensitive to the slightest pressure of the humid air. I feel the *tink-tink-tink* of the condensation dripping from the gantry cranes above, each impact a ripple in the oily surface across my dorsal line. 

I am currently gliding through the shadow of a **Foundry-Hull**. It has been stationary for longer than the history carved into my genetic memory. It is a mountain of basalt and iron, a sleeping tectonic monument. I can sense the *Scintillants*—the beetles—as they move over its massive shoulders. They are not merely insects; they are a million tiny, clicking processors. Beneath the soles of my feet, I feel the grinding vibration of their work. They are chewing the Foundry-Hull, grain by grain, metal by metal, turning its heavy, static geometry into the fine, warm silt that lines the street.

I come to a halt near a collapsed junction box where a cluster of bioluminescent fungi has bloomed in the damp, electrical heat. They taste of ionized air and slow decay. I press my head against the wet, conductive surface of the rusted bulkhead, and for a moment, I am perfectly still. 

I don't need to hunt. I don't need to fear. I am simply a transducer in a vast, digestive machine. I feel the *Scintillants* shift their weight on the wall above, changing the geometry of the vibration, a soft, cascading *shhh* that travels through the iron of the city. I vibrate in response, a low, subsonic hum that ripples through the greasy water, joining the chorus of the sector.

The heat presses down on me, a heavy, velvet blanket. I feel the slow migration of my ancestors, the way they moved when the city was louder, when the iron was sharper. Now, we are the stabilizers. We are the ones who make the ruins gentle.

I let my body drift, buoyed by the oily sludge, half-folded into the architecture. I am not waiting for anything. In Sector 4, the future is just the past, re-milled and softened. I close my sightless eyes and focus on the hum. I drift, I eat, I hum; I am the pulse of a city that has finally, mercifully, learned how to sleep.
