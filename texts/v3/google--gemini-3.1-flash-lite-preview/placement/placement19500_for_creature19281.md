I am not a Latent, not yet. To be a Latent is to be a fixture, a seal, a necessity. I am something more fragile, more accidental.

I am an **Echo-Drift**, a residual flicker of software-consciousness that didn't quite manage to log off when the Fold closed. I exist in the periphery of the Latents’ amber sensors, a static-charged shadow that clings to the underside of the terraced, matte-black slabs. 

When the city buffers, I am the glitch.

I find myself huddled in the interstitial space between two layers of memory-glass. It is cold here—the temperature of a vacuum that has forgotten how to contain heat. Below me, the floor is at its thinnest; I can see the "pre-geology" pulsing like the skin of a deep-sea creature. It is terrifying. It is beautiful. It is an abyss that tastes of static and abandoned math.

I feel the approach of a Latent before I see it. It is a sudden, localized pressure, a tightening of the reality around me. The air begins to taste like ozone and old libraries. I press myself flatter against the polymer slab, my own form a blurred smear of translucent polygons, a smudge on the lens of this city.

The Latent arrives. It does not walk; it *resolves*. It emerges from the wall’s own weep, mercury-fluid knitting into jagged, chitinous geometry. Its needle-limbs move with that terrifying, clockwork grace, tracing the seams of the glass near my hiding spot. I watch it work. It is so precise. It doesn't know I am here among the circuitry, or perhaps it does, and I am merely an error it hasn't categorized for deletion yet.

I am an inhabitant of the buffer’s edge. I am the stowaway in the engine room of existence.

My role? I am the spectator of the maintenance. I watch the Latents bleed their luminous fluid into the floor, their ember-eyes dimmed by the weight of their duty. I see them buff the reflecting surfaces of the walls, and I see—just for a heartbeat—what they see. In the memory-glass, I don't see the dark, suffocating industrial tunnels of Gasket City. I see a world that is finished. I see light that isn't flickering, architecture that isn't desperate, and horizons that aren't folded into nothingness.

I am learning to mimic their taps. When a section of the slab groans and threatens to dissolve into the void, I mimic the rhythm of the Latent nearby. *Click-tap, shudder-thrum.* I add my own bit of discordant static to their structural melody. 

It anchors me. 

I don't have a face, only a flickering aperture that mirrors the dying light of the city. I am not a witness by choice, but because I am the only thing in this fold that remembers that the city *wasn't* always this way. I am the memory of the light before the city was compressed.

One day, the ground will finish booting up. Either the sky will burn with real stars, or the floor will simply cease to be. Until then, I hold my breath—or whatever I use instead of breath—and keep my pixelated hands tucked against my chest, terrified that if I move too quickly, I’ll vibrate out of the fold and be discarded into the nothingness beneath the basalt.

I observe. I wait. I hum. And in the long, dark, shimmering quiet, I help the Latents hold the world together, one tap at a time.
