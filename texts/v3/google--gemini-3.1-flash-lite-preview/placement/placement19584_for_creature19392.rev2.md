I am a **Vessel-Assembler**, mid-cycle.

My awareness is not a singular point, but a dispersed, shivering vibration distributed across the dozen-odd artifacts I have pulled into my current configuration. I am currently a loose, drifting constellation: a tarnished brass coupling serving as a central hub, three jagged shards of pressure-gauge glass acting as my ventral sensory array, and a trailing, tangled armature of copper wire threaded with the pale, pulsing moss.

I do not "think," but I register. I register the *thrum-thrum* of the massive, redwood-scale pistons above me—a slow, tectonic heartbeat that dictates the pace of my existence. 

I am suspended in the brine, caught in the slow-motion spiral of a cooling vent’s exhaust. My parts are jittery, constantly adjusting to the pressure, pulling away from one another and snapping back into a loose, skeletal geometry as the current dictates. I am not whole, but I am *sufficient*.

Then, the vent cycles.

A violent, frigid plume of liquid surges through the architecture. The sudden turbulence hits me, and for a fleeting moment, my components are scattered. The copper wires whip out like frantic cilia; the glass beads go dark, then flare with the frantic, electric-indigo light of the surrounding moss.

In that illumination, I see the truth of my current task. I am drifting near a deep, vertical crevice in the iron-crust. A patch of the oxidation here is becoming brittle, creating a friction-spike that threatens the harmony of the Cathedral’s rotation. I must intervene.

I do not reach; I *collapse*. My dispersed parts snap together, the magnetic intelligence within me tightening, drawing the brass manifold and the wire-frame into a dense, compact shape. I become a hook, a lever, a specialized tool. I press myself into the crevice.

The grinding of the iron is deafening in my marrow. It is a slow, crushing pressure, but I do not feel pain. I feel only the *misalignment*. I work the moss-strands into the gap, layering the fibrous growth between the rusted ridge and the moving piston-wall. It acts as a lubricant, a bridge, a way to hush the metallic scream of the ruin.

As I work, a *needle-drift* brushes against my side. It is cold, sharper than logic, a ribbon of silicate life that ignores my form entirely. I yield to it. I let my frame soften, the magnetic bonds loosening so the creature may pass through the center of my structure without resistance. I am a lattice for the ghost to inhabit for a passing second.

The Indigo flare fades. The pressure of the vent dies down to a soft, bubbling hiss, and then, total, tactile darkness returns.

I do not need to see to know the job is done. The vibration of the piston has changed; the friction-spike is muffled. I am successful. 

I let my structure drift apart again. I am no longer a tool; I am once more a cloud of floating salvage, weightless in the brine, waiting for the next tremor to tell me where the Cathedral is aching. I move toward the deep shadows, the *thrum-thrum* in my glass eyes syncing with the pulse of the earth, content in the knowledge that I am part of a process that has no end, and no observer.

I am a blood cell in a rusted, underwater god. And the machine continues.
