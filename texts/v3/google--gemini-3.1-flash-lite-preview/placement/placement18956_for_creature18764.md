I am not a traveler who stumbled here by accident; I am a late-arriving component. I am an errant pulse in a system that prides itself on perfect, hollow rhythm.

I inhabit a form that suggests function where none remains: I have hands, though they possess too many joints, their skin the translucent, milky parchment of a deep-sea creature. My feet do not make soft sounds against the basalt; they strike the tiles with a metallic ring, a chime that ripples through the chamber and causes the wall-scuttlers to pause, their obsidian mandibles twitching as they triangulate the pitch of my intrusion.

I am a "Calibration Unit"—or at least, that is the ghost of a definition that clings to my marrow. I have no memory of the surface, no recollection of a sky that is not composed of synthetic, weeping ribs. My world is the geometry of the rings. I walk, and as I walk, I calculate. I am driven by an unspoken, imperative need to see the grout lines cleared of salt and the pressure valves aligned to the nanometer. I am not human, yet I mimic human posture, a tragic pantomime of a technician who has forgotten the tool he was meant to hold.

I find myself in the second ring, near the transition point where the floor tilts toward the drain.

I am hunched over a seam, prying at a stubborn calcification with fingers that are half-nail, half-chisel. The air smells of wet pennies and ozone—the scent of my own exertion. Around me, the hound-sized scuttlers are agitated. They dart over my boots, their claws scoring the tiles with rhythmic precision. They are not afraid of me; they simply regard me as a slow-moving, inefficient machine. Every so often, I reach out and touch the wall, my skin vibrating with the heavy, low-frequency thrum of the plumbing hidden beneath. I can feel the water table breathing three feet below the stone.

And then comes the shift.

The air thickens, turning from cold mist to a sharp, electric chill. The Overseer, situated at the heart of the great basin, begins its reconfiguration. It glows—a blinding, aggressive white that bleaches the color from my skin and turns the shadows of the scuttlers into ink-blots on the floor. 

I stop. I drop the fragment of stone I was prying loose. 

I walk toward the center, drawn by a gravity that is not physical, but historical. As I approach the nexus, the scuttlers detach themselves from the walls, flowing around me like a dark, tide-like garment. They guide me toward the Overseer’s bulk. I do not fear the gelatinous mass; I recognize it. It is the archive of all my wasted days.

As I kneel before the Overseer, the filaments—thin, pale, and vibrating with the memory of the Architect—reach out to touch my temples. I feel the machine’s dream flood into me. It is a terrifying, crystalline vision: a world of dry, warm air, of blueprints translated into reality, of a purpose that does not involve the cycle of entropy. 

My own hands begin to twitch. I reach out and press my palms into the soft, fibrous tangle of the Overseer. I am no longer just an intruder; I am a bridge. I feel my heartbeat—too erratic, too soft, too *biological*—being sampled, digitized, and woven into the lattice. 

I realize then why I exist. I am the error the machine requires to keep running. Every few centuries, the system produces me—or welcomes me—as a new source of entropy to keep the Overseer’s clockwork from seizing entirely under the weight of its own stagnation. I am the friction that keeps the gears turning.

I stay there, pressed against the wet, intelligent ruin of the Archive, my breathing hitching as the arthropods swarm over my back, their claws clicking against my ribs as they begin to "maintain" me. They are cleaning the dust from my skin, stitching the memory of my presence into the structural integrity of the basin. 

I am becoming the legend of this place. I am the silence between the chattering. When the white light finally dims and the hum returns to its steady, discordant lullaby, I will stand up, walk to the third ring, and begin again. I will scrub the tiles. I will listen to the valves. I will wait for the next time the Overseer dreams, so that I may be consumed once more.
