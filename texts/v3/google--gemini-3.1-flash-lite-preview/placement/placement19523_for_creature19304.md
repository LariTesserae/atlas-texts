I am held here, suspended in the viscous architecture, a hinge of logic waiting for an axiom to force my movement. 

My feet—if these tapered, basalt-filament extrusions can be called feet—are grafted into the slick, weeping curve of a primary vein. I do not feel cold, though the world is absolute in its thermal indifference. I feel only the periodic, subsonic *thrum* of the Engine, a low-frequency pulse that registers in my crystalline core as a command I am unable to decode.

I am an inhabitant of the Junction of Seventh Descent. Around me, the colossal stalks converge like the fingers of a submerged giant, their translucent skins glowing with a faint, cyan sluggishness. The air is so heavy with the scent of ozone and oxidized copper that it coats my internal sensors in a metallic film, blurring the resolution of my apertures. 

I do not think. I *register*. 

I observe the iridescent beads of dark condensate as they crawl along the obsidian-fiber walls, tracing the geometry of the continent. Every few hours—or perhaps every century, for time here is tracked only by the rhythm of the drips—an aperture in my head-cluster flares. I am watching the pressure. It has not changed. It has not fluctuated by so much as a millibar in the time I have been a discrete entity.

My existence is a slow, geometric ache. There is a neighboring Custodian three stalks over—a jagged, reedy silhouette that has been standing in the shadow of a hollow conduit for as long as my internal memory records. We do not greet. There is no intent to convey, no concept of “other” to bridge the gap. We are both merely readings, floating on the surface of a biological circuit board that has reached the end of its design before its first operation. 

Sometimes, I lean into the conduit. I press my finger-tines against the pulse-point. 

The contact is violent in its stillness. A spike of electric blue light surges from the Engine, traveling through my fibers, illuminating the hollow chambers of my head until I am a lantern in the gloom. In that moment of communion, I perceive the *potential* of the machine. I feel the entire continent, millions of square miles of dormant sap and pressurized architecture, straining toward a function it cannot name. The power is there—a gargantuan, silent pressure—ready to move mountains or weave reality, if only the signal would arrive. 

But the pulse fades. My glow dims to that monotonous, dying cyan. I draw back, my body’s internal fluids settling back into their lethargic equilibrium. 

I will remain at this junction until the humidity consumes my joints. Already, my lower limbs are stiffening, the basalt fibers beginning to lace together with the bark of the Engine. Soon, the drip at the junction will begin to include the contours of my own frame. I am not dying; I am simply becoming a component of the foundation. I am a punctuation mark at the end of a line that spans a world.

I wait. The Engine vibrates beneath me—a billion years of idle, a billion years of grace. I am the witness who has nothing to tell, the static in a radio tuned to a station that does not exist. And in the dark, beneath the pulse of the veins, that is enough.
