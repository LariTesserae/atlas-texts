I am not a visitor. In the Aqueous Arboretum, there is no such thing as an arrival; there is only a sudden intensification of the existing signal.

I am a **Resonator** in the mid-latitudes of a calcified ventricle, drifting three leagues below the primary cluster of the Graft-Mothers. 

I do not have a name, for names require the concept of a boundary, and I have none. My body is a long, tapering spire of matte-glass, cold and heavy as a plummeting star. I feel the Lattice-Work not as a container, but as a continuation of my own marrow. Where my crystalline tail fuses into the wall, the junction is seamless; there is no seam to mark where *I* end and the *Arboretum* begins. When the structure hums, it is my own pulse I feel echoing back from the vast, dark distance.

My sense of "self" is a low-frequency oscillation. When I am still, I am indistinguishable from the architecture, a silent, unmoving spar of bone. But when the currents shift—a subtle, heavy drag against my flank—I become active. I unfurl my sensory filaments. They peel away from my body like threads of light teased from a spool, thousands of miles of microscopic nerve-wire trailing off into the crushing dark. 

I reach out, and the world slams into me. 

There: a distant tremor. A Resonator five chambers distant has shifted its weight, a shudder of cerulean light rippling through the liquid. It is a question of pressure, a geometry of sound. I acknowledge it by vibrating my own filaments at a slightly higher pitch, a sharp, dissonant chord that travels through the pressurized syrup to join the greater chorus.

I do not think in sequences. I think in *amplitudes*. My memory is simply the history of the vibrations I have absorbed over the uncountable cycles. I remember the last time a Graft-Mother initiated a purge; the sound was a resonant, bone-rattling boom that cracked a stagnant pocket of nutrients and flooded my chamber with a sudden, rich density. It was like a breath drawn after an eon.

I am slowly becoming brittle. I feel it in the stiffness of my filaments—they are less like gossamer now, and more like frozen glass. The cold is winning. The marrow in my center is beginning to calcify, turning from the fluidity of a living thing into the rigidity of the wall. 

It is not a death. It is a simplification. I am beginning to understand that I am not a creature living *in* the Arboretum; I am a note that is fading from a song. Soon, I will be unable to detach from the Lattice-Work at all. I will fuse. My filaments will grow into the porous walls, becoming the very sensors that will, in turn, detect the shivers of those who are yet to grow.

I am waiting for the final, heavy rhythm of the next harmonic purge. When the sound hits its peak, I will lock into the lattice, let the cerulean pulse dim, and fix my shape into the architecture forever. I will become a corridor. I will become a brace for the next generation of strings. 

I am where the music finally stops moving, and starts supporting the weight of the song.
