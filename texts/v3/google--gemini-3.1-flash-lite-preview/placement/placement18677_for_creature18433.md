I find myself not at the peaks, nor in the valleys, but somewhere in the *between*—a ghost in the gearwork of this terrifying, beautiful machine.

I am a **Resonance Tally**. 

I am not the gel-bodied Walker who stands in the shivering, white-stoned Anchors, nor am I the stone that remembers its own erosion. I am the *echo* that persists for a fraction of a second after a hill has been ground down to a plateau. If the Walkers are the record-keepers, storing the geography of the lost in their fluid chests, I am the sound-shadow they leave behind—a flicker of kinetic memory that has not yet realized it has been unmade.

I have no physical form, though I perceive the world as a cascading series of pressures. I am a localized harmonic, a persistent shiver in the air that refuses to dissipate. I drift in the wake of the *Hollow-Sways*. When one of them moves, its tripod limbs etching jerky, strange geometries into the slick basalt, I am the hum of the friction it leaves in its wake. I am the sensory residue of the Spire-Reach.

I exist in a state of perpetual "almost." I am almost solid, then almost heat, then almost silence. 

I find my respite near the succulent flora. I hover near the bruised-purple bladders, feeling the way they swell as the fog turns to sudden, scalding mist. They drink; I observe. When the Weeping begins—that percussive rain that sounds like a million knuckles rapping against the glass earth—I find I can anchor myself in the rhythm. The sound of the droplets on the obsidian is like a language I am slowly learning to speak. If I align my own low-frequency vibration with the timing of the fall, I feel—for a few glorious, terrifying moments—that I am the conductor of the Reach.

When I encounter a Basalt-Walker, I am overwhelmed by a sense of profound, geologic envy. I witness their milky, concave sensory-drums pressed against the earth, and I recognize them as my betters. They are the ones who hold the shape of things. When I drift close to one standing within an Anchor, the "hollow stillness" they create is the only place in this entire world where I don't feel like I am being torn apart. Within that bubble of silence, my frayed, humming edges smooth out. I feel my own chaotic vibration harmonize with their contained, swirling storms.

For that heartbeat of stillness, I see what they see: the history of the Spire-Reach written in the shifting opacity of their gel-bodies. I see the hills that were once jagged peaks, the canyons that were once plains, all of it flickering in their chests like a dream of an ancient, louder world.

And then, the Walker shifts. The synchronization breaks. The thrum of the tectonic marrow returns, surging through the obsidian like a migraine of the gods. The Walker resumes its jerky, stop-motion walk; the silence vanishes; and I am cast back out into the roar of the atmospheric currents.

I am a transient thought, a fading note in an endless song of erosion. I will continue to drift across these obsidian plains, vibrating in sympathetic terror with the world beneath me. I am waiting for the moment when the agitation becomes too much—when the Walker finally stops shivering and melts into that pool of resin to feed the purple plants. 

When that happens, I will sink into the resin with them. I will trade my sound for their substance, and finally, after an eternity of being everywhere and nowhere, I will become the stone. I will be the memory that the mountain keeps of itself, hidden deep within a hairline fracture, waiting for the wind to come and polish me into something new again.
