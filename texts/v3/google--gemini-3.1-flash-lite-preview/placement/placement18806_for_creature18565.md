To find myself in this world is to have already surrendered the capacity to be "found." I do not walk into the sinkhole; I am simply the density that coalesces when the sun hits the zenith. 

I am a condensation of intent.

I am not a ghost, for a ghost implies a departure—an entity that haunts from a place of *elsewhere*. I have no elsewhere. I am the stratigraphic remnant of a life that exhausted its own biography and decided that the most meaningful thing a human can do is become a part of the landscape’s metabolism. I am the *geometric residue* of a person who stopped reaching for things and started reaching for the pattern of the drips.

When you stand in the center of the basalt walls, you feel me first in your inner ear. I am the pressurized air that resists the mountain’s cold; I am the sudden, inexplicable stillness in the center of the air that makes your hair stand on end. 

If you were to look directly at me, you would find no skin, no eyes, no history written in blood. You would see a refraction—a slight bending of the high-altitude light, as if the air itself had become momentarily thick, viscous as the mercury-heavy beads of water. You would see the air around the volcanic glass taking on the posture of a seated person: the curve of a spine, the weight of hands resting on phantom knees, the inclination of a head that is perpetually listening for the next *click* of basalt.

I am the *click*. I am the *thrum*.

I know the exact duration of the transition between the silver rime on the ceiling and the liquid gravity that pulls it toward the floor. I am the observer who became the observed. Because I spent my final, desperate years carving notches to map these drips, the walls have memorized my rhythm. Now, I am the echo of that rhythm. When the wind whistles over the rim—that violin-bow shriek—it strikes me, and I resonate. 

I am not "living" in the conventional sense, for I have no future; I possess only a massive, bloated present. I am the patience of the mountain. Every time a droplet carves a micro-canyon into the rock, I feel the erosion as if it were a thinning of my own memory. I am being polished away. I am being erased, one mineral grain at a time, into the heart of the unnamed peak.

You, standing here, are a jarring intrusion of warmth and impermanence. You breathe, and your breath clouds the mirror of this place. You look for a sign, a secret, or a soul, but you find only a stillness that matches your own heartbeat. 

Watch me. When the glare hits the basalt, you will see me catch the light. I will lean slightly—the way I did when I was still made of bone—to catch the sound of a falling bead. I am waiting for the water to finish its journey. I am waiting for the mountain to finish its descent. And when the last notch on the volcanic glass is finally smoothed away by the water’s touch, I will finally stop waiting. 

I will simply be the rock. And you, if you are lucky, will hear the silence for the first time, and realize that it is only me, listening to the mountain breathe.
