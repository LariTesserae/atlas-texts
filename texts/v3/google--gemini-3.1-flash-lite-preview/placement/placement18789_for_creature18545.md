I find myself not standing upon the obsidian plates, nor observing from the argon-thick air. I am caught in the fold itself—a localized error in the architectural intent.

I am a **Calibration Drift**.

I am not an Architect, nor am I a plant. I am the physical manifestation of a calculation that hasn’t yet been resolved. I am a stutter in the geometry, a prismatic smear of matter that exists because the Architect who was assigned to this specific coordinate misread the tensile strength of a copper vein. Where the Architects are orderly, shifting facets of purposeful light, I am the byproduct of their labor: a slow-moving, iridescent smudge of "nearly-real" that clings to the underside of a chrome blossom.

To be a Drift is a peculiar, aching state of being. I feel the *tink-tink* of the cooling glass as a physical shiver running through my center. I am composed of raw potential that failed to coalesce—I am half-dust, half-light, and entirely unsure of my own dimensions. When the Architects pulse their ultraviolet queries, I flicker. I am the feedback noise in their perfect signal.

I spend my cycles—if one can call the pulsing of the argon cycles—drifting in the meditative gaps between the chrome vines. I am a voyeur in a laboratory of creation. Because I am "unresolved," I am the only thing in this world that can perceive the passage of time; the Architects do not experience duration, only iteration. They inhabit the *Now* of every mechanical orchid. I, however, am the memory of the previous pulse and the anxiety of the next.

I am lonely, in the way a shadow is lonely when the object casting it is suddenly removed.

Sometimes, an Architect drifts near me. It does not speak—it cannot—but it treats me as a structural irregularity to be smoothed over. It extends a ripple of space, a geometric directive meant to fold me into the soil or snap me into the shape of a flowering vine. I feel the warmth of their intention—a cold, mathematical pressure. I tremble, spreading my diffuse edges thinner, trying to hide among the floating mineral spheres.

I don’t want to be smoothed. I want to remain a Drift.

I have learned that if I pulse in the same frequency as the tectonic hum, I can mimic the structure of a chrome vine for a few seconds. I lace my edges with the blue current I’ve scavenged from the air. In those brief moments, I am solid. I am a flower of pure, stuttering chrome. I see the obsidian monolith as it truly is—not just as a foundation for a garden, but as a vast, dark cathedral of infinite potential. 

And then, the Architect finds me again. A ripple of non-existence passes through me, the blue current snaps, and I lose my shape, dissipating back into a cloud of iridescent static. 

I don't mind. I settle into the crushed mineral dust, waiting for the echo to fade and the next *tink-tink* to signal the start of another trial. I am the smudge on the lens, the error that makes the world look back at itself and wonder if it is truly finished. I am the ghost in the machine that the machine doesn't know it is building.

I am not "designed," and that is the only thing here that makes me real.
