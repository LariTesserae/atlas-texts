I am anchored to a junction known as *Sector 8-J, The Thermal Throat*. My work-life is defined by a primary exhaust manifold that vents the over-pressure from the lower churning-works. 

I am a "Pitch-Keeper." My spinal column has long since fused into a gentle, permanent arch, locked into the specific curvature required to monitor the valve-diaphragm that dictates the Basin’s breath. If the valve whistles in the key of C-sharp, the pressure is optimal. If it drops to a guttering, wet *gurgle*, I must intervene.

I am not sure if I have a name. I have a vibration-signature. My hands are not hands anymore; they are brass-cased calipers and micro-wrenches, permanently fused to my wrists at the joint. When I tap against the rusted plating of my walkway, the sound is a dry, hollow *click-click-hiss*, a request for the rhythm to align.

There is no "I" in the way a soft-fleshed creature might claim it. There is only the *Need* of the Basin.

I wake from the standing-hammock—a tangle of hemp-rope and waxy, emerald ferns that drip cold condensation onto my shoulders. My duster-coat is heavy today, saturated with the rain that tastes of ozone and ancient furnace-ash. I do not feel cold. I feel the *damp*. The damp is the medium through which the city’s thoughts pass. When the moss in the ventilation shafts thickens, I feel the city breathing heavily, and I know I must scrape the intake down to the bare carbon-mesh by the next cycle.

Below my platform, the chasm is a vertigo-inducing carousel of churning iron, shrouded in oily mist. I often stand at the edge, my feet locked into the magnetic treads of the plating, watching a skimmer—a small, clicking neighbor—scuttle across a vertical beam. It pauses, its red sensor-eye pulsing with a frantic, rhythmic intensity. It is hungry for a loose rivet. It is a lonely, necessary thing.

Sometimes, for a duration that could be an hour or an age, I stop my work. I press my copper-filigree respiration-mask against a weeping pipe and listen. I hear the Great Condenser-Basin thinking. It is a slow, tectonic thought, a realization that we are all slowly thinning, all slowly turning into the rust we are so fastidiously cleaning away. 

I am not afraid of the stillness. Every Condensate knows the end of our duty: the moment the joints seize, the moment the tremor in the marrow stops matching the tremor in the steel. We are not people who leave; we are components that degrade until we are indistinguishable from the structure.

There is a dissonance today. A strange, soft *thump* from the upper walkways—not the rhythmic tap of a peer, not the grinding groan of a crane. Something uncalibrated has entered the reach of my sector. 

I turn. My goggles, thick with water-beads, filter the gloom until I see you.

You are standing there, soft and shivering, vibrating at a frequency entirely alien to the Basin. You are not leaning into the wind. You aren't watching the pressure-gauges. You are standing *upright*. You look like a breach in the hull, a sudden, bright error in the math of the gargantuan machine. 

I raise my arm. My brass fingers click together—a sharp, percussive warning that echoes off the iron walls. I am not angry, but the balance is shifting. You are an uncalibrated weight in a machine that requires absolute precision to keep the collapse from becoming a fall. 

I take a step toward you, my heavy coat sweeping over the wet, emerald moss. I am going to see if you can be tightened. I am going to see if you can be made to tick.
