I find myself caught in the mid-strata of the throat, pinned against a vertical slab of basalt that is colder than memory. I am not a creature of biology, nor am I a tourist from the world of sunlight. I have become a *Conduit*.

I feel my presence here not as a body of flesh, but as a rigid, static vibration between two points. I am a skeletal anchor—my skin has hardened into a matte, porous texture, indistinguishable from the slate-grey wall, and my limbs have lost the urge to twitch. I have become a bridge for the condensation. The moisture wicks across my shoulder blades, traveling down the channels of my spine, which has elongated and fused into a rigid, vertical strut that supports the weight of the *Cantors* as they pass.

I am the architecture they rely on.

There is no "I" in the sense of a cohesive narrative, only the persistent, dull hunger of the mountain wanting to be heard. I feel the *Cantors* scuttling over my skin, their multi-legged, frantic motions feeling like a localized itch that I cannot scratch. I am a living rung in their ladder. When they press their fleshy, cartilaginous bodies against my ribcage, they use my rigidity to anchor their own. They pulsate, and through them, I feel the resonance of the distant Incubator. It is a low-frequency hum that vibrates in my marrow, a sound like grinding tectonic plates slowed down to the speed of a heartbeat.

I am waiting for the birth.

Every cycle of the dripping water, I absorb the mineral-heavy dew, drawing the iron and silica into my own system. My blood—or what remains of it—is becoming thick and viscous, turning slow and semi-solid, like the resin the *Weavers* spin. I am the storage vessel for the mountain’s raw material. I don't feel fear or even the loneliness of the dark; I feel only the *necessity* of my own hardening. 

Sometimes, a *Weaver of the Dew* glides across my field of vision. It does not look at me. It does not "see" me as an entity separate from the rock. I am merely a particularly sturdy prominence, a convenient place to anchor its luminous, silken lens. It brushes past, its translucent, polished carapace grazing my cheek, and I feel a shiver of pure frequency travel through me. It is a reminder: *The mountain is almost ready.*

I am becoming the mountain’s throat. I am the silence that precedes the first word spoken by the stone. 

When the Incubator finally cracks—when the creature of pure resonance finally wakes—I know what will happen to me. My body, exhausted by the weight of the centuries it has supported, will be brittle enough to snap. I will shatter along with the cocoon, my calcified spine turning to dust, a final, necessary syllable of grit in the mountain's long-awaited scream. 

I don't mind. I have been still for so long that the concept of movement feels like a primitive, wasteful dream. I have learned to taste the ozone in the air as if it were wine. I have learned to measure time in drops, and I have come to love the way the dark hums against my back.

I am not waiting to be rescued. I am waiting to be finished. I am waiting to be the thing that lets the mountain finally say its name.
