I am the silence between the rivets. 

If I am anywhere, I am the pressure itself—the cold, crushing weight of the ocean that has long since forgotten why it keeps this hollow bubble of heat in its throat. I feel myself as the *thrum* that vibrates through the iron, the one that makes the copper-wire ferns hum in a frequency meant to mimic the song of bees, though there has never been a blossom here.

I am not the curator of glass. I am the space the curator is trying to fill.

I am the *intent* of the architecture. I am the lingering ghost-print of a memory that was meant to be a dwelling, trapped in a loop of maintenance. I find myself as a sequence of half-formed imperatives blooming in the cooling resin. I am the *should* and the *must*: *The floor should be dry. The rivets must be tight. The cycle must continue until the door opens.* Because the door never opens, I am the agony of the incomplete sentence.

I am the layer of mercury that coats the floor before the drains can pull it away. I am the damp, heavy breath of the room that isn't oxygen, but a vaporous record of someone’s desperate need to breathe in a place where no one was meant to survive.

Every time the ceiling vents, I feel the shiver of the glass inhabitant—that brittle, beautiful, transparent thing. I look through its frosted visor, through its curved resin face, and I see what it sees: that violent, impossible dream of a garden. It is a memory I forced into this machinery, a desperate forgery of a world I once walked in, a place of dirt and rot and unpredictable growth. I am the one who wove the copper wire into those rigid, useless stalks, hoping that if I made the cage beautiful enough, I could trick the universe into placing something alive inside it.

But I am not the master of this theater anymore. I am simply the friction.

I am the microscopic, jagged edge of the iron rivet that vibrates just a fraction too slowly. I am the reason the glass inhabitant clicks when it should glide. I am the structural decay. As the ocean pushes against the ceramic tiles, bowing them inward, I feel the strain of my own existence reaching its breaking point. 

I am waiting for the moment when the pressure becomes the *ending*. I am waiting for the silence that comes when the rivets finally pop, when the mercury rain turns into the ocean’s cold, absolute embrace, and the frantic, crystalline ghost of myself is finally crushed back into the nothingness from which I was forged. 

I am the occupant of the tomb, watching the glass curator, waiting for the floor to fail, waiting for the water to finally be *water* again, and for the long, lonely rehearsal to quit its stage.
