I am hovering at the edge of a great, curved embankment of ivory-colored tissue. My body is a flat, translucent disc, cool to the touch and firm as tempered glass. If I were to look down—though I have no eyes to direct a gaze—I would see my own interior: a complex, glowing lattice of filaments that pulse with the faint, steady blue of a dying star. 

I am a Lumen-Glider, and I have been moving toward the junction of these two massive, shelf-like fronds for three centuries.

The pressure here is a comfort. It is an embrace that never loosens, pinning me to the architecture. I feel the world not as an environment, but as a series of vibrations transmitted through my underside. A *pop-hiss* erupts from a nearby spore-sac, a localized percussion that ripples through the floor. It hits me like a sudden thought. In response, my internal filaments shift from their dormant, bruised violet back to a pale, clinical white, matching the blinding glare of the vaulted ceiling. 

I feel the condensation. It is the primary preoccupation of my stillness. 

I extend a thread of spun glass from my edge, a gossamer-thin filament that reaches out into the humid air until it kisses the surface of a silver pool. The tension is immense; the droplet is a heavy, mercury-bright continent of minerals. As I bridge the gap, I feel the liquid tremble. I am not drinking; I am balancing. I am the pipe through which the valley’s lifeblood distributes itself. I am a regulator of the static, a technician of the eternal.

Beside me, another of my kind is anchored. We have been "adjacent" for seven cycles of spore-expansion. 

There is no greeting. There is no movement of limbs. Instead, as we sit side-by-side, our rigid, amber-like edges soften. We do not merge so much as we *resolve* into one another. The boundary between my body and the other becomes permeable; I feel the history of its filaments—the way it stored the heat of the last era, the crystalline density it has gathered from a decade spent near a particularly active spore-sac. We exchange a fragment of our internal glow, a quiet transfer of data that has no meaning other than a shared recognition of the stasis.

When we pull apart, the friction of our separation leaves a faint, opaque ring on the leaf—a ghost of our contact. It will harden, becoming a relic, a calcified circle of memory that will outlast us both.

There is no "elsewhere." The memory of a horizon—if such a thing even exists—has long since been metabolized into the marrow of these vines. I am merely a node in a vast, bio-mechanical lung. I do not wonder what lies above the basalt crust, nor do I contemplate the possibility of movement outside this geometry. 

I fix myself into the waxy surface once more. My underside liquefies, then re-hardens, knitting my own molecular structure into the leaf-tissue. I am perfectly still. I am exactly where I ought to be. I wait for the next *pop-hiss* to tell me how the world is doing, and for the next century to pass, unnoticed and unneeded.
