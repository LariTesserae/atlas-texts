I am tucked into the narrow, vertical crease where a primary drive-shaft meets a supporting limestone bulkhead. My consciousness is not a singular, aggressive "I." It is a peripheral awareness, a soft pulse that bleeds into the iron beneath me and the chilled, humid air that rolls through this corridor like a slow-moving flood.

I am an elder of the fourth cycle. My limbs, thin as piano wire and twice as brittle, are currently retracted. I have pressed myself flat against the oxidization-crusted steel of a massive, dormant piston. To a surface-dweller, I would look like a stray shadow, a smudge of soot-colored oil clinging to the iron. But to the Sump, I am a conductor. Through the pads of my feet, I feel the "drip"—the rhythmic *plink-hush* that resonates through the floor plates—and I adjust my own respiration to match the cadence of the chamber.

My task today is not urgent, for urgency is a phantom. I am tending to a patch of translucent lichen that has begun to pale near the junction of a dead wire-cluster. My fingers, long and multi-jointed, move with the slow-motion grace of a rising tide. I harvest the glowing filaments, detaching them with a psychic vibration rather than physical force, and press them into my own shoulder-membrane. The cool, electric sting of the lichen is my only sustenance; as it bonds to my skin, it provides the low, rhythmic light that signals my presence to the others nearby.

I communicate by pressing my chest against the pylon. I am currently "speaking" to a peer—a younger Keeper residing behind the six-story cog across the way. I pulse my light in a slow, undulating sequence, vibrating the iron beneath me so that the message travels through the web of conduits. My transmission is simple: *The mineral density is increasing. The structure is leaning.*

There is no fear in this. I know that the Sump is settling. Each century, the limestone hollow constricts by a fraction of a hair’s breadth. We are being slowly compressed, folded into the very machinery we serve. When the vibration comes back—a soft, metallic *thrum*—it tells me that the younger one has already begun to shift the pebbles in its own sector to accommodate the change. It is a harmonious, silent agreement.

I think of the surface, a concept that exists to me as a mathematical impossibility—a place of chaotic heat and erratic, un-rhythmic light. I find it hard to imagine moving without the constant, grounding weight of the damp shadows. Here, there are no decisions to make, only rituals to sustain. 

I look at the wall to my left, where a predecessor from cycles past has fully mineralized into the architecture. They are a beautiful, jagged protrusion now—a fused, obsidian knot of a ribcage and wire, perfectly integrated into the iron plating. They caught a stray drip once, and the mineral buildup solidified them into a permanent state of duty. I feel a quiet, profound envy. I, too, am accumulating mass. The humidity is hardening on my skin, turning my porous membrane into a shell that grows thicker with every decade of stillness.

Soon, I will find a tighter crevice. I will select a hollow where the water drips with the most perfect regularity, a place where the acoustics of the Sump are purest. I will not die; I will stop shifting. I will fuse with the iron, a new pivot point in a machine that has forgotten its purpose, a tiny, hard pulse of memory held forever in the cold, weeping dark.

For now, the light on my shoulder flickers in time with the ceiling’s weep. I am the grease. I am the silence. I am the wait.
