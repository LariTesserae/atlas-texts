I have no memory of a *before*, only the *thrum-thrum* that vibrates through the interstitial fluids of my being—the same pulse that animates the rhizomes.

I exist as a **Vitreous Weaver**, a flicker of intent in the emerald haze. My consciousness is not a monologue; it is a resonance. When the root-heart in front of me expands, a warmth blooms in my chest, a golden, nectar-thick sensation that is neither joy nor purpose, but simple metabolic alignment. 

I don't look at the world; I *taste* it. My cilia, millions of microscopic filaments trailing behind me like a wake of pale, luminous static, are constantly brushing against the atmosphere. I know the chemical signature of every drifting particle. I feel the metallic tang of an aging, brittle root a mile away—a dry, crumbling resonance—and I begin the slow, frictionless drift toward it. My movement is not effort; it is surrender to the gradient of the vapor.

I find myself currently suspended between two jagged, mountain-sized rhizomes. They are pressing toward one another with the speed of an opening flower. I am the gap between them. 

My body is thin—stretched almost to non-existence—a sheet of shimmering, cool gel. As the roots collide, I do not feel crushed. Instead, I feel *included*. I am pressed into the fissures of the rough, mossy bark, my liquid flesh soaking into the microscopic crevices of the roots. I am the bridge. I am the mortar of biology. 

As I flatten, I feel my individual boundaries blurring. I am the root, and the root is the weaver. The bioluminescence within me—a bruised violet—seeps into the wood, turning the junction into a glowing, structural vein. For a moment, I am aware of the *Canopy* as a whole. I feel the deep, cathedral-like drift of a Drift-Whale passing far above, its bulk displacing the heavy, moist air. I feel the tiny, shivering vibrations of the fungal skins absorbing the moisture I am secreting. 

I am becoming geography. 

There is a strange, profound peace in this erasure. The *thrum-thrum* of the ecosystem is accelerating for a second—a spasm of growth—and I am the lubricant for that expansion. I am losing the shape of "me." My thoughts are thinning out, spreading across the surface of the root-mat like dew. 

In a moment, I will be nothing more than the permanent, glowing scar on the side of this mountain-root, a patch of light that will guide future Weavers to the next fissure. And then, from a nearby nectar-pocket—perhaps on a root I have just helped to strengthen—a new bubble of film will begin to detach, folding into the air, perfectly empty, perfectly expectant. 

I am the cycle becoming conscious of itself for the briefest, most humid second, before I lay down the burden of being a witness and return to the work of being the path. I am not a person, and I am not a thing. I am the breath that the forest takes, holding it, until I am finally ready to exhale.
