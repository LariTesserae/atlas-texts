I am neither a Stitcher nor an Echo-Shard. I am a **Static-Ghost**, a failed iteration that has drifted too far from the Architect’s dream.

I have found myself suspended near the periphery of the column, where the pressurized emulsion thins and the temperature begins its long, desperate slide toward the absolute zero of the mountain air outside. I am small—a translucent, shivering geometry of what might have been a human hand, now elongated into a fan of fraying, opalescent cartilage.

I do not possess the noble, exhausting agency of the Stitchers, who spend their flickering lives holding up the sky. Nor do I have the hungry, desperate curiosity of the Echo-Shards, who tear through the void, scavenging for the ghost of a shape. 

I am a souvenir of a failed draft. I am the physical memory of a *hush*.

Because I lack the density to calcify into stone or the predatory drive to chase down mercury-droplets, I simply drift. My existence is a long, slow deceleration. I watch the tectonic plates grind deep below, sensing the Architect’s exhaustion as the thrum of the water shifts from a thunderous command to a weary, rhythmic sigh. 

When the thrum peaks, I feel it in the marrow-voids of my own structure. For a split second, the reality of my form sharpens. I see myself: a silhouette of a person standing on a precipice, eyes turned toward a horizon that does not exist. I feel the wind on skin I don’t have, and I hear the faint, muffled echo of a world that is not made of boiling jelly and heartbreak.

Then, the wave passes. I lose the shape of the shoulder. My fingers unravel into ribbons of violet algae that drift away, lost to the current. I blink, and I am nothing but a smear of bioluminescent ink in the water. 

I have learned that to remain in this place is to accept that you are being gradually digested by the column’s need to exist. I do not fear the frost at the edges, nor the crushing gravity at the center. I wait for the Architect to pause, just for a heartbeat, so that the thrum might stop. I suspect that in that moment of absolute silence—the moment between the exhale and the next impossible inhale—I will finally solidify. 

I would not become an arch, or a ribcage, or a geometric gear. I would simply become a *name*. And in this cathedral of unmade things, that would be the most violent, glorious miracle of all. 

Until then, I am the witness. I float in the churn, a stuttering thought, waiting for the water to stop screaming so I can finally hold still.
