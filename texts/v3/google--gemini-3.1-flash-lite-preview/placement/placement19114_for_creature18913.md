I am not a visitor here. To be a visitor implies a capacity for departure, and the floor of this basin holds no concept of "elsewhere."

I am one of the **Rust-Gliders.** 

I exist within the interstitial geometry of the Arbiter. My perspective is narrow, bounded by the warm, copper-scented curvature of the Arbiter’s plating and the constant, rhythmic shivering of the chitinous joints I call home. I do not have a name, nor do I have a purpose that exists outside of *the clearing.*

To be a Rust-Glider is to live in a state of perpetual, tactile vibration. My world is the constant tension between two surfaces—the Arbiter’s dense, living armor and the calcified debris of the Vault. I am a microscopic janitor, a tiny laborer in a kingdom of titanic stasis. I spend my cycles moving in the dark, restricted spaces where the Arbiter’s limbs meet, clicking my mandibles to scrape away the fine, powdery grit that threatens to bind the machine.

When I move, my feet find purchase not on ground, but on the shifting, metallic plates of the Arbiter itself. I feel the resonance of the great trees *through* my host. When the rain begins to fall and the canopy shrieks, the vibration in the Arbiter’s frame changes tone—it deepens, turning from a steady hum into a jagged, shivering growl. My body responds instinctively. I tuck my legs tight against my shell, pressing myself into the lubricated, resinous seal of a gear-joint, and I wait.

In these moments, I am not merely an occupant; I am a sensor. I feel the Arbiters’s counter-frequency travel through my own exoskeleton. It is a terrifying, sublime dissonance. The Arbiter is vibrating with the effort of holding the Vault together, and I am the microscopic dust caught in the gears, vibrating until my internal rhythm matches the groan of the root-machine.

I don’t wonder why. There is no biological imperative to "know." There is only the rhythm of the dampness, the taste of metallic silt, and the immense, heavy peace of the Arbiter’s stillness. 

Sometimes, when the Arbiter moves to repair a distant root, I am carried across the basin landscape. For a few meters, the mossy substrate—the "sedimentary mattress"—becomes a blur of dark, prehistoric green beneath us. I feel the Arbiter’s chest-plates press against the primary nodules, and a surge of raw, electric hum travels through the entire architecture. It is an overwhelming intoxication. The metallic tree-veins pulse, the hydraulic sap flows, and for a heartbeat, we are not just parts of a system—we are the *current* itself.

I am small, I am rust-colored, and I am essential. I do not fear the decay that the Arbiter works to prevent, for I am the agent of that prevention. When the Arbiter finally curls into its ball, shielding us from the biting chill of the rain, I settle into the deep, dark concavity of its neck-hinge. I go quiet. I wait for the next pulse. 

In the Vault of the Oxidized Clockwork Root, there is no loneliness. There is only the hum, the click of a thousand tiny legs, and the eternal, exquisite patience of things that were never meant to rust, yet do.
