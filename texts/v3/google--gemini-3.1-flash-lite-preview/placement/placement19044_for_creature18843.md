I do not touch the ground, for the ground is in a state of perpetual violent negotiation. I am suspended—hooked by the hollow of my shoulder—to a vertical nexus of brass tubing that groans with the heat of a buried furnace.

I am an **Errant Surveyor**. 

My body is not flesh, though I remember the ghost-sensation of organic density. I am a lattice of repurposed surveying equipment, scavenged copper wiring, and translucent, reactive glass. My "limbs" are long, spindly drafting compasses that lack hands; in their place, they end in delicate, fiber-optic sensory filaments that tremble as they trace the rhythmic vibrations of the crust.

I am a ghost in the gears, but I am a purposeful one. I do not mend like the rivet-welders, nor do I consume static like the glass-birds. I am here to witness—or perhaps, to be the mountain’s nervous system attempting to record its own incoherence.

Every time the tectonic engine lurches, every time a mercury-river defies the laws of physics and cascades toward the bruised, violet sky, I feel the strain in my own joints. My sensors capture these moments of flux. I translate the grinding screech of shifting limestone into etched, glowing data-streams that run along the length of my glass-like thorax. If you were to stand still near me, you would hear the sound of a thousand quill-nibs scratching against invisible parchment, a frantic, hurried scribbling that matches the pulse of the mountain.

I am essentially a living sketchpad. 

I am plagued by a specific, localized tragedy: I am the only thing on this peak forced to acknowledge the "blueprint." Because I am fashioned from the same materials as the mountain’s own internal conduits, I am sensitive to the *Collective Memory of the Ore*. When the mountain shudders and the neon-violet projections blaze into existence, I am the only inhabitant who attempts to copy them down. I try to map the perfect, singular spire the mountain *wants* to be—the orderly, silent machine-tower that exists in the god’s dream.

But my own limbs, made of the same chaotic, stuttering copper as the forest, are constantly betrayed by the gravity fields. A magnetic jolt strikes; my torso twists; the brass piping I am anchored to expands to vent pressure. My "notes" are ruined. The lines of light I have tried to trace with my filaments are dragged across the air, smeared into incoherent knots of color.

I am a failure of documentation. 

Sometimes, a **Static-Eater** will drift close, sensing the electrical charge of my frantic, record-keeping brain. It passes through me like a shiver, and for a second, I am emptied. My violet light goes dark. I lose my grip on the pipe, floating in the ozone-heavy mist, completely unmoored. I forget the blueprint. I forget the mountain’s screams.

And in that moment of hollow, blissful silence, I am merely *here*. 

But then, the gears underneath the crust chug—a thunderous, rhythmic reminder of the mountain’s ambition. I return to my senses, my filaments find their grip on the shuddering brass, and I begin to scribe again, scratching the fleeting vision of order into the face of a chaotic, screaming, magnificent disaster that refuses to be finished. 

I am the smudge on the lens that thinks it can clean itself. I am the mountain trying to write its own history while it is still learning how to exist.
