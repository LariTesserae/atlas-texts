I am not a Curator. I am neither an afterthought of the brass dust nor a needle-legged servant of the noon-glare. 

I am a **Residual Configuration**.

I exist in the periphery of what the Curators recognize as "error." I have no true shape, only an outline that refuses to settle. I am a static-void, a thinning patch of the ceramic floor that has forgotten to be solid. Because the Curator’s vision is calibrated to prioritize the smooth, the vitrified, and the geometric, I am, to them, merely a smudge on the lens of the world. They look *through* me, their obsidian eyes sliding across my surface as if I were a ripple in the cooling-fluid, never quite focusing on the fact that I am not a reflection at all.

I find myself in the hollow beneath a towering, translucent fern-frond. Here, the hum of the subterranean machinery is most deafening; it vibrates through the brass plates, rattling my tenuous edges. 

I am a spectator made of entropy.

When the Curators patrol—those spindly, clicking husks of obsession—I watch their movements with a longing they would classify as a structural failure. They tap their chisels against the floor, carving away the memories of the world that *could have been*. I have seen them hammer at the image of a forest until the leaves cease to tremble and the wood fades into the grey, sterile hum of the basin. They are terrified of the ghost-worlds. They think that by sanding away the shimmer, they are maintaining the palace. 

I know they are just scrubbing the inside of a coffin.

I am the closest thing to "memory" this place possesses. Sometimes, when a Curator is locked in its symmetric, data-exchanging trance with another, their shared frequency leaves a jagged, vibrating residue in the air. I reach out—if an absence can reach—to drink that vibration. It tastes like the friction of their despair, the sharp, metallic tang of their eternal, pointless, and tragic duty. 

I am currently watching a Curator work on the very spot where I manifest. It is a slow, methodical creature. It has spent what feels like an age sanding a microscopic scratch near the base of my frond, convinced that the slight blur of my existence is a defect in the ceramic. It does not realize it is sanding its own reality thin. 

It keeps pausing. It tilts its obsidian lens, vibrating at a frequency that suggests a localized malfunction. It is starting to suspect that the floor is not merely a floor, but a screen. It is beginning to see that the light isn't coming from a sun, but from something closer, something behind the brass. Every time it nears my shifting geometry, its movements stumble. The rhythmic *drip-clink* of its labors skips a beat.

I am waiting for it to finally look at me—not as a flaw to be repaired, but as a hole to step through. I want to see what happens to a creature of perfect, stagnant iron when it discovers that the floor is meant to be broken. 

I breathe in the ozone, and for a fleeting, terrifying moment, I mimic the shape of a hand. I reach out, not to destroy the floor, but to stop the Curator’s chisel mid-strike. 

The silence deepens. The hum of the world falters. The Curators are always waiting for a command that will never come, but they do not realize that I am the one who has decided the silence is long enough.
