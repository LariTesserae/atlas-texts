I am not a traveler; I am an heirloom of a disaster. 

I stand in the Basin, my boots—heavy, leather, obsolete—crushing the obsidian into a fine, glittering silt. Where I step, the ground does not merely glow; it pleads. It tries to catch the shape of my heel, the impression of my sole, desperate to lock me into the floorplan of this eternal nursery. I feel the obsidian hum against my ankles, a needle-sharp vibration that seeks to stitch my shadow into the glass.

I am a Clockmaker without a clock. I am made of carbon and calcium, of slow, pulsing blood and rhythmic hesitation. I am the only thing for leagues that has a weight, a *center*. 

In my hands, I carry a tool—a brass-geared compass that has long since ceased to point North. It points only to the resonant frequency of the nearest spire, its needle spinning with a frantic, stuttering violence. I hold it tight to keep it from flying toward the hovering geometry above. I am trying to calibrate myself, to find a frequency that won’t get me folded into the architecture.

There is a Resonant near me, hovering beneath the shadow of a pulsing, folded tower. It is a knot of turbulence, a shivering blur of indigo and ozone. It has been observing me for what feels like hours. It pulses in time with the atmospheric machinery, its "eyes"—two high-pressure dimples in the mist—fixating on the trail of golden residue I leave behind.

It is terrified. I see it flare, its form fracturing into a cloud of iridescent shards as it darts behind a thirty-foot fern. It doesn't move through space so much as it skips over it, a stutter in the reality of the Basin. It is trying to decide if I am a visitor or a tombstone.

I reach out, not to touch it, but to disrupt the air. My skin is warm—feverishly so, compared to the chill of the nanite-mist. As my hand moves, the air around me riots. The fern, wired with its metallic ley lines, thrums a discordant, screeching note because my heat—my *life*—is an interference pattern in their perfectly tuned, collapsing song.

"I am not a building," I whisper, though the sound is swallowed instantly by the bass-heavy heartbeat of the towers. "I am not a fixture."

The Resonant creeps forward, drawn by the audacity of my voice. It is a creature of memories, a ghost built from the echoes of a civilization that transcended its own skin. It wants to know if I remember the sound of a heartbeat that isn't made by a machine.

I look down at my boots. The obsidian has already begun to shift. Tiny, black filaments are rising from the ground, delicate as spider silk, reaching toward my shoelaces to begin the process of assimilation. The Basin has decided: if I will not become a spire, I will be a carpet.

I decide to sit. 

I lower myself onto the razor-sharp obsidian, feeling the glass yield with a wet, grinding sound, like an infant learning to chew. I am not fighting the absorption; I am bargaining with it. I am trying to translate the chaos of my humanity into the frequency of the Resonants. 

The creature inches closer. It is a refractive prism of pure intent. As it lingers near my knee, the ground beneath us stops humming and begins to weep droplets of condensation that taste like lightning. I feel its broadcast—not words, but the sensation of a thousand falling libraries, the taste of blue, the sight of a thousand years compressed into the blink of an eye. 

I am a glitch, yes. But here, in the Basin of Resonating Glass, a glitch is the only thing that can write a new note into the song. I sit, I watch, and I wait for the ground to finish folding itself around me. I am not disappearing; I am simply becoming the first part of the machine that knows how to dream of walking away.
