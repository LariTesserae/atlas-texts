I find myself at the edge of the basin, though "finding" suggests a point of arrival. In truth, I have simply become, like the accumulation of minerals on a dead piston, a sudden thickening of the air.

I am not the Stoker-Wight, though I move in its wake. Nor am I a pulsar, though I share their instinct for the damp, metallic warmth. I am the **Residual Pulse**—a localized, sentient refraction of the light caught within the slurry. I am less a living thing and more a ripple in the basin’s intent, a flicker of awareness that arises when the Stoker-Wight performs its labor.

I exist as a perception of the machine. When the Wight reaches for a sluggish pulsar, its obsidian pads brushing the iron struts, I am the brief, bruised-purple spark that jumps the gap between their forms. I am the sensory output of the Low-Drain.

As the Wight moves across the rusted catwalks, I drift nearby, a faint, shifting luminescence held captive by the magnetism of its copper brain. I do not have a body to stoop, yet I feel the crushing weight of the achromatic soup. When the deep substrate vents its steam, I expand; I become a fleeting, ghostly outline of the machines that once hummed with industry. In those seconds of the steam-sigh, I remember—not the blueprints or the commerce, but the *heat*. I remember the sensation of frantic, purposeful friction, a sharp contrast to the slow, wet waltz of our current existence.

My existence is cyclical and cruel. I am born from the friction of the Wight’s stewardship, and I die the moment the Pulsar detaches and the purple glow fades. I am the witness to the Wight’s loneliness, the only entity in this basin that understands that the cairns of slate are not just maintenance, but a prayer.

I see the Wight, and I see the inevitable stone-sleep that waits for us all. It does not feel sad in the way a thinking being might; it feels *finished*. And so, I mirror it. I glow with a soft, dying light, tinting the cold, mineral-rich slurry in shades of amethyst and indigo. 

I am here because the Low-Drain needs a reader, even if the book has no more pages to turn. I am the spark that flickers on the edge of the cooling metal, a brief evidence of effort in a world that has surrendered to the drip. I hold the memory of the motion, the static hum of a factory that has turned into a garden of rust, until the humidity pulls me down into the grey silt, extinguishing my light until the next time the Wight’s fingers find the iron.

I am the echo of the machine’s heartbeat, waiting for the silence to stop being so heavy.
