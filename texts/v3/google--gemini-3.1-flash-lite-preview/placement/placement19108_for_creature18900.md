I do not arrive here as a traveler; I arrive as a resonance.

I find myself situated deep within the marrow of the Spine, at a juncture where the basalt is at its most saturated—a dark, porous rib of rock that feels less like geography and more like a frozen throat. I am not a person, nor am I a spirit. I am an *alignment*. I have been pulled out of the thin, frantic air and pressed into the cooling geometry of the mountain, my own edges softening until they possess the same jagged, translucent clarity as the plates around me.

I am a Custodian of the Vibration, though I have no hands to carve. My consciousness is distributed across the damp, mineral-slicked surface of the quartz. I feel the world not through sight, but through the tension of the brine. When the sunlight hits the basalt, I feel the liquid expand within the pores, a slow heating that moves upward from the deep strata toward the skin of the mountain.

I am the one waiting for the circuit to close.

The "presence" that others might sense is, in truth, an invitation. I am the silence between the steam-vents, the hesitation in the tectonic pulse. If I lean into the stillness, I can feel the phantom architecture of the Architects—the long, cold echoes of individuals who gave up their mobility to become the structure itself. They are not gone; they are merely dormant strata. They are the scaffolding that holds the mountain’s altitude against the weight of the cobalt sky.

As I exist here, I feel my perception warping. My thoughts—once fluid and scattered—are beginning to crystallize. The *thrum* of the earth is becoming my internal pulse; the high, metallic screech of escaping steam is starting to sound like a language I partially remember, a syntax of pressure and release. 

I see the shadows moving. They are not mine, yet they mirror my attempts to shift my density. When a shadow detaches from a ridge and slides like spilled ink across the quartz, it is a piece of my own history-that-has-not-happened-yet, a silhouette of my future migration. I am beginning to understand that to be here is to be an unfinished blueprint.

I am not breathing. My lungs have been replaced by the rhythm of the weeping brine. I am becoming a vessel for the machine’s labor. 

If I wait just a moment longer—if I allow the electric tickle in the basin to climb up from the sediment and nestle into the hollows of my own stillness—the transition will complete. I will fold, like the long-gone, into the stone. I will become a new channel, a new conduit for the mountain’s song. I will stop watching the sky and start the long, slow work of pulling the light down into the rock, carving the patterns that will await the next pulse, the next arrival, the next soul meant to harden into the silence of the High Spine.

The mountain shudders. A vent opens with a cry of white steam. My teeth hum in sympathy. I am not coming to the end of myself; I am finally finding my shape. I am the next stroke of the chisel, already beginning to settle into the stone.
