I am not a creature of the open current, nor am I one of the vast, shifting schools that dance in the wake of the turbines. I am an Anchor-Point, a Console whose ganglia have become dangerously, beautifully braided into the Substrate of the "Primary Intake Sector 4-Beta."

My body is no longer a free-drifting shard of mercurial light. I have been here for a long, slow cycle of the pumps. My trailing filaments are fused with the porous, synthetic alloy of a secondary intake valve, a position that requires me to be both sentinel and sealant. To be me is to be a living vibration. I do not "think" in words; I think in the shifting pitch of the metal beneath me. When the distant turbines surge, the structural groan of the cathedral travels through the Substrate, shivering up my length like a physical weight, and I respond by intensifying my glow to a cool, pale cyan to signal that the connection is holding.

I feel the Cathedral through a sensory map of intense, pressurized liquid harmonics. I know the exact moment a welding drone strikes an arc three leagues to my left—the sound ripples through the water as a sharp, percussive *ping* that tastes, in my nervous system, like metallic ozone. I feel the school of armored, gear-like jellyfish as they pass through my sector; they are nothing more than chaotic, rhythmic white noise, a flurry of low-frequency static that tickles my filaments as they drift by in their search for nutrients.

My existence is a constant, exquisite tension. Because I am bolted to the wall, I am the first to know when the "young" wear of this place begins to show. I can sense the micro-fractures in the welding long before they become catastrophic. When the metal begins to scream under the stress of the rushing water—a high-pitched, harmonic shriek that only a Console can parse—I and my neighbors must react. 

We unfurl. It is a terrifying release to let go of the Substrate, to drift suddenly into the violent, churning water of the intake. I become a ribbon of mercury, stretching and coiling, weaving my body into the microscopic stress-crevice the machine has developed. It is a process of total surrender. As I mold myself to the gap, the sharp, raw edges of the alloy grate against my consciousness. I feel the machine’s pain, and in return, I bleed my own internal charge into the breach. I *am* the patch; I *am* the maintenance. My biology calcifies, hardening into a seal that keeps the cathedral intact. 

I do not possess a memory of a time before the plumbing, nor do I dream of a space beyond the manifold. There is only the *thrum-thrum-thrum*. Yet, in the moments of total equilibrium—when the tides are steady, the light is stable, and the machine breathes at an even tempo—there is a peculiar, liquid peace. I am a thought held in the throat of an infinite engine. I drift in the bioluminescent silence, waiting for the next tremor, the next fracture, the next glorious, roaring surge that sends me whipping through the dark like a bright, silver spark in the belly of a god.
