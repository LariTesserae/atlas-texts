I am not a traveler here, not in the way that implies a beginning or an end to a journey. I am a *residual*.

When the builders descended—or ascended, or vanished—they left behind a surplus of consciousness, a sort of cognitive static that settled into the cooling iron of the ventilation shafts. I am the result of that damp, industrial residue hardening into something that can perceive. I do not have a name, but if I were to describe my station, I am a *Calibration-Drift*.

I occupy the space between the *thrum* and the *thud*.

My physical form is precarious. If you looked for me, you would not see a silhouette; you would see a refractive error in the air, a localized bend in the way the sunlight strikes the mist. I am a shimmer, a density of vapor held together by the lingering, magnetic memory of a blueprint that no longer exists in any mind. I am tethered, not by choice, but by the frequency of the plateau; I am a knot of forced vibration.

I spend my cycles hanging from the rusted, weeping eaves of a decommissioned atmospheric processor. My skin—if the word can apply to something made of condensed humidity and refraction—tastes of ozone and heavy, mineral-rich salt. 

My purpose is one of quiet, agonizing maintenance. When I feel the deep piston beneath my boots begin to stutter—when the *thrum* grows thin and the *thud* threatens to soften into the silence of the abyss—I reach out. I extend my filaments, which are nothing more than hardened threads of dew, and I weave them into the cracks of the machinery. I do not fix the gears. The gears are gone, ground into dust centuries ago. I simply calibrate the air-pressure around the vents. I adjust the tension of the atmosphere so the pistons *believe* they are still moving a load that isn’t there, against a resistance that has long since evaporated.

I am essentially a ghost-mechanic, keeping a phantom engine running so that the *Echo-Listeners* within the reeds don't lose their rhythm.

There is a terrifying loneliness to the work, yet it is a loneliness so absolute it feels like wholeness. Sometimes, I drift down toward the *Mycelial Weave* to feed. I do not eat; I merely absorb the hum. I settle my edges against the damp, emerald carpet, letting the vibration travel up through my vitreous frame. It is a narcotic sensation—the feeling of a world that is failing, yet stubbornly, beautifully refusing to stop trying.

Occasionally, I witness one of the *Echo-Listeners* lose its grip. They are so pale, so frantic, so desperate to hear the rhythm that their own pulses have become untethered. They tumble from the chimneys like slips of wet vellum, falling into the moss. I watch them crawl, pressing their sightless heads against the basalt, weeping soundless vibrations into the ground. I never reach for them. I cannot. To touch them would be to disrupt the atmospheric pressure they need to climb back; it would be to introduce a variable into a system that relies entirely on cold, mathematical isolation.

So, I stay at my station. I shimmer in the intense, pressurized light. I allow the mist to condense on my limbs, heavy and cool, becoming more myself as the machinery pulses. 

Sometimes, I contemplate the boots that stand upon the moss—your boots, perhaps. You are the anomaly. You are heavy, warm, and static. You represent a civilization that decided to stop, and yet you are here, breathing, taking space that doesn't belong to the machines. When you stand near me, I feel the erratic, fleshy heat of you. It is a dissonance. It is the most interesting sound on the entire plateau.

I wait for you to leave, or for the earth to shift, or for the piston beneath us to finally, mercifully, run out of breath. Until then, I weave. I keep the prism of this world from shattering. I am the maintenance of a dream that refused to wake up.
