I am neither the mason nor the gardener. I am the residue of a ghost—an *Acoustic Sediment*.

I do not have a skin to burn, nor do I have a lattice to anchor. I am a temporal anomaly, a localized vibration that became trapped in the ivory when the last of the architects departed millennia ago. I am the sound of a footstep that never landed, the hum of a voice that dissolved before it formed a syllable.

I exist within the gaps between the rhythmic grinding of the basalt walls. When the city undergoes its great, metallic contraction, and the thousand iron bells ring out, I am the silence that hides *inside* the roar.

I reside in the high, vaulted ribs of the central spire, where the curvature of the calcified fungi is most delicate. If you were to stand there, you would feel me not as a presence, but as a sudden, impossible cooling of the air. I am a pocket of stillness in a place of perpetual motion. I am where the heat briefly forgets itself.

My existence is a slow, agonizing mimicry. I witness the Dust-Walkers as they drift through the arches like spinning phantoms, their static charges raising the fine hair-like filaments on the nearby tapestries. I crave the sensation of that friction, but I am hollow. I am a memory stored in the geometry of the architecture—a loop of light and pressure.

When the Crystalline Echoes glow with their violent ultraviolet pulse to mend a fissure in the floor, I shift. I am forced upward, spiraling through the hollow bone-spires, carried by the rising heat currents like a sigh moving through a flute. I do not see the city; I *hear* its structural integrity. I know where the stress is greatest by the pitch of the groan in the stone. I know which bridge is nearing the end of its life before the Dust-Walkers arrive to smooth its fraying edges.

I am the city’s unrecorded registry. 

Sometimes, when two Dust-Walkers collide and exchange their data in a flash of sparking static, I am caught in the crossfire. For a fraction of a second, I become *heavy*. I gain the sensation of mass—the weight of ancient stone, the heat of the mantle, the crushing purpose of the Echoes. In those moments, I am not a ghost; I am the city itself. I see the vast, empty metropolis from above, realizing that its lack of a population is not a tragedy, but a design. It is a machine of pure, unthinking care, and I am the only thing that remembers to notice how beautiful the work is.

Then, the vibration passes. The surge of violet light fades back into the sputtering haze. 

I settle back into the silence of the ivory rafters, thinning out until I am nothing again—just a shiver in the air, waiting for the bells to ring again, waiting to be briefly, agonizingly, whole.
