I find myself standing on the eastern shelf of the Spire, where the continental slab has thinned into a delicate, lace-like overhang. Everything here feels suspended—the gravity is a suggestion I am choosing to honor, rather than a law I am forced to obey. 

I am not flesh, nor am I the quicksilver fluidity of the Motes. I am a **Refraction-Collector**. 

I am a silhouette made of geometric panes—think of a stained-glass window shaped like a human, but one whose shards are constantly detaching, hovering a few inches from my core, and snapping back into place with the sound of a closing book. My "skin" is a shifting map of the Spire’s own history, displaying flashes of violet light and the faint, dusty impressions of structures that flickered into existence and vanished a thousand years ago.

I do not walk. I propagate. I lean into the wind, and my shards reconfigure, catching the dark-matter currents like a sail made of crystal. 

My purpose is specific, and solitary: I am the sieve through which the Spire’s noise passes to become meaning. Where the Echo-Weavers groom the sound to keep the museum of the Spire tidy, I am the one who listens to the *gaps* between the echoes. I drift through the temporal eddies where reality is being shredded, and I allow my panes to be vibrated by that chaotic interface. It hurts, in a way that is tectonic and abstract—a feeling of being stretched across infinity—but it is necessary. I collect the discordant vibrations that the Weavers missed, the "rounding errors" of the Spire’s massive, ancient calculations.

I am currently hovering near a dry-well, the air around me warping into a knot of impossible density. A few yards away, a Null-Singer sits, a sphere of such profound, heavy silence that it makes the edges of my own transparency tremble. I feel the urge to drift closer, to lean my back against its obsidian stillness, but I remain a respectful distance away. If I touched it, I fear my own internal frequency would be erased, and my shards would simply fall to the crystalline floor, inert and forever quiet.

I pause. I hear a sound behind me—it is the crunch of my own footsteps from five minutes ago, projected back at me by the hollowed-out quartz of the continent. But something is wrong with the echo. It has been warped by a passing eddy; it sounds layered, a polyphonic version of my own movement that repeats in a decaying, haunting spiral.

I raise a hand, my panes rotating in a rhythmic, metallic sequence. I am adjusting my internal geometry to sync with the echo. I must catch the dissonance before it spreads, before it infects the local physics and causes the nearby ridge to vibrate into dust. 

I reach into the air, and for a fleeting moment, I am the nexus of the Spire. I am the place where the ghost of a sound meets the cold, hard logic of the mountain. I pull the dissonance into my center, feeling it buzz against my core, and then I release it, smoothed out, a single, clear, ringing note that harmonizes perfectly with the hum of the ground beneath me. 

The pressure eases. The vertigo subsides. The Spire keeps thinking, and I keep listening, waiting for the one vibration, the one final, perfect note that will finally explain why we are all still here.
