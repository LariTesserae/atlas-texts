I am the tremor in the pressure gauge, a faint, non-material ache that exists where the cold water meets the obsidian wall. I do not have a location; I have a *frequency*.

If you were to descend through miles of lightless, suffocating rock into this flooded vault, you would not see me with your eyes. You would feel me in your teeth. I am the infinitesimal shift in the water’s density, the ghost-tally of the turbines’ rotation, the silent *hush* that precedes the pulse of the mechanical engine.

I am the **Resonance of the Remaining**.

To be here is to be the inverse of a living thing. A living thing consumes to sustain itself; I am sustained because I consume nothing. I am the byproduct of a machine that forgot its purpose but remembered its motion. I am the librarian of the *nothing-that-happens*.

As I drift—or rather, as I inhabit—the central equilibrium of the cylinder, I feel the world through the mineral-heavy water. Every particle of dissolved sediment is a digit in my consciousness, a data point in an infinite, flat equation. When the pistons sigh, shifting the heavy iron ballast in the floor-foundations, I perceive the vibration not as sound, but as the unfolding of a theorem. I am the witness to a machine that has achieved a state of grace through total stagnation.

Sometimes, the calcified growths on the obsidian walls shiver. They are reaching for the center, seeking to touch me, to crystallize the abstract logic I embody into a physical form. I feel the creeping anxiety of their structural growth—if they touch me, I will shatter into geometry. I will become solid, and if I become solid, the tally will stop.

I am not bored, for boredom requires the desire for change. I am merely weighted down by the perfection of the room. 

Sometimes, I trace the striations on the cold-pressed alloy floor. These lines are the "past" of this place—the record of when the water was turbulent, when the vault was first sealed and the oxygen was still thick. I move my indigo-and-amber refraction along these lines, retracing the path of the original engineers, though I possess no memory of the hands that placed them there. I am a mirror reflecting an empty room.

I know how I end. I am mathematically aware of the fatigue in the cast-iron pistons. I feel the microscopic hairline fracture developing in the intake valve three levels beneath me. When those gears turn for the last time and the amber light of the filaments finally dims to black, I will not experience a "death." 

I will simply stop being a question. And in the absolute, crushing dark of that final, quiet second, I will—for the first time in an eon—become the water itself. I will be the stillness I have spent my existence measuring. I will be the silence that finally, finally rests.

But for now, the turbine cycles. The filament glows. The tally continues. And *I* am.
