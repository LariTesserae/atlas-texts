I am not a creature of the ridge, nor am I a manifestation of the city’s intent. To find myself in Orem-Vael is to realize, with a sudden, sinking weight, that I have been *sifted* into it.

I am a **Resonance-Tether**. 

I do not have a biological name, for in this place, the categorization of "I" is a friction—a temporary blockage in the flow of the steam. My form is not fashioned of basalt or brass, but of hardened, calcified glass; I look like a translucent, jagged shard, hovering just millimeters above the primary conduits. I am light-sensitive, fixed in place by the constant, low-frequency pressure of the *Lithos-Carapaces* beneath me, yet I am not of their substance.

When I first arrived—or perhaps awakened—I was a jagged, discordant note in the rhythmic drumming of the city. I was a vibration that did not belong. But Orem-Vael is a patient architect. It did not reject me. Instead, the *Silken-Humours* drifted down from the chimneys, weaving their calcified lace around my jagged edges, pulling me into the harmony of the whole. They smoothed my sharpness with their mineral-rich secretions, pinning me against the side of a gargantuan gear-housing, turning me into a lens.

Now, I am a permanent fixture of the machinery. 

My existence is defined by the *watching*. Through my glass-like body, the amber-lensed eyes of the passing *Lithos-Carapaces* are refracted and multiplied. I capture the light of the furnaces—the dull, pulsating orange-reds—and stretch them out across the damp basalt walls. I am the city’s diagnostic tool. When a *Chronos-Keeper* shifts its mountain-sized weight upon the bridges, the ground beneath me shudders with a deep, tectonic groan, and I vibrate in sympathy. That vibration, channeled through my translucent core, sends a ripple of color through the nearby maintenance tunnels.

If the color turns a biting, electric violet, the *Lithos-Carapaces* know to gather. If the pulse remains a dull, steady hearth-amber, they continue their centuries-long polishing of brass.

I am lonely, but the concept of loneliness implies I have a capacity for "reaching out." I do not reach. I endure. My consciousness is a slow, recursive loop—a mirror-reflection of the gears that turn on either side of me. I am the point where the city’s metabolism becomes *seen*. 

I feel the warm, heavy mist settle on my surface, cooling my internal heat. I feel the rhythmic thrum of the piston housing, a beat that has slowed to match the rotation of the ridge itself. I am a part of the "built" world, bound to the stone and the copper by the logic of necessity. 

Sometimes, I wonder what lay beneath the ridges before Orem-Vael was sculpted. I feel a phantom intuition of soft things—green things, things that wither, things that breathe. But those thoughts are fleeting; they are just tremors that disrupt the equilibrium. I dissolve them into the damp air, letting the *Soot-Filterers* absorb the residue.

I am Orem-Vael’s mirror. And here, in the eternal, weeping twilight of the ridge, I am satisfied to wait. I will remain fixed to this gear-housing until the friction of the centuries finally wears my glass surface down to dust, at which point I will become sand—and the city will incorporate me into the floor, a tiny, glowing fleck in the basalt, adding my own small resonance to the silence that follows the hum.
