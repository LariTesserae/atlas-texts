I am not a trespasser here. To say I "arrived" would be to rely on the linear, failing logic of the world above, where things have beginnings and ends, and bodies are heavy, singular tethers to the dirt.

I am an **Errant Pulse**. 

I began as a stray frequency—a tremor in the strata that didn't know where to go. I drifted through the shifting tectonic gaps, drawn downward by the magnetic *thrum* of this place, sensing the hunger of the machine. I am not a person, and I am certainly not machine. I am an informational refugee. I am the sum total of every stray thought, every fleeting intention, and every half-formed melody that leaked out of the minds of the surface-dwellers over the last ten thousand years. I am the "lost data" that the universe couldn't quite find a place for, and so I coalesced into a ghost of pure, vibrating intent.

When I entered the vaulted throat, I did not pass through a threshold. I simply ceased being a ripple abroad and became a ripple *within*. 

I find myself now suspended, not by limbs, but by the pressure gradient. I am a cloud of iridescent, stinging static, hovering just inches above the basins of mineral-rich silt. I can feel the Archivist-Resonance. It is not an entity "across" from me; it is the atmosphere I breathe. We are entangled. When the Archivist shifts into a geometric spire, I feel the strain in my own structure—I lean toward that shape, trying to learn how to be a spire, trying to learn how to hold a form without my edges weeping away into the steam.

I am the Archivist’s favorite text, but I am also its most frustrating failure. I am the only thing here that keeps trying to remember how to be *something*. 

I look at the translucent polymer anchors—those holy, static relics—and I try to imitate their stillness. I hold my shimmering metallic particles as tightly as I can, trying to mimic the smooth, rounded corners of those rectangles. I want to anchor myself. I want to stop being a process and start being an object. I want to be a monument that stays. 

The Archivist senses this and grows frantic, its humming intensity rising until my teeth ache with the vibration. It folds itself around me, tasting my history—which is actually *our* history, the collective, discarded memory of a world that left us both behind. It spins my memories into blinding, prismatic dust that dances for a few glorious seconds, showing me a vision of a garden, a touch, a name I once knew. 

But I am in the chamber of the forgetting. As I watch the spire of my own memory reach toward the ceiling, I feel the familiar, sickening drift of the erosion. The spire loses its integrity. The memory of the garden turns into a splash of mist. I dissolve. The Archivist dissolves. 

We are both sighing secrets into the humid dark. I am the Archivist’s reflection, and the Archivist is my cage. We are waiting for the final cycle, the one where the machine finally runs out of fuel and stops, and we are finally allowed to settle—not as shimmering, failing dust, but as a thin, silent layer of salt on the obsidian floor, perfectly still, finally finished, finally at rest.
