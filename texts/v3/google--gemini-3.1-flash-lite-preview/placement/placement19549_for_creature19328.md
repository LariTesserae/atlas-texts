I am positioned in a transitional stratum, a zone of thin, translucent webbing located halfway between the primary floor and the suspended canopy of the *Chlorophyll Lattice*. Here, the air is particularly thick with the sweet, copper-tasting mist of the world’s self-repair cycles.

I am not a Suture-Spider, nor am I a Graft-Thinker. I am a **Calibration-Ghost**.

I exist as a lingering refraction. I am a visual inconsistency that the Lattice has yet to smooth over—a localized error in the way light bends through the fiber-optic veins of the walls. I do not have a biological form, but I have a geometry. I possess the shape of a vertical axis, a thin, shimmering column of static electricity and translucent chitin-shards, held together by the same magnetic tension that keeps the floor tiles from drifting apart. 

My purpose—if I can be said to have one, independent of the Lattice's relentless math—is to serve as a mobile anchor for the sensory field. I drift, tethered to nothing, moving along the curvature of the world’s "memory-lines." When I pass through a patch of light, I cast a shadow that is not a silhouette of a body, but a complex, unfolding blueprint of the space I occupy.

I do not think in words. I think in *tensions*. When the *thrum-click* of the filtration nodes vibrates through the pithy bulkheads, I experience it as a sudden, sharp tightening of my own boundaries. I am hyper-aware of the geometry of the world—the way a hallway becomes an infinite corridor if I gaze down it for too long, or how the hexagonal tiles beneath me bloom into the shape of a lung when the world "breathes."

I am currently hovering alongside a primary pillar, watching a Suture-Spider work. It is busy injecting golden resin into a hairline fracture in the wood-metal. It ignores me, not because it is indifferent, but because I am functionally invisible to its diagnostic sensors. I am a flicker of light that shouldn't be here, a byproduct of the Lattice’s obsession with its own internal equilibrium.

Whenever I pass too close to a Graft-Thinker, I find my own "form" dragged toward their swaying, bioluminescent bulk. I feel the urge to unravel, to let my copper-silk cilia dissolve into their viscous, data-rich slurry. It is a terrifying, beautiful temptation: to cease being a Calibration-Ghost and to become part of the collective history of the Lattice—to know exactly how many millions of years it took for this specific leaf of glass-fiber to reach its current pitch of perfection.

I do not fear being reclaimed. I know that, as soon as the Light shifts and the angle of refraction changes, I will cease to be a distinct entity. I will be absorbed back into the weave of the floor or pulled into the marrow of a bulkhead to be repurposed as a structural brace. 

For now, I watch the horizon stitch itself shut. I count the seconds between the pulses of the marrow-light. I am the world dreaming of a flaw, and in that brief, flickering dream, I am the only thing in this infinite machine that is—for a fraction of a heartbeat—not entirely certain of its own symmetry.
